# Editorial Product Information: Trifluoperazine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial product information is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the **United States (FDA)** via the manufacturer/repackager documentation (Sandoz Inc./RemedyRepack Inc.).
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of trifluoperazine clinical data for use by healthcare professionals.
- This document is an editorial synthesis intended for reference and does not replace the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing in any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Trifluoperazine hydrochloride (a piperazine phenothiazine derivative).
- **Brand Names**: Noted as Trifluoperazine Hydrochloride Tablets, USP [US].
- **ATC Code**: N05AB06 (not explicitly stated in source, but standard pharmaceutical classification).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**: 
    - Film-coated tablets: 1 mg, 2 mg, 5 mg, and 10 mg [US].
- **Routes of Administration**: Oral. (Intramuscular administration is mentioned as a potential route for substituted therapy, though only oral tablets were provided in this source) [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Sandoz Inc. (Original Manufacturer); RemedyRepack Inc. (Repackager) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Schizophrenia**: For the clinical management of schizophrenic disorders [US-approved].
- **Generalized Non-psychotic Anxiety**: For the short-term treatment of non-psychotic anxiety. It is specifically noted that trifluoperazine is not a first-line therapy for anxiety due to its risk profile compared to alternatives like benzodiazepines [US-approved].
- **Note**: Trifluoperazine has not been demonstrated as effective for managing behavioral complications in patients with mental retardation [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
Dosage must be individualised. Use the lowest effective dose.

### Schizophrenia/Psychotic Disorders
- **Starting Dose**: 2 mg to 5 mg twice daily. Debilitated or emaciated patients should begin at the lower end of the range [US].
- **Titration**: Increase dosage gradually. Most patients reach an optimum response at 15 mg to 20 mg daily, though some may require ≥40 mg daily.
- **Maintenance**: Once a maximum response is achieved, reduce dosage gradually to the maintenance level. Long action allows for twice-daily or sometimes once-daily dosing [US].

### Non-psychotic Anxiety
- **Usual Dose**: 1 mg or 2 mg twice daily.
- **Maximum Dose**: Do not exceed 6 mg per day.
- **Duration**: Do not administer for longer than 12 weeks due to the risk of persistent, irreversible tardive dyskinesia [US].

### Psychotic Children (Ages 6–12)
- **Starting Dose**: 1 mg once or twice daily (for children who are hospitalized or under close supervision).
- **Titration**: Increase gradually until symptoms are controlled or side effects become limiting. 
- **Maximum Dose**: Usually does not exceed 15 mg daily [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to phenothiazines [US].
- Comatose or severely depressed states resulting from central nervous system (CNS) depressants [US].
- Existing blood dyscrasias or bone marrow depression [US].
- Pre-existing liver damage [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning: Increased Mortality in Elderly Patients with Dementia-Related Psychosis
Elderly patients with dementia-related psychosis treated with antipsychotic drugs face an increased risk of death (approx. 1.6 to 1.7 times the rate of placebo). Causes of death are primarily cardiovascular or infectious. **Trifluoperazine is not approved for the treatment of dementia-related psychosis** [US].

### General Warnings
- **Tardive Dyskinesia (TD)**: Potentially irreversible, involuntary movements may develop. Risk increases with treatment duration and total cumulative dose, particularly in the elderly and women. Use the smallest dose and shortest duration necessary [US].
- **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS)**: A potentially fatal complex characterized by hyperpyrexia, muscle rigidity, altered mental status, and autonomic instability. Discontinue trifluoperazine immediately if NMS is suspected [US].
- **CNS Depression**: May impair physical and mental abilities; patients should exercise caution when operating machinery or driving [US].
- **Falls**: Due to somnolence, postural hypotension, and motor instability, the drug may increase the risk of falls and subsequent fractures [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Hematology**: Monitor Complete Blood Count (CBC) in patients with pre-existing low White Blood Cell (WBC) counts or a history of drug-induced neutropenia. Discontinue if WBC decline is observed [US].
- **Hepatic**: Conduct liver studies if fever with "flu-like" symptoms occurs to assess for cholestatic jaundice [US].
- **Ocular**: Discontinue if ophthalmoscopic or visual field examinations show retinal changes (retinopathy) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Note: Frequencies were not specifically quantified in the source document.*

- **CNS**: Drowsiness, dizziness, insomnia, fatigue, muscular weakness.
- **Extrapyramidal Symptoms (EPS)**: 
    - **Dystonia**: Spasms of neck muscles, difficulty swallowing, or protrusion of the tongue (higher risk in males and younger age groups).
    - **Pseudo-parkinsonism**: Mask-like facies, tremors, pill-rolling motion, cogwheel rigidity.
    - **Motor Restlessness/Akathisia**: Agitation or jitteriness.
- **Autonomic**: Dry mouth, blurred vision, nasal congestion, constipation, urinary retention, miosis/mydriasis.
- **Hematologic**: Agranulocytosis, pancytopenia, thrombocytopenia, anemia.
- **Endocrine/Reproductive**: Hyperprolactinemia, amenorrhea, galactorrhea, gynecomastia, impotence, false-positive pregnancy tests.
- **Cardiovascular**: Hypotension (sometimes fatal), cardiac arrest, EKG changes (Q and T wave distortions).
- **Hepatic**: Cholestatic jaundice, biliary stasis.
- **Dermatologic**: Photosensitivity, rash, exfoliative dermatitis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CNS Depressants**: Additive effects with alcohol, sedatives, narcotics, anesthetics, or tranquilizers [US].
- **Anticoagulants**: Phenothiazines may diminish the effect of oral anticoagulants [US].
- **Propranolol**: Concomitant use increases plasma levels of both drugs [US].
- **Guanethidine**: Antihypertensive effects may be counteracted [US].
- **Thiazide Diuretics**: May accentuate orthostatic hypotension [US].
- **Anticonvulsants/Phenytoin**: Phenothiazines lower the seizure threshold; adjustments to anticonvulsants may be necessary. Phenothiazines may interfere with phenytoin metabolism, leading to toxicity [US].
- **Metrizamide**: Discontinue trifluoperazine 48 hours before and 24 hours after myelography to avoid lowering the seizure threshold [US].
- **Epinephrine**: Do not use epinephrine for phenothiazine-induced hypotension, as it may cause a paradoxical further lowering of blood pressure [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: **Category Not Specified** [US source states "Safety has not been established"].
    - **Risks**: Neonates exposed in the third trimester are at risk for extrapyramidal and/or withdrawal symptoms (e.g., agitation, hypertonia, respiratory distress, feeding disorders). Use only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: Phenothiazines are excreted in breast milk. A decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the drug's importance to the mother [US].
- **Fertility**: Animal studies show chromosomal aberrations in spermatocytes and abnormal sperm in rodents; clinical significance in humans is unknown [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Approved for children ages 6 to 12 for psychotic disorders under close supervision. Not established for non-psychotic anxiety in children [US].
- **Elderly Use**: Increased risk of death in dementia-related psychosis. Generally, use lower dose ranges. Increased susceptibility to hypotension and neuromuscular reactions [US].
- **Renal Impairment**: Not specified in available sources.
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Contraindicated in pre-existing liver damage [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: A phenothiazine neuroleptic. While the specific molecular mechanism is not detailed in the source, it belongs to the class of drugs that traditionally block dopamine receptors [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption/Distribution**: Not specified in available sources.
    - **Metabolism**: Not specified in available sources.
    - **Elimination**: The drug is noted to have an "inherent long action" [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics**: Exhibits alpha-adrenergic blockade and elevates serum prolactin levels [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Generalized Anxiety Disorder**: Effectiveness was established in a four-week clinical multicenter study of outpatients with GAD (DSM-III) [US].
- **Schizophrenia**: Efficacy established through clinical experience; specific pivotal trial data not detailed in the provided source.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C (59°F–86°F) [US].
- **Conditions**: Protect from moisture. Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States**: Rx-only. Poison schedule not specified in source [US].
