# Editorial Product Information: Elvitegravir, Cobicistat, Emtricitabine, and Tenofovir Alafenamide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the provided Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically focused overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should always refer to the current approved documentation in their local region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** 
    - Elvitegravir (Integrase strand transfer inhibitor)
    - Cobicistat (Pharmacokinetic enhancer; CYP3A inhibitor)
    - Emtricitabine (Nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor)
    - Tenofovir alafenamide fumarate (Nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor prodrug)
- **Brand Name:** **GENVOYA** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** J05AR09
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Film-coated, capsule-shaped, green tablets debossed with ‘GSI’ and ‘510’. Each tablet contains:
    - Elvitegravir: 150 mg
    - Cobicistat: 150 mg
    - Emtricitabine: 200 mg
    - Tenofovir alafenamide: 10 mg (equivalent to 12.04 mg of tenofovir alafenamide fumarate)
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Gilead Sciences Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **HIV-1 Infection:** Indicated as a complete single-tablet regimen for the treatment of HIV-1 infection in:
    - Adults and pediatric patients weighing at least 25 kg.
    - Patients may be treatment-naïve or virologically suppressed (HIV-1 RNA <50 copies/mL) on a stable antiretroviral regimen for at least 6 months.
- **Criteria for Switch:** In virologically suppressed patients, the regimen is intended to replace the current antiretroviral therapy. Patients must have no history of treatment failure and no known mutations associated with resistance to the individual components of the combination.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric ≥25 kg):** One tablet taken once daily.
- **Administration Instructions:** Must be taken **with food**.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - No dose adjustment is required in adults with estimated creatinine clearance (CrCl) ≥30 mL/min.
    - Not recommended for initiation in patients with CrCl <30 mL/min.
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - No dose adjustment required for mild (Child-Pugh Class A) or moderate (Child-Pugh Class B) impairment.
    - Not recommended in patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class C).
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in provided source.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to any of the active ingredients or excipients (including lactose).
- **CYP3A-Dependent Drugs:** Co-administration with drugs highly dependent on CYP3A for clearance, where elevated levels are associated with serious/life-threatening events:
    - **Alpha 1-adrenoreceptor antagonists:** Alfuzosin.
    - **Ergot derivatives:** Dihydroergotamine, ergonovine, ergotamine, methylergonovine.
    - **GI motility agents:** Cisapride.
    - **Lipid-modifying agents:** Lovastatin, simvastatin, lomitapide.
    - **Neuroleptics:** Pimozide.
    - **PDE-5 inhibitors:** Sildenafil (when used for pulmonary arterial hypertension).
    - **Sedative/hypnotics:** Orally administered midazolam, triazolam.
- **CYP3A Inducers:** Co-administration with potent inducers of CYP3A (potential for loss of virologic response):
    - **Anticonvulsants:** Carbamazepine, phenobarbital, phenytoin.
    - **Antimycobacterials:** Rifampicin.
    - **Herbal products:** St. John’s wort (*Hypericum perforatum*).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **HBV Co-infection:** Discontinuation in patients co-infected with HIV and Hepatitis B Virus (HBV) may cause severe acute exacerbations of hepatitis. Clinical and laboratory monitoring is required for several months post-treatment.
- **Immune Reconstitution Syndrome:** Inflammatory reactions to latent or opportunistic pathogens (e.g., CMV retinitis, mycobacterial infections) may occur during the initial phase of treatment. Autoimmune disorders (e.g., Graves' disease) have also been reported.
- **Renal Toxicity:** Post-marketing cases of acute renal failure and Fanconi syndrome have been reported with tenofovir prodrugs. Monitor CrCl, urine glucose, and urine protein in all patients; monitor serum phosphorus in patients with chronic kidney disease.
- **Lactic Acidosis/Hepatomegaly with Steatosis:** While more common with older NRTIs, cases (including fatal ones) have been reported. Suspend treatment if clinical or laboratory findings suggest lactic acidosis or pronounced hepatotoxicity.
- **Lower Exposures in Pregnancy:** Not recommended for use during pregnancy due to significantly lower exposures of cobicistat and elvitegravir during the second and third trimesters.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Nausea (11%).
- **Common (1–10%):**
    - Diarrhea (7%)
    - Headache (6%)
    - Fatigue (5%)
    - Abdominal pain, dyspepsia, flatulence, rash, and vomiting.
- **Serious/Post-marketing Effects:**
    - **Renal:** Acute renal failure, proximal renal tubulopathy, Fanconi syndrome.
    - **Skin:** Angioedema, urticaria.
    - **Hepatobiliary:** Autoimmune hepatitis.
- **Laboratory Abnormalities (Grades 3-4):** Creatine kinase elevation (11%), LDL-cholesterol elevation (11%), fasting total cholesterol (4%), amylase (3%), and hematuria (3%).
- **Cobicistat Effect:** Cobicistat increases serum creatinine (mean increase ~0.04 mg/dL) by inhibiting tubular secretion without affecting actual glomerular filtration rate (GFR).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Enhancer (Cobicistat):** A strong inhibitor of CYP3A and a substrate of CYP3A. It also inhibits CYP2D6 and transporters P-gp, BCRP, OATP1B1, and OATP1B3.
- **Polyvalent Cations:** Elvitegravir concentrations are significantly reduced by antacids or supplements containing Mg, Al, Ca, Fe, or Zn. **Separate administration by at least 2 hours.**
- **Other Antiretrovirals:** Should not be co-administered with other HIV antiretroviral products, as it is a complete regimen. Do not use with products containing tenofovir disoproxil fumarate, lamivudine, adefovir dipivoxil, or ritonavir.
- **Specific Management Notes:**
    - **Anticoagulants:** Monitor INR with warfarin. Co-administration with apixaban or rivaroxaban is not recommended.
    - **Antibacterials:** Reduce clarithromycin dose by 50% if CrCl is 30–60 mL/min.
    - **Hormonal Contraceptives:** Use a product containing at least 30 mcg ethinylestradiol. Non-hormonal contraception is recommended.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy Category:** **Category B3** [Australian].
- **Recommendation:** **Not recommended during pregnancy.** Lower exposures of elvitegravir and cobicistat in the 2nd and 3rd trimesters increase the risk of virologic failure and mother-to-child transmission. 
- **Breastfeeding:** Breastfeeding is not recommended due to the potential for HIV transmission and the secretion of emtricitabine (and likely other components) into human milk.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for children weighing ≥25 kg. Data in treatment-naïve children aged 6–12 is limited. 
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required. Clinical trials included a limited number of patients ≥65 years, with no significant differences in safety or efficacy noted compared to younger adults.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment for CrCl ≥30 mL/min. Safety not established for CrCl <30 mL/min.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for mild/moderate (Child-Pugh A/B). Avoid in severe (Child-Pugh C) impairment.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Elvitegravir:** Integrase strand transfer inhibitor (INSTI) that prevents HIV-1 DNA integration into host genomic DNA.
    - **Cobicistat:** PK enhancer with no antiviral activity; inhibits CYP3A to increase elvitegravir systemic exposure.
    - **Emtricitabine:** Cytidine analogue NRTI; inhibits HIV-1 reverse transcriptase causing chain termination.
    - **Tenofovir Alafenamide (TAF):** A phosphonamidate prodrug of tenofovir. TAF is more stable in plasma than the older tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF), allowing higher intracellular concentrations in lymphoid tissues with lower systemic tenofovir exposure.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Tmax ranges from 1 hour (TAF) to 4 hours (Elvitegravir). 
    - **Food Effect:** High-fat meals increase elvitegravir exposure by 87%. Must be taken with food.
    - **Elimination:** Elvitegravir (fecal 94.8%), Cobicistat (fecal 86.2%), Emtricitabine (renal 86%), TAF (metabolized to tenofovir, then renal).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Treatment-Naïve Adults (Studies 104 and 111):** At Week 144, Genvoya (84%) demonstrated statistical superiority in virologic suppression (HIV-1 RNA <50 copies/mL) compared to Stribild (80%). Genvoya showed more favorable effects on bone mineral density (BMD) and renal laboratory parameters compared to the TDF-containing regimen.
- **Virologically Suppressed Adults (Study 109):** Switching to Genvoya was superior (93%) to staying on a TDF-based regimen (89%) at Week 96, with significant improvements in BMD and renal markers.
- **Renal Impairment (Study 112):** In patients with CrCl 30–69 mL/min, switching to Genvoya was well-tolerated and maintained virologic suppression (83.1% at Week 144).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Container:** High-density polyethylene (HDPE) bottle with a child-resistant closure, containing a silica gel desiccant and polyester coil.
