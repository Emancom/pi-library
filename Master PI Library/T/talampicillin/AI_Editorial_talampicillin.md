# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **United States (FDA)** for levocarnitine injection. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory guidance.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements in their region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - **Administration:** Delivered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose:** Often utilised in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours, and never less frequently than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported administered dose is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** 
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - **Administration:** Delivered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Monitoring and Titration
- **Metabolic Disorders:** It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Monitoring should be weekly or monthly and include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target range for free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].
- **ESRD:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) levels <40 to 50 µmol/L. Downward dose adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be implemented as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued and appropriate medical treatment initiated [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **General Precautions:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of the oral formulation in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of the patient populations (ESRD or metabolic disorders).

### Frequent Adverse Effects (≥5% in ESRD clinical trials) [US]:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (30–35%), flu syndrome (25–29%), headache (22–37%), abdominal pain (5–21%), fever (5–12%), accidental injury (8–12%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), chest pain (6–15%), tachycardia (5–9%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%), rhinitis (6–11%).
- **Neurological:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%).

### Other Reported Effects:
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, hypervolemia [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis, melena [US].
- **Miscellaneous:** Body odor, transient nausea/vomiting [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalised Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Clinical Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not assigned to a specific letter category in the current PI]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. However, no adequate, well-controlled studies exist in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased milk concentrations of levocarnitine following exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders apply to paediatric patients (see Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in provided sources [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. Safety/efficacy of the *oral* formulation is not established in renal insufficiency due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule essential for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound (including albumin). Distribution half-life is approximately 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Levocarnitine clears acylCoA compounds, the accumulation of which can lead to life-threatening acidosis in patients with fatty acid metabolism defects [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, IV levocarnitine (20 mg/kg post-dialysis) increased mean pre-dialysis plasma concentrations from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. 
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** A study (n≈30 per group) comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed a dose-proportional increase in trough levocarnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks.
- **Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the impact on clinical outcomes in this specific population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and avoid excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives. Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].

- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources [US].
