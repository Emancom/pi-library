# Editorial Product Information: Thalidomide

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (TGA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of thalidomide, synthesising clinical data for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians must consult local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Thalidomide
- **Chemical Class:** Immunomodulatory agent; cytostatic anti-cancer therapy.
- **Brand Name:** **THALOMID** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** L04AX01
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Oral Capsules: 50 mg and 100 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Bristol-Myers Squibb Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Multiple Myeloma (MM):**
    - In combination with melphalan and prednisone (MPT) for untreated MM in patients aged ≥ 65 years or those ineligible for high-dose chemotherapy [Australian].
    - In combination with dexamethasone as induction therapy prior to high-dose chemotherapy with autologous stem cell rescue for untreated MM [Australian].
    - Monotherapy for MM following the failure of standard therapies [Australian].
- **Erythema Nodosum Leprosum (ENL):**
    - Acute treatment of moderate to severe cutaneous manifestations of ENL. (Note: Not indicated as monotherapy if moderate to severe neuritis is present) [Australian].
    - Maintenance therapy for the prevention and suppression of recurrent cutaneous ENL manifestations [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- **Timing:** Administer as a single daily dose in the evening to reduce daytime sedation [Australian].
- **Food:** Should be taken at least one hour after food [Australian].
- **Supervision:** Must be initiated and monitored by specialists experienced in oncology, haematology, or leprosy management [Australian].

### Multiple Myeloma Dosing
- **Untreated MM (Combination with Melphalan/Prednisone):** 200 mg daily. For patients > 75 years, the recommended starting dose is 100 mg daily. Maximum duration is 12 cycles of 6 weeks each [Australian].
- **Untreated MM (Combination with Dexamethasone):** 200 mg daily for 4 cycles of 4 weeks. In elderly patients with poor performance status, start at 50 mg daily and titrate to 200 mg over 4 weeks [Australian].
- **Refractory MM (Monotherapy):** Start at 200 mg daily; increase by 100 mg weekly to a maximum of 400 mg daily based on tolerance [Australian].

### Erythema Nodosum Leprosum (ENL) Dosing
- **Adult Dosing:** Start at 100 mg daily. If symptoms are uncontrolled, increase by 100 mg at weekly intervals to a maximum of 400 mg daily [Australian].
- **Tapering:** Attempt to taper every 3–6 months in 50 mg decrements every 2–4 weeks once the active reaction is controlled [Australian].

### Dose Adjustments
- **Thromboembolism:** Discontinue thalidomide and initiate anticoagulation. Restart at the original dose only after stabilisation and risk-benefit assessment [Australian].
- **Peripheral Neuropathy:** Discontinue if neuropathy occurs. May restart at a 50% dose reduction if symptoms improve to Grade 1. Permanently discontinue for Grade 3 or 4 [Australian].
- **Skin Rash:** Consider interruption for Grade 2–3 rash. Permanently discontinue for Grade 4 rash or suspected SJS/TEN/DRESS [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy:** Absolutely contraindicated due to severe teratogenicity [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to thalidomide or any excipients [Australian].
- **Paediatric:** Patients under 12 years of age [Australian].
- **Reproductive Safety:** Women of childbearing potential not using/unable to use adequate contraception; males unable/unwilling to comply with required contraceptive measures [Australian].
- **Alternative Therapy:** Women of childbearing potential if an alternative treatment of non-inferior efficacy is available [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Teratogenicity [Australian]:** Thalidomide is a potent human teratogen. Even a single dose can cause severe, life-threatening birth defects (e.g., phocomelia, amelia) or foetal death. 
- **Risk Management Program:** Restricted availability via the "i-access" program. Requires written informed consent and strict adherence to pregnancy testing and contraception [Australian].
- **Thromboembolism:** Significantly increased risk of venous thromboembolism (VVT) and arterial thromboembolism, especially when combined with dexamethasone or cytotoxic agents. Prophylaxis (e.g., LMWH or warfarin) is recommended for high-risk patients [Australian].
- **Peripheral Neuropathy:** Very common and potentially irreversible. Requires monthly clinical assessment and baseline/6-monthly sensory nerve action potential (SNAP) monitoring [Australian].
- **Skin Reactions:** Risk of fatal SJS, TEN, and DRESS. Do not rechallenge if these occur [Australian].
- **Haematological Effects:** Grade 3/4 neutropenia and thrombocytopenia reported. Monitor ANC; consider withholding if ANC < 750/mm³ [Australian].
- **Other Precautions:**
    - Bradycardia and syncope [Australian].
    - Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy (PML) [Australian].
    - Somnolence and sedation [Australian].
    - Seizures [Australian].
    - Reactivation of Hepatitis B [Australian].
    - Thyroid monitoring for hypothyroidism [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Nearly all patients experience adverse effects.
- **Very Common (>10%):** DVT, constipation, peripheral oedema, tremor, dizziness, fatigue, somnolence, peripheral neuropathy, neutropenia, anaemia, paraesthesia [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Pneumonia, depression, confusional state, bradycardia, dyspnoea, vomiting, dry skin, pyrexia [Australian].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Teratogenicity, thromboembolism, SJS/TEN, DRESS, myocardial infarction, and PML [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Sedatives:** Enhances effects of alcohol, barbiturates, benzodiazepines, and other hypnotics/anxiolytics [Australian].
- **Bradycardic Agents:** Use caution with beta-blockers or anticholinesterases [Australian].
- **Neurotoxic Agents:** Use caution with vincristine, zalcitabine, or didanosine [Australian].
- **Contraceptive Interactions:** CYP-inducing agents (e.g., dexamethasone, phenytoin, rifampin) may reduce the efficacy of hormonal contraceptives; two additional methods are required [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Not extensively metabolised by CYP450; no significant interaction with warfarin or digoxin observed [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy Category:** **Category X [Australian]**. Thalidomide must not be used during pregnancy. Women must use one highly effective method and one additional method of contraception for 4 weeks before, during, and 4 weeks after treatment.
- **Male Patients:** Thalidomide is present in semen. Males must use condoms during treatment and for 4 weeks after cessation when engaging in sexual activity with women of childbearing potential [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety not established; breastfeeding is contraindicated [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for patients < 12 years. Limited data for ages 12–17 [Australian].
- **Elderly:** No specific PK changes. Increased risk of serious adverse events; dose reduction (100 mg) recommended for patients > 75 years in MPT regimens [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No specific studies conducted; not specified in available sources [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Likely involves TNF-α suppression, inhibition of leukocyte migration, anti-angiogenesis, and direct inhibition of myeloma cell growth [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Slow; $T_{max}$ ~4–5 hours. Food effect is minimal [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** 55%–65% protein bound. Present in semen [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily non-enzymatic hydrolysis in plasma; not significantly metabolised by the liver [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily excreted as metabolites in urine; half-life ~5–7 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **IFM 99-06:** In untreated MM (aged 65–75), MPT showed superior median overall survival (51.6 months) vs. MP (33.2 months) [Australian].
- **MM-003:** Thalidomide/Dexamethasone showed superior progression-free survival (64.4 weeks) vs. Placebo/Dexamethasone (28.0 weeks) [Australian].
- **ENL Studies:** Demonstrated significant reduction in skin lesions and steroid requirements (60% reduction) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Packaging:** Store in the original package to protect from light [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** 5 years [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
- **Control Measures:** Highly restricted through mandatory Risk Management Programs (i-access) [Australian].
