# Editorial Product Information: Triamcinolone, Neomycin, Nystatin, and Gramicidin

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of the combination therapy containing triamcinolone acetonide, neomycin sulfate, nystatin, and gramicidin. It is intended for use by healthcare professionals as a clinical reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in specific jurisdictions. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** 
    - **Triamcinolone acetonide:** A synthetic corticosteroid with anti-inflammatory properties.
    - **Neomycin (as sulfate):** An aminoglycoside antibiotic.
    - **Nystatin:** A polyene antifungal.
    - **Gramicidin:** A polypeptide antibiotic.
- **Brand Names:** Kenacomb [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Topical Ointment:** Each gram contains triamcinolone acetonide 1 mg (0.1%), neomycin (as sulfate) 2.5 mg, gramicidin 0.25 mg, and nystatin 100,000 IU [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Topical
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aspen Pharmacare Australia Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Dermatoses:** Indicated for the symptomatic relief of inflammatory and pruritic manifestations of dermatoses that are either already infected or likely to become infected by susceptible organisms [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** Apply a thin layer to the affected areas 2 to 3 times daily [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - For skin use only; avoid contact with eyes [Australian].
    - Inguinal applications should be used sparingly and with loose-fitting clothing to prevent occlusion [Australian].
    - Even if symptoms improve early, the full prescribed course should be completed to prevent recurrence [Australian].
- **Occlusive Dressings:** Avoid the use of occlusive dressings (including tight-fitting plastic pants or incontinence garments) as they increase the risk of systemic absorption and sensitivity reactions [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to triamcinolone, neomycin, nystatin, gramicidin, or any excipients [Australian].
- **Ophthalmic Use:** Contraindicated for use in the eyes [Australian].
- **Specific Skin Infections:** Tuberculous lesions and most viral skin lesions (including *Herpes simplex*, vaccinia, and varicella) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Systemic Absorption:** Topical corticosteroids and neomycin can be absorbed through the skin, particularly when used over large surface areas, on damaged skin, or for prolonged periods. This may lead to:
    - Reversible hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis suppression.
    - Cushing's syndrome, hyperglycaemia, and glucosuria [Australian].
- **Ototoxicity and Nephrotoxicity:** Due to the neomycin component, use must be avoided in patients with extensive skin damage where significant absorption is possible [Australian].
- **Superinfection:** Prolonged use may result in the overgrowth of non-susceptible organisms, including fungi other than *Candida*. If superinfection occurs, appropriate antimicrobial therapy should be initiated [Australian].
- **Masking of Reactions:** The corticosteroid component may mask signs of hypersensitivity reactions to the anti-infective ingredients [Australian].
- **Visual Disturbances:** Use of systemic and topical corticosteroids has been associated with visual disturbances, including cataract, glaucoma, and rare conditions such as central serous chorioretinopathy (CSCR). Patients reporting blurred vision should be referred to an ophthalmologist [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Frequency (Uncommon):** The following local reactions have been reported with topical corticosteroids (listed in decreasing order of occurrence): burning, itching, irritation, dryness, folliculitis, hypertrichosis, acneform eruptions, hypopigmentation, perioral dermatitis, allergic contact dermatitis, skin maceration, secondary infection, skin atrophy, striae, and miliaria [Australian].
- **Component-Specific Effects:**
    - **Nystatin:** Generally well-tolerated, though irritation and contact dermatitis have occurred [Australian].
    - **Neomycin:** Delayed-type hypersensitivity; sensitisation may occur with prolonged use [Australian].
    - **Gramicidin:** Sensitivity reactions have been reported [Australian].
- **Serious Effects:** Systemic ototoxicity and nephrotoxicity (associated with neomycin absorption) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **General:** No specific interaction data provided in available sources [Australian].
- **Laboratory Tests:** If therapeutic response is lacking, repeated KOH smears or cultures are recommended. Urinary free cortisol and ACTH stimulation tests are useful for evaluating HPA axis suppression [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category D:** Aminoglycosides (like neomycin/gentamicin) cross the placenta and have been associated with selective uptake by the foetal kidney and eighth cranial nerve damage in utero. While topical absorption is lower than systemic, potential nephrotoxicity and ototoxicity to the foetus must be considered [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if topical administration results in sufficient absorption to be detectable in breast milk. While systemically administered corticosteroids are secreted into milk, they are unlikely to affect the infant at low levels. Caution is advised [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Long-term animal studies evaluating carcinogenic or mutagenic potential or effects on fertility have not been performed [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Children are at higher risk for systemic toxicity and HPA axis suppression due to a higher skin surface area to body weight ratio.
    - Reported effects include linear growth retardation, delayed weight gain, and intracranial hypertension (e.g., bulging fontanelles, headaches, bilateral papilloedema) [Australian].
    - Use should be restricted to the lowest effective amount for the shortest duration [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available in provided sources [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified, though systemic neomycin absorption poses a risk of nephrotoxicity [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:**
    - **Triamcinolone acetonide:** Provides anti-inflammatory, antipruritic, and vasoconstrictive actions [Australian].
    - **Nystatin:** Binds to sterols in fungal cell membranes, altering permeability and causing leakage of intracellular components. It is primary active against *Candida albicans* [Australian].
    - **Neomycin:** Inhibits protein synthesis in susceptible gram-negative organisms (excluding *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*) [Australian].
    - **Gramicidin:** Alters cell membrane permeability in many gram-positive organisms [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Nystatin and gramicidin are not absorbed through intact skin/mucosa. Triamcinolone and neomycin can be absorbed through inflamed or damaged skin [Australian].
    - **Metabolism/Excretion:** Absorbed corticosteroids are metabolised in the liver and excreted by the kidneys. Absorbed neomycin is excreted unchanged by the kidneys with a half-life of 2–3 hours [Australian].
