# Editorial Product Information: Ticagrelor

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following official Product Information (PI) source:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: "Product Information — BRICALOR (ticagrelor) Tablets"
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a single, clinically actionable reference.
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis by a clinical pharmacist and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI. Prescribers must consult the local approved PI before initiating therapy.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Ticagrelor
- **Chemical Class**: Cyclopentyltriazolopyrimidines (CPTP)
- **Brand Names**: **BRICALOR** [Australian]
- **ATC Code**: B01AC24
- **Dosage Form and Strength**: 
    - Film-coated tablets: 90 mg [Australian]
- **Route of Administration**: Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Pharmacor Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Coronary Syndromes (ACS)**: Indicated in combination with aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid) for the prevention of atherothrombotic events (cardiovascular death, myocardial infarction, and stroke) in adults with:
    - Unstable Angina (UA)
    - Non-ST elevation Myocardial Infarction (NSTEMI)
    - ST elevation Myocardial Infarction (STEMI)
- **Clinical Management**: Approved for use in patients managed medically, as well as those undergoing percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) or coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Loading Dose**: Initiate treatment with a single 180 mg dose (two 90 mg tablets).
- **Maintenance Dose**: 90 mg taken twice daily.
- **Concomitant Therapy**: Must be used with daily aspirin unless specifically contraindicated.
    - Following an initial loading dose of aspirin, the recommended maintenance dose of aspirin is 75–150 mg daily (target 100 mg). 
    - **Note**: Maintenance aspirin doses >300 mg are not recommended due to reduced efficacy of ticagrelor at higher aspirin doses [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- May be taken with or without food.
- **Dysphagia**: For patients unable to swallow whole tablets, 90 mg tablets may be crushed to a fine powder, mixed in half a glass of water, and consumed immediately. The glass should be rinsed with another half-glass of water to ensure the full dose is taken.
- **Nasogastric (NG) Tube**: Crushed tablets (90 mg) can be administered via a CH8 or larger NG tube. The dispersion should be prepared with 50 mL of water, followed by a 50 mL flush and a final 25 mL syringe flush [Australian].

### Switching and Discontinuation
- **Switching**: When switching from other anti-platelet therapy (e.g., clopidogrel), the first dose of ticagrelor should be administered 24 hours after the last dose of the previous medication.
- **Missed Dose**: Skip the missed dose and take the next dose at the scheduled time. 
- **Duration**: Recommended for at least 12 months unless clinically indicated otherwise. Premature cessation increases the risk of CV death or MI.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to ticagrelor or any excipient.
- Active pathological bleeding (e.g., peptic ulcer or intracranial haemorrhage).
- History of intracranial haemorrhage.
- Moderate to severe hepatic impairment.
- Co-administration with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, clarithromycin, nefazodone, ritonavir, atazanavir).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Bleeding Risk**: As with all anti-platelet agents, ticagrelor increases the risk of bleeding. Use with caution in patients with a propensity to bleed (e.g., recent trauma, surgery, or coagulation disorders) or those taking anticoagulants, NSAIDs, or fibrinolytics. 
- **Surgical Procedures**: Discontinue ticagrelor 5 days prior to elective surgery if an anti-platelet effect is not desired.
- **Dyspnoea**: Reported in approximately 13.8% of patients. Usually mild to moderate and often resolves without treatment discontinuation. Use with caution in patients with asthma or COPD.
- **Bradyarrhythmia**: Holter monitoring has shown an increased frequency of mostly asymptomatic ventricular pauses. Use caution in patients at risk of bradycardic events (e.g., sick sinus syndrome or high-grade AV block without a pacemaker).
- **Laboratory Abnormalities**:
    - **Creatinine**: Levels may increase; check renal function after one month of therapy and regularly thereafter, especially in patients ≥75 years.
    - **Uric Acid**: Hyperuricaemia and gout may occur. Use caution in patients with a history of hyperuricaemia or gouty arthritis.
- **Central Sleep Apnoea**: Cheyne-Stokes respiration has been reported post-marketing.
- **TTP**: Thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura has been reported very rarely and requires prompt plasmapheresis.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by frequency based on the PLATO study:

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Dyspnoea, Blood disorder bleedings (e.g., bruising), Hyperuricaemia. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Epistaxis, Gastrointestinal haemorrhage, Diarrhoea, Nausea, Dizziness, Syncope, Subcutaneous/dermal bleeding, Pruritus, Urinary tract bleeding, Post-procedural haemorrhage, Increased blood creatinine, Gout. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Intracranial haemorrhage, Eye haemorrhage, Ear haemorrhage, Retroperitoneal haemorrhage, Muscular bleeding, Reproductive system bleeding, Confusion, Tumour bleedings. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects**: Cardiac failure (1.1%), non-cardiac chest pain (0.9%), and fatal bleeding (0.2%).
- **Note**: In Asian/Japanese populations, a higher incidence of "Minor Bleeding" has been observed compared to clopidogrel [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors**: (e.g., Ketoconazole) Contraindicated; can increase ticagrelor exposure by >7-fold.
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inducers**: (e.g., Rifampicin, phenytoin, carbamazepine) May decrease ticagrelor exposure and efficacy.
- **Statins**: Ticagrelor increases simvastatin and atorvastatin concentrations. Simvastatin doses >40 mg daily are not recommended.
- **P-glycoprotein (P-gp) Substrates**: Ticagrelor may increase exposure to P-gp substrates. For **Digoxin**, close clinical and laboratory monitoring is required.
- **Morphine/Opioids**: May delay and decrease ticagrelor absorption; consider parenteral P2Y12 inhibitors in ACS patients where rapid inhibition is vital.
- **Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors (SSRIs)**: May increase the risk of cutaneous bleeding.
- **Rosuvastatin**: Ticagrelor increases rosuvastatin exposure, potentially increasing the risk of myopathy.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: **Category B1** [Australian]. No clinical data in pregnant women. Animal studies showed no direct harmful effects, but use is not recommended during pregnancy.
- **Breastfeeding**: Excreted in animal milk. It is unknown if ticagrelor is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended.
- **Fertility**: No effect on male or female fertility observed in animal studies.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and efficacy not established in patients under 18 years.
- **Elderly Use**: No dose adjustment required. Patients ≥75 years are at a higher risk of bleeding and should be monitored closely for renal function.
- **Renal Impairment**: No dose adjustment required for patients with renal impairment or those on haemodialysis. Ticagrelor is not dialysable.
- **Hepatic Impairment**: 
    - **Mild**: No dose adjustment required.
    - **Moderate to Severe**: Contraindicated [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Ticagrelor is a reversible P2Y12 receptor antagonist. It prevents ADP-mediated platelet activation and aggregation. It also inhibits the equilibrative nucleoside transporter-1 (ENT-1), increasing local concentrations of adenosine, which may contribute to its clinical profile (including dyspnoea and vasodilatation).
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Rapid (median $t_{max}$ 1.5h). Absolute bioavailability is approximately 36%.
    - **Metabolism**: Primarily via CYP3A4 to an active metabolite (AR-C124910XX).
    - **Elimination**: Mostly via hepatic metabolism; 58% in faeces, 26% in urine. Mean half-life is ~7 hours for ticagrelor and ~8.5 hours for the active metabolite.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **PLATO Study**: A phase 3 trial ($N=18,624$) comparing ticagrelor to clopidogrel.
    - **Primary Outcome**: Ticagrelor showed a 16% relative risk reduction (RRR) in the composite of CV death, MI, and stroke at 12 months (9.8% vs 11.7%; p=0.0003).
    - **Mortality**: Ticagrelor significantly reduced all-cause mortality (4.5% vs 5.9%; p=0.0003).
    - **Bleeding**: No significant difference in PLATO-defined Total Major Bleeding, but ticagrelor had higher rates of non-CABG major bleeding.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage**: Store below 25°C.
- **Packaging**: PVC-PVdC/Alu blister packs.
