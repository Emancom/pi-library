# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) provided by the United States (US Food and Drug Administration).
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for use by healthcare professionals. 
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labelling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN).
*   **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/amino acid derivative (carrier for long-chain fatty acids).
*   **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
*   **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Metabolic Disorders:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
*   **Carnitine Deficiency in End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
*   **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion. A loading dose is often employed in severe metabolic crises, followed by equivalent doses divided every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours. Daily maintenance doses are generally 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
*   **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight. Administration should occur as a slow (2–3 minute) bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Administration and Monitoring
*   **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to initiating parenteral therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine levels: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
*   **Dose Adjustments:** In ESRD patients, therapy may be initiated if trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels are below normal (40–50 µmol/L). Adjustments (including downward titration to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of treatment based on trough concentrations [US].
*   **Visual Inspection:** Inspect for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occurred within minutes of injection. If severe reactions occur, discontinue the drug and initiate appropriate medical management [US].
*   **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with reports of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
*   **Accumulation of Metabolites:** In patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD, chronic high-dose oral administration (not specific to IV, but noted for the molecule) may lead to accumulation of trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are potentially toxic [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Clinical Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned under current labelling, but reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm]. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Not specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased milk concentrations of levocarnitine following exogenous administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake by the infant must be weighed against the benefit of supplementation for the mother. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].
*   **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, yeast) were negative. Long-term animal carcinogenicity studies have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy are established for use in neonates and children; dosing is based on mg/kg body weight (refer to Section 3) [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources.
*   **Renal Impairment:** In ESRD patients, IV levocarnitine is indicated. However, the safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for beta-oxidation. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated [US].
*   **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine clears accumulated toxic acyl-CoA compounds and increases plasma carnitine concentrations [US].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    *   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    *   **Elimination:** Following a single IV dose (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; the mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Dialysis Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg after each dialysis session, trough plasma levocarnitine levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. While IV levocarnitine effectively increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the impact on specific clinical outcomes in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
*   **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion as the formulation is preservative-free [US].
*   **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
