# Editorial Product Information: Tacrolimus (Topical)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) sourced from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically focused overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing, dispensing, or administering the medication in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labeling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tacrolimus (as monohydrate)
- **Chemical Class:** Calcineurin inhibitor; immunosuppressant
- **Brand Name(s):** AZematop [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** D11AH01
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Ointment: 0.1% (1.0 mg/g) [Australian]
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Moderate to Severe Atopic Dermatitis:** Indicated for adults and adolescents aged 16 years and older [Australian].
    - **Flare Treatment:** For patients who are not adequately responsive to, or are intolerant of, conventional treatments such as topical corticosteroids [Australian].
    - **Maintenance Treatment:** To prevent flares and extend flare-free intervals in patients with a high frequency of exacerbations (i.e., occurring 4 or more times per year) who have previously responded to a maximum of 6 weeks of twice-daily tacrolimus treatment [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Adolescents ≥16 years)
- **Flare Treatment:** Apply a thin layer to affected skin twice daily. Treatment should continue until the lesion is cleared. If symptoms return, twice-daily application should be restarted. Clinicians should attempt to reduce application frequency as the condition improves. If no improvement is observed after 2 weeks of treatment, alternative therapies should be considered [Australian].
- **Maintenance Treatment:** Apply once daily, twice weekly (e.g., Monday and Thursday) to areas commonly affected by atopic dermatitis. There should be 2–3 days without treatment between applications. After 12 months, the physician should review the patient's condition to determine the necessity of continued maintenance, as safety data beyond 12 months is limited [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Apply as a thin layer to any part of the body, including the face, neck, and flexure areas.
- **Contraindicated Sites:** Do not apply to mucous membranes [Australian].
- **Occlusion:** Do not apply under occlusion, as this has not been clinically studied and may increase systemic absorption [Australian].
- **Hygiene:** Patients should wash hands after application unless the hands are the target area for treatment.
- **Water Exposure:** Advise patients not to bathe, shower, or swim immediately after application to prevent the medication from being washed off [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to tacrolimus, macrolides in general, or any excipient in the formulation (e.g., white soft paraffin, liquid paraffin, beeswax) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Ultraviolet (UV) Exposure:** Minimize skin exposure to sunlight. Avoid the use of tanning beds, UVB therapy, or UVA in combination with psoralens (PUVA). Patients should use appropriate sun protection (sunscreen, clothing) [Australian].
- **Malignancy:** Do not apply to lesions considered potentially malignant or pre-malignant. While a definitive link between topical tacrolimus and malignancies (e.g., lymphoma, skin cancer) has not been confirmed, it is recommended to use the lowest strength and frequency for the shortest duration necessary [Australian].
- **Skin Barrier Defects:** Not recommended for patients with defects such as Netherton’s syndrome, lamellar ichthyosis, or generalised erythroderma, as these conditions significantly increase systemic absorption [Australian].
- **Immunosuppression:** Not recommended for use in patients with congenital or acquired immunodeficiencies or those on systemic immunosuppressive therapies [Australian].
- **Infections:** Efficacy and safety have not been evaluated in clinically infected atopic dermatitis. Clear clinical infections at treatment sites before starting therapy. Use may be associated with an increased risk of folliculitis and herpes viral infections [Australian].
- **Lymphadenopathy:** If lymphadenopathy occurs, investigate the etiology. In the absence of a clear etiology or in the presence of infectious mononucleosis, discontinuation should be considered [Australian].
- **Emollients:** Do not apply emollients to the same area within 2 hours of applying tacrolimus ointment [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Approximately 50% of patients experience some form of skin irritation at the application site.
- **Very Common (>10%):**
    - Application site burning
    - Application site pruritus
- **Common (1–10%):**
    - Local skin infections (e.g., folliculitis, herpes simplex, eczema herpeticum)
    - Alcohol intolerance (facial flushing or skin irritation after alcohol consumption)
    - Paraesthesias and dysaesthesias (hyperaesthesia, burning sensation)
    - Application site warmth, erythema, pain, irritation, and rash
    - Pruritus (generalised)
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):**
    - Acne
    - Lymphadenopathy (0.8% in trials)
- **Post-Marketing/Frequency Unknown:**
    - Seizures, rosacea, application site edema, and increased drug levels.
    - Rare reports of malignancies including lymphomas and skin cancers [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Topical Interactions:** Not metabolised in human skin; therefore, percutaneous interactions are unlikely. Avoid applying other topical preparations or emollients within 2 hours of application [Australian].
- **Systemic Interactions:** Systemic tacrolimus is metabolised via **CYP3A4**. While systemic exposure from topical use is generally low (<1.0 ng/mL), caution is advised when co-administering known CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., erythromycin, itraconazole, ketoconazole, diltiazem) in patients with widespread or erythrodermic disease [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category C**. Tacrolimus crosses the placenta. Human data from systemic use suggests increased risks of miscarriage, preterm delivery, and low birth weight. Use during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Tacrolimus is excreted into breast milk after systemic administration. It is recommended that mothers do not breastfeed while receiving treatment [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies (subcutaneous) showed decreased sperm counts in males and embryolethality/reproductive toxicity in females at doses higher than clinical topical exposure [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** The 0.1% strength ointment is **not** indicated for children under 16 years of age [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific dosage adjustments are required based on clinical experience, though specific studies in the elderly are lacking [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution due to extensive hepatic metabolism, although blood concentrations remain low after topical application [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No specific dose adjustment is suggested for topical use, though cases of acute renal failure have been reported post-marketing in patients with skin barrier defects [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A calcineurin inhibitor. It binds to the cytoplasmic immunophilin FKBP12, inhibiting calcium-dependent signal transduction pathways in T cells. This prevents the transcription and synthesis of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-2, IL-3, IL-4, IL-5, GM-CSF, TNF-α, and IFN-γ [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Systemic exposure is minimal and transient (typically <1.0 ng/mL). Absorption decreases as the skin heals.
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is >98.8% (mainly albumin and α-1-acid glycoprotein).
    - **Metabolism:** Any systemically absorbed drug is metabolised by the liver via CYP3A4.
    - **Elimination:** The average half-life after repeated topical application is approximately 75 hours in adults [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Comparative Efficacy:** In a study against 1% pimecrolimus cream, 0.1% tacrolimus ointment showed significantly greater improvement in EASI scores (57% vs 39% reduction) by week 6 [Australian].
- **Maintenance Study:** A 12-month study in adults demonstrated that twice-weekly application significantly reduced the number of major flares compared to a vehicle control [Australian].
- **Comparison to Corticosteroids:** Trials showed 0.1% tacrolimus to be as effective as mid-potent to potent corticosteroids (e.g., hydrocortisone-17-butyrate) for adult atopic dermatitis, with superior efficacy over 0.005% fluticasone for facial lesions [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C.
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any remaining ointment 3 months after the tube is first opened [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4) [Australian].
