# Editorial Product Information: Tramadol Hydrochloride and Paracetamol

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of the combination of tramadol hydrochloride and paracetamol for healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI or local clinical guidelines for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Not all dosage forms or indications mentioned may be approved in all regions.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Tramadol hydrochloride (opioid analgesic) and paracetamol (non-opioid analgesic).
- **Chemical Class:** Tramadol is a centrally acting synthetic opioid analgesic; Paracetamol is a non-salicylate, non-opioid antipyretic/analgesic.
- **Brand Name(s):** APO-Tramadol/Paracetamol 37.5/325 [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N02AX52.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Film-coated tablets containing 37.5 mg tramadol hydrochloride and 325 mg paracetamol [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Apotex Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Short-term management of severe pain:** Indicated for use in patients where alternative treatment options (e.g., non-opioid analgesics) have failed, are contraindicated, not tolerated, or are otherwise inappropriate to provide sufficient management of pain [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Adolescents ≥ 12 years)
- **Initial Dose:** Two tablets (75 mg tramadol / 650 mg paracetamol) is recommended.
- **Titration:** Additional doses may be taken as needed every 6 hours.
- **Maximum Daily Dose:** 8 tablets (300 mg tramadol / 2600 mg paracetamol) [Australian].
- **Weight-Based Restrictions:**
    - Patients 37.5 kg to 50 kg: Maximum 6 tablets daily.
    - Patients < 37.5 kg: Not recommended/should not receive this formulation.

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets must be swallowed whole with sufficient liquid; they must not be broken or chewed.
- The treatment duration should be kept to the absolute minimum necessary.

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to tramadol, paracetamol, or any excipients.
- Acute intoxication with alcohol, hypnotic drugs, centrally-acting analgesics, opioids, or psychotropic drugs.
- Concomitant use of Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs) or use within two weeks of their withdrawal.
- Severe hepatocellular insufficiency or hepatic failure/decompensated active liver disease.
- Severe or acute respiratory disease and respiratory depression.
- Epilepsy that is not controlled by treatment.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [Australian]
- **Limitations of Use:** Use only when other treatment options are inadequate due to opioid risks.
- **Hazardous and Harmful Use:** Potential for abuse, misuse, and addiction, which can lead to fatal overdose.
- **Life-Threatening Respiratory Depression:** Serious or fatal depression may occur; monitor closely at initiation and following dose increases.
- **Concomitant Use with CNS Depressants:** Use with benzodiazepines, alcohol, or other CNS depressants may result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Paracetamol has been linked to acute liver failure, typically at doses exceeding 4,000 mg/day. Caution is required when using other paracetamol-containing products.
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Risk is increased when used with other serotonergic agents (e.g., SSRIs, SNRIs, MAOIs).
- **Seizure Risk:** Convulsions have been reported even at recommended doses; risk increases in patients with a history of epilepsy or those taking medications that lower the seizure threshold.
- **Sleep-Related Breathing Disorders:** Opioids can cause central sleep apnoea (CSA) in a dose-dependent manner.
- **Head Injury/Increased Intracranial Pressure:** Use with caution; miosis may obscure clinical signs of intracranial pathology.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following table reflects treatment-emergent adverse events (>1%) from pooled clinical trials [Australian]:

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Nausea, dizziness, somnolence, constipation, headache. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Vomiting, dry mouth, diarrhoea, abdominal pain, dyspepsia, flatulence, confusional state, insomnia, hyperhidrosis, pruritus, trembling. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Arrhythmia, tachycardia, palpitations, hypertension, dyspnoea, dysphagia, melaena, involuntary muscle contractions, tinnitus, depression, hallucinations, amnesia. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Ataxia, convulsions, speech disorders, delirium, drug dependence, miosis, mydriasis. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Respiratory depression, anaphylaxis, and severe skin reactions (rare).

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **MAOIs:** Contraindicated; risk of serotonergic syndrome.
- **CNS Depressants (Alcohol, Benzodiazepines):** Increased risk of sedation and fatal respiratory depression.
- **Serotonergic Drugs (SSRIs/SNRIs/Triptans):** Risk of Serotonin Syndrome.
- **Opioid Agonists-Antagonists (Buprenorphine, Pentazocine):** May reduce analgesic effect and precipitate withdrawal symptoms.

### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP2D6 Inhibitors (Fluoxetine, Amitriptyline):** May increase tramadol levels and decrease the active metabolite (M1).
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** May inhibit tramadol metabolism.
- **Carbamazepine:** Significant decrease in tramadol serum concentrations; reduces analgesic efficacy.
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased INR; periodic evaluation of prothrombin time is required.
- **Ondansetron:** May increase the dose requirements of tramadol for postoperative pain.
- **Flucloxacillin:** Caution advised due to risk of pyroglutamic acidosis (PGA), particularly in patients with glutathione depletion.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian **Category C**. This combination should not be used during pregnancy. Chronic use may lead to neonatal withdrawal syndrome.
- **Breastfeeding:** Use is not recommended. Tramadol and its metabolites are excreted in milk (approx. 0.1% of maternal dose).
- **Fertility:** No specific studies on the combination. In animal studies, high-dose tramadol did not affect fertility, but paracetamol showed some effects on offspring (clinical significance unknown).

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in children < 12 years; use is not recommended.
- **Elderly Use:** No adjustment usually needed up to 75 years. For patients > 75 years, the elimination half-life of tramadol increases by ~15%; dose intervals should be extended if necessary.
- **Renal Impairment:** Elimination is delayed. In severe renal impairment (CKD stages 4/5), use is not recommended. For other stages, prolonging the dosage interval is advised.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe impairment. In chronic/active hepatic disease (non-severe), paracetamol dose must not exceed 3 g/day.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:**
    - **Tramadol:** A μ-opioid receptor agonist and a weak inhibitor of the reuptake of noradrenaline and serotonin.
    - **Paracetamol:** Mechanism not fully elucidated; involves central and peripheral analgesic effects.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Tramadol absolute bioavailability is ~75% (rising to 90% with repeated dosing). Paracetamol is rapidly and almost completely absorbed.
    - **Metabolism:** Tramadol is metabolised via CYP2D6 to the active M1 metabolite. Paracetamol is metabolised primarily via glucuronidation and sulphation in the liver.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal. Half-life is ~5-6 hours for tramadol and ~2-3 hours for paracetamol.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Acute Pain:** Pivotal single-dose studies in dental pain (1200 subjects) demonstrated that the 75mg/650mg combination provided superior pain relief compared to its individual components or placebo.
- **Chronic Pain:** Five placebo-controlled studies (up to 3 months) showed statistically significant pain reduction in patients with osteoarthritis and chronic low back pain [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Packaging:** Typically provided in PVC/PVDC-Al blister packs.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only.
