# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data to support informed clinical decision-making.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Generic/Baxter).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injection (solution); 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
    - **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Administered every 3 to 4 hours; should not be administered less frequently than every 6 hours.
    - **Maximum Dose:** Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].

- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations (normal range 40–50 µmol/L). Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate medical management [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Monitoring:** 
    - Obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to initiating parenteral therapy.
    - Conduct weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** Chronic high-dose oral administration in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may result in the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5% in ESRD clinical trials)
| Body System | Adverse Event | Frequency (%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain | 7–33% |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia | 6–20% |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting | 8–16% |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia | 6–9% |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness | 13% |
| **Respiratory** | Cough, dyspnea, pharyngitis, rhinitis | 7–23% |

### Other Significant Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; body odor and gastritis (less frequent).
- **Neurological:** Seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. 
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels in patients on warfarin therapy following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not formally assigned in provided text, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. 
    - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased milk concentrations of levocarnitine. Risks to the infant from excess intake must be weighed against maternal benefits. Consider discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg weight (see Section 3). Safety and efficacy in paediatrics are consistent with adult populations for metabolic indications.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency.
    - IV levocarnitine is specifically indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
- **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours.
- **Half-life:** Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Trials:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x weekly), mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine concentrations increased from ~20 µmol/L (baseline) to 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging:** Trough concentrations showed dose-proportional increases across 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg cohorts over 12 and 24 weeks.
- **Clinical Outcome Note:** While IV levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the effect on specific signs, symptoms, or clinical outcomes in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing.
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s solution (range 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) when stored in PVC bags at room temperature [US].
