# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) for Levocarnitine Injection provided by the following regulatory jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a concise reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority for the most current and applicable full prescribing information.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (also known as L-carnitine).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Metabolic):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism which result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
- **Acute Management:** In patients with severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often given, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours and never less than every 6 hours.
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are recommended in the range of 50 mg/kg or as required by the clinical situation. The highest dose administered in clinical settings has been 300 mg/kg.

### 3.2 ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels below 40–50 µmol/L. Adjustments should be guided by these trough concentrations; downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### 3.3 General Instructions
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations before therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target free carnitine range: 35 to 60 µmol/L).
- **Inspection:** Inspect parenteral products visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occurred within minutes of injection. If a reaction occurs, discontinue the drug and initiate appropriate medical treatment.
- **Seizures:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed.
- **Oral Formulation Warning:** The safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of the oral formulation in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD on dialysis may result in the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites: trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### 6.1 Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events occurring at a frequency ≥5% in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials, regardless of causality:

| Body System | Adverse Event (Frequency ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%), Flu syndrome (25%), Headache (22%), Infection (15%), Accidental injury (10%), Asthenia (9%), Abdominal pain (9%), Chest pain (12%), Fever (7%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%), Palpitations (5%) |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%), Nausea (8%), Anorexia (5%) |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia (9%), Anemia (6%), Hyperkalemia (6%), Hypervolemia (5%) |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness (13%), Paresthesia (5%), Insomnia (4%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (23%), Cough increase (9%), Rhinitis (9%), Dyspnea (7%) |

### 6.2 General Adverse Reactions
- Transient nausea and vomiting.
- Less frequent: Body odor and gastritis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. 
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels in patients on warfarin therapy after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US]: Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. However, there are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased milk concentrations following exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine intake must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, S. cerevisiae, S. pombe) indicate the drug is not mutagenic. Long-term animal studies for carcinogenic potential have not been performed.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is based on mg/kg (see Section 3). Specific age-range safety profiles are not further subdivided in available sources.
- **Geriatric Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used therapeutically in ESRD. Note the risk of metabolite accumulation with *oral* use (see Section 5).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Mean distribution half-life of 0.585 hours. It is not bound to plasma protein or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels).
    - **Bioavailability:** Absolute bioavailability of oral formulations is approximately 15-16% [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients on hemodialysis, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis significantly increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Trough levels reached 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks compared to a baseline of 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L.
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses demonstrated dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks. 
- **Note:** While the efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations is demonstrated, the effect on specific clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature when mixed in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) in PVC bags.
- **Disposal:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial; the formulation contains no preservative.
