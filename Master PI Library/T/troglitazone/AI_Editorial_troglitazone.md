# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesised from official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical and regulatory data into a concise reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for clinical guidance and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Intravenous Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic management of patients with metabolic defects resulting in secondary deficiency [US].
- **Carnitine Deficiency in End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients undergoing chronic hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:**
    - **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
    - **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; intervals should never be less than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though up to 300 mg/kg may be required based on clinical response [US].

- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
    - **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if concentrations are high [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- None [US].

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known seizure disorder, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **TMA/TMAO Accumulation:** In patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD, chronic high-dose oral administration (not specifically injection) may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Plasma Concentrations:** Monitoring of plasma free carnitine (target range 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended weekly or monthly [US].
- **Clinical Monitoring:** Routine blood chemistries, vital signs, and overall clinical status should be assessed regularly [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (>10%):** Transient nausea and vomiting [US].
- **Other Reported Effects:** Body odor, gastritis, abdominal pain, headache, and injection site reactions [US].
- **Hemodialysis Specific (≥5% regardless of causality):** 
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%), chest pain (6–15%) [US].
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%) [US].
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%) [US].
    - **General:** Infection (10–24%), fever (5–12%), asthenia (8–12%) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Clinical Management:** Monitor INR levels regularly upon initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine in patients receiving anticoagulants [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine is not protein-bound, reducing the likelihood of displacement-based interactions [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned a letter, but the PI states it should be used "only if clearly needed." Animal reproductive studies in rats and rabbits (up to 3.8 times the human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is naturally present in human milk. Studies in dairy cows indicate milk concentrations increase with exogenous administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant should be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg for metabolic disorders; specific safety profiles in neonates and premature infants are defined by age-related normal carnitine concentrations [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available source documents.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically in ESRD patients; however, high oral doses (if applicable) carry a risk of metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO) in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This process provides substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Trials:** Two double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in chronic hemodialysis patients demonstrated that 20 mg/kg levocarnitine injection three times weekly increased pre-dialysis (trough) plasma carnitine concentrations to approximately 190–243 µmol/L after 8–24 weeks (baseline ~20 µmol/L) [US].
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Clinical efficacy is supported by its ability to clear toxic acyl-CoA compounds by forming acylcarnitine, which is rapidly excreted [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability in Solutions:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only [US].
- **Poison/Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
