# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction:
    - **United States** (Food and Drug Administration - FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative [(R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt].
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Generic/Baxter).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - Frequency: Every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    - Maximum administered dose reported: 300 mg/kg.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - Administration: Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - Adjustments: Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives.

### Monitoring
- **Baseline:** Obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to starting parenteral therapy.
- **Ongoing:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of plasma free carnitine (target: 35 to 60 µmol/L), blood chemistries, and vital signs is recommended.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Discontinue immediately if severe reactions occur [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Oral Formulation Caution:** Professional guidelines note that the safety/efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral use in ESRD may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO) [US].
- **Monitoring:** Periodic monitoring of plasma carnitine levels is essential to avoid supratherapeutic levels and guide dose adjustments.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following adverse effects were reported in clinical trials involving ESRD patients (frequency $\ge$ 5%).

| Body System | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), flu syndrome (25%), headache (22%), chest pain (12%), fever (7%), abdominal pain (9%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), palpitations (5%) |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), anorexia (5%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%) |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness (13%), paresthesia (5%), insomnia (4%) |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%), hypervolemia (5%) |
| **Other** | Body odor (transient, frequency not specified), Anemia (6%), Pruritus (5%) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. 
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels frequently after initiating levocarnitine or following any dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned a letter, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. 
    - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. While supplemental use in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied, dairy cow studies show increased milk concentrations after exogenous administration. Risks of excess intake by the infant should be weighed against maternal benefits [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal reproductive studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg weight; see Section 3.
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours.
    - **Dialysis:** Readily removed from plasma by hemodialysis.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Dose-Ranging Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean trough carnitine levels from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Stability of Trough Levels:** Studies involving 12- and 24-week treatments demonstrated that carnitine concentrations remained stable or increased over time with consistent post-dialysis dosing (10, 20, or 40 mg/kg) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) when mixed in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s in PVC bags (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL).
