# Editorial Product Information: Telithromycin

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the Product Information for telithromycin, designed as a concise reference for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is not a substitute for the official Product Information mandated in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents before prescribing. 
- *Note: At the time of this editorial, only the United States (FDA) source documentation was provided for synthesis.*

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Telithromycin (a semisynthetic ketolide antibacterial).
- **Brand Name:** **Ketek** [US].
- **ATC Code:** J01FA15 (Noted by class; specifically identified as a ketolide).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 300 mg and 400 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** sanofi-aventis U.S. LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Community-Acquired Pneumonia (CAP):** Treatment of mild to moderate severity CAP caused by susceptible isolates of *Streptococcus pneumoniae* (including multi-drug resistant isolates [MDRSP]), *Haemophilus influenzae*, *Moraxella catarrhalis*, *Chlamydophila pneumoniae*, or *Mycoplasma pneumoniae*. Approved for patients 18 years of age and older [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Standard Adult Dose:** 800 mg (two 400 mg tablets) taken orally once every 24 hours.
- **Duration of Therapy:** 7 to 10 days [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets may be taken with or without food and should be swallowed whole [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal/Hepatic Adjustment:** See *Section 9: Use in Special Populations*.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Myasthenia Gravis:** Use is strictly contraindicated due to reports of fatal and life-threatening respiratory failure [US].
- **Hepatotoxicity:** History of hepatitis or jaundice associated with the previous use of telithromycin or any macrolide antibiotic [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to telithromycin, any of its excipients, or any macrolide antibiotic [US].
- **Drug Interactions:**
    - Co-administration with **cisapride** or **pimozide** [US].
    - Co-administration with **colchicine** in patients with renal or hepatic impairment [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatotoxicity (Boxed Warning):** Acute hepatic failure and severe liver injury, sometimes fatal, have been reported. These can occur during or immediately after treatment, even after only a few doses. Telithromycin must be discontinued immediately if signs of hepatitis (jaundice, dark urine, liver tenderness) occur [US].
- **QTc Prolongation:** Telithromycin may prolong the QTc interval. It should be avoided in patients with congenital QTc prolongation, uncorrected hypokalemia/hypomagnesemia, or those taking Class IA (e.g., quinidine) or Class III (e.g., dofetilide) antiarrhythmic agents [US].
- **Visual Disturbances:** May cause blurred vision, diplopia, or difficulty focusing. Patients should exercise caution when driving or operating machinery [US].
- **Loss of Consciousness:** Reports of transient loss of consciousness, sometimes associated with vagal syndrome, have occurred [US].
- **Pseudomembranous Colitis:** *Clostridium difficile*-associated diarrhea (CDAD) has been reported, ranging from mild diarrhea to fatal colitis [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequencies from Phase III clinical trials (n=2702):

| Adverse Event | Frequency (Overall) | Frequency (Drug-Related) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Diarrhea** | 10.8% | 10.0% |
| **Nausea** | 7.9% | 7.0% |
| **Headache** | 5.5% | 2.0% |
| **Dizziness** | 3.7% | 2.8% |
| **Vomiting** | 2.9% | 2.4% |
| **Loose Stools** | 2.3% | 2.1% |
| **Dysgeusia** | 1.6% | 1.5% |

- **Serious/Rare Effects (<0.2%):** Anxiety, bradycardia, erythema multiforme, hypotension, and rare reports of anaphylaxis or angioedema [US].
- **Hepatobiliary:** Elevated transaminases (ALT/AST >3x ULN) in 1.6% of patients [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibition:** Telithromycin significantly increases plasma concentrations of CYP3A4 substrates.
    - **Statins:** Avoid **simvastatin**, **lovastatin**, and **atorvastatin**. Simvastatin AUC increased 8.9-fold [US].
    - **Benzodiazepines:** Increased AUC of **midazolam** (2- to 6-fold); monitor and adjust dose if necessary [US].
- **Colchicine:** Increased colchicine levels; toxicity can be fatal. Reduce colchicine dose in patients with normal renal/hepatic function; avoid altogether in renal/hepatic impairment [US].
- **Digoxin:** Increased peak and trough levels by 73% and 21% respectively; monitor digoxin levels [US].
- **Metoprolol:** AUC increased by approximately 38%. Use with caution in patients with heart failure [US].
- **Theophylline:** Increased AUC by 17%. Administer 1 hour apart to reduce GI side effects [US].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** **Rifampin** decreases telithromycin AUC by 86%. Avoid co-administration with strong inducers (phenytoin, carbamazepine, phenobarbital) [US].
- **Ergot Alkaloids:** Potential for acute ergot toxicity (vasospasm); co-administration is not recommended [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category C [US]:** No adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use only if potential benefit justifies the risk to the fetus. Rat studies showed no teratogenicity but did show delayed fetal maturation at maternally toxic doses.
- **Breastfeeding:** Telithromycin is excreted in the milk of rats. It is unknown if it is excreted in human milk; use with caution [US].
- **Fertility:** Moderate reductions in fertility indices were noted in animal studies at 1.8–3.6 times the human dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment required based on age alone, though greater sensitivity in some older individuals cannot be ruled out [US].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - Severe impairment ($Cl_{cr} < 30$ mL/min): Reduce dose to 600 mg once daily [US].
    - Severe impairment with co-existing hepatic impairment: Reduce dose to 400 mg once daily [US].
    - Hemodialysis: Administer 600 mg dose after the dialysis session [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dosage adjustment required for mild, moderate, or severe hepatic insufficiency provided renal function is normal [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits bacterial protein synthesis by binding to domains II and V of 23S rRNA of the 50S ribosomal subunit. Its 3-keto function prevents resistance induction via methylase gene expression (erythromycin-inducible resistance) [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** 57% absolute bioavailability; unaffected by food. Peak concentration ($C_{max}$) reached in ~1 hour [US].
    - **Distribution:** 60-70% protein bound (albumin). High concentrations achieved in bronchial mucosa and alveolar macrophages [US].
    - **Metabolism:** ~70% of dose is metabolized; ~50% via CYP3A4 and ~50% via CYP450-independent pathways [US].
    - **Elimination:** Half-life is ~10 hours. Excreted in feces (7% unchanged), urine (13% unchanged), and via hepatic metabolism (37%) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Community-Acquired Pneumonia (CAP):** Studied in four randomized, double-blind trials. Clinical cure rates for telithromycin (800 mg for 7–10 days) ranged from 88.3% to 94.6%, comparable to clarithromycin, trovafloxacin, and amoxicillin [US].
- **MDRSP:** In a pool of 36 evaluable patients with multi-drug resistant *S. pneumoniae*, telithromycin achieved a 91.7% clinical success rate [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
