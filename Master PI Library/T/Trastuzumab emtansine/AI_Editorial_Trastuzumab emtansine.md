# Editorial Product Information: Trastuzumab emtansine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of clinical data and regulatory requirements for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an expert editorial overview and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved labeling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Trastuzumab emtansine, a HER2-targeted antibody-drug conjugate (ADC).
- **Brand Name:** **Kadcyla** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01FD03.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Lyophilised powder for concentrate for solution for infusion.
    - Available in 100 mg and 160 mg single-use vials [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only. Do not administer as an IV push or bolus.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Roche Products Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Early Breast Cancer (EBC):** Indicated as a single agent for the adjuvant treatment of HER2-positive early breast cancer in patients who have residual invasive disease following neoadjuvant treatment with a taxane and trastuzumab [Australian].
- **Metastatic Breast Cancer (MBC):** Indicated as a single agent for the treatment of HER2-positive metastatic (Stage IV) breast cancer in patients who previously received trastuzumab and a taxane. Patients must have either received prior therapy for metastatic disease or developed recurrence during or within six months of completing adjuvant therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Standard Dose:** 3.6 mg/kg administered intravenously every 3 weeks (21-day cycle).
- **Duration of Treatment:** 
    - **EBC:** Total of 14 cycles, or until disease recurrence/unmanageable toxicity.
    - **MBC:** Until disease progression or unmanageable toxicity.

### Administration Instructions
- **Reconstitution:** Reconstitute with Sterile Water for Injection (SWFI) to a concentration of 20 mg/mL. Swirl gently; **do not shake**.
- **Dilution:** Add the calculated volume to an infusion bag containing 250 mL of 0.45% or 0.9% sodium chloride. **Dextrose (5%) must not be used** as it causes protein aggregation.
- **Filtration:** If diluted in 0.9% sodium chloride, a 0.22 micron in-line polyethersulfone (PES) filter is required.
- **Infusion Rate:**
    - Initial dose: 90-minute infusion. Observe for 90 minutes post-dose.
    - Subsequent doses: 30-minute infusion if prior doses were well-tolerated. Observe for 30 minutes post-dose.

### Dose Modifications
- **Dose Reduction Schedule:** 
    - Starting dose: 3.6 mg/kg
    - First reduction: 3 mg/kg
    - Second reduction: 2.4 mg/kg
    - Further reduction: Discontinue treatment.
- **Re-escalation:** The dose should not be re-escalated after a reduction has been made.
- **Guidance:** Specific modifications are required for hepatotoxicity (transaminases, bilirubin), thrombocytopenia, left ventricular dysfunction, and pulmonary toxicity.

### Missed Dose
- Administer the missed dose as soon as possible. Do not wait for the next planned cycle. Adjust the schedule to maintain a 3-week interval between doses.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to **Kadcyla** or any of its excipients (succinic acid, sodium hydroxide, sucrose, polysorbate 20) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- **Medication Errors [Australian]:** Do not substitute **Kadcyla** for or with trastuzumab or trastuzumab deruxtecan. Verification of the vial label is critical to prevent dosing errors.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Serious and fatal cases of drug-induced liver injury (DILI) and liver failure have occurred. Monitor serum transaminases and bilirubin before each dose. Permanently discontinue if transaminases > 3 x ULN and bilirubin > 2 x ULN.
- **Nodular Regenerative Hyperplasia (NRH):** Rare cases of NRH (benign transformation of liver parenchyma) leading to portal hypertension have been identified. Confirm via histopathology if symptoms of portal hypertension arise. Permanently discontinue **Kadcyla** if NRH is diagnosed.
- **Left Ventricular Dysfunction:** Risk of reduced LVEF and congestive heart failure (CHF). Assess LVEF at baseline and every 3 months during treatment.
- **Pulmonary Toxicity:** Interstitial lung disease (ILD) and pneumonitis have been reported. Permanently discontinue for ILD/pneumonitis, except for low-grade radiation-related pneumonitis.
- **Thrombocytopenia:** Risk of severe bleeding and decreased platelet counts. Monitor platelets before each dose. Higher incidence and severity reported in Asian patients [Australian].
- **Neurotoxicity:** Peripheral neuropathy (mainly Grade 1 sensory) is common. Temporarily discontinue if Grade 3 or 4 symptoms occur.
- **Infusion/Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Interrupt for severe reactions; permanently discontinue for life-threatening reactions. 
- **Extravasation:** Monitor the infusion site closely. Reactions (erythema, tenderness) may occur within 24 hours; rare epidermal injury/necrosis may appear days to weeks later.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by frequency based on clinical trials (KATHERINE and EMILIA) [Australian].

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Fatigue, nausea, musculoskeletal pain, transaminases increased, thrombocytopenia, headache, haemorrhage (including epistaxis), anaemia, pyrexia, constipation, vomiting, stomatitis, arthralgia, insomnia, cough, peripheral neuropathy. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Neutropenia, dizziness, dysgeusia, dry eye, vision blurred, lacrimation increased, dyspepsia, pruritus, urinary tract infection, hypertension, hypokalaemia, peripheral oedema. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Pneumonitis, hepatic failure, portal hypertension, nodular regenerative hyperplasia, asthenia. |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Hepatotoxicity, LVEF reduction, interstitial lung disease, Grade 3/4 thrombocytopenia, and severe haemorrhage.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No formal human drug-drug interaction studies have been conducted. 
- **CYP3A4/5 Influence:** In vitro studies indicate the cytotoxic component DM1 is metabolised mainly by CYP3A4 and to a lesser extent by CYP3A5. Concurrent use with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, clarithromycin) may increase DM1 exposure and requires close monitoring [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Use caution when co-administering with anticoagulants or antiplatelet agents due to the risk of haemorrhage and thrombocytopenia.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category D:** Trastuzumab emtansine can cause embryo-foetal death or birth defects. Microtubule inhibition (DM1) and HER2 interference are associated with oligohydramnios and fatal pulmonary hypoplasia.
    - **Contraception:** Females of childbearing potential and male patients with partners of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during treatment and for **7 months** after the final dose [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if the drug is excreted in human milk. Nursing should be discontinued prior to treatment and may only be resumed 7 months after the last dose.
- **Fertility:** Human data is unavailable. Animal studies (rats) showed degeneration of reproductive organs at doses ≥ 20 mg/kg.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children under 18 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment is required for patients aged 65–75 years. Data are insufficient for patients ≥ 75 years [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No starting dose adjustment for mild or moderate impairment. Not studied in severe renal impairment (limited data for CLcr < 30 mL/min).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No starting dose adjustment for mild or moderate hepatic impairment. Not studied in severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class C). Due to potential hepatotoxicity, treat with caution.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Kadcyla** combines the HER2-binding properties of trastuzumab with the cytotoxicity of DM1 (a maytansine derivative). Upon binding to HER2, the ADC is internalised and degraded in lysosomes, releasing DM1-containing catabolites. DM1 binds to tubulin, causing G2/M cell cycle arrest and apoptosis. Trastuzumab also mediates ADCC and inhibits HER2 signalling.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is approximately 3.13 L (approximating plasma volume). DM1 is 93% protein-bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily proteolysis in lysosomes; DM1 is a substrate of CYP3A4/5 and P-gp.
    - **Elimination:** Clearance is approximately 0.68 L/day with an elimination half-life of ~4 days. Excretion is mainly via bile; minimal urinary excretion.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **KATHERINE (Early Breast Cancer):** A phase III trial (n=1486) compared **Kadcyla** to trastuzumab. **Kadcyla** reduced the risk of invasive disease recurrence or death by 50% (HR=0.50, p<0.0001). The 3-year IDFS rate was 88.3% for **Kadcyla** vs. 77.0% for trastuzumab [Australian].
- **EMILIA (Metastatic Breast Cancer):** A phase III trial (n=991) compared **Kadcyla** to lapatinib plus capecitabine. **Kadcyla** significantly improved median progression-free survival (9.6 vs. 6.4 months, HR=0.65) and overall survival (30.9 vs. 25.1 months, HR=0.68) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store vials in a refrigerator at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze.
- **Shelf Life:** 
    - **Reconstituted solution:** Use immediately. If necessary, store for up to 24 hours at 2°C to 8°C. Discard thereafter.
    - **Diluted solution:** Stable for up to 24 hours at 2°C to 8°C.
- **Handling:** Appropriate aseptic and chemotherapeutic preparation techniques must be used.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
