# Editorial Product Information: Thiothixene

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Thiothixene (cis isomer of N,N-dimethyl-9-[3-(4-methyl-1-piperazinyl)propylidene]thioxanthene-2-sulfonamide).
- **Chemical Class:** Thioxanthene derivative.
- **Brand Names:** Generic formulations (e.g., Amneal) [US].
- **ATC Code:** N05AF03 (not explicitly stated in source but standard for thiothixene).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Capsules:** 1 mg, 2 mg, 5 mg, and 10 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Amneal Pharmaceuticals NY LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Schizophrenia:** Management of schizophrenia [US-approved].
- **Note:** Effectiveness in managing behavioral complications in patients with mental retardation has not been evaluated [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** Dosage must be individualised based on the severity and chronicity of symptoms. Small initial doses should be gradually titrated to the optimal effective level.
- **Dosing by Severity [US]:**
    - **Milder conditions:** Initial dose of 2 mg three times daily. If needed, increase to 15 mg/day.
    - **More severe conditions:** Initial dose of 5 mg twice daily.
- **Maintenance:** The usual optimal range is 20 mg to 30 mg daily. Some patients may be maintained on once-daily dosing.
- **Maximum Dose:** 60 mg/day. Exceeding 60 mg/day rarely provides additional clinical benefit [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Circulatory collapse** [US].
- **Comatose states** [US].
- **Central Nervous System (CNS) depression** due to any cause [US].
- **Blood dyscrasias** [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to thiothixene. Cross-sensitivity with phenothiazines should be considered [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Increased Mortality in Elderly Patients with Dementia-Related Psychosis.** Antipsychotic drugs (both atypical and conventional) are associated with an increased risk of death in elderly patients treated for dementia-related psychosis. Thiothixene is NOT approved for this indication [US].
- **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** Potentially fatal; clinical signs include hyperpyrexia, muscle rigidity, altered mental status, and autonomic instability. Immediate discontinuation is required [US].
- **Tardive Dyskinesia:** A potentially irreversible, involuntary movement syndrome. Risk increases with treatment duration and cumulative dose. Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest necessary duration [US].
- **Leukopenia, Neutropenia, and Agranulocytosis:** Monitor Complete Blood Count (CBC) frequently in the first few months if there is a history of low White Blood Cell (WBC) counts. Discontinue if Absolute Neutrophil Count (ANC) <1000/mm³ [US].
- **Seizure Threshold:** Use with extreme caution in patients with a history of convulsive disorders or alcohol withdrawal [US].
- **Hyperprolactinemia:** Antipsychotics elevate prolactin levels. Chronic elevation may lead to galactorrhea, amenorrhea, or gynecomastia [US].
- **Ophthalmic Effects:** Long-term use may cause lenticular pigmentation or pigmentary retinopathy [US].
- **Occupational Hazard:** May impair physical or mental abilities required for hazardous tasks (e.g., driving) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Note: Frequencies were not explicitly quantified in percentages in the source document.*

- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, hypotension, lightheadedness, syncope. **Warning:** If hypotension occurs, do NOT use epinephrine as it may cause a paradoxical further drop in blood pressure [US].
- **CNS:** Drowsiness (usually mild/transient), restlessness, agitation, insomnia, seizures.
- **Extrapyramidal Symptoms (EPS):** Pseudoparkinsonism, akathisia, and dystonia. Dystonia is more common in males and younger age groups [US].
- **Hepatic:** Transient elevations in serum transaminases and alkaline phosphatase [US].
- **Hematologic:** Transient leukopenia and leukocytosis [US].
- **Endocrine/Reproductive:** Lactation, menstrual irregularities, moderate breast enlargement, and impotence [US].
- **Autonomic:** Dry mouth, blurred vision, nasal congestion, constipation, and increased sweating/salivation [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and changes in appetite/weight [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** NMS, Tardive Dyskinesia, Agranulocytosis, and Anaphylaxis (rare).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Hepatic Enzyme Inducers (e.g., Carbamazepine):** Significantly increases thiothixene clearance, potentially reducing effectiveness [US].
- **CNS Depressants and Alcohol:** Possible additive effects and potentiation of hypotension [US].
- **Hypotensive Agents:** Additive effect; monitor for excessive hypotension [US].
- **Anticonvulsants:** Thiothixene may lower the seizure threshold; however, anticonvulsant dosages should generally not be reduced when thiothixene is added [US].
- **Epinephrine:** Contraindicated for the treatment of thiothixene-induced hypotension [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Safety has not been established. Use only when the benefit to the mother outweighs the potential risk to the fetus [US].
- **Non-teratogenic Effects:** Neonates exposed to antipsychotics during the third trimester are at risk for EPS and/or withdrawal symptoms (e.g., agitation, respiratory distress, feeding disorders) [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed a decrease in conception rate and litter size [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use in children under 12 years of age is not recommended as safety and efficacy have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of mortality in dementia-related psychosis. Generally, the prevalence of tardive dyskinesia is highest among the elderly, especially women [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources, though liver damage has been reported with related drugs [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Antipsychotic thioxanthene derivative; chemical and pharmacological similarities to piperazine phenothiazines [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Metabolism:** Clearance is increased by hepatic microsomal enzyme inducers [US].
    - **Other details:** Detailed PK parameters (absorption, half-life) not specified in available source.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Possesses antiemetic effects and weak anticholinergic properties [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from light. Dispense in tight, light-resistant containers [US].
