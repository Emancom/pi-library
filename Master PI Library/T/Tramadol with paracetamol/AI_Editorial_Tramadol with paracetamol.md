# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **US (FDA)**. 
- **Note:** While the user instruction referenced a "Tramadol with paracetamol" document from Australia, the source material provided for analysis is exclusively for **Levocarnitine Injection, USP** by Baxter Healthcare Corporation. Consequently, this editorial focuses solely on Levocarnitine.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport).
- **Chemical Class:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US]; Manufactured by Baxter Pharmaceuticals India Private Ltd.

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - Administration: Slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose is often given, followed by an equivalent dose over the next 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours.
    - **Maximum Dose:** Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - Administration: Slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - **Titration:** Dose adjustments (e.g., downward to 5 mg/kg) should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known seizure disorder, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring:** 
    - **Metabolic Disorders:** Monitor plasma carnitine levels (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L), blood chemistries, and vital signs weekly and monthly.
    - **ESRD:** Monitor trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels (normal range 40–50 µmol/L).
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** The safety/efficacy of oral levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of the oral form in ESRD may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis and laryngeal edema.
- **Neurological:** Seizures.

### Common Adverse Effects (Hemodialysis Population)
Based on placebo-controlled trials in ESRD patients (incidence ≥5% regardless of causality):
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (5–21%), headache (3–37%), infection (10–24%), injection site reaction (27–38%), pain (21–35%), fever (5–12%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%), chest pain (6–15%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (9–35%), nausea (5–12%), vomiting (9–21%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hypervolemia (3–12%).
- **Neurological:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%).

### Other Reported Reactions
- **Less Frequent:** Body odor, gastritis.
- **Postmarketing:** Drug dependence, vertigo, anemia, weight changes, and various respiratory/special sense disorders.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred with concurrent levocarnitine use. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating treatment or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not explicitly lettered, but stated as: "Should be used during pregnancy only if clearly needed" [US].
    - **Data:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. Risk to the infant (excess carnitine intake) must be weighed against maternal benefit [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is weight-based (see Section 3).
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in provided sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - IV levocarnitine is indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. 
    - **Caution:** Oral levocarnitine formulations are not recommended for chronic use in patients with severely compromised renal function due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) and γ-butyrobetaine.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine over 24 hours (excluding endogenous carnitine). Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, a 20 mg/kg dose post-dialysis results in a significant increase in plasma carnitine concentrations, with trough levels exceeding normal ranges after one week of treatment [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** Double-blind, placebo-controlled trials (n=130) evaluated doses of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg in patients on chronic hemodialysis. While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations was demonstrated, the effect on specific clinical outcomes in this population has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C [US].
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of the opened vial as it contains no preservative.
