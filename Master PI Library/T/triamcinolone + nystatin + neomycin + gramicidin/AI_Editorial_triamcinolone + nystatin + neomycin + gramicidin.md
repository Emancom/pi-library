# Editorial Product Information: triamcinolone + nystatin + neomycin + gramicidin

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **triamcinolone + nystatin + neomycin + gramicidin**
- Chemical class:
  - Triamcinolone acetonide: topical corticosteroid (synthetic glucocorticoid)
  - Nystatin: polyene antifungal
  - Neomycin (as sulfate): aminoglycoside antibiotic
  - Gramicidin: topical peptide antibiotic (acts on bacterial cell membrane)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration: Topical (dermal application only).
- DO NOT include brand or trade names, dosage form descriptions, tablet physical descriptions, or PBS/schedule listings.

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval: 30 September 1991 (as stated in available sources).
- Date of last revision/update of the PI provided: 13 October 2021 (as stated in available sources).
- Withdrawal, suspension or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Indicated for relief of the inflammatory and pruritic manifestations of dermatoses that are likely to become infected or are already infected.
- No additional approved indications are specified in the available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Apply to affected area 2 to 3 times daily.

### Dosing by indication
- Specific dosing by dermatologic diagnosis is not specified in available sources.

### Administration instructions
- Apply topically to the affected skin only.
- Avoid contact with eyes.
- Do not bandage, cover or wrap the treated skin unless specifically directed by a prescriber (avoid occlusive dressings because they increase percutaneous absorption and the risk of sensitivity reactions).
- When used in the inguinal area, apply sparingly and wear loosely fitting clothing.
- Patients should be instructed to continue the prescribed course even if symptomatic relief occurs early, unless adverse effects or lack of efficacy require cessation.

### Titration schedules
- Not specified in available sources.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to triamcinolone, neomycin, nystatin or gramicidin.
- Ophthalmic use (not for use in the eye).
- Use on tuberculous lesions of the skin.
- Use on most viral lesions of the skin (for example Herpes simplex), and particularly on vaccinia and varicella lesions.
- Any other contraindications: Not specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- Boxed warnings: Not specified in available sources as a boxed/black-box warning. Present safety concerns and precautions are below.

- General warnings and precautions:
  - If sensitivity or irritation develops, discontinue use and institute appropriate therapy.
  - Hypersensitivity reactions to the anti-infective components (neomycin, nystatin, gramicidin) may be masked by the corticosteroid component.
  - Because of potential nephrotoxicity and ototoxicity associated with aminoglycosides, do not use on patients with extensive skin damage or where significant systemic absorption of neomycin is possible.
  - Avoid occlusive dressings unless directed by a clinician; occlusion increases percutaneous absorption (notably of triamcinolone and neomycin) and risk of sensitivity reactions.
  - Prolonged use of topical antimicrobial agents can result in overgrowth of nonsusceptible organisms (including fungi other than Candida); corticosteroids can exacerbate or mask infections. Monitor for superinfection; if it occurs, provide appropriate antimicrobial therapy and discontinue this product if necessary.
  - If a favourable response does not occur promptly, discontinue application until infection is controlled by other measures.
  - Systemic absorption of topical corticosteroids can cause reversible HPA axis suppression, manifestations of Cushing's syndrome, hyperglycaemia and glucosuria. Factors that increase systemic absorption include use of more potent steroids, treatment over large surface areas, use on inflamed or damaged skin, use under occlusion, and prolonged use.
  - Consider evaluation for HPA axis suppression (e.g., urinary free cortisol, ACTH stimulation test) in patients receiving large doses or in situations where systemic absorption may be enhanced.
  - Recovery of HPA axis function is usually prompt and complete after discontinuation; infrequently steroid withdrawal symptoms may require supplemental systemic corticosteroids.
  - Visual disturbance: systemic and topical corticosteroids have been associated with blurred vision and other visual disturbances; consider ophthalmology referral if symptoms such as blurred vision occur to evaluate for cataract, glaucoma or rare disorders such as central serous chorioretinopathy.

- Monitoring requirements:
  - Monitor treated areas for signs of local irritation, sensitisation or secondary infection.
  - Where systemic absorption is possible or suspected, consider urinary free cortisol and ACTH stimulation testing to assess HPA axis function.
  - In paediatric patients receiving extensive or prolonged treatment, monitor for signs of HPA axis suppression (growth parameters, cortisol levels) and for intracranial hypertension.

- Specific clinical precautions:
  - Use the least amount for the shortest duration compatible with effective therapy, especially in paediatric patients.
  - Use caution in nursing mothers; it is unknown whether topical administration could produce detectable quantities in breast milk.
  - Use caution when treating patients with conditions that may increase systemic exposure (extensive skin disease, broken skin, occlusive dressings).

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- Pregnancy:
  - The available sources state that long-term animal studies for carcinogenicity, mutagenicity, or fertility impairment have not been performed.
  - The product is assigned Pregnancy Category D in the available sources [Australian classification]. The PI explains that aminoglycosides cross the placenta, may be selectively taken up by fetal kidney and have been associated with nephron damage and eighth cranial nerve (auditory/vestibular) damage after in utero exposure; therefore aminoglycosides should be considered potentially nephrotoxic and ototoxic to the fetus. Caution is advised.
  - Specific clinical recommendations for use in pregnancy (beyond caution and the stated category): Not specified in available sources.

- Breastfeeding:
  - It is not known whether topical administration could produce sufficient systemic absorption to result in detectable quantities in breast milk. Systemically administered corticosteroids are secreted into breast milk in quantities not likely to have deleterious effects on the infant; nevertheless, exercise caution when administering to nursing women.

- Fertility:
  - Effects on fertility: Long-term animal studies to evaluate impairment of fertility have not been performed; information not available in the sources.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
- Paediatric use:
  - Use over large surface areas or for prolonged periods in paediatric patients could produce sufficient systemic absorption to cause systemic effects.
  - Paediatric patients may be more susceptible to HPA axis suppression and Cushing's syndrome than adults because of a larger skin surface area to body weight ratio.
  - Reported paediatric adverse manifestations: HPA axis suppression (linear growth retardation, delayed weight gain, low plasma cortisol levels and absence of response to ACTH stimulation), and intracranial hypertension manifestations (bulging fontanelles, headaches, bilateral papilloedema).
  - Recommendation: Limit use in children to the least amount for the shortest duration compatible with effective therapy and closely monitor for systemic effects.

- Elderly use:
  - No specific data available in the sources. (The PI states "No data available" for elderly use.)

- Renal impairment:
  - No dosing adjustments or specific guidance for renal impairment are provided in the available sources.
  - Pharmacokinetic note: Neomycin is excreted rapidly and unchanged by the kidneys; systemic absorption can lead to nephrotoxicity — exercise caution if systemic absorption is possible.

- Hepatic impairment:
  - No dosing adjustments or specific guidance for hepatic impairment are provided in the available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Triamcinolone acetonide: a topical corticosteroid with anti-inflammatory, antipruritic and vasoconstrictive properties; mechanism of anti-inflammatory activity is not fully elucidated.
- Nystatin: binds to sterols in susceptible fungal cell membranes, increasing membrane permeability and causing leakage of intracellular components; does not exhibit activity against bacteria, protozoa or viruses. Resistance to nystatin generally does not develop during therapy.
- Neomycin: an aminoglycoside antibiotic that inhibits bacterial protein synthesis; active primarily against certain gram-negative organisms but not Pseudomonas aeruginosa. Resistant strains may develop.
- Gramicidin: exerts antibacterial activity against many gram-positive organisms by altering cell membrane permeability.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption:
  - Percutaneous absorption of topical corticosteroids depends on vehicle, skin integrity and occlusion; topical corticosteroids can be absorbed from intact skin and absorption increases with inflammation or other skin disease.
  - Nystatin and gramicidin: not absorbed from intact skin or mucous membranes (as stated).
  - Neomycin: can be absorbed through inflamed or damaged skin.
- Distribution:
  - Corticosteroids are variably bound to plasma proteins.
- Metabolism:
  - Corticosteroids are primarily metabolised in the liver.
- Elimination:
  - Corticosteroid metabolites are eliminated primarily by the kidneys, with some biliary excretion.
  - Once absorbed, neomycin is rapidly excreted unchanged by the kidneys; reported half-life approximately 2–3 hours (as stated in available sources).

### Pharmacodynamics
- A correlation between vasoconstrictor potency and clinical efficacy has been suggested for topical corticosteroids; this is used as one of several methods to compare potencies.
