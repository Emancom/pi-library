# Editorial Product Information: Ketorolac Tromethamine (Ophthalmic)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised clinical overview for healthcare professionals. 
- This editorial synthesis is intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ketorolac tromethamine (a member of the pyrrolo-pyrrole group of nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs [NSAIDs]).
- **Brand Names:** Ketorolac Tromethamine Ophthalmic Solution (Apotex) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Ophthalmic solution 0.5% (5 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Topical ophthalmic.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Apotex Corp. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Seasonal Allergic Conjunctivitis:** For the temporary relief of ocular itching [US].
- **Postoperative Inflammation:** For the treatment of inflammation following cataract extraction [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Seasonal Allergic Conjunctivitis:** One drop applied to the affected eye(s) four times daily [US].
- **Post-Cataract Surgery Inflammation:** One drop applied to the affected eye(s) four times daily. Treatment should begin 24 hours after surgery and continue through the first two weeks of the postoperative period [US].

### Administration Instructions
- If using multiple topical ophthalmic medications, allow at least 5 minutes between the administration of different products [US].
- To prevent contamination, avoid touching the dropper tip to the eye, surrounding structures, or any other surface [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to ketorolac tromethamine or any other ingredient in the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Delayed Healing:** Topical NSAIDs may slow or delay corneal healing. Use in conjunction with topical corticosteroids may further increase the risk of healing complications [US].
- **Cross-Sensitivity:** There is a potential for cross-sensitivity to aspirin, phenylacetic acid derivatives, and other NSAIDs. Use with caution in patients with a history of asthma or known sensitivities to these drugs; bronchospasm or asthma exacerbations have been reported [US].
- **Increased Bleeding Time:** Ocular NSAIDs may interfere with thrombocyte aggregation and increase bleeding in ocular tissues (including hyphemas) during surgery. Use with caution in patients with known bleeding tendencies or those taking medications that prolong bleeding time [US].
- **Corneal Effects:** Use may result in keratitis. Susceptible patients (e.g., those with complicated ocular surgeries, corneal denervation, epithelial defects, diabetes mellitus, or rheumatoid arthritis) are at increased risk for epithelial breakdown, corneal thinning, erosion, ulceration, or perforation. These events may be sight-threatening. Use for more than 24 hours prior to surgery or beyond 14 days post-surgery increases the risk of corneal adverse events [US].
- **Contact Lens Wear:** Ketorolac tromethamine 0.5% should not be administered while wearing contact lenses [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Reactions
- **Very Common (>10%):** Transient stinging and burning upon instillation (reported in up to 40% of patients) [US].
- **Common (1–10%):** Allergic reactions (eye swelling, hyperemia, pruritus), corneal edema, iritis, ocular inflammation, ocular irritation, ocular pain, superficial keratitis, and superficial ocular infections [US].

### Rare and Serious Reactions
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Corneal infiltrates, corneal ulcer, eye dryness, headaches, and visual disturbances (blurry vision) [US].
- **Postmarketing Experience (Frequency Not Known):** Corneal erosion, corneal thinning, epithelial breakdown, corneal perforation, and bronchospasm or exacerbation of asthma [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Topical Ophthalmic Medications:** Ketorolac tromethamine has been safely administered alongside antibiotics, alpha-agonists, beta-blockers, carbonic anhydrase inhibitors, cycloplegics, and mydriatics [US]. 
- **Administration Note:** Maintain at least a 5-minute interval between different ophthalmic drops [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **US Category:** Not assigned under current labeling; however, no evidence of teratogenicity was observed in animal studies at clinically relevant doses [US].
  - **Late Pregnancy Warning:** Use should be avoided during late pregnancy due to the known risk of premature closure of the fetal ductus arteriosus by prostaglandin-inhibiting drugs [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if ketorolac is excreted in human milk following topical administration. Exercise caution when prescribing to nursing women [US].
- **Fertility:** Not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients younger than 2 years of age [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness have been observed between elderly and younger adult patients [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources for topical ophthalmic use.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Ketorolac tromethamine is an NSAID that inhibits the cyclooxygenase enzyme system, thereby inhibiting prostaglandin biosynthesis. Prostaglandins are known mediators of certain types of intraocular inflammation [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The drug demonstrates analgesic, anti-inflammatory, and anti-pyretic activity when administered systemically; topical application focuses these effects on ocular tissues [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
  - **Ocular Penetration:** Following instillation (2 drops, 12 hours and 1 hour before surgery), mean aqueous humor concentrations were 95 ng/mL [US].
  - **Systemic Absorption:** Minimal systemic absorption occurs. Following TID dosing, detectable plasma concentrations (11–23 ng/mL) were found in only a small portion of subjects, representing approximately 4–8% of the steady-state minimum plasma levels seen with oral 10 mg dosing [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Seasonal Allergic Conjunctivitis:** Two controlled clinical trials demonstrated that ketorolac tromethamine was significantly more effective than its vehicle in relieving ocular itching [US].
- **Postoperative Inflammation:** Two controlled trials involving patients treated for two weeks post-cataract surgery showed those using ketorolac were significantly less likely to have measurable signs of inflammation (cell and flare) compared to vehicle-treated patients [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from light. Keep the bottle tightly closed when not in use [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only (Prescription Only) [US].
