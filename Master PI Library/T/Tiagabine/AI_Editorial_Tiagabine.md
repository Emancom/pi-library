# Editorial Product Information: Tiagabine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This synthesis is an editorial summary intended for professional reference and is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tiagabine hydrochloride monohydrate.
- **Chemical Class:** Antiepileptic agent; nipecotic acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Gabitril** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N03AG06.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
  - Film-coated tablets: 5 mg (round, marked 251), 10 mg (oval, marked 252), and 15 mg (oval, marked 253) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Teva Pharma Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Partial Seizures:** Indicated as adjunctive (add-on) therapy for the treatment of partial seizures in patients who are not sufficiently controlled by other antiepileptic drugs [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Administration:** **Gabitril** must be taken with food. It is administered in three divided doses daily [Australian].
- **Adults and Children (>12 years):**
  - **Initial Dose:** 7.5 mg to 15 mg daily.
  - **Titration:** Increase by 5 mg to 15 mg daily at weekly intervals based on clinical response.
  - **Maintenance Dose:** Usually 30 mg to 50 mg daily. 
  - **Maximum Dose:** Doses up to 70 mg daily are reported to be well-tolerated [Australian].
- **Dosing in Non-Enzyme Inducing Therapy:** In patients not taking enzyme-inducing drugs, the initial maintenance dose should be lower, ranging from 15 mg to 30 mg daily [Australian].
- **Discontinuation:** Treatment should be tapered gradually over a period of 2 to 3 weeks to minimise the risk of increased seizure frequency [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to tiagabine or any excipients (including lactose) [Australian].
- Severe hepatic impairment [Australian].
- Concomitant use with **St John’s Wort** (*Hypericum perforatum*) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Seizures in Patients Without Epilepsy:** Post-marketing reports indicate new-onset seizures and status epilepticus in patients without a history of epilepsy, often associated with overdose or concomitant medications that lower the seizure threshold (e.g., antidepressants, antipsychotics). Use is only recommended for approved indications [Australian].
- **Non-convulsive Status Epilepticus (NCSE):** Paradoxical NCSE has been reported, particularly during dose titration. If NCSE occurs, dose reduction or discontinuation is required [Australian].
- **Suicidal Behaviour and Ideation:** As with all antiepileptic drugs (AEDs), there is an increased risk of suicidal thoughts or behaviour. Monitoring for signs of depression or mood changes is essential [Australian].
- **Visual Field Defects:** Rare cases of visual field defects (e.g., concentric field defects, hemianopia) have been reported. Referral to an ophthalmologist is recommended if visual symptoms develop [Australian].
- **Aggravation of Absence Seizures:** Due to its GABAergic mechanism, tiagabine may exacerbate absence seizures in patients with generalised epilepsy [Australian].
- **Dermatological Reactions:** Serious rashes, including vesiculobullous rash, have occurred [Australian].
- **General Precautions:** Monitoring for spontaneous bruising is advised; if observed, a full blood count (including platelets) should be performed [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events are generally mild to moderate, occurring most frequently during the titration phase.

| Frequency | Adverse Event [Australian] |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Dizziness (29%), Tiredness (22%), Somnolence (19%), Accidental injury (17%), Infection (15%), Headache (15%), Nausea (12%), Nervousness (11%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Tremor (10%), Flu syndrome (9%), Abdominal pain (8%), Vomiting (8%), Diarrhoea (8%), Pharyngitis (8%), Rhinitis (8%), Insomnia (6%), Concentration difficulties (6%), Ataxia (6%), Rash (6%), Confusion (5%), Depressed mood (<5%), Emotional lability (<5%) |
| **Post-Marketing/Rare** | Non-convulsive status epilepticus, encephalopathy, psychotic reactions (hallucinations, delusions), bruising, bullous dermatitis, exfoliative dermatitis, thrombocytopenia (isolated cases) |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Enzyme-Inducing AEDs:** Drugs such as phenytoin, carbamazepine, phenobarbitone, and primidone induce CYP3A and significantly enhance tiagabine metabolism. Tiagabine plasma concentrations may be reduced by a factor of 1.5 to 3 in these patients [Australian].
- **St John’s Wort:** Potent induction of CYP3A4 leads to significantly lower tiagabine exposure and loss of efficacy; this combination is contraindicated [Australian].
- **Protein Binding:** Tiagabine is 96% protein-bound. Potential exists for displacement interactions with other highly protein-bound drugs (e.g., salicylates, valproate), potentially increasing free fractions of either drug [Australian].
- **Effect of Tiagabine on Other Drugs:** Tiagabine does not appear to have clinically significant effects on the plasma concentrations of phenytoin, carbamazepine, valproate, warfarin, digoxin, or oral contraceptives [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category B3**. 
  - Clinical experience in humans is limited. Animal studies show increased post-implantation loss and foetal abnormalities at maternotoxic doses. 
  - Treatment should only be continued if the benefit to the mother outweighs the risk to the foetus. Monotherapy at the lowest effective dose is recommended [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Tiagabine and its metabolites are excreted in the milk of lactating rats. Use in breastfeeding humans is only recommended if the potential benefit outweighs the risk, as human data are unavailable [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal data showed increased post-implantation loss in rats and reduced ovarian/uterine weights in dogs following long-term treatment [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children under 12 years of age have not been established. Use is not recommended in this age group [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Pharmacokinetics are not significantly altered in the elderly, but clinical data are limited. Use with caution [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
  - **Mild to Moderate:** Metabolism is reduced and half-life is prolonged (12–16 hours). Dosage should be adjusted by reducing individual doses or extending the dosing interval.
  - **Severe:** Contraindicated [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Renal clearance is negligible; no specific dose adjustments are provided in available sources [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Tiagabine is a potent and selective inhibitor of GABA uptake by both neurons and glia. This increases the availability of GABA at post-synaptic receptors, enhancing GABAergic inhibition [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** It lacks significant affinity for other neurotransmitter receptor sites or uptake carriers [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Rapid and nearly complete (80–90% bioavailability). Food decreases the rate but not the extent of absorption.
  - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is ~1 L/kg; 96% protein-bound.
  - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised in the liver via CYP3A. No active metabolites identified.
  - **Elimination:** Half-life is 7–9 hours in healthy volunteers, reduced to 2–5 hours in patients taking enzyme-inducing drugs. Less than 2% is excreted unchanged [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Trials (TIA-106, 107, 109):** Efficacy as add-on therapy was established in three parallel, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials (n=769). 
- **Outcomes:** 
  - **TIA-106:** Demonstrated significant reduction in complex partial seizure rates at doses of 32 mg and 56 mg daily compared to placebo. 
  - **TIA-109:** Showed that both twice-daily (BID) and four-times-daily (QID) regimens were effective, with 20–30% of patients achieving a ≥50% reduction in seizure frequency [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C. 
- Do not refrigerate.
- Shelf life is as indicated on the secondary packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
