# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA). 
*   **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection, synthesised for use by healthcare professionals. 
*   **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial summary based on the available Product Information (PI) from the listed jurisdictions. It is not a substitute for the official, full PI required for prescribing in a specific region. 
*   **Note on Jurisdictions:** Only the US FDA Product Information was provided for this synthesis. Consequently, references to other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European) are excluded where information was not available.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
*   **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule for fatty acid transport; amino acid derivative.
*   **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    *   Intravenous solution: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
*   **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    *   **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
    *   **Frequency:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose may be followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    *   **Titration:** Subsequent daily doses should be in the range of 50 mg/kg or as clinically required. Doses as high as 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].
*   **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    *   **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    *   **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    *   **Titration:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels <40 to 50 µmol/L. Adjustments (e.g., downward to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
*   **Visual Inspection:** Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
*   **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occurred within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate medical management.
*   **Seizures:** Reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
*   **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation Precaution):** While this document covers the injection, it is noted that chronic high-dose oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
*   **Monitoring:**
    *   **Plasma Carnitine:** Monitor weekly/monthly; maintain free carnitine levels between 35 and 60 µmol/L.
    *   **General:** Monitor blood chemistries, vital signs, and overall clinical condition [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Serious Adverse Effects
*   **Anaphylaxis/Hypersensitivity:** Reported post-marketing.
*   **Seizures:** Reported in patients with and without history of epilepsy.

### Common Adverse Effects (≥5% in ESRD clinical trials)
Adverse events reported in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials for ESRD patients (regardless of causality) include:
*   **General:** Headache (22%), injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), flu syndrome (25%), asthenia (9%), abdominal pain (9%), fever (7%).
*   **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), chest pain (12%).
*   **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), dyspepsia (5%), anorexia (5%).
*   **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%).
*   **Neurologic:** Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), paresthesia (5%).
*   **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%), hypervolemia (5%) [US].

### Other Reported Effects
*   **Body Odour:** Transient nausea, vomiting, and body odour have been observed with carnitine supplementation [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) when used concurrently with levocarnitine.
    *   **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   **US Classification:** Not explicitly assigned a letter category in the current labelling, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. 
    *   **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. However, studies in dairy cows indicate increased milk concentrations of levocarnitine following administration. 
    *   **Recommendation:** Weigh the risks to the child of excess carnitine intake against the benefits of supplementation to the mother. Consider discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy for the treatment of metabolic disorders resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency are established. Dosing is consistent with adult weight-based recommendations (50 mg/kg) [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available documentation regarding dose adjustments [US].
*   **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically to treat deficiency in ESRD patients on dialysis (see Section 3). The safety of the **oral** formulation in renal impairment is not established due to metabolite accumulation [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule essential for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation and energy production [US].
*   **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism (e.g., organic acidemias), levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted. This prevents the disruption of intermediary metabolism caused by toxic acyl-CoA accumulation [US].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    *   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily feces).
    *   **Elimination:** Following a slow 3-minute IV bolus (20 mg/kg), the distribution half-life is 0.585 hours and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis showed that trough levels (pre-dialysis) increased from approximately 20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
*   **Dose-Ranging Study:** Evaluated 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses in ESRD patients (N≈30 per group). Trough concentrations remained stable or increased over 24 weeks, with higher doses yielding proportionally higher plasma levels (e.g., 456 ± 162 µmol/L at 40 mg/kg vs. 148 ± 50 µmol/L at 10 mg/kg) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
*   **Protection:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
*   **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial; the formulation contains no preservatives [US].
