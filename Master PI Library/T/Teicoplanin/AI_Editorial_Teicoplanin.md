# Editorial Product Information: Teicoplanin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. At the time of this publication, only the Australian PI was available for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Teicoplanin (a glycopeptide antibiotic produced by *Actinoplanes teichomyceticus*)
- **Brand Name:** Targocid [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** J01XA02
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Lyophilised powder for injection (400 mg vial). When reconstituted with the provided 3 mL diluent (Water for Injections), the solution contains 400 mg/3.0 mL of teicoplanin.
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (slow injection or infusion) or Intramuscular.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** sanofi-aventis australia pty ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Teicoplanin is indicated for serious staphylococcal or streptococcal infections where less toxic agents (including $\beta$-lactam antibiotics) are inappropriate or ineffective:
- **Bone infections:** Osteomyelitis (acute or chronic).
- **Joint infections:** Septic arthritis.
- **Bloodstream infections:** Non-cardiac bacteraemia and septicaemia.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
Dosage must be adjusted based on total body weight. A loading dose regimen is required to reach steady-state plasma levels rapidly.
- **Loading Regimen:** Three doses administered at 12-hourly intervals.
- **Maintenance Regimen:** Once-daily administration.

### Dosing by Indication
- **Septicaemia/Bacteraemia and Osteomyelitis:** Loading dose of 6–12 mg/kg IV every 12 hours for 3 doses, followed by a maintenance dose of 6 mg/kg/day (IV or IM).
- **Septic Arthritis:** Loading dose of 12 mg/kg IV every 12 hours for 3 doses, followed by a maintenance dose of 12 mg/kg/day (IV or IM).

### Administration Instructions
- **Intravenous:** Administer as a slow injection over 5 minutes or as an infusion over 30 minutes.
- **Intramuscular:** Maximum volume per injection site is 3 mL (400 mg).
- **Reconstitution:** Add diluent slowly; roll the vial gently between palms until dissolved. **DO NOT SHAKE**, as this creates stable foam. If foaming occurs, let stand for 15 minutes.
- **Contraindicated Route:** Must not be administered via the intraventricular route due to seizure risk.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to teicoplanin.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity and Anaphylaxis:** Serious and potentially fatal reactions (e.g., anaphylactic shock) may occur. Cross-hypersensitivity with vancomycin is possible; use with extreme caution in vancomycin-sensitive patients. 
- **Infusion-Related Reactions:** While "Red Man Syndrome" (pruritus, urticaria, erythema, hypotension) is rare compared to vancomycin, it can occur. Slower infusion (30 minutes) reduces risk.
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARS):** Life-threatening reactions including SJS, TEN, DRESS, and AGEP have been reported. Discontinue immediately if symptoms appear.
- **Nephrotoxicity:** Renal failure has been observed. Risk increases with high loading doses (12 mg/kg twice daily) and concurrent nephrotoxic agents.
- **Ototoxicity:** Hearing impairment may occur, especially with prolonged use, renal impairment, or co-administration with other ototoxic drugs.
- **Monitoring:** Periodic auditory function, hepatic, haematologic, and renal (creatinine) tests are advised during prolonged therapy.
- **Superinfection:** Potential overgrowth of non-susceptible organisms.

## 6. Adverse Effects
In clinical trials for bone and joint infections, 32% of patients reported adverse reactions, though only 17% discontinued treatment.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Fever, rash, nausea, vomiting, rigors, pruritus, diarrhoea. |
| **Uncommon/Rare** | Local reactions (pain, phlebitis, abscess), bronchospasm, anaphylaxis, urticaria, angioedema. |
| **Systemic/Serious** | **Hepatic:** Increased transaminases/alkaline phosphatase. **Haematologic:** Eosinophilia, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia; rarely agranulocytosis. **Renal:** Increased serum creatinine/urea, renal failure. **Neurological:** Dizziness, headache; seizures (intraventricular only). **Auditory:** Hearing loss, tinnitus, vertigo. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Nephrotoxic/Ototoxic Drugs:** Use with caution when co-administered with aminoglycosides, amphotericin B, ciclosporin, cisplatin, furosemide, or ethacrynic acid.
- **Incompatibilities:** Teicoplanin solutions are physically incompatible with aminoglycosides and must not be mixed in the same syringe.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B3]. Animal studies showed no teratogenicity but indicated increased stillborn pups and reduced pup weight at high doses. Use only if benefits clearly outweigh risks.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if teicoplanin is excreted in human milk. Use with caution; only if benefits outweigh risks.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Limited data available in the source PI. A report of overdose (95 mg/kg) in a newborn resulted in agitation.
- **Elderly Use:** Dose as for adults, provided renal function is normal.
- **Renal Impairment:** Adjustment is required starting from Day 4 of treatment:
    - **Mild (CrCl 40–60 mL/min):** Halve the dose (either half-dose daily or full dose every two days).
    - **Severe (CrCl < 40 mL/min) / Haemodialysis:** One-third of the dose (either one-third dose daily or full dose every three days).
- **Note:** Teicoplanin is not removed by dialysis. Trough levels should be monitored (target < 15 µg/mL generally; < 30 µg/mL for septic arthritis).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits bacterial cell wall biosynthesis at a site distinct from $\beta$-lactams. It is bactericidal or bacteriostatic against susceptible Gram-positive organisms, including methicillin-resistant staphylococci.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** 100% bioavailability after IM injection.
    - **Distribution:** 90–95% protein bound. Penetrates well into bone and blister fluid; penetrates poorly into CSF. $V_d$ is 0.6 L/kg.
    - **Metabolism:** Minimal (~3%).
    - **Elimination:** Elimination half-life is 70–100 hours. Approximately 80% is excreted unchanged in urine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Data regarding specific pivotal trials were not detailed in the provided source, although an open clinical trial in bone and joint infections was referenced regarding adverse effect frequencies (32%).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Retains potency for 48 hours at 25°C or 7 days at 4°C. For safety, it is recommended to discard reconstituted solutions after 24 hours.
- **Diluted Infusions:** Should be used immediately after preparation.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
