# Editorial Product Information: Terazosin

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of terazosin for use by healthcare professionals. It aims to consolidate clinical data into a structured format for quick reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Terazosin hydrochloride (a quinazoline derivative).
- **Chemical Class:** Alpha-1-selective adrenoceptor blocking agent.
- **Brand Names:** Terazosin (various generics; source document relates to Bryant Ranch Prepack/Jubilant Cadista).
- **ATC Code:** G04CA03
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Oral Capsules: 1 mg, 2 mg, 5 mg, and 10 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Jubilant Cadista Pharmaceuticals Inc. (Distributed by Bryant Ranch Prepack) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (BPH):** Treatment of symptomatic BPH. [US-approved]
- **Hypertension:** Treatment of hypertension, either as monotherapy or in combination with other antihypertensive agents (e.g., diuretics, beta-blockers). [US-approved]

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
To minimize the risk of severe hypotensive response and syncope, the initial dose of 1 mg must be administered at bedtime. This applies to both BPH and hypertension.

### Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia
- **Initial Dose:** 1 mg at bedtime. This dose must not be exceeded as a starting dose.
- **Titration:** Increase in a stepwise fashion to 2 mg, 5 mg, or 10 mg once daily.
- **Maintenance:** 10 mg once daily is generally required for clinical response. A trial of 4 to 6 weeks at this dose may be necessary to assess efficacy.
- **Maximum Dose:** While some patients respond to 20 mg/day, data is insufficient to conclude additional benefit at higher doses [US].

### Hypertension
- **Initial Dose:** 1 mg at bedtime.
- **Titration:** Slowly increase based on individual blood pressure response. 
- **Maintenance:** Usual range is 1 mg to 5 mg once daily.
- **Maximum Dose:** 20 mg/day [US]. Doses exceeding 40 mg have not been studied.

### Administration Instructions
- If administration is discontinued for several days or longer, therapy must be reinstituted using the initial 1 mg bedtime dosing regimen.
- Blood pressure should be monitored at the end of the dosing interval (trough) to ensure control.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to terazosin hydrochloride [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Syncope and "First-Dose" Effect:** Terazosin can cause significant hypotension and syncope, particularly with the first dose or early in treatment. This may also occur if therapy is interrupted and restarted or if doses are increased rapidly.
- **Priapism:** Rare reports of sustained, painful erections. This is a medical emergency that can lead to permanent impotence if not treated immediately.
- **Intraoperative Floppy Iris Syndrome (IFIS):** Observed during cataract surgery in patients currently or previously treated with alpha-1 blockers. Surgeons should be prepared to modify techniques.
- **Prostatic Cancer:** Since BPH and prostate cancer share similar symptoms, malignancy must be ruled out before starting terazosin for BPH.
- **Orthostatic Hypotension:** Common symptoms include dizziness, lightheadedness, and palpitations. Patients in hazardous occupations should exercise particular caution.
- **Laboratory Findings:** Small decreases in hematocrit, hemoglobin, WBCs, total protein, and albumin have been observed, likely due to hemodilution [US].

**Serious/Other Adverse Effects:**
- **Post-marketing:** Thrombocytopenia, atrial fibrillation, and anaphylaxis (rare).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Verapamil:** Concomitant use increases terazosin AUC and Cmax (approx. 24% and 25% increase respectively after 3 weeks).
- **Captopril:** Terazosin Cmax increases linearly with dose when co-administered; however, terazosin does not influence captopril disposition.
- **PDE-5 Inhibitors:** Use with sildenafil, tadalafil, or vardenafil may lead to additive blood pressure lowering and symptomatic hypotension [US].
- **Other Antihypertensives:** Caution is required when adding terazosin to other agents (e.g., diuretics, beta-blockers) to avoid excessive hypotension. Dosage reduction and re-titration may be necessary.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [US]. Animal studies show increased fetal resorptions and decreased fetal weight at high doses (60-280x human dose). Use only if the potential benefit justifies the risk to the fetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** Not known if terazosin is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution when administering to nursing women [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed decreased sperm count in vaginal smears and testicular atrophy at doses ≥40 mg/kg/day [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in children have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** In patients ≥70 years, plasma clearance is decreased by ~31.7% and half-life is slightly prolonged (14.0 hours vs 11.4 hours in younger adults). No specific dose adjustment is mandated beyond cautious titration.
- **Renal Impairment:** Impaired renal function does not significantly affect terazosin elimination. Dosage adjustment for hemodialysis (~10% removal) is not required [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources (though the drug undergoes minimal hepatic first-pass metabolism).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selectively blocks alpha-1-adrenoceptors. In BPH, it relaxes smooth muscle in the bladder neck and prostate, reducing resistance to urine flow. In hypertension, it decreases total peripheral resistance through vasodilation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Virtually complete; food delays Tmax by ~40 minutes but does not affect extent of absorption.
    - **Metabolism:** Minimal hepatic first-pass metabolism; circulates primarily as parent drug.
    - **Distribution:** 90–94% protein bound.
    - **Elimination:** Half-life is approximately 12 hours. Excretion: ~40% via urine (10% as parent drug), ~60% via feces (20% as parent drug).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **BPH:** Studied in 1222 men. Significant improvements in Boyarsky Index (symptom scores) and peak urine flow rates were observed within 2 weeks. Long-term studies (up to 30 months) showed maintained efficacy [US].
- **Hypertension:** Clinical trials showed blood pressure reductions persisting 24 hours post-dose. Standing blood pressure falls are typically larger (1–3 mmHg) than supine falls [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from light and moisture. Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
