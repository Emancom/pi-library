# Editorial Product Information: triamterene + hydrochlorothiazide

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **triamterene + hydrochlorothiazide**
- Chemical class:
  - Triamterene: potassium‑sparing (antikaliuretic) diuretic; pteridine derivative
  - Hydrochlorothiazide: thiazide diuretic (benzothiadiazine sulfonamide)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of hypertension or edema in patients who develop hypokalemia on hydrochlorothiazide alone.
- Use as an alternative for patients who require a thiazide diuretic but in whom the development of hypokalemia cannot be risked (for example, patients receiving digitalis or with a history of cardiac arrhythmias).
- NOT indicated for initial therapy of edema or hypertension except in individuals in whom development of hypokalemia cannot be risked.

## 5. Contraindications
List includes all contraindications stated in the available sources:
- Hyperkalemia (serum potassium greater than or equal to 5.5 mEq/L).
- Concomitant antikaliuretic (potassium‑conserving) therapy: do not give with other potassium‑conserving agents (examples listed in sources: spironolactone, amiloride, other formulations containing triamterene).
- Concomitant potassium supplementation: do not use with potassium supplements, potassium‑containing salt substitutes, or potassium‑enriched diets.
- Impaired renal function: anuria, acute and chronic renal insufficiency, or significant renal impairment.
- Hypersensitivity to triamterene or hydrochlorothiazide, or to other sulfonamide‑derived drugs.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- None specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- **Hyperkalemia:** Abnormal elevation of serum potassium (≥5.5 mEq/L) can occur with potassium‑conserving diuretic combinations, including this product. Hyperkalemia risk is increased in patients with renal impairment, diabetes (even without overt renal impairment), elderly or severely ill patients. Serum potassium should be monitored frequently, especially at initiation, following dose changes, or during intercurrent illness. If hyperkalemia develops, discontinue the combination and substitute a thiazide alone. For severe hyperkalemia (serum potassium >6.5 mEq/L), emergent measures (e.g., IV calcium, sodium bicarbonate, glucose + insulin, cation exchange resins, dialysis) may be required.
- Use with ACE inhibitors and other agents that raise serum potassium: because ACE inhibitors can raise potassium, use cautiously with this combination; frequent potassium monitoring is required if used together.
- **Electrolyte imbalance and BUN/creatinine increases:** Monitor serum electrolytes (sodium, potassium, chloride, bicarbonate, magnesium), BUN and creatinine at appropriate intervals. Thiazide‑related electrolyte abnormalities include hyponatremia, hypochloremic alkalosis, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia. Triamterene reduces thiazide‑induced kaliuresis but may produce hyperkalemia in some patients.
- **Hepatic impairment / hepatic coma:** Use caution in patients with impaired hepatic function or progressive liver disease, since small changes in fluid and electrolytes may precipitate hepatic encephalopathy.
- **Renal stones:** Triamterene has been reported in renal calculi in association with other stone components; use with caution in patients with a history of nephrolithiasis.
- **Folic acid deficiency:** Triamterene is a weak folic acid antagonist and may contribute to megaloblastic anemia in patients with low folate stores; periodic blood evaluations recommended in such patients.
- **Metabolic and endocrine effects:** Thiazides may decrease serum protein‑bound iodine (PBI) without clinical thyroid disturbance; decrease urinary calcium excretion and may be associated with hypercalcemia and hypophosphatemia in rare prolonged use; thiazides should be discontinued before parathyroid testing. Thiazides can alter glucose tolerance—latent diabetes may manifest or insulin requirements may change.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Sensitivity reactions to thiazides may occur in patients with or without a history of allergy or bronchial asthma. Possible exacerbation or activation of systemic lupus erythematosus has been reported with thiazides.
- **Acute transient myopia and secondary angle‑closure glaucoma:** Hydrochlorothiazide (a sulfonamide) can produce an idiosyncratic reaction causing acute transient myopia and acute angle‑closure glaucoma that may occur within hours to weeks of initiation; discontinue hydrochlorothiazide promptly if symptoms occur and seek urgent ophthalmologic management if intraocular pressure remains elevated.
- **Metabolic/respiratory acidosis:** Avoid potassium‑conserving therapy in severely ill patients at risk for metabolic or respiratory acidosis; acidosis may be associated with rapid rises in serum potassium.
- **Use in diabetics:** Hyperkalemia has been reported in diabetic patients treated with potassium‑conserving agents even without apparent renal impairment; such patients should avoid this product if possible and require frequent monitoring if used.

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and periodic monitoring of serum electrolytes (potassium, sodium, chloride, bicarbonate, magnesium), BUN and creatinine.
- Monitor for signs/symptoms of hyperkalemia (paresthesias, muscle weakness, fatigue, flaccid paralysis, bradycardia, hypotension/shock) and obtain ECG if hyperkalemia suspected.
- Monitor blood glucose in diabetic patients and adjust antidiabetic therapy as required.
- Periodic monitoring of hematologic parameters (if folate deficiency suspected) and uric acid in patients with gout history.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Avoid combined use with other potassium‑sparing agents or potassium supplementation.
- Avoid in patients with significant renal impairment or anuria.
- Use caution when coadministered with NSAIDs (including indomethacin) because of reported cases of acute renal failure with the combination.
- Lithium: use generally should be avoided because diuretics reduce lithium clearance and increase risk of lithium toxicity.
- Monitor closely if used with digitalis; electrolyte disturbances (hypokalemia) can potentiate digitalis toxicity.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Potentiation of other antihypertensive agents: Thiazides may add to or potentiate antihypertensive drugs; dosage adjustments of concomitant antihypertensives may be necessary.
- ACE inhibitors and other potassium‑raising agents: Combined use increases risk of hyperkalemia; monitor serum potassium frequently. [Cautionary statement from available sources; region‑specific restrictions not provided]
- Lithium: Diuretics reduce renal lithium clearance and greatly increase the risk of lithium toxicity; lithium generally should not be given with diuretics.
- Potassium‑sparing agents and potassium supplements: Concomitant use markedly increases the risk of hyperkalemia and is contraindicated.
- Digitalis glycosides: Hypokalemia increases susceptibility to digitalis toxicity; monitor potassium and digitalis response.
- NSAIDs (including indomethacin): Caution—NSAIDs can blunt diuretic and antihypertensive effects and have been associated with acute renal failure when used with triamterene + hydrochlorothiazide; use caution and monitor renal function.

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Hydrochlorothiazide is excreted largely unchanged in urine; triamterene is metabolized primarily to the sulfate conjugate of hydroxytriamterene (metabolite levels exceed parent compound). Specific interacting metabolic pathway modifiers (CYP data) are not provided in available sources.

### Drug/laboratory test interactions
- Triamterene and quinidine have similar fluorescence spectra; this combination may interfere with the measurement of quinidine.
- Thiazides may affect laboratory tests related to parathyroid function and thyroid PBI measurements; discontinue thiazides before parathyroid testing.

### Management notes
- Avoid coadministration with potassium supplements, potassium‑containing salts, or other potassium‑sparing diuretics.
- If concomitant use of ACE inhibitors, ARBs, or other potassium‑raising drugs is necessary, institute frequent serum potassium monitoring.
- Avoid lithium use if possible; if unavoidable, monitor lithium levels closely.
- Monitor renal function when NSAIDs are coadministered; consider alternative analgesics if risk is high.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Routine use of diuretics in otherwise healthy pregnant women is considered inappropriate and may expose mother and fetus to unnecessary risk; diuretics do not prevent development of preeclampsia (toxemia of pregnancy).
- Thiazides may be indicated in pregnancy when edema is due to pathologic causes (same indication as in non‑pregnant patients).
- Dependent edema from mechanical effects of pregnancy should usually be managed by conservative measures (leg elevation, support hose); only in rare, severe symptomatic cases should a short course of diuretics be considered.
- **Triamterene + hydrochlorothiazide use in pregnancy:** Safe use has not been established in humans; the combination should be used during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus.
- Animal reproduction data:
  - Triamterene: Reproduction studies in rats at doses up to 20 times MRHD (body‑weight basis) did not show fetal harm. Animal data may not predict human response.
  - Hydrochlorothiazide: Specific animal teratogenicity details in the available source are truncated and not fully specified.

- Pregnancy category systems: Specific jurisdictional pregnancy category assignments (e.g., Australian category, US letter category) are not specified in available sources.

### Breastfeeding

### Fertility

Hydrochlorothiazide had no adverse effects on fertility in animal studies at specified doses (see carcinogenesis/mutagenesis section for limited fertility study details).

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Use in children: Not specified in available sources.

### Elderly use
- Pharmacokinetic considerations: Renal clearances of hydrochlorothiazide and the pharmacologically active triamterene metabolite (sulfate ester of hydroxytriamterene) are reduced and plasma levels are increased in elderly patients and in patients with impaired renal function.
- Clinical considerations: Elderly patients have increased risk of hyperkalemia and renal function changes; monitor serum electrolytes, BUN and creatinine frequently.

### Renal impairment
- Contraindicated in anuria, acute and chronic renal insufficiency, or significant renal impairment.
- Patients with mild renal impairment: should not receive this product without frequent and continuing monitoring of serum electrolytes and renal function. Cumulative drug effects may occur.

### Hepatic impairment
- Use with caution in patients with impaired hepatic function or progressive liver disease; small changes in fluid and electrolyte balance may precipitate hepatic coma.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Hydrochlorothiazide: Blocks renal tubular reabsorption of sodium and chloride in the distal convoluted tubule, producing natriuresis and diuresis. Diuresis is accompanied by secondary loss of potassium and bicarbonate. Antihypertensive mechanism is not fully established but may involve sodium excretion and redistribution of body sodium and reduced peripheral resistance.
- Triamterene: Potassium‑conserving diuretic that acts on the distal renal tubule to inhibit sodium reabsorption in exchange for potassium and hydrogen, thereby conserving potassium and reducing thiazide‑induced kaliuresis. Not a mineralocorticoid antagonist; acts independently of aldosterone.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption:
  - Hydrochlorothiazide: Peak plasma levels approximately 2 hours after oral administration.
  - Triamterene: Rapidly absorbed; peak plasma levels typically within 1 hour after dosing.
  - Food does not influence absorption of either component from the combination product.
- Distribution: Not fully specified in available sources.
- Metabolism:
  - Hydrochlorothiazide: Excreted rapidly and largely unchanged in urine.
  - Triamterene: Primarily metabolized to hydroxytriamterene sulfate (sulfate conjugate); plasma and urine levels of this metabolite exceed triamterene levels.
- Elimination: Hydrochlorothiazide is excreted unchanged in urine; renal clearance and metabolite elimination are reduced in elderly and renally impaired patients.
- Bioequivalence: The combination product components are reported to be bioequivalent to liquid preparations of the individual components; the hydrochlorothiazide component is bioequivalent to single‑entity hydrochlorothiazide tablets (per available sources).
- Half‑life, clearance values, and volume of distribution: Not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Onset & duration:
  - Hydrochlorothiazide: Onset of diuretic effect within ~2 hours; peak action at ~4 hours; duration ~6–12 hours.
  - Triamterene: Onset and duration similar to hydrochlorothiazide; peak plasma levels ~1 hour after dosing.
- Clinical effect: Hydrochlorothiazide provides the principal diuretic/antihypertensive effect; triamterene primarily reduces excessive potassium loss from hydrochlorothiazide therapy.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Dose‑response evaluations in hypertensive subjects who developed hypokalemia on hydrochlorothiazide were performed to determine optimal triamterene amounts to add to hydrochlorothiazide:
  - For hydrochlorothiazide 50 mg once daily, single daily doses of triamterene at 75 mg produced greater increases in serum potassium than 25 mg and 50 mg; doses above 75 mg did not produce additional potassium elevation.
  - For hydrochlorothiazide 25 mg once daily, single daily triamterene 37.5 mg produced greater increases in serum potassium than 25 mg; higher triamterene doses (75 mg and 100 mg) produced no additional potassium increase.
  - In a study where hydrochlorothiazide 25 mg twice daily induced hypokalemia, triamterene given twice daily increased serum potassium in a dose‑related manner. However, the combination given twice daily appeared to increase frequency of BUN and creatinine elevations; the largest increases in serum potassium, BUN and creatinine were observed with triamterene 50 mg twice daily—the largest dose tested.
- Other pivotal trial details, randomized controlled trial names, primary efficacy endpoints, and comprehensive efficacy/safety data are not specified in available sources.
