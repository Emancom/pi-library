# Editorial Product Information: telotristat ethyl

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): telotristat ethyl
- Chemical class: Not specified in available sources
- ATC code: A16AX15
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of carcinoid syndrome diarrhoea in adults who remain inadequately controlled on somatostatin analogue (SSA) therapy, when used in combination with SSA therapy.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General adult dosing
- Recommended dose: 250 mg three times daily (tid).

### Dosing by clinical situation
- If no clinical response within approximately 12 weeks, reassess the benefit of continued therapy.
- Higher doses are not recommended due to potential accumulation and increased adverse events.

### Administration instructions
- Take with food.
- If short-acting octreotide is required concomitantly, administer short-acting octreotide at least 30 minutes after telotristat ethyl administration to reduce the likelihood of reduced telotristat exposure.

### Titration
- Not specified in available sources.

### Missed dose
- Take the next scheduled dose at the usual time; do not double the next dose to make up for a missed dose.

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to telotristat ethyl (active substance) or to any of the excipients.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatic enzyme elevations:** Elevations in hepatic enzymes have been observed (see Adverse Effects). Monitor liver enzymes prior to and during treatment as clinically indicated. In patients with suspected liver injury, discontinue telotristat ethyl; do not resume unless another cause is established.
- **Constipation / serious bowel events:** Telotristat ethyl reduces bowel movement frequency. Monitor for constipation. Serious constipation and faecaloma have been reported at higher than recommended doses; evaluate telotristat ethyl and other medications affecting bowel motility if constipation develops.
- **Depressive disorders:** Depression, depressed mood and decreased interest have been reported. Advise patients to report new or worsening mood changes; monitor and manage appropriately.
- **Use in hepatic impairment:** See Section 10 for dose adjustments and recommendations; continuous monitoring for adverse events and liver function deterioration is advised.
- **Use in renal impairment:** See Section 10 for guidance; not recommended in patients with end-stage renal disease requiring dialysis (use has not been established).
- **Ability to drive/use machines:** Telotristat ethyl may cause fatigue; this may impair ability to drive or operate machinery.
- **Monitoring requirements:** Baseline and periodic liver enzyme testing as clinically indicated; monitor bowel function and mood; monitor for signs of drug interactions that may reduce efficacy of concomitant medications (see Section 8).

## 7. Adverse Effects
- Reporting of suspected adverse reactions is important for ongoing safety monitoring.
- Summary: Most commonly reported adverse reactions were abdominal pain (~26%), gamma‑glutamyl transferase increase (~11%), and fatigue (~10%). Reactions were generally mild to moderate. Abdominal pain was the most frequent cause of discontinuation.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Summary and clinical implications
- Telotristat ethyl and its active metabolite have multiple in vitro and in vivo interaction potentials; monitor for reduced efficacy of concomitant medications and for altered exposure of co‑medications. Where clinically significant interactions are possible, consider monitoring therapeutic effect and safety of the concomitant medication; adjust therapy as clinically indicated.

### Specific interactions and management
- Short-acting octreotide:
  - Concomitant short-acting octreotide markedly decreased systemic exposure of telotristat ethyl and active metabolite in a pharmacokinetic study. If short-acting octreotide is required, administer it at least 30 minutes after telotristat ethyl.
- Carboxylesterase 2 (CES2) inhibitors and substrates:
  - Telotristat ethyl is metabolised by CES2; in vitro inhibition data show potential interaction with CES2 inhibitors (example IC50 for loperamide ~5.2 μM). In clinical trials, routine use with loperamide did not show changes in telotristat exposure or safety signals.
  - Telotristat ethyl inhibits CES2 in vitro (IC50 ~0.56 μM). Concomitant use may change exposure of CES2 substrate drugs (e.g. prasugrel, irinotecan, capecitabine, flutamide); if co‑administration is unavoidable, monitor for altered efficacy and safety.
- Cytochrome P450 enzymes:
  - CYP2B6: Telotristat (active metabolite) induced CYP2B6 in vitro. Concomitant use may decrease exposure and efficacy of CYP2B6 substrates (examples: valproic acid, bupropion, sertraline). Monitor for suboptimal efficacy.
  - CYP3A4: In vitro data did not show clinically relevant systemic induction at expected concentrations, but telotristat ethyl inhibited CYP3A4 in vitro and in vivo data demonstrated decreased midazolam exposure after multiple doses of telotristat ethyl 500 mg tid (twice the recommended dose): midazolam AUC decreased ~48% and Cmax ~25%. This suggests telotristat ethyl may reduce exposure of some CYP3A4 substrates; monitor for reduced efficacy of CYP3A4 substrates (examples include midazolam, everolimus, sunitinib, simvastatin, atorvastatin, ethinylestradiol, amlodipine, nifedipine, verapamil, diltiazem, ciclosporin, carbamazepine, topiramate, valproic acid).
  - Other CYPs: Based on available in vitro data, clinically relevant interactions with other CYP isoenzymes are not expected.
- P‑glycoprotein (P‑gp) and MRP‑2:
  - Telotristat ethyl is an inhibitor of P‑gp (IC50 ~2 μM) and inhibited MRP2‑mediated transport (~98% inhibition in vitro). However, a clinical study showed only a small increase (~16%) in fexofenadine exposure (P‑gp/MRP2 substrate) when co‑administered with telotristat ethyl 500 mg tid. Clinically meaningful interactions with P‑gp and MRP2 substrates are considered unlikely at recommended dosing, but consider monitoring when co‑administering narrow‑therapeutic‑index substrates.
- BCRP:
  - Telotristat ethyl inhibited BCRP in vitro (IC50 ~20 μM); active metabolite did not significantly inhibit. In vivo interaction potential considered low.
- Transporter substrate status:
  - Telotristat ethyl and telotristat are not substrates of P‑gp or MRP‑2.
- Clinical interaction study considerations:
  - Many interaction signals (e.g. midazolam reduction) were observed at 500 mg tid (twice the recommended dose); nevertheless, monitor for interactions at therapeutic dosing and manage accordingly.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- Pregnancy:
  - Use in pregnancy: **Not recommended** during pregnancy and in women of childbearing potential not using contraception.
  - Pregnancy category [Australian]: Category B3.
  - Human data: No data from use in pregnant women available.
  - Animal data:
    - Rats: Telotristat etiprate up to 750 mg/kg/day during organogenesis did not affect embryo‑foetal development; no teratogenicity observed.
    - Rabbits: Maternal toxicity and increased post‑implantation loss observed at ≥ 250 mg/kg/day; reduced foetal weights at 500 mg/kg/day.
- Breastfeeding:
  - It is unknown whether telotristat ethyl or its active metabolite are excreted in human milk. A risk to newborns/infants cannot be excluded. Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment.
  - Animal data: Administration to rats at 500 mg/kg/day from gestation day 6 to lactation day 20 resulted in increased pup mortality early postnatally; no effects observed at 200 mg/kg/day.
- Fertility:
  - No human fertility studies; in rats, no effect on fertility at oral doses up to 500 mg/kg/day (up to ~2.5× clinical exposure based on AUC).

## 10. Use in Special Populations
- Paediatric use:
  - Safety and efficacy in children and adolescents (<18 years) have not been established. Not specified for dosing in paediatric populations.
- Elderly (≥65 years):
  - No dosage adjustment required solely on the basis of age.
- Renal impairment:
  - No dosage change required for mild, moderate, or severe renal impairment in patients not requiring dialysis.
  - Use in end‑stage renal disease requiring dialysis (eGFR < 15 mL/min/1.73 m²) has not been established and is **not recommended**.
- Hepatic impairment:
  - Mild hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh A): dose reduction to 250 mg twice daily may be necessary depending on tolerability.
  - Moderate hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh B): dose reduction to 250 mg once daily may be necessary depending on tolerability.
  - Severe hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh C): use is **not recommended**.
- Dose adjustments described above should be guided by clinical response and tolerability; monitor liver function closely.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Telotristat ethyl (a prodrug) and its active metabolite telotristat inhibit tryptophan hydroxylase isoforms TPH1 and TPH2, the rate‑limiting enzymes in serotonin biosynthesis. By inhibiting peripheral TPH1, telotristat reduces peripheral serotonin production, which is implicated in secretion, motility, inflammation and sensation in the gastrointestinal tract and contributes to carcinoid syndrome symptoms.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Administration of telotristat ethyl reduces whole blood serotonin and 24‑hour urinary 5‑hydroxyindoleacetic acid (u5‑HIAA) in healthy subjects and in patients with carcinoid syndrome; statistically significant reductions in u5‑HIAA were shown in clinical trials at 250 mg tid versus placebo.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption/Administration: Telotristat ethyl should be taken with food. Specific bioavailability, Tmax, Cmax and elimination half‑life values are not specified in available sources.
- Metabolism: Telotristat ethyl is metabolised (including by carboxylesterase 2/CES2) to the active metabolite telotristat. In vitro interactions implicate CES2 involvement.
- Elimination: Not specified in available sources.
- Inter‑subject variability and accumulation: High inter‑subject variability has been reported; accumulation in a subset of patients with carcinoid syndrome cannot be excluded.
- Transporters and enzymes: Telotristat ethyl is an inhibitor of P‑gp and MRP2 in vitro; telotristat ethyl and telotristat are not substrates of P‑gp or MRP2. Telotristat induces CYP2B6 in vitro; various in vitro/in vivo effects on CYP3A4 noted (see Section 8).

## 12. Clinical Trials
### Key pivotal trial (TELESTAR)
- Design: Randomised, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled, multicentre Phase 3 study in adult patients with well‑differentiated metastatic neuroendocrine tumours, carcinoid syndrome, on stable long‑acting SSA therapy, with ≥4 daily bowel movements at baseline. Patients randomized 1:1:1 to placebo, telotristat ethyl 250 mg tid or telotristat ethyl 500 mg tid for a 12‑week double‑blind treatment period, with an open‑label extension.
- Population: 135 patients randomized; mean age ~63.6 years; both sexes included; all on SSA therapy.
- Primary endpoint: Change from baseline in mean number of daily bowel movements (BMs) averaged over 12 weeks.
- Results (250 mg tid vs placebo):
  - Baseline mean BMs/day: placebo ~5.2; telotristat 250 mg ~6.1.
  - Change averaged over 12 weeks: placebo −0.6; telotristat 250 mg −1.4; treatment difference ~−0.8 BMs/day (statistically significant, p<0.001).
  - Change from baseline in BMs/day at Week 12: placebo −0.9; telotristat 250 mg −1.7 (difference ~−0.8; p value variable across analyses).
  - Durable response (≥30% reduction in daily BMs for ≥50% of time during double‑blind period): placebo 20.0% (9/45); telotristat 250 mg 44.4% (20/45); odds ratio approximately 3.5 (p=0.011).
  - Proportion with reductions in mean daily BMs averaged over 12 weeks:
    - ≥1 BM/day reduction: 66.7% (telotristat 250 mg) vs 31.1% (placebo)
    - ≥1.5 BM/day reduction: 46.7% vs 20.0%
    - ≥2 BM/day reduction: 33.3% vs 4.4%
- Secondary endpoints:
  - Telotristat 250 mg tid produced statistically significant reductions in u5‑HIAA excretion at Week 12 versus placebo (mean percent change: placebo +14.4% vs telotristat −42.3%; treatment difference approx −53.4%).
  - Effects on flushing and abdominal pain were secondary and variably reported.
- Time course:
  - Reductions in BM frequency were observed as early as week 3, with the largest effect during the final 6 weeks of the 12‑week double‑blind treatment period.
- Safety:
  - Most commonly reported adverse events included abdominal pain, elevated GGT, and fatigue; serious constipation observed at higher doses.

### Other studies and extensions
- A long‑term open‑label extension (TELEPATH) enrolled additional patients and included telotristat etiprate exposure up to mean ~103 weeks; no new adverse effects were identified, although a substantial proportion of extension subjects received 500 mg tid in that study.

## 13. Overdose
- Human experience: Limited. Single doses of 1,500 mg in healthy subjects produced gastrointestinal symptoms (nausea, diarrhoea, abdominal pain, vomiting).
- Management: General supportive and symptomatic care. Contact local poison information services as appropriate.
