# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following jurisdictional Product Information (PI): 
    - **US (FDA)**: Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation).
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note**: This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidance required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Levocarnitine ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
- **Chemical Class**: Carrier molecule (amino acid derivative).
- **Brand Names**: Not specified in provided source [US: Levocarnitine Injection, USP].
- **ATC Code**: Not specified in available source.
- **Dosage Form and Strength**: Injection (solution), 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration**: Intravenous only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism)**: For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD)**: For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing**: The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Administration**: Administered as a slow bolus injection (over 2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Loading Doses**: Often utilised in patients presenting with severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency**: Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Monitoring**: Subsequent daily doses should be adjusted based on clinical response and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L). The highest reported dose is 300 mg/kg.

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose**: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration**: Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Adjustments**: Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels (target: >40–50 µmol/L).

## 4. Contraindications
- **None Known**: No contraindications have been identified in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions**: Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have occurred, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may manifest within minutes of administration. If severe reactions occur, treatment must be discontinued and appropriate medical intervention initiated [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements**: Treatment should be guided by plasma carnitine concentrations (weekly/monthly). Monitoring should also include blood chemistries and vital signs [US].
- **Oral Safety in Renal Impairment**: While this document covers the injection, it is noted that safety for the *oral* formulation has not been established in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5%)
In clinical trials of ESRD patients on hemodialysis, the following events were reported regardless of causality:
- **General**: Injection site reaction (33–59%), pain (30–49%), flu syndrome (25–40%), headache (22–37%), chest pain (12–15%), fever (5–12%), and abdominal pain (5–21%) [US].
- **Cardiovascular**: Hypertension (14–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%).
- **Gastrointestinal**: Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%).
- **Respiratory**: Pharyngitis (15–33%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–19%).
- **Neurological**: Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%).

### Serious and Post-Marketing Adverse Effects
- **Neurologic**: Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with existing conditions, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Gastrointestinal**: Transient nausea, vomiting, and body odor have been reported.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin**: Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management**: Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: [US: Category not explicitly lettered in PI, but standard "clearly needed" text applies]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate, well-controlled studies exist in humans [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine intake must be weighed against maternal benefits [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Dosing is based on mg/kg weight (see Section 3).
- **Elderly Use**: Not specifically detailed in available sources [US].
- **Renal Impairment**: Safely used intravenously in ESRD; however, caution is advised regarding the accumulation of metabolites if oral formulations are used in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Distribution**: Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism**: Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination**: Following IV administration (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The distribution half-life is 0.585 hours and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies**: Pharmacokinetic data in ESRD patients (N=12) showed that a 20 mg/kg dose three times per week significantly increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Mean pre-dialysis (trough) levels increased from approximately 21.3 µmol/L at baseline to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks of therapy [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Data**: Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses confirmed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions**: Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing [US].
- **Stability**: Compatible and stable for 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL [US].
- **Handling**: Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives.
