# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP), a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Name:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Names:** Not specified in source (Generic: Levocarnitine Injection).
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing hemodialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:**
    *   **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg given as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
    *   **Acute Management:** A loading dose is often administered in severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the next 24 hours.
    *   **Frequency:** Doses should be administered every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    *   **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    *   **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    *   **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    *   **Dose Adjustments:** Should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration before use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL).

### Monitoring
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels before therapy.
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L).
- **ESRD:** Monitor trough levels (normal range 40 to 50 µmol/L).

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a reaction occurs.
- **Seizure Risk:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity may occur.
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation Note):** The safety/efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal failure. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO).

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic errors.

- **Very Common (>10%):**
    *   **General:** Injection site reaction (38–59%), pain (21–49%), flu syndrome (15–40%), headache (3–37%), infection (10–24%), abdominal pain (5–21%).
    *   **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (14–21%), hypotension (3–19%).
    *   **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%).
    *   **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–33%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–19%).
    *   **Nervous:** Dizziness (10–18%).

- **Common (1–10%):**
    *   Chest pain, fever, asthenia, back pain, tachycardia, palpitations, anorexia, constipation, dyspepsia, anemia, peripheral edema, hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, weight changes, leg cramps, depression, insomnia, paresthesia, pruritus, rhinitis.

- **Serious/Postmarketing:**
    *   Seizures, anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm.
    *   Transient body odor and gastritis have been observed.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concomitantly with levocarnitine.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate studies exist in pregnant women. Use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine milk concentrations after administration. Risks to the infant of excess carnitine should be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation.
- **Fertility:** No mutagenic effects were found in standard bacterial/yeast assays. Long-term animal carcinogenicity studies have not been performed.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is weight-based (see Section 3).
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed; however, dosing should generally be cautious, reflecting the higher frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function.
- **Renal Impairment:** The injection is specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients undergoing dialysis. Dose adjustments are based on pre-dialysis trough levels.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    *   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    *   **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, dialysis leads to carnitine loss; IV administration increases plasma concentrations to normal or supra-normal levels.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg three times weekly), mean pre-dialysis trough levels increased from 21.3 µmol/L at baseline to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations at 12 and 24 weeks. While carnitine levels increased, the effect on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
