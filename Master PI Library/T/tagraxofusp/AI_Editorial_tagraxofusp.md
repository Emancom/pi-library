# Editorial Product Information: Tagraxofusp

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial product information has been synthesized from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tagraxofusp-erzs; a CD123-directed cytotoxin (fusion protein comprising recombinant human interleukin-3 and truncated diphtheria toxin).
- **Brand Name:** **ELZONRIS** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injection for intravenous use; 1,000 mcg in 1 mL (1,000 mcg/mL) in a single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Stemline Therapeutics, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Blastic Plasmacytoid Dendritic Cell Neoplasm (BPDCN):** Indicated for the treatment of BPDCN in adults and in pediatric patients aged 2 years and older [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** 12 mcg/kg intravenously over 15 minutes once daily on days 1 to 5 of a 21-day cycle.
- **Cycle Management:** The dosing period may be extended up to day 10 to accommodate dose delays. Treatment should continue until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.
- **Weight-Based Dosing:** Dose is calculated based on the patient's actual body weight.
- **Premedication:** Administer the following approximately 60 minutes prior to each infusion:
    - H1-histamine antagonist (e.g., diphenhydramine hydrochloride).
    - H2-histamine antagonist (e.g., famotidine).
    - Corticosteroid (e.g., 50 mg IV methylprednisolone or equivalent).
    - Acetaminophen (or paracetamol).
- **Setting of Administration:** 
    - **Cycle 1:** Must be administered in an inpatient setting. Patients must be observed for at least 24 hours after the final infusion.
    - **Subsequent Cycles:** May be administered in an inpatient or appropriate outpatient setting equipped for monitoring patients with hematopoietic malignancies. Observation for at least 4 hours post-infusion is required.
- **Preparation Highlights:**
    - Requires a 2-step dilution process to reach a final concentration of 100 mcg/mL using 0.9% Sodium Chloride.
    - Use a 0.2 micron polyethersulfone in-line filter for administration.
    - Prepared doses should be administered within 4 hours and kept at room temperature during this window.

## 4. Contraindications
- None specified in available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Capillary Leak Syndrome (CLS) [US]:** 
    - CLS can be life-threatening or fatal. In clinical trials, the incidence was 53%, with a 3% fatality rate.
    - Median time to onset is 4 days; most events occur in Cycle 1.
    - **Baseline Requirement:** Serum albumin must be ≥3.2 g/dL before initiating treatment.
    - **Monitoring:** Monitor weight, vital signs, and serum albumin levels prior to each dose. Assess for edema, pulmonary edema, and hypotension.
    - **Management:** Withhold dose if albumin <3.5 g/dL or if weight gain is ≥1.5 kg over the previous day. Treat with IV albumin (25g), diuretics, and/or corticosteroids as indicated.
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Reported in 43% of patients. Monitor for rash, pruritus, or stomatitis. Permanently discontinue for severe or life-threatening reactions.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Elevations in ALT (79%) and AST (76%) are common, typically occurring in Cycle 1. Monitor transaminases before each infusion. Withhold treatment if levels exceed 5 times the upper limit of normal (ULN).

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Reactions (Incidence ≥30%)
- Capillary leak syndrome (53%)
- Nausea (45%)
- Fatigue (45%)
- Pyrexia (43%)
- Peripheral edema (39%)
- Weight increase (31%)

### Common Laboratory Abnormalities (Incidence ≥50%)
- **Increases:** Glucose (89%), ALT (79%), AST (76%).
- **Decreases:** Albumin (72%), Platelets (68%), Hemoglobin (61%), Calcium (57%), Sodium (52%).

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Fatalities:** 3% of patients in trials experienced fatal CLS.
- **Tumor Lysis Syndrome:** Reported in <10% of patients.
- **Febrile Neutropenia:** Grade 3 or higher reported in 16% of patients.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No formal drug-drug interaction studies have been conducted.
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Risk Summary:** No available human data. Based on the mechanism of action, **ELZONRIS** may cause fetal harm. Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted [US].
    - **Pregnancy Testing:** Verify pregnancy status within 7 days prior to initiation in females of reproductive potential.
    - **Contraception:** Females should use effective contraception during treatment and for 1 week after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for 1 week after the final dose due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in the child [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies on fertility have not been conducted.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established for patients ≥2 years old. Efficacy is extrapolated from adult data. Safety for patients <2 years is not established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Patients ≥75 years experienced a higher incidence of altered mental status (e.g., delirium, encephalopathy, confusion) compared to younger patients.
- **Renal Impairment:** No clinically significant PK differences observed in mild to moderate renal impairment (eGFR 30–89 mL/min). Effect of severe impairment is unknown.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No clinically significant PK differences observed in mild to moderate hepatic impairment. Effect of severe impairment (bilirubin >3x ULN) is unknown.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A fusion protein targeting CD123 (interleukin-3 receptor alpha). Upon binding, the molecule is internalized, and the truncated diphtheria toxin (DT) moiety inhibits protein synthesis by inactivating elongation factor 2 (EF-2), leading to apoptosis in CD123-expressing cells.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Mean volume of distribution is 5.1 L.
    - **Metabolism/Elimination:** Mean clearance is 7.1 L/hr. Terminal half-life is approximately 0.7 hours.
- **Immunogenicity:** High incidence of anti-drug antibodies (ADA). 96% of patients had pre-existing ADA (likely due to diphtheria vaccination), and 99% developed treatment-emergent ADA by the end of Cycle 2. ADA presence is associated with reduced free drug concentration.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study STML-401-0114:** A multicenter, single-arm trial including 13 treatment-naive BPDCN patients. 
    - **Primary Outcome:** Rate of complete response (CR) or clinical complete response (CRc).
    - **Results:** The CR/CRc rate was 53.8% (95% CI: 25.1, 80.8). The median time to response was 57 days.
- **Relapsed/Refractory Cohort:** In a cohort of 15 patients with relapsed/refractory BPDCN, one patient achieved CR (111 days) and one patient achieved CRc (424 days).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store in a freezer between -25°C and -15°C (-13°F and 5°F).
- **Light Protection:** Store in the original package until the time of use.
- **Thawing:** Thaw at room temperature (15°C to 25°C) for 15–30 minutes before preparation. Do not refreeze once thawed.
