## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class**: Carrier molecule (amino acid derivative) involved in fatty acid metabolism.
- **Brand Names**: Not specified in available sources (Generic Levocarnitine Injection, Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code**: Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s)**: 
    - **Injection**: 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration**: Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism**: Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD)**: Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Administration Route**: Strictly for intravenous use only [US].
- **Parenteral Inspection**: Solutions must be visually inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [US].

### Dosing by Indication
- **Metabolic Disorders**:
    - **Recommended Dose**: 50 mg/kg as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose**: Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the next 24 hours [US].
    - **Frequency**: Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Monitoring**: It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine levels prior to therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is advised [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis**:
    - **Starting Dose**: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Administration**: Administer as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
    - **Dose Adjustment**: Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma carnitine levels (normal range: 40 to 50 µmol/L). Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made by the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions**: Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These can occur within minutes of IV administration, particularly in ESRD patients on dialysis. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks/benefits before re-administration [US].
- **Seizure Activity**: Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known seizure disorder, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Accumulation of Metabolites**: In patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD on dialysis, chronic high-dose **oral** levocarnitine (not specified for IV, but noted as a general precaution) may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites: trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5%)
The following frequencies are derived from clinical trials in ESRD patients on chronic hemodialysis:

| Body System | Adverse Event (Frequency ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain (9%), Asthenia (9%), Chest pain (12%), Fever (7%), Flu syndrome (25%), Headache (22%), Infection (15%), Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%) |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%), Nausea (8%), Anorexia (5%) |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia (9%), Hyperkalemia (6%), Hypervolemia (5%) |
| **Nervous System** | Dizziness (13%), Insomnia (4%), Paresthesia (5%), Depression (5%) |

### Serious and Other Adverse Effects
- **Neurologic**: Seizures (Postmarketing experience).
- **Gastrointestinal**: Transient nausea and vomiting; body odor and gastritis have been observed less frequently.
- **Hypersensitivity**: Anaphylaxis and laryngeal edema.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin**: Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management**: Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin anticoagulation [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **US Classification**: Pregnancy Category not explicitly lettered in the provided source; however, reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: 
    - Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. 
    - Studies in dairy cows show levocarnitine concentrations in milk increase after administration. 
    - Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the maternal benefits. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].
- **Fertility**: No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: See section 3 for weight-based dosing (mg/kg), which applies to paediatric patients with metabolic disorders or ESRD [US].
- **Elderly Use**: Not specifically detailed in available sources; however, dosing should generally be cautious, reflecting the greater frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function.
- **Renal Impairment**: 
    - Safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. 
    - For IV use in ESRD, see specific dosing in section 3. Monitoring for toxic metabolite accumulation is required in severe renal impairment [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. it facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Distribution**: Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism**: Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination**: Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), the distribution half-life is approximately 0.585 hours and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics**: In ESRD patients, IV administration increases plasma carnitine concentrations. Normal plasma free carnitine levels are 40 to 50 µmol/L; levels <20 µmol/L define deficiency [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies**: A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis showed that trough concentrations reached 54–180 µmol/L after one week and remained stable. 
- **Dose-Ranging Trials**: Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Clinical Outcomes Note**: While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, its effects on specific clinical outcomes or symptoms of carnitine deficiency in this population have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection**: Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing [US].
- **Handling**: Vials do not contain preservatives; discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial [US].
- **Compatibility**: Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
