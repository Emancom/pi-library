# Editorial Product Information: Trihexyphenidyl

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian** (Therapeutic Goods Administration).
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data into a single, coherent reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidelines required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Trihexyphenidyl (also known as benzhexol) hydrochloride.
- **Chemical Class:** Synthetic tertiary amine antimuscarinic agent.
- **Brand Name(s):** Artane [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N04AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
  - Oral Tablets: 2 mg and 5 mg [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Parkinsonism:** As an adjunct in the treatment of all forms of Parkinsonism, including postencephalitic, arteriosclerotic, and idiopathic varieties [Australian].
- **Extrapyramidal Disorders:** Prevention or control of extrapyramidal symptoms induced by Central Nervous System (CNS) drugs, such as phenothiazines [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Dosage must be strictly individualised, beginning with a low dose and increasing gradually, particularly in patients over 60 years of age.

### Parkinsonism
- **Initial Therapy:** 1 mg on the first day [Australian].
- **Titration:** Increase by 2 mg increments at intervals of three to five days.
- **Maintenance:** Most patients require 6 mg to 10 mg daily in divided doses. 
- **Maximum Dose:** Postencephalitic patients may require higher doses, ranging from 12 mg to 15 mg daily [Australian].
- **Dosing Schedule:** Doses up to 10 mg are typically divided into three parts and taken at mealtimes. Doses exceeding 10 mg daily should be divided into four parts (three at mealtimes and one at bedtime) [Australian].

### Drug-Induced Extrapyramidal Disorders
- **Dosing Range:** Usually between 5 mg and 15 mg daily, though some cases are controlled with 1 mg daily [Australian].
- **Titration:** Therapy may commence with 1 mg; if symptoms are not controlled within hours, subsequent doses are increased progressively until stabilization.

### Administration Instructions
- **Relation to Food:** If trihexyphenidyl causes excessive mouth dryness, it is best taken before meals unless it induces nausea. If taken after meals, thirst can be managed with mints, chewing gum, or water. Postencephalitic patients with excessive salivation may prefer post-prandial administration [Australian].
- **Substitution:** When replacing other parasympathetic inhibitors, use partial substitution initially, gradually reducing the previous medication as trihexyphenidyl is increased.

## 4. Contraindications
Trihexyphenidyl is contraindicated in patients with:
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to trihexyphenidyl hydrochloride or any excipients [Australian].
- **Narrow-Angle Glaucoma:** Use is contraindicated as it may precipitate angle closure; blindness has been reported following long-term use in these patients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** Potentially fatal NMS has been reported following dose reduction or abrupt withdrawal. Symptoms include hyperpyrexia, muscle rigidity, and autonomic instability [Australian]. 
- **Abrupt Withdrawal:** Sudden cessation of therapy may lead to an acute exacerbation of Parkinsonism or a withdrawal syndrome (anxiety, tachycardia, orthostatic hypotension, and insomnia). Doses should be tapered gradually [Australian].
- **Ocular Effects:** May induce cycloplegia and mydriasis. Patients should undergo gonioscopic evaluation and regular intraocular pressure monitoring. Dry eyes may necessitate the use of lubricant drops or discontinuation of contact lenses [Australian].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Anhidrosis/Hyperthermia:** Caution is required in patients with fever, those exercising, or those in hot environments (especially alcoholics or those with CNS disease). Severe anhidrosis and fatal hyperthermia can occur [Australian].
- **Tardive Dyskinesia:** Not recommended for patients with tardive dyskinesia unless they have concomitant Parkinson’s disease, as it may aggravate symptoms [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Disease:** Due to its positive chronotropic effect, it may aggravate hypertension, tachycardia, and coronary ischaemia. Monitor patients with heart failure or arrhythmias closely [Australian].
- **Psychiatric Effects:** May induce confusion, delusions, and hallucinations. Treatment may need to be discontinued if these occur.
- **Drug Abuse:** Potential for abuse due to stimulating and euphoriant effects, potentially leading to anticholinergic delirium [Australian].
- **Myasthenia Gravis:** Avoid or use with extreme caution as it may worsen clinical symptoms [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are primarily extensions of pharmacological anticholinergic activity. Approximately 30% to 50% of patients experience side effects, which may diminish with continued therapy or dose adjustment [Australian].

| System Organ Class | Adverse Reactions |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Immune System** | Hypersensitivity |
| **Psychiatric** | Hallucinations, delusions, agitation, paranoia, confusion, insomnia |
| **Nervous System** | Dizziness, headache, nervousness, euphoria, memory impairment, sedation, choreiform movements, NMS (on withdrawal) |
| **Eye** | Mydriasis, blurred vision, photophobia, increased intraocular pressure, narrow-angle glaucoma |
| **Cardiac** | Tachycardia, paradoxical sinus bradycardia |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Dry mouth (xerostomia), constipation, vomiting, nausea, paralytic ileus, suppurative parotitis |
| **Renal/Urinary** | Urinary hesitancy, urinary retention, dysuria |
| **General** | Anhidrosis (decreased sweating), heat intolerance, weakness, pyrexia |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CNS Depressants:** Cannabinoids, barbiturates, and opiates may have additive effects. Alcohol should be avoided as it may increase trihexyphenidyl metabolism, reducing efficacy [Australian].
- **Anticholinergics:** Additive effects occur with MAOIs, TCAs, antihistamines, phenothiazines, and amantadine. This increases the risk of dry mouth, urinary retention, and constipation [Australian].
- **Neuroleptics:** Concomitant use may increase the risk of tardive dyskinesia [Australian].
- **Levodopa:** Dose reduction of either agent may be necessary as combined therapy can increase drug-induced involuntary movements [Australian].
- **Anticonvulsants:** May exacerbate seizures in patients with epilepsy [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal Agents:** 
  - **Prokinetics:** Antagonises the effects of metoclopramide and domperidone [Australian].
  - **Magnesium Hydroxide:** Inhibits trihexyphenidyl absorption; space doses by at least 2 hours [Australian].
- **Carbonic Anhydrase Inhibitors:** Acetazolamide may decrease trihexyphenidyl excretion. Topiramate plus trihexyphenidyl increases the risk of hyperthermia [Australian].
- **Memantine:** May enhance anticholinergic side effects [Australian].
- **Food:** Acidic foods/juices may decrease drug effects; large amounts of coffee may enhance euphoric effects [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category B1. No animal reproduction studies have been conducted. Use only if the potential benefit outweighs the unknown risk to the foetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Excretion in human milk is unknown. It may suppress lactation. Use only if benefits to the mother outweigh potential risks to the infant [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established. Reported adverse events in literature include hyperkinesia, psychosis, and weight loss [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Patients >60 years are highly sensitive and require strict dosage regulation. Increased risk of cognitive dysfunction (confusion, memory loss) and urinary retention (particularly in males with prostatic hypertrophy) [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Maintain under close observation; reduced metabolism may increase the risk of side effects [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Exerts a direct inhibitory effect on the parasympathetic nervous system and has a relaxing effect on smooth muscle (both direct and indirect) [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Small doses may cause CNS depression; larger doses may cause CNS stimulation similar to atropine toxicity. Tolerance may develop with prolonged use [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Not specified in available sources (listed as "No data available") [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No pivotal trial data provided in the available sources [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Container:** HDPE bottles of 200 tablets [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
