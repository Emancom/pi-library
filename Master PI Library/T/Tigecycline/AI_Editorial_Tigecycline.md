# Editorial Product Information: Tigecycline

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of tigecycline for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, regulatory requirements, and safety information into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, legally approved Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved PI for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tigecycline, a glycylcycline antibacterial (a subcategory of the tetracycline class).
- **Brand Names:** Tigecycline-AFT [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Lyophilised powder or cake for injection; 50 mg per vial [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AFT Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Complicated skin and skin structure infections (cSSSI):** Treatment in adults, including those involving methicillin-resistant *Staphylococcus aureus* (MRSA), specifically when there is suspected or proven resistance/intolerance to other agents or when co-morbidities preclude the use of other available therapies [Australian].
- **Complicated intra-abdominal infections (cIAI):** Treatment in adults where there is suspected or proven resistance/intolerance to other agents or when co-morbidities preclude the use of other available therapies [Australian].
- **Note on Non-Approved Indications:** Tigecycline is **not** approved for the treatment of hospital-acquired pneumonia (including ventilator-associated pneumonia), community-acquired pneumonia, or diabetic foot infections [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):**
    - **Initial Loading Dose:** 100 mg.
    - **Maintenance Dose:** 50 mg every 12 hours.
- **Administration:** Administer via IV infusion over 30 to 60 minutes.
- **Duration of Therapy:** Typically 5 to 14 days, guided by infection severity, site, and clinical/bacteriological progress [Australian].
- **Preparation:** 
    - Reconstitute 50 mg vial with 5.3 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride or 5% glucose to reach 10 mg/mL.
    - Swirl gently until dissolved. 
    - Withdraw 5 mL (equivalent to 50 mg) and add to a 100 mL IV bag for infusion [Australian].
- **Line Compatibility:** Use a dedicated line or Y-site. If a common line is used, it must be flushed with 0.9% sodium chloride or 5% glucose before and after tigecycline administration [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to tigecycline or any excipients (e.g., lactose monohydrate) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **All-cause Mortality:** An increase in all-cause mortality has been observed in Phase 3 and 4 clinical trials (4.0% for tigecycline vs 3.0% for comparators). This risk was highest in hospital-acquired pneumonia, particularly ventilator-associated pneumonia. This imbalance should be considered when choosing treatment options [Australian].
- **Anaphylaxis:** Life-threatening anaphylactic/anaphylactoid reactions have been reported [Australian].
- **Tetracycline-Class Effects:** Tigecycline may cause adverse effects similar to tetracyclines, including photosensitivity, pseudotumor cerebri, and anti-anabolic actions (leading to increased urea, azotaemia, and acidosis) [Australian].
- **Acute Pancreatitis:** Cases, some fatal, have occurred. Discontinue treatment if pancreatitis is suspected [Australian].
- **Coagulation Monitoring:** Monitoring of blood coagulation parameters (including fibrinogen) is recommended at baseline and regularly during treatment [Australian].
- **Tooth Discolouration:** May cause permanent tooth discolouration if used during tooth development [Australian].
- **Pseudomembranous Colitis:** *Clostridium difficile*-associated diarrhoea (CDAD) ranges from mild to life-threatening.
- **Intestinal Perforation:** Exercise caution when using tigecycline monotherapy in cIAI secondary to clinically apparent intestinal perforation [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Approximately 40.6% of patients in clinical trials experienced drug-related adverse reactions.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Nausea (29.5%), Vomiting (19.7%), Diarrhoea (12.7%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Infection, Abscess, Headache, Dizziness, Abdominal pain, Dyspepsia, AST/ALT increased, Anaemia, Thrombocythaemia, Hypoproteinaemia, Rash, Pruritus, Fever, Injection site reaction |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Pancreatitis (acute), Jaundice, Thrombophlebitis, International Normalised Ratio (INR) increased |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Hypofibrinogenaemia |
| **Frequency Not Known** | Anaphylactic reactions, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome, Cholestasis |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Tigecycline may prolong prothrombin time; monitor anticoagulation tests closely [Australian].
- **Oral Contraceptives:** Antibiotic use may decrease the efficacy of oral contraceptives. Patients should use an additional barrier method [Australian].
- **Calcineurin Inhibitors:** Concomitant use with tacrolimus or ciclosporin may increase their serum trough concentrations. Monitor levels to avoid toxicity [Australian].
- **Digoxin:** Tigecycline slightly decreases the Cmax of digoxin (13%) but does not affect its AUC or pharmacodynamics. No dose adjustment is required [Australian].
- **P-glycoprotein (P-gp):** Tigecycline is a substrate of P-gp; inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole) or inducers (e.g., rifampicin) may affect its pharmacokinetics [Australian].
- **Cytochrome P450:** Tigecycline does not inhibit major CYP450 isoforms (1A2, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 3A4) [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. May cause foetal harm. Animal studies show decreased foetal weights and skeletal anomalies. Use only if the potential benefit outweighs the risk to the foetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if tigecycline is excreted in human milk. While animal data show excretion in rat milk, systemic exposure in nursing pups is likely limited due to low oral bioavailability. Exercise caution [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no impairment of mating or fertility at 5 times the human daily dose [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for patients under 18 years of age due to lack of safety and efficacy data and risks of tooth discolouration [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment is generally required, though greater sensitivity to adverse events in some older individuals cannot be ruled out [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is necessary, including for patients undergoing haemodialysis [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild to Moderate (Child-Pugh A and B):** No adjustment required.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Dose should be reduced to a 100 mg loading dose followed by **25 mg every 12 hours**. Treat with caution and monitor response [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits protein translation by binding to the 30S ribosomal subunit, blocking the entry of amino-acyl tRNA into the A site. It is generally bacteriostatic [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Efficacy is linked to the AUC/MIC ratio.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Extensively distributed into tissues (Vss 500–700 L). Plasma protein binding ranges from 71% to 89% [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Minimally metabolised; primary material excreted is unchanged tigecycline.
    - **Excretion:** Primarily via biliary/faecal excretion (59%) and renal excretion (33%) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **cSSSI:** Two pivotal studies showed clinical cure rates of 86.5% for tigecycline vs 88.6% for vancomycin/aztreonam in the clinically evaluable population [Australian].
- **cIAI:** Two pivotal studies showed clinical cure rates of 86.1% for tigecycline vs 86.2% for imipenem/cilastatin in the microbiologically evaluable population [Australian].
- **MRSA:** Study 307 demonstrated an 81.4% cure rate for MRSA infections compared to 83.9% for vancomycin [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store below 25°C prior to reconstitution [Australian].
- **Reconstituted/Diluted Solution:** Should be used immediately. If necessary, may be stored below 25°C for up to 6 hours or refrigerated (2°C to 8°C) for up to 24 hours [Australian].
- **Preservatives:** Contains no antimicrobial preservatives; intended for single use only.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
