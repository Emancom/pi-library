# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **United States (FDA)** for **Levocarnitine Injection** (Baxter Healthcare Corporation). 
- Note: While the user instructions referenced Tadalafil as an example, the provided source documentation pertains exclusively to **Levocarnitine**.
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN), a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (trimethylammonium-substituted butyrate).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for intravenous use; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
    - A loading dose is often employed in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    - Maximum reported dose: 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - Administration: Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - Dose adjustments: Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect parenteral products for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Use only clear, colorless solutions.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known:** There are currently no listed contraindications for levocarnitine injection in the provided source material [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks/benefits before re-administration [US].
- **Seizures:** Both new-onset seizures and increased seizure frequency/severity have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity following oral or intravenous levocarnitine [US].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **ESRD and Oral Formulations:** The safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) normally excreted in urine [US].
- **Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trials Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported with a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials include:

| Body System | Adverse Event (Incidence ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, palpitations |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, dyspepsia, anorexia |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia, hyperkalemia, peripheral edema, weight change |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia, depression |
| **Other** | Pruritus, back pain, leg cramps, urinary tract infection |

### Other Reported Effects
- **Body Odor:** Transient nausea and vomiting may occur; less frequent reports of body odor and gastritis [US].
- **Post-marketing:** Seizures, anaphylaxis, and laryngeal edema [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended that INR levels be monitored upon initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not assigned a letter category in this PI]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine after exogenous administration. Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of supplementation for the mother [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for inborn errors of metabolism is calculated by weight (50 mg/kg) and is applicable to paediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** specifically indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis (see Dosage and Administration). Safety of oral formulations in severe renal impairment is not established [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein or albumin bound [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Excretion:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with IEM or ESRD, levocarnitine increases plasma carnitine concentrations and promotes the excretion of toxic acyl-CoA esters by forming acylcarnitines [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** Efficacy was demonstrated in trials involving ESRD patients where 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly significantly increased mean pre-dialysis (trough) levocarnitine concentrations from ~20 µmol/L to ranges of 189–243 µmol/L over 12–24 weeks. While concentrations increased, the specific effect on clinical outcomes in this population remains undetermined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
