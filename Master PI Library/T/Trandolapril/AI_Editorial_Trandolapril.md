# Editorial Product Information: Trandolapril

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **trandolapril** for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to local guidelines and the current official PI for legal compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Trandolapril
- **Chemical Class:** Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme (ACE) inhibitor (Ethyl ester prodrug)
- **Brand Names:** Dolapril [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** C09AA10
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Capsules: 0.5 mg, 1 mg, 2 mg, and 4 mg [Australian]
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypertension:** Treatment of high blood pressure. (Note: Data supporting use in renovascular hypertension are currently unavailable [Australian]).
- **Post-Myocardial Infarction Left Ventricular Dysfunction:** For patients following myocardial infarction (MI) with a left ventricular ejection fraction $\le$ 35% or a Wall Motion Index (WMI) $\le$ 1.2 [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Administration:** Capsules may be taken regardless of meals as food does not significantly affect absorption. Doses should be taken once daily at 24-hour intervals [Australian].
- **Hypertension:** 
    - Starting dose: 1 mg once daily.
    - Titration: May be increased to 2 mg once daily if response is inadequate. 
    - Maximum dose: 4 mg daily [Australian].
- **Post-Myocardial Infarction LV Dysfunction:**
    - Therapy may begin as early as day 3 post-MI.
    - Starting dose: 0.5 mg test dose, followed by 1 mg once daily for 3 days.
    - Titration: Increase to 2 mg once daily for 4 weeks, then to a maximum of 4 mg once daily as tolerated [Australian].

### Special Administration Instructions
- If multiple capsules are required for a single dose, they must be administered together as one daily dose.
- **Missed Doses:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to **trandolapril**, any other ACE inhibitor, or any excipients (e.g., lactose).
- History of hereditary and/or idiopathic angioedema, or angioedema associated with previous ACE inhibitor therapy.
- Pregnancy and lactation.
- **Children:** Not recommended for paediatric use [Australian].
- **Concomitant Medications:** 
    - Use with neprilysin inhibitors (e.g., sacubitril, racecadotril). **Sacubitril/valsartan** must not be initiated within 36 hours of the last **trandolapril** dose.
    - Use with aliskiren-containing products in patients with diabetes mellitus or renal impairment (GFR < 60 mL/min/1.73m²).
- **Extracorporeal Treatments:** Use of high-flux polyacrylonitrile (AN69) membranes during haemodialysis or LDL apheresis with dextran sulfate (risk of anaphylactoid reactions).
- **Liver Disease:** Liver cirrhosis with ascites [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Angioedema:** May occur at any time (face, extremities, lips, tongue, glottis, larynx). Higher incidence reported in Black patients. Immediate discontinuation is required if airway obstruction is threatened. Intestinal angioedema should be considered in patients with abdominal pain.
- **Hypotension:** Most likely in salt/volume-depleted patients, those on diuretics, or those with heart failure. A 0.5 mg test dose is recommended in high-risk patients.
- **Renal Impairment:** ACE inhibitors can cause deterioration of renal function, particularly in patients with renal artery stenosis or severe congestive heart failure.
- **Dual Blockade of RAAS:** Combined use of ACE inhibitors with ARBs or aliskiren increases the risk of hypotension, hyperkalaemia, and renal failure; this combination is generally not recommended.
- **Neutropenia/Agranulocytosis:** Risk is higher in patients with collagen vascular disease or renal impairment. Periodic monitoring of white blood cell counts is advised in these groups.
- **Hyperkalaemia:** Risk factors include renal impairment, diabetes, and use of potassium-sparing diuretics or potassium supplements.
- **Cough:** A persistent, dry, non-productive cough is common and resolves upon discontinuation.
- **Surgery/Anaesthesia:** May enhance the hypotensive effects of anaesthetic agents.
- **Excipients:** Contains lactose; not suitable for patients with galactose intolerance or glucose-galactose malabsorption [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally mild to moderate and consistent with the ACE inhibitor class.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Headache, dizziness, cough, asthenia, bronchitis, URTI, gastrointestinal pain, diarrhoea, skin rash. |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Palpitations, syncope, nausea, vomiting, gastritis, dyspepsia, pruritus, muscle cramps, depression, insomnia, libido decreased, somnolence, dyspnoea, impotence. |
| **Rare** (< 0.1%) | Angioedema, myocardial infarction, TIA, cerebral haemorrhage, cholestatic jaundice, hepatitis, pancreatitis, ileus, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, alopecia, psoriasis-like reactions. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Angioedema, anaphylactoid reactions, acute renal failure, neutropenia, and agranulocytosis.
- **Post-MI (TRACE Study):** Specific common events included cough (35%), dizziness (23%), and hypotension (11%) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Neprilysin Inhibitors (Sacubitril, Racecadotril):** Contraindicated due to increased risk of angioedema.
- **Diuretics:** Additive hypotensive effect. Discontinue or reduce dose before starting **trandolapril**.
- **Potassium-Sparing Agents/Supplements:** Increased risk of hyperkalaemia. Includes spironolactone, triamterene, amiloride, and heparin.
- **Lithium:** Increased serum lithium levels and toxicity. Monitoring required.
- **NSAIDs (including Aspirin > 3g/day):** May reduce antihypertensive effect and increase risk of renal impairment.
- **Antidiabetics (Insulin, Sulfonylureas):** May increase the blood glucose-lowering effect (hypoglycaemia risk).
- **Vildagliptin/mTOR Inhibitors (e.g., Sirolimus):** Increased risk of angioedema.
- **Gold (Sodium Aurothiomalate):** Rare nitritoid reactions (flushing, nausea, hypotension).
- **Antacids:** May decrease bioavailability; should be taken separately [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D [Australian]**. Contraindicated. Exposure during the first trimester is associated with a higher risk of congenital malformations. Exposure during the second and third trimesters may cause foetal hypotension, renal failure, skull hypoplasia, oligohydramnios, and death.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. **Trandolapril** is excreted in rat milk; human data are unavailable. Alternative treatments with better safety profiles are preferred while nursing [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no effect on fertility at doses up to 100 mg/kg/day [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established; use is contraindicated [Australian].
- **Elderly:** No dose adjustment needed if renal and hepatic functions are normal. Start at 0.5 mg if impairment or sodium deficiency is present.
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - CrCl 10–50 mL/min: Start at 0.5 mg; maximum dose 1 mg daily.
    - CrCl < 10 mL/min: Start and maintain at 0.5 mg daily.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - Start at 0.5 mg daily. 
    - In alcoholic cirrhosis, plasma concentrations of the active metabolite (trandolaprilat) are doubled [Australian].
- **Cardiac Failure:** Start at 0.5 mg daily under close medical supervision [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Trandolapril** is a prodrug of **trandolaprilat**, which inhibits ACE. This results in decreased production of angiotensin II, reduced aldosterone secretion, and increased plasma renin activity. It also inhibits the degradation of bradykinin.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Highly lipophilic, allowing high tissue penetration. Antihypertensive activity begins within 1 hour and lasts > 24 hours. The trough/peak ratio is near 100% at steady state [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; peak plasma levels of **trandolapril** at 30 minutes.
    - **Metabolism:** Hydrolysed in the liver to the active metabolite, trandolaprilat (peak concentration at 4–6 hours).
    - **Distribution:** Protein binding is 80% (parent) and 94% (metabolite).
    - **Excretion:** Effective half-life of trandolaprilat is 22 hours. 33% excreted in urine, 66% in faeces [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **TRACE Study (Trandolapril Cardiac Evaluation):** A multicentre, double-blind, placebo-controlled study in 1,749 patients with LV dysfunction post-MI (WMI $\le$ 1.2).
    - **Outcome:** **Trandolapril** significantly reduced all-cause mortality (34.7% vs 42.3% for placebo; RR 0.78).
    - **Benefits:** Included reduction in cardiovascular mortality, sudden death, and progression to severe heart failure [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to expiry date on the packaging.
