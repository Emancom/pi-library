# Editorial Product Information: Trimipramine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA). 
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised clinical overview of trimipramine, synthesized from the provided official Product Information (PI) for use by healthcare professionals.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official PI. Clinicians should consult the current local regulatory documentation for prescribing requirements in their specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Trimipramine maleate (a tricyclic dibenzazepine derivative).
- **Brand Names:** Trimipramine Capsules, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - Oral Capsules: 25 mg, 50 mg, and 100 mg (as trimipramine base) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Breckenridge Pharmaceutical, Inc. (Distributor); Rivopharm SA (Manufacturer) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Depression:** Relief of symptoms of depression. It is noted that endogenous depression is more likely to be responsive than other depressive states [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing
- **Outpatients:** Initiation at 75 mg/day in divided doses, titrated up to 150 mg/day. Dosages exceeding 200 mg/day are not recommended [US].
- **Hospitalized Patients:** Initiation at 100 mg/day in divided doses. This may be increased to 200 mg/day over several days. If no improvement occurs within 2–3 weeks, a maximum dose of 250 mg to 300 mg/day may be considered [US].
- **Maintenance:** Following remission, therapy should continue at the lowest effective dose (typically 50–150 mg/day) for approximately three months to minimize relapse [US].

### Adolescent and Geriatric Dosing
- **Initiation:** 50 mg/day with gradual increments up to 100 mg/day based on tolerance and clinical response [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Timing:** For maintenance or improved compliance, the total daily dose may be administered at bedtime [US].
- **Therapeutic Lag:** Clinical response usually requires 10 days to 4 weeks of treatment. Dose increases during this period do not accelerate response but may increase adverse effects [US].
- **Switching from/to MAOIs:** A minimum 14-day washout period is required when switching between trimipramine and Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Concomitant use or use within 14 days of discontinuing an MAOI is contraindicated due to the risk of serotonin syndrome. This includes linezolid and intravenous methylene blue [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to trimipramine or other tricyclic antidepressants (potential cross-sensitivity with other dibenzazepines) [US].
- **Myocardial Infarction:** Use is contraindicated during the acute recovery period following a myocardial infarction [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning (Suicidality):** Antidepressants increase the risk of suicidal thinking and behavior in children, adolescents, and young adults (up to age 24). Close monitoring for clinical worsening or unusual behavioral changes is mandatory, particularly during the first few months of therapy or at dose adjustments [US].
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** A potentially life-threatening condition may occur, especially with concomitant serotonergic agents (e.g., SSRIs, SNRIs, triptans, fentanyl, lithium) [US].
- **Cardiovascular Risk:** Extreme caution is required in patients with cardiovascular disease (conduction defects, arrhythmias, tachycardia, or history of stroke/MI) [US].
- **Bipolar Disorder:** Patients should be screened for bipolar disorder prior to initiation; trimipramine is not approved for bipolar depression and may precipitate a mixed/manic episode [US].
- **Angle-Closure Glaucoma:** Pupillary dilation may trigger an angle-closure attack in patients with anatomically narrow angles without a patent iridectomy [US].
- **Seizure Threshold:** Trimipramine may lower the seizure threshold; use with caution in patients with a history of seizure disorders [US].
- **Cognitive/Motor Impairment:** May impair mental or physical abilities; patients should be cautioned against operating heavy machinery or driving [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Note: Due to pharmacological similarities among tricyclic antidepressants (TCAs), many of these effects are considered class risks even if not specifically reported for trimipramine.*

| System | Adverse Reactions |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypotension, hypertension, tachycardia, palpitations, arrhythmias, heart block, myocardial infarction, stroke. |
| **Psychiatric** | Confusional states (especially in elderly), hallucinations, disorientation, anxiety, restlessness, insomnia, nightmares, hypomania, exacerbation of psychosis. |
| **Neurological** | Numbness, paresthesias, ataxia, tremors, seizures, extrapyramidal symptoms, tinnitus, peripheral neuropathy. |
| **Anticholinergic** | Dry mouth, blurred vision, mydriasis, constipation, urinary retention, paralytic ileus (rare). |
| **Gastrointestinal**| Nausea, vomiting, anorexia, epigastric distress, diarrhea, stomatitis, peculiar taste. |
| **Hematologic** | Bone marrow depression (agranulocytosis, thrombocytopenia, eosinophilia, purpura). |
| **Endocrine** | Gynecomastia, breast enlargement, galactorrhea, changes in libido, impotence, blood sugar fluctuations. |
| **Other** | Jaundice, altered liver function, weight changes, perspiration, alopecia, parotid swelling. |

**Withdrawal Symptoms:** Abrupt cessation after prolonged therapy may cause nausea, headache, and malaise.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP2D6 Inhibitors:** Drugs that inhibit cytochrome P450 2D6 (e.g., quinidine, cimetidine, and SSRIs like fluoxetine or paroxetine) can significantly increase trimipramine plasma concentrations. Poor metabolizers of CYP2D6 also face higher toxicity risks [US].
- **Cimetidine:** Inhibits TCA elimination; dose reductions of trimipramine may be necessary during co-administration [US].
- **Alcohol:** May potentiate the CNS depressant and exaggerated effects of trimipramine [US].
- **Catecholamines/Sympathomimetics:** TCAs can potentiate the effects of epinephrine, norepinephrine, and local decongestants [US].
- **Anticholinergic Drugs:** Effects of atropine-like drugs may be pronounced when used concomitantly [US].
- **Guanethidine:** Trimipramine may block the antihypertensive effects of guanethidine or similar agents [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category C]. Animal studies (rats/rabbits) at 20 times the human dose showed evidence of embryotoxicity or major anomalies. No adequate, well-controlled studies in humans exist. Use only if potential benefits outweigh risks to the fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not specified in available sources, though TCAs are generally noted for renal excretion [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed degeneration of seminiferous tubules at high doses (60 mg/kg/day). Human studies showed no significant change in sperm morphology [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established. Trimipramine is not approved for use in pediatric patients [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Generally requires lower starting doses (50 mg/day). Increased risk of confusional states, sedation, and cardiovascular sensitivity. Substantially excreted by the kidney, necessitating caution in those with renal impairment [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution due to the risk of hepatic congestion or increased liver enzymes [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** The exact mode of action on the central nervous system (CNS) is unknown. It is an antidepressant with an anxiety-reducing sedative component. It does not act by monoamine oxidase inhibition or primary CNS stimulation [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Exhibits anticholinergic properties and potential to lower the seizure threshold.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Metabolism:** Partially metabolized by CYP2D6.
    - **Excretion:** Substantially excreted by the kidney [US].
    - **Optical Isomerism:** Prepared as a racemic mixture of levorotatory and dextrorotatory isomers [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Comparative Efficacy:** In studies of neurotic outpatients, trimipramine appeared equivalent to amitriptyline in less-depressed patients but was somewhat less effective in severely depressed cohorts. In hospitalized patients, trimipramine and imipramine demonstrated equal efficacy [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Keep containers tightly closed and dispense in a tight container [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only [US].
