# Editorial Product Information: Tapinarof

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of tapinarof for healthcare professionals, synthesizing clinical and regulatory information into a single reference.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for jurisdiction-specific legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tapinarof
- **Chemical Class:** Aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR) agonist
- **Brand Name(s):** VTAMA [US]
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Topical cream, 1% (10 mg of tapinarof per gram) [US]
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Organon LLC [US]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Plaque Psoriasis:** Topical treatment of plaque psoriasis in adults [US].
- **Atopic Dermatitis:** Topical treatment of atopic dermatitis in adults and pediatric patients aged 2 years and older [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric $\ge$ 2 years):** Apply a thin layer to affected areas once daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - For external use only.
    - Not for oral, ophthalmic, or intravaginal use [US].
    - Patients should wash hands after application unless the hands are the intended treatment area [US].
    - Avoid application to unaffected areas of the skin [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- None identified in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
- **General Precautions:**
    - **Local Site Reactions:** Contact dermatitis and folliculitis have been reported and may lead to treatment discontinuation [US].
    - **External Use Only:** Caution should be exercised to ensure the product is not applied to mucosal surfaces or ingested [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Not specified in available sources.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are categorized by indication.

### Plaque Psoriasis (Adults)
Based on 12-week clinical trials (incidence $\ge$ 1% and exceeding vehicle):
- **Very Common (>10%):** Folliculitis (20%), Nasopharyngitis (11%) [US].
- **Common (1–10%):** Contact dermatitis (7%), Headache (4%), Pruritus (3%), Influenza (2%) [US].

### Atopic Dermatitis (Adults and Pediatrics $\ge$ 2 years)
Based on 8-week clinical trials (incidence $\ge$ 1% and exceeding vehicle):
- **Very Common (>10%):** Upper respiratory tract infection (12%) [US].
- **Common (1–10%):** Folliculitis (9%), Lower respiratory tract infection (5%), Headache (4%), Asthma (2%), Vomiting (2%), Ear infection (2%), Pain in extremity (2%), Abdominal pain (1%) [US].

### Serious and Notable Adverse Effects
- **Urticaria and Drug Eruption:** Reported in long-term safety trials (1.0% and 0.7% respectively) [US].
- **Discontinuation Rates:** In psoriasis trials, discontinuation occurred due to contact dermatitis (2.9%) and folliculitis (2.8%) [US].
- **Pediatric Variations:** Pediatric subjects (2–6 years) showed higher rates of upper respiratory tract infection (16.3%) and ear infection (5.7%) compared to older cohorts [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No clinical drug-drug interaction studies have been conducted [US].
- **In Vitro Data:**
    - **CYP Enzymes:** Tapinarof is not an inhibitor of CYP2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, or 3A4/5. It is not an inducer of CYP1A2, 2B6, or 3A4 [US].
    - **Transporters:** Tapinarof is not an inhibitor of BCRP, MATE1, MATE-2K, OAT1, OAT3, OATP1B1, OATP1B3, OCT1, OCT2, or P-gp. It is not a substrate for BCRP, OATP1B1, OATP1B3, or P-gp [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned under current labeling rules]. Available human data are insufficient to evaluate for drug-associated risks of major birth defects or miscarriage. Animal studies (subcutaneous administration) in rats and rabbits showed no significant malformations at doses up to 264 times and 16 times the Maximum Recommended Human Dose (MRHD), respectively [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on the presence of tapinarof in human milk or its effects on the breastfed infant. Tapinarof was detected in the milk of lactating rats following subcutaneous administration [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats showed no impairment of female fertility at doses up to 264 times the MRHD [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - **Atopic Dermatitis:** Safety and efficacy established in patients aged 2 years and older [US].
    - **Plaque Psoriasis:** Safety and efficacy not established in pediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in efficacy, safety, or tolerability were observed between subjects aged 65 years and older and younger subjects in clinical trials [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources. Juvenile animal data showed renal pelvic dilatation in rats at high doses (165 times MRHD) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources. Tapinarof is metabolized in the liver [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Tapinarof is an aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR) agonist. The specific downstream mechanisms resulting in therapeutic action for psoriasis and atopic dermatitis are unknown [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Low systemic absorption. In psoriasis, mean $C_{max}$ decreased from Day 1 (0.90 ng/mL) to Day 29 (0.12 ng/mL) due to skin barrier improvement. Systemic exposure is higher in pediatric patients with atopic dermatitis than in adults with psoriasis [US].
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is approximately 99% in vitro [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolized in the liver via oxidation, glucuronidation, and sulfation pathways [US].
    - **Elimination:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** At recommended dosages, tapinarof does not prolong the QTc interval to a clinically relevant extent [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Plaque Psoriasis (PSOARING 1 & 2):** Two 12-week trials in 1,025 adults. Treatment success (PGA 0 or 1 and $\ge$ 2-grade improvement) was achieved by 36–40% of tapinarof patients vs. 6% for vehicle [US].
- **Atopic Dermatitis (ADORING 1 & 2):** Two 8-week trials in 811 patients (adult and pediatric $\ge$ 2 years). Treatment success (vIGA-AD 0 or 1 and $\ge$ 2-grade improvement) was achieved by 45–46% of tapinarof patients vs. 14–18% for vehicle [US].
- **Remission:** In psoriasis patients achieving clear skin (PGA 0), the median time to first worsening after drug withdrawal was 114 days [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F and 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from excessive heat. Do not freeze [US].
