# Editorial Product Information: Tafamidis

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not intended to replace the official PI for prescribing purposes in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to the current approved PI in their specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tafamidis (micronised).
- **Chemical Class:** Transthyretin (TTR) selective stabiliser.
- **Brand Name(s):** **VYNDAMAX** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N07XX08.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 61 mg soft capsules [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Transthyretin Amyloid Cardiomyopathy (ATTR-CM):** Indicated for the treatment of adult patients with wild-type or hereditary transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 61 mg orally once daily.
- **Indication-Specific Dosing:** Same as general dosing for ATTR-CM.
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Capsules should be swallowed whole; they must not be crushed or cut.
    - May be taken with or without food.
- **Interchangeability Warning:** A single 61 mg **VYNDAMAX** capsule is bioequivalent to 80 mg of tafamidis meglumine (**VYNDAQEL**) but is NOT interchangeable on a per mg basis [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Patients should take the missed dose as soon as remembered. If it is nearly time for the next dose, skip the missed dose and resume the regular schedule. Do not double the dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to the active substance or any excipients (including sorbitol and sulfites) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **BCRP Substrate Interaction:** Tafamidis inhibits the efflux transporter BCRP. Caution is required when co-administering with BCRP substrates (e.g., methotrexate, rosuvastatin) due to the risk of increased substrate exposure and related adverse reactions.
- **Sorbitol Content:** Each capsule contains up to 44 mg of sorbitol. Consider the additive effect of other sorbitol-containing products and potential impacts on the bioavailability of concomitant oral medicines.
- **Liver Function:** Increases in liver function tests have been observed; monitoring is recommended.
- **Laboratory Test Interference:** Tafamidis may decrease serum total thyroxine (T4) concentrations by reducing thyroxine binding to TTR. This occurs without changes in free T4 or thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) and is not typically associated with clinical thyroid dysfunction.
- **Organ Transplant Patients:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in this population.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **General Profile:** In clinical trials, the frequency of adverse events in patients treated with tafamidis was generally similar to placebo.
- **Common (1–10% and > placebo):**
    - Asthenia (11.0%)
    - Balance disorder (6.4%)
    - Sinusitis (5.7%)
    - Cataract (4.5%)
    - Flatulence (3.8%)
    - Myalgia (3.8%)
    - Skin ulcer (3.8%)
- **Post-marketing Experience:** 
    - **Common:** Diarrhoea.
- **Laboratory Abnormalities:** Higher incidence of decreased thyroxine (<0.8 x LLN) and low neutrophil counts compared to placebo.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **BCRP Substrates:** Tafamidis may double the exposure (AUC) of BCRP substrates such as rosuvastatin. Dose adjustments for these substrates may be necessary.
- **UGT1A1:** Tafamidis may inhibit intestinal UGT1A1 activities, though systemic interactions at clinical concentrations are unlikely.
- **OAT1/OAT3:** Potential for inhibition exists, but clinical risk assessments suggest significant interactions with substrates (e.g., NSAIDs, furosemide, methotrexate) are unlikely at the 61 mg dose.
- **CYP Enzymes:** No significant induction or inhibition of CYP3A4 has been observed clinically. In vitro data suggests low potential for interactions via other major CYP enzymes (1A2, 2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category D:** There are no adequate data in pregnant women. Animal studies have shown developmental toxicity (skeletal malformations, reduced fetal weight). Not recommended during pregnancy.
    - **Contraception:** Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during treatment and for one month after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. Nonclinical data shows tafamidis is secreted in rat milk; risk to nursing infants cannot be excluded.
- **Fertility:** No effects on fertility or reproductive performance were observed in rat studies at doses up to 30 mg/kg/day.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established in patients under 18 years of age.
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment is required for patients ≥65 years. The majority of clinical trial participants (90.5%) were in this age group.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required. Data are limited for patients with severe impairment (CrCl ≤30 mL/min).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required for mild to moderate impairment (Child-Pugh 7-9). Tafamidis has not been studied in patients with severe hepatic impairment; caution is advised.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Tafamidis selectively binds to the two thyroxine-binding sites on the native TTR tetramer with negative cooperativity. This stabilises the tetramer and prevents its dissociation into monomers, which is the rate-limiting step in the amyloidogenic process.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** TTR stabilisation is dose-dependent. Stabilisation has been demonstrated for wild-type TTR and multiple variants.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Median $t_{max}$ is within 4 hours (fasted). Food does not affect the extent of absorption.
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein bound (>99%). Volume of distribution is ~18.5 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised via glucuronidation.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 59% recovered in faeces (mostly unchanged) and 22% in urine (mostly as glucuronide). Mean half-life is approximately 49 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Study:** A multicentre, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (n=441) evaluated tafamidis meglumine (20 mg or 80 mg) versus placebo over 30 months. 
- **Primary Outcome:** Tafamidis demonstrated a significant reduction in the hierarchical combination of all-cause mortality and frequency of cardiovascular-related hospitalisations (p=0.0006).
- **Survival:** A 30.2% reduction in the risk of death was observed relative to placebo (Hazard Ratio 0.698).
- **Functional Capacity:** Significant improvements in 6-Minute Walk Test (6MWT) distance and KCCQ-OS scores were observed beginning at month 6.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging.
- **Special Handling:** Do not use if the capsule is damaged.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
