# Editorial Product Information: Tebentafusp

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information from Australia (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is designed to aggregate clinical and regulatory data into a single, structured reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tebentafusp (a bispecific gp100-targeted T cell receptor fusion protein).
- **Chemical Class:** Bispecific T-cell engager; bispecific fusion protein.
- **Brand Name:** **KIMMTRAK** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01XX75.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Concentrated solution for infusion; each single-dose vial contains 100 micrograms (0.1 mg) of tebentafusp in 0.5 mL.
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Medison Pharma Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Uveal Melanoma:** Indicated for the treatment of HLA-A*02:01-positive adult patients with unresectable or metastatic uveal melanoma [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
Treatment follows a weekly intra-patient dose escalation schedule:
- **Day 1:** 20 micrograms
- **Day 8:** 30 micrograms
- **Day 15:** 68 micrograms
- **Subsequent weeks:** 68 micrograms once weekly.

### Administration Instructions
- **Preparation:** Requires a specific two-step dilution process. 
    - **Step 1:** Prepare the 100 mL 0.9% sodium chloride bag by adding human albumin to a final concentration of 250 mcg/mL to prevent adsorption of the drug to the bag and infusion set.
    - **Step 2:** Add the calculated volume of **KIMMTRAK** to the prepared bag.
- **Infusion:** Administer over 15–20 minutes through a dedicated line with a 0.2 micron in-line filter.
- **Setting:** The first three doses must be administered in a healthcare setting capable of managing Cytokine Release Syndrome (CRS), with monitoring for at least 16 hours post-infusion.
- **Maintenance Setting:** If the patient is stable (no Grade $\ge$ 2 hypotension) after the third dose, subsequent doses may be given in an ambulatory setting with a minimum 30-minute observation period.

### Titration and Modifications
- Dose reductions are not recommended; however, doses should be withheld for Grade 3 or 4 adverse reactions (e.g., CRS, skin reactions, or elevated liver enzymes) until symptoms resolve to $\le$ Grade 1.
- **KIMMTRAK** must be permanently discontinued for Grade 4 CRS, Grade 4 skin reactions, or any other Grade 4 adverse event.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to tebentafusp or any of the excipients (citric acid monohydrate, dibasic sodium phosphate, mannitol, trehalose dihydrate, polysorbate 20) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Cytokine Release Syndrome (CRS):** [Australian] CRS can be serious or life-threatening. Manifestations include fever, hypotension, hypoxia, and organ dysfunction (hepatic, renal, cardiac, etc.). Ensure patients are euvolemic before infusion. 
- **Skin Reactions:** Very common ($\ge$ 90%), including rash, pruritus, and cutaneous oedema. Median onset is 1 day. Treat with antihistamines and topical or systemic steroids.
- **Elevated Hepatic Enzymes:** Frequent elevations in ALT/AST and bilirubin. Monitor liver function tests (LFTs) before and during treatment. Most elevations occur within the first three doses.
- **Traceability:** The name and batch number of the biological product must be clearly recorded to improve traceability.
- **Monitoring:** Vital signs and fluid status must be monitored closely, particularly during the initial escalation phase.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Serious adverse events occurred in 28% of patients. 
- **Fatal Event:** One patient (0.4%) experienced a fatal pulmonary embolism.
- **Laboratory Abnormalities:** Very common ($\ge$ 50%) abnormalities include decreased lymphocyte count, increased creatinine, increased AST/ALT, and increased glucose.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP450 Substrates:** No formal drug interaction studies have been conducted. However, the transient release of cytokines may suppress CYP450 enzymes, particularly during the first three doses.
- **Management:** Exercise caution and monitor for toxicity in patients taking concomitant CYP450 substrates with a narrow therapeutic index (e.g., warfarin, cyclosporine).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian: Category C]. Based on its mechanism, tebentafusp may cause foetal harm. No animal reproduction studies have been conducted. 
- **Contraception:** Females of childbearing potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for at least 1 week after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if tebentafusp is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for at least 1 week after the last dose.
- **Fertility:** No human or animal data are available regarding the effects on fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients under 18 years have not been established.
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment is required for patients $\ge$ 65 years. No meaningful differences in safety or efficacy were observed between older and younger adults.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild to moderate impairment. Not studied in severe renal impairment (CLcr < 30 mL/min).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild impairment. Not studied in moderate to severe hepatic impairment.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Tebentafusp is a bispecific fusion protein. One end is a T cell receptor (TCR) that binds to a gp100 peptide presented by HLA-A*02:01 on uveal melanoma cells. The other end is an anti-CD3 antibody fragment that binds and activates polyclonal T cells, leading to the release of inflammatory cytokines and direct lysis of tumour cells.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Steady-state volume of distribution is 7.56 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Expected to be catabolised into small peptides and amino acids.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 7.5 hours. Clearance is 16.4 L/d.
- **Immunogenicity:** Anti-drug antibodies (ADA) were detected in 29-33% of patients. High titre ADAs can increase clearance and significantly decrease drug exposure.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study IMCgp100-202:** A randomised, open-label Phase 3 trial (n=378) comparing **KIMMTRAK** to investigator’s choice (pembrolizumab, ipilimumab, or dacarbazine) in previously untreated metastatic uveal melanoma.
- **Primary Outcome:** Overall Survival (OS).
- **Results:** Median OS was 21.7 months for **KIMMTRAK** vs. 16.0 months for the control group (Hazard Ratio 0.51; p < 0.0001). Progression-Free Survival (PFS) was also significantly improved (3.3 vs. 2.9 months).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vial Storage:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze. Keep the vial in the outer carton to protect from light.
- **Diluted Solution:** If not used immediately, the diluted solution is stable for up to 24 hours at 2°C to 8°C. Total time from preparation to completion of infusion must not exceed 4 hours if kept at room temperature (below 30°C).
