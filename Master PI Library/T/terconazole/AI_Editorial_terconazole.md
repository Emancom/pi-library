# Editorial Product Information: Terconazole

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority:
    - **US (FDA)**: Cosette Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (Revised 02/2021)
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising available regulatory data into a clinically focused reference.
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing. At the time of this writing, only United States regulatory documentation was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Terconazole (a triazole derivative).
- **Chemical Class**: Azole antifungal.
- **Brand Names**: Terconazole Vaginal Suppositories [US].
- **ATC Code**: G01AG02 (Inferred based on class; not specified in source).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**: 
    - Vaginal Suppository: 80 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration**: Intravaginal.
- **Sponsor/Manufacturer**: Cosette Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Vulvovaginal Candidiasis**: Indicated for the local treatment of moniliasis caused by the genus *Candida* [US].
- **Diagnostic Requirement**: Clinical efficacy is limited to *Candida* species; diagnosis should be confirmed via KOH smears and/or fungal cultures prior to treatment [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult)**: One 80 mg suppository administered once daily at bedtime for three consecutive days [US].
- **Administration Instructions**: 
    - Suppositories are for intravaginal use only.
    - May be inserted with or without the provided applicator. 
    - The therapeutic effect is not compromised by menstruation [US].
- **Re-treatment**: If symptoms persist or recur, the diagnosis must be reconfirmed via microbiology to rule out other pathogens before a second course is initiated [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity**: Known hypersensitivity to terconazole or any excipients in the suppository formulation (e.g., triglycerides derived from coconut/palm kernel oil, butylated hydroxyanisole) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Severe Skin/Immune Reactions**: Rare cases of anaphylaxis and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN) have been reported. Therapy must be permanently discontinued if these reactions occur [US].
- **Latex/Rubber Interaction**: The suppository base may interact with and weaken certain rubber or latex products, including vaginal contraceptive diaphragms and condoms. Concurrent use is not recommended [US].
- **General Sensitization**: Treatment should be discontinued if local irritation, fever, chills, or flu-like symptoms develop [US].
- **Administration Site**: For vulvovaginal use only; not for ophthalmic or oral administration [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions reported in controlled US clinical trials (n=284) compared to placebo (n=295):

| Frequency | System Organ Class | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Nervous System | Headache (30.3% vs 20.7% placebo) |
| | General/Site | Vaginal burning (15.2% vs 11.2% placebo) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Reproductive | Genital pain (4.2%), Pruritus (1.8%) |
| | General | Body pain (3.9%), Fever (2.8%), Chills (1.8%) |

- **Discontinuation**: The most frequent reasons for treatment cessation were vaginal burning (2.5%) and pruritus (1.8%) [US].
- **Post-marketing Experience**: The following have been reported voluntarily:
    - **General**: Asthenia, influenza-like illness, malaise, nausea, vomiting, myalgia, arthralgia.
    - **Immune**: Anaphylaxis, face edema, urticaria.
    - **Nervous/Respiratory**: Dizziness, bronchospasm.
    - **Integumentary**: Rash, toxic epidermal necrolysis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Oral Contraceptives**: The therapeutic efficacy of terconazole is not affected by the concurrent use of oral contraceptives [US].
- **Latex Products**: Physical interaction with diaphragms and condoms may occur; refer to Section 5 [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **US Category C**: There is no evidence of teratogenicity in animal studies at high oral/subcutaneous doses. However, embryotoxicity (reduced litter size, delayed ossification) was observed in rats and rabbits at doses 20–40 mg/kg [US].
    - **Clinical Recommendation**: Because terconazole is absorbed systemically from the vagina, it should be avoided during the **first trimester** unless the physician deems it essential for the patient's welfare. Use in the second and third trimesters is permitted if the benefits outweigh fetal risks [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: It is unknown if terconazole is excreted in human milk. Animal studies showed decreased survival in offspring exposed via milk at high maternal doses (40 mg/kg). Caution should be exercised, and a decision made between discontinuing nursing or the drug, considering the mother's clinical need [US].
- **Fertility**: No impairment of fertility was observed in female rats at oral doses up to 40 mg/kg/day [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and effectiveness in children have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use**: Clinical trials did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65 and over to determine if they respond differently than younger subjects [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment**: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: An azole antifungal that inhibits fungal cytochrome P-450-mediated 14 alpha-lanosterol demethylase. This prevents the conversion of lanosterol to ergosterol, leading to the accumulation of 14 alpha-methyl sterols and subsequent disruption of the fungal cell wall [US].
- **Microbiology**: Active against *Candida albicans* and other *Candida* species. It does not affect *Lactobacillus* spp. typically found in the human vagina (MIC ≥128 mcg/mL) [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Approximately 10% of an 80 mg intravaginal dose is systemically absorbed. Peak plasma concentrations occur 5–10 hours post-application [US].
    - **Distribution**: Highly protein-bound (94.9%); binding is independent of concentration [US].
    - **Metabolism**: Systemically absorbed drug is extensively metabolised (>95%) [US].
    - **Elimination**: Mean elimination half-life ranges from 6.4 to 8.5 hours. Excreted via urine (3–10%) and feces (2–6%) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **US Controlled Studies**: Trials involving 284 patients using the 80 mg suppository for 3 days demonstrated efficacy against vulvovaginitis caused by *Candida*. Primary adverse events noted were headache and local irritation (see Section 6) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Shelf Life**: Not specified in available sources; refer to the expiration date on the packaging.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States**: Rx-only. Poison schedule/classification not applicable under US FDA system for this product.
