# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA). 
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. 
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of preparation, only the United States (FDA) source document was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP); chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
*   **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
*   **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
*   **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    *   **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV).
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
*   **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It should be administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
*   **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). Administration should be via slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].

### Administration Instructions
*   Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use [US].
*   **Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often given in severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].

### Monitoring and Titration
*   **Metabolic Disorders:** Subsequent daily doses should be adjusted based on clinical response, typically in the range of 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].
*   **ESRD:** Initiation of therapy may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations <40–50 µmol/L. Dose adjustments (e.g., downward to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough levels [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
*   None specified in available sources.

### General Warnings and Precautions
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, the drug should be discontinued immediately [US].
*   **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
*   **Plasma Carnitine Levels:** Monitoring of plasma free carnitine (target range: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended weekly or monthly, alongside blood chemistries and vital signs [US].
*   **Renal Function:** The safety/efficacy of oral levocarnitine has not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events observed in chronic hemodialysis trials (frequency ≥5% regardless of causality) [US]:

| Body System | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, palpitations |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, dyspepsia, anorexia |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis |
| **Neurological** | Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia |
| **Other** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia, pruritus, eye disorder |

*   **Transient reactions:** Nausea and vomiting are common. 
*   **Less frequent:** Body odor and gastritis [US].
*   **Post-marketing:** Reports of drug dependence and vertigo [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating treatment or following dose adjustments [US].
*   **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** None specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned a letter, but source states use only if clearly needed.
    *   Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. There are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Not specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations following exogenous administration. The benefits of maternal supplementation must be weighed against potential risks to the child; discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].
*   **Fertility:** Animal studies revealed no evidence of impaired fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** See dosage and administration for metabolic disorders (weight-based dosing) [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically in ESRD patients on dialysis. High-dose oral levocarnitine is cautioned in patients with severely compromised renal function due to metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO) [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
*   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
*   **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
*   **Bioavailability:** Absolute bioavailability of oral formulations is approximately 15–16% [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Bioequivalence:** Studies in healthy volunteers confirmed bioequivalence between levocarnitine tablets and oral solution [US].
*   **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly after dialysis showed significant increases in plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Trough levels rose from a baseline of 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
*   **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses in ESRD patients demonstrated a dose-dependent increase in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
*   **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservative [US].
*   **Stability:** When mixed in Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL), the solution is stable for 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
