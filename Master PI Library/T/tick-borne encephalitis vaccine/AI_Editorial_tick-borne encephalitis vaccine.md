# Editorial Product Information: Tick-Borne Encephalitis Vaccine (TICOVAC)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (Food and Drug Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **TICOVAC** for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory data into a single, clinically focused reference. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved labelling for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tick-borne encephalitis (TBE) virus (Neudoerfl strain), inactivated.
- **Brand Name:** **TICOVAC** [US].
- **ATC Code:** J07BA01 (Inactivated, whole virus).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):**
    - **Pediatric Presentation:** 0.25 mL suspension in a single-dose pre-filled syringe (contains 1.2 µg of inactivated TBE virus) [US].
    - **Adult Presentation:** 0.5 mL suspension in a single-dose pre-filled syringe (contains 2.4 µg of inactivated TBE virus) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intramuscular injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Inc. (Manufactured by Pfizer Ireland Pharmaceuticals) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Active Immunization against TBE:** Indicated for the prevention of tick-borne encephalitis in individuals 1 year of age and older [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
The vaccine is administered as a primary three-dose series followed by a booster if indicated. Dosage volume is determined by age.

### Primary Vaccination Schedule
| Dose | Pediatric (1 through 15 years) | Adult (16 years and older) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Volume** | **0.25 mL** | **0.5 mL** |
| **First** | Day 0 | Day 0 |
| **Second** | 1 to 3 months after first dose | 14 days to 3 months after first dose |
| **Third** | 5 to 12 months after second dose | 5 to 12 months after second dose |

### Booster Dosing
- A fourth dose (booster) may be administered at least 3 years after the completion of the primary series if continued exposure or re-exposure to the TBE virus is expected [US].

### Administration Instructions
- The primary series should be completed at least one week prior to potential TBE virus exposure.
- Bring the syringe to room temperature before use.
- Shake well until a homogenous, off-white, opalescent suspension is formed.
- Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration; discard if the suspension remains discolored or contains particles after shaking.
- Administer via intramuscular injection only.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Severe Allergic Reaction:** History of anaphylaxis or other severe allergic reaction to any component of the vaccine [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Acute Allergic Reactions:** Facilities for the management of anaphylaxis must be immediately available during and after administration.
- **Altered Immunocompetence:** Individuals with impaired immune responsiveness (due to immunosuppressive therapy or genetic defect) may have a diminished immune response to the vaccine [US].
- **Human Albumin Content:** This product contains human albumin. While screened and processed to minimize risk, there is a remote theoretical risk for the transmission of viral diseases or Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease (CJD) [US].
- **Vaccine Effectiveness:** Vaccination may not result in protection for all recipients.
- **Latex:** The tip cap and rubber plunger of the pre-filled syringes are not made with natural rubber latex [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency by Population
**Pediatric (1 through 15 years):**
- **Very Common (>10%):** Local tenderness (18.1%), local pain (11.2%), headache (11.1%).
- **Common (1–10%):** Fever (9.6%), restlessness (9.1% in ages 1-5), fatigue (6.3% in ages 6-15).
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Lymphadenopathy (axillary/inguinal).

**Adult (16 through 65 years):**
- **Very Common (>10%):** Local tenderness (29.9%), local pain (13.2%).
- **Common (1–10%):** Fatigue (6.6%), headache (6.3%), muscle pain (5.1%), malaise (4.9%).

### Post-marketing Experience (European Union reports)
The following have been reported spontaneously but frequencies are not established:
- **Nervous System:** Convulsions (including febrile), demyelinating disorders (Guillain-Barré syndrome, transverse myelitis), encephalitis, facial paresis, aseptic meningitis, vertigo.
- **Immune System:** Anaphylactic reactions, aggravation of autoimmune disorders (e.g., multiple sclerosis).
- **Cardiac/Respiratory:** Tachycardia, dyspnea.
- **Skin:** Urticaria, dermatitis, hyperhidrosis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Immunosuppressive Therapies:** May reduce the immune response to **TICOVAC** [US].
- **Other Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Data are insufficient to establish vaccine-associated risks in pregnant women. Developmental and reproductive toxicity studies in animals have not been conducted [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data regarding the presence of the vaccine in human milk or its effects on milk production or the breastfed infant. The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need for the vaccine and the risk of TBE [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in infants under 1 year of age [US].
- **Geriatric:** Clinical trials did not include a sufficient number of subjects aged 65 and older to determine if they respond differently than younger subjects [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **TICOVAC** induces TBE virus-neutralising antibodies. A specific protective antibody threshold has not been formally defined [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics/Pharmacodynamics:** As a vaccine, traditional PK studies (absorption, distribution, metabolism, elimination) are not typically conducted.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Immunogenicity:** In clinical study 209 (children 1-15 years), the neutralization test (NT) seropositivity rate was 99.5% 21 days after the third dose. In studies of adults 16-64 years, NT seropositivity rates ranged from 98.8% to 100% [US].
- **Field Effectiveness:** Retrospective studies in Austria (2000–2011) estimated vaccine effectiveness for preventing hospitalized TBE at 96.3% to 98.7% following at least three doses [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F).
- **Light:** Keep the syringe in the outer carton to protect from light.
- **Prohibitions:** **Do not freeze.** Discard if the vaccine has been frozen [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only. Supplied in packages of 1 or 10 pre-filled syringes.
