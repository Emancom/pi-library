# Editorial Product Information: tofersen

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): tofersen
- Chemical class: antisense oligonucleotide (targeted to SOD1 mRNA)
- ATC code: N07XX22
- Route(s) of administration: intrathecal (lumbar puncture)

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources.
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources.
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of adults with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) associated with a mutation in the superoxide dismutase 1 (SOD1) gene. [Australian: approval described as provisional; continued approval depends on additional data]
- Other jurisdictions: Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General dosing (adult)
- Recommended dose: 100 mg per treatment (administered as 100 mg/15 mL; 6.7 mg/mL).
- Loading regimen: three loading doses administered at 14-day intervals.
- Maintenance regimen: thereafter, a dose every 28 days.

### Dosing by indication
- Indication-specific dosing: The above regimen applies to SOD1-associated ALS as described. No other indications or alternative dosing regimens are specified in available sources.

### Missed or delayed dose instructions
- If the second loading dose is delayed or missed: administer tofersen as soon as possible and give the third loading dose 14 days after that administration.
- If the third loading dose is delayed or missed: administer tofersen as soon as possible; the first maintenance dose should then be administered 28 days later.
- If a maintenance dose is delayed or missed: administer tofersen as soon as possible; subsequent maintenance doses should be given every 28 days from the date of the last dose.

### Administration instructions
- Intrathecal administration by lumbar puncture performed by, or under the direction of, healthcare professionals experienced in lumbar puncture techniques.
- Procedural considerations:
  - Aseptic technique must be used for preparation and administration.
  - Consider sedation or image-guided lumbar puncture based on clinical condition.
  - Evaluate patients before and after injection for conditions related to lumbar puncture to minimise procedural complications.
- Preparation and handling (clinical steps):
  - Allow the product to reach room temperature prior to administration; do not apply external heat sources.
  - Do not shake the product; visually inspect for particulate matter and discoloration; administer only if solution is clear and colourless to slightly yellow.
  - Withdraw the required 15 mL dose using appropriate sterile technique and an appropriate needle inserted through the overseal/closure into the container.
  - Do not dilute the product. External in-line filters are not required.
  - It is recommended to remove approximately 10 mL of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) prior to administration.
  - Administer as an intrathecal bolus through the lumbar puncture needle over 1 to 3 minutes.
  - The product contains no preservatives; once drawn into the syringe, administer immediately (the manufacturer’s allowable time from removal from refrigerated storage to administration was specified in source documents — adhere to local institutional policies).
  - Single patient use only; discard any residue.

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to tofersen or to any of the excipients listed in the full product composition (specific excipients: Not specified in available sources).
- Other contraindications: Not specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed/warning highlights
- Not specified in available sources as a formal boxed warning; however, serious neurological inflammatory events have been reported (see below) and require prompt evaluation and management.

### General warnings and precautions
- Lumbar puncture–related risks: headache, back pain, post–lumbar puncture syndrome, infection and other known complications of lumbar puncture have been observed in patients receiving intrathecal tofersen.
- Inflammatory neurological events:
  - Cases of myelitis (including transverse myelitis and neurosarcoidosis) and radiculitis (including radiculopathy and lumbar radiculopathy) have been reported. If symptoms suggesting these conditions occur, initiate diagnostic workup and treat according to standard of care.
  - Serious events of increased intracranial pressure and papilloedema have been reported; investigate and manage per standard clinical practice if suspected.
  - Aseptic or chemical meningitis has been reported; monitor for signs/symptoms and manage appropriately.
- Hematologic and coagulation:
  - Thrombocytopenia and coagulation abnormalities (including acute severe thrombocytopenia) have been observed with some systemically administered antisense oligonucleotides. Prior to administration of tofersen, platelet count and coagulation tests are recommended if clinically indicated.
- Renal:
  - Renal toxicity has been observed with some systemically administered antisense oligonucleotides. If clinically indicated, perform urine protein testing (preferably first morning specimen). Persistent elevated urinary protein should prompt further evaluation.
- Visual disturbances:
  - Visual disturbance may occur; patients who develop visual problems should be cautioned regarding tasks such as driving or operating machinery.
- Procedural precautions:
  - Consider sedation or image guidance when clinically indicated.
  - Evaluate patient readiness prior to opening the product container/overseal.
  - Post-injection monitoring: standard post–lumbar puncture care is recommended; no additional routine monitoring beyond standard care specified.

### Monitoring requirements
- Prior to administration:
  - Consider platelet and coagulation testing if clinically indicated.
  - Consider urine protein testing if clinically indicated.
- After administration:
  - Monitor for signs of meningitis, myelitis, radiculitis, increased intracranial pressure, and other neurological symptoms; perform diagnostic workup as indicated.
  - Routine post–lumbar puncture observation per institutional protocol.

### Overdose
- No cases of overdose with tofersen were reported in clinical studies.
- For management of overdose, contact local poison information services or appropriate emergency resources.

## 7. Adverse Effects

- Summary from clinical experience:
  - Safety data derive from subjects with SOD1‑ALS enrolled in clinical trials (combined datasets included up to 147 tofersen‑treated subjects with median exposure ~200 weeks). - Additional safety notes:
  - Serious events of myelitis or radiculitis were observed in clinical studies (eight tofersen‑treated subjects); two discontinued treatment and both events resolved; the other events did not lead to treatment discontinuation in the reported cases.
  - Serious events of elevated intracranial pressure and/or papilloedema were reported; these were manageable with standard care and did not necessitate discontinuation in reported cases.
  - Serious aseptic/chemical meningitis events have been reported; one led to treatment discontinuation in study reports.
  - Thrombocytopenia and coagulation abnormalities and renal toxicity have been observed with other antisense oligonucleotides administered systemically; these are potential concerns and warrant appropriate baseline and follow-up testing if clinically indicated.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Pharmacokinetic interactions:
  - No formal interaction studies with tofersen have been performed.
  - In vitro data indicate a low likelihood of transporter‑mediated interactions: tofersen is not a substrate of BCRP, MDR1 (P‑glycoprotein), MATE1, MATE2‑K, OAT1, OAT3, OATP1B1, OATP1B3, OCT1, OCT2; and it is not an inhibitor of MATE1, MATE2‑K, OAT1, OAT3, OATP1B1, OATP1B3, OCT1, OCT2, BCRP, BSEP, or MDR1.
  - Tofersen is not an inducer or inhibitor of CYP450 enzymes in vitro and therefore is not expected to affect medications primarily metabolised via CYP450 pathways.
- Pharmacodynamic interactions:
  - Co‑administration of other intrathecal medicinal products with tofersen has not been evaluated; the safety of such combinations is unknown.
- Clinically significant interactions and management:
  - No specific clinically significant drug interactions are detailed in available sources. Exercise caution when considering concomitant intrathecal therapies due to lack of data.
  - [If platelet or coagulation‑affecting drugs are co‑administered, consider baseline and periodic coagulation and platelet monitoring if clinically indicated—this recommendation derives from observed risks with related oligonucleotides and the product warnings.]

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

### Pregnancy
- Australian pregnancy classification: Category B2.
- Data in humans: No clinical data on use in pregnant women are available.
- Animal data:
  - Developmental toxicity studies in mice and rabbits using subcutaneous administration did not identify embryo‑foetal developmental effects at the highest doses tested. However, the oligonucleotide is not pharmacologically active in mice and rabbits due to sequence mismatches, limiting translational relevance.
  - Reproductive toxicology in mice: at high doses (30 mg/kg every 2 days SC; ~3.7× clinical AUC) male mice had minimal to mild testicular findings (seminiferous tubular changes and hypospermia), but no adverse effects on mating, fertility or sperm parameters were observed. Non‑human primate (NHP) studies (13‑ and 39‑week toxicology) showed no treatment‑related changes in reproductive tissues.
- Recommendations:
  - Tofersen is not recommended during pregnancy and in women of childbearing potential who are not using effective contraception.
- Other jurisdictions: Not specified in available sources.

### Breastfeeding
- Human data: No data available on excretion of tofersen in human milk; potential risk to newborns/infants cannot be excluded.
- Animal data: Tofersen was detected in mouse milk in animal studies; no maternal or offspring adverse effects were observed at doses up to those tested.
- Recommendation: A decision should be made whether to discontinue breastfeeding or discontinue/abstain from tofersen therapy, taking into account the benefits of breastfeeding and the benefits of therapy.

### Fertility
- Animal data:
  - High‑dose male mice showed histologic testicular changes at exposures higher than clinical AUC, but no effect on mating or fertility was observed. Female mice showed no effects on mating or fertility at highest tested doses.
  - NHP reproductive tissues showed no treatment‑related changes in toxicology studies.
- Human fertility effects: Not specified in available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology

### Mechanism of action
- **Tofersen** is an antisense oligonucleotide complementary to a portion of the 3′ untranslated region (3′UTR) of human SOD1 mRNA. Hybridisation of tofersen to SOD1 mRNA elicits RNase H‑mediated degradation of the target mRNA, resulting in reduced synthesis of SOD1 protein. This aims to reduce accumulation of toxic SOD1 protein species implicated in SOD1‑mutant ALS.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Total CSF SOD1 protein (biomarker of target engagement):
  - In clinical evaluation, tofersen produced reductions in total CSF SOD1 protein. In one study population, at Week 28 there was an approximate 35% reduction in total CSF SOD1 versus a 2% decrease with placebo (nominal p < 0.0001 for the difference in geometric mean ratios).
- Plasma neurofilament light chain (NfL), a biomarker of axonal injury:
  - In clinical evaluation, plasma NfL was reduced by approximately 55% from baseline at Week 28 in tofersen‑treated subjects versus a 12% increase with placebo (difference in geometric mean ratios ≈60%; nominal p < 0.0001). NfL reductions were sustained through long‑term follow‑up visits in extension studies.
- Cardiac electrophysiology:
  - ECG monitoring in clinical study populations did not demonstrate clinically meaningful QTc prolongation attributable to tofersen. Concentration‑effect analyses did not show a clinically relevant relationship between tofersen concentrations and QTcF.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Detailed systemic pharmacokinetic parameters (absorption/distribution/metabolism/excretion) after intrathecal administration: Not specified in available sources.
- Transporters and metabolism:
  - In vitro data indicate tofersen is unlikely to participate in transporter‑mediated drug interactions and is not an inducer or inhibitor of CYP450 oxidative metabolism.

## 12. Clinical Trials

### Key pivotal trial(s)
- Study 101 Part C (randomised, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled):
  - Population: Adults (23–78 years) with weakness attributable to ALS and confirmed SOD1 mutation.
  - Design: 28‑week randomised trial; 2:1 randomisation to tofersen 100 mg versus placebo (3 loading doses followed by maintenance dosing).
  - Randomised subjects: 108 total (72 tofersen, 36 placebo).
  - SOD1 mutations: 42 unique SOD1 mutations evaluated; the most common included p.Ile114Thr, p.Ala5Val, p.Gly94Cys, and p.His47Arg.
  - Efficacy biomarker outcomes:
    - Total CSF SOD1: ~35% reduction from baseline with tofersen at Week 28 vs ~2% decrease with placebo (nominal p < 0.0001).
    - Plasma NfL: ~55% reduction from baseline with tofersen at Week 28 vs 12% increase with placebo (nominal p < 0.0001). Reductions were observed to be sustained in longer‑term follow‑up.
  - Clinical efficacy endpoints (functional, survival outcomes): Specific numeric outcomes for clinical endpoints (e.g., ALSFRS‑R, respiratory measures, survival) are not fully specified in available sources; partial prespecified analysis populations and enrichment criteria are described (modified ITT populations with SVC cutoffs and prognosis‑based enrichment rules).
- Long‑term/open‑label extension studies:
  - Study 102 (open‑label extension) and integrated analyses reported sustained biomarker reductions and safety data with longer exposure (median exposure ~199.7 weeks among 147 SOD1‑ALS subjects evaluated).
  - Safety events of note in extension data included cases of myelitis, radiculitis, increased intracranial pressure/papilloedema, and aseptic meningitis; most events were manageable and did not require permanent discontinuation in the majority of cases.

- Additional trial details (e.g., full primary clinical efficacy outcomes, subgroup analyses, statistical plans) beyond the above summaries: Not specified in available sources.
