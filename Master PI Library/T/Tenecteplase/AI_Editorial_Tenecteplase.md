# Editorial Product Information: Tenecteplase

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: Metalyse (tenecteplase) 40 mg and 50 mg powder for injection.
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. It is designed to consolidate clinical data into a concise, structured format for quick reference.
- **Prescribing Note**: This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official, current Product Information approved in a specific jurisdiction for the purpose of prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Tenecteplase (rch); a recombinant tissue plasminogen activator (t-PA).
- **Brand Name(s)**: Metalyse [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: B01A D11.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s)**: 
    - Lyophilised powder for injection in glass vials: 40 mg (8,000 U) and 50 mg (10,000 U) [Australian].
    - Supplied with a pre-filled syringe of Water for Injections (WFI) (8 mL for the 40 mg vial; 10 mL for the 50 mg vial) and a vial adapter.
    - Reconstituted concentration: 5 mg (1,000 U) per mL.
- **Route of Administration**: Intravenous (IV) bolus injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Boehringer Ingelheim Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Myocardial Infarction (AMI)**: Thrombolytic treatment of the acute phase of myocardial infarction. Treatment should be initiated as soon as possible, ideally within 12 hours of the onset of symptoms [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult)**: Dosing is based on body weight, with a maximum dose of 50 mg (10,000 U). 
- **Dosing Table**:
| Patient Body Weight (kg) | Tenecteplase (U) | Tenecteplase (mg) | Reconstituted Volume (mL) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| < 60 | 6,000 | 30 | 6 |
| ≥ 60 to < 70 | 7,000 | 35 | 7 |
| ≥ 70 to < 80 | 8,000 | 40 | 8 |
| ≥ 80 to < 90 | 9,000 | 45 | 9 |
| ≥ 90 | 10,000 | 50 | 10 |
- **Administration Instructions**:
    - Administer as a **single intravenous bolus** over approximately 10 seconds.
    - **Incompatibility**: Must NOT be administered in a line containing dextrose (incompatible).
    - Flush the line after administration to ensure proper delivery.
- **Adjunctive Therapy**: Antithrombotic therapy (e.g., aspirin and heparin) is recommended according to current international guidelines for ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
Tenecteplase is contraindicated where thrombolytic therapy carries a high risk of bleeding:
- **Bleeding Disorders**: Current or within the past 6 months significant bleeding disorder; known haemorrhagic diathesis.
- **CNS Damage**: History of central nervous system damage (neoplasm, aneurysm, intracranial or spinal surgery).
- **Hypertension**: Severe uncontrolled hypertension (Systolic > 180 mmHg and/or Diastolic > 110 mmHg).
- **Trauma/Surgery**: Major surgery, biopsy of a parenchymal organ, or significant trauma within the past 2 months; recent head/cranial trauma.
- **Resuscitation**: Prolonged or traumatic CPR (> 2 minutes) within the past 2 weeks.
- **Organ Dysfunction**: Severe hepatic dysfunction (failure, cirrhosis, portal hypertension, active hepatitis).
- **Gastrointestinal/Genitourinary**: Active peptic ulceration (within 3 months); recent (within 10 days) GI or GU bleeding.
- **Stroke**: Haemorrhagic stroke or stroke of unknown origin at any time; ischaemic stroke or TIA in the preceding 6 months.
- **Other**: Arterial aneurysm; known malformations; neoplasm with increased bleeding risk; acute pericarditis; subacute bacterial endocarditis; acute pancreatitis; recent (10 days) obstetric delivery or non-compressible vessel puncture.
- **Anticoagulation**: Patients receiving effective oral anticoagulants (e.g., warfarin with INR > 1.3).
- **Hypersensitivity**: Known hypersensitivity to tenecteplase, gentamicin (trace residue), or excipients.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Bleeding**: The most common complication. Internal bleeding (any site) or superficial bleeding (puncture sites) may occur. If serious bleeding occurs, discontinue concomitant heparin and antiplatelets immediately. Avoid IM injections and non-essential handling.
- **Coronary Intervention**: 
    - If primary Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI) is scheduled, tenecteplase should not be given (based on ASSENT-4 PCI study data).
    - Patients receiving tenecteplase should be transferred to a PCI-capable facility for angiography and timely intervention within 6–24 hours or earlier if indicated.
- **Arrhythmias**: Reperfusion may induce arrhythmias, which can be life-threatening and require antiarrhythmic therapy.
- **Cholesterol Embolism**: Reported rarely with thrombolytics; can be lethal and affects multiple organ systems.
- **Hypersensitivity**: Anaphylactoid reactions are rare. No sustained antibody formation has been observed, but there is no experience with re-administration.
- **Traceability**: Trade name and batch number should be recorded.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following frequencies are based on clinical trials (ASSENT-2) [Australian]:

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (>10%) | Superficial bleeding (punctures/damaged vessels), reperfusion arrhythmias, hypotension, heart rate/rhythm disorders, angina pectoris. |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Ecchymosis, urogenital haemorrhage (haematuria), epistaxis, GI haemorrhage, nausea, vomiting, fever, required transfusion. |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Pulmonary haemorrhage, retroperitoneal haemorrhage, intracranial haemorrhage (stroke, haematoma), eye haemorrhage, embolism, anaphylactoid reactions. |
| **Rare** (<0.1%) | Pericardial haemorrhage, fat embolism. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects**: Intracranial haemorrhage (ICH) is a major risk, with incidence increasing with age. In the ASSENT-2 study, the ICH rate was approximately 0.93%.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions**: 
    - Agents affecting coagulation or platelet function (e.g., LMWH, heparin, GP IIb/IIIa inhibitors) increase bleeding risk when used before, during, or after tenecteplase.
- **Clinical Management**: No formal interaction studies have been performed, but data from >12,000 patients revealed no clinically relevant interactions with medications commonly used in AMI management.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: **Category C** [Australian]. Thrombolytic agents may cause placental haemorrhage, fetal loss, or prematurity. Animal studies showed embryonic death in rabbits. Use only if potential benefits outweigh risks to the fetus.
- **Breastfeeding**: It is unknown if tenecteplase is excreted in human milk. Caution is advised; consider discontinuing breastfeeding for the first 24 hours post-administration.
- **Fertility**: No clinical or nonclinical data available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and efficacy not established; use not recommended [Australian].
- **Elderly (≥ 75 years)**: Administer with caution due to higher bleeding risk. 
    - **Editorial Note**: In the STREAM study (pharmaco-invasive strategy), the tenecteplase dose was halved for patients $\ge$ 75 years to reduce ICH risk [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment**: Kidney involvement in elimination is not expected; renal dysfunction is not expected to affect pharmacokinetics.
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Severe dysfunction is a contraindication. The specific effect of hepatic impairment on tenecteplase pharmacokinetics in humans is unknown.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Tenecteplase is a modified recombinant t-PA with higher fibrin specificity and greater resistance to inactivation by plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 (PAI-1) than native t-PA. It binds to fibrin and converts thrombus-bound plasminogen to plasmin, which degrades the thrombus matrix.
- **Pharmacodynamics**: Causes minimal consumption of fibrinogen (<15%) and plasminogen (<25%) compared to alteplase.
- **Pharmacokinetics**: 
    - **Distribution**: Biphasic elimination. Initial dominant half-life is ~24 minutes.
    - **Metabolism**: Cleared primarily by binding to specific receptors in the liver and catabolised to small peptides.
    - **Elimination**: Plasma clearance is ~119 mL/min. Clearance increases with body weight and decreases slightly with age.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ASSENT-2**: A large mortality trial (~17,000 patients) demonstrating that single-bolus tenecteplase is therapeutically equivalent to accelerated alteplase infusion in 30-day mortality (6.2%). Tenecteplase showed a lower incidence of non-intracranial bleeding and lower transfusion requirements.
- **ASSENT-4 PCI**: Demonstrated that full-dose tenecteplase administered immediately prior to primary PCI (within 60–180 mins) resulted in higher mortality and adverse events compared to primary PCI alone.
- **STREAM**: Evaluated a pharmaco-invasive strategy (tenecteplase within 3 hours of symptoms for patients unable to undergo PCI within 1 hour). Resulted in similar outcomes to primary PCI but required a protocol amendment to half the tenecteplase dose in patients $\ge$ 75 years to mitigate ICH risk.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vial Storage**: Store below 30°C. Keep in the outer carton to protect from light.
- **Reconstituted Solution**: 
    - Chemical and physical stability demonstrated for 24 hours at 2–8°C or 6 hours at room temperature.
    - From a microbiological perspective, the product should be used immediately. If necessary, it can be held at 2–8°C for no more than 24 hours.
