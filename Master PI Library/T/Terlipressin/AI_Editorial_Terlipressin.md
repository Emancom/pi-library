# Editorial Product Information: Terlipressin

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (TGA).
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of terlipressin, synthesized from official Product Information (PI) sources for use by healthcare professionals. 
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local clinical guidelines required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Terlipressin (as terlipressin acetate).
*   **Chemical Class:** Synthetic analogue of the posterior pituitary hormone vasopressin (dodecapeptide).
*   **Brand Name(s):** **Glypressin** [Australian].
*   **ATC Code:** H01BA04.
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Solution for injection/infusion: 0.85 mg terlipressin in 8.5 mL of solution (0.1 mg/mL) [Australian].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) injection or IV infusion.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Ferring Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Bleeding Oesophageal Varices (BOV):** Treatment of acute episodes [Australian-approved].
*   **Type 1 Hepatorenal Syndrome (HRS):** Treatment of patients who are actively being considered for liver transplant [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Bleeding Oesophageal Varices (BOV)
*   **Initial Dose:** 1.7 mg via IV injection every 4 hours.
*   **Maintenance:** Once bleeding is controlled, the dose may be reduced to 0.85 mg IV every 4 hours.
*   **Adjustments:** In patients weighing <50 kg or if adverse effects occur, the dose may be reduced to 0.85 mg IV every 4 hours after the initial dose.
*   **Maximum Duration:** Treatment should not exceed 48 hours.

### Type 1 Hepatorenal Syndrome (HRS)
*   **Bolus Regimen:** 0.85 mg via slow IV injection every 6 hours for 7 to 14 days.
*   **Titration:** If serum creatinine (SCr) has not decreased by at least 30% from baseline after 2 days, increase the dose to a maximum of 1.7 mg every 6 hours.
*   **Continuous Infusion:** An alternative to bolus injection; start at 1.7 mg/24 hours, increasing to a maximum of 10.2 mg/24 hours.
*   **Reversal Criteria:** Maintain treatment until approximately 2 days after achieving HRS reversal (SCr ≤ 132.6 micromoles/L).
*   **Discontinuation:** Stop treatment if the patient undergoes dialysis/transplant, or if no response/partial response occurs within 14 days.
*   **Co-therapy:** Should be administered in conjunction with human albumin [Australian].

### Administration and Dilution
*   **Dilution:** For HRS infusion, may be diluted in up to 250 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride or 5% glucose.
*   **Incompatibility:** Diluted product is incompatible with polyurethane (PUR) lines; use PVC or polyethylene (PE) lines [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
*   **Pregnancy:** Strictly contraindicated due to uterine contractions and reduced fetal perfusion [Australian].
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to terlipressin or any excipients.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Cardiovascular Effects:** Terlipressin may induce ischaemia and pulmonary vascular congestion. Avoid in unstable angina or recent acute myocardial infarction. Use with extreme caution in uncontrolled hypertension, arrhythmias, or peripheral vascular disease.
*   **Respiratory Failure:** Fatal cases of respiratory failure, including those due to fluid overload, have been reported in HRS patients. Stabilise breathing difficulties before the first dose.
*   **Monitoring Requirements:** Mandatory regular monitoring of blood pressure, ECG/heart rate, oxygen saturation, serum electrolytes (sodium/potassium), and fluid balance. 
*   **Sepsis/Septic Shock:** Patients with HRS should be monitored daily for signs of infection. Do not use in patients with septic shock and low cardiac output.
*   **Skin Necrosis:** Extreme caution in patients with peripheral venous hypertension, diabetes, or obesity. Administer strictly intravenously to avoid local necrosis.
*   **Torsades de Pointes:** Cases of QT prolongation and ventricular arrhythmias have been reported, usually in patients with pre-existing electrolyte abnormalities or concomitant QT-prolonging medications.

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (>10%) | Abdominal pain [Australian]; Respiratory failure, Dyspnoea (in HRS population) [Australian]. |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Hyponatraemia, headache, chest pain, bradycardia, tachycardia, vasoconstriction, peripheral ischaemia, hypertension, pallor, cyanosis, pulmonary oedema, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea. |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Atrial fibrillation, ventricular extrasystoles, myocardial infarction, Torsades de Pointes, cardiac failure, intestinal ischaemia, skin necrosis (unrelated to injection site), injection site necrosis. |
| **Rare** (<0.1%) | Ventricular fibrillation. |
| **Frequency Not Known** | Uterine hypertonus, uterine ischaemia. |

*   **Note:** Continuous IV infusion is associated with lower rates of severe adverse events compared to IV bolus administration [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Beta-blockers:** Terlipressin increases the hypotensive effect of non-selective beta-blockers on the portal vein.
*   **Bradycardic Agents:** Concomitant use with propofol or sufentanil may significantly lower heart rate and cardiac output.
*   **QT-prolonging Medications:** Extreme caution with Class IA/III antiarrhythmics, erythromycin, certain antihistamines, and tricyclic antidepressants due to the risk of Torsades de Pointes.
*   **Diuretics:** Agents causing hypokalaemia or hypomagnesaemia may potentiate the risk of arrhythmias.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Terlipressin causes uterine contractions, increased intrauterine pressure, and decreased uterine blood flow. It may cause spontaneous abortion or fetal malformations.
*   **Breastfeeding:** No data available regarding excretion into breast milk. Use is not recommended during breastfeeding.
*   **Fertility:** Animal studies indicate a risk of reduced fertility and testicular atrophy. No human data available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Limited experience; special precaution is required. No specific dosage recommendations available.
*   **Elderly Use:** Limited experience; use with special precaution. No specific dosage recommendations available.
*   **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution. Avoid in advanced renal dysfunction (baseline SCr ≥ 442 micromoles/L) in HRS treatment unless benefits outweigh risks, as efficacy is reduced and mortality risk increased in this group [Australian].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Avoid in severe liver disease (ACLF grade 3 or MELD score ≥ 39) for HRS treatment unless benefits outweigh risks, due to increased risk of respiratory failure and mortality [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Terlipressin is a pro-drug. It is enzymatically cleaved into lysine vasopressin, which acts primarily on V1a receptors on vascular smooth muscle. This causes potent vasoconstriction in the splanchnic and portal circulation, reducing portal venous pressure. It has minimal activity at V2 receptors (approximately 3% of native vasopressin activity).
*   **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    *   **Distribution:** Two-compartment model. Distribution half-life ~8 mins; elimination half-life ~40 mins.
    *   **Metabolism:** Nearly complete metabolism by peptidases; only 1% is excreted unchanged in urine. 
    *   **Active Metabolite:** Lysine vasopressin appears in plasma ~30 mins after administration, peaking at 60–120 mins.

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **BOV Studies:** Multiple placebo-controlled trials (Walker et al., Freeman et al., Söderlund et al.) demonstrated significantly higher rates of bleeding control and reduced transfusion requirements compared to placebo. One study (Abid et al.) established non-inferiority to octreotide when combined with endoscopic banding.
*   **HRS Studies:** The pivotal OT-0401/NCT00089570 study (Sanyal et al.) showed significantly higher HRS reversal rates with terlipressin (33.9%) vs. placebo (12.5%, p=0.008). 
*   **Infusion vs. Bolus:** A multicentre trial in HRS patients found that continuous infusion resulted in significantly lower rates of treatment-related adverse events (35% vs. 62%) and severe adverse events (21% vs. 43%) compared to bolus dosing [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C.
*   **Protection:** Keep in the original packaging to protect from light.
*   **Post-dilution:** Stable for 24 hours at 25°C; however, immediate use is recommended to reduce microbiological hazard.
