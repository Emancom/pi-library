# Editorial Product Information: Tafluprost

## Editorial Note
- Jurisdictional PI documents contributing to this editorial include: Australian (TGA) and US (FDA).
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising information from multiple regulatory authorities into a single clinical reference.
- This document is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Always consult the local approved PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tafluprost
- **Chemical Class:** Fluorinated analogue of prostaglandin F2α; selective prostanoid FP receptor agonist.
- **Brand Names:** **Saflutan** [Australian]; **Tafluprost Ophthalmic Solution** [US].
- **ATC Code:** S01EE05
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Sterile ophthalmic solution 0.0015% (15 micrograms/mL).
    - Supplied in single-dose containers (0.3 mL) containing 4.5 micrograms of tafluprost.
- **Route of Administration:** Topical ophthalmic.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Mundipharma Pty Limited [Australian]; Sandoz Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- Reduction of elevated intraocular pressure (IOP) in patients with open-angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension.
- **Saflutan** is specifically indicated as monotherapy or as adjunctive therapy to beta-blockers [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** One drop in the conjunctival sac of the affected eye(s) once daily in the evening.
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - The dose must not exceed once daily, as more frequent administration may diminish the IOP-lowering effect.
    - Single-dose containers are for one-time use; one container is sufficient to treat both eyes. Discard immediately after use.
    - If using multiple topical ophthalmic products, space administration by at least 5 minutes.
    - **Contact Lenses:** Remove lenses prior to administration; they may be reinserted 15 minutes after dosing [US].
    - **Systemic Absorption:** To reduce the risk of eyelid skin darkening and systemic absorption, patients should blot off excess solution from the skin and perform nasolacrimal occlusion or gentle eyelid closure following instillation [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to tafluprost or any of the excipients [Australian].
- The [US] labeling lists no specific contraindications.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Pigmentation:** May cause permanent increased brown pigmentation of the iris, which may not be noticeable for several months or years. Eyelid skin (periorbital tissue) and eyelash pigmentation may also occur; these changes are typically reversible upon discontinuation. Unilateral treatment may result in permanent heterochromia between eyes.
- **Eyelash Changes:** May gradually change eyelashes and vellus hair, including increased length, thickness, number, and colour. These changes are generally reversible.
- **Macular Oedema:** Use with caution in aphakic patients, pseudophakic patients with a torn posterior lens capsule, or patients with known risk factors for macular oedema (e.g., iritis or uveitis).
- **Intraocular Inflammation:** Use with caution in patients with active intraocular inflammation, as the condition may be exacerbated.
- **Glaucoma Subtypes:** No experience exists for use in neovascular, angle-closure, narrow-angle, or congenital glaucoma [Australian].
- **Respiratory Function:** Use with caution in patients with compromised lung function or pulmonary insufficiency. Post-marketing cases of dyspnoea and asthma exacerbation have been reported [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most common drug-related adverse reaction is conjunctival/ocular hyperaemia, reported in 4% to 20% of patients. Discontinuation due to ocular adverse reactions occurs in approximately 1% to 2.1% of patients.

### Ocular Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Ocular hyperaemia.
- **Common (1–10%):** Ocular stinging/irritation, pruritus (including allergic conjunctivitis), eye pain, dry eye, eyelash growth/darkening/thickening, eyelash discolouration, increased lacrimation, eyelid erythema, foreign body sensation, blurred vision, photophobia, reduced visual acuity, cataracts, punctate keratitis, eye discharge, eyelid oedema, and iris hyperpigmentation.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Blepharal pigmentation.
- **Post-marketing/Rare:** Deepening of the eyelid sulcus, iritis/uveitis. Very rare cases of corneal calcification have been reported in association with phosphate-containing drops in patients with significantly damaged corneas [Australian].

### Non-Ocular Adverse Effects
- **Common (1–10%):** Headache, common cold, cough, and urinary tract infection [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- No specific drug interaction studies have been performed.
- Interactions are considered unlikely due to extremely low systemic concentrations following ocular dosing.
- Clinical trials demonstrated safe concomitant use with **timolol** 0.5% without evidence of increased adverse events.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B3:** Should not be used during pregnancy unless the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus. Women of childbearing potential should use adequate contraceptive measures.
    - **US Classification:** Animal data shows embryofetal toxicity at intravenous exposures significantly higher than clinical doses (5 to 2362 times the human dose).
- **Breastfeeding:** Caution should be exercised. Studies in rats show excretion of tafluprost/metabolites in milk. It is unknown if tafluprost is excreted in human milk.
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on mating performance or fertility were observed in rat studies at doses up to 14,000 times the maximum clinical exposure.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended. Safety and effectiveness have not been established. The [US] source notes potential safety concerns regarding increased pigmentation with long-term chronic use.
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment is required for patients >65 years. Efficacy and safety profiles are similar to younger adults.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not studied in these populations; use with caution [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Tafluprost acid is a potent, selective agonist of the human prostanoid FP receptor. It reduces IOP by increasing uveoscleral outflow of aqueous humour.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absorbed through the cornea and hydrolysed to the active acid metabolite. Plasma $C_{max}$ is reached within 10 minutes.
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (>99% to human serum albumin).
    - **Metabolism:** Hydrolysed in the eye; further metabolised via fatty acid $\beta$-oxidation and phase II conjugation. The CYP450 system is not involved.
    - **Elimination:** Rapidly cleared from plasma; concentrations fall below quantification limits (10 pg/mL) within 30 minutes of dosing.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** IOP reduction begins 2 to 4 hours post-instillation, reaching maximum effect at 12 hours. The effect is maintained for at least 24 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Monotherapy:** In Phase III studies, tafluprost 0.0015% demonstrated a significant IOP-lowering effect of 5 to 8 mmHg. It showed non-inferiority to **timolol** 0.5% and sustained effects for up to 24 months.
- **Comparison to Latanoprost:** A study comparing tafluprost 0.0015% to **latanoprost** 0.005% showed IOP reductions of 6 to 8 mmHg and 7 to 9 mmHg, respectively. While non-inferiority was not demonstrated at the primary 6-month endpoint in one analysis, it was demonstrated at multiple other time points (3, 18, and 24 months).
- **Adjunctive Therapy:** When added to **timolol** 0.5%, tafluprost provided an additional 5 to 6 mmHg reduction in IOP compared to 3 to 4 mmHg for vehicle plus timolol.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened foil pouches:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze.
- **After opening the foil pouch:** 
    - Store below 25°C in the original foil pouch to protect from moisture.
    - Discard unused single-dose containers after **28 days** [Australian] or **30 days** [US] from the date the pouch was first opened.
    - Use single-dose containers immediately after opening and discard any remaining solution.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australian:** Poison Schedule S4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **US:** Rx Only.
