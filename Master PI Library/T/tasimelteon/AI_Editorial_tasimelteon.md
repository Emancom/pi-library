# Editorial Product Information: Tasimelteon

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been derived from the following regulatory source: **United States (FDA)** Product Information for tasimelteon capsules (Apotex Corp.).
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis intended for use by healthcare professionals. It provides a structured overview of the clinical and regulatory profile of tasimelteon based on the provided source.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tasimelteon (melatonin receptor agonist).
- **Brand Names:** Not specified as a proprietary brand name in the provided source (listed as **Tasimelteon Capsules**).
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 
    - Oral capsule: 20 mg [US].
    - Note: An oral suspension exists but is not interchangeable with capsules on a milligram-to-milligram basis [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Apotex Corp. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Non-24-Hour Sleep-Wake Disorder (Non-24):** Indicated for the treatment of Non-24 in adults [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** 20 mg once daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Take approximately one hour before the desired bedtime [US].
    - Administer at the same time every night [US].
    - **Food Effect:** Must be taken without food (fasted state). Taking with a high-fat meal significantly reduces $C_{max}$ and delays absorption [US].
    - Capsules should be swallowed whole [US].
- **Non-Interchangeability:** Tasimelteon capsules and tasimelteon oral suspension are not substitutable [US].
- **Clinical Onset:** Due to individual variations in circadian rhythms, the full therapeutic effect may not be observed for several weeks or months [US].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed at the scheduled time, it should be skipped, and the patient should resume the next dose at the regular time the following night [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None identified in the provided source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Somnolence:** Tasimelteon causes sedation. After administration, patients should limit activities to preparing for bed. It can impair the performance of activities requiring complete mental alertness if performed before sleep [US].
- **Monitoring:** Periodic monitoring of alanine aminotransferase (ALT) may be considered, as elevations were noted in clinical trials [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions reported with an incidence >5% and at least twice the rate of placebo are summarized below:

| Adverse Reaction | Tasimelteon (n=42) | Placebo (n=42) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Headache | 17% | 7% |
| Alanine aminotransferase (ALT) increased | 10% | 5% |
| Nightmares or abnormal dreams | 10% | 0% |
| Upper respiratory tract infection | 7% | 0% |
| Urinary tract infection | 7% | 2% |

- **Discontinuation Rates:** In placebo-controlled studies, 6% of patients discontinued treatment due to adverse events, compared to 4% for placebo [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Strong CYP1A2 Inhibitors (e.g., fluvoxamine):** Combination should be avoided. Co-administration with fluvoxamine increases tasimelteon exposure (AUC) by 7-fold, significantly increasing the risk of adverse reactions [US].
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., rifampin):** Combination should be avoided. Co-administration with rifampin decreases tasimelteon exposure by approximately 90%, likely resulting in reduced efficacy [US].
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole):** Increased tasimelteon exposure (approx. 50%) occurs; monitor for increased sedation [US].
- **Beta-Adrenergic Receptor Antagonists (e.g., metoprolol, acebutolol):** May reduce the efficacy of tasimelteon by inhibiting the endogenous production of melatonin [US].
- **Alcohol:** A single dose of ethanol showed a trend toward an additive effect on psychomotor performance tests when combined with tasimelteon [US].
- **Effect of Tasimelteon on other drugs:** No clinically significant induction of CYP3A4 or CYP2C8 was observed at the 20 mg dose [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified as a letter grade (Post-2015 labeling format).
    - **Risk Summary:** No sufficient human data to evaluate risks of birth defects or miscarriage. Animal data (rats and rabbits) demonstrated embryofetal toxicity (reduced body weight, delayed ossification, and neurobehavioral impairment) only at doses significantly higher (approx. 200x) than the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** No data available on the presence of tasimelteon in human milk, effects on the infant, or effects on milk production. The developmental benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need and potential adverse effects [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats showed estrus cycle disruption and decreased fertility at doses $>5$ mg/kg/day [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established in patients under 18 years of age [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Exposure is increased approximately 2-fold in patients $>65$ years compared to younger adults. Risk of adverse reactions may be higher [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required for patients with any degree of renal impairment, including end-stage renal disease (ESRD) on hemodialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild to Moderate (Child-Pugh Class A or B):** No dose adjustment necessary.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh Class C):** Not recommended, as it has not been studied in this population [US].
- **Smokers:** Exposure is decreased by approximately 40% in smokers (due to CYP1A2 induction). Efficacy may be reduced in this population [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Tasimelteon is an agonist at $MT_1$ and $MT_2$ receptors. These receptors are believed to be involved in the regulation of circadian rhythms. It has a higher affinity for the $MT_2$ receptor than the $MT_1$ receptor [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is 38.3%. Median $T_{max}$ is 0.5 to 3 hours [US].
    - **Distribution:** Approximately 90% bound to plasma proteins [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolized, primarily via oxidation by CYP1A2 and CYP3A4, followed by phenolic glucuronidation [US].
    - **Elimination:** Mean terminal half-life ($t_{1/2}$) is 1.3 hours. 80% of radioactivity is excreted in urine (less than 1% as parent drug) and 4% in feces [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** Effectiveness was established in two randomized, double-masked, placebo-controlled trials (Study 1 and Study 2) involving totally blind patients with Non-24 [US].
- **Outcomes:** 
    - **Study 1:** Significant improvement in nighttime sleep time (+50 min vs +22 min for placebo) and reduction in daytime nap duration (-49 min vs -22 min for placebo) [US].
    - **Study 2 (Randomized Withdrawal):** Demonstrated maintenance of effect; patients switching to placebo showed a significant worsening of sleep parameters compared to those continuing tasimelteon [US].
- **Responder Analysis:** 29% of patients treated with tasimelteon met a stringent responder criterion ($\ge 45$ min increase in night sleep and $\ge 45$ min decrease in day nap) compared to 12% on placebo [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at $20^\circ\text{C}$ to $25^\circ\text{C}$ ($68^\circ\text{F}$ to $77^\circ\text{F}$); excursions permitted between $15^\circ\text{C}$ and $30^\circ\text{C}$ [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from light and moisture [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** 
    - **United States:** Prescription only (Rx-only). Not a controlled substance [US].
