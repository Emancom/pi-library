# Editorial Product Information: Tamoxifen

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of tamoxifen for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises available regulatory data into a single clinical reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and must not be used as a substitute for the official, current Product Information approved in a specific jurisdiction for the purposes of prescribing or dispensing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tamoxifen (as citrate).
- **Chemical Class:** Non-steroidal triphenylethylene-based Selective Estrogen Receptor Modulator (SERM).
- **Brand Names:** Genox [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L02BA01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Oral tablets: 10 mg and 20 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Viatris (Alphapharm Pty Ltd) [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Treatment of Breast Cancer:** Indicated for the management of breast cancer [Australian].
- **Primary Reduction of Breast Cancer Risk:** Indicated for women at moderately increased risk (1.5 to 3 times population average) or high risk (greater than 3 times population average) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Breast Cancer Treatment
- **Initial Dosage:** 20 mg orally once daily.
- **Advanced Disease:** If no response is observed, the dose may be increased to 40 mg once daily [Australian].

### Primary Reduction of Breast Cancer Risk
- **Standard Dosage:** 20 mg orally once daily for a duration of 5 years.
- **Note:** Data are insufficient to support higher doses or longer treatment durations for this indication [Australian].

### General Administration
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Assessment:** Prior to risk reduction therapy, a formal risk assessment using validated algorithms (considering age, family history, and genetic factors) is essential [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy:** Tamoxifen is strictly contraindicated during pregnancy [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to tamoxifen or any tablet excipient [Australian].
- **Risk Reduction Specifics:** For the indication of primary breast cancer risk reduction, tamoxifen is contraindicated in:
    - Women requiring concomitant coumarin-type anticoagulant therapy.
    - Women with a history of deep vein thrombosis (DVT) or pulmonary embolism (PE) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Endometrial Changes:** Increased risk of endometrial hyperplasia, polyps, uterine cancer, and uterine sarcoma (e.g., malignant mixed Mullerian tumours). Prompt investigation of abnormal vaginal bleeding is mandatory [Australian].
- **Thromboembolic Events:** Increased incidence of DVT and PE. Risk is further elevated when combined with chemotherapy. Benefit-risk must be carefully weighed in patients with a history of thromboembolism [Australian].
- **Ocular Effects:** Reports of cataracts, retinopathy, and corneal changes. Blindness has occurred in rare cases of optic neuropathy [Australian].
- **Hematological Effects:** Caution in patients with pre-existing leucopenia or thrombocytopenia. Periodic complete blood counts are recommended [Australian].
- **QTc Prolongation:** May prolong the QTc interval in patients with underlying cardiac comorbidities or electrolyte imbalances [Australian].
- **Hereditary Angioedema:** Tamoxifen may induce or exacerbate symptoms in susceptible patients [Australian].
- **Bone Mineral Density:** In premenopausal women, tamoxifen may reduce bone mineral density. Monitoring and measures to maintain bone health are advised [Australian].
- **Surgery/Immobility:** For risk reduction therapy, consider stopping tamoxifen 3 weeks prior to elective surgery or during periods of prolonged immobility to mitigate VTE risk [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Categorisation
- **Very Common (>10%):** Hot flushes, nausea, vomiting, vaginal discharge, suppression of menstruation, fatigue, and depression [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Vaginal bleeding, pruritus vulvae, fluid retention, alopecia, skin rash, leg cramps, myalgia, sensory disturbances (paraesthesia, dysgeusia), and elevation of serum triglycerides [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Hypercalcaemia (especially in patients with bony metastases at treatment initiation), leucopenia, thrombocytopenia, interstitial pneumonitis, and pancreatitis [Australian].
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Endometrial cancer, uterine sarcoma, agranulocytosis, optic neuritis, and severe liver abnormalities (hepatitis, cirrhosis, liver failure) [Australian].
- **Very Rare:** Cutaneous lupus erythematosus, porphyria cutanea tarda, and radiation recall [Australian].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Thromboembolism:** Ischaemic cerebrovascular events and VTE.
- **Uterine Malignancy:** Endometrial adenocarcinoma and uterine sarcoma.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Potentially fatal liver injury.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Anticoagulants (Coumarin-type):** Significant increase in anticoagulant effect. Concomitant use is contraindicated for the risk reduction indication and requires intensive monitoring in breast cancer treatment [Australian].
- **Cytotoxic Agents:** Increased risk of thromboembolic events when used in combination [Australian].
- **CYP2D6 Inhibitors:** Strong inhibitors (e.g., paroxetine) may reduce plasma levels of the active metabolite, endoxifen, potentially reducing efficacy [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Rifampicin may reduce tamoxifen plasma levels [Australian].
- **Aromatase Inhibitors:** Combination therapy has not demonstrated improved efficacy over tamoxifen monotherapy in adjuvant settings [Australian].
- **Hormone Replacement Therapy (HRT):** May reduce tamoxifen effectiveness; safety of concomitant use is unknown [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B3]. Must not be administered. Premenopausal women must exclude pregnancy before initiation and use effective non-hormonal contraception during and for 9 months after treatment completion [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Excretion into human milk is unknown; use is not recommended during lactation [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Tamoxifen displays complex estrogenic/anti-estrogenic effects on various tissues; menstruation is often suppressed in premenopausal women [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not indicated for children. In girls with McCune Albright Syndrome (MAS), increased uterine volume was observed, and clearance was lower in younger patients [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific dose adjustment is provided, though risk-benefit assessment for risk reduction should consider that VTE and endometrial cancer risks are higher in women >50 years [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Tamoxifen is extensively metabolised in the liver; accumulation of the drug and metabolites is possible. Dose adjustments may be necessary, though specific guidelines are not provided in the source [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A SERM that acts primarily as an anti-estrogen in breast tissue by competing with estrogen for binding to the estrogen receptor. It exhibits estrogenic effects in other tissues (e.g., endometrium, bone, and liver) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absorbed from the GI tract; peak serum levels reached in 3–6 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Found in high concentrations in the uterus, ovary, and liver. Highly protein-bound (>99%) to serum albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised in the liver via CYP3A4 (demethylation) and CYP2D6. The major metabolite is N-desmethyltamoxifen.
    - **Excretion:** Primarily via faeces as conjugates; elimination is slow.
    - **Half-life:** 5–7 days for tamoxifen; 10–14 days for N-desmethyltamoxifen [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Primary Risk Reduction:** Based on large-scale trials including IBIS-1, NSABP P1, and the Royal Marsden study.
    - **NSABP P1:** Demonstrated a 75% risk reduction in women with atypical hyperplasia.
    - **IBIS-1:** Confirmed efficacy in reducing breast cancer risk in premenopausal women.
    - **Meta-analysis:** Data from >28,000 women showed a Hazard Ratio (HR) for all breast cancer of 0.67 (95% CI 0.59-0.76), with effects lasting more than 10 years post-treatment [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Conditions:** Protect from light [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
