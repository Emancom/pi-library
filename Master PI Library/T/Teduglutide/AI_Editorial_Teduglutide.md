# Editorial Product Information: Teduglutide

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the **Australian (TGA)** regulatory authority. 
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a structured format for quick reference.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and is not intended to replace the official PI for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to the full, locally approved PI for definitive prescribing guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Teduglutide (rDNA origin), a 33-amino acid glucagon-like peptide-2 (GLP-2) analogue.
- **Brand Name:** REVESTIVE [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A16AX08.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Powder for solution for injection. Each single-use vial contains 5 mg of teduglutide. After reconstitution with 0.5 mL of solvent, the concentration is 10 mg/mL.
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Takeda Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Short Bowel Syndrome (SBS):** Indicated for the treatment of adult and paediatric patients (aged 2 years and above) who are dependent on parenteral support.
- **Pre-requisite:** Patients must be stable on their parenteral support regimen for at least 4 weeks prior to initiating therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Standard Dose:** 0.05 mg/kg body weight administered via subcutaneous injection once daily.
- **Paediatric Considerations:** Should not be administered to children weighing less than 10 kg [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Sites:** Subcutaneous injection into the abdomen (preferred) or thigh (if abdomen is not possible). 
- **Rotation:** Injection sites should be alternated. 
- **Preparation:** Dissolve powder by adding all solvent from the pre-filled syringe. Do not shake; roll the vial between palms and turn upside-down once.
- **Stability:** Use within 3 hours of reconstitution [Australian].

### Clinical Assessment and Evaluation
- **Adults:** Evaluate treatment effect on an ongoing basis. If no overall improvement occurs after 12 months, the necessity of continued treatment should be reassessed [Australian].
- **Paediatrics:** Evaluate treatment after 6 months. If no improvement is achieved, reassess the need for continued therapy. No data are available for paediatric patients beyond 6 months of treatment [Australian].

### Missed Dose
- If a dose is missed, it should be administered as soon as possible on that day.
- Do not administer two doses on the same day.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to teduglutide or any excipients (histidine, mannitol, monobasic sodium phosphate monohydrate, dibasic sodium phosphate heptahydrate).
- Active or suspected malignancy.
- History of gastrointestinal (GI) tract malignancies, including the hepatobiliary system, within the previous 5 years [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Colorectal Polyps and Neoplasia
- **Adult Screening:** Colonoscopy of the entire colon with polyp removal must be performed within 6 months prior to starting treatment. Follow-up colonoscopy is recommended 1–2 years after starting treatment, then every 5 years (or more frequently for high-risk patients).
- **Paediatric Screening:** Faecal occult blood testing (FOBT) is required prior to initiation and annually thereafter. Colonoscopy/sigmoidoscopy is required if unexplained blood is found, after one year of treatment, and every 5 years thereafter.
- **Malignancy:** If colorectal cancer is diagnosed, therapy must be discontinued. For non-GI malignancies, continue based on risk-benefit assessment [Australian].

### Gallbladder, Bile Tract, and Pancreas
- **Monitoring:** Bilirubin, alkaline phosphatase, lipase, and amylase should be assessed prior to and during treatment.
- **Clinical Action:** If significant changes occur, perform imaging (gallbladder/biliary tract or pancreas) and reassess the need for treatment.

### Intestinal Obstruction
- Intestinal obstruction has been reported. If obstruction or stomal obstruction occurs, temporarily discontinue teduglutide. It may be restarted once the obstruction resolves if clinically indicated.

### Fluid and Electrolyte Balance
- **Fluid Overload:** Increased absorption can lead to fluid overload and congestive heart failure, particularly in the first 4 weeks of therapy. Careful downward titration of parenteral support is required.
- **Monitoring:** Monitor for sudden weight gain, facial swelling, or dyspnoea, especially in patients with cardiovascular history.
- **Dehydration:** Discontinuation of treatment can lead to fluid imbalance and dehydration; monitor status closely upon cessation [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most common adverse events identified in placebo-controlled studies include:
- **Very Common ($\ge$ 10%):** Abdominal pain (28.4%), nausea (25.7%), abdominal distension (16.5%), vomiting (13.8%), headache (15.6%), injection site reactions (e.g., haematoma, erythema), nasopharyngitis (12.8%), urinary tract infection (12.8%), and gastrointestinal stoma complications (15.6%).
- **Common (1–10%):** Flatulence, fatigue, pyrexia, peripheral oedema, influenza, decreased appetite, arthralgia, insomnia, cough, dyspnoea, and cholecystitis [Australian].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Pancreatitis, intestinal obstruction, colonic stenosis, and cardiac failure.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interaction:** Due to increased intestinal absorption, there is a risk of increased systemic exposure to concomitant oral medications. 
- **Management:** Monitor patients receiving oral medications that require titration or have a narrow therapeutic index (e.g., benzodiazepines, opioids, digoxin, anti-hypertensives) [Australian].
- **Cytochrome P450:** Based on *in vitro* studies, teduglutide is unlikely to inhibit or induce CYP450 enzymes.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category B1. Case reports have not identified a specific risk of major birth defects, but a risk to the foetus cannot be excluded. Use only if the potential benefit outweighs the risk [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if teduglutide is excreted in human milk. A decision must be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the risk of tumorigenicity seen in animal studies.
- **Fertility:** No human data available. No effects on fertility were observed in rat studies at doses 180 times the human dose.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for ages 2 years and older (weight $\ge$ 10 kg). Pharmacokinetic profiles show lower exposure (AUC) and shorter half-life compared to adults [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required for patients > 65 years. Limited experience in those $\ge$ 75 years.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
  - **Moderate, Severe, or End-stage (CrCl < 50 mL/min):** Reduce daily dose by 50%.
  - **Mild:** No dose adjustment required.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild to moderate impairment (Child-Pugh B). Not studied in severe hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Teduglutide binds to GLP-2 receptors in the intestine, triggering the release of mediators (IGF-1, nitric oxide, KGF). This promotes mucosal repair and growth, increasing villus height and crypt depth to enhance absorption.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is 88%. Peak plasma levels (Cmax) occur at 3–5 hours post-dose.
  - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution (103 mL/kg) is similar to blood volume.
  - **Metabolism:** Likely degraded into small peptides and amino acids via catabolic pathways.
  - **Elimination:** Primarily cleared by the kidneys. Mean terminal half-life is approximately 1.1 hours in SBS patients [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adult Efficacy (Study CL0600-020):** A 24-week double-blind study (n=86) showed 63% of teduglutide patients (0.05 mg/kg) were responders ($\ge$ 20% reduction in parenteral nutrition) vs. 30% for placebo (p=0.002). Mean PN/IV volume reduction was 4.4 L/week for teduglutide vs. 2.3 L/week for placebo.
- **Paediatric Efficacy (TED-C14-006):** In a 24-week study of patients aged 1–17 years, 69% of the 0.05 mg/kg group achieved a $\ge$ 20% reduction in parenteral support volume, and 12% achieved enteral autonomy by week 24 [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Handling:** Do not freeze. 
- **Shelf Life:** 4 years [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Prescription Medicine [Australian].
