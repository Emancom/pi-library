# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- At the time of this editorial review, only the United States PI (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) was available for assessment. As such, jurisdictional comparisons are limited to this source.
- This document serves as a harmonised clinical overview for healthcare professionals and is not a substitute for the official PI or the specific regulatory requirements for prescribing within a particular jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available source.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for intravenous injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic management of patients with metabolic defects resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) on Hemodialysis:** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose: 50 mg/kg.
    - Administration: Slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - Loading Dose: In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose may be given, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - Frequency: Every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    - Maintenance: Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though up to 300 mg/kg may be required based on clinical response.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - Administration: Slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - Dose Adjustments: Adjustments should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration before use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring:** 
    - Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels.
    - Weekly/monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- **Oral Formulation Caution:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been established in patients with renal insufficiency. High doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5%)
In clinical trials of ESRD patients on hemodialysis, the following events were reported regardless of causality:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%), Flu syndrome (25%), Headache (22%), Chest pain (12%), Fever (7%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%), Nausea (8%), Abdominal pain (9%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), Cough increase (9%), Dyspnea (7%), Rhinitis (9%).
- **Neurological:** Dizziness (13%), Paresthesia (5%), Insomnia (4%).

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Anaphylaxis/Hypersensitivity:** Rare but potentially life-threatening [US].
- **Seizures:** Frequency not specified; reported in postmarketing surveillance [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - [US]: Not formally assigned a letter category in the provided document, but reproductive studies in rats/rabbits (3.8 times human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after exogenous administration. Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing guidelines for metabolic disorders and ESRD apply to pediatric patients [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Not specified in available source.
- **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is specifically indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD patients undergoing dialysis. Dosing is adjusted post-dialysis to manage losses [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available source.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Levocarnitine clears accumulated acyl-CoA compounds (toxic in certain inborn errors) by forming acylcarnitine, which is rapidly excreted.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x weekly post-dialysis), trough carnitine levels increased from ~20 µmol/L at baseline to 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Comparisons of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses over 24 weeks showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat. Discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservative [US].
