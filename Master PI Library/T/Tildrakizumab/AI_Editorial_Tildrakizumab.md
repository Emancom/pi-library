# Editorial Product Information: Tildrakizumab

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (TGA). *Note: At the time of this editorial synthesis, only the Australian Product Information was provided for review.*
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of tildrakizumab for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a single clinical reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tildrakizumab (rch).
- **Chemical Class:** Humanised IgG1/k monoclonal antibody (interleukin-23 inhibitor).
- **Brand Name:** **ILUMYA** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Solution for injection in a single-use pre-filled syringe containing 100 mg/mL of tildrakizumab [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sun Pharma ANZ Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Plaque Psoriasis:** Treatment of moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis in adults who are candidates for systemic therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Standard Adult Dosing:** 100 mg administered by subcutaneous injection at Week 0, Week 4, and every 12 weeks thereafter [Australian].
- **Dose Escalation:** In patients with a high disease burden or those weighing >90 kg who exhibit an unsatisfactory response to the 100 mg dose, a dose of 200 mg may be considered [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Administer the missed dose as soon as possible, then resume the original scheduled dosing interval [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Inject into the abdomen, thighs, or upper arm. 
    - Avoid areas within 5 cm of the navel, or skin that is tender, bruised, indurated, or affected by psoriasis. 
    - Do not inject into scars, stretch marks, or blood vessels. 
    - The 200 mg dose requires two separate 100 mg injections [Australian].
    - Visually inspect for particulates or discolouration; do not shake the syringe [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to tildrakizumab or any of the excipients (histidine, polysorbate 80, sucrose) [Australian].
- **Infection:** Clinically significant active infections, such as active tuberculosis [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Infections:** Tildrakizumab may increase the risk of infection. Use with caution in patients with chronic or recurrent infections. If a serious infection develops, monitor the patient closely. Treatment must not be initiated in patients with active tuberculosis (TB) [Australian].
- **TB Screening:** Evaluate patients for TB infection prior to treatment. Monitor for signs of active TB during and after therapy. Consider anti-TB therapy before initiation in patients with a history of latent or active TB where a full course of treatment cannot be confirmed [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** If a serious hypersensitivity reaction occurs, discontinue tildrakizumab immediately and initiate appropriate medical therapy [Australian].
- **Immunisations:** Complete all required vaccinations according to current guidelines prior to starting therapy. Avoid live vaccines during treatment and for at least 17 weeks following the last dose [Australian].
- **Malignancy:** Exercise caution in patients with a history of malignancy or those who develop malignancy during treatment, as the impact of IL-23 inhibition on malignancy risk is not fully established [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following table summarises adverse reactions reported in clinical trials [Australian]:

| System Organ Class | Adverse Reaction | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Infections** | Nasopharyngitis | Very Common (≥10%) |
| | Sinusitis | Common (1–10%) |
| **Nervous System** | Headache | Common (1–10%) |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Diarrhoea, Nausea | Common (1–10%) |
| **Musculoskeletal** | Arthralgia, Back pain, Pain in extremity | Common (1–10%) |
| **General/Site** | Fatigue, Injection site pain | Common (1–10%) |

- **Immunogenicity:** Approximately 6.5% of patients developed antibodies to tildrakizumab. Neutralising antibodies were associated with slightly lower serum concentrations and minor reductions in efficacy, but no specific safety concerns [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP450 Enzymes:** Tildrakizumab does not have a clinically relevant effect on the pharmacokinetics of substrates for CYP1A2, CYP2C9, CYP2C19, CYP2D6, or CYP3A4 [Australian].
- **Live Vaccines:** Concurrent administration is contraindicated (see Section 5) [Australian].
- **Combination Therapy:** The safety and efficacy of tildrakizumab in combination with other immunomodulators or phototherapy have not been evaluated [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B1.** 
    - Limited data in humans. As a precaution, avoid use during pregnancy. 
    - Women of childbearing potential should use effective contraception during and for at least 17 weeks after treatment [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if tildrakizumab is excreted in human milk. Animal data show low levels in breast milk (milk/serum ratio ≤0.002). A decision should be made to either discontinue breastfeeding or the drug, weighing the benefits of each [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on reproductive organs or hormones were observed in animal studies [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use (≥65 years):** No dose adjustment is required [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No specific studies have been conducted; no dose recommendations can be made [Australian].
- **Body Weight:** Exposure decreases as body weight increases. Patients >90 kg may have ~30% lower exposure compared to those ≤90 kg (refer to Section 3 for dosing) [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Tildrakizumab is a monoclonal antibody that binds specifically to the p19 subunit of the IL-23 cytokine. This inhibits the interaction of IL-23 with its receptor, blocking the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines and chemokines involved in psoriasis [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability of 73–90%; Tmax is approximately 6.2 days [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Limited extravascular distribution (Vd/F of 10.8 L) [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Catabolised into component amino acids via general protein degradation; not cleared by CYP450 enzymes [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Half-life is approximately 23.4 days [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **reSURFACE 1 and 2:** These Phase 3 trials (N=1862) evaluated tildrakizumab 100 mg and 200 mg vs placebo and etanercept (in reSURFACE 2). At Week 12, significantly more patients achieved PASI 75 (approx. 61–64%) and PGA "clear" or "minimal" compared to placebo. Efficacy was maintained through 64 weeks in responders [Australian].
- **Scalp Psoriasis:** In a dedicated trial (NCT03897088), 49% of patients achieved an IGA scalp response (0 or 1) at Week 16 compared to 7% for placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Refrigeration:** Store at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze [Australian].
- **Room Temperature:** Stable for up to 30 days at 25°C [Australian].
- **Light Protection:** Keep in the outer carton until use to protect from light [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
