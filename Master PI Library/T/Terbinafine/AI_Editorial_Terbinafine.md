# Editorial Product Information: Terbinafine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authorities: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a single, coherent reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Prescribers should always consult the approved PI in their specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Terbinafine (present as terbinafine hydrochloride)
- **Chemical Class:** Allylamine antifungal
- **Brand Name(s):** APO-Terbinafine [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** D01B A02
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 250 mg uncoated biconvex tablets [Australian]
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
Terbinafine is indicated for the treatment of the following fungal infections when oral therapy is considered appropriate due to the site, severity, or extent of the infection, particularly when topical therapy has failed:
- **Onychomycosis:** Fungal infection of the fingernails or toenails caused by dermatophyte fungi in adults.
- **Tinea Corporis and Tinea Cruris:** Ringworm of the body and groin caused by dermatophytes such as *Trichophyton* (e.g., *T. rubrum*, *T. mentagrophytes*, *T. verrucosum*, *T. violaceum*), *Microsporum canis*, and *Epidermophyton floccosum* in adults.
- **Tinea Pedis:** Fungal infection of the feet (interdigital, plantar, or moccasin type) caused by the aforementioned dermatophytes in adults.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Standard Dose:** 250 mg once daily.
- **Administration:** Tablets are for oral use. Bioavailability is not significantly affected by a light meal.

### Dosing by Indication
- **Tinea Pedis (interdigital, plantar/moccasin):** 2 to 6 weeks.
- **Tinea Corporis / Tinea Cruris:** 2 to 4 weeks.
- **Onychomycosis (Fingernails):** 6 weeks to 3 months (younger patients with normal nail growth may respond to shorter courses).
- **Onychomycosis (Toenails):** Usually 3 months; however, infections of the big toe or cases with poor nail growth may require up to 6 months.

### Administration Instructions
- Clinical resolution of signs and symptoms may not be evident until several weeks after mycological cure and completion of treatment, particularly in onychomycosis, as it depends on the outgrowth of healthy nail tissue.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to terbinafine or any excipients in the formulation.
- Pre-existing severe, chronic, or active hepatic disease [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Terbinafine is associated with rare cases of liver failure, which may lead to death or liver transplant. Liver function tests (LFTs) must be performed prior to treatment and periodic monitoring (after 4–6 weeks of therapy) is recommended. Treatment must be discontinued immediately if LFTs become elevated.
- **Haematological Effects:** Very rare cases of agranulocytosis, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, and pancytopenia have been reported. Rare cases of thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP), including fatal outcomes, have been reported. Discontinue therapy if clinical or laboratory findings suggest TTP or other blood dyscrasias.
- **Dermatological Effects:** Serious skin reactions, including Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), and drug rash with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms (DRESS), have occurred. Discontinue if a progressive skin rash develops.
- **Autoimmune Conditions:** Use with caution in patients with pre-existing psoriasis or lupus erythematosus, as exacerbations have been reported post-marketing.
- **Ocular Effects:** While refractile irregularities were noted in the retinas of monkeys at high doses, clinical trials in humans showed no confirmed increase in ophthalmic abnormalities compared to placebo.
- **Laboratory Monitoring:** In patients with known or suspected immunodeficiency, consider monitoring complete blood counts if treatment exceeds six weeks due to potential transient decreases in absolute lymphocyte counts (ALC).

## 6. Adverse Effects

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP2D6 Inhibition:** Terbinafine is a potent inhibitor of CYP2D6. It may increase the plasma concentrations of drugs metabolised by this enzyme, including tricyclic antidepressants, beta-blockers, SSRIs, Class 1A, 1B, and 1C antiarrhythmics, and MAOIs Type B.
- **Metabolic Inducers:** **Rifampicin** increases terbinafine clearance by 100%, potentially requiring dose adjustment.
- **Metabolic Inhibitors:** **Cimetidine** decreases terbinafine clearance by 33%. **Fluconazole** significantly increases terbinafine Cmax and AUC through inhibition of CYP2C9 and CYP3A4.
- **Ciclosporin:** Terbinafine may increase the clearance of ciclosporin by 15% [Australian].
- **Warfarin:** Spontaneous reports of altered prothrombin time have occurred, though a causal relationship is not established.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Oral Contraceptives:** Cautious use is advised due to reports of breakthrough bleeding and menstrual irregularities, though incidence remains within background levels.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category B1. Animal studies suggest no foetal toxicity. However, clinical experience in pregnant women is limited; use only if potential benefits outweigh potential risks.
- **Breastfeeding:** Terbinafine is excreted in breast milk. Mothers receiving oral terbinafine treatment must not breastfeed.
- **Fertility:** No data available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use is not recommended as there is no established experience in children [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No evidence suggests a need for dosage adjustments unless there is concurrent renal or hepatic impairment.
- **Renal Impairment:** Use is not recommended in patients with creatinine clearance < 50 mL/min or serum creatinine > 300 µmol/L, as clearance is reduced by approximately 50%.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with active or chronic hepatic disease.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Terbinafine inhibits squalene epoxidase, a key enzyme in fungal sterol biosynthesis. This leads to a deficiency in ergosterol and an intracellular accumulation of squalene, resulting in fungal cell death.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** >70% absorbed; bioavailability is ~40% due to first-pass metabolism. Peak plasma concentration (0.83 µg/mL) is reached within 2 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Strongly protein-bound (99%). It is lipophilic and concentrates in the stratum corneum, sebum, hair follicles, and nail plates.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised into inactive metabolites.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (elimination half-life ~17 hours; effective half-life ~36 hours).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Ocular Safety:** Placebo-controlled trials showed an incidence of ophthalmic abnormalities of 1.1% in terbinafine-treated patients versus 1.5% in the placebo group [Australian].
- **ALC monitoring:** In controlled trials, 1.7% of terbinafine patients experienced a decrease in absolute lymphocyte counts below 1000/mm³ on multiple occasions [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C.
- **Handling:** Protect from light.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].
