# Editorial Product Information: Treosulfan

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been derived from the following Product Information (PI) source:
    - Australian (TGA): Trecondi (treosulfan) Product Information.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals.
- **Clinical Caution:** This synthesis is intended as an expert clinical reference and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** **Treosulfan** (a bifunctional alkylating agent pro-drug).
- **Brand Name:** Trecondi [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01AB02.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Powder for solution for infusion (1 g and 5 g vials).
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Link Medical Products Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Adults with Acute Myeloid Leukaemia (AML) or Myelodysplastic Syndrome (MDS):** Indicated in combination with fludarabine as part of conditioning treatment prior to allogeneic haematopoietic stem cell transplantation (alloHSCT) in patients at increased risk for standard conditioning therapies [Australian].
- **Paediatric Patients (Malignant and Non-Malignant):** Indicated for patients aged 1 month and older with malignant and non-malignant haematological diseases, in combination with fludarabine (with or without thiotepa), prior to alloHSCT [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Regimen:** 10 g/m² body surface area (BSA) per day via a 2-hour IV infusion.
- **Schedule:** Administered on three consecutive days (day -4, -3, -2) before stem cell infusion (day 0).
- **Total Dose:** 30 g/m².
- **Co-administration:** Fludarabine (30 mg/m²) is given for five consecutive days (day -6 to -2). **Treosulfan** should be administered before fludarabine on overlapping days.

### Paediatric Dosing (≥ 1 Month Old)
Dosing is adapted based on BSA to ensure comparable exposure across age groups:
- **BSA < 0.4 m²:** 10.0 g/m² per day.
- **BSA ≥ 0.4 to < 0.9 m²:** 12.0 g/m² per day.
- **BSA ≥ 0.9 m²:** 14.0 g/m² per day.
- **Schedule:** Three consecutive days (day -6, -5, -4) prior to transplant.

### Administration and Reconstitution
- **Infusion Duration:** 2 hours.
- **Reconstitution:** Must use pre-warmed (25°C–30°C) sodium chloride 4.5 mg/mL (0.45%) solution.
- **Final Concentration:** 50 mg/mL.
- **Handling:** **Treosulfan** is a cytotoxic agent; use Luer-lock fittings and avoid inhalation or skin contact. Pregnant personnel must be excluded from handling.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to **treosulfan**.
- Active, non-controlled infectious disease.
- Severe concomitant cardiac, lung, liver, or renal impairment.
- Fanconi anaemia and other DNA breakage repair disorders.
- Pregnancy and lactation.
- Concomitant administration of live vaccines [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Myelosuppression:** Profound pancytopenia is the intended therapeutic effect. Frequent monitoring of blood counts is required until haematopoietic recovery.
- **Secondary Malignancies:** **Treosulfan** is classified as a human carcinogen (IARC). Survivors of alloHSCT face a risk of second malignancies; the specific contribution of **treosulfan** is unknown.
- **Mucositis:** Oral mucositis (including high-grade) is very common. Prophylaxis (e.g., topical antimicrobials, hygiene) is recommended.
- **Seizures:** Reported in infants (≤ 4 months) with primary immunodeficiencies. Monitoring is required; clonazepam prophylaxis may be considered for children < 1 year.
- **Respiratory Toxicity:** Significant association with age in paediatrics; infants < 1 year (especially those with pre-existing infections) are at higher risk of Grade III/IV toxicity.
- **Skin Disorders:** Use of sodium bicarbonate-containing hydration may increase skin reactions (rash, dermatitis) due to pH-dependent activation of the drug. Diaper dermatitis can occur in small children; frequent nappy changes (every 6–8 hours) are required.
- **Extravasation:** **Treosulfan** is considered an irritant.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Summary Profile
The most frequent adverse reactions involve gastrointestinal and haematological systems.

| Adverse Reaction | Adult Frequency | Paediatric Frequency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Overall Infections** | 10.1% | 11.6% |
| **Nausea** | 38.0% | 26.4% |
| **Stomatitis/Mucositis**| 36.4% | 66.1% |
| **Vomiting** | 22.5% | 42.1% |
| **Diarrhoea** | 14.4% | 33.1% |
| **Abdominal Pain** | 9.6% | 17.4% |
| **Hepatotoxicity** | 0.3% | 26.4% |
| **Febrile Neutropenia** | 10.1% | 1.7% |
| **Bilirubin Increase** | 17.1% | 6.6% |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Veno-occlusive Liver Disease (VOD):** Reported in 0.8% of adults, primarily at higher dose levels (14 g/m²).
- **Septic Shock:** Frequency not known [Australian].
- **Cardiac Disorders:** Arrhythmias (1.0%), cardiac failure, and myocardial infarction have been reported in rare instances.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP Interactions:** In vitro data suggest **treosulfan** is a reversible inhibitor of CYP2C19 and CYP3A4. PBPK modelling predicts weak to moderate interactions. Avoid drugs with a narrow therapeutic index that are substrates for these enzymes during treatment.
- **Transporters:** Potential for P-glycoprotein (P-gp) inhibition at clinical concentrations.
- **Management:** To minimize interaction potential, dose concomitant medications 2 hours before or 8 hours after the 2-hour **treosulfan** infusion.
- **Live Vaccines:** Contraindicated due to the risk of systemic infection in immunocompromised patients.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category D. **Treosulfan** is contraindicated. It is genotoxic and likely teratogenic. Females of reproductive potential must have a negative pregnancy test within 7 days of initiation and use effective contraception during and for at least 6 months after treatment.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated during and for 6 months after the last dose [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Likely to cause irreversible infertility in men and women. Men should seek advice on cryo-conservation of sperm prior to therapy.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for patients > 1 month. Dosing must be adjusted by BSA (see Section 3). High incidence of hepatotoxicity and stomatitis compared to adults.
- **Elderly Use:** No specific dose adjustment is necessary in the elderly; however, geriatric patients were included in the "increased risk" adult population in clinical trials.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild to moderate impairment. Contraindicated in severe impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A pro-drug that spontaneously (non-enzymatically) converts under physiological pH and temperature into a monoepoxide intermediate and eventually L-diepoxybutane. These active epoxides alkylate DNA, inducing cross-links that deplete haematopoietic stem cells.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Exhibits broad antineoplastic and immunosuppressive effects (targeting T and NK cells).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Rapid; does not bind to plasma proteins. Very low blood-brain barrier penetration in adults, but higher exposure seen in juvenile models.
    - **Metabolism:** Spontaneous conversion; weak metabolism by CYP2D6.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 2 hours. 25–40% is excreted unchanged in the urine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Phase III Trial (Adults):** Compared **treosulfan**/fludarabine (FT10) to busulfan/fludarabine (FB2) in 551 patients with AML/MDS at increased risk.
- **Outcomes:** FT10 was non-inferior and statistically superior to FB2 regarding 2-year event-free survival (EFS). 2-year EFS was 65.7% for **treosulfan** vs 51.2% for busulfan (p < 0.001 for superiority).
- **Overall Survival:** 72.7% (treosulfan) vs 60.2% (busulfan) at 24 months.
- **Paediatric Trials:** Demonstrated 2-year overall survival of 85.7% in malignant diseases and 96.1% (1-year survival) in non-malignant diseases.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Vial:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Chemical and physical stability demonstrated for 24 hours at 25°C.
- **Microbiological Note:** Should be used immediately unless reconstituted under controlled aseptic conditions.
- **Precipitation Risk:** **Do not refrigerate** the reconstituted solution (2°C–8°C) as this may cause precipitation.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Poison Schedule S4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
