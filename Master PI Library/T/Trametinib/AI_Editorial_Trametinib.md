# Editorial Product Information: Trametinib

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **trametinib** for use by healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data, dosing guidelines, and safety information.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved labeling for definitive prescribing information.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Trametinib (as dimethyl sulfoxide).
- **Chemical Class:** Reversible allosteric inhibitor of mitogen-activated extracellular signal-regulated kinase 1 (MEK1) and kinase 2 (MEK2).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Mekinist** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01EE01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Film-coated tablets:** 0.5 mg (yellow) and 2 mg (pink) [Australian].
    - **Powder for oral solution:** 0.05 mg/mL (constituted) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Novartis Pharmaceuticals [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Unresectable or Metastatic Melanoma:**
    - As monotherapy for BRAF V600 mutation-positive Stage III or IV melanoma in patients who are intolerant to, or for whom BRAF inhibitors cannot be used [Australian].
    - In combination with **dabrafenib** for BRAF V600 mutation-positive unresectable Stage III or metastatic (Stage IV) melanoma [Australian].
- **Adjuvant Treatment of Melanoma:** In combination with **dabrafenib** for BRAF V600 mutation-positive melanoma with lymph node involvement following complete surgical resection [Australian].
- **Anaplastic Thyroid Cancer (ATC):** In combination with **dabrafenib** for locally advanced or metastatic BRAF V600 mutation-positive ATC with no satisfactory locoregional treatment options [Australian].
- **Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC):** In combination with **dabrafenib** for advanced BRAF V600 mutation-positive NSCLC [Australian].
- **Low-Grade Glioma (LGG):** In combination with **dabrafenib** for paediatric patients (1 year and older) with BRAF V600E mutation requiring systemic therapy [Australian].
- **High-Grade Glioma (HGG):** In combination with **dabrafenib** for paediatric patients (1 year and older) with BRAF V600E mutation who have progressed following prior treatment [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- The recommended dose is **2 mg** orally once daily, whether used as monotherapy or in combination with **dabrafenib** [Australian].
- **Administration:** Must be taken on an empty stomach, at least **1 hour before or 2 hours after** a meal.
- **Interchangeability:** Tablets and oral solution are **not** fully bioequivalent; frequent switching is discouraged [Australian].

### Dosing by Indication
- **Adjuvant Melanoma:** Treatment is limited to a maximum of 12 months [Australian].
- **Paediatric Glioma:** Dosing is weight-based (see below).

### Paediatric Dosing (Glioma)
- **Tablets:** For patients $\ge$ 26 kg. Doses range from 1 mg to 2 mg daily based on weight tiers (26–37 kg, 38–50 kg, and $\ge$ 51 kg) [Australian].
- **Oral Solution:** For patients $\ge$ 8 kg. Dosing is tiered by body weight (starting at 0.3 mg daily for 8 kg) [Australian].

### Missed Dose
- If a dose is missed, it should only be taken if the next scheduled dose is more than 12 hours away.

### Titration and Modification
- Dose reductions should occur in increments: 2 mg $\rightarrow$ 1.5 mg $\rightarrow$ 1 mg. Doses below 1 mg daily are not recommended for adults [Australian].
- **Pyrexia:** If temperature $\ge$ 38°C, withhold **trametinib** (and **dabrafenib** if used in combination). Restart at the same or reduced dose once symptom-free for 24 hours [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **trametinib** dimethyl sulfoxide or any of the product excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **BRAF Mutation Status:** Confirmation of BRAF V600 mutation using a validated test is mandatory before initiating therapy. **Trametinib** is not indicated for wild-type BRAF tumours [Australian].
- **Haemorrhage:** Major events, including fatal intracranial and gastrointestinal haemorrhage, have occurred. Permanently discontinue for Grade 4 events or Grade 3 events that do not improve [Australian].
- **Cardiac Effects:**
    - **LVEF Reduction:** Monitor LVEF at baseline, one month after initiation, and then every three months. Withhold if LVEF decreases $\ge$ 10% from baseline and is below the lower limit of normal [Australian].
    - **Bradycardia and QT Prolongation:** Reported especially in combination therapy.
- **Ocular Toxicity:**
    - **Retinal Vein Occlusion (RVO):** May lead to permanent vision loss. **Trametinib** is not recommended in patients with a history of RVO. Discontinue permanently if RVO is diagnosed [Australian].
    - **Retinal Pigment Epithelial Detachment (RPED):** Withhold therapy; may resume at a lower dose if resolved within three weeks [Australian].
- **Respiratory Toxicity:** Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD) or pneumonitis. Permanently discontinue if ILD/pneumonitis is diagnosed [Australian].
- **Skin Toxicity:** Severe cutaneous adverse reactions (SCARs), including Stevens-Johnson syndrome and DRESS, have been reported.
- **Venous Thromboembolism (VTE):** Deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism have been observed.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Categories
*   **Very Common (>10%):** Rash, diarrhoea, fatigue, peripheral oedema, nausea, dermatitis acneiform, pyrexia, chills, headache, arthralgia, hypertension, cough, vomiting, constipation, abdominal pain, and dry skin.
*   **Common (1–10%):** Anaemia, dehydration, vision blurred, periorbital oedema, bradycardia, lymphoedema, epistaxis, pneumonitis, stomatitis, skin fissures, erythema, and increased transaminases.
*   **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Retinal vein occlusion (RVO), retinal detachment, cardiac failure, interstitial lung disease, GI perforation, colitis, and rhabdomyolysis.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Fatal intracranial haemorrhage.
- New primary non-cutaneous malignancies (when used with **dabrafenib**).
- Severe pyrexia complicated by hypotension, dehydration, or renal failure.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** **Trametinib** is primarily metabolised via deacetylation by hydrolytic enzymes (carboxylesterases). It is **not** a significant substrate, inhibitor, or inducer of major CYP450 enzymes at clinical exposures [Australian].
- **Transporters:** **Trametinib** is an in vitro substrate of P-gp and BCRP, but clinical relevance is low due to high passive permeability.
- **Combination with Dabrafenib:** Co-administration results in a small (12–16%) change in exposure for both drugs, which is not considered clinically significant [Australian].
- **Hormonal Contraceptives:** When used with **dabrafenib**, hormonal contraceptives may be rendered ineffective; non-hormonal methods are required [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. **Trametinib** can cause fetal harm. Animal studies showed decreased fetal weights and increased post-implantation loss at sub-therapeutic exposures.
- **Contraception:** Females of reproductive potential must use effective contraception during treatment and for **4 months** after the last dose [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** No data available. Discontinuation of breastfeeding or the drug is advised based on the importance of the drug to the mother.
- **Fertility:** Animal data suggests **trametinib** may impair female fertility; no effects were observed on male reproductive tissues [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for LGG and HGG in patients $\ge$ 1 year old. Safety and efficacy in children < 1 year or as monotherapy have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No initial dose adjustment required for patients $\ge$ 65 years. However, these patients experience higher rates of dose interruptions and reductions [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No adjustment for mild or moderate impairment. Data are lacking for severe impairment; use with caution [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for mild impairment. Use with caution in moderate to severe impairment, though data suggests limited impact on exposure [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Trametinib** is an allosteric inhibitor of MEK1 and MEK2. It blocks the RAS/RAF/MEK/ERK pathway, which is frequently hyperactivated in BRAF-mutant cancers.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ is 1.5 hours. Bioavailability is 72%. High-fat meals significantly reduce $C_{max}$ (70%) and AUC (10%) [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** High plasma protein binding (97.4%). Large volume of distribution (1,060 L).
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily deacetylation.
    - **Elimination:** Long terminal half-life of 5.3 days. Excreted mainly in faeces (>80%) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Melanoma (METRIC Study):** Monotherapy **trametinib** significantly improved median PFS (4.8 months) compared to chemotherapy (1.5 months; HR 0.45, p<0.0001) [Australian].
- **Melanoma Adjuvant (COMBI-AD):** Combination therapy showed a 53% risk reduction in relapse or death (HR 0.47) compared to placebo [Australian].
- **NSCLC:** Combination therapy achieved an ORR of 61.1% in treatment-naïve patients and 66.7% in previously treated patients [Australian].
- **Paediatric Glioma (G2201):** In LGG, combination therapy showed an ORR of 46.6% compared to 10.8% with carboplatin/vincristine (p<0.001) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Film-coated tablets:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate). Protect from light and moisture. Once opened, the bottle is stable for 30 days at temperatures up to 30°C [Australian].
- **Powder for oral solution:** Store at 2°C to 8°C before reconstitution. After reconstitution, store below 25°C; discard any unused solution after 35 days [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** **Schedule 4** (Prescription Only Medicine).
