# Editorial Product Information: Tipranavir

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of tipranavir for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the United States (FDA) documentation was available for inclusion; therefore, all information should be considered specific to the United States requirements unless otherwise stated.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tipranavir (a non-peptidic protease inhibitor of the 4-hydroxy-5,6-dihydro-2-pyrone sulfonamide class).
- **Brand Name(s):** **APTIVUS** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 250 mg pink, oblong, liquid-filled soft gelatin capsules [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Boehringer Ingelheim Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **HIV-1 Infection:** Indicated for combination antiretroviral treatment in treatment-experienced adult and pediatric patients (weighing 36 kg or more) who are infected with HIV-1 strains resistant to more than one protease inhibitor [US-approved].
- **Important Limitations of Use:** 
    - Tipranavir must be co-administered with ritonavir to achieve therapeutic effect.
    - It is not recommended for treatment-naïve patients [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** 500 mg (two 250 mg capsules) co-administered with 200 mg ritonavir, taken twice daily [US].
- **Pediatric Dosing (Weight $\ge$ 36 kg):** 500 mg co-administered with 200 mg ritonavir twice daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Must be taken with food [US].
    - Capsules must be swallowed whole; they should not be opened or chewed [US].
    - Pediatric patients must be assessed for their ability to swallow capsules before prescribing [US].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, patients should take it as soon as they remember and then return to their normal schedule. Patients should not double the next dose [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Patients with moderate or severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class B or C) [US].
- **Drug Interactions:** Co-administration with drugs highly dependent on CYP3A for clearance or potent CYP3A inducers, including but not limited to:
    - **Alpha 1-adrenoreceptor antagonists:** Alfuzosin.
    - **Antiarrhythmics:** Amiodarone, bepridil, flecainide, propafenone, quinidine.
    - **Antimycobacterials:** Rifampin.
    - **Ergot derivatives:** Dihydroergotamine, ergonovine, ergotamine, methylergonovine.
    - **GI motility agents:** Cisapride.
    - **Herbal products:** St. John’s wort.
    - **HMG CoA reductase inhibitors:** Lovastatin, simvastatin.
    - **Antipsychotics:** Pimozide, lurasidone.
    - **PDE-5 inhibitors:** Sildenafil (when used for pulmonary arterial hypertension).
    - **Sedatives/hypnotics:** Oral midazolam, triazolam [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings [US]:**
    - **Hepatotoxicity:** Clinical hepatitis and hepatic decompensation, including fatalities, have been reported. Extra vigilance is required in patients co-infected with chronic hepatitis B or C.
    - **Intracranial Hemorrhage (ICH):** Reports of both fatal and non-fatal ICH have been documented in patients receiving tipranavir.
- **Hepatic Monitoring:** Liver function tests (LFTs) must be performed prior to initiation and monitored frequently during therapy. Treatment should be discontinued if ALT/AST levels exceed 10x the upper limit of normal (ULN), or if they are 5–10x ULN accompanied by elevated total bilirubin [US].
- **Sulfa Allergy:** Tipranavir contains a sulfonamide moiety; use with caution in patients with known sulfonamide allergies [US].
- **Bleeding Risk:** Use with caution in patients at risk of increased bleeding (trauma, surgery) or those taking anticoagulants/antiplatelets. Inhibition of platelet aggregation has been observed *in vitro* [US].
- **Rash:** Reported in approximately 10% of patients. Severe cases (accompanied by fever or systemic symptoms) require discontinuation. There is an increased risk of rash in women taking estrogen-based contraceptives [US].
- **Metabolic Effects:** New onset or exacerbation of diabetes mellitus, hyperglycemia, and significant elevations in total cholesterol and triglycerides may occur. Lipid monitoring is required [US].
- **Immune Reconstitution Syndrome:** May occur, potentially leading to inflammatory responses to residual opportunistic infections or autoimmune disorders [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions of moderate to severe intensity (Grades 2–4) reported in $\ge$ 2% of adult subjects [US]:
- **Very Common (>10%):** Diarrhea (15.0%).
- **Common (1–10%):** Nausea (8.5%), pyrexia (7.5%), vomiting (5.9%), fatigue (5.7%), headache (5.2%), abdominal pain (4.4%), hypertriglyceridemia (3.9%), anemia (3.3%), rash (3.1%), weight decreased (3.1%), hyperlipidemia (2.5%), myalgia (2.3%), and dehydration (2.1%).
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Hepatotoxicity (including hepatic failure), intracranial hemorrhage, and pancreatitis [US].
- **Pediatric Considerations:** The adverse reaction profile is generally similar to adults, though rash was reported more frequently in pediatric subjects (21% all grades) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Profile:** Tipranavir (with ritonavir) is a potent CYP3A inhibitor and can also induce or inhibit P-glycoprotein (P-gp). It also induces CYP1A2 and CYP2C19 [US].
- **Significant Interactions:**
    - **Nucleoside Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors (NRTIs):** Significant reductions in AUC for abacavir (~40%) and zidovudine (~35%). Didanosine should be separated from tipranavir dosing by at least 2 hours [US].
    - **Protease Inhibitors (PIs):** Co-administration with other PIs (fosamprenavir, lopinavir, saquinavir) is generally not recommended due to significantly decreased concentrations of the co-administered PI [US].
    - **Integrase Inhibitors:** Significant reduction in dolutegravir levels; consult dolutegravir PI for dosing [US].
    - **Antifungals:** Fluconazole increases tipranavir levels; doses of fluconazole >200 mg/day are not recommended [US].
    - **Warfarin:** Requires frequent INR monitoring [US].
    - **PDE-5 Inhibitors:** Increased risk of toxicity (hypotension, visual changes). Strictly limit doses (e.g., sildenafil $\le$ 25 mg within 48 hours) [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Pregnancy Category:** Not specified as a lettered category in the latest US revision, but the document provides a Risk Summary [US].
    - **Human Data:** Limited data (approx. 17 live births) show no birth defects to date. Tipranavir crosses the placenta [US].
    - **Animal Data:** Fetal toxicities (decreased weight, ossification issues) were observed in rats and rabbits, but only at maternally toxic doses [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Breastfeeding is not recommended for HIV-infected mothers to avoid postnatal transmission of the virus. It is unknown if tipranavir is excreted in human milk, though it is present in rat milk [US].
- **Fertility:** No effect on fertility or early embryonic development was observed in rats at doses equivalent to human exposure [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for patients weighing $\ge$ 36 kg. Safety and effectiveness established in patients $\ge$ 2 years of age, but use is restricted by the lack of a suitable pediatric formulation for those under 36 kg [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include enough subjects $\ge$ 65 years to determine if they respond differently. Use caution due to potential decreased organ function [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is expected as renal clearance of tipranavir is negligible [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild (Child-Pugh A):** Use with caution; monitor closely [US].
    - **Moderate/Severe (Child-Pugh B/C):** Contraindicated [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A non-peptidic HIV-1 protease inhibitor that binds to the enzyme's active site, preventing the cleavage of Gag and Gag-Pol polyproteins, resulting in the production of immature, non-infectious virions [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Limited absorption; steady state achieved after 7–10 days. Food increases tolerability and is required for specific ritonavir formulations [US].
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (>99.9%) [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized by CYP3A4. When co-administered with ritonavir, tipranavir metabolism is minimal [US].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily fecal (82.3%); renal excretion is minimal (4.4%). Mean elimination half-life is approximately 4.8–6 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adult Studies (RESIST 1 and 2):** Two 48-week, randomized, open-label trials in highly treatment-experienced patients. Tipranavir/ritonavir (500/200 mg BID) was compared to a comparator protease inhibitor (CPI) boosted with ritonavir. 
- **Outcomes:** 33.8% of the tipranavir group achieved a $\ge$ 1 log reduction in HIV-1 RNA compared to 14.9% in the CPI group at 48 weeks. Response was significantly improved when combined with enfuvirtide [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Bottles:** Must be stored in a refrigerator at $2^\circ\text{C}$ to $8^\circ\text{C}$ ($36^\circ\text{F}$ to $46^\circ\text{F}$) [US].
- **Opened Bottles:** May be stored at room temperature ($20^\circ\text{C}$ to $25^\circ\text{C}$ or $68^\circ\text{F}$ to $77^\circ\text{F}$) for up to 60 days. Any remaining capsules after 60 days must be discarded [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
