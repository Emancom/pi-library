# Editorial Product Information: Tirofiban

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a single, clinically focused reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the Australian source was available for analysis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tirofiban hydrochloride monohydrate (a non-peptide glycoprotein IIb/IIIa receptor antagonist).
- **Brand Name:** **AGGRASTAT** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Concentrate for IV infusion; each mL contains 0.281 mg tirofiban hydrochloride monohydrate (equivalent to 0.25 mg tirofiban). Supplied in a 50 mL glass vial.
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only (must be diluted).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Correvio Australia Pty Ltd; Distributed by Aspen Pharmacare Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Coronary Syndrome:** Indicated for use in combination with heparin for patients with unstable angina or non-Q-wave myocardial infarction to prevent cardiac ischaemic events [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Initial Loading Infusion:** 0.4 µg/kg/min for 30 minutes.
- **Maintenance Infusion:** 0.1 µg/kg/min.
- **Concomitant Therapy:** Should be administered with heparin (e.g., 5000 U bolus at start of tirofiban, followed by ~1000 U/hr titrated to an APTT approximately twice the normal value). Most clinical trial patients also received aspirin unless contraindicated.

### Administration Instructions
- **Dilution Required:** The concentrate must be diluted to a final concentration of 0.05 mg/mL prior to use. 
    - *Example:* Withdraw 50 mL from a 250 mL bag of 0.9% saline or 5% glucose and replace with 50 mL (one vial) of **AGGRASTAT** concentrate.
- **Equipment:** Use a calibrated infusion device.
- **Incompatibilities:** Do not administer in the same IV line as diazepam.
- **Duration:** Typically continued for 48 to 108 hours. Infusion may continue through angiography and for 12–24 hours post-angioplasty/atherectomy.

### Missed Dose
- Not applicable as this is a continuous hospital-based IV infusion.

## 4. Contraindications
**AGGRASTAT** is contraindicated in patients with:
- Known hypersensitivity to any component of the product.
- Active internal bleeding or history of bleeding diathesis within the last 30 days.
- History of intracranial haemorrhage, neoplasm, arteriovenous malformation, or aneurysm.
- History of thrombocytopenia following prior tirofiban exposure.
- Stroke within the last 30 days or any history of haemorrhagic stroke.
- Major surgery or severe physical trauma within the last month (including epidural/spinal anaesthesia).
- Symptoms or findings suggestive of aortic dissection.
- Severe uncontrolled hypertension (SBP >180 mmHg and/or DBP >110 mmHg).
- Concomitant use of another parenteral GP IIb/IIIa inhibitor.
- Acute pericarditis.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Bleeding Risk:** Most common complication. Use with extreme caution with other drugs affecting haemostasis. Fatal bleedings have been reported rarely.
- **Femoral Access:** Minor increases in bleeding occur at the arterial access site. Avoid Seldinger (through-and-through) technique; puncture only the anterior wall of the femoral artery.
- **Thrombocytopenia:** May occur during initial treatment or readministration. If platelet count drops <90,000 cells/mm³, exclude pseudothrombocytopenia. Discontinue **AGGRASTAT** and heparin if thrombocytopenia is confirmed.
- **Monitoring Requirements:**
    - Platelet count, haemoglobin, and haematocrit (prior to treatment, 6 hours post-loading, and daily thereafter).
    - APTT (to monitor heparin effect).
    - Activated clotting time (ACT) for sheath removal (ensure <180 seconds or 2–6 hours after stopping heparin).

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Bleeding
- **Very Common (>10%):** Minor bleeding (10.5% vs 8.0% heparin alone) [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Major bleeding (1.4%), transfusion requirement (4.0%).
- **Post-Marketing/Rare:** Intracranial, retroperitoneal, pulmonary (alveolar), and spinal-epidural haematoma.

### Non-Bleeding Effects
| Adverse Effect | Frequency (Aggrastat + Heparin) | Frequency (Control) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Nausea | 1.7% | 1.4% |
| Fever | 1.5% | 1.1% |
| Headache | 1.1% | 1.2% |

### Other Reactions
- **Hypersensitivity:** Rash, urticaria, and severe anaphylactic reactions (can occur on day 1 or upon readministration).
- **Thrombocytopenia:** Acute/severe drops in platelet counts, sometimes associated with chills and low-grade fever.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic:** Increased bleeding risk when used with heparin, aspirin, and other anticoagulants (e.g., warfarin) or thrombolytics.
- **Non-interactants:** No clinically significant interactions were observed with beta-blockers, calcium channel blockers, NSAIDs, nitrates, digoxin, diuretics, or benzodiazepines (among others).
- **Compatibility:** **AGGRASTAT** may be co-administered with heparin in the same line.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category B1**. No adequate/controlled human studies. Animal studies showed no maternal or foetal toxicity. Use only if potential benefit outweighs risk.
- **Breastfeeding:** Excreted in rat milk; human excretion is unknown. A decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the mother's need for the medication.
- **Fertility:** No effect on fertility or reproductive performance in animal models (rats).

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children have not been established.
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required. However, elderly patients (≥65 years) have a higher incidence of bleeding and non-bleeding adverse events compared to younger patients. Plasma clearance is ~19–26% lower in the elderly.
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Severe (CrCl <30 mL/min):** Reduce loading and maintenance infusion rates by **50%**.
    - **Haemodialysis:** Tirofiban is removed by haemodialysis.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No significant difference in plasma clearance for mild to moderate hepatic insufficiency; no data for severe cases.
- **Gender:** Clearance is similar between males and females; no dose adjustment required based on sex.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Reversible antagonist of the GP IIb/IIIa receptor. It prevents fibrinogen from binding to the receptor, thereby blocking platelet cross-linking and aggregation.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Produces >90% inhibition of ADP-induced platelet aggregation. Platelet function returns to baseline within hours of discontinuation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Low protein binding (35% unbound); volume of distribution 22–42 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Minimal metabolism; primarily excreted unchanged.
    - **Elimination:** 66% in urine, 23% in faeces. Plasma half-life is approximately 1.4 to 2.2 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **PRISM-PLUS Trial:** In patients with unstable angina/non-Q-wave MI, tirofiban + heparin showed a 32% risk reduction in the composite endpoint (death, MI, or refractory ischaemia) at 7 days compared to heparin alone. Benefit was maintained at 30 days and 6 months.
- **PRISM Trial:** Evaluated tirofiban alone vs heparin alone. Tirofiban showed a significant reduction in risk at 48 hours (33%), but this benefit was not statistically significant at 7 or 30 days.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Concentrate:** Store below 30°C. Do not freeze. Protect from light during storage.
- **Diluted Solution:** Use as soon as practicable. If necessary, may be stored at 2–8°C for up to 24 hours.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **S4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
