# Editorial Product Information: Tapentadol

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of tapentadol immediate-release (IR) for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or clinical guidelines required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to local regulatory documents for final prescribing authority.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tapentadol (as hydrochloride).
- **Chemical Class:** Centrally acting synthetic analgesic (mu-opioid receptor agonist and noradrenaline reuptake inhibitor).
- **Brand Name(s):** PALEXIA® IR [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Immediate-release film-coated tablets in 50 mg (white), 75 mg (pale yellow), and 100 mg (pale pink) strengths [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Seqirus Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Severe Pain:** Indicated for the short-term management of severe pain in instances where alternative treatment options (e.g., non-opioid analgesics) have failed, are not tolerated, are contraindicated, or are otherwise inappropriate [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** Dosing must be individualised based on pain severity, prior treatment history, and monitoring capability. The recommended starting dose is 50 mg, 75 mg, or 100 mg every 4 to 6 hours [Australian].
- **First Day Protocol:** On the first day of treatment, a second dose may be administered as early as one hour after the initial dose if adequate analgesia is not reached [Australian].
- **Maximum Dosing:** 
    - Total starting daily dose (Day 1): Not recommended to exceed 700 mg [Australian].
    - Maintenance daily dose: Not recommended to exceed 600 mg [Australian].
- **Administration:** Tablets should be swallowed whole with sufficient liquid and may be taken with or without food [Australian].
- **Discontinuation:** Abrupt cessation in physically dependent patients may trigger withdrawal. Doses should be tapered gradually (e.g., 10% to 25% reduction every 2 to 4 weeks) [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to tapentadol or any excipients [Australian].
- Severe or acute respiratory disease and respiratory depression [Australian].
- Confirmed or suspected paralytic ileus [Australian].
- Acute intoxication with alcohol, hypnotics, centrally acting analgesics, or psychotropic medications [Australian].
- Concurrent use of Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs) or use within the preceding 14 days [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings [Australian]:**
    - **Hazardous and Harmful Use:** Risk of addiction, abuse, and misuse, which can lead to fatal overdose.
    - **Respiratory Depression:** Potential for serious, life-threatening, or fatal respiratory depression.
    - **CNS Depressant Interactions:** Concomitant use with benzodiazepines, alcohol, or other CNS depressants may cause profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death.
- **General Warnings:**
    - **Chronic Non-Cancer Pain:** Use is generally not recommended for long-term management of chronic non-cancer pain [Australian].
    - **Seizures:** Use with caution in patients with a history of seizure disorders or those taking medications that lower the seizure threshold [Australian].
    - **Serotonin Syndrome:** Risk of serotonin syndrome when used with other serotonergic agents (SSRIs, SNRIs, triptans) [Australian].
    - **Intracranial Pressure:** Use with caution in head injury or brain tumours due to potential effects on respiratory drive and CO2 retention [Australian].
    - **Adrenal Insufficiency:** Reported with opioid use, particularly after long-term exposure [Australian].
    - **Sleep-Related Breathing Disorders:** Can cause central sleep apnoea (CSA) in a dose-dependent manner [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Dizziness, somnolence, headache, nausea, vomiting [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Decreased appetite, anxiety, confusional state, hallucinations, sleep disorders, tremors, flushing, constipation, diarrhoea, dyspepsia, dry mouth, pruritus, hyperhidrosis, rash, muscle spasms, fatigue, and asthenia [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Depressed mood, agitation, memory impairment, presyncope, sedation, ataxia, visual disturbance, increased heart rate, decreased blood pressure, respiratory depression, and drug withdrawal syndrome [Australian].
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Drug hypersensitivity, thinking abnormal, convulsion, depressed level of consciousness, and impaired gastric emptying [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **MAOIs:** Contraindicated; risk of additive effects on noradrenaline leading to adverse cardiovascular events [Australian].
- **CNS Depressants:** Additive effects increase risks of sedation and respiratory depression. Includes alcohol, benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, and cannabis [Australian].
- **Serotonergic Drugs:** Potential for serotonin syndrome when combined with SSRIs, SNRIs, or TCAs [Australian].
- **Mixed Agonist/Antagonists:** Caution required when using agents like buprenorphine or pentazocine, as they may reduce analgesic efficacy or precipitate withdrawal [Australian].
- **Anticholinergics:** May increase the risk of urinary retention and severe constipation [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category C. Tapentadol should only be used if the potential benefit justifies the risk to the fetus. Use during labor and delivery is not recommended due to the risk of respiratory depression in the neonate. Long-term use may cause Neonatal Opioid Withdrawal Syndrome (NOWS) [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Use is not recommended. Physicochemical data suggest tapentadol is excreted in breast milk, and risks to the suckling child cannot be excluded [Australian].
- **Fertility:** In animal models, high doses associated with maternal toxicity reduced implantations and increased pre- and post-implantation losses; clinical relevance is unknown [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for patients under 18 years of age due to insufficient safety and efficacy data [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Generally, no dose adjustment is required for patients with normal renal/hepatic function, but caution is advised as they are more susceptible to decreased organ function and adverse effects [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No adjustment for mild or moderate impairment. Use is not recommended in severe renal impairment [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild:** No dose adjustment required.
    - **Moderate:** Initiate at 50 mg no more frequently than every 8 hours (max 3 doses in 24 hours).
    - **Severe:** Use is not recommended [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Dual action: mu-opioid receptor agonist and noradrenaline reuptake inhibitor. Analgesic effects are exerted directly without active metabolites [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; absolute bioavailability ~32%. Peak plasma concentration (Cmax) at ~1.25 hours [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Widely distributed (Vz ~540 L); protein binding is low (~20%) [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via Phase 2 glucuronidation (UGT1A6, UGT1A9, UGT2B7). Phase 1 oxidative metabolism is a minor pathway (~2%) [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Excreted almost exclusively (99%) via kidneys. Terminal half-life is approximately 4 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Orthopaedic Surgery (Bunionectomy):** Pivotal trials (KF5503/32 and KF5503/37) demonstrated tapentadol IR (50 mg, 75 mg, 100 mg) was significantly superior to placebo in reducing pain intensity (SPID48) with a numerical trend of increasing efficacy with higher doses [Australian].
- **Abdominal Surgery (Hysterectomy):** Trial KF5503/35 showed all tapentadol IR dose groups provided significantly greater pain relief over 24 hours compared to placebo [Australian].
- **End-Stage Joint Disease:** Trial KF5503/33 showed 50 mg and 75 mg tapentadol IR were superior to placebo over a 5-day period for chronic pain flares [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 30°C [Australian].
- Shelf life information is contained within the specific product packaging [Australian].
