# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonized editorial overview of Levocarnitine injection, synthesized for healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this review, only the United States (FDA) documentation for Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) was provided for analysis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/amino acid derivative; (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Names:** Not specified in available sources (Generic Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US]).
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Sterile solution for intravenous use: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:**
    - **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
    - **Initial Crisis Management:** A loading dose is often administered in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Doses should be given every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically in the range of 50 mg/kg, though higher doses may be required. The maximum recorded dose is 300 mg/kg [US].

- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
    - **Administration:** Administer as a 2 to 3 minute slow bolus into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
    - **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of treatment [US].

### Administration and Monitoring
- **Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Preparation:** Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration before use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including **anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm**, have been reported. These often occur within minutes of administration, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks/benefits before re-administration [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine may cause seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulation Caution):** While this PI covers the injection, it is noted that safety/efficacy of oral levocarnitine in renal impairment is unverified. Chronic high-dose oral use in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO) [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** In ESRD patients, therapy should be prompted by trough concentrations below the normal range (40–50 µmol/L) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions in ESRD patients are often confounded by the underlying pathology.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects (Regardless of Causality) [US] |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site reaction (33–59%), Pain (30–49%), Flu syndrome (25–40%), Pharyngitis (15–33%), Headache (22–37%), Diarrhea (16–35%), Hypertension (20–21%), Vomiting (15–21%), Nausea (5–12%), Abdominal pain (5–21%), Cough increase (9–18%), Dizziness (10–18%). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Fever, Asthenia, Chest pain, Tachycardia, Hypotension, Hypercalcemia, Hyperkalemia, Peripheral edema, Depression, Dyspnea, Rhinitis, Pruritus, Kidney failure. |
| **Serious/Notable** | **Anaphylaxis**, **Seizures**, Arrhythmia, Atrial fibrillation, Hemorrhage [US]. |

*Note: Body odor and gastritis have also been reported as less frequent reactions [US].*

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. **Recommendation:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned a letter, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after administration. For nursing mothers, the benefits to the mother must be weighed against the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impairment in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is calculated by weight (50 mg/kg), similar to adults. See Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically in ESRD patients undergoing dialysis; however, caution is advised regarding metabolite accumulation with the oral form (see Section 5) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, IV administration increases plasma carnitine concentrations. Normal plasma free carnitine is 40–50 µmol/L; levels below 20 µmol/L at one week post-term are considered deficient [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x weekly), trough concentrations increased from 21.3 µmol/L at baseline to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While carnitine levels effectively increase, the impact on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of an opened vial, as the formulation is preservative-free [US].

- **United States:** Classified as **Rx-only** [US].
