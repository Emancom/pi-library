# Editorial Product Information: thrombin

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **thrombin**
- Chemical class: bovine-derived thrombin protein (activated prothrombin produced from bovine prothrombin)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Topical application to bleeding tissue surface only (do not inject)

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval/registration: 1986 [US]
- Date of last revision/update of available source PI: Revised 5/2026 [US]
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources
- Registration/approval dates for other jurisdictions: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Aid hemostasis for oozing blood and minor bleeding from capillaries and small venules when the bleeding site is accessible and control by standard surgical techniques (suture, ligature, cautery) is ineffective or impractical. (Indication stated in available sources.)  
- May be used in conjunction with absorbable gelatin sponge or absorbable gelatin powder delivery systems to assist hemostasis.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Reconstitute with sterile isotonic saline to recommended concentrations of 1,000 to 2,000 International Units (IU) per mL for routine use.
- For profuse bleeding (e.g., abraded liver or spleen surfaces), concentrations at approximately 1,000 IU/mL may be required.
- For general use in procedures such as plastic surgery, dental extractions, skin grafting, solutions of approximately 100 IU/mL are frequently used.
- Thrombin may also be used in dry form applied to oozing surfaces.

### Dosing by indication / special applications
- Use with absorbable gelatin sponge: saturate sponge strips with prepared thrombin solution, remove trapped air by kneading, and apply saturated sponge to bleeding area; hold in place until hemostasis occurs.
- Use with absorbable gelatin powder delivery systems: reconstitute and prepare according to the delivery system instructions; bleeding control may vary with thrombin concentration within the delivery system.
- Topical nasal (epistaxis) use: prepare solution to desired strength and apply to nasal mucosa via appropriate nasal delivery device; device may be removed immediately or held briefly after application.

### Administration instructions
- For topical use on bleeding tissue only. **Do not inject.**
- Surface should be sponged (not wiped) free of blood prior to application to optimize contact. The most effective hemostasis occurs when thrombin mixes freely with blood on the surface.
- Application methods: spray, flooding with syringe and small gauge needle, or use of saturated absorbable gelatin sponge. Avoid sponging treated surfaces after application so the clot remains intact.
- Reconstitution: transfer sterile isotonic saline diluent into the thrombin container using a sterile syringe or sterile transfer device; prepare intermediate strengths by diluting to desired IU/mL.
- Use reconstituted solution promptly. If necessary, reconstituted solution may be refrigerated at 2°C–8°C for up to 24 hours or stored at room temperature for up to 8 hours after reconstitution.

### Titration schedules
- Prepare concentration appropriate for the site and severity of bleeding (examples above); no formal titration schedule specified.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not applicable for single-use topical haemostatic application.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to thrombin, its excipients, or material of bovine origin.  
- Presence of known or suspected antibodies to bovine thrombin and/or factor V — do not re-expose such patients to thrombin.  
- Administration by injection into the circulatory system (intravenous, intra-arterial) — **do not inject** (use is topical only).  
- Use for treatment of severe or brisk arterial bleeding is contraindicated (not appropriate for this product).  
- Any other contraindications: Not specified in available sources.

(Where additional jurisdiction-specific contraindications exist, they are not specified in the available source material.)

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warning
- **Severe bleeding and thrombosis complications:** Development of antibodies to bovine thrombin may lead to thrombosis. Development of antibodies to factor V may cross-react with human factor V and cause bleeding due to acquired factor V deficiency. Do not re-expose patients with known or suspected antibodies to bovine thrombin and/or factor V. Apply topically only. **Do not inject.** [US]

### General warnings and precautions
- Hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylactic and anaphylactoid reactions, have been reported. Institute intensive supportive measures and treat symptoms; secure airway and ensure adequate respiratory exchange when needed.
- Thrombin causes thrombosis if it enters the circulatory system. Apply topically only; injection may cause extensive intravascular clotting or death.
- Immunogenicity: inhibitory or neutralizing antibodies may develop following exposure and interfere with hemostasis. If antibodies to bovine thrombin and/or factor V are known or suspected, do not re-expose.
- Do not administer to patients with a history of hypersensitivity to thrombin or bovine-derived materials.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor patients for abnormal coagulation laboratory values (including PT/INR and aPTT), bleeding, or thrombosis following exposure.
- Be vigilant for signs of acquired factor V deficiency (bleeding) or thromboembolic complications (thrombosis).

### Specific clinical precautions
- Avoid application to severe brisk arterial bleeding (not indicated).
- When used with gelatin sponges or powder delivery systems, ensure proper saturation and removal of trapped air to optimize hemostatic effect.
- Use caution and clinical judgment in elderly patients; dose selection should be cautious because of possible decreased organ function and comorbid disease.
- Reconstitution and use should follow sterile technique.

## 7. Adverse Effects
- All adverse reactions listed are those reported in the available source PI. Frequencies are presented where data were provided; where frequency is not stated, frequency is reported as "not known."

### Key adverse effects and frequency information (from clinical trials and postmarketing reports)
- Most common adverse reactions (incidence ≥ 2% in aggregated reports): hypersensitivity, bleeding, anemia, post‑operative wound infection, thromboembolic events, hypotension, pyrexia, tachycardia, thrombocytopenia.
- Clinical trial-observed frequencies (example results from randomized trials):
  - Cardiac events: 18% (reported in pooled trial population)
  - Hypersensitivity: 17% (pooled trial)
  - Other infections: 15% (pooled trial)
  - Bleeding: 11% (pooled trial)
  - Postoperative wound infection: 10% (pooled trial)
  - Thromboembolic events: 5% (pooled trial)
- Seroconversion / immunogenicity rates reported in trials (examples):
  - In one trial, among 200 patients evaluated, 10 (5%) were antibody-positive at baseline and 43 (21.5%) after treatment; seroconversion rate in the bovine thrombin group was 18.4%.
  - In another study, 16/126 (12.7%) demonstrated seroconversion for at least one of the four antibodies assayed; anti-bovine thrombin 10/126 (7.94%); anti-bovine factor V/Va 12/126 (9.52%); anti-human thrombin 3/126 (2.38%); anti-human factor V/Va 0/126 (0%).
  - In a repeat-exposure study in diabetic foot ulcers, no serious adverse reactions related to treatment were reported.

### Postmarketing serious adverse reactions
- Anaphylactic reactions (including anaphylactic/anaphylactoid responses)
- Prolonged prothrombin time (PT)
- Prolonged activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT)
- Disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC)
- Acquired factor V deficiency
- Post-procedural hematoma
- Wound swelling
- Staphylococcal wound infection

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- Pregnancy:
  - Animal reproduction studies: Not conducted (no data available).
  - Fetal risk: It is not known whether topical thrombin can cause fetal harm when administered to a pregnant woman. Thrombin should be given to a pregnant woman only if clearly needed.
  - Pregnancy category systems: No jurisdictional pregnancy category is stated in the available source. [US: narrative risk statement provided; no letter category specified]
- Breastfeeding:
  - It is not known whether thrombin is excreted in human milk. Consider benefits of breastfeeding, the clinical need for thrombin in the mother, and potential adverse effects on the breastfed infant.
- Fertility:
  - No studies on fertility; carcinogenicity, mutagenicity, and impairment of fertility studies have not been undertaken.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **thrombin** requires no intermediate physiologic agent for its action. It activates platelets and catalyzes the conversion of fibrinogen to fibrin, essential steps for clot formation. The speed of clot formation depends on the concentrations of thrombin and fibrinogen.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption, distribution, metabolism, elimination after topical application: Not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Effect on clot formation: Dependent on thrombin concentration at the bleeding surface and available fibrinogen. Topical application produces local activation of clotting at the site.

## 12. Clinical Trials
### Summary of key trials and findings (as reported in available sources)
- Trial 1: Randomized, double-blind, controlled trial comparing recombinant human thrombin versus bovine thrombin as adjuncts to hemostasis in liver resection, spine, peripheral arterial bypass, and dialysis access surgeries.
  - Patients: 206 received bovine thrombin; 205 received recombinant human thrombin; 401 completed the trial.
  - Adverse events reported in both groups included cardiac events (18%), hypersensitivity (17%), other infections (15%), bleeding (11%), postoperative wound infection (10%), and thromboembolic events (5%).
  - Immunogenicity: Among approximately 200 patients evaluated for antibodies, 10 (5%) were antibody-positive at baseline and 43 (21.5%) after treatment; seroconversion rate in the bovine thrombin group was 18.4%.

- Trial 2: Multicenter, prospective, randomized, double-blind, controlled trial comparing plasma-derived human thrombin to bovine thrombin applied topically with a gelatin sponge.
  - Patients: 152 received bovine thrombin; 153 received human thrombin.
  - Serious adverse reactions in two patients receiving bovine thrombin (pyrexia and post‑procedural hematoma).
  - Immunogenicity: 16/126 (12.7%) who received bovine thrombin demonstrated seroconversion for at least one antibody. Specific ELISA results in that cohort: anti-bovine thrombin 10/126 (7.94%); anti-bovine factor V/Va 12/126 (9.52%); anti-human thrombin 3/126 (2.38%); anti-human factor V/Va 0/126 (0%).

- Trial 3: Multicenter, prospective, randomized, double-blind, controlled trial evaluating repeat exposure in diabetic foot ulcers using a gel prepared with bovine thrombin and autologous platelet-rich plasma applied weekly for 12 weeks.
  - Patients: 40 patients treated with the gel at 14 sites.
  - Results: Safety assessment during 12 weeks plus three-month follow-up showed no serious adverse reactions related to gel treatment.

- Trial 4: Prospective, randomized, phase 2 non-inferiority study comparing topical human thrombin with bovine thrombin during various open surgical procedures (vascular, hepatic, soft tissue, spinal).
  - Patients: 205 randomized (2:1) to human thrombin (n=137) or bovine thrombin (n=68).
  - Most common treatment-emergent adverse reactions (>5% within a treatment group) included procedural pain, nausea, constipation, pruritus, muscle spasms, insomnia, pyrexia, and vomiting.
  - Two patients in the bovine thrombin group (3.2% of treated patients) showed low-level titers of antibodies to bovine factor V.

### Notes on clinical trials
- Because trials were conducted under a variety of conditions, observed adverse event rates may not reflect rates in broader clinical practice.
- Immunogenicity (development of antibodies to bovine thrombin and bovine factor V) was observed in multiple studies and may be associated with bleeding or thrombotic complications.
