# Editorial Product Information: Tetrabenazine

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of tetrabenazine, synthesised for healthcare professionals. 
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) relevant to the clinician's specific jurisdiction. Information from other major regulatory regions (e.g., US, Europe, Canada) was not available in the provided source material and is therefore not included in this comparison.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Tetrabenazine
*   **Chemical Class:** Synthetic derivative of benzylquinolizine; vesicular monoamine transporter 2 (VMAT2) inhibitor.
*   **Brand Name(s):** Not specified in available sources [Australian].
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 25 mg uncoated, yellowish, biplanar tablets with "CL25" marking and a break bar [Australian].
*   **Route of Administration:** Oral.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** iNova Pharmaceuticals (Aust) Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   Control of chorea [Australian].
*   Control of hemiballismus [Australian].
*   Tardive and buccolingual dyskinesias [Australian].
*   Certain dystonic syndromes [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
*   **Initial Dose:** 25 mg twice daily [Australian].
*   **Titration:** Increase by 25 mg per day every 3 to 4 days until the desired clinical effect is achieved or side effects occur [Australian].
*   **Maximum Dose:** 200 mg per day [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing
*   **Initial Dose:** 12.5 mg twice daily [Australian].
*   **Titration:** Increments of 12.5 mg every 3 to 4 days [Australian].
*   **Maximum Dose:** 3 mg/kg/day or the point of therapeutic effect/intolerance [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
*   Dosage is a guide only and must be individualised via titration [Australian].
*   If no clinical improvement is observed after 7 to 10 days at the maximum tolerated dose, therapy is unlikely to be beneficial [Australian].
*   Absorption is not affected by food [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
*   **Concomitant Therapy:** Use with reserpine or levodopa. Tetrabenazine must not be administered within 24 hours of reserpine [Australian].
*   **MAOIs:** Use within 14 days of monoamine oxidase inhibitor (MAOI) therapy [Australian].
*   **Medical Conditions:** Patients with Parkinsonism or current depression [Australian].
*   **Lactation:** Tetrabenazine is contraindicated during breastfeeding [Australian].
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known sensitivity to tetrabenazine or any of its excipients (e.g., lactose) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Depression and Suicidality:** Tetrabenazine can cause or exacerbate depression and suicidal ideation/behaviour. Use with extreme caution in patients with a history of depression or prior suicide attempts. If depression is profound or persists, consider discontinuation and initiation of antidepressants [Australian].
*   **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** A rare but serious risk, particularly early in treatment or following dose changes. Symptoms include hyperthermia, rigidity, mental status changes, and autonomic instability. Immediate withdrawal is required [Australian].
*   **Parkinsonism:** May induce or worsen Parkinsonian symptoms; dose reduction may be required [Australian].
*   **QTc Prolongation:** Causes a modest increase in the QTc interval (approx. 8 msec; up to 30 msec in slow metabolisers). Use with caution in patients with congenital long QT syndrome or those taking other QTc-prolonging medications [Australian].
*   **Dysphagia and Choking:** Choking attacks and dysphagia have been reported and may lead to aspiration pneumonia. Discontinue therapy if these occur [Australian].
*   **Orthostatic Hypotension:** May induce postural dizziness or syncope at therapeutic doses [Australian].
*   **Lactose Intolerance:** Contains lactose; unsuitable for patients with galactose intolerance or glucose-galactose malabsorption [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Data
| Body System | Adverse Event | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nervous System** | Drowsiness | 20% |
| | Parkinsonism | 2–12% (higher doses) |
| | Akathisia, Dizziness, Dystonia | Common |
| | Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome | Rare (<0.01%) |
| **Psychiatric** | Depression | 6–10% |
| | Anxiety, Insomnia | 2–9% |
| | Agitation | 2.7% |
| | Confusion | 1% |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Dysphagia and choking | 4% |
| | Nausea, Constipation, Dry Mouth | Common |
| **Cardiovascular** | Postural hypotension | 1% |

### Serious Adverse Effects
*   Suicidal ideation and behaviour.
*   Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS).
*   Aspiration pneumonia secondary to dysphagia.
*   Severe Parkinsonism [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Levodopa/Reserpine:** Tetrabenazine antagonises the effects of these agents [Australian].
*   **MAOIs:** Risk of hypertensive crisis. Maintain a 14-day washout period [Australian].
*   **CYP2D6 Inhibitors:** (e.g., fluoxetine, paroxetine, quinidine, duloxetine). These may increase plasma levels of tetrabenazine and its active metabolites. Dose reduction should be considered [Australian].
*   **CNS Depressants:** Additive sedative effects with alcohol, opioids, hypnotics, and neuroleptics [Australian].
*   **Neuroleptic Agents:** Increased risk of dopamine depletion, Parkinsonism, and NMS when used with drugs like haloperidol or metoclopramide [Australian].
*   **Antihypertensives/Beta-blockers:** Increased risk of orthostatic hypotension [Australian].
*   **QTc-Prolonging Drugs:** Caution with antipsychotics, certain antibiotics, and Class IA/III antiarrhythmics [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category B3.** Inadequate evidence of safety in humans; known to cross the placenta in animal models. Use is not recommended during pregnancy [Australian].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Tetrabenazine is excreted in milk [Australian].
*   **Fertility:** No human data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Limited data available. Dosing should be weight-based (up to 3 mg/kg/day) [Australian].
*   **Elderly Use:** No specific studies performed, but dosage reductions may be necessary [Australian].
*   **Renal Impairment:** Not studied; dose reduction may be required [Australian].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Dose reduction may be required [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Reversibly inhibits the vesicular monoamine transporter 2 (VMAT2), leading to the depletion of dopamine and other monoamines in CNS presynaptic nerve terminals [Australian].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    *   **Absorption:** Extensive (>75%) and rapid oral absorption [Australian].
    *   **Metabolism:** Complex; primarily metabolised to active $\alpha$- and $\beta$-dihydrotetrabenazine (HTBZ). HTBZ is further metabolised via O-dealkylation and hydroxylation [Australian].
    *   **Elimination:** Primarily via urine in metabolised form. The elimination half-life of HTBZ is approximately 5 to 6 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   Not specified in available sources (The provided PI notes that tetrabenazine is an established drug with clinical benefits for hyperkinetic movement disorders based on its mechanism of monoamine depletion) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Storage:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
*   **Container:** HDPE Bottle containing 112 tablets [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **Australian Classification:** Prescription Only Medicine - Schedule 4 (S4).
