# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine (Injection)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It serves as a clinical reference for the pharmacology, dosing, and safety profile of the drug.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved labelling before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for intravenous use; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance haemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. This may be administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or via infusion [US]. 
    - **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. 
    - **Frequency:** Dosing should be every 3 to 4 hours, and no less frequent than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Maintenance:** Daily maintenance doses typically remain around 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
- **ESRD on Haemodialysis:** 
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow 2–3 minute bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - **Adjustment:** Dose reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough carnitine levels [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter or discolouration before use [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) [US].
- **Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, it is recommended to obtain baseline carnitine levels and monitor weekly/monthly. Target free carnitine plasma concentrations should be between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US]. In ESRD, therapy is prompted by trough levels below 40–50 µmol/L [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal oedema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These often occur within minutes of administration, particularly in ESRD patients. If a reaction occurs, therapy should be discontinued immediately [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine may increase the frequency or severity of seizures in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a prior history of epilepsy [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Levocarnitine Note):** While this PI concerns the injection, clinicians should note that high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are frequently confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disorders.
- **Very Common (>10%):** Injection site reaction (up to 38%), hypertension (up to 21%), nausea/vomiting (up to 21%), abdominal pain (up to 21%), headache (up to 37%), pharyngitis (up to 27%), and diarrhoea (up to 35%) [US].
- **Common (1–10%):** Fever, chest pain, asthenia, dizziness, insomnia, paraesthesia, dyspnoea, rhinitis, pruritus, and hypercalcaemia [US].
- **Uncommon (<1%):** Body odour and gastritis [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, and seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate that levocarnitine may increase the International Normalised Ratio (INR) when co-administered with warfarin. Monitoring of INR is recommended upon initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine in patients on anticoagulation [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Pregnancy Category not explicitly assigned under current labelling, but based on legacy standards, it aligns with Category B/C]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly required [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies in nursing mothers have not been conducted. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine levels after administration. The risk of excess carnitine intake by the infant should be weighed against the benefit of supplementation for the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No mutagenic effects were found in standard assays (Salmonella, yeast). Long-term animal carcinogenicity studies have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg) as described in section 3. Clinical trials included paediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources (General caution regarding dose selection in the elderly is standard practice due to decreased organ function) [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically in ESRD. In this population, monitoring trough carnitine levels is essential to prevent excessive accumulation and to guide downward dose titration [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with organic acidemias, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly cleared [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in faeces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours, and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV thrice weekly after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis carnitine levels from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. Trough levels remained stable or increased over the course of treatment [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg thrice weekly showed dose-proportional increases in trough concentrations at 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Clinical Outcomes Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the definitive impact on specific clinical outcomes in this population remains undetermined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **US:** Rx-only (Prescription Only Medicine).
