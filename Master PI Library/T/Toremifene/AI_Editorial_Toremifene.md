# Editorial Product Information: Toremifene Citrate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- At the time of writing, official PI documents from other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian, Japanese) were not provided for comparison. Therefore, the information presented reflects the current US regulatory standing and clinical guidelines.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Toremifene citrate (a nonsteroidal triphenylethylene derivative).
- **Brand Names:** Fareston (original brand; not specified as current in source), generic equivalents available (e.g., Novadoz) [US].
- **ATC Code:** L02BA02.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Oral tablets; 60 mg (equivalent to 88.5 mg toremifene citrate).
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Novadoz Pharmaceuticals LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metastatic Breast Cancer:** Treatment of metastatic breast cancer in postmenopausal women with estrogen-receptor (ER) positive or unknown tumors [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** 60 mg administered once daily.
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets may be taken with or without food.
- **Duration of Therapy:** Treatment is generally continued until evidence of disease progression is observed.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specifically addressed in the available source; standard clinical practice suggests taking the missed dose as soon as remembered unless it is nearly time for the next dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to toremifene citrate or any component of the formulation.
- **QT Prolongation:** Patients with congenital or acquired QT prolongation.
- **Electrolyte Imbalance:** Uncorrected hypokalemia or uncorrected hypomagnesemia [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: QT Prolongation:** Toremifene causes dose- and concentration-related prolongation of the QTc interval. This can lead to Torsade de pointes, syncope, seizure, and death. Avoid use with other drugs known to prolong the QT interval or strong CYP3A4 inhibitors [US].
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Grade 3 and 4 transaminitis, hyperbilirubinemia, jaundice, and hepatitis have been reported. 
- **Hypercalcemia and Tumor Flare:** May occur during the first weeks of treatment in patients with bone metastases. Severe hypercalcemia requires discontinuation.
- **Uterine Malignancy:** Increased risk of endometrial cancer, hyperplasia, and polyps. Long-term use in patients with pre-existing endometrial hyperplasia is not established.
- **Thromboembolic Events:** Patients with a history of thromboembolic disease should generally not be treated with toremifene.
- **Laboratory Monitoring:** 
    - Periodic Complete Blood Count (CBC).
    - Calcium levels (especially in patients with bone metastases).
    - Liver Function Tests (LFTs).
    - Baseline and annual gynecological examinations.
    - Baseline and periodic ECGs in patients at increased risk for QT prolongation.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are primarily antiestrogenic in nature and typically manifest at the start of therapy.

### Common Adverse Effects (Incidence 1–10% or greater)
| Adverse Reaction | Frequency (%) [US Data] |
| :--- | :--- |
| Hot Flashes | 35% |
| Sweating | 20% |
| Nausea | 14% |
| Vaginal Discharge | 13% |
| Dizziness | 9% |
| Edema | 5% |
| Vomiting | 4% |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Cardiac:** Myocardial infarction, cardiac failure, arrhythmia.
- **Ocular:** Cataracts (10%), dry eyes (9%), corneal keratopathy, and glaucoma.
- **Thromboembolic:** Pulmonary embolism, thrombophlebitis, CVA/TIA.
- **Hepatobiliary:** Significant elevations in AST and Alkaline Phosphatase.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **Strong CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., Rifampin, Phenytoin, Carbamazepine):** Decrease toremifene serum concentrations; avoid coadministration.
    - **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors (e.g., Ketoconazole, Itraconazole, Clarithromycin):** Increase toremifene serum concentrations and risk of QT prolongation; avoid coadministration.
    - **CYP2C9 Substrates (e.g., Warfarin, Phenytoin):** Toremifene is a weak inhibitor of CYP2C9. Use with caution and monitor for increased exposure (e.g., INR monitoring for warfarin).
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **QT Prolonging Agents:** Avoid use with Class 1A (e.g., quinidine) and Class III (e.g., amiodarone, sotalol) antiarrhythmics, certain antipsychotics, and certain antibiotics.
    - **Thiazide Diuretics:** May decrease renal calcium excretion, increasing the risk of hypercalcemia.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **US Category D.** Toremifene can cause fetal harm. Animal studies show increased pregnancy loss and malformations. Use in premenopausal women requires effective non-hormonal contraception [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if toremifene is excreted in human milk (it is excreted in rat milk). Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in infants, a decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug.
- **Fertility:** Animal data show impairment of fertility and conception in both males and females.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No indication for use in the pediatric population [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness observed, though elimination half-life is longer (7.2 days vs 4.2 days) and volume of distribution is higher in elderly females compared to young males.
- **Renal Impairment:** Pharmacokinetics are similar to those in healthy individuals; no dose adjustment specified.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Elimination half-life may increase nearly twofold in patients with cirrhosis or fibrosis. Use with caution [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A nonsteroidal triphenylethylene derivative that binds to estrogen receptors. It competes with estrogen for binding sites in tumors, blocking growth-stimulating effects.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Decreases vaginal cornification index and serum gonadotropins (FSH/LH).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Well absorbed; Tmax approx. 3 hours. Not affected by food.
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein bound (>99.5%), primarily to albumin. Vd approx. 580 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolized via CYP3A4 to N-demethyltoremifene (major metabolite, also antiestrogenic).
    - **Elimination:** Primarily via feces; ~10% via urine. Terminal half-life approx. 5 days.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **North American, Eastern European, and Nordic Studies:** Three randomized trials compared toremifene 60 mg/day to tamoxifen (20 or 40 mg/day). 
- **Outcomes:** Toremifene demonstrated comparable response rates (approx. 20–31%) and survival rates to tamoxifen. In the Nordic study, tamoxifen showed a significantly longer time to progression compared to toremifene. High-dose arms (200–240 mg) did not provide superior efficacy but increased toxicity.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F).
- **Excursions:** Permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from heat and light [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources [Australian status not provided].
