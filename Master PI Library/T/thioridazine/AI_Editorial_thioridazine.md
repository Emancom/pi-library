# Editorial Product Information: Thioridazine Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of thioridazine hydrochloride based on official product information from the United States. It is intended for use by healthcare professionals as a clinically accurate synthesis of regulatory data.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Thioridazine hydrochloride
- **Chemical Class:** Phenothiazine (specifically a 2-methylmercapto-10-[2-(N-methyl-2-piperidyl) ethyl] phenothiazine)
- **Brand Names:** Noted generically as thioridazine hydrochloride [US]
- **ATC Code:** N05AC02 (Not explicitly stated in source, but standard classification)
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 10 mg, 25 mg, 50 mg, and 100 mg [US]
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Safecor Health LLC (Distributor); Mylan Pharmaceuticals Inc. (Manufacturer) [US]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Schizophrenia:** Indicated for the management of schizophrenic patients who have failed to respond adequately to treatment with other antipsychotic drugs. Due to significant proarrhythmic risks, it should be reserved as a second-line or later treatment option. It is strongly recommended that patients undergo at least two trials with different antipsychotic products at adequate doses and durations before initiating thioridazine [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Starting Dose:** 50 mg to 100 mg three times daily [US].
- **Titration:** Increments should be gradual [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** 800 mg daily [US].
- **Maintenance:** Once symptoms are controlled, the dosage should be reduced to the minimum effective maintenance level. The typical daily range is 200 mg to 800 mg, divided into two to four doses [US].

### Pediatric Dosing
- **Schizophrenia (unresponsive to other agents):** Initial dose of 0.5 mg/kg/day in divided doses. This may be increased gradually until the maximum dose of 3 mg/kg/day or optimum therapeutic effect is reached [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets should be swallowed whole; the 100 mg strength is specifically designated for use only in severe neuropsychiatric conditions [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Concurrent Medications:** Coadministration with drugs known to prolong the QTc interval [US].
- **Cardiovascular Conditions:** Congenital long QT syndrome, a history of cardiac arrhythmias, or extreme hypertensive/hypotensive heart disease [US].
- **Metabolic Profile:** Patients with known genetic defects leading to reduced Cytochrome P450 2D6 (CYP2D6) activity (approximately 7% of the population) [US].
- **CYP2D6 Inhibitors:** Concurrent use of drugs that inhibit CYP2D6, such as fluoxetine, paroxetine, fluvoxamine, propranolol, and pindolol [US].
- **CNS Status:** Severe central nervous system depression or comatose states from any cause [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
- **QTc Prolongation and Sudden Death:** Thioridazine prolongs the QTc interval in a dose-related manner. This is associated with Torsades de pointes-type arrhythmias and sudden death. 
- **Increased Mortality in Elderly Patients with Dementia-Related Psychosis:** Antipsychotic drugs are associated with an increased risk of death in this population. Thioridazine is not approved for dementia-related psychosis.

### Cardiovascular Precautions
- **Monitoring:** Perform baseline ECG and measure serum potassium before treatment. Normalize potassium before initiation. Patients with a QTc interval >450 msec should not receive thioridazine [US].
- **Discontinuation:** Discontinue if the QTc interval exceeds 500 msec or if symptoms of Torsades de pointes (syncope, palpitations, dizziness) occur [US].

### Neurological Precautions
- **Tardive Dyskinesia:** Potentially irreversible, involuntary, dyskinetic syndrome. Risk increases with treatment duration and cumulative dose [US].
- **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** Rare but fatal complex (hyperpyrexia, muscle rigidity, altered mental status, autonomic instability) [US].

### Other Precautions
- **Falls:** Due to somnolence and orthostatic hypotension [US].
- **Hematologic:** Leukopenia, neutropenia, and agranulocytosis have been reported. Monitor CBC frequently during the first few months in patients with low baseline WBC [US].
- **Ocular:** Pigmentary retinopathy (brownish coloring of vision, impaired night vision) may occur, primarily at doses exceeding recommended limits [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
In recommended dosage ranges, most effects are mild and transient.

| System | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Central Nervous System** | Drowsiness (common early in treatment), pseudoparkinsonism, extrapyramidal symptoms, nocturnal confusion, restlessness, headache (rare). |
| **Autonomic** | Dryness of mouth, blurred vision, constipation, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, miosis, urinary retention/incontinence. |
| **Cardiovascular** | Dose-related QTc prolongation, hypotension (rarely leading to cardiac arrest), ECG changes (T-wave inversion). |
| **Endocrine** | Galactorrhea, breast engorgement, amenorrhea, inhibition of ejaculation, weight gain, prolactin elevation. |
| **Skin/Allergic** | Dermatitis, urticaria, photosensitivity (rare), exfoliative dermatitis. |
| **Serious/Rare** | Priapism, parotid swelling, jaundice, biliary stasis, NMS, agranulocytosis. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP2D6 Inhibitors:** (e.g., fluoxetine, paroxetine) appreciably inhibit metabolism, increasing thioridazine levels and arrhythmia risk. **Contraindicated** [US].
- **Fluvoxamine:** Increases thioridazine, mesoridazine, and sulforidazine levels 3-fold. **Contraindicated** [US].
- **Propranolol/Pindolol:** Increases plasma levels of thioridazine by 50% to 400%. **Contraindicated** [US].
- **QTc-Prolonging Drugs:** Additive effects on cardiac repolarization. **Contraindicated** [US].
- **CNS Depressants:** Potentiates effects of alcohol, anesthetics, opiates, and barbiturates. Severe respiratory depression may occur [US].
- **Epinephrine:** Phenothiazines may induce a "reversed epinephrine effect," leading to severe hypotension. Use levarterenol or phenylephrine instead [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not lettered in source, but described as follows]: No teratogenic effects in animals. Neonates exposed during the third trimester are at risk for extrapyramidal and/or withdrawal symptoms post-delivery. Use only if potential benefit justifies the risk to the fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Fertility:** Reproductive studies in animals failed to show teratogenicity [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Reserved for schizophrenia unresponsive to other agents in children (see Section 3 for dosing) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of mortality in dementia-related psychosis. Generally lower tolerance for phenothiazines, with higher risk of parkinsonism, akathisia, and agranulocytosis [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Dosing adjustments not specified, but caution is advised in cases of jaundice or biliary stasis [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Antipsychotic activity similar to other phenothiazines but with minimal extrapyramidal stimulation [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Potent QTc prolongation. Elevates prolactin levels which persist during chronic administration [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP2D6. Active metabolites include mesoridazine and sulforidazine [US].
    - **Slow Hydroxylators:** Poor metabolizers of debrisoquin/CYP2D6 exhibit 4.5-fold higher AUC and 2.4-fold higher Cmax compared to rapid hydroxylators [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **QTc Study:** A crossover study in nine healthy males comparing 10 mg and 50 mg doses showed a mean maximum QTc increase of 23 msec with the 50 mg dose, demonstrating dose-related prolongation [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from light [US].
- **Note:** Unit dose packages are not child-resistant and are for institutional use only [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only. Poison/Schedule classification not specified in source [US].
