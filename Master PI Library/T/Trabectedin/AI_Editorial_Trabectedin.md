# Editorial Product Information: Trabectedin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- Data regarding specific populations (Japanese) mentioned within the primary source have been incorporated and attributed accordingly.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a synthesis for clinical reference and does not substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Trabectedin (Antineoplastic agent).
- **Chemical Class:** Synthetic agent originally derived from the marine tunicate *Ecteinascidia turbinata*.
- **Brand Name:** **YONDELIS** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01CX01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Powder for solution for infusion; 1 mg per vial.
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion (Central venous line strongly recommended).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Specialised Therapeutics Pharma Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Soft Tissue Sarcoma:** Treatment of patients with unresectable or metastatic liposarcoma or leiomyosarcoma who have previously received an anthracycline-containing regimen.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** 1.5 mg/m² body surface area (BSA).
- **Schedule:** Administered as a continuous intravenous infusion over 24 hours, with a three-week interval between cycles.
- **Premedication:** All patients must receive intravenous corticosteroids (e.g., 20 mg dexamethasone) 30 minutes prior to infusion. This serves as anti-emetic prophylaxis and provides apparent hepatoprotective effects.
- **Treatment Criteria:**
    - Absolute Neutrophil Count (ANC): $\ge 1.5 \times 10^9$/L.
    - Platelets: $\ge 100 \times 10^9$/L.
    - Bilirubin: $\le$ Upper Limit of Normal (ULN).
    - Alkaline Phosphatase: $\le 2.5 \times$ ULN.
    - Albumin: $\ge 25$ g/L.
    - ALT and AST: $\le 2.5 \times$ ULN.
    - Creatinine Clearance: $\ge 30$ mL/min.
    - Creatine Phosphokinase (CPK): $\le 2.5 \times$ ULN.
    - Haemoglobin: $\ge 90$ g/L.
- **Dose Adjustments:**
    - **Reduction Level 1:** 1.2 mg/m².
    - **Reduction Level 2:** 1.0 mg/m².
    - **Permanent Discontinuation:** Required for rhabdomyolysis, capillary leak syndrome, Grade 3/4 cardiac adverse events, or LVEF below the lower limit of normal (LLN).
- **Administration Instructions:** Reconstitute the 1 mg vial with 20 mL of water for injections (0.05 mg/mL). Further dilute with 0.9% sodium chloride or 5% glucose. 
    - **Central line:** Concentration $\le 0.030$ mg/mL in $\ge 50$ mL diluent.
    - **Peripheral line:** If central access is not feasible, use $\ge 1,000$ mL of diluent to minimize the risk of severe injection site reactions.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to trabectedin or any excipients (sucrose, monobasic potassium phosphate, phosphoric acid, potassium hydroxide).
- Concurrent serious or uncontrolled infection.
- Breastfeeding.
- Concomitant use with yellow fever vaccine.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hematologic Toxicity:** Grade 3/4 neutropenia and thrombocytopenia are very common. Neutropenic sepsis can be fatal. Monitor CBC weekly for the first two cycles.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Reversible acute increases in AST/ALT are common. Patients with elevated bilirubin at baseline must not be treated.
- **Rhabdomyolysis:** Monitor CPK closely. Discontinue if rhabdomyolysis is suspected. Avoid concomitant statins if possible.
- **Cardiac Dysfunction:** Monitor LVEF via echocardiogram or MUGA scan at baseline and every 2–3 months during treatment.
- **Capillary Leak Syndrome (CLS):** Rare but potentially fatal. Monitor for unexplained oedema or rapid decline in serum albumin.
- **Extravasation:** May cause tissue necrosis requiring debridement. No specific antidote exists.
- **Additional Monitoring:** [Australian] This product is subject to additional monitoring for quick identification of new safety information.

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Note: Frequencies based on Study ET743-SAR-3007.*

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Nausea (75%), Fatigue (69%), ALT increased (49%), AST increased (38%), Anaemia (41%), Neutropenia (31%), Vomiting (46%), Diarrhoea (35%), Decreased appetite (37%), Constipation (37%), Thrombocytopenia (20%), Dyspnoea (25%). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Hypokalaemia, Dehydration, Headache, Dizziness, Back pain, Myalgia, Insomnia, Cardiac disorders. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Septic shock, Capillary leak syndrome, Pulmonary oedema, Rhabdomyolysis, Soft tissue necrosis. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Hepatic failure. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** 63.2% of patients experience Grade 3/4 toxicities. Fatal outcomes occurred in 3.8% of patients, often due to pancytopenia, sepsis, or multiorgan failure.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic (CYP3A4):** 
    - **Inhibitors:** Potent inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, ritonavir, clarithromycin) increase trabectedin exposure (AUC increased by 66% with ketoconazole). Avoid combination or monitor strictly.
    - **Inducers:** Potent inducers (e.g., rifampicin, phenobarbital, St John’s Wort) decrease exposure (AUC reduced by 31% with rifampicin). Use should be avoided.
- **P-glycoprotein (P-gp):** Trabectedin is a P-gp substrate; inhibitors (e.g., cyclosporine, verapamil) may alter distribution.
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **Phenytoin:** Trabectedin may reduce phenytoin absorption, exacerbating convulsions.
    - **Alcohol:** Must be avoided due to additive hepatotoxicity.
    - **Vaccines:** Live attenuated vaccines are not recommended.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category D]. Trabectedin is genotoxic and suspected to cause serious birth defects. Highly effective contraception is required during treatment and for **8 months** after the last dose for women.
- **Fertility:** Men must use highly effective contraception during treatment and for **5 months** after. Genetic counselling and advice on sperm/ovule conservation are recommended.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated during treatment and for **3 months** thereafter.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for children <18 years due to insufficient efficacy data in paediatric sarcomas.
- **Elderly Use:** No specific dose adjustments are required based solely on age.
- **Renal Impairment:** No adjustment for mild/moderate impairment. Not studied and **not recommended** for patients with CrCl <30 mL/min.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Clearance is reduced and exposure is doubled in moderate impairment. **Avoid use** if bilirubin is elevated at baseline. 
- **Japanese Population:** PK analysis shows that a dose of **1.2 mg/m²** in Japanese patients yields equivalent exposure to **1.5 mg/m²** in Western populations [Japanese/Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Binds to the minor groove of DNA, bending the helix toward the major groove. This interferes with transcription factors, DNA binding proteins, and repair pathways, causing cell cycle arrest.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** High apparent volume of distribution (>5,000 L); 94-98% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP3A4.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is long (~180 hours). Low renal excretion (<1%); primarily faecal elimination (58%).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Trial (ET743-SAR-3007):** A randomised (2:1) open-label trial comparing trabectedin (1.5 mg/m² 24h infusion) to dacarbazine (1,000 mg/m²) in 518 patients with liposarcoma or leiomyosarcoma.
- **Outcomes:** 
    - **PFS:** Significant improvement with trabectedin (4.2 months vs 1.5 months; HR 0.55, p<0.001).
    - **OS:** No statistically significant difference (13.7 vs 13.1 months).
    - **ORR:** 9.9% for trabectedin vs 6.9% for dacarbazine.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Vials:** Store in a refrigerator at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze.
- **Shelf Life:** 60 months.
- **Reconstituted/Diluted Solution:** Chemical and physical stability demonstrated for 30 hours up to 25°C. For microbiological safety, use immediately or store for no longer than 24 hours at 2°C to 8°C.
