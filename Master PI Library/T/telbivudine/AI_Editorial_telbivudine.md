# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised clinical overview of levocarnitine injection for healthcare professionals. It synthesises regulatory data into a structured format for rapid clinical reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official PI. Clinicians must refer to the current approved PI in their specific jurisdiction before prescribing or administering this medication.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP); chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [United States].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [United States].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [United States].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [United States].
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Doses should be administered every 3 or 4 hours, and never less frequently than every 6 hours.
- **Titration:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg or as clinically required. Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [United States].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
- **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are supratherapeutic [United States].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature (25°C) when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Contraindications:** None known [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These may occur within minutes of intravenous administration, particularly in ESRD patients on dialysis. If a reaction occurs, discontinue the drug immediately [United States].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine may increase seizure frequency or severity in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a prior history of epilepsy [United States].
- **Renal Impairment & Oral Formulations:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [United States].
- **Monitoring:** In metabolic disorders, monitor blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) weekly or monthly [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5% in ESRD clinical trials)
- **General:** Abdominal pain (21%), fever (5–12%), injection site reaction (27–38%), chest pain (6–15%), headache (3–37%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hypervolemia (3–12%).

### Other Reported Effects
- **Body Odor:** Reported with levocarnitine therapy.
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (see Warnings and Precautions).
- **Serious:** Anaphylaxis and severe respiratory distress [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels at the initiation of levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [United States: Category not specified in current labeling; however, reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm]. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after administration. Risks to the infant must be weighed against the maternal benefit of treatment [United States].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal reproductive studies [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders apply to paediatric patients (see Section 3) [United States].
- **Geriatric Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specific dosing provided for ESRD patients on hemodialysis. High-dose oral therapy should be avoided in severe renal compromise due to metabolite accumulation [United States].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein or albumin bound. Distribution half-life is approximately 0.585 hours.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and gamma-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels) [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg post-dialysis), mean trough levocarnitine levels rose from 21.3 µmol/L (baseline) to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg three times weekly demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [United States].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription Only).
