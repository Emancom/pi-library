# Editorial Product Information: Tibolone

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- **Note:** At the time of this editorial synthesis, only the Australian PI was provided for review. Consequently, clinical recommendations and regulatory details are primarily based on Australian standards.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a clinical synthesis and is not intended to replace the official PI used for prescribing in specific jurisdictions.
- Information not present in the provided source is marked as "Not specified in available sources."

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tibolone (a synthetic steroid with estrogenic, progestogenic, and androgenic properties).
- **Brand Names:** APO-Tibolone [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 2.5 mg uncoated tablets [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Short-term treatment of symptoms** (e.g., vasomotor symptoms) resulting from natural or surgical menopause in postmenopausal women [Australian].
- **Second-line therapy for prevention of bone mineral density (BMD) loss** in postmenopausal women at high risk of future osteoporotic fractures, specifically for those who are intolerant of or contraindicated for other approved therapies [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dose:** 2.5 mg (one tablet) once daily.
- **Administration:** Tablets should be swallowed whole with water or other liquid, preferably at the same time each day. Improvement is generally noted within weeks, though optimal results usually require at least 3 months of therapy [Australian].
- **Initiation:** 
    - Women with natural menopause should wait at least 12 months after their last natural bleed before starting.
    - Women with surgical menopause may begin treatment immediately.
- **Switching from HRT:** 
    - If switching from an estrogen-only preparation, a withdrawal bleed should be induced first.
    - If switching from sequential HRT, start after the progestogen phase is complete.
    - If switching from continuous-combined HRT, treatment may start at any time.
- **Missed Dose:** Take as soon as remembered unless more than 12 hours overdue. If more than 12 hours late, skip the dose and take the next one at the usual time. Do not double the dose.
- **Duration of Therapy:** Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration. The risk-benefit ratio should be formally reviewed every 6 months [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy and lactation.**
- **Known, past, or suspected breast cancer.**
- **Known or suspected estrogen-dependent malignant tumours** (e.g., endometrial cancer).
- **Undiagnosed genital bleeding.**
- **Untreated endometrial hyperplasia.**
- **Venous thromboembolism (VTE):** Previous or current (e.g., DVT, pulmonary embolism).
- **Known thrombophilic disorders** (e.g., protein C, protein S, or antithrombin deficiency).
- **Arterial thromboembolic disease:** History of angina, myocardial infarction, stroke, or transient ischaemic attack (TIA).
- **Acute or history of liver disease:** Contraindicated as long as liver function tests have not returned to normal.
- **Porphyria.**
- **Hypersensitivity** to the active substance or any excipients (including lactose) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Stroke Risk:** Tibolone increases the risk of ischaemic stroke from the first year of treatment. This risk is strongly age-dependent and is particularly elevated in women over 60 years of age [Australian].
- **Endometrial Cancer:** Observational studies suggest an increased risk of endometrial cancer in clinical practice. Risk increases with duration of use.
- **Breast Cancer:** Evidence is inconclusive but suggests a risk comparable to estrogen-only HRT, which is lower than the risk associated with combined (estrogen-progestogen) HRT.
- **Venous Thromboembolism (VTE):** HRT is associated with a 1.3–3 fold increased risk of VTE. Tibolone should be stopped 4–6 weeks before major elective surgery or during prolonged immobilisation.
- **Monitoring Requirements:** 
    - Complete personal and family medical history before initiation.
    - Annual physical examinations (pelvic and breast) and mammography according to local screening practices.
    - Immediate investigation of any unscheduled vaginal bleeding.
- **Conditions Requiring Supervision:** Uterine fibroids, endometriosis, hypertension, liver disorders (e.g., adenoma), diabetes mellitus, cholelithiasis, migraine, systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), epilepsy, asthma, and otosclerosis.
- **Immediate Withdrawal:** Treatment must stop if jaundice, significant blood pressure increase, or new migraine-type headaches occur.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common (1% – 10%)
- **Gastrointestinal:** Lower abdominal pain.
- **Integumentary:** Abnormal hair growth.
- **Reproductive:** Vaginal discharge, endometrial thickening, postmenopausal haemorrhage, breast tenderness, genital pruritus, vaginal candidiasis, pelvic pain, cervical dysplasia, vulvovaginitis.
- **Investigations:** Weight increase, abnormal cervical smear.

### Uncommon (0.1% – 1%)
- **Integumentary:** Acne.
- **Reproductive:** Breast discomfort, fungal infection/mycosis, nipple pain.

### Post-Marketing/Other
- Dizziness, headache, migraine, depression, rash, pruritus, visual disturbances (blurred vision), gastrointestinal upset, oedema, arthralgia, myalgia, and altered liver function parameters [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin/Anticoagulants:** Tibolone increases blood fibrinolytic activity and significantly enhances the effect of warfarin. Careful monitoring and dose adjustment of anticoagulants are required [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Substrates:** Midazolam pharmacokinetics are affected to a moderate extent; caution is advised with other CYP3A4 substrates.
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Compounds like barbiturates, carbamazepine, hydantoins, rifampicin, and **St. John’s Wort** may increase tibolone metabolism and reduce therapeutic efficacy.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Tibolone is contraindicated. It has shown teratogenic effects in animal studies (microphthalmia, hydrocephaly, cleft palate). If pregnancy occurs, stop treatment immediately.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not specified in available sources; no data available.
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment necessary [Australian]. However, clinicians must carefully consider the increased baseline risk of stroke in patients over 60 years.
- **Renal Impairment:** Pharmacokinetic parameters are independent of renal function. No specific data available for dosage adjustment.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in acute liver disease or where liver function tests remain abnormal [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Tibolone is rapidly metabolised into three active compounds:
    - **3α-OH and 3β-OH metabolites:** Predominantly estrogenic activity (acting on bone, vagina, and brain thermoregulatory centres).
    - **Δ4-isomer and parent compound:** Predominantly progestogenic and androgenic activity.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Substitutes for the loss of estrogen in postmenopausal women. It inhibits the sulphatase enzyme in breast tissue, reducing local active estrogen levels. It also decreases HDL-cholesterol, triglycerides, and VLDL-C [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid and extensive; food has no significant effect.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic; plasma levels of the parent compound and the Δ4-isomer are very low.
    - **Excretion:** Mainly as sulfated metabolites in faeces and urine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **LIFT Study:** In women with a mean age of 68, 1.25 mg tibolone reduced vertebral fractures but was associated with a 2.2-fold increase in stroke risk compared to placebo.
- **Million Women Study (MWS):** Reported a significant increase in breast cancer risk (though lower than combined HRT) and an increased risk of ovarian cancer (similar to other HRT).
- **THEBES Study:** Assessed endometrial safety; confirmed that tibolone does not induce endometrial hyperplasia in healthy postmenopausal women, though some cases of breast cancer were adjudicated.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Conditions:** Protect from light and moisture.
- **Container:** PVC/Al blister packs of 28 tablets.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 - Prescription Only Medicine [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Not specified for other jurisdictions.
