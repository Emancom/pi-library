# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview designed for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises available regulatory data into a single clinical reference.
- **Note:** This editorial synthesis is not a substitute for the official PI. Clinicians should consult the current approved PI in their specific jurisdiction before prescribing or administering this medication.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. 
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [United States].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Intravenous Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [United States].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [United States-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) on Hemodialysis:** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [United States-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - Administration: Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
    - Acute Management: A loading dose is often given in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the next 24 hours.
    - Frequency: Administer every 3 or 4 hours; intervals should never be less than every 6 hours.
    - Maintenance: Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg or as clinically required. Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended Starting Dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - Administration: Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - Adjustment: Dose adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations and may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature (25°C) when mixed in Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8 mg/mL.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and initiate medical treatment.
- **Seizures:** Reports exist of seizures occurring in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed.
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Levocarnitine Note):** While this PI concerns the injection, clinicians should note that chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [United States].
- **Monitoring:** Pre-treatment plasma carnitine levels are recommended. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L).

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5% in ESRD Trials)
Adverse events reported in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials for ESRD patients (regardless of causality) include:

| Body System | Adverse Event | Frequency (Levocarnitine 10–40 mg/kg) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain, Headache, Asthenia, Fever, Injection site reaction | 5%–33% |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, Hypotension, Tachycardia | 6%–20% |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, Vomiting, Nausea, Anorexia | 5%–16% |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, Cough increase, Dyspnea, Rhinitis | 7%–23% |
| **Nervous System** | Dizziness, Paresthesia, Depression | 5%–13% |

### Serious/Other Adverse Effects
- **Neurologic:** Seizures.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; gastritis.
- **Other:** Body odor (infrequent).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine.
- **Management:** It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin anticoagulation.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [United States: Category not explicitly assigned in provided PI]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine in milk following administration. The benefits to the mother must be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant.
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg for metabolic disorders). In premature infants and newborns, secondary deficiency is defined by plasma concentrations below age-related normal values.
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** See "Dosage and Administration" for ESRD protocols. Accumulation of metabolites is a concern with oral dosing in this population, but the injection bypasses the gut where TMA is formed [United States].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring molecule required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria for oxidation. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are quickly eliminated.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels).
    - **Half-life:** Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In clinical trials, ESRD patients received 10, 20, or 40 mg/kg after dialysis 3 times per week. Therapy resulted in a significant increase in trough levocarnitine concentrations, reaching stable levels within 8 to 24 weeks. While carnitine concentrations increased, the effect on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives.
