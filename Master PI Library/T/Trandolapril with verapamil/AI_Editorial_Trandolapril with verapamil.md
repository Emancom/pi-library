# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, dosing protocols, and safety profiles.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing within a particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/amino acid derivative.
- **Chemical Name:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection solution: 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours.
    - **Maximum Dose:** Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered in clinical practice [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Initial Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - **Administration:** Administer as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of injection. If a reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and initiate medical treatment [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Monitoring:** 
    - Obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations.
    - Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels.
    - **Target Range:** Plasma free carnitine concentrations should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency; chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trials Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported at a frequency of $\ge 5\%$ in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials (regardless of causality):

| Body System | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain. |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, arrhythmia [US]. |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, anorexia, dyspepsia. |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia, weight decrease/increase, peripheral edema. |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia, depression. |
| **Respiratory** | Cough increase, dyspnea, pharyngitis, rhinitis. |
| **Special Senses** | Amblyopia, taste perversion. |
| **Urogenital** | Kidney failure, urinary tract infection [US]. |

### Other Adverse Effects
- **Common:** Transient nausea and vomiting.
- **Uncommon/Rare:** Body odor, gastritis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have been observed with concurrent use. **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category C/Not specified by current naming convention]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant should be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg weight for metabolic disorders; safety and efficacy profiles are consistent with adult populations [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources, though general caution is advised due to potential co-morbidities.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. Monitoring is essential to avoid metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Levocarnitine clears accumulated toxic acylCoA compounds (common in organic acidemias) by forming acylcarnitine, which is rapidly excreted.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Studies in ESRD:** In patients undergoing hemodialysis for $\ge 6$ months receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly, mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine concentrations increased from $\sim 20$ µmol/L at baseline to $\sim 190$ µmol/L after 8 weeks of therapy [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
- **Stability:** When mixed in Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL), the solution is stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not applicable in US jurisdiction; corresponds to prescription-only status.
