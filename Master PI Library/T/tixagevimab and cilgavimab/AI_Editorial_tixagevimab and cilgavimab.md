# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions’ Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: US (FDA). 
- Note: Australian (TGA), European (EMA), UK (MHRA), Canadian (Health Canada), and Japanese (PMDA) sources were not provided in the reference material; sections relevant to these jurisdictions are marked as "Not specified in available sources" or based solely on the provided US documentation.
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for healthcare professionals. 
- This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not replace official, jurisdiction-specific PI documents required for prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Names:** Not specified in available sources (Generic: Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US]).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for both acute and chronic management of patients with inborn metabolic defects that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose: 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - Loading Dose: In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 or 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours).
    - Maintenance: Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised based on clinical requirement.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - Administration: Slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - Adjustment: Trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels should guide adjustments. Reductions to 5 mg/kg may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of treatment if levels are adequate.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial.

## 4. Contraindications
- **US:** None known.
- **Other Jurisdictions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported. These often occur within minutes of administration, particularly in ESRD patients. Treatment should be discontinued immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Neurologic (Seizures):** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with known seizure activity, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Clinical Monitoring:**
    - Metabolic Disorders: Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
    - ESRD: Monitor trough (pre-dialysis) plasma carnitine levels. Normal ranges are typically 40–50 µmol/L [US].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** Note that the safety of the *oral* formulation in renal impairment has not been established; high-dose oral carnitine in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disease.

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and seizures [US].
- **Other:** Body odor and gastritis have been reported less frequently [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels closely upon initiation of levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US:** Not formally assigned a letter category in the current Baxter PI, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of fetal harm or impaired fertility. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
    - **Australian:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Animal data (dairy cows) indicate increased milk concentrations of carnitine following exogenous administration. The benefits to the mother should be weighed against the potential risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg); see Section 3.
- **Elderly Use:** Specific dose adjustments for the elderly are not detailed; however, care should be taken in ESRD patients regardless of age [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. Intravenous carnitine is easily removed by dialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with metabolic defects or ESRD, it promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. 
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x weekly post-dialysis), trough carnitine levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses confirmed a dose-dependent increase in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Clinical Outcomes:** While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations is proven, the effects on specific clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, cardiomyopathy) in the ESRD population have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL concentrations) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].

- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only.
