# Editorial Product Information: travoprost + timolol

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **travoprost + timolol**
- Chemical class:
  - Travoprost: prostaglandin F2α analogue (prodrug; FP receptor agonist)
  - Timolol: non‑selective beta‑adrenergic (β‑blocker) agent
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Topical ocular (eye drops)

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Reduction of elevated intraocular pressure in patients with open‑angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension for whom single‑agent therapy provides insufficient intraocular pressure reduction.  
  - Note: The combination product is intended for patients requiring both travoprost and timolol; it should not be used to initiate therapy.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Instil one drop in the conjunctival sac of the affected eye(s) once daily at about the same time each day.
- Do not administer more than once daily; travoprost component is most effective with once‑daily dosing.
- If inadequate response to the combination, consideration should be given to use of the individual agents, with timolol dosed twice daily.

### Dosing by indication
- No separate dosing regimens by specific indication provided beyond the above.

### Administration instructions
- If more than one topical ophthalmic product is being used, administer eye drops at least five minutes apart.
- When switching from another topical antiglaucoma agent, discontinue the other agent and start the combination the following day.
- To minimise systemic absorption, instruct patients to occlude the nasolacrimal duct (by gentle nasolacrimal occlusion) for two minutes after instillation.
- Contact lenses: remove contact lenses prior to instillation and do not re‑insert for at least 15 minutes after instillation.

### Titration schedules
- Not specified in available sources.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
List includes all contraindications provided in available sources:
- Known hypersensitivity to travoprost, timolol, or any excipients (including preservative components) contained in the product.
- Pregnancy or women attempting to become pregnant. [Australian]
- Reactive airway disease, including bronchial asthma, or a history of bronchial asthma.
- Severe chronic obstructive pulmonary disease.
- Sinus bradycardia.
- Sick sinus syndrome, including sino‑atrial block.
- Second or third degree atrioventricular block.
- Overt cardiac failure.
- Cardiogenic shock.
- Not for injection or oral ingestion (topical ocular product only).

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Systemic absorption: Like other topical ophthalmic agents, systemic absorption may occur. Due to the timolol component, systemic beta‑blocker adverse reactions may occur.
- Cardiovascular/respiratory reactions: Timolol may cause or aggravate bradycardia, hypotension, bronchospasm, worsening of congestive cardiac failure, and aggravation of Prinzmetal (variant) angina. Monitor patients with existing cardiovascular disease and consider alternative therapies where appropriate.
- Respiratory disease: Use with extreme caution in patients with a history of asthma or severe COPD; serious respiratory reactions including bronchospasm and death have been reported with topical timolol.
- Anaphylaxis: Patients with history of atopy or prior severe anaphylactic reactions may be more reactive to allergens while receiving beta‑blockers and may be less responsive to adrenaline (epinephrine) treatment.
- Concomitant therapy: The systemic beta‑blocker effects may be potentiated by co‑administration of other beta‑blocking agents (including oral) and other interacting drugs. Use caution and monitor closely when co‑prescribing interacting medicines.
- Beta‑blocker class effects:
  - May mask signs/symptoms of hypoglycaemia (important in insulin‑dependent or labile diabetes).
  - May mask signs of hyperthyroidism; abrupt withdrawal may precipitate symptom worsening.
  - May aggravate symptoms of myasthenia gravis.
- Surgical anaesthesia: Beta‑blocking ophthalmic preparations may antagonise systemic beta‑agonist effects; inform anaesthesiologists of timolol use.
- Ocular effects related to travoprost:
  - Increased iris pigmentation (brownification), usually gradual and potentially permanent; more frequently observed in patients with mixed‑colour irides but can occur in brown eyes. Unilateral treatment can cause heterochromia.
  - Periorbital/eyelid skin darkening and deepening of the eyelid sulcus.
  - Eyelash changes (increased length, thickness, pigmentation and/or number).
- Inflammatory ocular conditions: Limited experience in inflammatory ocular disease; use with caution in patients with active intraocular inflammation and in patients with risk factors for uveitis.
- Macular oedema: Although not reported in pivotal combination trials, macular oedema including cystoid macular oedema has been reported with prostaglandin F2α analogues, mainly in aphakic/pseudophakic patients with torn posterior capsule or anterior chamber lenses, or in patients with risk factors for macular oedema; exercise caution.
- Choroidal detachment: Reported with aqueous suppressant therapy after filtration procedures; use with caution post‑filtration.
- Contact lens wear: See administration instructions (remove lenses before instillation and wait 15 minutes before reinsertion).
- Procedures to reduce systemic exposure: Nasolacrimal occlusion recommended.
- Hepatic and renal impairment: No dosage adjustment necessary according to available sources.
- Use in the elderly: No overall differences in safety and effectiveness reported between elderly and other adults.
- Paediatric use: Not recommended; safety and effectiveness in paediatric patients have not been established.
- Effects on laboratory tests: No data available.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor intraocular pressure response.
- Monitor for signs of systemic beta‑blockade (bradycardia, hypotension, bronchospasm).
- Monitor for ocular pigmentary changes, eyelash and periocular changes, signs of intraocular inflammation, and symptoms suggesting macular oedema.
- Monitor patients with cardiovascular or respiratory disease for deterioration.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Avoid concomitant use of two topical beta‑blockers or two topical prostaglandin analogues.
- The combination product is not recommended for initiating glaucoma therapy; consider individual agents first if initiating treatment.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- No pharmacokinetic interactions were observed between travoprost and timolol following topical ocular administration of the combination product.
- Travoprost active free acid exhibits moderate plasma protein binding (~80%); protein‑binding interactions are considered unlikely.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Additive effects potentially resulting in hypotension and/or marked bradycardia may occur when timolol ophthalmic drops are administered with:
  - Oral calcium channel blockers
  - Catecholamine‑depleting drugs
  - Other β‑adrenergic blocking agents (including oral)
  - Antiarrhythmics (including amiodarone and quinidine)
  - Digitalis glycosides
  - Parasympathomimetics
  - Narcotics
  - Monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs)
- Potentiated systemic beta‑blockade (e.g., decreased heart rate, depression) has been reported during combined treatment with CYP2D6 inhibitors (examples given: quinidine, fluoxetine, paroxetine) and timolol.
- Beta‑blockers can decrease the response to adrenaline used to treat anaphylactic reactions; exercise special caution in patients with history of atopy or anaphylaxis.
- Timolol may increase the hypoglycaemic effect of antidiabetic agents.
- Mydriasis has occasionally been reported when timolol is given with adrenaline.

### Clinically significant interactions — management notes
- Monitor heart rate and blood pressure when combining timolol with other cardiovascular agents or CYP2D6 inhibitors.
- Avoid concurrent use of two topical beta‑blockers or two topical prostaglandin analogues.
- Inform emergency and anaesthesia teams of timolol use due to potential interaction with systemic beta‑agonists and anaesthetic drugs.

### Jurisdiction‑specific interactions
- Not specified in available sources beyond the general interactions above.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Pregnancy category/system:
  - [Australian] Pregnancy Category C.
- Recommendations/risks:
  - The combination product may interfere with the maintenance of pregnancy and should not be used by women during pregnancy or by women attempting to become pregnant. Increased embryofetal loss and teratogenic findings were described in animal studies with travoprost at high multiples of human exposure; timolol systemic therapy has been associated in epidemiological studies with intrauterine growth retardation when administered orally.
  - Travoprost and/or its metabolites crossed the placenta in animal studies; teratogenic effects and increased post‑implantation loss described at high systemic exposures in animal models.
  - No adequate and well‑controlled studies in pregnant women.

### Breastfeeding
- Travoprost: No human milk data available. Travoprost and/or metabolites were excreted in rat milk; adverse effects in rat pups observed at low doses; nursing women should be advised to stop breastfeeding if using the product.
- Timolol: Timolol maleate has been detected in human milk following oral and ocular administration.
- Recommendation: Nursing women should use caution due to potential for serious adverse reactions in breastfed infants; stopping breastfeeding is recommended in the animal‑study‑based precaution described.

### Fertility
- No human data on effects of the combination on fertility.
- Travoprost: No effects on mating behaviour or fertility in rats at reported doses, but embryo‑foetal resorption increased at higher doses in animal studies.
- Timolol: No effects on fertility reported in animal studies at tested doses.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- The combination contains two agents with complementary mechanisms:
  - Travoprost: an ester prodrug of a prostaglandin F2α analogue, hydrolysed to the active free acid which is an FP receptor agonist and reduces intraocular pressure primarily by increasing aqueous humour outflow via the trabecular meshwork and uveoscleral pathways.
  - Timolol: a non‑selective β‑adrenergic receptor blocker that reduces intraocular pressure by decreasing aqueous humour production.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Following administration, IOP reduction begins within approximately 30 minutes, with maximum effect at about 12 hours. Significant IOP reduction is maintained for at least 24 hours after multiple treatments.
- Clinical studies indicate the combination provides additional IOP reduction compared to either component alone and produces IOP‑lowering effects comparable to concomitant administration of the individual components once daily.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Travoprost:
  - Travoprost is a prodrug converted to the active free acid.
  - Plasma protein binding of the active free acid is approximately 80%.
  - Reported human dose exposure: travoprost human dose with recommended ocular dosing is approximately 2.2 μg/day (0.044 μg/kg/day) with plasma concentrations up to ~0.020 ng/mL (as reported in available sources).
- Timolol:
  - Timolol is extensively metabolised in the liver.
  - Timolol has been detected in human milk.
  - Timolol is not readily dialysable.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Clinical development included trials of duration ranging from 6 weeks to 12 months.
- In three clinical trials of the polyquaternium‑1 (POLYQUAD)‑preserved formulation, a total of 372 patients/subjects were exposed for up to 12 months; the most frequently reported treatment‑related adverse effect was ocular hyperaemia (~11.8%).
- Pivotal clinical studies demonstrated:
  - The combination provides additional IOP reduction compared with either component administered alone.
  - The IOP‑lowering effect of the combination is comparable to concomitant administration of travoprost 0.004% and timolol 0.5% once daily.
- Trial designs and primary outcome measures: Not fully detailed in available sources beyond the above efficacy and exposure summaries.
