# Editorial Product Information: Tetanus Toxoid-Containing Vaccines (dTpa and Hib Conjugate)

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (TGA), US (FDA).
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised clinical editorial overview of two distinct vaccine preparations containing tetanus toxoid: a diphtheria, tetanus, and acellular pertussis (dTpa) booster vaccine [Australian] and a *Haemophilus influenzae* type b (Hib) conjugate vaccine [US]. 
- This editorial is intended for use by healthcare professionals as a synthesis of multi-jurisdictional information and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Prescribing Information required for clinical practice in a specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** 
    - **Adacel [Australian]:** Diphtheria toxoid, Tetanus toxoid, Pertussis toxoid, Pertussis filamentous haemagglutinin, Pertactin, and Pertussis fimbriae 2+3.
    - **ActHIB [US]:** *Haemophilus influenzae* type b capsular polysaccharide (PRP) covalently bound to Tetanus Toxoid.
- **Brand Names:** 
    - **Adacel** [Australian]
    - **ActHIB** [US]
- **ATC Code:** J07AJ52 [Australian]; Not specified in US source.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Adacel [Australian]:** Sterile suspension for injection. Each 0.5 mL dose contains: Diphtheria toxoid ($\ge$ 2 IU), Tetanus toxoid ($\ge$ 20 IU), Pertussis toxoid (2.5 mcg), Pertussis filamentous haemagglutinin (5 mcg), Pertactin (3 mcg), and Pertussis fimbriae 2+3 (5 mcg).
    - **ActHIB [US]:** Lyophilized powder for reconstitution with 0.4% Sodium Chloride diluent. Each 0.5 mL reconstituted dose contains 10 mcg of purified capsular polysaccharide conjugated to 24 mcg of inactivated tetanus toxoid.
- **Route of Administration:** Intramuscular (IM) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** 
    - **Adacel [Australian]:** Sanofi-aventis australia pty ltd.
    - **ActHIB [US]:** Sanofi Vaccines US Inc.

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Active Immunization against Tetanus, Diphtheria, and Pertussis:** Indicated as a booster dose in individuals aged 10 years and older [Australian].
- **Maternal Immunization:** Administration during pregnancy to prevent pertussis in young infants via transplacental antibody transfer [Australian].
- **Prevention of Invasive Hib Disease:** Indicated for the prevention of invasive disease caused by *H. influenzae* type b in infants and children aged 2 months through 5 years [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Dose Volume:** 0.5 mL administered via the intramuscular route.
- **Adacel Booster [Australian]:** A single dose is recommended according to national guidelines, typically at 5- to 10-year intervals. For those with unknown or incomplete primary series, Adacel is given as one part of a three-dose series (the remaining two doses should be diphtheria and tetanus containing vaccines).
- **ActHIB Series [US]:** A four-dose series at 2, 4, and 6 months of age, followed by a booster at 15–18 months of age.

### Administration Instructions
- **Site:** The preferred site is the deltoid muscle [Australian, US] or the anterolateral aspect of the thigh in infants [US].
- **Preparation:** 
    - **Adacel [Australian]:** Shake the pre-filled syringe well to obtain a uniform, cloudy, white suspension. 
    - **ActHIB [US]:** Reconstitute the lyophilized vial only with the provided 0.4% Sodium Chloride diluent. Agitate thoroughly until clear and colorless.
- **Contraindicated Routes:** Do not administer intravenously [Australian, US], intradermally, or subcutaneously [US]. Do not inject into the gluteal area [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Severe Allergic Reaction:** History of anaphylaxis or severe reaction after a previous dose of these vaccines or any vaccine component [Australian, US].
- **Encephalopathy [Australian]:** History of encephalopathy of unknown origin within 7 days of a previous pertussis-containing vaccine.
- **Neurological Complications [Australian]:** Previous neurological complications following immunisation with antigens contained in the vaccine.
- **Acute Disease [Australian]:** Moderate or severe febrile or acute disease requires postponement of vaccination (low-grade fever is not a contraindication).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Guillain-Barré Syndrome (GBS):** If GBS occurred within 6 weeks of a prior tetanus toxoid-containing vaccine, the decision to vaccinate must be based on a careful risk-benefit assessment [Australian, US].
- **Syncope:** Fainting can occur; procedures should be in place to prevent falling injuries [Australian, US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Adrenaline (epinephrine) must be immediately available to manage potential anaphylactic reactions [Australian, US].
- **Bleeding Disorders:** Administer with caution to individuals with thrombocytopenia or coagulation disorders due to the risk of bleeding following IM injection [Australian].
- **Altered Immune Status:** Immunocompromised individuals may have a reduced immune response. Vaccination should ideally be delayed until the completion of immunosuppressive treatment [Australian, US].
- **Latex [Australian]:** The pre-filled syringe tip caps contain natural rubber latex, which may cause reactions in sensitive individuals. (Note: **ActHIB [US]** vial stoppers are not made with natural rubber latex).
- **Limitations:** Vaccination may not protect 100% of individuals. **ActHIB** does not substitute for routine tetanus immunization [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequencies in Adolescents and Adults (Adacel) [Australian]
- **Very Common ($\ge$ 10%):** Injection site pain, swelling, redness; headache, decreased energy, myalgia (body ache).
- **Common (1–10%):** Fever, chills, nausea, diarrhea, sore or swollen joints.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Vomiting.

### Frequencies in Infants and Children (ActHIB) [US]
- **Very Common ($\ge$ 10%):** Fussiness/irritability (75%), inconsolable crying (58%), decreased activity/lethargy (51%), and drowsiness (36.4%).
- **Common (1–10%):** Injection site tenderness (20% in 15-20 month olds), anorexia, and fever ($>$ 39.0°C).

### Serious and Post-marketing Events
- **Immune System:** Anaphylactic reactions, angioedema, urticaria [Australian, US].
- **Nervous System:** Convulsions, paraesthesia, brachial neuritis, facial palsy, myelitis [Australian].
- **Injection Site:** Large reactions ($>$ 50 mm), extensive limb swelling, sterile abscess [Australian, US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Concomitant Vaccination:** May be administered simultaneously with other vaccines (e.g., Influenza, Hepatitis B, MMR, IPV) at separate injection sites [Australian, US]. 
- **Specific Interactions [Australian]:** Concomitant administration with Menactra resulted in lower antibody responses to pertussis antigens (PRN and FIM), though the clinical significance is unknown.
- **Immunosuppressive Therapy:** May reduce the vaccine's immune response [Australian, US].
- **Laboratory Test Interference [US]:** Hib capsular polysaccharide antigens may be detected in urine for 1–2 weeks following **ActHIB** vaccination, potentially leading to false-positive Hib diagnostic results.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:**
    - **Adacel [Australian]:** Category A. Extensive data from clinical trials and observational studies (over 80,000 outcomes) show no increased risk of malformations or fetal/neonatal harm.
    - **ActHIB [US]:** Not approved for use in adults; no human or animal data available.
- **Breastfeeding:**
    - **Adacel [Australian]:** The effect on breastfed infants has not been studied. As the vaccine is inactivated, risk is considered improbable; clinical assessment of risk-benefit is recommended.
    - **ActHIB [US]:** Not approved for use in adults; impact on milk production or the infant is unknown.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - **Adacel [Australian]:** Approved for children $\ge$ 10 years; not for primary immunisation.
    - **ActHIB [US]:** Approved for infants and children 2 months through 5 years. Safety not established below 6 weeks or above 5 years.
- **Elderly Use [Australian]:** Adacel is indicated for use in the elderly.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Adacel [Australian]:** Induces specific antibodies against *C. tetani*, *C. diphtheriae*, and *B. pertussis* toxins and antigens.
    - **ActHIB [US]:** Induces a T-dependent immune response against the PRP capsular polysaccharide of *H. influenzae* type b.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Not required for vaccines [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adacel [Australian]:** Pivotal trials (TC9704, Td9805, Td9707) demonstrated that a single booster dose provides antibody levels for tetanus and diphtheria comparable to Td vaccines. Long-term studies indicate protection is maintained for at least 10 years.
- **ActHIB [US]:** US trials in infants showed that 90% achieved protective anti-PRP levels ($\ge$ 1.0 mcg/mL) after a primary series, increasing to 98% following the booster dose.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 2°C to 8°C. Refrigerate; do not freeze [Australian, US].
- **Reconstituted Stability [US]:** Reconstituted **ActHIB** should be used within 24 hours if stored at 2°C to 8°C.
- **Temperature Excursions [Australian]:** **Adacel** is stable at temperatures up to 25°C for 72 hours.
- **Shelf Life [Australian]:** 48 months.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australian [TGA]:** Schedule S4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **US [FDA]:** Rx Only.
