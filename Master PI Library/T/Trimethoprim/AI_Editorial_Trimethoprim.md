# Editorial Product Information: Trimethoprim

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of trimethoprim for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, current PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved documentation for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Trimethoprim (a synthetic antibacterial; pyrimidine derivative).
- **Brand Names:** Alprim [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J01EA01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - 300 mg tablets [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Alphapharm Pty Ltd trading as Viatris [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Urinary Tract Infections (UTI):** Treatment of acute infections caused by susceptible organisms [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Administration:** May be taken with food to reduce gastrointestinal upset. For maximal urinary concentration, bedtime administration may be advantageous [Australian].
- **Adults and Children (>12 years):** 300 mg once daily for 7 days [Australian].
- **Children (6 to 12 years):** 150 mg (half of a 300 mg tablet) once daily for 7 days [Australian].
- **Children (<6 years):** Appropriate dosing information is currently unavailable in source documentation [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to trimethoprim or any excipients (e.g., lactose, sulfites) [Australian].
- Severe renal impairment (creatinine clearance < 10 mL/min) unless repeated plasma concentration monitoring is available [Australian].
- Severe hepatic insufficiency [Australian].
- Severe haematological disorders or documented megaloblastic anaemia due to folate deficiency [Australian].
- **Pregnancy and Lactation:** Contraindicated during pregnancy and the nursing period [Australian].
- **Paediatrics:** Contraindicated in premature infants or children under 4 months of age [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
- **Folate Deficiency:** Caution is required in patients with suspected folate deficiency. Supplemental folinic acid (3–6 mg/day) may be used without compromising antibacterial efficacy (except in *Enterococci* infections). Monthly blood counts are recommended for long-term therapy [Australian].
- **Electrolyte Abnormalities:** Risk of hyperkalaemia, particularly in the elderly, those with renal insufficiency, diabetes, or those taking concomitant potassium-sparing drugs, ACE inhibitors, or ARBs. Monitoring of serum potassium and sodium (risk of hyponatraemia) is advised [Australian].
- **Dermatologic Reactions:** Trimethoprim must be discontinued immediately if a skin rash appears [Australian].
- **Porphyria:** Considered unsafe; associated with acute attacks [Australian].
- **Phenylketonuria:** Impairs phenylalanine metabolism, though clinical significance in patients on dietary restriction is low [Australian].
- **Haematopoiesis:** Rare interference with blood cell production. Long-term treatment requires haematological monitoring, especially in the elderly [Australian].
- **Hereditary Conditions:** Contains lactose; contraindicated in patients with galactose intolerance, total lactase deficiency, or glucose-galactose malabsorption [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally mild and reversible upon discontinuation.

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (>1%)** | Rash, pruritus (incidence ~7.9%), headache, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, glossitis, monilial overgrowth [Australian]. |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | SJS/TEN (Lyell’s syndrome), photosensitivity, erythema nodosum, pancytopenia, agranulocytosis, aseptic meningitis (reversible), uveitis, hepatic necrosis (potentially fatal), cholestatic jaundice, pancreatitis, pseudomembranous colitis [Australian]. |
| **Unknown Frequency** | DRESS (Drug Rash with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms), hyperkalaemia, hyponatraemia, raised serum creatinine/BUN [Australian]. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Antimalarials:** Pyrimethamine increases the risk of megaloblastic anaemia [Australian].
- **Anticoagulants:** May potentiate the effect of Warfarin and other coumarins [Australian].
- **Antiepileptics and Cardiac Glycosides:** Increased serum levels of phenytoin, digoxin, and procainamide [Australian].
- **Antivirals:** Reduced renal excretion/increased concentrations of zidovudine, zalcitabine, and lamivudine [Australian].
- **Immunosuppressants:** Increased risk of nephrotoxicity with ciclosporin; increased haematologic toxicity with azathioprine or mercaptopurine [Australian].
- **RAAS Inhibitors/Diuretics:** ACE inhibitors, ARBs, and potassium-sparing diuretics increase hyperkalaemia risk. Thiazides increase the risk of thrombocytopenia with purpura in the elderly [Australian].
- **Antibiotics:** Rifampicin may decrease trimethoprim concentrations [Australian].
- **Antidiabetics:** May enhance the hypoglycaemic effect of repaglinide [Australian].
- **Folate Antagonists:** Concomitant use with methotrexate increases the risk of pancytopenia [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3 [Australian].** Trimethoprim interferes with folic acid metabolism. High-dose animal studies show birth defects typical of folate antagonism. Use is contraindicated in pregnancy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Trimethoprim is excreted in breast milk. Use is contraindicated; alternative feeding arrangements are required if the mother must take the drug [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No human data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Contraindicated in infants <4 months. Limited data for children <6 years [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased susceptibility to blood disorders and electrolyte imbalances. Lower doses may be advisable [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - CrCl 15–30 mL/min: Reduced dose should be considered.
    - CrCl < 15 mL/min: Use is not recommended.
    - CrCl < 10 mL/min: Contraindicated unless plasma monitoring is available [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution; contraindicated in severe insufficiency [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Synthetic antibacterial that reversibly inhibits dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR), blocking the conversion of dihydrofolic acid to tetrahydrofolic acid. It is highly selective for bacterial DHFR over mammalian DHFR [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; peak plasma levels in 0.6 to 4 hours. Food reduces AUC by ~20%.
    - **Distribution:** ~44% protein bound. High concentrations in vaginal secretions, prostate tissue, and kidneys.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily excreted unchanged.
    - **Elimination:** Chiefly renal (glomerular filtration/tubular secretion). Half-life is 8–12 hours in normal renal function [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No clinical trial data provided in the source documentation [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C.
- **Container:** PVC/PVDC/Al blister packs.
- **Disposal:** Unused medicine should be returned to a pharmacy [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
