# Editorial Product Information: Tralokinumab

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesizes the Product Information (PI) for tralokinumab based on the documentation provided from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of tralokinumab for use by healthcare professionals. It serves as a clinically accurate synthesis of regulatory information across jurisdictions (where available).
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official, legally approved Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s current approved PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tralokinumab-ldrm. 
- **Chemical Class:** Interleukin-13 (IL-13) antagonist; human IgG4 monoclonal antibody.
- **Brand Name:** ADBRY [United States].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Single-dose prefilled syringe with needle guard:** 150 mg/mL [United States].
    - **Single-dose autoinjector:** 300 mg/2 mL [United States].
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** LEO Pharma Inc. [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Atopic Dermatitis:** Treatment of moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis in adults and pediatric patients 12 years of age and older whose disease is not adequately controlled with topical prescription therapies or when those therapies are not advisable. **Tralokinumab** may be used with or without topical corticosteroids [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Initial Loading Dose:** 600 mg (administered as four 150 mg injections or two 300 mg injections) [United States].
- **Maintenance Dose:** 300 mg every other week (Q2W) [United States].
- **Weight-Based Adjustment:** For adults weighing <100 kg who achieve clear or almost clear skin after 16 weeks of treatment, a maintenance dose of 300 mg every 4 weeks may be considered [United States].

### Dosing in Pediatrics (12–17 years)
- **Initial Loading Dose:** 300 mg (administered as two 150 mg injections) [United States].
- **Maintenance Dose:** 150 mg every other week (Q2W) [United States].

### Administration Instructions
- Administer via subcutaneous injection into the abdomen or thigh. The upper arm may be used if administered by a caregiver. Do not inject within 2 inches (5 cm) of the navel.
- Rotate injection sites with each dose.
- **Autoinjector Restriction:** In the United States, the 300 mg autoinjector is approved for use in adults only.
- **Supervision:** In pediatric patients ≥12 years, it is recommended that the drug be administered by or under the supervision of an adult [United States].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- If a dose is missed, administer it as soon as possible. Resume the regular scheduled dosing thereafter [United States].

### Preparation for Use
- **Prefilled Syringe:** Allow 30 minutes to reach room temperature before injection.
- **Autoinjector:** Allow 45 minutes to reach room temperature before injection [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **tralokinumab** or any of its excipients [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity:** Systemic reactions, including anaphylaxis and angioedema, have been reported. If a serious reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate appropriate therapy [United States].
- **Ocular Adverse Reactions:** Conjunctivitis and keratitis occurred more frequently in patients receiving **tralokinumab**. Patients should report new onset or worsening eye symptoms (e.g., eye pain, vision changes) [United States].
- **Parasitic (Helminth) Infections:** It is unknown if **tralokinumab** influences the immune response against helminth infections. Pre-existing infections should be treated before initiation. If a patient becomes infected during treatment and does not respond to anti-helminth therapy, discontinue **tralokinumab** until the infection resolves [United States].
- **Vaccinations:** Complete all age-appropriate vaccinations prior to initiation. Avoid the use of live vaccines during treatment. Data on non-live vaccines suggest antibody responses are not significantly impaired [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Data
- **Very Common (>10%):** Upper respiratory tract infections (including nasopharyngitis and pharyngitis) [United States].
- **Common (1–10%):** 
    - Conjunctivitis (including allergic conjunctivitis) [United States].
    - Injection site reactions (pain, swelling, erythema) [United States].
    - Eosinophilia (including increased eosinophil count) [United States].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Keratoconjunctivitis, keratitis [United States].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Hypersensitivity reactions (anaphylaxis, angioedema).
- Severe keratitis (rarely reported leading to discontinuation).

### Clinical Laboratory Findings
- **Eosinophilia:** A mean initial increase in eosinophil count from baseline was observed (typically by week 4) but generally declined to baseline levels with continued treatment [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions (CYP450):** Tralokinumab does not exert a clinically significant effect on the pharmacokinetics of CYP450 substrates (midazolam, warfarin, omeprazole, metoprolol, or caffeine) [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Classification:** Not assigned a specific letter category; the PI provides a risk summary.
    - Data in humans are limited. Monoclonal antibodies are known to be transported across the placenta, particularly in the third trimester. Animal studies (monkeys) at 10 times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) showed no adverse developmental effects [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on the presence of **tralokinumab** in human milk or its effects on the breastfed infant. Maternal IgG is known to be present in human milk. The potential risks to the infant should be weighed against the benefits of treatment to the mother [United States].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in monkeys showed no effects on reproductive organs, menstrual cycles, or sperm analysis [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established for patients 12–17 years of age. Safety and efficacy in patients <12 years have not been established [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** Data for patients ≥65 years are limited (n=77 in trials). No specific dose adjustments are currently recommended, though caution is advised due to limited sample size [United States].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for mild or moderate renal impairment. The effect of severe renal impairment has not been studied [United States].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for mild hepatic impairment. The effect of moderate to severe hepatic impairment has not been studied [United States].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Tralokinumab** is a human IgG4 monoclonal antibody that specifically binds to the cytokine IL-13 and inhibits its interaction with the IL-13Rα1 and IL-13Rα2 subunits. This blocks the IL-13/IL-4Rα receptor complex signaling, thereby inhibiting the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines, chemokines, and IgE [United States].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Treatment is associated with decreased concentrations of Th2 and Th22 immunity biomarkers (e.g., TARC/CCL17, periostin, IL-22, serum IgE) [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability estimated at 76%; $t_{max}$ is 5 to 8 days.
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is approximately 4.2 L.
    - **Elimination:** Half-life is approximately 3 weeks; clearance is 0.149 L/day.
    - **Metabolism:** Expected to be degraded into small peptides via catabolic pathways [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adult Studies (ECZTRA 1, 2, and 3):** Three randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in 1,934 adults with moderate-to-severe AD. 
    - **Primary Endpoints:** Proportion of subjects with IGA 0 or 1 ("clear" or "almost clear") and EASI-75 at Week 16.
    - **Results:** Significantly higher proportions of tralokinumab-treated patients achieved IGA 0/1 and EASI-75 compared to placebo [United States].
- **Pediatric Study (ECZTRA 6):** Trial in 289 subjects aged 12–17. At Week 16, 21% of those on 150 mg Q2W achieved IGA 0/1 compared to 4% on placebo [United States].
- **Long-term Safety (ECZTEND):** Open-label extension study assessing safety up to 5 years (268 weeks); the safety profile remained consistent with earlier trials [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Refrigeration:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) in the original carton to protect from light [United States].
- **Room Temperature Stability:** Once removed from the refrigerator, the product may be kept at room temperature (up to 30°C/86°F) for a maximum of 14 days. If not used within 14 days, the product must be discarded. Do not return to the refrigerator after it has reached room temperature [United States].
- **Special Handling:** Do not freeze. Do not shake. Do not expose to heat or direct sunlight [United States].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription Only) [United States].
