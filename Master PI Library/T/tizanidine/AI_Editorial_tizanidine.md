# Editorial Product Information: Tizanidine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a single, clinically focused reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource only. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tizanidine hydrochloride, a central alpha-2-adrenergic agonist.
- **Brand Names:** Tizanidine (Generic); repackaged versions available (e.g., RemedyRepack) [US].
- **ATC Code:** M03BX02.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Tablets:** 2 mg and 4 mg [US].
    - **Capsules:** (Pharmacokinetic data provided, though specific strengths for this manufacturer focus on 4 mg tablets) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Unichem Pharmaceuticals (USA) Inc. (Manufacturer); RemedyRepack Inc. (Repackager) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Spasticity:** Indicated for the management of spasticity. Because of the short duration of therapeutic effect, treatment should be reserved for those daily activities and times when relief of spasticity is most important [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** The recommended starting dose is 2 mg every 6 to 8 hours. Tizanidine may be administered up to a maximum of three doses in a 24-hour period [US].
- **Titration:** Dosage may be increased gradually by 2 mg to 4 mg per dose, with 1 to 4 days between titration steps. 
- **Maximum Dose:** 36 mg total daily dosage. Single doses exceeding 16 mg have not been studied [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Tizanidine can be taken with or without food, but administration must be **consistent**. 
    - Pharmacokinetics vary significantly between the fed and fasted states, and between tablets and capsules. Switching between these states or dosage forms may result in altered tolerability or symptom control [US].
- **Discontinuation:** To avoid withdrawal symptoms (rebound hypertension, tachycardia, hypertonia), the dose should be tapered slowly by 2 mg to 4 mg per day, especially in patients on high doses (20–28 mg/day) for long periods [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Strong CYP1A2 Inhibitors:** Concomitant use with potent CYP1A2 inhibitors, such as fluvoxamine or ciprofloxacin, is contraindicated due to significant increases in tizanidine serum concentrations [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Contraindicated in patients with known hypersensitivity to tizanidine or any of its excipients. Anaphylaxis and angioedema have been reported [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypotension:** Tizanidine can cause clinically significant hypotension (syncope, orthostatic effects). Risk is increased with concurrent antihypertensives and strong CYP1A2 inhibitors. Monitor blood pressure closely during titration [US].
- **Liver Injury:** Hepatocellular injury may occur. Monitoring of aminotransferase (ALT) levels is recommended at baseline and 1 month after reaching the maximum dose, or if hepatic injury is suspected [US].
- **Sedation:** Very common; may interfere with daily activities. Effects are additive with alcohol and other CNS depressants [US].
- **Hallucinosis/Psychotic-Like Symptoms:** Associated with visual hallucinations or delusions (reported in 3% of patients in clinical trials). Discontinuation should be considered if these occur [US].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Monitor for signs of anaphylaxis, urticaria, or angioedema [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are dose-related. In multiple-dose studies, three-quarters of reactions were mild to moderate [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Strong CYP1A2 Inhibitors:** (e.g., Fluvoxamine, Ciprofloxacin). Contraindicated. Fluvoxamine increases tizanidine AUC by 33-fold; ciprofloxacin increases AUC by 10-fold [US].
- **Moderate/Weak CYP1A2 Inhibitors:** (e.g., Zileuton, Amiodarone, Mexiletine, Propafenone, Verapamil, Cimetidine, Famotidine, Acyclovir, Ticlopidine). Avoid concomitant use; if necessary, use with extreme caution [US].
- **Oral Contraceptives:** Concomitant use is not recommended as they may reduce tizanidine clearance by 50% [US].
- **Alcohol and CNS Depressants:** Additive sedative effects. Alcohol increases tizanidine AUC and peak concentrations [US].
- **Alpha-2-Adrenergic Agonists:** (e.g., Clonidine). Use is not recommended due to cumulative hypotensive effects [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned under PLLR]. Animal data show developmental toxicity (embryofetal and postnatal mortality) at doses lower than the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD). Use only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Presence in human milk is unknown. Animal studies show tizanidine is excreted in the milk of lactating animals. Caution is advised [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats showed reduced fertility in both males and females at high doses [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established. Juvenile animal data show delayed sexual maturation and neurobehavioral deficits [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clearance is decreased four-fold in older adults. Use with caution; monitor renal function as tizanidine is substantially excreted by the kidneys [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** In patients with CrCl <25 mL/min, clearance is reduced by more than 50%. Use lower individual doses during titration. If higher doses are needed, increase the dose amount rather than the frequency [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Tizanidine is extensively metabolised by the liver. Use with caution; dose reduction is recommended [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Central alpha-2-adrenergic receptor agonist. It reduces spasticity by increasing presynaptic inhibition of motor neurons, primarily through polysynaptic pathways.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Peak effects on muscle tone occur 1–2 hours post-dose and dissipate by 3–6 hours [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Essentially complete; absolute bioavailability is approximately 40% due to extensive first-pass metabolism.
    - **Distribution:** Vd of 2.4 L/kg; 30% bound to plasma proteins.
    - **Metabolism:** 95% metabolised, primarily via CYP1A2. Metabolites are inactive.
    - **Elimination:** Half-life is approximately 2.5 hours. Excreted via urine (60%) and feces (20%) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Single-Dose Study (MS):** 140 patients received 8 mg or 16 mg. Significant reduction in Ashworth score (muscle tone) was seen at 1, 2, and 3 hours post-dose, correlating with plasma concentration [US].
- **Seven-Week Study (Spinal Cord Injury):** 118 patients titrated up to 36 mg/day. Significant reduction in muscle tone and spasm frequency observed. No consistent advantage was seen in activities of daily living [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F).
- **Packaging:** Dispense in tight, light-resistant containers [US].
