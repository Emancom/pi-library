# Editorial Product Information: Terbutaline Sulfate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following Product Information (PI) document:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: Bricanyl Injection (terbutaline sulfate) solution for injection.
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of terbutaline sulfate for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory information into a single clinical reference.
- **Note**: This is an editorial synthesis and is not intended to replace the official PI for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Terbutaline sulfate (a sympathomimetic bronchodilator; selective $\beta_2$-adrenoceptor agonist).
- **Brand Names**: Bricanyl [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: R03CC03 (Note: Not explicitly in source, but standard for terbutaline).
- **Dosage Form and Strength**: 
    - Solution for injection: 0.5 mg/mL [Australian].
- **Route of Administration**: Subcutaneous injection [Australian].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: AstraZeneca Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Bronchospasm Relief**: For the treatment of acute bronchospasm associated with asthma or chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) [Australian].
- **Prophylaxis**: For acute prevention of exercise-induced asthma or other known triggers of bronchospasm [Australian].
- **Note**: The injection solution is recommended for acute use only [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Children > 12 years)
- **Standard Dose**: 0.5 mL (0.25 mg) administered by subcutaneous injection [Australian].
- **Frequency**: May be repeated as required, up to every 6 hours [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Route**: Strictly for subcutaneous use [Australian]. 
- **Inspection**: Solutions should be clear and colourless; do not use if the solution is discoloured [Australian].

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources (primarily indicated for acute/PRN use).

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to sympathomimetic amines or any excipient in the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- None listed in available sources.

### Cardiovascular Effects
- **General Caution**: Use with caution in patients with hypertension, coronary artery disease, or arrhythmias [Australian].
- **Myocardial Ischaemia**: Rare reports of myocardial ischaemia have been associated with $\beta$-agonists. Patients with severe underlying heart disease should seek medical attention if they experience chest pain or worsening symptoms [Australian].
- **Arrhythmogenic Potential**: $\beta_2$-agonists may induce cardiac arrhythmias, which must be considered for every patient [Australian].

### Metabolic and Endocrine Precautions
- **Hyperthyroidism**: Use with caution in patients with thyrotoxicosis or uncontrolled hyperthyroidism [Australian].
- **Diabetes**: $\beta_2$-agonists may increase blood glucose. Initial monitoring of blood glucose is recommended in diabetic patients [Australian].
- **Hypokalaemia**: Potentially serious hypokalaemia may occur, especially in acute severe asthma. This risk is augmented by hypoxia and concomitant treatments. Monitoring of serum potassium is recommended [Australian].
- **Lactic Acidosis**: Reported with high therapeutic doses of parenteral short-acting $\beta$-agonists. Consider lactic acidosis in patients not responding adequately to acute therapy [Australian].

### Monitoring Requirements
- Serum potassium (in severe asthma or if on concomitant potassium-depleting therapy).
- Blood glucose (in diabetic patients).
- Cardiovascular status (heart rate, rhythm, and symptoms of ischaemia).

## 6. Adverse Effects
Side effects are characteristic of sympathomimetic amines. The incidence and severity are dose-dependent and typically minimal at recommended therapeutic doses.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (>1%)** | Tremor, headache, nervousness, tachycardia, palpitations, tonic muscle cramps, hypokalaemia. |
| **Less Common (<1%)** | Ectopic beats, nausea, vomiting, bad taste, diarrhoea, sweating, muscle twitching, drowsiness, dizziness, sleep/behavioural disturbances (agitation, hyperactivity, restlessness), rash, urticaria, exanthema. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Lactic acidosis, cardiac arrhythmias (atrial fibrillation, supraventricular tachycardia, extrasystoles), myocardial ischaemia. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Other Sympathomimetic Amines**: Use with caution as excess sympathetic stimulation may occur [Australian].
- **$\beta$-Adrenergic Blocking Drugs**: These agents (including eye drops) may inhibit the effect of terbutaline and increase airway resistance in asthmatics [Australian].
- **Halogenated Anaesthetics**: Halothane should be avoided due to increased risk of arrhythmias. Other halogenated anaesthetics require cautious use [Australian].
- **Potassium-Depleting Agents**: Concomitant use with diuretics, methyl xanthines, or corticosteroids increases the risk of hypokalaemia and subsequent cardiac arrhythmias (and predisposes to digoxin toxicity) [Australian].
- **Systemic Corticosteroids**: Combination with parenteral $\beta$-agonists increases blood glucose and the risk of pulmonary oedema when used with intravenous fluids [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **Category A [Australian]**: No adverse effects on pregnant women or fetuses reported; however, caution is recommended during the first trimester.
    - **Preterm Infants**: Transient hypoglycaemia reported in preterm newborns following maternal $\beta_2$-agonist treatment [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding**: Terbutaline is secreted into breast milk at concentrations similar to maternal plasma. The infant receives approximately 0.2–0.7% of the maternal dose. No signs of $\beta$-adrenoceptor stimulation were seen in limited infant studies [Australian].
- **Fertility**: No data available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Dosing provided for children over 12 years (0.5 mL SC). No specific data available for younger children in provided sources [Australian].
- **Elderly Use**: No specific data available [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment**: Caution is required as terbutaline sulfate is largely excreted in the urine [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Hepatic failure does not appear to influence metabolism; however, caution is advised in patients with impaired liver function [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Selective $\beta_2$-adrenoceptor agonist. It provides bronchodilation by stimulating pulmonary $\beta_2$-receptors with minimal effect on cardiac $\beta_1$-receptors [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics**: 
    - **Onset of Action**: Rapid; reaches maximum effect about 30 minutes after subcutaneous injection [Australian].
    - **Duration of Action**: 4 to 5 hours [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Metabolism**: Not metabolised by COMT (due to phenolic hydroxyl groups). Oral/swallowed drug is conjugated in the GI mucosa [Australian].
    - **Elimination**: Absorbed unchanged from the respiratory tract and excreted primarily as such in the urine. Total elimination occurs within 72 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No data provided in the source documents.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Light Protection**: Protect from light. Terbutaline is sensitive to excessive heat and light [Australian].
- **Shelf Life**: Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].
