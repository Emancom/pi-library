# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, regulatory requirements, and pharmacological profiles from official product labeling.
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis designed for professional reference and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not specified in available source, provided for clinical context).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Doses should be administered every 3 or 4 hours; intervals must not be less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Titration:** Dose adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations [US].

### Monitoring and Instructions
- **Target Plasma Levels:** Aim for free carnitine concentrations between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Preparation:** Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** There are no officially listed contraindications in the available source; however, see Warnings for hypersensitivity reactions [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occurred within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate risks before re-administration [US].
- **Neurologic Effects (Seizures):** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a known seizure history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** While the safety of the intravenous form is established, chronic high-dose *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Monitoring:** Periodic monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations is recommended [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic errors.

### Very Common (>10%) [US]
- **General:** Injection site reaction (27–59%), Pain (21–49%), Flu syndrome (15–40%), Chest pain (6–15%), Headache (3–37%), Fever (5–12%), Abdominal pain (5–21%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (14–21%), Hypotension (3–19%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), Vomiting (9–21%), Nausea (5–12%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–33%), Cough increase (10–18%), Dyspnea (3–11%), Rhinitis (6–11%).
- **Neurologic:** Dizziness (10–18%), Paresthesia (3–12%).

### Common (1–10%) [US]
- **General:** Asthenia, Back pain, Infection, Allergic reaction.
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, Palpitations, Arrhythmia, Atrial fibrillation.
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia, Hyperkalemia, Hypervolemia, Peripheral edema, Weight increase/decrease.
- **Digestive:** Anorexia, Constipation, Dyspepsia, Melena.
- **Nervous:** Depression, Insomnia, Anxiety, Vertigo.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Anaphylaxis and Angioedema:** Potentially life-threatening hypersensitivity [US].
- **Seizures:** Reported post-marketing in both predisposed and non-predisposed individuals [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. **Management:** Monitor INR levels frequently after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate, well-controlled studies exist in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine milk concentrations following administration. The benefits of maternal supplementation must be weighed against the potential risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg). Safety and efficacy are established for the indicated uses [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources, though clinical trials for ESRD included adult populations.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. Dose adjustments are based on pre-dialysis trough levels [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Distributed to all tissues except the brain (fatty acids are not a primary energy source there) [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly cleared, thereby mitigating metabolic acidosis in organic acidopathies [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Efficacy:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x weekly post-dialysis), mean trough levels increased from 21.3 µmol/L at baseline to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) demonstrated a dose-dependent increase in trough concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Outcome Limitations:** While IV levocarnitine effectively increases carnitine plasma concentrations in ESRD, the impact on specific clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, malaise) in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Precautions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Single-use:** Discard any unused portion of an opened vial as the formulation is preservative-free [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription Only Medicine). NDC 36000-368-10 (10-pack).
