# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesised from official Product Information (PI) source documents provided for the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a structured reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource only. It is not a substitute for the official, legally approved Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for mitochondrial fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The standard dose is 50 mg/kg. This may be administered as a slow bolus (over 2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion. 
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is typically followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and not less than every 6 hours).
    - Subsequent daily maintenance doses are generally in the range of 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been used [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting range is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). 
    - Administration should occur as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line immediately following each dialysis session [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for discoloration or particulate matter before use.
- **Compatibility:** Levocarnitine is stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s solution (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

### Monitoring and Titration
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels is recommended. The target plasma free carnitine concentration is 35 to 60 µmol/L [US].
- **ESRD:** Initiation of therapy may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma carnitine levels <40 to 50 µmol/L. Dose adjustments (e.g., reduction to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of treatment based on trough levels [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- Not specified in available sources.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in dialysis patients. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued and appropriate medical treatment initiated. Re-administration requires careful risk-benefit assessment and close monitoring [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- **ESRD/Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been established in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trials Experience (ESRD)
Adverse events reported in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials at a frequency of ≥5% (regardless of causality) include:

| Body System | Adverse Effect (Incidence ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, back pain, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, palpitations, arrhythmia |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, anorexia, dyspepsia, constipation |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional**| Hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight changes |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia, depression |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis |
| **Skin/Special Senses** | Pruritus, rash, taste perversion, eye disorders |

### Serious and Notable Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting are common. Body odor and gastritis are observed less frequently [US].
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (postmarketing reports) [US].
- **Immune:** Anaphylaxis and other severe hypersensitivity reactions [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
    - **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not specified in available sources (FDA lettered categories are no longer used in current labelling for new updates, though historical data is provided)]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (BSA) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. In dairy cows, carnitine concentrations in milk increase following administration. Clinicians should weigh the benefits of maternal therapy against the potential risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies indicate no impairment of fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations are provided based on body weight (mg/kg) for metabolic disorders; refer to Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** See Section 5. Caution is required regarding the accumulation of metabolites if using oral formulations in patients with severely compromised renal function [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
- **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Study:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly showed that trough concentrations reached 54–180 µmol/L (normal range is 40–50 µmol/L) after one week (3 doses) and remained stable or increased thereafter [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg three times weekly in ESRD patients (N≈30 per group) demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Special Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing.
- **Stability:** Vials are single-dose only and contain no preservative; discard any unused portion [US].
