# Editorial Product Information: Trofinetide

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of trofinetide for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Prescribing Information required for clinical practice in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Trofinetide (a synthetic analogue of the amino-terminal tripeptide of insulin-like growth factor 1 [IGF-1]).
- **Brand Name(s):** **DAYBUE**, **DAYBUE STIX** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Oral Solution: 200 mg/mL (strawberry-flavored, pink to red solution) [US].
    - For Oral Solution (Packets): 5,000 mg, 6,000 mg, and 8,000 mg of white to pinkish powder [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral; Gastrostomy (G) tube; Gastrojejunal (GJ) tube (must use G-port).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Acadia Pharmaceuticals Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Rett Syndrome:** Treatment of Rett syndrome in adult and paediatric patients aged 2 years and older [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 General Dosing
Trofinetide is administered twice daily (morning and evening) with or without food. Dosage is determined by patient weight.

**Table 1: Recommended Dosage (Adults and Paediatrics ≥ 2 years)**
| Patient Weight | Recommended Dosage |
| :--- | :--- |
| 9 kg to < 12 kg | 5,000 mg (25 mL) twice daily |
| 12 kg to < 20 kg | 6,000 mg (30 mL) twice daily |
| 20 kg to < 35 kg | 8,000 mg (40 mL) twice daily |
| 35 kg to < 50 kg | 10,000 mg (50 mL) twice daily |
| 50 kg or more | 12,000 mg (60 mL) twice daily |

### 3.2 Preparation and Administration
- **Oral Solution:** Use a calibrated measuring device (e.g., oral syringe). 
- **Packets (STIX):** Must be dissolved in 15 mL to 60 mL (per packet) of cold to room temperature water or water-based beverage (juice, tea, etc.). Stir until dissolved and administer immediately. Do not use partial packets.
- **Enteral Tubes:** May be given via G-tube. For GJ-tubes, administration must occur through the G-port.

### 3.3 Missed Dose
The next dose should be taken at the scheduled time. Doses must not be doubled.

## 4. Contraindications
- None reported in available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Diarrhea:** Highly prevalent (85% in clinical trials). Most cases are mild to moderate, but persistent or recurrent diarrhea is common. **Clinical Management:** Discontinue laxatives prior to starting trofinetide. If diarrhea occurs, initiate antidiarrheal treatment (e.g., loperamide), increase fluid intake, and consider dose reduction or interruption if severe. 
- **Weight Loss:** Significant weight loss (>7% from baseline) was observed in 12% of patients. **Monitoring:** Regularly monitor weight and consider dose adjustments or discontinuation if significant loss occurs.
- **Vomiting and Aspiration Risk:** Vomiting occurred in 29% of treated patients. Patients with Rett syndrome are at baseline risk for aspiration; aspiration pneumonia has been reported following vomiting during trofinetide treatment. **Clinical Management:** Interrupt or reduce dose if vomiting is severe or persistent.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### 6.1 Frequency Data
Adverse reactions occurring in ≥5% of patients and at least 2% more frequently than placebo:

| Adverse Reaction | Trofinetide (n=93) | Placebo (n=94) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Diarrhea** | 82% | 20% |
| **Vomiting** | 29% | 12% |
| **Fever** | 9% | 4% |
| **Seizure** | 9% | 6% |
| **Anxiety** | 8% | 1% |
| **Decreased Appetite** | 8% | 2% |
| **Fatigue** | 8% | 2% |
| **Nasopharyngitis** | 5% | 1% |

### 6.2 Discontinuation Rates
Approximately 19% of patients discontinued treatment due to adverse reactions, with diarrhea (15%) being the primary cause [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 and P-gp Substrates:** Trofinetide is a weak inhibitor of CYP3A4 and an inhibitor of P-gp. Co-administration with sensitive substrates (e.g., loperamide) can increase substrate plasma concentrations (e.g., ~2-fold increase in loperamide Cmax/AUC). **Management:** Closely monitor for adverse reactions when co-administering trofinetide with sensitive CYP3A4 or P-gp substrates where small concentration changes may lead to toxicity.
- **Metabolism:** Trofinetide is not significantly metabolized by CYP450 enzymes and is not a substrate of major drug transporters.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** There are no adequate human data on developmental risks. Animal studies (rats/rabbits) at clinically relevant exposures did not show adverse embryofetal effects [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** No data are available regarding the presence of trofinetide in human milk or its effects on the infant. The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need for the drug.
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility or reproductive function were observed in animal studies.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for patients ≥ 2 years. Safety and effectiveness in patients < 2 years have not been established.
- **Geriatric Use:** Clinical studies did not include patients ≥ 65 years. Caution is advised as elderly patients may have reduced renal function.
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Mild (eGFR 60–89 mL/min):** No dosage adjustment required.
    - **Moderate (eGFR 30–59 mL/min):** Dosage adjustment is required.
    - **Severe (eGFR < 30 mL/min):** Not recommended.

**Table 2: Dosage for Moderate Renal Impairment [US]**
| Weight | Moderate Renal Dosage |
| :--- | :--- |
| 9 kg to < 12 kg | 2,500 mg (12.5 mL) twice daily* |
| 12 kg to < 20 kg | 3,000 mg (15 mL) twice daily* |
| 20 kg to < 35 kg | 4,000 mg (20 mL) twice daily* |
| 35 kg to < 50 kg | 5,000 mg (25 mL) twice daily |
| 50 kg or more | 6,000 mg (30 mL) twice daily |
*\*Note: Doses < 5,000 mg must be administered using the oral solution.*

- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not studied; however, since the drug is primarily renally excreted and not significantly metabolized by the liver, hepatic impairment is not expected to significantly impact exposure.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** The precise mechanism in Rett syndrome is unknown.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Trofinetide does not prolong the QT interval to a clinically relevant extent.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ is 2–3 hours. Bioavailability is ≥84%. High-fat meals reduce $C_{max}$ by ~20% but do not affect total AUC.
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is <6%. Apparent volume of distribution ($V_d$) is ~80 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Not significantly metabolized by CYP450 enzymes.
    - **Elimination:** Effective half-life is ~1.5 hours. Primarily excreted unchanged in urine (~80%).

## 11. Clinical Trials
The efficacy of trofinetide was established in a 12-week, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study (Study 1) of 187 female patients (aged 5–20 years).
- **Primary Outcomes:**
    - **Rett Syndrome Behaviour Questionnaire (RSBQ):** Significant improvement from baseline compared to placebo (p=0.018).
    - **Clinical Global Impression-Improvement (CGI-I):** Significant improvement compared to placebo (p=0.003).
- **Paediatric Data (2–4 years):** Supported by an open-label study showing similar safety and PK profiles to older populations.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Oral Solution:** Store upright and refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze. Discard any unused solution 14 days after opening.
- **Packets (STIX):** Store at room temperature (20°C to 25°C). Dissolved solution must be used immediately.
