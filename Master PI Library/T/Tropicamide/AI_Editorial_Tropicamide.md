# Editorial Product Information: Tropicamide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)** regulatory authority. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing. At the time of writing, only the Australian PI was available for review; clinicians in other regions should consult their local regulatory authorities (e.g., US FDA, European EMA) for region-specific nuances.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tropicamide (an antimuscarinic agent).
- **Chemical Class:** Synthetic tertiary amine antimuscarinic.
- **Brand Names:** Minims Tropicamide [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** S01FA06.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Sterile ophthalmic solution (single-use, preservative-free): 0.5% (5 mg/mL) and 1% (10 mg/mL) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Topical ophthalmic.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Bausch & Lomb (Australia) Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Mydriasis:** Induction of pupillary dilation for diagnostic purposes [Australian].
- **Cycloplegia:** Induction of ciliary muscle paralysis for refraction or ophthalmic examination [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing (including Elderly)
- **Mydriasis:** Instill 1 or 2 drops of the 0.5% solution into the eye(s) 15 to 20 minutes prior to examination [Australian].
- **Cycloplegia:** Instill 1 or 2 drops of the 1% solution, repeated once after 5 minutes. If an extended effect is required, an additional drop may be administered after 20 to 30 minutes [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- For topical ophthalmic use only; **not for injection**.
- To limit systemic absorption, apply pressure to the tear duct (nasolacrimal occlusion) immediately following instillation [Australian].
- **Contact Lenses:** Soft contact lenses must be removed prior to application. Patients should wait at least 15 minutes before reinsertion [Australian].
- Each single-use unit should be discarded immediately after use [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to tropicamide or any excipients in the formulation [Australian].
- Known or suspected narrow-angle glaucoma, as the drug may increase intraocular pressure and precipitate an acute attack [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Intraocular Pressure (IOP):** Tropicamide may significantly increase IOP. Use with caution in patients where increased ocular pressure may be a concern, particularly the elderly [Australian].
- **Systemic Absorption:** Absorption through the conjunctiva is significantly increased in the presence of ocular hyperaemia (inflamed eyes) [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Sensitivity:** Use with caution in patients with conditions sensitive to heart rate acceleration, including:
    - Thyrotoxicosis.
    - Heart failure.
    - Patients undergoing cardiac surgery.
    - Acute myocardial infarction (ischaemia and infarction may be exacerbated by tachycardia) [Australian].
- **Visual Impairment:** Causes transient blurring of vision and photophobia. Patients should be cautioned against driving or operating machinery until vision has cleared and should protect eyes from bright light during dilation [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by physiological system. Frequency data is not specified in available sources.

- **Ocular:** Increased intraocular pressure, photophobia, corneal irritation, stinging/smarting, corneal staining, severe eyelid oedema, and rhinitis [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular:** Transient bradycardia followed by tachycardia, palpitations, and arrhythmias [Australian].
- **Central Nervous System:** Drowsiness, sedation, fatigue, inability to concentrate, psychotic reactions, and behavioural disturbances (higher incidence in paediatric populations) [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Dryness of the mouth, thirst, difficulty swallowing/talking, nausea, vomiting, constipation, and reduced gastrointestinal motility [Australian].
- **Genitourinary:** Difficulty in micturition (urination) [Australian].
- **Respiratory:** Bronchospasm and reduced bronchial secretions [Australian].
- **Integumentary:** Erythematous rash, pruritus, skin flushing, dryness, and increased sweating [Australian].
- **Immune System:** Anaphylaxis [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Antimuscarinic Agents:** Concomitant use of other drugs with antimuscarinic properties may result in additive effects [Australian].
- **Procaine:** Pre-instillation of procaine has been reported to prolong the mydriatic and cycloplegic effects of tropicamide [Australian].
- **Cisapride:** Tropicamide may counteract the gastromotility effects of cisapride [Australian].
- **Procainamide:** Co-administration may lead to additive antivagal effects on atrioventricular (AV) nodal conduction [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category B2. Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. It is unknown if tropicamide causes foetal harm or affects reproductive capacity; use should only occur if the potential benefit justifies the risk [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is not known whether tropicamide is excreted in human milk. Caution should be exercised [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No animal data available to evaluate effects on fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use with extreme caution. Tropicamide has been reported as potentially inadequate for cycloplegia in children. It may cause dangerous CNS disturbances, psychotic reactions, and cardio-respiratory collapse in infants and children [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of elevated intraocular pressure; use with caution and monitor accordingly [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Tropicamide is a topical antimuscarinic that blocks the cholinergic response of the iris sphincter muscle and the ciliary body muscle. This results in pupillary dilation (mydriasis) and paralysis of accommodation (cycloplegia) [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** 
    - Similar action to atropine but with a more rapid onset and shorter duration.
    - **Mydriasis:** Onset 20–40 minutes; duration approximately 6 hours.
    - **Cycloplegia:** Maximal within 30 minutes; recovery of accommodation usually complete within 6 hours [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Not specified in available sources.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No clinical trial data provided in the available source material [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store between 2°C and 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze) [Australian].
- **Handling:** Protect from light [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Schedule 4 (S4) – Prescription Only Medicine.
