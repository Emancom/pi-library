# Editorial Product Information: Tranexamic Acid

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: This editorial was developed based on the official Product Information (PI) from the **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**. No other regional PI documents were provided for comparison at the time of writing.
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory information into a single reference.
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local clinical guidelines required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Tranexamic acid (an antifibrinolytic of the amino acid derivative class).
- **Brand Names**: APO-Tranexamic Acid [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: B02AA02 (Not explicitly specified in the source document).
- **Dosage Form and Strength**: 500 mg film-coated, capsule-shaped, biconvex tablets with a break-line [Australian].
- **Route of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Southern Cross Pharma Pty Ltd [Australian]; Distributed by Arrotex Pharmaceuticals [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hereditary angioneurotic oedema** [Australian].
- **Menorrhagia** [Australian].
- **Traumatic hyphaema**: Short-term use [Australian].
- **Coagulopathies**: Short-term use in patients with established coagulopathies undergoing minor surgery [Australian].
- **Prostatectomy**: Prevention and treatment of haemorrhage [Australian].
- **Dental surgery**: Prevention of haemorrhage in patients with coagulopathies undergoing dental extraction or operations [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Adult Dosing
- **Menorrhagia**: 1 g (2 tablets) four times daily for four days, initiated at the onset of visible bleeding. May be increased to a maximum of 1.5 g (3 tablets) four times daily if required. Patients should be reassessed after three months of use [Australian].
- **Traumatic Hyphaema**: 1 g to 1.5 g every 8 hours for six to seven days [Australian].
- **Hereditary Angioneurotic Oedema**: 1 g to 1.5 g (2-3 tablets) two to three times daily. Treatment may be intermittent (based on symptom onset) or continuous [Australian].
- **Prostatectomy**: 1 g orally six hours pre-operatively, followed by 1 g three to four times daily until macroscopic haematuria resolves. Use beyond two weeks is not recommended [Australian].
- **Minor Surgery (e.g., Conisation of the Cervix)**: 1 g to 1.5 g every 8 to 12 hours for 12 days post-operatively [Australian].
- **Dental Operations/Extraction**: 25 mg/kg administered two hours pre-operatively (often in conjunction with Factor VIII and IX), followed by 25 mg/kg three to four times daily for 6 to 8 days post-operatively [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets may be taken with or without food; parallel food intake does not affect gastrointestinal absorption [Australian].

### Renal Dose Adjustments
Oral dosing must be reduced based on the estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) [Australian]:
| eGFR (mL/min/1.73m²) | Dose | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 60 – 89 | 15 mg/kg body weight | Twice daily |
| 30 – 59 | 15 mg/kg body weight | Daily |
| < 29 | 7.5 mg/kg body weight | Daily |

## 4. Contraindications
- **Active thromboembolic disease**: Including deep vein thrombosis (DVT), pulmonary embolism, and cerebral thrombosis [Australian].
- **Thrombosis risk**: History of or high risk for thrombosis, unless concurrent anticoagulant therapy is possible [Australian].
- **Subarachnoid haemorrhage**: Due to the risk of cerebral oedema and infarction [Australian].
- **Vision disorders**: Acquired disturbances of colour vision [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity**: Known allergy to tranexamic acid or any excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Thromboembolic Risk**: Venous and arterial thrombosis (including central retinal artery/vein obstruction) have been reported. Use with extreme caution in patients with a history of thromboembolic events or family history of such diseases. 
- **Hormonal Contraceptives**: Risk of thrombosis may be increased; consider non-hormonal alternatives [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment**: Risk of drug accumulation; dosage reduction is mandatory [Australian].
- **Urinary Tract Bleeding**: Use in the upper urinary tract carries a risk of clot retention in the renal pelvis or ureter, which may cause obstruction [Australian].
- **Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC)**: Must be used under the strict supervision of a specialist experienced in treating DIC [Australian].
- **Ophthalmological Effects**: Visual abnormalities are a frequently reported post-marketing event. Patients requiring treatment for longer than several days should undergo an ophthalmological examination (visual acuity, colour vision, fundoscopy, visual fields) before and during treatment. Discontinue if changes occur [Australian].
- **Convulsions**: Reported in association with treatment [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Data
- **Very Common (>10%)**: Gastrointestinal discomfort (nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea) occurs in >30% of patients receiving doses of 6 g/day; symptoms usually resolve with dose reduction [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%)**: Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%)**: Allergic dermatitis [Australian].

### Post-Marketing Experience (Frequency Not Specified)
- **Immune System**: Hypersensitivity, including anaphylaxis [Australian].
- **Nervous System**: Convulsions (seizures), dizziness [Australian].
- **Eye Disorders**: Visual impairment, chromatopsia [Australian].
- **Vascular**: Embolism [Australian].
- **Renal**: Renal cortical necrosis (particularly following severe postpartum haemorrhage) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Factor IX Complex / Anti-inhibitor Coagulant Concentrates**: Concomitant use increases the risk of thrombosis [Australian].
- **Anticoagulants**: Simultaneous treatment must be strictly supervised by a specialist [Australian].
- **Prothrombotic Agents**: Avoid concurrent use as they may synergistically increase the risk of thromboembolic events [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: **Category B1** [Australian]. Limited clinical data (approx. 88 reported cases). While animal studies show no teratogenic effects at high oral doses, tranexamic acid crosses the placenta (cord blood levels equal maternal levels). Use only if clearly needed [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding**: Secreted in breast milk at approximately 1/100th of the maternal serum concentration. While an antifibrinolytic effect in the infant is unlikely, caution is advised [Australian].
- **Fertility**: No adverse effects on fertility observed in animal models (rats) [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: No clinical experience is available for menorrhagia in females under 15 years. Dose reductions for renal impairment apply to children $\ge$ 2 years; use is not recommended in children with severe renal impairment [Australian].
- **Elderly**: No specific data available, though dosing should generally account for age-related renal decline [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment**: Linear increase in plasma levels with decreasing renal function. Dose reduction is required (see Section 3) [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Since the drug is excreted unchanged in the urine, no dose adjustment is required for hepatic insufficiency [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Tranexamic acid is a competitive inhibitor of plasminogen activation. At high concentrations, it acts as a noncompetitive inhibitor of plasmin. It is approximately 10 times more potent *in vitro* than aminocaproic acid [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics**: It does not influence platelet count, coagulation time, or coagulation factors at therapeutic concentrations, though it may prolong thrombin time at very high concentrations ($>$1 mg/mL) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Approximately 50% absorbed from the GI tract; peak serum levels occur ~3 hours post-dose [Australian].
    - **Distribution**: 3% protein binding (primarily to plasminogen). Crosses the placenta and blood-brain barrier. Diffuses rapidly into joint fluid [Australian].
    - **Metabolism**: Only a small fraction ($<$5%) is metabolised via acetylation or deamination [Australian].
    - **Elimination**: Excreted via glomerular filtration ($>$95% unchanged in urine). Half-life is approximately 2 hours after IV administration [Australian].
