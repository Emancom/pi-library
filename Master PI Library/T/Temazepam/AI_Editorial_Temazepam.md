# Editorial Product Information: Temazepam

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the Australian source was available for synthesis; therefore, regional comparisons with other jurisdictions (e.g., US, European) are not included.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Temazepam (a 1,4 benzodiazepine).
- **Chemical Class:** Benzodiazepine derivative.
- **Brand Names:** APO-TEMAZEPAM [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 10 mg tablets (white to off-white, biconvex, with stylised “S” on one side) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aspen Pharmacare Australia Pty Ltd; Distributed by Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Insomnia:** Adjunctive therapy for the short-term management of insomnia in adults [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosage:** 10 mg to 30 mg taken approximately 30 minutes before retiring [Australian].
- **Geriatric/Debilitated Dosage:** Initial recommended dose is 10 mg [Australian].
- **Duration of Therapy:** Recommended for short periods only (e.g., 2–4 weeks). Continuous long-term use is not recommended. The need for continued therapy should be periodically evaluated [Australian].
- **Dose Tapering:** Discontinuation after prolonged use must be gradual. Withdrawal schedules may range from four weeks to four months depending on the level of dependence [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to benzodiazepines or any formulation excipients (e.g., lactose) [Australian].
- Chronic obstructive airways disease with incipient respiratory failure [Australian].
- Sleep apnoea [Australian].
- **Depression:** Use as monotherapy to treat depression or anxiety associated with depression, due to the risk of suicide [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in the provided Australian source.
- **Opioid Interaction:** Concomitant use of benzodiazepines and opioids may result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death. Reserve concomitant use for patients with no alternative treatment options; use the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration [Australian].
- **Amnesia:** Transient amnesia or memory impairment may occur [Australian].
- **Complex Sleep Behaviours:** "Sleep-driving" and other complex behaviours (e.g., preparing food, making phone calls while not fully awake) have been reported. Discontinuation is highly recommended if these occur [Australian].
- **Angioedema:** Cases involving the tongue, glottis, or larynx have been reported and may be fatal [Australian].
- **Psychiatric Reactions:** Paradoxical reactions (e.g., rage, stimulation, excitement) may occur, particularly in children and the elderly. Use with caution in patients with depression, psychosis, or schizophrenia as it may uncover suicidal tendencies [Australian].
- **Dependence and Abuse:** Physical and psychological dependence can occur. Risk increases with dose, duration, and in patients with a history of alcohol or drug abuse [Australian].
- **General Clinical Precautions:** Use with caution in patients with myasthenia gravis, acute narrow-angle glaucoma, and those at risk of hypotension [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally consistent with the benzodiazepine class.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects [Australian] |
| :--- | :--- |
| **More Common (>10%)** | Dizziness, headache, vertigo, sedation, fatigue, drowsiness, ataxia. |
| **Less Common (0.1–10%)** | Asthenia, palpitations, tachycardia, macular rash, pruritus, dry mouth, nausea, vomiting, gastrointestinal upset, loss of taste, leg cramps, weakness, confusion, disorientation, muzziness, blurred vision, irritability, vivid dreams. |
| **Frequency Undetermined** | Hypersensitivity/anaphylactic reactions, SIADH, hyponatraemia, hypotension, thrombocytopaenia, agranulocytosis, pancytopaenia, extrapyramidal symptoms, visual disturbances (diplopia), dysarthria, slurred speech, convulsions, amnesia, suicidal ideation, alopecia. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **CNS Depressants:** Additive effects with alcohol, barbiturates, sedatives, tricyclic antidepressants, non-selective MAOIs, phenothiazines, antipsychotics, skeletal muscle relaxants, antihistamines, and anaesthetics [Australian].
    - **Opioids:** Increased risk of fatal respiratory depression and coma [Australian].
    - **Theophylline/Aminophylline:** May reduce the sedative effects of benzodiazepines [Australian].
    - **Anticholinergics:** Effects of drugs like atropine or antihistamines may be potentiated [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **Cytochrome P450:** The CYP450 system is not shown to be involved in temazepam disposition; pharmacokinetic interactions involving this system have not been observed [Australian].
    - **Anticonvulsants:** Serum concentrations may be altered; monitoring is recommended when co-prescribed [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C [Australian]**. Temazepam crosses the placenta. Benzodiazepines should be avoided during the first trimester due to a suggested risk of congenital malformations. Use in late pregnancy or during labour may cause neonatal hypoactivity, hypotonia, respiratory depression, and withdrawal symptoms [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Temazepam is excreted in human milk. Caution is advised as it may cause drowsiness and feeding difficulties in the infant [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies (rats) showed no adverse effects on male or female fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in children under 16 years of age [Australian].
- **Elderly/Debilitated Use:** Increased susceptibility to sedative effects, ataxia, and confusion, increasing the risk of falls. Initial dose should be limited to 10 mg [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution; dosage reduction may be advisable. Periodic blood counts are recommended [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution. Temazepam may worsen hepatic encephalopathy. Dosage reduction may be necessary; periodic liver function tests are recommended [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Temazepam potentiates the inhibitory effects of gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) by binding to specific benzodiazepine receptors in the CNS. It shortens sleep onset and increases total sleep time [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** CNS depression is dose-dependent [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Well absorbed; peak plasma levels in 30–120 minutes [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Approximately 96% protein bound [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised in the liver via direct conjugation to inactive glucuronide; a minor portion is demethylated to oxazepam [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Mean elimination half-life is approximately 10 hours (range 5–15 hours). 80% excreted in urine, 12% in faeces [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C. Protect from moisture [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Expiry date is located on the packaging [Australian].
