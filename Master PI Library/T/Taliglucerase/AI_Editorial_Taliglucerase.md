# Editorial Product Information: Taliglucerase alfa

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This harmonised editorial synthesis is based on Product Information (PI) from the following regulatory authority: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a consolidated clinical overview of taliglucerase alfa for healthcare professionals. It is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.
- **Note on Sources:** As only the Australian PI was provided for this synthesis, clinical recommendations and regulatory statuses reflect [Australian] standards.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Taliglucerase alfa (recombinant plant carrot (rpc)).
- **Chemical Class:** Recombinant human lysosomal glycoprotein enzyme (β-glucocerebrosidase).
- **Brand Name(s):** **ELELYSO** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A16AB11.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Powder for injection, 200 units per vial. After reconstitution, the solution contains 40 units/mL (1.2 mg/mL).
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Type 1 Gaucher Disease:** Indicated for long-term enzyme replacement therapy (ERT) in adult and paediatric patients with a confirmed diagnosis of Type 1 Gaucher disease. The diagnosis must be associated with at least one of the following clinical manifestations:
    - Splenomegaly
    - Hepatomegaly
    - Anaemia
    - Thrombocytopenia [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Initial Dose (Adult and Paediatric):** 30 units/kg to 60 units/kg of body weight once every 2 weeks [Australian].
- **Dose Individualisation:** Requirements should be adjusted based on the achievement of therapeutic goals and regular clinical evaluation.
- **Switching from Imiglucerase:** Patients currently stable on imiglucerase may switch to taliglucerase alfa at the same dose [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Preparation:** Reconstitute each 200-unit vial with 5.1 mL Water for Injections (yielding 5.3 mL total). Dilute in 100–200 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride.
- **Filtration:** Must be administered through an in-line, low protein-binding 0.2 µm filter [Australian].
- **Infusion Duration:** Typically 60 to 120 minutes.
- **Infusion Rates:**
    - **Adults:** Initial rate of 1.2 mL/minute; may increase to 2.2 mL/minute once tolerability is established.
    - **Paediatrics (<30 kg):** Rate should not exceed 1 mL/minute.
    - **Paediatrics (>30 kg):** Initial rate of 1 mL/minute; may increase to 2 mL/minute once tolerability is established [Australian].

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources; however, ERT is typically administered on a strict biweekly schedule.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** History of severe allergic reactions (e.g., anaphylaxis) to taliglucerase alfa, any of its excipients (mannitol, sodium citrate dihydrate, polysorbate 80), or other similar glucocerebrosidase enzymes [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Infusion-related Reactions (IRRs):** IRRs, including hypersensitivity and anaphylaxis, have been reported. Most occur within 24 hours of infusion and are most frequent during the first 3 months of treatment.
- **Management of IRRs:** If a reaction occurs, the infusion rate should be decreased or stopped. Pre-treatment with antihistamines and/or corticosteroids may be considered for subsequent doses.
- **Immunogenicity:** Anti-drug antibodies (ADA) develop in a significant portion of patients (approx. 53% of treatment-naïve adults). Neutralising antibodies have been detected, though their clinical significance regarding efficacy remains unclear. Testing should be considered in patients with a lack of therapeutic response or severe IRRs [Australian].
- **Carrot Allergy:** As the enzyme is expressed in carrot cells, caution should be exercised in patients with known carrot allergies [Australian].
- **Pulmonary Hypertension:** A known complication of Gaucher disease; patients should be monitored for respiratory symptoms.
- **Sodium Content:** Contains sodium (as sodium citrate dihydrate and within the 0.9% NaCl diluent), which must be considered for patients on a controlled sodium diet [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Table (Adult and Paediatric Combined)
| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Headache, dizziness, vomiting, abdominal pain, arthralgia, pain in extremity. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Hypersensitivity, flushing, throat irritation, nausea, pruritus, erythema, rash, bone pain, back pain, infusion site pain, fatigue, peripheral oedema, weight increased. |
| **Frequency Unknown** | Angioedema, anaphylactic reaction, urticaria [Australian]. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Type 1 hypersensitivity reactions and anaphylaxis. One paediatric case of gastroenteritis was reported as a treatment-related serious adverse event in trials [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Dynamic Interactions:** No formal interaction studies have been conducted.
- **Compatibility:** In the absence of compatibility studies, this medicinal product must not be mixed with other products except for 0.9% sodium chloride [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B1]. Animal studies (rats/rabbits) show no evidence of fetal harm. Human data is limited; taliglucerase alfa should only be used if the potential benefit outweighs the risk to the fetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if the drug is excreted in human milk. Caution is advised when administering to breastfeeding women [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility or reproductive performance were observed in animal studies [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for ages 2 to 17 years. Safety and efficacy profiles are similar to adults, though vomiting and abdominal pain are more frequent in children [Australian].
- **Elderly Use (≥65 years):** Limited clinical trial data (n=8). Caution is advised, typically starting at the lower end of the dosing range due to higher frequencies of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No specific studies have been conducted in these populations [Australian].
- **Neuronopathic Gaucher Disease:** No clinical experience in Type 2 Gaucher disease. Limited experience in Type 3c [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Taliglucerase alfa is a recombinant form of human glucocerebrosidase. It replaces the deficient endogenous enzyme, hydrolysing glucocerebroside into glucose and ceramide, thereby preventing accumulation in macrophages (Gaucher cells).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Metabolism:** Likely degraded into small peptides and amino acids via protein catabolic pathways.
    - **Elimination:** Rapidly eliminated from systemic circulation. In adults, the half-life is approximately 19–32 minutes.
    - **Distribution:** Target-mediated uptake is achieved via terminal mannose structures that bind to mannose receptors on macrophages [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adult Naïve Study (PB-06-001):** A 9-month Phase III trial in 31 patients. Both 30-unit/kg and 60-unit/kg doses achieved significant reductions in spleen volume (26.9% and 38.0% respectively) and improvements in haemoglobin and platelet counts.
- **Paediatric Naïve Study (PB-06-005):** A 12-month trial in 11 patients (ages 2–14). Demonstrated significant increases in haemoglobin and reductions in organ volumes.
- **Switch Study (PB-06-002):** A 9-month study in 31 stable patients switching from imiglucerase. Organ volumes and haematological parameters remained stable after the switch [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Vials:** Store and transport at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate). Do not freeze. Keep the vial in the outer carton to protect from light.
- **Reconstituted/Diluted Solution:** Use immediately. If not used immediately, the combined storage time for reconstituted and diluted solutions must not exceed 24 hours at 2°C to 8°C under light protection [Australian].
