# Editorial Product Information: Telotristat

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. 
- At the time of compilation, only the Australian PI was provided for review. As such, jurisdictional variations from other regions (e.g., US, European, Canadian) are not reflected in this version.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. It is a clinical synthesis and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Telotristat ethyl (as telotristat etiprate).
- **Chemical Class:** Tryptophan hydroxylase inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** **XERMELO** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A16AX15.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Film-coated tablets containing 250 mg of telotristat ethyl (equivalent to 327.9 mg telotristat etiprate).
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Clinect Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Carcinoid Syndrome Diarrhoea:** Treatment of carcinoid syndrome diarrhoea in adults, used in combination with somatostatin analogue (SSA) therapy in patients whose symptoms are inadequately controlled by SSA therapy alone [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Standard Adult Dose:** 250 mg taken three times daily.
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets must be taken with food.
- **Clinical Response Timing:** Response is typically observed within 12 weeks. If no clinical benefit is achieved within this timeframe, the necessity of continued therapy should be reassessed.
- **Dose Limitation:** Doses higher than 250 mg TID are not recommended due to the risk of drug accumulation and increased adverse effects (specifically constipation).
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, the patient should wait and take the next dose at the regular scheduled time. **Do not** double the dose to compensate for a missed one.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to telotristat ethyl or any of the inactive excipients (including lactose) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatic Enzyme Elevations:** Increases in liver enzymes (ALT, ALP) have been observed, primarily at doses higher than recommended. 
    - *Monitoring:* Liver function tests (LFTs) should be obtained prior to initiation and periodically during treatment as clinically indicated.
    - *Management:* In cases of suspected liver injury, discontinue telotristat. Do not resume unless an alternative cause for the injury is identified.
- **Constipation:** Telotristat reduces bowel movement frequency. Patients should be monitored for signs of constipation. If constipation develops, the use of telotristat and other motility-altering agents must be re-evaluated.
- **Depressive Disorders:** Depression, depressed mood, and decreased interest have been reported in clinical trials and post-marketing. Patients should be advised to report any mood changes to their healthcare provider.
- **Excipients:** Contains lactose; not recommended for patients with rare hereditary galactose intolerance, total lactase deficiency, or glucose-galactose malabsorption.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally mild to moderate in intensity. Abdominal pain was the most frequent reason for discontinuation (7.1%).

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Abdominal pain (26%), nausea, Gamma-glutamyltransferase (GGT) increased (11%), fatigue (10%). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Decreased appetite, depression, depressed mood, headache, abdominal distension, constipation, flatulence, ALT increased, AST increased, ALP increased, peripheral oedema, pyrexia. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Faecaloma, intestinal obstruction. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Intestinal obstruction and faecaloma have been reported, primarily in patients receiving higher than recommended doses (500 mg TID) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Short-acting Octreotide:** Concomitant use significantly reduces the systemic exposure of telotristat (Cmax and AUC reduced by >80%). If both are required, administer short-acting octreotide at least **30 minutes after** the telotristat dose.
- **CYP3A4 Substrates:** Telotristat may decrease the efficacy of CYP3A4 substrates (e.g., midazolam, simvastatin, ethinylestradiol, amlodipine, cyclosporin) by inducing metabolism. Monitor for suboptimal efficacy of these agents.
- **CYP2B6 Substrates:** Telotristat may induce CYP2B6, potentially reducing the plasma levels of substrates such as valproic acid, bupropion, or sertraline.
- **Carboxylesterase 2 (CES2) Substrates:** Telotristat may alter the exposure of CES2 substrates (e.g., prasugrel, irinotecan, capecitabine).
- **Transporters:** Telotristat ethyl inhibits P-gp and BCRP in vitro; however, clinically significant interactions with substrates like fexofenadine are unlikely.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category: B3.** (Drugs which have been taken by only a limited number of pregnant women and women of childbearing age, without an increase in the frequency of malformation or other direct or indirect harmful effects on the human fetus having been observed. Studies in animals have shown evidence of an increased occurrence of fetal damage, the significance of which is considered uncertain in humans).
    - **Recommendation:** Not recommended during pregnancy or in women of childbearing potential not using effective contraception.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if telotristat or its metabolites are excreted in human milk. A risk to the infant cannot be excluded. Breastfeeding should be discontinued during treatment.
- **Fertility:** No human data available. Animal studies showed no effect on fertility at exposures up to 2.5 times the clinical dose.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients <18 years have not been established. No data available.
- **Elderly Use:** No specific dose adjustments are required based on age [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - *Mild to Severe (eGFR ≥15 mL/min/1.73 m²):* No dose adjustment required.
    - *End-stage Renal Disease (ESRD) requiring dialysis:* Not recommended; safety and efficacy have not been established.
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - *Mild (Child-Pugh A):* Dose reduction to 250 mg **twice daily** may be necessary based on tolerability.
    - *Moderate (Child-Pugh B):* Dose reduction to 250 mg **once daily** may be necessary based on tolerability.
    - *Severe (Child-Pugh C):* Not recommended.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Telotristat ethyl and its active metabolite, telotristat, are inhibitors of L-tryptophan hydroxylases (TPH1 and TPH2). TPH1 is the rate-limiting enzyme in peripheral serotonin synthesis. By inhibiting TPH1, the drug reduces serotonin production, thereby alleviating carcinoid syndrome symptoms (primarily diarrhoea).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - *Absorption:* Rapidly absorbed and converted to the active metabolite (telotristat). High-fat meals significantly increase systemic exposure.
    - *Distribution:* >99% protein bound.
    - *Metabolism:* Primarily hydrolysed via carboxylesterases to the active metabolite.
    - *Elimination:* Excreted primarily in the faeces (93%). The terminal half-life is approximately 11 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **TELESTAR Study:** A 12-week, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 3 trial in patients with carcinoid syndrome and ≥4 bowel movements (BM) per day despite stable SSA therapy.
    - *Outcome:* Patients receiving 250 mg TID experienced a statistically significant reduction in daily BM frequency (-1.4 vs -0.6 for placebo; p<0.001). 
    - *Durable Response:* 44.4% of telotristat patients achieved a durable response (≥30% reduction in BMs for ≥50% of the time) compared to 20% for placebo.
- **TELECAST Study:** A Phase 3 study in patients with lower BM frequency (<4 per day).
    - *Outcome:* Significant reduction in urinary 5-HIAA (a serotonin metabolite) compared to placebo (-33.2% vs +97.7%; p<0.001).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Packaging:** Supplied in PVC/PCTFE/PVC/Aluminium blister packs.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australian Classification:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
