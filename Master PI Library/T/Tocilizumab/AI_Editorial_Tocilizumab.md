# Editorial Product Information: Tocilizumab

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of tocilizumab for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory guidance into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI or local clinical guidelines. Prescribers should refer to the current official PI in their specific jurisdiction for legal and prescribing purposes. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tocilizumab
- **Chemical Class:** Recombinant humanised monoclonal antibody (IgG1 subclass)
- **Brand Name:** **Actemra** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** L04AC07
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths [Australian]:**
    - **Intravenous (IV) Infusion:** Concentrated solution in single-use vials: 80 mg/4 mL, 200 mg/10 mL, and 400 mg/20 mL (20 mg/mL).
    - **Subcutaneous (SC) Injection:** 162 mg/0.9 mL solution in a single-use pre-filled syringe (with needle safety device) or a pre-filled pen (ACTPen).
- **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous infusion or Subcutaneous injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Roche Products Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
[All indications listed below are Australian-approved]
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA):** Treatment of moderate to severe active RA in adults who have had an inadequate response or intolerance to one or more disease-modifying anti-rheumatic drugs (DMARDs). Used in combination with methotrexate (MTX) or as monotherapy.
- **Giant Cell Arteritis (GCA):** Treatment of GCA in adult patients (SC formulation only).
- **Polyarticular Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis (pJIA):** Treatment of moderate to severe active pJIA in patients aged 2 years and older who have responded inadequately to MTX.
- **Systemic Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis (sJIA):** Treatment of active sJIA in patients aged 1 year and older (SC) or 2 years and older (IV).
- **Cytokine Release Syndrome (CRS):** Treatment of chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T cell-induced severe or life-threatening CRS in adults and paediatric patients aged 2 years and older (IV formulation only).
- **Coronavirus Disease 2019 (COVID-19):** **Provisional Approval** for hospitalised adults receiving systemic corticosteroids who require supplemental oxygen or mechanical ventilation (IV formulation only).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult RA)
- **IV Administration:** 8 mg/kg every 4 weeks. Doses exceeding 800 mg per infusion are not recommended [Australian].
- **SC Administration:** 162 mg once every week.

### Indication-Specific Dosing
- **GCA (SC only):** 162 mg once every week in combination with a tapering course of glucocorticoids. A dose of 162 mg every other week may be prescribed based on clinical considerations.
- **COVID-19 (IV only):** A single 60-minute infusion of 8 mg/kg (max 800 mg).
- **CRS (IV only):** 8 mg/kg (patients ≥ 30 kg) or 12 mg/kg (patients < 30 kg). Up to 3 additional doses can be given if no improvement, at least 8 hours apart.
- **pJIA:** 
    - **IV (q4w):** 10 mg/kg (< 30 kg); 8 mg/kg (≥ 30 kg). 
    - **SC:** 162 mg every 3 weeks (< 30 kg); 162 mg every 2 weeks (≥ 30 kg).
- **sJIA:** 
    - **IV (q2w):** 12 mg/kg (< 30 kg); 8 mg/kg (≥ 30 kg).
    - **SC:** 162 mg every 2 weeks (< 30 kg); 162 mg every week (≥ 30 kg).

### Administration Instructions
- **IV:** Dilute in 0.9% sterile sodium chloride to a final volume of 100 mL (50 mL for paediatric patients < 30 kg). Infuse over 60 minutes.
- **SC:** Administer in the abdomen, thigh, or upper arm. Rotate sites. The pre-filled pen (ACTPen) is not intended for children < 12 years of age [Australian].
- **Missed Dose (RA/GCA):** If a weekly dose is missed within 7 days, administer on the next scheduled day. If a fortnightly dose is missed within 7 days, administer immediately and the next dose on the scheduled day.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to tocilizumab, Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cell products, or any excipients.
- Active, severe infections [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Serious Infections:** Increased risk of bacterial, viral, fungal, and opportunistic infections (e.g., TB, pneumonia, cellulitis, herpes zoster). Treatment should not be initiated during an active infection.
- **Gastrointestinal (GI) Perforation:** Reported primarily as a complication of diverticulitis. Use with caution in patients with a history of intestinal ulceration or diverticulitis.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Transient or intermittent mild-to-moderate elevations in hepatic transaminases are common. Serious drug-induced liver injury, including acute liver failure and jaundice, has been reported.
- **Hematological Abnormalities:** Decreases in absolute neutrophil count (ANC) and platelet counts have occurred. 
    - *Initiation Criteria:* ANC > 2 x $10^9$/L; Platelets > 100 x $10^9$/L (for RA, GCA, pJIA, sJIA).
- **Lipid Parameters:** Elevations in total cholesterol, LDL, HDL, and triglycerides have been observed.
- **Hypersensitivity/Anaphylaxis:** Serious and fatal reactions have occurred with both IV and SC routes, sometimes during the first infusion.
- **Vaccinations:** Concurrent use of live or live-attenuated vaccines is not recommended.
- **Demyelinating Disorders:** Physicians should be vigilant for symptoms of central demyelinating disorders.

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Liver Enzymes (ALT/AST):** Every 4–8 weeks for the first 6 months, then every 12 weeks.
- **ANC and Platelets:** 4–8 weeks after starting, then according to clinical practice.
- **Lipids:** 4–8 weeks after initiation.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Category (RA)
- **Very Common (>10%):** Upper respiratory tract infections (URTIs), Nasopharyngitis.
- **Common (1–10%):** Hypertension, Headache, Increased ALT/AST, Dizziness, Injection site reactions (SC), Rash, Gastritis, Mouth ulceration.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Diverticulitis, Nephrolithiasis, Hypothyroidism.
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Anaphylaxis (fatal), Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS).

### Serious Adverse Effects
- GI perforation, serious infections, hepatotoxicity, and anaphylaxis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP450 Substrates:** IL-6 suppresses the expression of CYP450 enzymes. By inhibiting IL-6, tocilizumab may normalise enzyme activity, potentially decreasing the levels of drugs metabolised via CYP3A4, 1A2, 2C9, or 2C19 (e.g., simvastatin, warfarin, phenytoin, theophylline). Monitor patients on narrow therapeutic index drugs [Australian].
- **Combination Therapy:** Not recommended with other biological DMARDs (e.g., TNF antagonists, abatacept, rituximab) due to lack of safety data and potential for increased infection risk.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category C.** Tocilizumab should not be used during pregnancy unless clearly necessary. Adequate data in pregnant women are lacking. Embryo-foetal toxicity was observed in animal studies (cynomolgus monkeys) at high doses.
- **Breastfeeding:** Excretion in human milk is unknown. IgG is excreted in milk. A decision must be made to discontinue nursing or the drug, weighing the benefit to the mother vs the child.
- **Fertility:** Preclinical data do not suggest an effect on fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety/efficacy not established for conditions other than pJIA, sJIA, or CRS. IV use not studied in pJIA/CRS < 2 years. SC use not studied in sJIA < 1 year or pJIA < 2 years.
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required for patients aged 65 years and older.
- **Renal Impairment:** No adjustment required for mild to moderate impairment. Not studied in severe renal impairment.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not studied in patients with hepatic impairment; no dose recommendations can be made.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Tocilizumab is an Interleukin-6 (IL-6) receptor antagonist. It binds specifically to both soluble and membrane-bound IL-6 receptors (sIL-6R and mIL-6R), inhibiting IL-6 mediated signalling, which is a key driver of inflammation and joint destruction.
- **Pharmacokinetics [Australian]:**
    - **Absorption:** SC bioavailability is 80%.
    - **Distribution:** Central volume of distribution is 3.5 L in RA patients.
    - **Elimination:** Biphasic elimination. Total clearance is concentration-dependent (non-linear). At steady state, the apparent half-life ($t_{1/2}$) is up to 11 days (4 mg/kg IV) or 13 days (8 mg/kg IV).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **RA:** Efficacy demonstrated in five Phase III trials (AMBITION, LITHE, OPTION, TOWARD, RADIATE) and two SC trials (SUMMACTA, BREVACTA). Tocilizumab showed superior ACR 20/50/70 responses compared to MTX or placebo + DMARD.
- **GCA:** Study GiACTA demonstrated that tocilizumab weekly or every other week plus a 26-week prednisone taper resulted in significantly higher steroid-free remission at 52 weeks compared to placebo plus prednisone tapers.
- **COVID-19:** Trial RECOVERY showed a significant mortality reduction in hospitalised patients with progressive COVID-19 (HR 0.85). Trials EMPACTA, COVACTA, and REMDACTA provided further evidence for use in various severities.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials/Syringes/Pens:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze). 
- **Light Protection:** Keep in the original carton to protect from light.
- **SC Specific:** Once removed from the refrigerator, the SC formulation can be stored at or below 30°C for up to 2 weeks [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4)** [Australian].
