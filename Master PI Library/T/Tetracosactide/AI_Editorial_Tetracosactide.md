# Editorial Product Information: Tetracosactide

## Editorial Note
- Jurisdictions contributing to this editorial synthesis include: **Australian (TGA)** and **US (FDA)**. 
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of tetracosactide acetate for use by healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory specifics from multiple regions.
- This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or prescribing information required for clinical practice within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tetracosactide acetate (also known as Cosyntropin [US]).
- **Chemical Class:** Synthetic polypeptide consisting of the first 24 amino acids of naturally occurring adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH).
- **Brand Names:** 
    - **Synacthen** [Australian]
    - **Cortrosyn** [US]
- **ATC Code:** H01AA02
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):**
    - **Powder for injection/compounding:** 1 g bulk ingredient vial [US-veterinary/compounding].
    - **Solution for injection:** 250 micrograms (0.25 mg) in 1 mL [Australian, US].
    - **Depot injection:** 1 mg/1 mL (as zinc complex) [Australian-approved; not currently available in US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intramuscular (IM) or Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** 
    - Bluebay Shandong Co., Ltd (Bulk ingredient) [US].
    - Novartis/Link Medical Systems [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Diagnostic Use (Screening for Adrenocortical Insufficiency):** Approved in both [Australian] and [US] jurisdictions to test the functional integrity of the adrenal cortex.
- **Therapeutic Use:** In the [Australian] jurisdiction, the depot formulation is indicated for specific conditions including acute exacerbations in patients with multiple sclerosis, hypsarrhythmia (infantile spasms), and certain rheumatic or dermatological conditions where systemic glucocorticoid therapy is indicated. [Not US-approved for therapeutic use].
- **Veterinary Compounding:** Specifically noted for animal drug compounding [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Diagnostic (Rapid ACTH Stimulation Test):** 250 mcg (0.25 mg) administered as a single dose via IM or IV injection.
### Administration Instructions
- **IV Administration:** May be given as a direct injection over 2 minutes or as an intravenous infusion. 
- **Compounding:** Bulk powder must be handled according to local pharmacopeial standards and is restricted to pharmacy compounding [US].
- **Storage for use:** Use reconstituted solutions immediately.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to tetracosactide, synthetic ACTH, or any excipients.
- History of allergic disorders (e.g., asthma) [Australian].
- **Therapeutic Use Contraindications [Australian]:** 
    - Acute psychoses.
    - Infectious diseases (unless treated with concurrent antibiotics).
    - Peptic ulceration.
    - Cushing’s syndrome.
    - Treatment of primary adrenocortical insufficiency.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Anaphylaxis:** Tetracosactide can cause severe hypersensitivity reactions. Patients should be observed for at least 30 minutes following administration.
- **Monitoring Requirements:** In diagnostic tests, baseline and post-stimulation cortisol levels must be measured accurately. For therapeutic use, monitor electrolytes, blood pressure, and for signs of fluid retention.
- **General Warnings:** Bulk powder may cause skin irritation, eye irritation, or respiratory symptoms if inhaled [US-Bulk Ingredient]. 
- **Diagnostic Accuracy:** False negatives may occur in patients with recent-onset secondary adrenal insufficiency.

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (>1%)** | Local injection site reactions (pain, redness). |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Anaphylaxis, hypersensitivity reactions (urticaria, pruritus). |
| **Not specified** | Adrenal hemorrhage [US], Electrolyte imbalances (with therapeutic use) [Australian]. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylactic shock is the most significant risk associated with the rapid ACTH test.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **Corticosteroids:** May interfere with the results of the stimulation test; should be withheld on the morning of the test.
    - **Diuretics:** May exacerbate electrolyte imbalances (hypokalemia) during therapeutic use [Australian].
    - **Antidiabetics:** Tetracosactide may increase blood glucose, requiring dosage adjustment of insulin or oral hypoglycemics [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Estrogens may cause an increase in total cortisol levels, potentially leading to abnormally high baseline and stimulated levels in the diagnostic test.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category C:** Drugs which, because of their pharmacological effects, have caused or may be suspected of causing harmful effects on the human fetus or neonate without causing malformations. 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned/refer to clinical judgment; should be used only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been established. It is unknown if tetracosactide is excreted in human milk. 
- **Fertility:** Data not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - Diagnostic dose for children >2 years: 250 mcg.
    - Infants (<2 years): 125 mcg is often suggested [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific dose adjustment is typically required for diagnostic use; however, caution is advised due to the higher prevalence of underlying cardiovascular conditions.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specifically addressed in diagnostic labeling; standard clinical monitoring applies.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Stimulates the adrenal cortex (specifically the zona fasciculata) by binding to the ACTH receptor, leading to the synthesis and secretion of adrenocortical hormones (cortisol, corticosterone, and androgens).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed after IM injection.
    - **Distribution:** Rapidly leaves the plasma; high affinity for adrenal tissue.
    - **Metabolism/Elimination:** Rapidly degraded by serum proteases.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** A dose of 250 mcg provides a maximal adrenal stimulus for approximately 30–60 minutes.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Key trials primarily established the correlation between synthetic tetracosactide (1-24) and natural ACTH (1-39) in stimulating maximal cortisol output. Modern use is based on established clinical diagnostic standards rather than recent pivotal phase III trials.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Bulk Powder [US]:** Keep the product frozen at -20°C. Keep container tightly closed in a well-ventilated area.
- **Clinical Vials [Australian/US]:** Typically stored under refrigeration (2°C to 8°C) and protected from light.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4).
- **US:** Rx-only. 
- **Note:** The bulk ingredient (1 g) is labeled specifically for veterinary use and pharmacy compounding only [US].
