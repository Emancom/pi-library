# Editorial Product Information: Trazodone Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis was developed using official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of trazodone hydrochloride for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, regulatory warnings, and dosing guidelines into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved labelling for definitive legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Trazodone hydrochloride (a triazolopyridine derivative).
- **Chemical Class:** Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI) and 5HT2 receptor antagonist [US].
- **Brand Names:** Not specified in available sources (distributed as Trazodone Hydrochloride Tablets).
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - Scored oral tablets: 50 mg, 100 mg, 150 mg, and 300 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Zydus Pharmaceuticals (USA) Inc. / A-S Medication Solutions [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Major Depressive Disorder (MDD):** Indicated for the treatment of MDD in adults [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):**
    - **Initial Dose:** 150 mg/day administered in divided doses. 
    - **Titration:** Dosage should be initiated at a low level and increased gradually. The dose may be increased by 50 mg/day every three to four days based on clinical response and tolerability [US].
    - **Maximum Dose:** Usually should not exceed 400 mg/day for outpatients. For more severely depressed inpatients, the dose may be increased up to 600 mg/day in divided doses [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Tablets should be taken shortly after a meal or light snack to enhance absorption and reduce the risk of dizziness.
    - Tablets may be swallowed whole or broken in half along the score line; they must not be chewed or crushed [US].
    - If significant drowsiness occurs, a larger portion of the daily dose may be administered at bedtime, or the total dosage may be reduced [US].
- **Maintenance:** Once an adequate response is achieved, the dosage may be gradually reduced to the lowest effective level [US].
- **Discontinuation:** A gradual dose reduction is recommended rather than abrupt cessation to mitigate withdrawal symptoms [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Concomitant use of trazodone with an MAOI is contraindicated. At least 14 days must elapse between the discontinuation of an MAOI and the initiation of trazodone, and vice versa. This includes MAOIs such as linezolid or intravenous methylene blue [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Suicidal Thoughts and Behaviors [US]:** Antidepressants increase the risk of suicidal thinking and behavior in children, adolescents, and young adults (up to age 24). Trazodone is not approved for pediatric use. Patients of all ages should be monitored closely for clinical worsening or emergence of suicidality, particularly during the first few months of therapy or following dose adjustments [US].
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** A potentially life-threatening condition may occur, especially when co-administered with other serotonergic agents (e.g., triptans, tricyclic antidepressants, fentanyl, lithium, tramadol, St. John’s Wort) or drugs that impair serotonin metabolism. Discontinue immediately if symptoms occur [US].
- **Cardiac Arrhythmias:** Trazodone prolongs the QT/QTc interval. Avoid use in patients with known QT prolongation, history of cardiac arrhythmias, or those taking other QT-prolonging drugs. Use with caution in patients with pre-existing cardiac disease [US].
- **Orthostatic Hypotension and Syncope:** May occur; use caution when co-administering with antihypertensive medications [US].
- **Increased Risk of Bleeding:** Concomitant use of NSAIDs, aspirin, or anticoagulants (e.g., warfarin) may increase the risk of gastrointestinal or other bleeding events [US].
- **Priapism:** Cases of painful, prolonged penile erections have been reported. Immediate medical intervention is required for erections lasting more than 4 hours [US].
- **Activation of Mania/Hypomania:** Screen for bipolar disorder prior to initiation [US].
- **Angle-Closure Glaucoma:** Avoid use in patients with untreated anatomically narrow angles [US].
- **Hyponatremia:** May occur, often as a result of SIADH. Elderly patients and those on diuretics are at higher risk [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are categorised by frequency where available from clinical trial data [US]:

- **Very Common (>10%):** Drowsiness (24–41%), dry mouth (15–34%), dizziness/lightheadedness (20–28%), headache (10–20%), hypertension (20% in inpatients), fatigue (6–11%), nausea/vomiting (10–13%) [US].
- **Common (1–10%):** Edema (3–7%), blurred vision (6–15%), constipation (7–8%), hypotension (4–7%), syncope (3–5%), confusion (5–6%), nervousness (6–15%), aching/pains (5–6%), diarrhea (5%), nasal/sinus congestion (3–6%), weight gain (1–5%), weight loss (3–6%) [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Suicidality, serotonin syndrome, QT prolongation, priapism, severe hyponatremia, and liver enzyme alterations (cholestasis, jaundice) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **MAOIs:** Contraindicated due to serotonin syndrome risk [US].
- **Serotonergic Drugs:** Increased risk of serotonin syndrome [US].
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors (e.g., itraconazole, clarithromycin):** Increase trazodone exposure. Consider a dose reduction of trazodone [US].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., rifampin, carbamazepine, phenytoin):** Decrease trazodone exposure. An increase in trazodone dosage may be necessary [US].
- **Digoxin and Phenytoin:** Trazodone may increase serum levels of these narrow therapeutic index drugs; monitor concentrations [US].
- **Warfarin:** Monitor prothrombin time/INR for potential increases or decreases [US].
- **CNS Depressants:** Trazodone may enhance the sedative effects of alcohol, barbiturates, and other CNS depressants [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not specified by letter [US], but clinical data from decades of use have not identified a clear drug-associated risk of major birth defects or miscarriage. 
    - **Animal Data:** Teratogenic effects were seen in rats and rabbits only at doses many times the maximum human dose (associated with fetal resorption and congenital anomalies) [US].
    - **Clinical Note:** Untreated depression in pregnancy carries risks; consider the risk/benefit of continuing treatment [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Trazodone is excreted into human milk. Limited postmarketing data have not identified adverse effects on the breastfed child [US].
- **Fertility:** No effect on fertility was observed in animal studies at 7.3 times the maximum human dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established. Use is associated with an increased risk of suicidal thoughts and behaviors [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Generally, no overall differences in response compared to younger patients, but older adults may be at increased risk for hyponatremia and should be treated with caution [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not studied in this population; use with caution [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not studied in this population; use with caution [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** The antidepressant effect is thought to be related to the enhancement of serotonergic activity. It acts as an SSRI and a 5HT2 receptor antagonist [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Trazodone exhibits high affinity for 5-HT2A receptors and moderate affinity for 5-HT2B, 5-HT2C, and α1A-adrenergic receptors. It is a partial agonist at 5-HT1A. Its α1-adrenergic antagonism is likely responsible for orthostatic hypotension [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Well absorbed; peak plasma levels occur 1 hour (fasted) to 2 hours (with food) after administration. Food increases the total amount absorbed but decreases $C_{max}$ [US].
    - **Distribution:** 89% to 95% protein bound [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised by CYP3A4 via oxidative cleavage to the active metabolite m-chlorophenylpiperazine (mCPP) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Less than 1% is excreted unchanged in the urine [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **MDD Efficacy:** Efficacy was established in both inpatient and outpatient trials using the immediate-release formulation of trazodone for the treatment of major depressive disorder [US]. Specific trial design details were not provided in the primary source.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription Only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not a controlled substance [US].
