# Editorial Product Information: Human Tetanus Immunoglobulin

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Human Tetanus Immunoglobulin for use by healthcare professionals, synthesising available regulatory information.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Human Tetanus Immunoglobulin (at least 98% Immunoglobulin G [IgG]).
- **Chemical Class:** Plasma-derived immunoglobulin.
- **Brand Name(s):** Tetanus Immunoglobulin-VF [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available source documents.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile, preservative-free solution for injection. 
    - Strength: 160 mg/mL human plasma protein with tetanus antitoxin activity of not less than 100 IU/mL [Australian].
    - Available in single vials containing 250 IU human tetanus antitoxin [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intramuscular (IM) injection only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** CSL Behring (Australia) Pty Ltd; Distributed by Australian Red Cross Lifeblood [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Passive Tetanus Prophylaxis:** Indicated for individuals who have sustained a tetanus-prone wound and meet any of the following criteria:
    - Not actively immunised against tetanus.
    - Immunisation history is doubtful.
    - Fully immunised but more than 10 years have elapsed since the last vaccine dose.
- **Concurrent Active Immunisation:** In the above instances, active immunisation with a tetanus vaccine should be initiated simultaneously in the opposite limb using a separate syringe [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Minimum Routine Prophylactic Dose:** 250 IU [Australian].
- **Increased Dosing:** The dose should be doubled (500 IU) if the wound is grossly contaminated or if more than 24 hours have elapsed between the injury and seeking medical attention [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Route:** Must be administered via deep intramuscular injection. 
- **Preparation:** Bring the solution to room temperature before use. Do not use if the product is turbid or contains sediment. The solution is preservative-free and must be used immediately after opening; discard any unused portion [Australian].
- **Technique:** Care must be taken to aspirate before injection to ensure the needle is not in a blood vessel.
- **Divided Doses:** If large volumes are required, it is advisable to administer divided doses at different sites. This is recommended for:
    - Doses >2 mL in children up to 20 kg body weight.
    - Doses >5 mL in individuals above 20 kg body weight [Australian].
- **Adjuncts:** Hyaluronidase or a suitable local anaesthetic may be added to the injection if desired [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known true anaphylactic reaction to the active substance or any excipients [Australian].
- **IgA Deficiency:** Patients with immunoglobulin A (IgA) deficiency, unless they have been tested and shown not to have circulating anti-IgA antibodies (due to potential for severe reactions to trace amounts of IgA) [Australian].
- **Coagulation Disorders:** Patients with severe thrombocytopenia or any coagulation disorder where intramuscular injection is contraindicated [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in provided source.
- **Route Restriction:** **MUST NOT** be administered intravenously due to the risk of anaphylactic reactions. Intravenous preparations are available if large doses are required for the treatment of tetanus or for patients with significant haemostatic defects [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Use with caution in patients with a history of prior systemic allergic reactions to human immunoglobulin. 
- **Observation Period:** Patients should be observed for at least 20 minutes following administration. In cases of inadvertent intravenous injection, observation must be extended to at least 1 hour [Australian].
- **Pathogen Safety:** As a product derived from human plasma, there is a potential risk of transmitting infectious agents, including viruses and theoretically Creutzfeldt-Jakob Disease (CJD) agents. The manufacturing process includes screening and viral inactivation/removal steps (pasteurisation and nanofiltration) effective against HIV, HBV, HCV, HAV, and parvovirus B19 [Australian].
- **Trace IgA:** Contains less than 0.5 mg/mL IgA. Physicians must weigh the benefit of treatment against potential hypersensitivity risks in IgA-deficient patients [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Local Reactions:** Very common/Common. Includes local tenderness, erythema, stiffness, pain, bruising, and irritation at the injection site. These may persist for several hours [Australian].
- **General/Systemic Reactions:**
    - **Occasional:** Mild pyrexia, malaise, drowsiness, and urticaria.
    - **Rare:** Skin lesions, headache, dizziness, nausea, convulsions, and generalised hypersensitivity reactions. 
    - **Serious:** Precipitous fall in blood pressure with anaphylactic reaction, even in patients who tolerated previous doses [Australian].
- **Clinical Trial Data (Representative):** In a trial involving 58 healthy subjects using a related immunoglobulin (Hepatitis B), reported events included malaise (20/22 events), drowsiness (13/17 events), and induration (10/4 events) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Live Attenuated Vaccines:** Passively acquired antibodies may interfere with the immune response to live virus vaccines (e.g., poliomyelitis, measles). 
    - Defer live vaccine administration for at least 3 months after tetanus immunoglobulin.
    - In the case of measles, interference may persist for up to one year; antibody status checking is recommended [Australian].
- **Inactivated Vaccines:** May be administered concurrently with tetanus immunoglobulin, provided separate syringes and injection sites are used [Australian].
- **Laboratory Test Interference:** Transitory rise in transferred antibodies may lead to misleading positive results in serological testing (e.g., Coombs’ test, anti-A, anti-B, anti-D) [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Safety in human pregnancy has not been established in controlled clinical trials. Administer to pregnant women only with caution [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety during lactation has not been established. While immunoglobulins are excreted in breast milk, it is unknown if this applies to passively administered tetanus immunoglobulin. Administer to breastfeeding mothers only with caution [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No reproductive toxicity studies have been conducted. There are no clinical reports of effects on fertility associated with plasma-derived medicines [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in formal studies; however, this population is not over-represented in spontaneous adverse event reports [Australian]. 
- **Elderly Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in formal studies; however, this population is not over-represented in spontaneous adverse event reports [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Provides passive immunity through high levels of specific antibodies (mainly IgG) that neutralise the toxin produced by *Clostridium tetani* [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Peak Concentration:** Maximal serum IgG concentration is reached approximately 8.0 ± 5.5 days post-injection.
    - **Half-life:** Estimated IgG half-life is 27.2 ± 6.6 days [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The product contains 22.5 mg/mL glycine as a stabiliser and has a pH of 6.6 [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Specific Trials:** Formal clinical trials with Tetanus Immunoglobulin-VF have not been conducted.
- **Representative Data:** Comparative trials using Hepatitis B Immunoglobulin were used to investigate the effect of pasteurisation on the in vivo behaviour of intramuscular immunoglobulins, showing no significant differences between pasteurised (viral inactivated) and unpasteurised products [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze).
- **Light:** Protect from light.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging. Do not use after this date [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
