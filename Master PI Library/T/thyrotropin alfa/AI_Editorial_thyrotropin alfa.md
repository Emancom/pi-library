# Editorial Product Information: Thyrotropin alfa

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis incorporates information from the following provided sources:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: Product Information for **Thyrogen** (thyrotropin alfa) powder for injection.
    - **US (FDA)**: Drug Facts for **Guna-Awareness** (homeopathic solution/drops containing thyrotropin alfa).
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals.
- **Clinical Note**: The provided documents represent two fundamentally different therapeutic approaches: a high-purity recombinant human hormone (rhTSH) for diagnostic/therapeutic use in oncology [Australian] and a multi-ingredient homeopathic preparation [US]. This editorial synthesis is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Thyrotropin alfa (recombinant human thyroid stimulating hormone; rhTSH).
- **Chemical Class**: Heterodimeric glycoprotein.
- **Brand Names**: 
    - **Thyrogen** [Australian]
    - **Guna-Awareness** (multi-ingredient product) [US]
- **ATC Code**: H01AB01 [Australian].
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**:
    - **Powder for Injection**: 1.1 mg lyophilised powder (0.9 mg/mL after reconstitution) [Australian].
    - **Oral Solution (Drops)**: Homeopathic dilution 3 [hp_X] (as part of a multi-ingredient formula containing 30% ethyl alcohol) [US].
- **Routes of Administration**: 
    - Intramuscular (IM) [Australian].
    - Oral [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: 
    - sanofi-aventis australia pty ltd [Australian].
    - Guna spa [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Detection of Thyroid Cancer/Remnants**: Used in conjunction with serum thyroglobulin (Tg) testing, with or without radioactive iodine imaging, for the detection of thyroid remnants and well-differentiated thyroid cancer in post-thyroidectomy patients maintained on hormone suppression therapy [Australian].
- **Ablation of Thyroid Remnant Tissue**: Therapeutic use in combination with radioactive iodine for remnant ablation in post-thyroidectomy patients on hormone suppression therapy [Australian].
- **Mental and Emotional Balance**: Intended to support mental and emotional balance, specifically for difficulty concentrating, restlessness, and irritability [US-Homeopathic]. *Note: The US source states this product has not been evaluated by the FDA for safety or efficacy.*

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Recombinant Injection (Oncological Use) [Australian]
- **General Dosing (Adult)**: A two-injection regimen is recommended.
- **Dosing Schedule**: 0.9 mg by intramuscular (IM) injection, followed by a second 0.9 mg IM injection 24 hours later.
- **Administration Instructions**: Reconstitute the 1.1 mg vial with 1.2 mL Sterile Water for Injection; administer 1.0 mL (0.9 mg) to the buttock. Use as soon as practicable after reconstitution.
- **Timing for Diagnostics**: 
    - **Radioiodine administration**: 24 hours after the final injection.
    - **Diagnostic scanning**: 48 hours after radioiodine administration (72 hours after the final **Thyrogen** injection).
    - **Serum Tg testing**: Obtain sample 72 hours after the final injection.

### Oral Homeopathic Drops [US]
- **Adults and Children ≥12 years**: 20 drops in a little water, 2 times per day.
- **Children 6–12 years**: 10 drops in a little water, 2 times per day.
- **Children <6 years**: Consult a physician.
- **Administration**: Take 15 minutes before meals.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Injection**: There are no known contraindications for the recombinant injection [Australian].
- **Oral Drops**: Not specified in available sources, though general warnings apply to pregnancy and breastfeeding [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings**: None identified in provided sources.
- **Diagnostic Limitations**: **Thyrogen**-stimulated Tg levels are generally lower than and do not correlate with levels after thyroid hormone withdrawal. Risk of missing or underestimating the extent of disease remains even with combined testing [Australian].
- **Cardiac Effects**: Significant transient rises in thyroid hormone may occur. Exercise caution in patients with a history of heart disease (valvular disease, cardiomyopathy, coronary artery disease, tachyarrhythmia) and significant residual thyroid tissue. A case of fatal myocardial infarction has been reported [Australian].
- **Tumour Expansion**: Elevation of TSH levels may cause local oedema or focal haemorrhage at the site of metastases (e.g., brain, spinal cord, orbit, neck). This may lead to acute symptoms such as hemiplegia, hemiparesis, or swallowing difficulty. Corticosteroid pretreatment should be considered if expansion compromises vital structures [Australian].
- **Hyperthyroidism**: Transient hyperthyroidism (induced by rhTSH) has been linked to severe adverse outcomes, including stroke [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment**: TSH elimination is significantly slower in dialysis-dependent patients, potentially leading to prolonged elevation of TSH levels (up to two weeks) and increased risk of nausea and headache [Australian].
- **Homeopathic Warnings [US]**: 
    - Consult a doctor if symptoms persist more than 5 days or worsen.
    - Contains 30% ethyl alcohol.
    - Keep out of reach of children.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Recombinant Injection [Australian]
Adverse reaction data from clinical trials (N=481) and post-marketing surveillance:
- **Very Common (>10%)**: Nausea (11.9%).
- **Common (1–10%)**: Headache (7.3%), fatigue (3.3%), vomiting (2.9%), dizziness (2.5%), paraesthesia (1.7%), asthenia (1.5%), diarrhoea (1.2%).
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%)**: Influenza, feeling hot, ageusia, dysgeusia, neck pain.
- **Serious/Rare (<0.1%)**: 
    - Stroke (very rare cases reported post-marketing).
    - Fatal myocardial infarction.
    - Hypersensitivity: Urticaria, rash, pruritus, flushing, and respiratory signs (wheezing, bronchospasm).
    - Rapid enlargement of locally recurring papillary carcinoma (within 12–48 hours).

### Oral Drops [US]
- Not specified in available sources.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Thyroid Hormones**: No interactions were observed between **Thyrogen** and concurrently administered T3 or T4 in clinical trials [Australian].
- **Formal Studies**: Formal interaction studies have not been performed for either the injection [Australian] or the homeopathic solution [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**:
    - **Australian Category B2**: Studies in animals are inadequate, but available data show no evidence of increased foetal damage. Recombinant injection should not be used during pregnancy.
    - **US (Homeopathic)**: Ask a health professional before use.
- **Breastfeeding**:
    - **Injection**: It is unknown if the drug is excreted in human milk. Patients should not breastfeed [Australian].
    - **US (Homeopathic)**: Ask a health professional before use.
- **Fertility**: Studies have not been performed to evaluate effects on fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: 
    - **Injection**: Safety and effectiveness in children <18 years have not been assessed [Australian].
    - **Oral Drops**: Dosing provided for ages 6–12; consult a physician for children <6 years [US].
- **Elderly Use**: 
    - **Injection**: No difference in safety/efficacy for diagnostic indications in patients <65 vs. >65 years. Limited data for treatment indications. High-risk elderly patients require careful risk-benefit evaluation [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment**: No specific dose reduction guidelines are available, but slower elimination occurs. Activity of 131I should be carefully selected by a nuclear physician in patients with significant impairment [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**:
    - **rhTSH**: Binds to TSH receptors on thyroid epithelial cells or well-differentiated thyroid cancer tissue. It stimulates iodine uptake, organification, and the synthesis/secretion of thyroglobulin (Tg), T3, and T4 [Australian].
    - **Homeopathic**: Proposed to support neurotrophic and brain function via highly diluted components (e.g., Brain Derived Neurotrophic Factor, Lachesis mutus, Aurum metallicum) [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics (Injection)**:
    - **Peak Concentration**: 116 ± 38 mU/L reached at approximately 13 ± 8 hours (median 10 hours).
    - **Elimination Half-life**: 22 ± 9 hours.
    - **Clearance**: Likely involves the liver and kidneys [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Diagnostic Adjunct**: Two Phase III trials (n=358) showed **Thyrogen** scan detected remnant/cancer in the thyroid bed in 81–86% of patients in whom it was detected after hormone withdrawal. However, it failed to detect metastatic disease in 24% of patients where withdrawal scans were positive [Australian].
- **Remnant Ablation**: A comparative study (n=60) showed successful ablation rates with 100 mCi 131I were comparable between **Thyrogen**-treated and hormone-withdrawal patients (100% vs 100% based on no visible uptake) [Australian].
- **Quality of Life**: Patients receiving rhTSH reported significantly better quality of life (SF-36) and fewer hypothyroid symptoms compared to those undergoing hormone withdrawal [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Injection (Powder)**: Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate). Do not freeze. Protect from light. After reconstitution, store at 2°C to 8°C for no more than 24 hours [Australian].
- **Oral Drops**: Not specified in available sources beyond the warning to keep out of reach of children [US].
