# Editorial Product Information: Tiopronin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory guidance into a concise reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource only. It is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing in any particular region. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tiopronin (a reducing and cystine-binding thiol).
- **Brand Names:** VENXXIVA [US]. 
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Delayed-release tablets: 100 mg and 300 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Distributed by Cycle Pharmaceuticals Ltd. [US]; Manufactured for Torrent Pharmaceuticals Limited [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Prevention of Cystine Stone Formation:** Indicated for use in adults and paediatric patients (9 years of age and older) with severe homozygous cystinuria who have not responded sufficiently to high fluid intake, alkali therapy, and dietary modifications [US]. 

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Initial Dosage:** 800 mg per day [US].
- **Maintenance:** In clinical studies, the average effective dosage was approximately 1,000 mg per day [US].

### Paediatric Dosing
- **Initial Dosage (≥9 years):** 15 mg/kg/day [US].
- **Maximum Dosage:** Avoid dosages exceeding 50 mg/kg per day due to increased risks of proteinuria [US].
- **Limitations:** Not approved for use in paediatric patients weighing less than 20 kg or those unable to swallow tablets [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Timing:** Administer in three divided doses at the same times each day [US].
- **Food Interaction:** Must be taken **without food** [US].
- **Alcohol Interaction:** Avoid alcohol consumption 2 hours before and 3 hours after administration [US].

### Monitoring and Titration
- **Cystine Monitoring:** Measure urinary cystine 1 month after initiation and every 3 months thereafter. Adjust dosage to maintain a urinary cystine concentration below 250 mg/L [US].
- **Proteinuria Monitoring:** Assess for proteinuria prior to treatment and every 3 to 6 months during therapy [US].
- **History of Toxicity:** Consider starting at a lower dosage in patients with a history of severe toxicity to d-penicillamine [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to tiopronin or any excipients in the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Proteinuria:** Cases of proteinuria, including nephrotic syndrome and membranous nephropathy, have been reported. If proteinuria develops, the drug should be discontinued. Therapy may be cautiously restarted at a lower dose after the proteinuria resolves [US].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Reactions such as drug fever, rash, fever, arthralgia, and lymphadenopathy may occur. Patients should be monitored closely for these symptoms [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are common and may require dose adjustment or discontinuation.

### Frequency Data (Uncontrolled Trial, N=66)
| System Organ Class | Adverse Reaction | Frequency (Approx.) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea, emesis, diarrhea/soft stools, oral ulcers | ≥10% |
| **General** | Fatigue, fever | ≥10% |
| **Musculoskeletal** | Arthralgia | ≥5% |
| **Renal** | Proteinuria | ≥10% |
| **Skin** | Rash | ≥10% |

### Other Notable Reactions
- **Very Common (>10%):** Emesis, oral ulcers, and fatigue [US].
- **Common (1–10%):** Anorexia, weakness, peripheral edema, pruritus, urticaria, and skin wrinkling [US].
- **Postmarketing Reports:** Congestive heart failure, vertigo, jaundice, liver transaminitis, renal failure, ageusia (loss of taste), and pemphigus foliaceus [US].
- **Taste Disturbance:** A reduction in taste perception (hypogeusia) may develop, likely due to the chelation of trace metals. This is often self-limited [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Alcohol:** In vitro studies indicate that alcohol increases the dissolution rate of delayed-release tablets (dose dumping). The risk of adverse events when taken with alcohol is unknown. **Management:** Avoid alcohol 2 hours before and 3 hours after dosing [US].
- **d-penicillamine:** Patients with prior toxicity to d-penicillamine may be at higher risk for adverse reactions with tiopronin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned under current labelling rules. 
    - **Data:** Available case reports have not identified a drug-associated risk for major birth defects or miscarriage. Animal studies (mice/rats) using doses up to 2 times the maximum human dose did not show adverse developmental outcomes [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Breastfeeding is **not recommended** during treatment. There are no data on the presence of tiopronin in human milk; however, there is a potential for serious adverse reactions (e.g., nephrotic syndrome) in the infant, and the drug may suppress milk production [US].
- **Fertility:** High doses in animals have interfered with pregnancy maintenance and fetal viability. In male rats, 20 mg/kg/day (IM) for 60 days reduced sperm count and motility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Indicated for children ≥9 years of age. Safety in children <9 years or weighing <20 kg has not been established for this formulation [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Care should be taken in dose selection due to the higher frequency of decreased renal function in the elderly. Monitoring of renal function is recommended [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Tiopronin is substantially excreted by the kidneys. The risk of adverse reactions may be increased in patients with impaired renal function [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Tiopronin is an active reducing agent that undergoes thiol-disulfide exchange with cystine. This creates a water-soluble mixed disulfide (tiopronin-cysteine), reducing the concentration of poorly soluble cystine in the urine [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The reduction in urinary cystine is generally proportional to the dose. A dose of 1 g/day typically reduces cystine by 250–350 mg/day [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** For delayed-release tablets, the median Tmax is 3 hours (range 1–6 hours) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Up to 48% of an oral dose is excreted in the urine within 4 hours, and 78% by 72 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy Data:** Based on an uncontrolled trial of 66 patients (aged 9 to 68 years). In patients who previously stopped d-penicillamine due to toxicity, approximately 45% (22 of 49) were able to tolerate and continue treatment with tiopronin [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions are permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F and 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Keep in the original container with a child-resistant closure [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription Only Medicine) [US].
