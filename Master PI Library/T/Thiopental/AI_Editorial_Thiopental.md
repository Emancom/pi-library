# Editorial Product Information: Thiopental Sodium

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or clinical guidelines mandated in a specific jurisdiction. Prescribers must consult local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Thiopental sodium.
- **Chemical Class:** Thiobarbiturate (ultra-short-acting).
- **Brand Names:** Omegapharm Thiopental Sodium [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N01AF03.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 470 mg powder for injection, presented in glass vials. It is stabilised with 30 mg of anhydrous sodium carbonate [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Omegapharm Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Thiopental sodium is indicated for:
- Use as the primary anaesthetic agent for short surgical procedures.
- Induction of anaesthesia prior to the use of other anaesthetic agents.
- Control of convulsive states (short-term) [Australian].
- Supplementation of regional anaesthesia or low-potency agents (e.g., nitrous oxide) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Principles
There is no fixed dosage; the drug must be titrated to individual patient requirements based on age, sex, and body weight. 
- **Demographic Variations:** Younger patients generally require higher doses. The elderly and middle-aged require lower doses due to slower metabolism. Adult females require less than adult males. Obese patients require higher doses proportional to body weight compared to lean patients of the same weight [Australian].
- **Preparation:** Reconstitute aseptically with Water for Injections, 0.9% Sodium Chloride, or 5% Glucose. For intermittent IV administration, concentrations of 2.0% (20 mg/mL) or 2.5% (25 mg/mL) are standard. Concentrations below 2% in water for injections are avoided due to the risk of haemolysis [Australian].

### Specific Dosing
- **Test Dose:** A small test dose of 25 to 75 mg (1 to 3 mL of a 25 mg/mL solution) should be administered, followed by at least 60 seconds of observation to assess for unusual sensitivity [Australian].
- **Induction (Adult):** 50 to 75 mg injected at intervals of 20 to 40 seconds. For rapid induction in a 70 kg adult, 210 to 280 mg (3 to 4 mg/kg) is typically required [Australian].
- **Maintenance:** Small repeated injections (25 to 50 mg) or a continuous IV drip (0.2% or 0.4% concentration).
- **Convulsive States:** 75 to 125 mg (3 to 5 mL of a 25 mg/mL solution) administered immediately. If following local anaesthetic use, 125 to 250 mg may be required over a 10-minute period [Australian].
- **Increased Intracranial Pressure:** Intermittent bolus injections of 1.5 to 3.5 mg/kg [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
**Absolute Contraindications [Australian]:**
- Absence of suitable veins for administration.
- Hypersensitivity to barbiturates.
- Status asthmaticus or inability to maintain an adequate airway.
- Porphyria (latent, manifest, acute intermittent, or variegate).
- Constrictive pericarditis.
- Severe respiratory embarrassment.
- Inflammatory conditions of the mouth, jaw, or neck.

**Relative Contraindications (Caution required) [Australian]:**
- Severe cardiovascular disease, shock, or hypotension.
- Addison’s disease.
- Hepatic or renal dysfunction.
- Myxoedema.
- Severe anaemia.
- Myasthenia gravis.
- Increased blood urea.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Administration Safety:** Must only be administered by personnel qualified in intravenous anaesthesia. Resuscitative equipment for airway management and circulation must be immediately available [Australian].
- **Vascular Injury:** **Avoid extravasation and intra-arterial injection.** Intra-arterial injection may cause arteriospasm and gangrene. If this occurs, stop the injection immediately. Suggested treatments include dilute papaverine or procaine, or sympathetic block [Australian].
- **Respiratory Depression:** Thiopental has a high potential for respiratory depression; airway patency must be prioritised at all times.
- **Microbial Safety:** The product contains no bacteriostatic agents. Reconstituted solutions should be used immediately or stored at 2-8°C for no longer than 24 hours [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Frequency data is not specified in available sources, but the following effects are reported:
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypotension, myocardial depression (proportional to dose/concentration), cardiac arrhythmias, tachycardia [Australian].
- **Respiratory:** Respiratory depression, apnoea, laryngospasm, bronchospasm, sneezing, coughing [Australian].
- **Neurological:** Prolonged somnolence, recovery delay, excitatory phenomena (e.g., involuntary muscle movements, hiccups), shivering (thermal reaction) [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylactoid reactions (urticaria, oedema, vasodilatation) [Australian].
- **Other:** Immune haemolytic anaemia with renal failure (rare), radial nerve palsy (rare), taste of garlic prior to anaesthesia [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Synergistic/Potentiating Effects:** Benzodiazepines, CNS depressants, and ethanol increase the hypnotic/depressant effects. Phenothiazines may increase CNS excitation and hypotension [Australian].
- **Hypotension Risk:** Diuretics, diazoxide, and other hypotension-producing medications have additive effects. Ketamine increases the risk of hypotension and respiratory depression [Australian].
- **Prolonged Action:** Probenecid.
- **Antagonism:** Aminophylline [Australian].
- **Reduced Dose Requirement:** Sulfisoxazole [Australian].
- **Other:** Opioid analgesics may decrease antinociceptive action; magnesium sulfate (parenteral) may increase CNS depression [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category A]. Thiopental readily crosses the placental barrier. Fetal blood levels reach a maximum 2–3 minutes after maternal IV administration. Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. It should only be used in pregnancy if clearly needed [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Small amounts may be excreted in breast milk following high doses [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No specific problems documented to date; however, neonates may have an increased half-life and lower protein binding. There is a potential concern regarding **paediatric neurotoxicity** (cognitive deficits) following repeated or prolonged exposure early in life, though clinical significance is currently undetermined [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Recovery of cognitive and psychomotor functions is generally slower. Age-related increases in volume of distribution and half-life, coupled with decreased protein binding and hepatic function, often require dose reductions [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Use with extreme caution; hypnotic effects may be prolonged or intensified [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Thiopental is an ultra-short-acting CNS depressant that induces hypnosis and anaesthesia but not analgesia. It acts by enhancing GABA responses and diminishing glutamate responses, increasing membrane conductance to decrease neuronal excitability [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Hypnosis occurs within 30–40 seconds of IV injection. Recovery from a single dose occurs in 20–30 minutes [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Distribution:** Highly lipid-soluble. Approximately 80% is bound to plasma protein. It redistributes rapidly into fatty tissues, which act as a reservoir, potentially leading to prolonged anaesthesia with repeated doses [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily degraded in the liver.
    - **Elimination:** Elimination half-life is 3 to 8 hours. Metabolites are pharmacologically inactive and excreted in the urine [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Use as soon as possible. If storage is required, hold at 2-8°C for a maximum of 24 hours [Australian].
- **Incompatibilities:** Thiopental is highly alkaline (pH 10-11) and incompatible with acidic solutions (e.g., suxamethonium, tubocurarine). Do not use if a precipitate is visible [Australian].
