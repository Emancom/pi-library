# Editorial Product Information: Tafenoquine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of **tafenoquine** for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource only. It is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing within a particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tafenoquine (as succinate), an 8-aminoquinoline antimalarial.
- **Brand Name:** **Kozenis** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** P01BA07.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
  - 150 mg film-coated tablets (equivalent to 188.2 mg tafenoquine succinate) [Australian].
  - 50 mg film-coated dispersible tablets (equivalent to 62.74 mg tafenoquine succinate) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** GlaxoSmithKline Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Radical Cure of *Plasmodium vivax*:** Indicated for the prevention of relapse (radical cure) of *P. vivax* malaria in patients aged 2 years and older [Australian].
- **Combination Requirement:** Must be used in combination with a 3-day course of chloroquine for the treatment of the acute infection phase [Australian].
- **Limitations of Use:** Not indicated for the treatment of acute *P. vivax* malaria (blood-stage infection) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Timing:** Administer on Day 1 or Day 2 of the 3-day chloroquine regimen [Australian].
- **Administration:** Take with food to increase systemic absorption. Tablets (150 mg) and dispersible tablets (50 mg) must not be broken or crushed [Australian].
- **Dispersible Tablets:** Must be fully dissolved in water before administration.

### Adults, Adolescents, and Children (>35 kg)
- **Standard Dose:** A single 300 mg dose (administered as two 150 mg tablets) [Australian].

### Children and Adolescents (≥2 years, 10 kg to 35 kg)
Dosing is weight-based using 50 mg dispersible tablets [Australian]:
- **>10 kg to ≤20 kg:** 100 mg (two tablets) as a single dose.
- **>20 kg to ≤35 kg:** 200 mg (four tablets) as a single dose.

### Missed Dose and Vomiting
- **Vomiting:** If vomiting occurs within 60 minutes of dosing, a repeat dose should be administered. Re-dosing should not be attempted more than once. If vomiting occurs ≥60 minutes after the initial dose, re-dosing is not recommended [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **G6PD Deficiency:** Patients with glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency or unknown G6PD status, due to the high risk of haemolytic anaemia [Australian].
- **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated during pregnancy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated if the infant is G6PD deficient or has an unknown G6PD status [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to tafenoquine, other 8-aminoquinolines, or any excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Haemolytic Anaemia and G6PD Testing:** Quantitative G6PD testing must be performed prior to prescription. Tafenoquine must be withheld if G6PD enzyme levels are <70% of normal [Australian].
- **Methaemoglobinaemia:** Asymptomatic elevations have been observed. Use with caution in patients with NADH-dependent methaemoglobin reductase deficiency [Australian].
- **Psychiatric Effects:** While serious reactions were not reported at the 300 mg dose in trials, higher doses have been linked to depression and psychosis. Use with caution in patients with a history of serious psychiatric disorders [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Serious reactions (e.g., angioedema, urticaria) may occur. Do not re-administer if a reaction occurs [Australian].
- **Long Half-Life:** Due to the 15-day terminal half-life, the onset or duration of adverse reactions may be delayed for up to three months [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally based on a single 300 mg dose in clinical trials [Australian].

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Decreased haemoglobin, elevated methaemoglobin, insomnia, headache, dizziness, nausea, vomiting, increased blood creatinine |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Anxiety, somnolence, photophobia, vortex keratopathy, increased alanine aminotransferase |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Hypersensitivity (urticaria, angioedema), abnormal dreams |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Haemolytic anaemia (especially in G6PD deficiency) and potential psychiatric disturbances (psychosis/depression) [Australian].
- **Pediatric Specifics:** Vomiting was more frequently reported in children (approximately 20% in trials) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **OCT2 and MATE Substrates:** Tafenoquine inhibits organic cation transporter 2 (OCT2) and multidrug and toxin extrusion (MATE) transporters *in vitro*.
  - **Metformin:** Potential for increased metformin exposure and small risk of lactic acidosis; use with caution [Australian].
  - **Narrow Therapeutic Index Drugs:** Avoid co-administration with substrates like dofetilide, procainamide, or pilsicainide [Australian].
- **Antimalarials:**
  - **Chloroquine:** No clinically significant interaction [Australian].
  - **Dihydroartemisinin-piperaquine (DHA-PQP):** Concomitant use is NOT recommended; trials showed insufficient efficacy for radical cure [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category C]. Contraindicated. There is a risk of foetal haemolysis even if the mother is not G6PD deficient. Women should use effective contraception for 3 months following a dose [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated if the infant is G6PD deficient or status is unknown. Tafenoquine may persist in breast milk for up to 3 months [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed reduced viable foetuses and implantation sites at maternally toxic doses [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for children ≥2 years (weight >10 kg). Safety and efficacy in children <2 years or <10 kg have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Limited data in patients ≥65 years; however, no dose adjustment is currently recommended based on pharmacokinetic modelling [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specifically studied, but dose adjustments are unlikely to be required for a single-dose administration [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specifically studied; dose adjustments are unlikely to be required for a single-dose administration [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Eradicates *P. vivax* liver-stage hypnozoites to prevent relapse. The exact molecular target is unknown [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ is 12–15 hours. Bioavailability increases when taken with a high-fat meal (AUC $\uparrow$ 41%, $C_{max}$ $\uparrow$ 31%) [Australian].
  - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (>99.5%). Preferentially partitions into erythrocytes [Australian].
  - **Metabolism:** Undergoes very slow metabolism; no major human-specific metabolites identified [Australian].
  - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 15 days. Clearance is roughly 3 L/h [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adult/Adolescent (Trial TAF112582):** A double-blind study (N=522) compared tafenoquine + chloroquine to chloroquine alone. Recurrence-free efficacy at 6 months was 62% for the tafenoquine group vs. 28% for chloroquine alone ($p < 0.001$) [Australian].
- **Paediatric (Trial TAF113577):** An open-label trial (N=60) in patients aged 2–15 years. Using weight-based dosing, the 4-month recurrence-free rate was 95% [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C. Keep in the original package to protect from moisture [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** [Schedule 4] – Prescription Only Medicine. Subject to additional monitoring (Black Triangle Scheme) [Australian].
