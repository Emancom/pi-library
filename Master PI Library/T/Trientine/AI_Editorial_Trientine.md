# Editorial Product Information: Trientine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of trientine dihydrochloride for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a single clinical reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official, legally binding Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for final prescribing decisions.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Trientine dihydrochloride (a copper-chelating agent).
- **Chemical Class:** Polyamine.
- **Brand Names:** Trientine Dr. Reddy’s [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A16AX12.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 250 mg hard gelatin capsules (equivalent to 167 mg trientine free base) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Dr. Reddy’s Laboratories (Australia) Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Wilson’s Disease:** Indicated for the treatment of patients with Wilson's disease (hepatolenticular degeneration) who are intolerant of penicillamine [Australian].
- **Note:** Trientine is NOT indicated for the treatment of cystinuria, rheumatoid arthritis, or biliary cirrhosis [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Initial Dose:** 750 mg to 1,250 mg per day, administered in 2, 3, or 4 divided doses.
- **Maximum Dose:** 2,000 mg per day [Australian].
- **Dose Basis:** Dosing is expressed as the dihydrochloride salt. Note that 250 mg of trientine dihydrochloride corresponds to 167 mg of trientine free base [Australian].

### Pediatric Dosing (Children > 5 years)
- **Initial Dose:** 500 mg to 750 mg per day in 2 or 3 divided doses. 
- **Weight-based Initial Dose:** 20 mg/kg/day, rounded to the nearest whole capsule.
- **Maximum Dose:** 1,500 mg per day [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Empty Stomach:** Must be taken at least 1 hour before or 2 hours after meals.
- **Separation from Other Agents:** Administer at least 1 hour apart from any other medication, food, or milk. 
- **Method:** Capsules should be swallowed whole with water; do not open or chew.

### Titration and Maintenance
- Dose increases should only occur if the clinical response is inadequate or if free serum copper remains persistently >20 microgram/dL (3.1 micromol/L).
- Optimal maintenance dosing should be re-evaluated every 6 to 12 months [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to trientine dihydrochloride or any of the excipients (e.g., gelatin, colloidal silicon dioxide, magnesium stearate) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Neurological Worsening:** At the start of chelation therapy, neurological symptoms (e.g., dystonia, tremor, rigidity) may worsen due to an initial surge in free serum copper. Close monitoring and careful dose titration are required.
- **Iron Deficiency:** Trientine reduces serum iron levels. Monitor patients (especially women) for iron deficiency anaemia. If iron supplementation is required, it must be timed separately from trientine.
- **Zinc Interaction:** Combination with zinc is not recommended [Australian].
- **Lupus-like Reactions:** Reported in patients previously treated with D-penicillamine, though a causal link to trientine is not established.
- **Cystinuria/Rheumatoid Arthritis:** Unlike penicillamine, trientine lacks a sulfhydryl moiety and cannot bind cysteine; it is ineffective for these conditions.

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Frequency:** At least twice yearly; more frequent during initiation or dose adjustment.
- **Parameters:** Clinical symptoms, free serum copper (non-ceruloplasmin copper), and 24-hour urinary copper excretion.
- **Target:** Maintain free serum copper between 10–15 microgram/dL (1.5–2.3 micromol/L).

## 6. Adverse Effects
Clinical experience suggests trientine is generally better tolerated than D-penicillamine.

| System Organ Class | Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Common | Nausea |
| | Not Known | Colitis, duodenitis |
| **Blood/Lymphatic** | Uncommon | Anaemia, aplastic anaemia, sideroblastic anaemia |
| **Nervous System** | Uncommon | Dystonia, tremor |
| | Not Known | Dysarthria, muscle rigidity, neurological deterioration |
| **Immune System** | Not Known | Lupus-like syndrome, lupus nephritis |
| **Skin** | Uncommon | Rash |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Overtreatment carries a risk of copper deficiency, which is particularly hazardous to children and pregnant women as copper is essential for growth and mental development [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Iron:** Trientine inhibits iron absorption and vice versa. Separate doses by at least 2 hours [Australian].
- **Antacids:** While no direct evidence of reduced efficacy exists for calcium and magnesium antacids, it is considered good practice to separate administration.
- **Food/Milk:** Significantly inhibits absorption; adhere strictly to empty stomach administration.
- **Zinc:** Not recommended for concomitant use as it may interfere with copper chelation efficacy [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Animal studies show teratogenicity (fetal brain abnormalities, microcephaly) at clinically relevant doses, likely due to induced copper deficiency.
  - **Recommendation:** Use only if potential benefit justifies the risk. Monitor maternal serum copper levels closely; dose adjustments may be required to prevent copper deficiency in the fetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** Excretion in human milk is unknown. Caution should be exercised.
- **Fertility:** Data on human fertility is unavailable [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children < 5 years have not been established. The capsule form is not suitable for this age group [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Limited data. Start at the low end of the dosing range due to higher frequency of hepatic, renal, or cardiac impairment.
- **Renal Impairment:** Limited information. No specific dose adjustment is required, but close monitoring of renal function and copper levels is recommended as clearance may be partially dependent on GFR.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Limited information. No specific dose adjustment is required; however, liver enzyme elevations may occur during treatment.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A copper-chelating agent that forms a stable complex with absorbed copper, facilitating its elimination via the kidneys. It may also bind copper in the intestinal tract to inhibit absorption.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Principal action requires systemic exposure to facilitate urinary excretion.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Low and variable. $T_{max}$ is approximately 1.25 hours.
  - **Distribution:** Widely distributed in tissues; $V_d$ is high (central ~393 L).
  - **Metabolism:** Acetylated into two major metabolites: $N_1$-acetyltriethylenetetramine (MAT) and $N_1,N_{10}$-diacetyltriethylenetetramine (DAT). These have lower chelating affinity than the parent drug.
  - **Elimination:** Primarily renal excretion (as parent and metabolites). Elimination $T_{1/2}$ is approximately 10.2 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Weiss et al. (2013):** A retrospective analysis of 380 patients. In 103 patients receiving trientine as second-line therapy (post-penicillamine intolerance):
  - Hepatic improvement was seen in ~33% of all patients and >66% of symptomatic patients.
  - Neurological improvement was seen in ~25% of all patients and 50% of symptomatic patients.
  - Trientine showed significantly lower discontinuation rates due to adverse events compared to D-penicillamine ($p=0.039$) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C.
- **Handling:** Keep the container tightly closed to protect from moisture [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
