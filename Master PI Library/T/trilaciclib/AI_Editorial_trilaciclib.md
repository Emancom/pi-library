# Editorial Product Information: Trilaciclib

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis has been developed using Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, intended to facilitate clinical decision-making by consolidating regulatory information into a single, structured reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing within a particular region. Clinicians should refer to their local regulatory authority's approved labelling for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Trilaciclib (as trilaciclib dihydrochloride).
- **Chemical Class:** Kinase inhibitor; transient inhibitor of cyclin-dependent kinases (CDK) 4 and 6.
- **Brand Name:** **COSELA** [US].
- **ATC Code:** L01XE (Note: Not explicitly specified in the source document, but typically classified under antineoplastic agents/protein kinase inhibitors).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** For injection: 300 mg of trilaciclib (equivalent to 349 mg of trilaciclib dihydrochloride) supplied as a sterile, preservative-free, yellow, lyophilized cake in a single-dose vial for reconstitution and dilution [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pharmacosmos Therapeutics Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Myeloprotection in Small Cell Lung Cancer (SCLC):** Indicated to decrease the incidence of chemotherapy-induced myelosuppression in adult patients when administered prior to a platinum/etoposide-containing regimen or topotecan-containing regimen for extensive-stage small cell lung cancer (ES-SCLC) [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 General Dosing
- **Recommended Dose:** 240 mg/m² per dose [US].
- **Frequency:** Administered on each day that chemotherapy is provided.
- **Timing:** Completed within 4 hours prior to the start of chemotherapy [US].
- **Sequential Dosing:** The interval between doses on sequential days must not exceed 28 hours [US].

### 3.2 Dose Modifications for Hepatic Impairment
- **Mild (Child-Pugh A):** No dose adjustment required.
- **Moderate to Severe (Child-Pugh B and C):** Reduce the dose to 170 mg/m² [US].

### 3.3 Administration Instructions
- **Infusion Duration:** 300 mg/vial reconstituted to 15 mg/mL, further diluted to a final concentration of 0.5 mg/mL to 3 mg/mL, and administered as a 30-minute IV infusion [US].
- **Filtration:** Must use an infusion set with an in-line filter (0.2 or 0.22 micron). Compatible filter materials: polyethersulfone (PES), polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF), and cellulose acetate (CA) [US]. 
- **Incompatibility:** Do **not** use polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE) in-line filters (PTFE is only acceptable for air vent filters). Do not co-administer other drugs through the same line [US].
- **Flushing:** After the infusion, the line must be flushed with at least 20 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride or 5% Dextrose [US].

### 3.4 Missed Dose and Discontinuation
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, chemotherapy for that day should be discontinued. Resume both trilaciclib and chemotherapy at the next scheduled treatment day [US].
- **Treatment Discontinuation:** If trilaciclib is permanently discontinued, wait 96 hours after the final dose before resuming chemotherapy monotherapy [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Contraindicated in patients with a history of serious hypersensitivity reactions (e.g., anaphylaxis) to trilaciclib [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Injection-Site Reactions:** Common (21%), including phlebitis and thrombophlebitis. Monitor for pain, erythema, and tenderness. For Grade 1/2 reactions, flush the line after infusion and consider changing diluents or using central access. Permanently discontinue for Grade 3/4 reactions [US].
- **Acute Drug Hypersensitivity:** Reported in 6% of patients. Monitor for facial/tongue edema, urticaria, and pruritus. For Grade 2 reactions, hold until resolved (≤ Grade 1); permanently discontinue if Grade 2 recurs or for initial Grade 3/4 reactions [US].
- **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD)/Pneumonitis:** A class effect of CDK4/6 inhibitors. Monitor for cough, dyspnea, or hypoxia. Hold for Grade 2 and evaluate; permanently discontinue for recurrent Grade 2 or any Grade 3/4 ILD [US].
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Based on mechanism, can cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment [US].

### 6.2 Serious Adverse Effects
- **Serious Reactions:** Occurred in 30% of patients. Those reported in >3% included respiratory failure, hemorrhage, and thrombosis [US].
- **Fatalities:** Observed in 5% of patients, primarily due to pneumonia, respiratory failure, hemoptysis, and cerebrovascular accident [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### 7.1 Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Transporter-Mediated Interactions:** Trilaciclib is an inhibitor of **OCT2, MATE1, and MATE-2K**. Co-administration increases the concentration of substrates for these transporters, particularly in the kidney [US].
    - **Dofetilide:** Increased blood levels may lead to serious ventricular arrhythmias and QT prolongation. Benefits must be weighed against risks [US].
    - **Dalfampridine:** Increased levels increase seizure risk. Monitor and weigh benefits [US].
    - **Cisplatin:** Concurrent use may increase cisplatin accumulation in the kidney; monitor closely for nephrotoxicity [US].
- **Metabolic Enzymes:** Trilaciclib is a substrate of CYP3A4, CYP2C8, and Aldehyde Oxidase (AO). However, strong CYP3A inhibitors (itraconazole) or inducers (rifampin) do not clinically alter trilaciclib pharmacokinetics [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: No lettered category, but risk of fetal harm noted]. Based on mechanism of action, trilaciclib may cause fetal harm. There are no available human data on drug-associated risks.
- **Contraception:** Females of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for **3 weeks** after the final dose [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on the presence of the drug in human milk. Advise patients not to breastfeed during treatment and for **3 weeks** after the final dose [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed reversible reductions in ovary and uterus weights. Potential impact on human fertility has not been studied [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** In clinical trials, 46% of patients were ≥65 years. No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed compared to younger patients [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for mild to moderate impairment (eGFR 30–89 mL/min/1.73 m²). The effect of severe renal impairment or dialysis has not been studied [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for mild (Child-Pugh A). Reduce dose to 170 mg/m² for moderate or severe (Child-Pugh B or C) impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Transient, reversible inhibitor of CDK4 and 6. It induces G1 cell cycle arrest in hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (HSPCs), protecting them from the cytotoxic effects of chemotherapy [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Progenitor cell arrest occurs within 32 hours post-infusion. At 240 mg/m², no clinically relevant QTc prolongation (>10 msec) is observed [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Vss of 1130 L; plasma protein binding is 69% [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized via AO, CYP3A4, and CYP2C8 [US].
    - **Elimination:** Mean terminal half-life is approximately 14 hours. 79% excreted in feces (7% unchanged) and 14% in urine (2% unchanged) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy Data:** Established in three randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in ES-SCLC [US].
    - **Study 1 (E/P/Atezolizumab):** Significantly reduced duration of severe neutropenia (DSN) in Cycle 1 (0 vs 4 days) and the proportion of patients with severe neutropenia (2% vs 49%) [US].
    - **Study 2 (E/P):** Reduced DSN in Cycle 1 (0 vs 3 days) and severe neutropenia incidence (5% vs 42%) [US].
    - **Study 3 (Topotecan):** Reduced DSN in Cycle 1 (2 vs 7 days) and severe neutropenia incidence (41% vs 76%) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Store in vial for up to 4 hours at 20°C to 25°C. Do not refrigerate or freeze [US].
- **Diluted Solution:** 
    - In 5% Dextrose: Up to 12 hours at 20°C to 25°C [US].
    - In 0.9% Sodium Chloride: Up to 8 hours (PVC/EVA/PO bags) or 4 hours (PO/PA bags) at 20°C to 25°C [US].
