# Editorial Product Information: Tiotropium

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: **Australian (TGA)**.
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of tiotropium powder for inhalation, synthesised for healthcare professionals. 
- Clinical Note: This is an editorial synthesis and should not replace official Product Information (PI) when prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this writing, only the Australian PI for the brand **Braltus** was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tiotropium (as tiotropium bromide).
- **Chemical Class:** Long-acting, N-quaternary ammonium antimuscarinic (anticholinergic) agent.
- **Brand Names:** **Braltus** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** R03BB04.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Hard capsule containing 13 micrograms of tiotropium (equivalent to 15.6 micrograms of tiotropium bromide). The delivered dose (the amount leaving the mouthpiece of the inhaler) is 10 micrograms [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral inhalation.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Teva Pharma Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD):** Long-term maintenance treatment of bronchospasm and dyspnoea associated with COPD [Australian].
- **Exacerbation Prevention:** Indicated for the reduction and prevention of COPD exacerbations [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** Inhalation of the contents of one capsule once daily at the same time each day [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - For use only with the **ZONDA** inhalation device.
    - Capsules must **not** be swallowed.
    - The capsule should be placed in the chamber of the device, not the mouthpiece.
    - Patients should be trained in the correct inhalation technique by a healthcare professional [Australian].
- **Device Management:** The device should be discarded after 30 uses (one bottle of capsules). It is not intended for multi-bottle use [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources, however, the medication must not be used more than once daily [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known history of hypersensitivity to tiotropium bromide, atropine, or its derivatives (e.g., ipratropium, oxitropium).
- **Excipients:** Hypersensitivity to lactose monohydrate (which may contain trace milk proteins) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Acute Use:** Tiotropium is a maintenance bronchodilator and must **not** be used for rescue therapy in acute bronchospasm.
- **Immediate Hypersensitivity:** Reactions may occur immediately following administration.
- **Anticholinergic Precautions:** Use with caution in patients with:
    - Narrow-angle glaucoma.
    - Prostatic hyperplasia.
    - Bladder-neck obstruction.
- **Renal Function:** Close monitoring is required for patients with moderate to severe renal impairment (creatinine clearance ≤ 50 mL/min) due to increased plasma concentrations [Australian].
- **Cardiac Cautions:** Caution is advised in patients with recent myocardial infarction (<6 months), unstable or life-threatening arrhythmias, or recent hospitalisation for heart failure (NYHA Class III/IV) within the past year. These populations were excluded from clinical trials [Australian].
- **Paradoxical Bronchospasm:** As with other inhaled medicines, inhalation-induced bronchospasm may occur.
- **Ocular Safety:** Avoid allowing powder to enter the eyes. Signs of acute narrow-angle glaucoma (eye pain, blurred vision, halos) require immediate specialist consultation.
- **Dental Health:** Long-term dry mouth associated with anticholinergic therapy may increase the risk of dental caries.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are primarily attributed to the anticholinergic properties of the drug.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Dry mouth (usually mild) |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Dizziness, headache, taste disorder, blurred vision, atrial fibrillation, cough, dysphonia, pharyngitis, constipation, gastroesophageal reflux disease, oropharyngeal candidiasis, rash, urinary retention, dysuria |
| **Rare** (<0.1%) | Insomnia, glaucoma, increased intraocular pressure, palpitations, tachycardia, supraventricular tachycardia, bronchospasm, epistaxis, laryngitis, sinusitis, intestinal obstruction/ileus paralytic, dysphagia, gingivitis, glossitis, stomatitis, nausea, pruritus, angioneurotic oedema, urticaria, hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis, urinary tract infection |
| **Very Rare** (<0.01%) | Dental caries |
| **Not Known** | Dehydration, skin infection/ulcer, dry skin, joint swelling |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Anticholinergic Agents:** Co-administration with other anticholinergic medications (e.g., ipratropium, glycopyrronium, umeclidinium) has not been studied and is **not recommended** due to potential additive effects.
- **Common COPD Medications:** No clinical evidence of interactions with sympathomimetic bronchodilators (LABA), methylxanthines, or oral/inhaled steroids [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** In vitro studies indicate tiotropium does not inhibit major CYP450 enzymes (1A1, 1A2, 2B6, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 2E1, or 3A).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category B1**. Data in pregnant women are limited. Animal studies do not suggest direct or indirect harmful effects at clinically relevant doses. As a precaution, avoid use during pregnancy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Tiotropium is excreted in the milk of lactating rats. Clinical data for human lactation are unavailable. Use only if the expected benefit outweighs the risk to the infant [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No clinical data available. Animal studies showed no impairment of fertility in rats [Australian].

## 10. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients under 18 years have not been established; use is not recommended in this population [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** May be used at the recommended dose. However, clinicians should note that renal clearance typically decreases with age, and anticholinergic effects may increase.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - Mild (CLCr 50–80 mL/min): No dose adjustment required. 
    - Moderate to Severe (CLCr ≤ 50 mL/min): Monitor closely for anticholinergic effects [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment recommended; however, data are limited [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Long-acting, specific muscarinic receptor antagonist. It binds to M1 through M5 receptors. Bronchodilation is achieved via inhibition of M3-receptors at the airway smooth muscle.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Tiotropium shows slow dissociation from M3 receptors, providing a duration of action exceeding 24 hours. Bronchodilation is primarily a local effect on the airways.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is approximately 19.5% after inhalation. Peak plasma levels occur 5–7 minutes post-inhalation.
    - **Distribution:** 72% plasma protein binding; volume of distribution is 32 L/kg. It does not cross the blood-brain barrier significantly.
    - **Metabolism:** Minimal non-enzymatic cleavage to inactive metabolites.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is 27–45 hours. 74% of an intravenous dose is excreted unchanged in the urine. Steady state is reached by day 7 [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Bioequivalence:** **Braltus** (13 mcg tiotropium) has been demonstrated to be bioequivalent to the innovator product (18 mcg tiotropium) in terms of delivered dose and systemic availability [Australian].
- **Efficacy:** In one-year trials, tiotropium showed significant improvement in FEV1 (mean increase of 0.14 L at day 92) compared to placebo and ipratropium. 
- **Exacerbations:** Pooled data from one-year studies showed tiotropium significantly delayed the time to first COPD exacerbation and reduced the number of hospitalisations compared to placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C.
- **Moisture Protection:** Keep the bottle tightly closed and store in the original package. Do not refrigerate or freeze.
- **In-use life:** Discard the ZONDA inhaler after 30 uses [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
