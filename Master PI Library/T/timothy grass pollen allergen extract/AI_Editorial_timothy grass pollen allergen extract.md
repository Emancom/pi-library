# Editorial Product Information: timothy grass pollen allergen extract

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): timothy grass pollen allergen extract
- Chemical class: Pollen allergen extract (allergen immunotherapy)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Sublingual

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 2014 (U.S. approval date stated in source)
- Most recent revision/update of the provided U.S. product information: September 2022
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources
- Registration/approval dates for jurisdictions other than the United States: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Immunotherapy for treatment of grass pollen–induced allergic rhinitis with or without conjunctivitis confirmed by positive skin test or in vitro testing for pollen-specific IgE antibodies for timothy grass or cross‑reactive grass pollens. Approved use age range (as stated in the available sources): 5 through 65 years of age.
- Not indicated for immediate symptomatic relief of allergic symptoms.
- Jurisdictional differences in indication or age range: Not specified in available sources other than the U.S. information above.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult and pediatric)
- One sublingual tablet once daily (strength: as described in available sources; strength numeric label retained in source documents).
- Initiate at least 12 weeks before the expected onset of each grass pollen season and continue daily throughout the season.
- For sustained effectiveness for one grass pollen season after cessation, a daily course for three consecutive years (including offseason intervals) may be used.

### Administration instructions
- First dose should be administered in a healthcare setting under supervision of a clinician experienced in diagnosis and treatment of allergic diseases; observe the patient for at least 30 minutes after the first dose to monitor for severe systemic or severe local allergic reactions.
- If the first dose is tolerated, subsequent daily doses may be taken at home.
- Place the tablet under the tongue and allow to dissolve; do not swallow for at least 1 minute. Do not take with food or beverage; avoid food or drink for 5 minutes after dosing.
- Children should receive administration under adult supervision.

### Titration schedules
- No up‑titration schedule specified in available sources (fixed daily maintenance dosing).

### Missed dose instructions
- Data regarding the safety of restarting after missed doses are limited. In clinical trials, interruptions up to 7 days were allowed; specific restart regimens beyond that are not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
(Include all contraindications reported in the available sources)
- Severe, unstable, or uncontrolled asthma.
- History of any severe systemic allergic reaction.
- History of any severe local reaction after taking any sublingual allergen immunotherapy.
- History of eosinophilic esophagitis.
- Hypersensitivity to any of the inactive ingredients listed in the product (inactive ingredients specified in the available sources include gelatin, mannitol and sodium hydroxide).

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed/major warnings
- **Severe allergic reactions including life‑threatening anaphylaxis and severe laryngopharyngeal (upper airway) restriction can occur.** Observe patients for at least 30 minutes after the initial dose. Prescribe and train patients in the use of auto‑injectable epinephrine; instruct patients to seek immediate medical attention after use of epinephrine.
  - [US] This boxed warning and the above measures are explicitly emphasized.

### General warnings and precautions
- Do not administer to patients with severe, unstable, or uncontrolled asthma.
- Geriatric safety data are lacking for patients older than 65 years; use clinical judgement.
- Withhold immunotherapy during an acute asthma exacerbation. Reevaluate and consider discontinuation for patients with recurrent exacerbations.
- Concomitant administration with other allergen immunotherapy (sublingual or subcutaneous) has not been studied and may increase the risk of local or systemic reactions.
- Eosinophilic esophagitis has been reported in association with sublingual tablet immunotherapy; discontinue therapy and evaluate patients who develop severe or persistent gastroesophageal symptoms (e.g., dysphagia, chest pain).
- Stop treatment to allow healing in patients with oral inflammation or oral wounds (e.g., oral lichen planus, mouth ulcers, thrush, post‑dental extraction or oral surgery).
- GRASTEK may not be suitable for patients with medical conditions that reduce ability to survive a serious allergic reaction or increase risk from epinephrine administration (examples: markedly compromised lung function, unstable angina, recent myocardial infarction, significant arrhythmia, uncontrolled hypertension).
- GRASTEK may not be suitable for patients taking medications that can blunt or potentiate the effects of epinephrine (see Drug Interactions).

### Monitoring requirements
- Observe patient for at least 30 minutes after initial administration in a healthcare setting.
- Provide and ensure patient/caregiver training in recognition of severe allergic reaction signs and proper use of auto‑injectable epinephrine; instruct to discontinue timothy grass pollen allergen extract and seek immediate medical care if epinephrine is used.
- Reassess patients who develop escalating or persistent oropharyngeal reactions; consider discontinuation if airway compromise is a concern.

## 7. Adverse Effects
- Summary of clinical trial exposure: Adults — safety data from 6 randomized trials including 3,589 randomized adults; 1,669 subjects received timothy grass pollen allergen extract. Pediatrics — safety data from 3 randomized trials including 881 randomized subjects aged 5–17; 445 subjects received the extract.
- Discontinuation due to adverse reactions: Adults 4.9% (active) vs 0.9% (placebo); Pediatrics 6.3% (active) vs 0.7% (placebo).
- Treatment‑related systemic allergic reactions leading to discontinuation: Adults 7/1,669 (0.4%); Pediatrics 1/447 (0.2%).
- Eosinophilic esophagitis has been reported postmarketing; in at least one case symptoms resolved after discontinuation and recurred after re‑challenge.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Pharmacokinetic interactions: Not specified in available sources.
- Pharmacodynamic and treatment‑relevant interactions (clinical significance described in warnings):
  - Medications that may render epinephrine less effective:
    - Beta‑adrenergic blockers — may reduce responsiveness to epinephrine for treatment of systemic reactions (antagonize cardiostimulatory and bronchodilatory effects).
    - Alpha‑adrenergic blockers and ergot alkaloids — may antagonize the vasoconstricting/pressor effects of epinephrine.
  - Medications that may potentiate adverse effects of epinephrine:
    - Tricyclic antidepressants, levothyroxine sodium, monoamine oxidase inhibitors, and some antihistamines (chlorpheniramine, diphenhydramine) — may potentiate epinephrine adverse effects.
  - Cardiac glycosides and diuretics: patients receiving epinephrine while on these agents should be observed carefully for development of cardiac arrhythmias.
- Clinical management note: Prescribe auto‑injectable epinephrine and review concurrent medications that may affect epinephrine efficacy or safety; consider suitability of sublingual immunotherapy for patients taking these medications.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Risk summary (as stated in available sources): Available human data do not establish presence or absence of risk associated with timothy grass pollen allergen extract use in pregnancy.
- Animal data: In mice, reproductive and developmental toxicity studies with buccal administration at doses approximately 6.7 times the human sublingual dose revealed no evidence of harm to mother or fetus; fertility was normal, and there were no treatment‑related fetal malformations or variations in the described studies.
- Jurisdictional pregnancy category systems: Not specified in available sources for jurisdictions other than the reported animal/human data above.

### Breastfeeding
- It is not known whether timothy grass pollen allergen extract is excreted in human milk. Data are not available to assess effects on milk production or on a breastfed child. Consider the developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding together with maternal clinical need.

### Fertility
- Animal data from combined fertility/developmental toxicity study in mice indicated no adverse effect on fertility.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- timothy grass pollen allergen extract is an allergen extract administered as immunotherapy for grass pollen–induced allergic rhinitis/conjunctivitis. Its intended clinical effect is immunotherapy modulation of the allergic response to timothy grass (and cross‑reactive grass pollens). Detailed mechanistic descriptions beyond this immunotherapeutic indication are not specified in available sources.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Adult program: Six randomized clinical trials included a total of 3,589 adults (18–65 years) with timothy grass pollen–induced rhinitis/conjunctivitis; 1,669 subjects received active timothy grass pollen allergen extract. In these trials, approximately 25% had mild asthma and ~80% were polysensitized to other allergens. Common adverse reactions included oral pruritus, throat irritation, ear pruritus, and mouth edema. Seven adult recipients (7/1,669; 0.4%) experienced treatment‑related systemic allergic reactions; five of these occurred on Day 1.
- Pediatric program: Three randomized clinical trials enrolled 881 subjects aged 5–17; 445 received active treatment. In pediatric trials ~31% had mild asthma and ~86% were polysensitized. One pediatric participant (1/447; 0.2%) experienced a treatment‑related systemic allergic reaction on Day 1 and required epinephrine; the subject recovered.
- Efficacy timing and duration: Initiation of therapy is recommended at least 12 weeks before the grass pollen season. A treatment duration of daily dosing for three consecutive years may provide sustained effectiveness for one grass pollen season after stopping treatment.
- Safety of initiating therapy during pollen season and of restarting after prolonged interruptions: Not established (safety of starting in season not established; clinical trials allowed interruptions up to 7 days).
