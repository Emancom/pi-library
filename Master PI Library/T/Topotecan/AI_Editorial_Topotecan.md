# Editorial Product Information: Topotecan

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- At the time of compilation, only the Australian PI was provided for synthesis. Consequently, regional comparisons (e.g., with US or European sources) are not included unless otherwise noted.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview intended for healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single, structured reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for the most current and region-specific information.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Topotecan hydrochloride (a semi-synthetic derivative of camptothecin).
- **Chemical Class:** Topoisomerase-I inhibitor.
- **Brand Name(s):** Topotecan Accord [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01CE01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Concentrated injection for intravenous infusion; 1 mg/mL (available as 1 mg/1 mL and 4 mg/4 mL vials) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Accord Healthcare Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Topotecan is indicated for the treatment of the following malignancies:
- **Small Cell Lung Carcinoma (SCLC):** As single-agent therapy after failure of first-line chemotherapy [Australian].
- **Metastatic Ovarian Carcinoma:** As single-agent therapy after failure of first-line or subsequent therapy [Australian].
- **Cervical Carcinoma:** In combination with cisplatin for histologically confirmed Stage IV-B, recurrent, or persistent carcinoma of the cervix not amenable to curative surgery or radiation therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Baseline Requirements
Before initiating the first course, patients must have:
- Neutrophil count ≥ 1.5 x 10⁹/L.
- Platelet count ≥ 100 x 10⁹/L.
- Haemoglobin level ≥ 9 g/dL (after transfusion if necessary) [Australian].

### General Dosing (Adults and Elderly)
- **Ovarian and Small Cell Lung Carcinoma:** 1.5 mg/m² via IV infusion over 30 minutes daily for 5 consecutive days, repeated every 21 days. A minimum of 4 courses is recommended [Australian].
- **Cervical Cancer (Combination Therapy):** 0.75 mg/m² via IV infusion over 30 minutes on Days 1, 2, and 3. Cisplatin (50 mg/m²) is administered on Day 1 following the topotecan dose. The cycle is repeated every 21 days for 6 courses [Australian].

### Dose Adjustments for Toxicity
- **Neutropenia:** If severe neutropenia (count < 0.5 x 10⁹/L) persists for 7+ days, or is associated with fever/infection, or if treatment is delayed, reduce the dose by 0.25 mg/m²/day (e.g., from 1.5 to 1.25 mg/m²/day) for subsequent courses [Australian].
- **Thrombocytopenia:** Dose reduction is required if the platelet count falls below 25 x 10⁹/L [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Topotecan is a cytotoxic agent and must be handled according to hazardous drug protocols.
- **Dilution:** Must be diluted before use with either 0.9% Sodium Chloride or 5% Glucose [Australian].
- **Stability:** Diluted solutions are stable for up to 24 hours at 25°C; however, refrigeration (2–8°C) is recommended [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Severe history of hypersensitivity to topotecan or its excipients (tartaric acid, hydrochloric acid, sodium hydroxide) [Australian].
- **Pregnancy and Breastfeeding:** Use is contraindicated [Australian].
- **Bone Marrow Depression:** Severe pre-existing bone marrow depression prior to the first course (Neutrophils < 1.5 x 10⁹/L and/or Platelets < 100 x 10⁹/L) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Haematological Toxicity:** This is the dose-limiting toxicity. Myelosuppression (neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, and anaemia) is common and can be severe. Neutropenia can lead to sepsis and fatalities [Australian].
- **Neutropenic Colitis:** Fatal cases have been reported. Consider this diagnosis in patients presenting with fever, neutropenia, and abdominal pain [Australian].
- **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD):** Topotecan has been associated with ILD, which may be fatal. Monitor for symptoms (cough, dyspnoea, hypoxia) and discontinue if ILD is confirmed [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Full blood counts, including platelets, must be monitored regularly during treatment [Australian].
- **Administration Site:** Extravasation has occurred but reactions are generally mild [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Haematological (Small Cell Lung and Ovarian Carcinoma)
| Adverse Event | Incidence (% Patients) | Incidence (% Courses) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| Severe Neutropenia (< 0.5 x 10⁹/L) | 78% | 39% |
| Severe Leucopenia (< 1.0 x 10⁹/L) | 32% | 11% |
| Severe Thrombocytopenia (< 25 x 10⁹/L) | 27% | 9% |
| Moderate to Severe Anaemia (Hb ≤ 7.9 g/dL) | 37% | 15% |

### Non-Haematological
- **Very Common (>10%):** Nausea (56%), vomiting (36%), alopecia (31%), diarrhoea (21%), fatigue (24%), anorexia (which may be severe), and pyrexia [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Sepsis, hypersensitivity (including rash), stomatitis, constipation, abdominal pain, and malaise [Australian].
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Interstitial lung disease, extravasation [Australian].
- **Post-Marketing:** Gastrointestinal perforation, anaphylactic reactions, and mucosal inflammation [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Cytotoxics:** Co-administration with cisplatin increases the risk of myelosuppression. When combined, the topotecan dose is typically reduced (e.g., to 0.75 mg/m²) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** 
    - Topotecan is a substrate for **ABCG2 (BCRP)** and **ABCB1 (P-glycoprotein)**. Co-administration with elacridar (an inhibitor of these transporters) increased topotecan exposure by approximately 1.2-fold [Australian].
    - In vitro, topotecan does not inhibit major CYP450 enzymes (CYP1A2, 2A6, 2C8/9, 2C19, 2D6, 2E, 3A, or 4A) [Australian].
- **Other Medicines:** No significant effects on topotecan PK were observed with granisetron, ondansetron, morphine, or corticosteroids [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category D. Topotecan is contraindicated. It is genotoxic and can cause foetal harm [Australian].
- **Pregnancy Testing:** Verification of pregnancy status is required prior to initiation [Australian].
- **Contraception:** Females of reproductive potential must use effective contraception during treatment and for 6 months after. Males should use condoms and avoid fathering a child for 3 months post-treatment [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. It is unknown if topotecan is excreted in human milk, but serious adverse reactions in infants are possible [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended due to limited data [Australian].
- **Elderly:** No overall differences in effectiveness compared to younger adults, though diarrhoea may be more frequent (10% in patients >65 years) [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Monotherapy:** No adjustment for CrCl ≥ 0.66 mL/s. Reduce dose to 0.75 mg/m²/day if CrCl is between 0.33 and 0.65 mL/s. Insufficient data for CrCl < 0.33 mL/s [Australian].
    - **Combination Therapy:** Initiate only if serum creatinine ≤ 132.6 µmol/L [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment required if serum bilirubin is between 25.65 and 171 µmol/L [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Topotecan inhibits **Topoisomerase-I**, an enzyme that resolves DNA supercoils during replication. By stabilising the covalent complex between the enzyme and strand-cleaved DNA, topotecan causes protein-associated single-strand DNA breaks when the replication fork collides with the complex. This leads to S-phase dependent cytotoxicity [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** High volume of distribution (~132 L); low plasma protein binding (35%) [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Minor; pH-dependent hydrolysis of the active lactone ring to an inactive open-ring hydroxy acid [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Bi-exponential decline; terminal half-life of 2–3 hours. Excretion is both biliary and urinary (20–60% of dose) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Small Cell Lung Carcinoma:** In a comparative study against the CAV regimen (cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine), topotecan showed a response rate of 22% vs. 15% for CAV. Median survival was 25 weeks for topotecan vs. 22 weeks for CAV [Australian].
- **Ovarian Carcinoma:** In a study against paclitaxel, topotecan demonstrated a response rate of 21% vs. 14%. Median survival was 63 weeks vs. 53 weeks for paclitaxel [Australian].
- **Cervical Carcinoma:** The GOG 0179 trial compared topotecan + cisplatin to cisplatin alone. Combination therapy improved median overall survival (9.4 months vs. 6.5 months) and progression-free survival (4.6 months vs. 2.9 months) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Concentrated Injection:** Store below 25°C. Protect from light [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
