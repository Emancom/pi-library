# Editorial Product Information: Tazarotene

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a single clinical reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tazarotene (a member of the acetylenic class of retinoids).
- **Brand Names:** **Trilitas** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Topical Gel, 0.1% (1 mg tazarotene per gram) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Perfero Pharma, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Plaque Psoriasis:** Topical treatment of plaque psoriasis involving up to 20% of the body surface area [US].
- **Acne Vulgaris:** Topical treatment of mild to moderate facial acne vulgaris [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Administration:** Apply a thin film (approximately 2 mg/cm²) once daily in the evening to the affected area [US].
- **Psoriasis:** It is recommended to initiate treatment with tazarotene 0.05% gel (if available), increasing to 0.1% if tolerated and clinically indicated. Apply only to psoriatic lesions [US].
- **Acne:** Apply to the area where acne lesions appear after gentle cleansing and drying of the skin [US].

### Administration Instructions
- For topical use only. Not for ophthalmic, oral, or intravaginal use [US].
- Avoid application to unaffected skin or skin affected by eczema due to the risk of severe irritation [US].
- If emollients or moisturizers are used, they should be applied at least one hour prior to tazarotene application [US].
- Patients must wash hands thoroughly after application [US].

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy:** Tazarotene is a known teratogen and may cause fetal harm [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to tazarotene or any component of the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Embryofetal Toxicity:** [US Boxed Warning Equivalent] Tazarotene is teratogenic. Females of childbearing potential must have a negative pregnancy test (sensitivity of at least 25 mIU/mL) within 2 weeks prior to starting therapy. Treatment should begin during a normal menstrual period [US].
- **Local Irritation:** Excessive pruritus, burning, erythema, or peeling may occur. If severe, treatment should be paused until skin integrity is restored or the frequency of application reduced [US].
- **Photosensitivity:** Heightened susceptibility to sunburn. Patients should avoid sunlamps and minimize sunlight exposure. Daily use of broad-spectrum sunscreen and protective clothing is required. Use with caution in patients taking known photosensitizing drugs (e.g., tetracyclines, fluoroquinolones, thiazides) [US].
- **Weather Extremes:** Exposure to wind or cold may increase skin irritation during treatment [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (10–30% Frequency)
- **Psoriasis Patients:** Pruritus, burning/stinging, erythema, worsening of psoriasis, skin irritation, and skin pain [US].
- **Acne Patients:** Desquamation, burning/stinging, dry skin, erythema, and pruritus [US].

### Uncommon to Rare Adverse Effects (1–10% and Post-marketing)
- **Clinical Trials (1–10%):** Rash, irritant contact dermatitis, skin inflammation, fissuring, bleeding, localized edema, and skin discoloration [US].
- **Post-marketing Experience:** Blistering, dermatitis, urticaria, skin exfoliation, and skin hyper- or hypopigmentation [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Tazarotene (1.1 mg orally) did not affect the pharmacokinetics of combination oral contraceptives (norethindrone and ethinyl estradiol) [US].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** 
    - **Photosensitizers:** Potential for augmented photosensitivity when used with drugs such as thiazides, tetracyclines, fluoroquinolones, phenothiazines, and sulfonamides [US].
    - **Drying Agents:** Avoid concomitant use of topical medications or cosmetics with strong drying effects [US].
- **Management Note:** The impact of tazarotene on progestin-only "minipills" has not been evaluated [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Contraindicated. 
    - **Clinical Data:** Based on animal data, tazarotene causes fetal malformations (e.g., spina bifida, hydrocephaly) even at systemic exposures similar to or lower than the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if tazarotene is excreted in human milk. Radioactivity has been detected in the milk of lactating rats following topical application. Caution is advised, and the clinical need should be weighed against potential risks to the infant [US].
- **Contraception:** Females of reproductive potential must use effective contraception during treatment [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients under 12 years of age [US].
- **Elderly Use:** In psoriasis trials, patients >65 years experienced more adverse events and lower success rates compared to younger patients. Use in acne has not been evaluated for this age group [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Tazarotene is a retinoid prodrug converted to its active form, tazarotenic acid. It binds to all three members of the retinoic acid receptor (RAR) family (RARα, RARβ, RARγ) but shows relative selectivity for RARβ and RARγ [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Systemic absorption is <1% on normal skin, increasing to ~5% under occlusion or up to 14.8% on psoriatic skin [US].
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (>99%) [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Hydrolyzed by esterases to tazarotenic acid; further metabolized to sulfoxides and sulfones [US].
    - **Elimination:** Excreted via urinary and fecal pathways with a half-life of approximately 18 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Psoriasis:** Two vehicle-controlled trials demonstrated that tazarotene gel (0.05% and 0.1%) applied once daily for 12 weeks was significantly more effective than vehicle in reducing plaque elevation, scaling, and erythema. The 0.1% strength was more effective than 0.05%, though associated with more irritation [US].
- **Acne:** Two vehicle-controlled trials for mild to moderate facial acne showed significant reduction in lesion counts (inflammatory and non-inflammatory) after 12 weeks of once-daily treatment with the 0.1% gel [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Keep the tube tightly closed; do not use if the tamper-evident membrane is broken [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription only (Rx Only). Classified as a Human Prescription Drug [US].
