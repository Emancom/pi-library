# Editorial Product Information: Thiotepa

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically focused overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the local approved PI for definitive legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Thiotepa
- **Chemical Class:** Polyfunctional alkylating agent (nitrogen mustard derivative)
- **Brand Name(s):** **Tepadina** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** L01AC01
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Powder for injection: 15 mg and 100 mg vials (reconstituted to 10 mg/mL) [Australian]
    - Powder for injection and solvent for infusion: 400 mg dual-chamber bag (reconstituted to 1 mg/mL) [Australian]
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous infusion (via central venous catheter)
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
**Thiotepa** is indicated in combination with other chemotherapy agents:
- **Conditioning Treatment (Haematological):** Prior to allogeneic or autologous haematopoietic progenitor cell transplantation (HPCT) in adult and paediatric patients [Australian].
- **High-Dose Chemotherapy (Solid Tumours):** When HPCT support is appropriate for the treatment of solid tumours in adult and paediatric patients [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing Principles
Administration must be supervised by a physician experienced in HPCT conditioning. Prophylactic antiemetics should be considered.

### Adult Dosing (Autologous HPCT)
- **Haematological Diseases:** 125 mg/m²/day to 300 mg/m²/day as a single daily infusion for 2–4 consecutive days. Maximum cumulative dose: 900 mg/m² [Australian].
- **Lymphoma:** 125–300 mg/m²/day for 2–4 days. Max cumulative: 900 mg/m² [Australian].
- **CNS Lymphoma:** 185 mg/m²/day for 2 days. Max cumulative: 370 mg/m² [Australian].
- **Multiple Myeloma:** 150–250 mg/m²/day for 3 days. Max cumulative: 750 mg/m² [Australian].
- **Solid Tumours (Breast, CNS, Ovarian, Germ Cell):** Ranges vary from 120–250 mg/m²/day. Max cumulative doses range from 500–800 mg/m² [Australian].

### Adult Dosing (Allogeneic HPCT)
- **Haematological Diseases:** 185–481 mg/m²/day (divided into 1 or 2 infusions) for 1–3 days. Max cumulative: 555 mg/m² [Australian].
- **Thalassemia:** 370 mg/m²/day divided into two daily infusions. Max cumulative: 370 mg/m² [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing (Highlights)
- **Solid Tumours (Autologous):** 150–350 mg/m²/day for 2–3 days. Max cumulative: 1050 mg/m² [Australian].
- **Allogeneic HPCT:** Varies by disease; ranges from 125 mg/m² to 250 mg/m² per day [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Method:** Intravenous infusion over 2–4 hours via central venous catheter.
- **Filter:** Must use an infusion set equipped with a 0.2 µm in-line filter [Australian].
- **Flushing:** Flush the line with ~5 mL 0.9% sodium chloride before and after infusion.
- **Preparation Nuance:** Reconstituted solutions may show opalescence; these are still suitable for administration [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to **thiotepa**.
- Pregnancy [Australian].
- Breastfeeding [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Myelosuppression:** Profound and universal. Requires daily monitoring of WBC and platelets during therapy and for at least 30 days post-transplant. Growth factors (G-CSF) and blood product support are typically required.
- **Veno-occlusive Disease (VOD):** Increased risk in patients with prior radiation, >3 cycles of chemotherapy, or previous HPCT.
- **Skin Toxicity:** **Thiotepa** is excreted through the skin. To prevent irritation, patients must be advised to wash/shower four times a day starting with the first dose and continuing until 48 hours after the last dose [Australian].
- **Cardiac Toxicity:** Use with caution in those with pre-existing cardiac disease. Cases of cardiomyopathy and heart failure have occurred with high-dose regimens.
- **Pulmonary Toxicity:** May have additive effects with other cytotoxics (e.g., busulfan, cyclophosphamide).
- **Secondary Malignancy:** **Thiotepa** is a known human carcinogen; patients must be informed of the long-term risk of new malignancies.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Adult Population
- **Very Common (>10%):** Sepsis, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, anaemia, acute/chronic GvHD, anorexia, hyperglycemia, confusional state, headache, dizziness, blurred vision, arrhythmia, nausea/vomiting, stomatitis, diarrhoea, veno-occlusive liver disease, rash, alopecia, pyrexia, mucosal inflammation [Australian].
- **Common (1-10%):** Treatment-related second malignancy, hypopituitarism, anxiety, intracranial aneurysm, tachycardia, cardiac failure, gastrointestinal perforation/ileus, renal failure, cystitis [Australian].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Toxic shock syndrome, leukoencephalopathy (especially with prior methotrexate or radiotherapy), Stevens-Johnson syndrome, and toxic epidermal necrolysis [Australian].

### Paediatric Population
- **Unique/Noteworthy:** Growth retardation is listed as a very common adverse effect in children [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Cyclophosphamide:** Do NOT administer concurrently. **Thiotepa** must be delivered *after* the completion of cyclophosphamide infusion due to mutual pharmacokinetic interactions that reduce the metabolism of both agents [Australian].
- **CYP Interactions:** **Thiotepa** is metabolised by CYP2B6 and CYP3A4.
    - *Inhibitors* (e.g., azole antifungals, clarithromycin, clopidogrel): May increase **thiotepa** concentrations and decrease the active metabolite (TEPA).
    - *Inducers* (e.g., rifampicin, phenytoin): May increase TEPA concentrations.
- **Succinylcholine:** **Thiotepa** inhibits plasma pseudocholinesterase, potentially prolonging neuromuscular blockade by 5–15 minutes [Australian].
- **Vaccines:** Live virus/bacterial vaccines are contraindicated. At least 3 months should elapse between chemotherapy and vaccination [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Thiotepa is teratogenic and contraindicated during pregnancy. Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception and have a negative pregnancy test before starting treatment.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated [Australian]. It is unknown if it is excreted in human milk, but potential toxicity to the infant is significant.
- **Fertility:** May impair both male and female fertility. Male patients should seek sperm cryopreservation before treatment and should not father a child for one year after treatment [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for HPCT conditioning in haematological and solid tumours. Note risk of growth retardation.
- **Elderly Use:** No specific dose adjustments deemed necessary for patients >65 years based on available clinical data [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specifically studied. However, **thiotepa** is poorly excreted in urine; no modification is recommended for mild/moderate impairment, but caution is advised [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specifically studied. Caution is required, especially in severe impairment. Monitor transaminases and bilirubin regularly [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Polyfunctional cytotoxic agent. Releases ethylene imine radicals which disrupt DNA bonds by alkylating guanine at the N-7 position, leading to cross-linking and inhibition of DNA synthesis [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Highly lipophilic. Crosses the blood-brain barrier (CSF/plasma ratio ~0.93).
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly and extensively metabolised in the liver via CYP2B6 and CYP3A4 to its major active metabolite, triethylenephosphoramide (TEPA).
    - **Elimination:** Half-life is 1.5–4.1 hours. Primarily excreted as metabolites in the urine [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Efficacy is supported by multiple published studies using **thiotepa** as part of myeloablative conditioning.
- **Engraftment:** Achieved in 92% to 100% of allogeneic patients [Australian].
- **Survival:** In adult autologous haematological disease, 5-year disease-free survival (DFS) is estimated at 43% [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials/Unopened Bag:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze) [Australian].
- **Bag Protection:** Keep the 400 mg bag in its aluminium wrapper to prevent accidental activation.
- **Stability after Reconstitution:**
    - Vials (15mg/100mg): 8 hours at 2°C to 8°C.
    - Diluted solution: 24 hours at 2°C to 8°C and 4 hours at 25°C.
    - 400 mg Bag (activated): 4 hours at 25°C [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
