# Editorial Product Information: Tesamorelin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States** (Food and Drug Administration).
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonized editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** As only the United States PI was provided for this synthesis, clinical recommendations and regulatory classifications reflect that jurisdiction. This document is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tesamorelin (as an acetate salt).
- **Chemical Class:** Growth hormone-releasing factor (GHRF) analog.
- **Brand Name:** **EGRIFTA SV** [US].
- **ATC Code:** H01AC06.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Lyophilized powder for injection: 2 mg single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Theratechnologies Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **HIV-Associated Lipodystrophy:** Indicated for the reduction of excess abdominal fat in HIV-infected adult patients with lipodystrophy [US].

**Limitations of Use:**
- Long-term cardiovascular safety has not been established.
- Not indicated for weight loss management (weight-neutral effect).
- No evidence exists to support improved compliance with anti-retroviral therapies.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dose:** 1.4 mg (0.35 mL of the reconstituted solution) injected subcutaneously once daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Inject into the abdominal wall.
    - Rotate injection sites to different areas of the abdomen; avoid scar tissue, bruises, or the navel.
- **Reconstitution Procedure:** 
    - Reconstitute one 2 mg vial with 0.5 mL of the provided Sterile Water for Injection.
    - Roll the vial gently for 30 seconds; do not shake.
    - Use only if the solution is clear, colorless, and free of particulate matter.
    - Administer immediately after reconstitution.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Disruption of the hypothalamic-pituitary axis (e.g., due to hypopituitarism, pituitary tumors/surgery, head irradiation, or head trauma) [US].
- Active malignancy (any pre-existing malignancy must be inactive and treatment completed) [US].
- Known hypersensitivity to **tesamorelin** or any excipients (e.g., mannitol, histidine, polysorbate 20) [US].
- Pregnancy [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Increased Risk of Neoplasms:** Tesamorelin induces endogenous growth hormone (GH) release. Pre-existing malignancies should be confirmed inactive before initiation. Discontinue therapy if there is evidence of recurrent or new malignancy [US].
- **Elevated IGF-1 Levels:** Tesamorelin increases serum IGF-1. Monitor IGF-1 levels regularly. Consider discontinuation in patients with persistent elevations (e.g., >3 SDS), especially if the clinical response is poor [US].
- **Fluid Retention:** May manifest as peripheral edema, arthralgia, and carpal tunnel syndrome. These effects are typically transient or resolve upon treatment cessation [US].
- **Glucose Intolerance and Diabetes:** May develop or worsen. Evaluate glucose status before starting therapy and monitor periodically. Consider discontinuation in patients without a clear efficacy response who develop significant glucose intolerance [US].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Reported in 4% of clinical trial patients (e.g., pruritus, erythema, urticaria, rash). Discontinue immediately if a reaction is suspected [US].
- **Increased Mortality in Acute Critical Illness:** Based on reports involving pharmacologic amounts of GH, consider discontinuing tesamorelin in critically ill patients (e.g., following major surgery or respiratory failure) [US].

| **Arthralgia** | 13% | 11% |
| **Pain in extremity** | 6% | 5% |

**Serious Adverse Effects:**
- New or recurrent malignancies.
- Clinically significant hypersensitivity.
- Development of diabetes mellitus.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Cytochrome P450 (CYP450) Substrates:** GH may modulate CYP450-mediated clearance. Monitor patients closely when using tesamorelin with drugs metabolized by the liver (e.g., corticosteroids, sex steroids, anticonvulsants, cyclosporine) [US].
- **Glucocorticoids:** GH inhibits the 11β-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase type 1 (11βHSD-1) enzyme. Patients receiving glucocorticoid replacement for hypoadrenalism may require increased doses [US].
- **Specific Interaction Studies:** Co-administration with simvastatin or ritonavir resulted in minor, non-clinically significant changes in systemic exposure [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Contraindicated]. Modifying visceral adipose tissue provides no benefit during pregnancy and may cause fetal harm. Animal data show hydrocephaly and delayed skull ossification at doses near clinical exposure [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Mothers with HIV-1 should not breastfeed to avoid postnatal transmission of the virus. It is unknown if tesamorelin is excreted in human milk [US].
- **Fertility:** No effect on fertility was observed in rat studies at clinical exposure levels [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established. Use in children with open epiphyses may result in excessive linear growth [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No information is available for patients >65 years of age [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Pharmacokinetics have not been established in these populations [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Tesamorelin is a synthetic analog of human GHRF (44 amino acid sequence). It binds to pituitary GHRF receptors to stimulate the pulsatile release of endogenous growth hormone, which exerts lipolytic effects [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Increases GH, IGF-1, and IGFBP-3. It does not appear to significantly affect other pituitary hormones (TSH, LH, ACTH, or prolactin) [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is <4% after subcutaneous administration. Median $T_{max}$ is 0.15 hours [US].
    - **Distribution:** Mean volume of distribution is 4.8 ± 1.9 L/kg [US].
    - **Metabolism:** No formal studies in humans [US].
    - **Elimination:** Mean half-life ($t_{1/2}$) is approximately 8 minutes [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** Two 26-week, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in HIV-infected patients with abdominal lipohypertrophy.
- **Primary Outcome:** Both studies showed a significant reduction in visceral adipose tissue (VAT) as measured by CT scan (Study 1: -18% vs +2%; Study 2: -14% vs -2%) [US].
- **Extension Phase:** Patients continuing treatment for 52 weeks maintained VAT reduction; however, those switched to placebo experienced a regain of abdominal fat [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store at room temperature between 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Keep vials in the original carton to protect from light.
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Do not freeze or refrigerate. Discard any unused portion immediately [US].
