# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- At the time of compilation, PI documents from other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian, Japanese) were not provided.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or prescribing information required in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/amino acid derivative ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for intravenous injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **Carnitine Deficiency in End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion. 
- **Acute/Crisis Management:** A loading dose is often administered in patients with severe metabolic crisis, followed by equivalent doses over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are recommended in the range of 50 mg/kg or as clinically required. The maximum recorded dose is 300 mg/kg.
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels prior to therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine level: 35 to 60 µmol/L).

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels below 40 to 50 µmol/L. Adjustments should be guided by trough concentrations; downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect parenteral products for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately.
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with and without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity of seizures has been observed.
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulation Caution):** While this document concerns the injection, clinicians should note that the safety/efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO).

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions observed include transient nausea and vomiting, and less frequently, body odor and gastritis. Because of underlying pathologies in metabolic and ESRD patients, causality is often difficult to determine.

### Adverse Events in ESRD Patients (Frequency ≥5%)
| Body System | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain. |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, arrhythmia, palpitations. |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, anorexia, constipation, dyspepsia. |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia, depression, anxiety. |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis. |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight changes. |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity.
- Increased seizure frequency/severity.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin.
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine is not bound to plasma proteins, reducing the likelihood of protein-binding displacement interactions.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US]: Not formally assigned a letter category in the current PI, but reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. In dairy cows, exogenous carnitine increases milk concentrations. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against maternal benefits.
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders are applicable to the paediatric population.
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources; however, dose selection should generally be cautious, reflecting the greater frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specific dosing is provided for ESRD patients on hemodialysis (see Section 3). The drug is easily removed by dialysis.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, administration results in increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** Efficacy was demonstrated in a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients where 20 mg/kg post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis trough levels from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L. 
- **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg three times weekly showed dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing.
- **Stability:** Once mixed in Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL), the solution is stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags.
- **Handling:** Single-dose vial; discard unused portion.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only.
