# Editorial Product Information: Travoprost and Timolol Maleate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational use by healthcare professionals and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Travoprost (a prostaglandin F2α analogue) and Timolol maleate (a non-selective beta-adrenergic receptor blocker).
- **Brand Name:** DuoTrav [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** S01ED51.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile, isotonic ophthalmic solution containing travoprost 40 micrograms/mL (0.004%) and timolol 5 mg/mL (as timolol maleate 6.83 mg/mL). 
- **Excipients:** The formulation is benzalkonium chloride (BAK) free and utilizes polyquaternium-1 (POLYQUAD) as a preservative [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Topical ophthalmic.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Novartis Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Reduction of elevated intraocular pressure (IOP):** Indicated for patients with open-angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension who have achieved insufficient IOP reduction with single-agent therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing (including elderly):** Instil one drop into the conjunctival sac of the affected eye(s) once daily.
- **Timing:** Administration should occur at approximately the same time each day.
- **Optimization:** Dosing should not exceed once daily, as more frequent administration of travoprost may reduce the IOP-lowering effect.
- **Concomitant Topical Therapy:** If multiple topical ophthalmic products are used, they must be administered at least five minutes apart.
- **Administration Technique:** To minimize systemic absorption, patients should be instructed to gently occlude the nasolacrimal ducts for two minutes immediately following instillation.
- **Substitution:** When switching from another antiglaucoma agent, the previous agent should be discontinued and **DuoTrav** initiated the following day.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to travoprost, timolol, or any excipients.
- **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated in women who are pregnant or attempting to become pregnant [Australian].
- **Respiratory Disease:** Reactive airway disease, including bronchial asthma (or a history thereof) and severe chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD).
- **Cardiac Disease:** Sinus bradycardia, sick sinus syndrome (including sino-atrial block), second- or third-degree atrioventricular block, overt cardiac failure, or cardiogenic shock.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Systemic Absorption:** Like other topical ophthalmic agents, systemic absorption occurs. The timolol component may induce adverse reactions typical of systemic beta-blockers, including bradycardia, hypotension, and aggravation of Prinzmetal angina or peripheral circulatory disorders (e.g., Raynaud's phenomenon).
- **Respiratory/Cardiac Monitoring:** Therapy should be critically assessed in patients with cardiovascular disease. Respiratory and cardiac reactions, including rare fatalities due to bronchospasm or cardiac failure, have been reported following timolol use.
- **Hypoglycemia/Diabetes:** Beta-blockers should be used with caution in patients with spontaneous hypoglycemia or labile diabetes, as they may mask signs of acute hypoglycemia.
- **Hyperthyroidism/Myasthenia Gravis:** Beta-blockade may mask symptoms of hyperthyroidism or aggravate myasthenia gravis.
- **Ocular Effects:** 
    - **Iris Pigmentation:** Travoprost may gradually and permanently change eye color by increasing melanosomes in melanocytes. This is most common in mixed-colored irides.
    - **Periorbital Changes:** Eyelid skin darkening and deepening of the eyelid sulcus have been reported.
    - **Eyelash Changes:** Increased length, thickness, and number of lashes occur in over one-third of patients.
    - **Macular Oedema:** Caution is advised in aphakic/pseudophakic patients or those with known risk factors for macular oedema.
- **Surgical Anesthesia:** The anesthesiologist must be informed if the patient is using timolol, as beta-blockers may inhibit systemic beta-agonist effects (e.g., adrenaline).
- **Contact Lenses:** Lenses must be removed before instillation and may be reinserted after 15 minutes.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions observed in clinical trials for the POLYQUAD-preserved formulation [Australian]:

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Ocular hyperaemia (11.8%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Eye pain, ocular discomfort, dry eye, eye pruritus |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Hypersensitivity, headache, punctate keratitis, iritis, photophobia, blurred vision, conjunctivitis, eyelid margin crusting, growth of eyelashes, bradycardia, hypotension, skin discoloration, fatigue, decreased heart rate |

**Post-Marketing Experience (Frequency not estimated):**
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness, cerebrovascular accident, syncope, paraesthesia.
- **Psychiatric:** Hallucination, depression, nervousness.
- **Ocular:** Macular oedema, corneal disorder, eyelid ptosis, lid sulcus deepened, trichiasis.
- **Systemic:** Chest pain, palpitations, hypertension, bronchospasm, dyspnoea, asthma, alopecia, rash, dysgeusia.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic:** No specific interactions observed between travoprost and timolol. Travoprost is unlikely to involve protein-binding interactions (80% binding).
- **Pharmacodynamic (Timolol):** Potential for additive effects (hypotension/bradycardia) when used with:
    - Oral calcium channel blockers
    - Beta-adrenergic blocking agents
    - Antiarrhythmics (e.g., amiodarone, quinidine)
    - Digitalis glycosides or parasympathomimetics
    - CYP2D6 inhibitors (e.g., fluoxetine, paroxetine), which may potentiate systemic beta-blockade.
- **Adrenaline:** Mydriasis has been reported occasionally when timolol is used concomitantly with adrenaline.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy Category:** **Category C** [Australian].
- **Pregnancy Recommendation:** DuoTrav is contraindicated in women who are pregnant or intending to become pregnant. It may interfere with the maintenance of pregnancy.
- **Breastfeeding:** Use with caution. While there are no human data for travoprost, animal studies show excretion in milk. Timolol has been detected in human milk. Nursing women should be advised to stop breastfeeding [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No human data available. Animal studies for travoprost showed increased embryo-foetal resorption at high doses [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended; safety and efficacy have not been established in children.
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness compared to younger adults.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is necessary [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Travoprost:** A prostaglandin FP receptor agonist (prodrug) that increases aqueous humour outflow via trabecular meshwork and uveoscleral pathways.
    - **Timolol:** A non-selective beta-blocker that reduces IOP by decreasing aqueous humour formation in the ciliary epithelium.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** IOP reduction begins within 30 minutes, peaks at 12 hours, and is maintained for at least 24 hours.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Both actives are absorbed through the cornea. Peak plasma timolol occurs at 0.69 hours. Travoprost free acid is usually below quantifiable limits (0.01 ng/mL) in plasma.
    - **Metabolism:** Travoprost is hydrolyzed in the cornea to the active acid. Timolol is extensively metabolized in the liver.
    - **Elimination:** Travoprost acid plasma half-life is approximately 45 minutes; timolol is approximately 4 hours. Both are primarily excreted by the kidneys.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** In multicenter trials (n=982), DuoTrav dosed once daily in the morning demonstrated mean IOP reductions of 8.7 to 11.5 mmHg. 
- **Comparison:** DuoTrav provided significantly greater IOP reduction than monotherapy with either travoprost 0.004% or timolol 0.5%. Efficacy was found to be independent of morning vs. evening dosing.
- **Equivalence:** The POLYQUAD-preserved (BAK-free) formulation was shown to be clinically equivalent to the BAK-preserved formulation in a 6-week study of 388 patients [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Shelf Life:** Discard container four weeks after first opening.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
