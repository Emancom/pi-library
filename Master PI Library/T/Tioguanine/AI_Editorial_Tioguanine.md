# Editorial Product Information: Tioguanine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data into a concise, professional reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. At the time of writing, only the Australian PI was available for review; additional regional variations (e.g., US, European) are not included but should be consulted where applicable.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tioguanine (an analogue of the nucleic acid constituent guanine; 2-amino-1,7-dihydro-6H-purine-6-thione).
- **Chemical Class:** Purine antagonist (antimetabolite).
- **Brand Name(s):** **LANVIS** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01BB03.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 40 mg scored, uncoated tablets [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aspen Pharmacare Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Myeloblastic Leukaemia (AML):** Primary indication.
- **Chronic Granulocytic (Myeloid) Leukaemia:** Less common use; while busulfan is generally superior, tioguanine may be utilized during blast crises or periods of busulfan-induced thrombocytopenia [Australian].
- **Note on Limitations:** Tioguanine is not effective for the treatment of solid tumours or chronic lymphocytic leukaemia [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Children):** The usual starting dose is 2 to 2.5 mg/kg body weight per day. Doses are typically rounded to the nearest 20 mg (half-tablet) [Australian].
- **Administration:** The total daily dose may be administered at once, though some protocols require 12-hourly dosing.
- **Therapeutic Strategy:** Therapy must be initiated in specialised units with facilities for supportive care and is usually administered as part of a multi-drug protocol [Australian].
- **Maintenance Therapy Warning:** Tioguanine is **not recommended** for long-term continuous maintenance therapy due to a high risk of hepatotoxicity [Australian].
- **Pharmacogenomic Dose Adjustments:**
    - **TPMT Deficiency:** Patients with inherited low or absent thiopurine S-methyltransferase (TPMT) activity are at high risk for life-threatening myelotoxicity. Homozygous deficient patients require substantial dose reductions; an optimal starting dose for this group has not been established [Australian].
    - **NUDT15 Variant:** Patients with mutated NUDT15 genes (more common in Asian populations) are at increased risk of severe, early leukopenia and alopecia. Substantial dose reductions are generally required [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to tioguanine or any excipient [Australian].
- **Relative Contraindications (Prior to Correction):** Viraemic states, marrow aplasia, severe anaemia, leucopenia, bleeding states, severe liver dysfunction, and renal failure. Therapy may proceed if these conditions are corrected [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in the Australian source; however, it is emphasized that tioguanine is an active cytotoxic agent to be used only under the direction of experienced physicians.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** High risk of liver toxicity associated with vascular endothelial damage, particularly during long-term maintenance therapy. Manifestations include hepatic veno-occlusive disease (VOD) or portal hypertension (splenomegaly, thrombocytopenia, oesophageal varices). This is notably prevalent in male patients and children receiving maintenance for ALL [Australian].
- **Myelosuppression:** Strong suppression of bone marrow is expected. Treatment should be temporarily discontinued at the first sign of an abnormal fall in white cell or platelet counts [Australian].
- **Monitoring Requirements:**
    - Frequent full blood counts (daily during induction, weekly during maintenance).
    - Weekly liver function tests (LFTs); more frequent monitoring in patients with pre-existing liver disease.
    - Genotypic/phenotypic testing for TPMT and NUDT15 status before initiation [Australian].
- **Immunisation:** Live organism vaccines are not recommended in immunocompromised patients. Remission patients should wait at least 3 months post-chemotherapy before receiving live vaccines [Australian].
- **Photosensitivity:** Increased sensitivity to UV light; patients should use high-factor sunscreen and protective clothing [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (≥10%) | Bone marrow failure; Liver toxicity (VOD) when used in maintenance therapy. |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Stomatitis; Nausea, vomiting, and diarrhoea; Hyperuricaemia/urate nephropathy; Liver toxicity (VOD) during short-term cyclical therapy. |
| **Rare** (<0.1%) | Necrotising colitis and perforation; Hepatic necrosis (reported with high doses, alcohol, or oral contraceptives). |
| **Not Known** | Photosensitivity; Intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (ICP). |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Portal hypertension, oesophageal varices, agranulocytosis, and intestinal perforation [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Busulfan:** Concurrent use may increase the risk of nodular regenerative hyperplasia, portal hypertension, and oesophageal varices [Australian].
- **Aminosalicylates (e.g., Olsalazine, Mesalazine):** These may inhibit the TPMT enzyme, increasing the risk of tioguanine-induced myelosuppression. Use with caution [Australian].
- **Allopurinol:** Unlike mercaptopurine, the detoxification of tioguanine is not dependent on xanthine oxidase; therefore, allopurinol does **not** necessitate a dose reduction of tioguanine [Australian].
- **Myelotoxic Agents/Radiation:** Increased risk of severe bone marrow suppression.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category D.** Tioguanine is a potent teratogen. It has been associated with fetal chromosomal abnormalities and malformations. Use should be deferred until after the first trimester whenever possible. Benefit-risk analysis is mandatory [Australian].
- **Contraception:** Both male and female patients should use adequate contraceptive measures during treatment.
- **Breastfeeding:** Mothers receiving tioguanine should not breastfeed, as there is no data regarding the presence of the drug in human milk [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Potentially mutagenic and carcinogenic; specific human fertility data is not available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No specific data provided in available sources, though maintenance use in children is linked to high rates of hepatotoxicity [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Smaller doses may be advisable due to primarily renal excretion of metabolites [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Smaller doses are recommended as the drug is primarily metabolised in the liver. Close LFT monitoring is essential [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A purine antagonist that acts as an antimetabolite. Its effects include incorporation into DNA and RNA, feedback inhibition of de novo purine synthesis, and inhibition of purine nucleotide interconversion [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Tumour inhibition is achieved through the disruption of nucleic acid synthesis. Resistance may occur in patients deficient in the enzyme hypoxanthine guanine phosphoribosyl transferase (e.g., Lesch-Nyhan syndrome) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Not specified in detail in available sources. Metabolites are excreted renally [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No clinical trial data was provided in the source documentation.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C.
- **Conditions:** Protect from light and store in a dry place [Australian].
- **Container:** Amber glass bottle with HDPE/PP child-resistant cap [Australian].
