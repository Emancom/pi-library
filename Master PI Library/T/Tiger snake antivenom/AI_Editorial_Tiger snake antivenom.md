# Editorial Product Information: Tiger Snake Antivenom

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis incorporates information from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. No other international product information documents were provided for this synthesis.
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active ingredient**: Tiger snake antivenom (equine-derived).
- **Chemical class**: Purified equine immunoglobulins (plasma from horses immunised with *Notechis scutatus* venom).
- **Brand name(s)**: Tiger Snake Antivenom (Seqirus) [Australian].
- **ATC code**: J06AA03 (Antivenoms).
- **Dosage form(s) and strength(s)**: Concentrated injection for intravenous use. Each vial contains 3,000 units of antivenom (standardised to neutralise the average yield of venom from one tiger snake).
- **Route(s) of administration**: Intravenous (IV) infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/sponsor**: Seqirus Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- For the management of patients demonstrating clinical or laboratory evidence of systemic envenoming following a bite by a **tiger snake**, **copperhead**, or **black snake** [Australian-approved].
- *Note*: While effective against certain black snakes (genus *Pseudechis*), **Black Snake Antivenom** is specifically registered for king brown or mulga snake bites. Specialist advice is recommended for other members of the genus *Pseudechis* [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing**: The initial dose is a minimum of one vial (3,000 units). 
- **Dose Adjustment**: Patients with severe systemic envenoming, particularly those with established coagulopathy, may require several vials. The dose should be increased if the patient's condition deteriorates or if unusually large amounts of venom were injected.
- **Paediatric Dosing**: The dose for children is identical to the adult dose, as it is based on the quantity of venom to be neutralised rather than patient body weight.
- **Administration Instructions**:
    - Must be administered via slow intravenous infusion.
    - Dilute 1 in 10 (e.g., one vial in 0.9% sodium chloride or Hartmann’s Solution).
    - A 1 in 5 dilution may be used if the patient is at risk of fluid overload.
    - Do not administer via the intramuscular route.
    - Once diluted, the solution must be used immediately; do not store diluted product.
- **First Aid Interaction**: If a pressure immobilisation bandage and splint are in place, they must NOT be removed until antivenom is ready for infusion, as removal can trigger a sudden surge of systemic envenoming.

## 4. Contraindications
- There are no absolute contraindications to the use of tiger snake antivenom when clear evidence of systemic envenoming with potential for serious toxicity is present [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings**: None specified in available sources.
- **Anaphylaxis Risk**: As a horse-serum-derived product, there is a significant risk of severe allergic reactions, including fatal anaphylactic shock. Adrenaline must be prepared and ready for use prior to administration.
- **Pre-existing Allergies**: Patients with known allergies to horses or horse dander are at increased risk. Atopic individuals (allergies to sheep, dogs, or cats) may exhibit cross-reactivity. Use in patients who previously received equine tetanus antitoxin (prior to 1974 in Australia) requires careful risk-benefit evaluation.
- **Infectious Disease Transmission**: Because this product is derived from animal plasma, the risk of transmitting infectious agents cannot be totally excluded, although no cases of viral transmission have been recorded for this product.
- **Monitoring**: Patients must be monitored for at least six hours post-administration. Suspected snake bite cases should be observed for 12 hours post-bite or post-bandage removal.
- **Serum Sickness**: Delayed serum sickness (fever, rash, arthralgia) is common, occurring in at least 5% of first-time recipients and more frequently with high doses.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic interactions**: Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: Data are limited and inconclusive. No specific pregnancy category (e.g., US or Australian letter system) is assigned in the source. Recommendation: Weigh the risk of untreated envenoming against the potential risks of antivenom [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding**: No data available regarding use during lactation. Weigh the risks of untreated envenoming against potential risks [Australian].
- **Fertility**: No data available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric use**: Dose is the same as for adults; children may become critically ill more rapidly and may require more antivenom [Australian].
- **Elderly use**: No specific data available [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic impairment**: No specific dose adjustments provided; however, tiger snake venom contains myolytic toxins that can cause renal failure, necessitating monitoring of renal function [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: The antivenom contains specific antibodies that neutralise the toxins in tiger snake venom, including:
    - **Neurotoxins**: Which cause respiratory paralysis.
    - **Procoagulants**: Which convert prothrombin to thrombin, leading to afibrinogenaemia and haemorrhage.
    - **Myolytic toxins**: Which can cause muscle breakdown and subsequent renal failure.
- **Pharmacodynamics**: Neutralisation is measured *in vitro* to cover the average venom yield of *Notechis scutatus*.
- **Pharmacokinetics**: Not specified in available sources.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No clinical trial data are provided in the available source documents [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage**: Store between 2°C and 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze).
- **Protection**: Protect from light.
- **Shelf Life**: Refer to the expiry date on the packaging.
- **Latex Safety**: The vial and associated components do not contain natural rubber latex.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction

- **Legal Classification**: Available only by prescription or for emergency hospital use.
