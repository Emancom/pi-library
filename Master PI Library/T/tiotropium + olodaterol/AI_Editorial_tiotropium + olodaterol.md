# Editorial Product Information: tiotropium + olodaterol

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **tiotropium + olodaterol**
- Chemical class:
  - Tiotropium: long-acting muscarinic antagonist (anticholinergic; LAMA)
  - Olodaterol: long-acting β2-adrenergic agonist (LABA)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: inhalation (oral inhalation)

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source product information: Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Once‑daily maintenance bronchodilator treatment to relieve symptoms in adult patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD).
- Not indicated for treatment of asthma; efficacy and safety in asthma have not been established and LABA components carry asthma-related risk (see Warnings).

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Recommended adult dose: two inhalations once daily delivering a total of 5 micrograms tiotropium + 5 micrograms olodaterol (delivered dose: 2.5 µg + 2.5 µg per inhalation; two inhalations = one dose).
- Administer once daily at the same time each day.

### Dosing by indication
- COPD maintenance therapy: as above.
- There are no approved alternative indications listed in the available sources.

### Administration instructions
- For inhalation use only (oral inhalation).
- Patients should receive instruction and demonstration on correct inhaler technique from a physician or other qualified health professional prior to use.
- Care should be taken to avoid allowing solution/mist to enter the eyes.
- Contains benzalkonium chloride (0.0011 mg per actuation) — may cause bronchospasm in predisposed patients (see Warnings).

### Titration schedules
- Not specified in available sources.

### Missed dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to tiotropium or olodaterol.
- Hypersensitivity to any excipients contained in the product (contains benzalkonium chloride).
- History of hypersensitivity to atropine or its derivatives (e.g., ipratropium, oxitropium).
- Any other contraindications: Not specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- None specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Do not administer more frequently than once daily.
- Not for acute bronchospasm or rescue therapy.
- Should not be initiated in patients with acutely deteriorating COPD; seek medical review and re‑evaluation rather than increasing dose.
- Avoid concomitant use with other medicines containing LABAs or LAMAs.
- Contains benzalkonium chloride (0.0011 mg/actuation) which may cause wheezing and breathing difficulties in some patients, particularly those with asthma.

### Class effects and specific precautions
- **LABA class risk (important):** Long-acting β2-agonists (including olodaterol) have been associated with increased risk of asthma-related hospitalisations and death when used in asthma. Do not use for asthma treatment; consider this class risk when treating patients with possible or concomitant asthma.
- Hypersensitivity reactions including immediate allergic reactions and angioedema may occur; discontinue if these occur.
- Paradoxical bronchospasm may occur and may be life‑threatening; discontinue immediately if this occurs.

### Cardiovascular and systemic effects
- Use with caution in patients with cardiovascular disorders (coronary insufficiency, cardiac arrhythmias, hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy, hypertension), convulsive disorders, thyrotoxicosis, known or suspected QT prolongation, or in those unusually responsive to sympathomimetic amines.
- Olodaterol may cause increases in pulse rate and blood pressure and may produce ECG changes (T‑wave flattening, ST depression) of uncertain clinical significance.
- Patients with recent myocardial infarction (within 1 year), unstable or life‑threatening arrhythmias, hospitalization for heart failure within the previous year, or paroxysmal tachycardia (>100 bpm) were excluded from clinical trials; use with caution in these populations.

### Electrolyte and metabolic effects
- β2-agonists may produce hypokalaemia which can potentiate cardiac arrhythmias; this effect may be potentiated in severe COPD by hypoxia and concomitant therapies.
- Inhalation of high doses of β2-agonists may increase plasma glucose.

### Ophthalmic and urologic precautions
- Use with caution in patients with narrow-angle glaucoma, prostatic hyperplasia, or bladder-neck obstruction because of anticholinergic effects.
- Instruct patients to avoid getting mist in the eyes; symptoms such as eye pain, blurred vision, visual halos, red eyes may indicate acute narrow-angle glaucoma — seek specialist advice urgently. Miotic eye drops are generally not effective.
- Anticholinergic agents may cause urinary retention or micturition difficulties; monitor at‑risk patients.

### Hepatic and renal impairment
- Olodaterol is predominantly metabolised in the liver. Patients with mild or moderate hepatic impairment can use recommended dosing; no data available in severe hepatic impairment — these patients should be closely monitored.
- Tiotropium is predominantly renally excreted. Use should be monitored closely in patients with moderate to severe renal impairment (creatinine clearance ≤ 50 mL/min).

### Use in elderly, children
- Elderly: can use the recommended dose; exercise clinical caution as above.
- Paediatric: COPD does not normally occur in children; safety and effectiveness in paediatric patients have not been established.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor for cardiovascular signs/symptoms, ocular symptoms, urinary retention symptoms.
- Monitor electrolytes (potassium) if clinically indicated, particularly when patients are on concomitant therapies known to lower potassium.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Olodaterol:
  - In vitro studies indicate CYP450-mediated interactions are not expected.
  - Inhibitors of P‑glycoprotein (P‑gp), OAT1, OAT3 or OCT1 may alter systemic exposure or disposition of olodaterol.
  - Olodaterol was not an inhibitor of these transporters at clinically relevant concentrations.
  - Co‑administration with the strong dual CYP and P‑gp inhibitor ketoconazole increased systemic exposure ~1.7‑fold; no safety concerns were identified in clinical studies up to one year at doses up to twice the recommended dose; no dose adjustment specified.
- Tiotropium:
  - No formal drug–drug interaction studies reported; tiotropium has been used concomitantly with methylxanthines and inhaled/oral corticosteroids without evidence of clinically relevant interactions.
  - Chronic co‑administration with other anticholinergic medicines has not been studied and is not recommended.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Concomitant administration of other adrenergic agents may potentiate undesirable effects (e.g., cardiovascular).
- Xanthine derivatives, systemic corticosteroids, or non‑potassium‑sparing diuretics may potentiate hypokalaemic effects of β2‑agonists — monitor serum potassium.
- β‑adrenergic blockers may weaken or antagonise the effect of olodaterol; cardioselective β‑blockers could be considered but used with caution.
- Monoamine oxidase inhibitors, tricyclic antidepressants, or other drugs known to prolong QT interval may potentiate cardiovascular effects of olodaterol.
- Concurrent use with other LABA or LAMA agents is not recommended.

### Clinically significant interactions and management notes
- Monitor for enhanced cardiovascular effects when combining with other sympathomimetics or QT‑prolonging agents.
- Monitor serum potassium when used with agents that lower potassium.
- Avoid concomitant use with other inhaled long‑acting bronchodilators of the same classes.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Australian pregnancy category: Category B3.
- Limited clinical data for tiotropium in pregnant women; no clinical data available for olodaterol exposure in pregnancy.
- Nonclinical reproductive toxicity:
  - Tiotropium: no evidence of fetal malformations in rats and rabbits at high inhalational doses.
  - Olodaterol: no teratogenic effects in rats at very high inhalational doses; in rabbits, high inhalational doses produced fetal toxicity characteristic of β‑adrenoceptor stimulation (patchy ossifications, short/bent bones, cleft palate, cardiovascular abnormalities) at high exposures; no significant effects at lower high multiples.
  - Olodaterol and/or metabolites crossed the placenta in animal studies.
- Recommendation: avoid use during pregnancy where possible; use only if potential benefit justifies potential risk to fetus.

### Breastfeeding
- Clinical data in lactating women not available.
- Tiotropium and olodaterol and/or metabolites were detected in milk of lactating rats; human milk transfer unknown.
- Recommendation: do not use in lactating women unless expected benefit justifies potential risk to the infant.

### Fertility
- Clinical fertility data not available for combination.
- Tiotropium: no effect on fertility in animal inhalation studies at high multiples.
- Olodaterol: decreased epididymal and testicular weights in rats at certain inhalational doses, but no effect on sperm count/concentration/motility; no impairment of fertility observed up to very high inhalational doses in animals.

### Elderly
- Elderly patients can use recommended dosing. Exercise usual clinical caution and monitor for adverse events.

### Hepatic impairment

- Specific dose adjustments by severity: Not specified in available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Tiotropium: long‑acting muscarinic receptor antagonist (anticholinergic) that produces bronchodilation by inhibiting M3 receptors at the smooth muscle leading to bronchodilation.
- Olodaterol: long‑acting selective β2‑adrenergic receptor agonist that produces bronchodilation by relaxing airway smooth muscle.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: Not specified in available sources.
- Distribution: Not specified in available sources.
- Metabolism:
  - Olodaterol: predominantly metabolised in the liver; in vitro studies indicate CYP450-mediated interactions are unlikely. Inhibitors of P‑gp, OAT1, OAT3, OCT1 may alter exposure.
  - Tiotropium: Not specified beyond predominant renal excretion.
- Elimination:
  - Tiotropium: predominantly excreted renally.
  - Olodaterol: Not specified in full detail; systemic exposure increased by ketoconazole (CYP/P‑gp inhibition) by ~1.7‑fold.
- Special population pharmacokinetics: Limited data; mild/moderate hepatic impairment acceptable; severe hepatic impairment not studied; renal impairment requires monitoring.

### Pharmacodynamics

- Relationship of pharmacodynamic markers to clinical outcomes: Not specified in available sources.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Development program comprised more than 19,000 patients with COPD; over 5,900 patients treated with the combination at the 5 µg/5 µg dose.
- Key trials:
  - Two pivotal, active‑controlled, parallel‑group, 52‑week clinical trials (Trials 1 and 2) comparing the combination to monocomponents (tiotropium 5 µg and olodaterol 5 µg). Safety: overall incidence of adverse events was comparable between combination and olodaterol monotherapy (74.0% vs 76.6% respectively).
  - A third active‑controlled, parallel‑group, 52‑week trial (Trial 9) compared the combination to tiotropium 5 µg; pooled analysis (Trials 1, 2 and 9) showed comparable overall adverse event incidence versus tiotropium (74.1% vs 74.3%).
  - Trial safety findings: no new safety signals identified in the larger Trial 9; specific adverse‑event incidence data cited above (see Adverse Effects).
- Trial design details (primary efficacy endpoints, lung function metrics, statistical results) and certain trial outcomes: Not specified in available sources.

## Additional information
- Overdose:
  - Management: contact local Poison Information Centre for advice (e.g., in Australia contact Poison Information Centre on 131126).
  - Symptoms: Tiotropium overdose may lead to anticholinergic signs and symptoms (e.g., dry mouth, reduced salivary flow). Olodaterol overdose may produce exaggerated β2‑agonist effects including myocardial ischaemia, hypertension or hypotension, tachycardia, arrhythmias, palpitations, dizziness, nervousness, insomnia, anxiety, headache, tremor, dry mouth, muscle spasms, nausea, fatigue, malaise.
- Effects on ability to drive and use machines:
  - No dedicated studies; dizziness and blurred vision have been reported — advise caution when driving or operating machinery if affected.
