# Editorial Product Information: Tofacitinib

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: XELJANZ / XELJANZ XR (tofacitinib citrate)
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official PI for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians must refer to their local regulatory PI for legal and prescribing compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Tofacitinib (as citrate), a selective Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitor.
- **Brand Names**: XELJANZ, XELJANZ XR [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**:
    - **Film-coated Tablets**: 5 mg and 10 mg.
    - **Extended-Release (XR) Tablets**: 11 mg.
    - **Oral Solution**: 1 mg/mL.
- **Route of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA)**: Treatment of moderate to severe active RA in adults who have had an inadequate response or are intolerant to methotrexate. May be used as monotherapy or in combination with conventional synthetic DMARDs (csDMARDs).
- **Psoriatic Arthritis (PsA)**: Treatment of active PsA in adults with an inadequate response to prior DMARD therapy; must be used in combination with csDMARDs.
- **Ankylosing Spondylitis (AS)**: Treatment of active AS in adults with an inadequate response to conventional therapy.
- **Ulcerative Colitis (UC)**: Treatment of moderately to severely active UC in adults with an inadequate response, loss of response, or intolerance to conventional or biological therapies.
- **Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis (JIA)**: Treatment of active polyarticular course JIA (RF+ or RF-, extended oligoarthritis), systemic JIA (without systemic features), and juvenile PsA in patients aged 2 years and older with an inadequate response to prior DMARDs.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing
- **RA and PsA**: 5 mg twice daily (immediate release) or 11 mg once daily (extended-release).
- **Ankylosing Spondylitis**: 5 mg twice daily. Clinical improvement is typically seen within 16 weeks.
- **Ulcerative Colitis**: 
    - **Induction**: 10 mg twice daily for 8 weeks. May extend to 16 weeks if needed. Discontinue if no benefit by week 16.
    - **Maintenance**: 5 mg twice daily. 10 mg twice daily for maintenance is NOT recommended for patients with MACE or malignancy risk factors unless no alternative exists. Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration.

### Paediatric Dosing (pcJIA and jPsA)
Dosing is weight-based for patients ≥2 years of age:
- **10 kg to <20 kg**: 3.2 mg (3.2 mL oral solution) twice daily.
- **20 kg to <40 kg**: 4 mg (4 mL oral solution) twice daily.
- **≥40 kg**: 5 mg (5 mL oral solution or one 5 mg tablet) twice daily.

### Administration Instructions
- May be taken with or without food.
- **XELJANZ XR**: Tablets must be swallowed whole; do not crush, split, or chew.
- **Oral Solution**: Discard any remaining solution 60 days after the bottle is first opened.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to tofacitinib or any excipients.
- Severe hepatic impairment [Australian].
- Concomitant use with biological DMARDs or potent immunosuppressants (e.g., azathioprine, ciclosporin, tacrolimus).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning [Australian]
**XELJANZ/XELJANZ XR should only be used if no suitable treatment alternatives are available in patients:**
- Aged 65 years and older.
- With a history of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease or other cardiovascular risk factors (e.g., current or past long-time smokers).
- With malignancy risk factors (e.g., current or history of malignancy).

### General Warnings
- **Mortality**: Higher rates of all-cause mortality, including sudden cardiovascular death, observed in RA patients >50 years with CV risk factors compared to TNF inhibitors.
- **Major Adverse Cardiovascular Events (MACE)**: Increased risk of myocardial infarction and stroke. 
- **Thrombosis**: Serious and fatal events (DVT, PE, arterial thrombosis) have occurred. Avoid in patients at high risk of venous thromboembolism (VTE). Discontinue immediately if VTE is suspected.
- **Serious Infections**: Increased risk of bacterial, mycobacterial (TB), fungal, and viral infections. Do not initiate during active infection. 
- **Malignancies**: Increased risk of lymphoma, lung cancer (especially in smokers), and non-melanoma skin cancer (NMSC).
- **Gastrointestinal Perforation**: Use with caution in patients at risk (e.g., history of diverticulitis).

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Lymphocytes**: Monitor at baseline and every 3 months. Do not start if ALC <0.75 x 10⁹ cells/L.
- **Neutrophils**: Monitor at baseline, at 4–8 weeks, and every 3 months. Do not start if ANC <1.0 x 10⁹ cells/L.
- **Haemoglobin**: Monitor at baseline, at 4–8 weeks, and every 3 months. Do not start if Hb <90 g/L.
- **Lipids**: Assess 4–8 weeks after initiation.
- **Liver Enzymes**: Routine monitoring recommended.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%)**: Nasopharyngitis (UC), upper respiratory tract infections (pcJIA).
- **Common (1–10%)**: Headache, diarrhoea, nausea, hypertension, bronchitis, cough, rash, pyrexia, increased blood creatine phosphokinase, and hypercholesterolaemia.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%)**: Lymphopenia, neutropenia, hepatic steatosis, paraesthesia, dehydration, and venous thromboembolism.
- **Rare (<0.1%)**: GI perforation, sepsis, tuberculosis (disseminated or CNS), and necrotising fasciitis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Potent CYP3A4 Inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole)**: Reduce tofacitinib dose by half. If taking 5 mg BD, reduce to 5 mg QD.
- **Potent CYP2C19 + Moderate CYP3A4 Inhibitors (e.g., fluconazole)**: Reduce tofacitinib dose by half.
- **Potent CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., rifampicin)**: May result in loss of clinical response; co-administration is not recommended.
- **Immunosuppressants**: Combined use with multiple-dose ciclosporin or tacrolimus is contraindicated/not recommended due to the risk of additive immunosuppression.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: **Category D [Australian]**. Tofacitinib is teratogenic in animals. It should not be used during pregnancy. Women of reproductive potential must use effective contraception during and for a period after treatment.
- **Breastfeeding**: Tofacitinib is present in human milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment.
- **Fertility**: Animal studies showed reduced female fertility but no effect on male fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Approved for pcJIA and jPsA in children ≥2 years. Safety/efficacy in children <2 years or for other indications not established.
- **Elderly Use**: Significantly higher risk of serious infection, MACE, malignancy, and mortality in patients ≥65. Use only if no alternatives exist.
- **Renal Impairment**: 
    - Moderate (eGFR 30–50 mL/min) or Severe (eGFR <30 mL/min): Reduce dose by half (e.g., 5 mg QD if the normal dose is 5 mg BD).
- **Hepatic Impairment**:
    - Moderate (Child-Pugh B): Reduce dose by half.
    - Severe (Child-Pugh C): Contraindicated.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Tofacitinib inhibits the JAK family (JAK1, JAK2, JAK3, and TyK2). It preferentially inhibits signalling by heterodimeric receptors associated with JAK3 and/or JAK1, blocking the action of several cytokines (IL-2, -4, -7, -9, -15, -21) integral to lymphocyte function.
- **Pharmacokinetics**: 
    - **Absorption**: Rapid; peak plasma concentrations in 0.5–1 hour (immediate release) or 4 hours (XR). Bioavailability is 74%.
    - **Metabolism**: ~70% hepatic (primarily CYP3A4, minor CYP2C19); ~30% renal excretion.
    - **Elimination**: Half-life ~3 hours (immediate release) or ~6 hours (XR).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **RA**: Demonstrated efficacy in ORAL Solo (monotherapy), ORAL Sync (with csDMARDs), and ORAL Standard (vs adalimumab).
- **UC**: The OCTAVE program (Induction 1, 2 and Sustain) demonstrated significant remission rates and endoscopic improvement compared to placebo.
- **Safety Study (A3921133/ORAL Surveillance)**: Pivotal post-marketing study showing higher risks of MACE and malignancy with tofacitinib compared to TNF inhibitors in high-risk RA patients.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Tablets (5 mg, 10 mg, 11 mg)**: Store below 30°C.
- **Oral Solution**: Store below 25°C. Store in original bottle/carton to protect from light. Use within 60 days of opening.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule**: **Schedule 4 (Prescription Medicine) [Australian]**.
