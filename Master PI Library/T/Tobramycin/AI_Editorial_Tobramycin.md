# Editorial Product Information: Tobramycin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- **Note:** At the time of this writing, only the Australian PI was available for synthesis. Information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., US, European, Canadian, Japanese) is designated as "Not specified in available sources" or is limited to the provided Australian data.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tobramycin (aminoglycoside antibiotic).
- **Chemical Class:** Aminoglycoside derived from *Streptomyces tenebrarius*.
- **Brand Name(s):** DBL Tobramycin Injection BP [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J01GB01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Solution for injection: 80 mg/2 mL vials [Australian]. 
    - Paediatric injection: 20 mg/2 mL [Australian reference].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intramuscular (IM) or Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Tobramycin** is indicated for the treatment of serious infections caused by susceptible organisms, including:
- **Central Nervous System Infections:** Meningitis, septicaemia, and neonatal sepsis.
- **Gastro-intestinal Infections:** Including peritonitis.
- **Urinary Tract Infections (UTI):** Complicated and recurrent infections such as pyelonephritis and cystitis. (Note: Not indicated for initial uncomplicated UTIs unless organisms are resistant to less toxic antibiotics).
- **Lower Respiratory Tract Infections:** Pneumonia, bronchopneumonia, and acute bronchitis.
- **Skin, Bone, and Soft Tissue Infections:** Including infected burns.
- **Staphylococcal Infections:** May be considered when penicillins or other less toxic drugs are contraindicated.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Serious Infections:** 3 mg/kg/day administered in three equal doses every 8 hours.
- **Life-Threatening Infections:** Up to 5 mg/kg/day in 3 or 4 equal doses, reducing to 3 mg/kg/day as soon as clinically indicated.
- **Mild to Moderate UTI:** 2 to 3 mg/kg/day in two or three divided doses.

### Administration Instructions
- **Intramuscular:** Inject directly into a large muscle mass.
- **Intravenous:** Infuse the same dose as IM. Dilute in 50–100 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride or 5% glucose. Infuse over 20 to 60 minutes. Infusion periods <20 minutes may lead to toxic peak levels (>12 mcg/mL).
- **Obese Patients:** Calculate dose based on estimated lean body weight plus 40% of the excess weight.

### Monitoring
- **Peak Serum Levels:** Aim for 4–12 mcg/mL. Avoid prolonged levels >12 mcg/mL.
- **Trough Serum Levels:** Aim for 1–2 mcg/mL. Levels >2 mcg/mL may indicate tissue accumulation and increased risk of toxicity.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to tobramycin or any excipients (including sodium metabisulfite [Australian]).
- History of hypersensitivity or serious toxic reactions (ototoxicity/nephrotoxicity) to other aminoglycosides (due to cross-sensitivity).
- **Intrathecal administration** is contraindicated [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Ototoxicity:** Both vestibular and auditory (eighth cranial nerve) damage can occur. Auditory changes are often irreversible and bilateral. Risk increases with high peak/trough levels, dehydration, and advanced age.
- **Nephrotoxicity:** Aminoglycosides are potentially nephrotoxic. Monitor renal function (BUN, creatinine) closely, especially in patients with pre-existing impairment.
- **Neuromuscular Disorders:** Use with extreme caution in patients with myasthenia gravis or Parkinsonism, as aminoglycosides may aggravate muscle weakness.
- **Mitochondrial Mutations:** Patients with certain mitochondrial DNA mutations (especially the 12S rRNA nucleotide 1555 A to G substitution) are at higher risk for ototoxicity even with "normal" serum levels.
- **Clostridioides difficile-associated disease:** Reported with use; range from mild diarrhoea to fatal colitis.
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Rare cases of SJS, TEN, and erythema multiforme have been reported [Australian].
- **Sulfite Sensitivity:** Contains sodium metabisulfite, which may cause anaphylaxis or asthma in susceptible individuals.

## 6. Adverse Effects
| System Organ Class | Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ear and Labyrinth** | Common | Dizziness, vertigo, tinnitus, hearing loss (often irreversible). |
| **Renal and Urinary** | Common | Rising BUN/creatinine, oliguria, proteinuria, casts in urine. |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Common | Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea. |
| **Skin/Subcutaneous** | Uncommon | Maculopapular rash, urticaria, itching. |
| **Nervous System** | Rare | Lethargy, confusion, peripheral neuropathy, paraesthesia. |
| **Blood/Lymphatic** | Rare | Anaemia, granulocytopenia, thrombocytopenia, eosinophilia. |
| **Immune System** | Rare | Anaphylaxis, angioedema. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Potent Diuretics:** Ethacrynic acid and furosemide (frusemide) may increase ototoxicity or alter serum concentrations.
- **Nephrotoxic/Neurotoxic Agents:** Avoid concurrent/sequential use with cisplatin, ciclosporin, cephalosporins (especially cefalotin), vancomycin, and other aminoglycosides.
- **Beta-lactam Antibiotics:** In vitro incompatibility (inactivation) occurs when mixed. Administer separately. 
- **Neuromuscular Blockers:** Potentiates the effect of succinylcholine, tubocurarine, and decamethonium, potentially causing respiratory paralysis.
- **Anticoagulants:** May potentiate the effects of warfarin and phenindione.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category D:** Aminoglycosides cross the placenta and can cause fetal harm (including irreversible bilateral congenital deafness). Use only if potential benefits outweigh risks.
- **Breastfeeding:** Tobramycin is excreted in breast milk in low concentrations. Due to potential risks to the newborn, it is recommended to discontinue breastfeeding during therapy.
- **Fertility:** No data available in humans; animal studies (rats) showed no adverse effect on fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - **Neonates (≤1 week):** Up to 4 mg/kg/day in two equal doses every 12 hours.
    - **Infants/Children:** 6 to 7.5 mg/kg/day in 3 or 4 divided doses. 
    - Use with caution due to renal immaturity and prolonged half-life.
- **Elderly Use:** Risk of reduced renal function not always reflected in creatinine/BUN. Monitor renal function and serum levels strictly.
- **Renal Impairment:** Adjust dose by either reducing the dose amount at 8-hour intervals (Regimen I) or extending the interval between fixed doses (Regimen II). Serum level monitoring is mandatory.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal; inhibits protein synthesis by binding to the 30S ribosomal subunit, leading to mRNA misreading.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Primarily active against aerobic Gram-negative bacilli, particularly *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*. Also active against *Staphylococci*. Synergistic with certain beta-lactams against *Enterococci*.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Poorly absorbed GI; peak levels 30–90 mins after IM.
    - **Distribution:** Zero protein binding. Low CSF penetration even with inflammation.
    - **Metabolism:** Not metabolised.
    - **Elimination:** Excreted almost entirely by glomerular filtration. Half-life is 2–3 hours in normal renal function; up to 70 hours in impairment.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C.
- **Light:** Protect from light [Australian].
- **Stability:** Use as soon as practicable after dilution; if stored, hold at 2–8°C for no more than 24 hours.
