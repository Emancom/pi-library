# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the provided Product Information (PI) source from the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the United States regulatory documentation was provided for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Name:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (generic).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn errors of metabolism [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing maintenance haemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often utilised in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maximum Administered Dose:** 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Haemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Notes
- Inspect parenterals visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal oedema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate risks vs. benefits before re-administration [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine may increase the frequency or severity of seizures in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a prior history [US].
- **Monitoring:** In metabolic disorders, monitor plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L), blood chemistry, and vital signs [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** Chronic high doses of **oral** levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide). The safety of the oral formulation in renal insufficiency has not been evaluated [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (>10%):** Transient nausea and vomiting. In ESRD clinical trials, the following were reported at $\ge$ 5%:
    - **General:** Abdominal pain, chest pain, fever, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain.
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia.
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhoea, dyspepsia, vomiting.
    - **Metabolic:** Hypercalcaemia, hypervolaemia.
    - **Nervous:** Dizziness.
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis, cough, dyspnea, rhinitis.
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Seizures, anaphylaxis [US].
- **Other:** Mild body odour has been reported with levocarnitine therapy [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalised Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. Frequent monitoring of INR is recommended for patients on warfarin therapy following levocarnitine initiation or dose adjustment [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned/letter-less]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Dairy cow studies show increased milk carnitine concentrations. Benefits to the mother should be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg body weight (refer to Section 3). Specific age-range safety profiles beyond metabolic/ESRD indications are not detailed [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** See Section 3 for ESRD dosing. Safety of oral formulations is not established in renal failure [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also facilitates the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (faeces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Trials:** Two double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in chronic haemodialysis patients evaluated doses of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg. While treatment significantly increased plasma carnitine concentrations, the impact on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, malaise) in this specific population has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C [US].
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Stability in Mixture:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's in PVC bags at concentrations from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription Only Medicine).
