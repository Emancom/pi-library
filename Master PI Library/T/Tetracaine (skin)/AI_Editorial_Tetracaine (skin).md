# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. At the time of compilation, only the United States (FDA) documentation was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); chemical class: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. 
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (generic) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg, administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.

### Administration Instructions
- **Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered in patients with severe metabolic crisis, followed by equivalent doses over the subsequent 24 hours. Administration should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours by infusion or intravenous injection. Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].
- **ESRD Administration:** Dose should be administered as a slow (2 to 3 minute) bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine levels: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

### Dose Adjustments
- In ESRD patients, dose adjustments may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels below the normal range (40 to 50 µmol/L). Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate appropriate medical care [US].
- **Seizures:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Levocarnitine in Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

- **Other Reactions:** Transient nausea and vomiting, body odor, and gastritis have been observed [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating treatment or after dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not explicitly lettered, but described]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. There are no adequate/well-controlled studies in pregnant women; use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show carnitine concentrations in milk increase following administration. Benefits to the mother should be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Refer to Section 3 (Dosage and Administration). Specific age-based safety data was not provided beyond metabolic disorder dosing [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. Monitor trough concentrations to guide dosing [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, facilitating energy production via oxidation. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Administration to ESRD patients on dialysis results in increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Dose-Ranging Studies:** In trials involving patients receiving 10, 20, or 40 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis, mean trough levocarnitine concentrations increased proportionally with the dose. At 24 weeks, the 20 mg/kg group showed a mean trough of 240 ± 60 µmol/L (normal range 40–50 µmol/L). Clinical outcomes regarding signs and symptoms of carnitine deficiency in this population have not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial; the formulation contains no preservative [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) when stored at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
