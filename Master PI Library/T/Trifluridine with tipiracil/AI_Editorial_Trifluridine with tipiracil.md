# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA). 
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine injection, intended for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises available regulatory information into a single clinical reference.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of preparation, only the United States (FDA) source documentation was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule for fatty acid transport).
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
  - Recommended dose: 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
  - Severe metabolic crisis: A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
  - Frequency: Administer every 3 or 4 hours; should not be administered less frequently than every 6 hours.
  - Maximum dose: Up to 300 mg/kg has been administered in some cases [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
  - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
  - Administration: Slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
  - Dose adjustments: May be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial.

### Monitoring
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations prior to therapy.
- Conduct weekly and monthly monitoring, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine levels between 35 and 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in patients with ESRD on dialysis. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known seizure disorder, an increase in frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** Note that the safety of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic errors.

### Very Common (>10%)
- **General:** Injection site reaction (27–38%), Pain (21–35%), Headache (3–37%), Fever (5–12%), Abdominal pain (5–21%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), Hypotension (3–19%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), Vomiting (9–21%), Nausea (5–12%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), Cough increase (10–18%), Dyspnea (3–11%).
- **Other:** Flu syndrome (15–29%), Infection (10–24%).

### Common (1–10%)
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, Chest pain, Palpitations, Atrial fibrillation.
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia, Hyperkalemia, Hypervolemia, Peripheral edema, Weight changes.
- **Nervous:** Dizziness, Paresthesia, Insomnia, Depression.
- **Musculoskeletal:** Leg cramps.
- **Special Senses:** Taste perversion, Eye disorder, Amblyopia.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Anaphylaxis and severe respiratory distress (bronchospasm).
- Seizures.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified, but data provided]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations after exogenous administration. Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg). See Section 3.
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available source.
- **Renal Impairment:** The injection is indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. However, caution is advised regarding the accumulation of metabolites if using oral formulations in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. 
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) and γ-butyrobetaine.
- **Excretion:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels).
- **Half-life:** Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x weekly post-dialysis), mean trough levocarnitine concentrations increased from 21.3 µmol/L (baseline) to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** A study comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses in ESRD patients demonstrated a dose-dependent increase in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks.
- **Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the effect on specific clinical outcomes in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].

- **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription Only Medicine).
