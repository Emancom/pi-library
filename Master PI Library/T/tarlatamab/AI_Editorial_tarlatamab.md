# Editorial Product Information: Tarlatamab

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration) and United States (Food and Drug Administration).
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of tarlatamab product information, synthesised from multiple regulatory sources for use by healthcare professionals.
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tarlatamab (Australian); Tarlatamab-dlle (US).
- **Chemical Class:** Bispecific delta-like ligand 3 (DLL3)-directed CD3 T-cell engager.
- **Brand Name:** Imdelltra
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:**
    - Lyophilised powder for injection in single-dose vials: 1 mg and 10 mg.
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Amgen Inc. [US]; Amgen Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Extensive-Stage Small Cell Lung Cancer (ES-SCLC):** Indicated for the treatment of adult patients with ES-SCLC with disease progression on or after platinum-based chemotherapy. 
    - [Australian: Provisional approval based on overall response rate (ORR) and duration of response (DOR).]
    - [US: Accelerated approval based on ORR and DOR; subsequent data from confirmatory trials has established overall survival benefit.]

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Treatment involves a step-up dosing schedule to mitigate the risk of Cytokine Release Syndrome (CRS).
- **Cycle 1, Day 1:** 1 mg (Step-up dose).
- **Cycle 1, Day 8:** 10 mg.
- **Cycle 1, Day 15:** 10 mg.
- **Cycle 2 and subsequent cycles:** 10 mg every 2 weeks (biweekly).

### Administration Instructions
- **Infusion Duration:** Administer as an intravenous infusion over 1 hour.
- **Preparation:** Vials must be reconstituted with Sterile Water for Injections. **Important:** An IV Solution Stabiliser (IVSS) must be added to the 0.9% saline infusion bag before adding the reconstituted drug to prevent adsorption to the bag and tubing.
- **Monitoring:** 
    - [US: Monitor patients in an appropriate healthcare setting for 22 to 24 hours following Cycle 1 Day 1 and Cycle 1 Day 8 doses.]
    - [Australian: Monitor for 16 hours on Cycle 1 Day 1; recommend patients remain within 1 hour of a healthcare setting for a total of 24 hours after C1D1 and C1D8 doses, accompanied by a caregiver.]
    - For subsequent doses: Observe for 6–8 hours (Cycle 2), 3–4 hours (Cycles 3-4), and 2 hours (Cycle 5 onwards) [Australian, US].

### Premedication and Prophylaxis
For Cycle 1 (Days 1 and 8):
- **Dexamethasone:** 8 mg IV (or equivalent) within 1 hour prior to infusion.
- **Hydration:** 1 litre of normal saline IV over 2 to 4 hours [US] or 4 to 5 hours [Australian] immediately following the infusion.

### Restarting After Delay
If a dose is delayed, the restart procedure depends on the timing of the last dose. Delays longer than 2 weeks (for 1 mg dose) or 3–4 weeks (for 10 mg doses) generally require repeating the 1 mg step-up dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- [Australian: Hypersensitivity to the active substance or any of the excipients (Glutamic acid, Sucrose, Polysorbate 80, Sodium hydroxide).]
- [US: None listed.]

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [Australian, US]
- **Cytokine Release Syndrome (CRS):** Can be serious, life-threatening, or fatal. Management requires step-up dosing, premedication, and monitoring.
- **Neurologic Toxicity:** Includes Immune Effector Cell-Associated Neurotoxicity Syndrome (ICANS), which can be life-threatening or fatal.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Cytopenias:** Neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, and anaemia are common. Monitor CBC prior to each dose.
- **Infections:** Serious, including opportunistic and fatal infections, have occurred. Monitor for signs of infection and treat promptly.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Elevations in ALT, AST, and bilirubin can occur with or without CRS. Monitor liver enzymes prior to treatment and before each dose.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Rare severe events reported. Monitor for rash or bronchospasm.
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** May cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status before initiation.

### Monitoring Requirements
- CBC, liver enzymes, and bilirubin should be evaluated before each dose through Cycle 5, and then on Day 1 of each cycle starting with Cycle 6 [US].
- Frequent neurologic assessments for signs of ICANS.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Very Common (>10%)
- **Immune/General:** CRS (54–57%), pyrexia (33–39%), fatigue (39–51%), asthenia.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Constipation (30%), nausea (19–25%), dysgeusia (28–36%).
- **Metabolism:** Decreased appetite (34–38%), hyponatremia.
- **Respiratory:** Dyspnoea (17%), cough (17%).
- **Laboratory Abnormalities:** Decreased lymphocytes (65–84%), decreased white blood cells (44–50%), decreased haemoglobin (51–58%), decreased neutrophils (12–15%), decreased sodium (57–68%), increased uric acid.

### Serious and Notable Effects
- **Grade 3 or 4 Reactions:** Neutropenia, anemia, and laboratory abnormalities.
- **Fatalities:** Reported in clinical trials due to ICANS (0.2–0.4%), pneumonia, and sepsis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic:** No formal drug interaction studies have been conducted.
- **CYP450 Substrates:** Tarlatamab-induced cytokine release may transiently suppress CYP450 enzymes. Monitor patients taking concomitant CYP450 substrates with a narrow therapeutic index (e.g., warfarin, cyclosporine) and adjust doses as needed.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - [Australian: Category C.]
    - [US: Risk of fetal harm based on mechanism of action.]
    - Advise females of reproductive potential to use effective contraception during treatment and for 2 months after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if tarlatamab is excreted in human milk. Advise patients not to breastfeed during treatment and for 2 months after the last dose.
- **Fertility:** No clinical or animal data available regarding effects on fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established.
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy observed in patients ≥65 years. No dose adjustment required.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild or moderate impairment (eGFR ≥30 mL/min). Not studied in severe renal impairment (eGFR <30 mL/min).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild or moderate impairment. Not studied in severe hepatic impairment.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Tarlatamab binds to DLL3 on tumour cells and CD3 on T-cells. This bispecific binding induces T-cell activation and the release of cytotoxic proteins, leading to the lysis of DLL3-expressing tumour cells.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Steady-state volume of distribution is approximately 8.5 L.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 11 days. Systemic clearance is approximately 0.65–0.7 L/day.
    - **Metabolism:** Expected to be degraded into small peptides via catabolic pathways.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Transient elevation of cytokines (IL-2, IL-6, IL-8, IL-10, IFN-γ, and TNF-α) typically peaks within 24–48 hours after the first 1 mg dose.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **DeLLphi-301:** A Phase 2 trial in ES-SCLC patients previously treated with platinum chemotherapy. The 10 mg biweekly dose demonstrated an ORR of 40% [US] to 41% [Australian] with a median duration of response of 9.7 months.
- **DeLLphi-304:** A Phase 3 trial comparing tarlatamab to standard-of-care (SOC) chemotherapy. Tarlatamab significantly improved overall survival (13.6 months vs 8.3 months for SOC) and progression-free survival (4.2 months vs 3.2 months).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C in the original carton to protect from light. Do not freeze.
- **Post-reconstitution/Dilution:** 
    - Reconstituted vials may be held for up to 4 hours before further dilution. 
    - The prepared infusion bag is stable for a **total** of 8 hours at room temperature (20°C–25°C) or up to 7 days refrigerated (2°C–8°C). Total time includes reconstitution to end of infusion.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australian Classification:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **US Classification:** Rx-only.
