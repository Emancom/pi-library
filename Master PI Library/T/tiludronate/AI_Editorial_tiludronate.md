# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a single clinically focused reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (Generic/INN); also known as L-carnitine.
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Generic, Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection for intravenous use: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with metabolic defects resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
- **Initial Management:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the next 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance Dose:** Subsequent daily doses typically remain at 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg may be required based on clinical response [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
- **Administration:** Delivered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine levels. Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of treatment if levels exceed the target range [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
- **Target Plasma Levels:** Aim for free carnitine concentrations between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None Known:** There are no listed contraindications for levocarnitine injection in the provided source material [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in dialysis patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. In the event of a severe reaction, the drug must be discontinued and appropriate medical intervention initiated [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without a prior history of epilepsy. In patients with pre-existing seizure disorders, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures has been observed following both oral and intravenous administration [US].
- **Monitoring:** Periodic monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations is recommended [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events observed in clinical trials for ESRD patients (occurring at a frequency of ≥5% regardless of causality) include:

| Body System | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), flu syndrome (25%), headache (22%), infection (15%), abdominal pain (9%), fever (7%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), palpitations (5%) |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), anorexia (5%), dyspepsia (5%) |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness (13%), paresthesia (5%), insomnia (4%), depression (5%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), rhinitis (9%), dyspnea (7%) |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%), hypervolemia (5%) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, and seizures [US].
- **Other Notable Effects:** Transient nausea and vomiting; body odor and gastritis have been reported less frequently [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate that levocarnitine may increase the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended to monitor INR levels frequently after starting levocarnitine or following any dose adjustment in patients on anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not specified by lettered category]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits using doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate, well-controlled studies exist in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly required [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies on levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers have not been conducted. While studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine levels in milk, the risks to the infant from excess intake must be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use is approved; dosing is based on mg/kg body weight (refer to Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific dose adjustments for the elderly are not detailed in the source, though general clinical monitoring is advised [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal failure. High doses of oral formulations in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US]. (See Section 3 for IV dosing in ESRD).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation and subsequent ATP production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine helps clear accumulated toxic acyl-CoA esters by forming acylcarnitine, which is then rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV administration (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Efficacy:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis carnitine levels from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses confirmed a dose-dependent increase in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Clinical Outcomes Note:** While IV levocarnitine increases carnitine levels in ESRD patients, its specific impact on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, malaise) has not been definitively established in this population [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s; stable for 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
