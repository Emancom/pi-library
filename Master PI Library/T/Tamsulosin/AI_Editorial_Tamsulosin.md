# Editorial Product Information: Tamsulosin

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of tamsulosin hydrochloride for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises the clinical data and regulatory requirements available from the provided source.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tamsulosin hydrochloride
- **Chemical Class:** Alpha-1-adrenoceptor antagonist (selective)
- **Brand Names:** APO-TAMSULOSIN SR [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 
    - Modified-release tablet: 400 micrograms (mcg) [Australian]
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Southern Cross Pharma Pty Ltd; Distributed by Arrotex Pharmaceuticals [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- Relief of lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS) associated with benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dose:** One 400 mcg tablet once daily [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Tablets must be swallowed whole. 
    - Do not break, crush, or chew the tablet, as this compromises the modified-release properties.
    - May be taken with or without food, or on an empty stomach [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to tamsulosin hydrochloride (including drug-induced angioedema) or any excipient in the formulation.
- History of orthostatic hypotension.
- Severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh score >9).
- Severe renal impairment (creatinine clearance < 10 mL/min).
- Concurrent administration of other alpha-1-adrenoceptor antagonists [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Postural Hypotension and Syncope:** Treatment may cause postural hypotension, which rarely leads to syncope. Patients should be advised to sit or lie down if symptoms occur and avoid situations where injury could result from fainting.
- **Intra-operative Floppy Iris Syndrome (IFIS):** Observed during cataract and glaucoma surgery in patients currently or previously treated with alpha-1 blockers. Surgeons should be prepared to modify surgical techniques (e.g., use of iris hooks or visco-elastic substances). The benefit of stopping therapy prior to surgery is not established.
- **Exclusion of Prostatic Carcinoma:** Carcinoma of the prostate and other urological conditions must be excluded via digital rectal examination (and PSA if appropriate) before starting therapy and at regular intervals thereafter.
- **Sulfa Allergy:** Allergic reactions have been reported in patients with a history of sulfonamide allergy; caution is warranted.
- **Myocardial Ischaemia:** Safety has not been formally assessed in patients with myocardial infarction or angina pectoris within the preceding six months.
- **Dizziness:** May cause dizziness; patients should use caution when driving or operating machinery [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by frequency based on clinical trial data [Australian]:

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Dizziness (1.3%), ejaculation disorders (including retrograde ejaculation and failure), insomnia |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Palpitations, constipation, diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting, asthenia, headache, rhinitis, rash, pruritus, urticaria, postural hypotension |
| **Rare** (0.01–0.1%) | Syncope, angioedema |
| **Very Rare** (<0.01%) | Priapism |

**Post-Marketing Experience:** 
The following have been reported (frequency not estimable):
- **Vision:** Blurred vision, vision impairment.
- **Respiratory:** Epistaxis.
- **Skin:** Stevens-Johnson syndrome, erythema multiforme, exfoliative dermatitis, photosensitivity.
- **General:** Chest discomfort.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Priapism (requires immediate medical attention to prevent permanent erectile dysfunction) and angioedema [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Concurrent use with other alpha-1-adrenoceptor antagonists is contraindicated due to potential hypotensive effects [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **CYP3A4 and CYP2D6 Inhibitors:** Concomitant use with strong inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, paroxetine) increases tamsulosin Cmax and AUC. Tamsulosin 400 mcg should not be used with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors in patients known to be CYP2D6 poor metabolizers. 
    - **Cimetidine:** Increases tamsulosin plasma levels (no dose adjustment required).
    - **Furosemide:** Decreases tamsulosin plasma levels (no dose adjustment required).
    - **Diclofenac and Warfarin:** May increase the elimination rate of tamsulosin.
- **No Significant Interactions:** No interactions observed with atenolol, enalapril, nifedipine, theophylline, or digoxin [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category B2. Tamsulosin is intended for use only in males. Animal studies show no evidence of teratogenicity at maternal doses, but peri-/post-natal administration in rats was associated with increased stillbirths and reduced pup weight gain.
- **Breastfeeding:** Not applicable (intended for male use). In animal studies (rats), tamsulosin or its metabolites were excreted in milk.
- **Fertility:** Alpha-1 antagonists are known to affect male fertility (penile erection, emission, ejaculation). Animal studies showed reversible reductions in male and female fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not indicated for use in children [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific dose adjustment is required for elderly patients based on available data [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required in mild-to-moderate impairment. Contraindicated in severe impairment (CrCl < 10 mL/min) [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required in mild-to-moderate impairment. Contraindicated in severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh > 9) [Australian].
- **Other:** Not indicated for use in women [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selective, competitive antagonist of post-junctional alpha-1-adrenoceptors, particularly the alpha-1A subtype found in the human prostate. This results in the relaxation of prostate and urethral smooth muscle, reducing obstruction and improving urinary flow and symptoms.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Modified-release formulation. Approximately 55–59% of the dose is absorbed from the intestine. Food has a minimal effect on absorption.
    - **Distribution:** Approximately 99% bound to plasma proteins; low volume of distribution (0.2 L/kg).
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolised slowly in the liver (CYP3A4 and CYP2D6) with low first-pass effect. Unchanged drug is the primary component in plasma.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily excreted in urine; 4–6% is excreted as unchanged drug. Elimination half-life at steady state is approximately 14–15 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
Efficacy was established in two 12-week, randomised, placebo-controlled studies (617-CL-303 and 617-CL-307) involving 2,962 patients.
- **Primary Outcome:** Significant reduction in total International Prostate Symptom Score (I-PSS) compared to placebo.
- **Findings:** Tamsulosin 400 mcg daily demonstrated statistically significant improvements in both voiding (obstructive) and storage (irritative) symptoms and Quality of Life (QoL) scores compared to placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C.
- Protect from light.
- Shelf life is specified on the secondary packaging [Australian].
