# Editorial Product Information: Tropisetron

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of tropisetron, synthesised for healthcare professionals. It integrates clinical data and regulatory specifics from the provided Product Information (PI).
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tropisetron (as hydrochloride). It is a selective competitive 5-HT3 receptor antagonist.
- **Brand Names:** Tropisetron-AFT [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A04AA03 (Not explicitly in source, but standard for this class).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Solution for injection or infusion: 2 mg/2 mL ampoule and 5 mg/5 mL ampoule [Australian].
    - Note: Oral formulations are mentioned for maintenance therapy but specific oral product details were not provided in the source injection PI.
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (infusion or slow injection).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AFT Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Prevention of nausea and vomiting induced by cytotoxic therapy:** (5 mg/5 mL strength) [Australian-approved].
- **Treatment and prevention of post-operative nausea and vomiting (PONV) in adults:** (2 mg/2 mL strength) [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Chemotherapy-Induced Nausea and Vomiting (CINV):** 
    - A six-day course is recommended. 
    - **Day 1:** 5 mg administered intravenously shortly before chemotherapy. This may be given as an infusion (15 minutes) or a slow injection (no less than 1 minute). 
    - **Days 2–6:** Followed by oral administration of 5 mg per day [Australian].
- **Post-operative Nausea and Vomiting (PONV):**
    - **Prevention:** 2 mg administered intravenously shortly before the induction of anaesthesia.
    - **Treatment:** 2 mg administered intravenously.
    - Administration may be via infusion or slow injection (no less than 30 seconds) [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Dilution:** For infusion, ampoules can be diluted in 100 mL of various solutions including Sodium Chloride 0.9%, Glucose 5%, Ringer’s solution, Levulose 5%, Mannitol 10%, or mixtures of Potassium Chloride with Glucose or Saline.
- **Stability:** Diluted solutions remain physically and chemically stable for at least 24 hours [Australian].

### Titration and Missed Doses
- **Dexamethasone:** Efficacy in CINV may be enhanced by the addition of dexamethasone.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources for the intravenous initiation.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to tropisetron, other 5-HT3 receptor antagonists, or any excipients [Australian].
- **Pregnancy and Lactation:** Use is contraindicated based on current Australian PI [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiac Effects:** Caution is required in patients with cardiac rhythm or conduction disturbances. Prolongation of the QTc interval has been observed at high doses (up to 80 mg) and cannot be excluded at lower doses, particularly in patients undergoing general anaesthesia or taking concomitant QTc-prolonging medications [Australian].
- **Uncontrolled Hypertension:** Daily doses exceeding 10 mg should be avoided in patients with uncontrolled hypertension as they may further increase blood pressure [Australian].
- **Poor Metabolisers:** Approximately 8% of the Caucasian population are poor metabolisers of the sparteine/debrisoquine (CYP2D6) pathway. While half-life is significantly extended (4–5 times), no dose reduction is required for the standard 5 mg/day 6-day course [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Frequent monitoring of vital signs is recommended in cases of suspected overdose [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
In general, tropisetron is well-tolerated. Side effects are typically transient at recommended doses.

| Adverse Event | Placebo (n=473) | Tropisetron 2 mg (n=485) | Tropisetron 5 mg (n=176) |

| **Bradycardia** | 11% | 10% | 1% |

| **Headache** | 3% | 4% | 10% |

### Other Adverse Effects
- **Common (1–10%):** Constipation (especially at 5 mg dose), fatigue, somnolence, gastrointestinal disorders (abdominal pain, diarrhoea), and anorexia [Australian].
- **Uncommon (<1%):** Syncope, generalised urticaria [Australian].
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Rash, erythema, anaphylactic reactions/shock [Australian].
- **Serious/Very Rare:** Collapse and cardiovascular arrest have been reported in post-marketing settings, though often confounded by underlying disease or chemotherapy [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic (Enzyme Inducers):** Concomitant use with rifampicin, phenobarbital, or other enzyme-inducing drugs results in lower plasma concentrations of tropisetron. Extensive metabolisers (but not poor metabolisers) require a dosage increase [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetic (Enzyme Inhibitors):** Inhibitors like cimetidine have negligible effects on plasma levels and do not require dose adjustment [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamic:** Caution should be exercised when co-administered with drugs known to prolong the QT interval [Australian].
- **Anaesthetics:** No specific interaction studies have been performed with drugs used in anaesthesia [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category B3.** Reproductive studies in animals showed increased post-implantation loss and delayed fetal development at high doses. Tropisetron is contraindicated in pregnancy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Tropisetron is excreted in rat milk. It is unknown if it enters human milk; therefore, patients should not breastfeed while on this medication [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available in available sources [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use is not recommended as experience in children is currently limited [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No evidence suggests that elderly patients require different dosages or experience different side effects compared to younger patients [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage reduction is necessary for the standard 5 mg/day 6-day course in patients with impaired kidney function, although plasma concentrations may be up to 50% higher than in healthy extensive metabolisers [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dosage reduction is necessary for the standard 5 mg/day 6-day course in patients with liver cirrhosis or acute hepatitis [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Tropisetron is a potent and selective competitive antagonist of 5-HT3 receptors. It blocks serotonin-mediated emesis reflexes both at peripheral presynaptic 5-HT3 receptors and within the central nervous system (area postrema) [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** It has a 24-hour duration of action, facilitating once-daily dosing. It does not cause extrapyramidal side effects [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Nearly complete (>95%) from the GI tract; absolute bioavailability is ~60% due to first-pass metabolism (saturable at higher doses).
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is 400–600 L; 71% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolised via hydroxylation (CYP2D6 polymorphism) and subsequent conjugation. Metabolites are pharmacologically inactive.
    - **Elimination:** In extensive metabolisers, half-life is ~8 hours. In poor metabolisers, it may extend to 45 hours. Urine-to-faeces excretion ratio is 5:1 [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **PONV Studies:** Three prospective, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials supported the 2 mg dose as optimal for both prevention and treatment. In one study (n=908), 2 mg tropisetron showed similar efficacy to 4 mg ondansetron in preventing PONV following abdominal surgery (70% vs 72% response rates, respectively) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30 °C [Australian].
- **Microbial Safety:** Due to the risk of contamination, infusions should be used within 8 hours of preparation. If stored, they should be kept at 2–8°C [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **S4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
