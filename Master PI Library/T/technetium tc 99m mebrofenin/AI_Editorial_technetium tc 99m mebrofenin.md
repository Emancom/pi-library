# Editorial Product Information: technetium tc 99m mebrofenin

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **technetium tc 99m mebrofenin**
- Chemical class: iminodiacetic acid (HIDA) derivative
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intravenous

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction:
  - [US] First approval (NDA): 01/21/1987
- Date of last revision/update of source product information (as stated in available source):
  - Revised December 2024
  - Revised November 2025
- Withdrawals, suspensions, restrictions: Not specified in available sources
- If other jurisdictional registration dates/updates are required: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Indicated as a hepatobiliary imaging agent (single, general indication).

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General adult dosing
- Nonjaundiced adult patient: 74–185 MBq (2–5 mCi) administered intravenously.
- Patient with serum bilirubin > 1.5 mg/dL: 111–370 MBq (3–10 mCi) administered intravenously.

### Dose measurement and timing
- Measure the patient dose using a suitable radioactivity calibration system immediately prior to administration.
- Patients should preferably be fasting for 4 hours prior to imaging to avoid false non-visualization due to recent gallbladder emptying.
- Allow at least 24 hours before repeating the examination.
- Technetium Tc 99m mebrofenin should be formulated no more than 18 hours prior to clinical use.

### Preparation and administration instructions (aseptic technique required)
- Use aseptic technique and waterproof gloves; perform manipulations using appropriate radiation shielding.
- Maintain an inert (nitrogen) atmosphere in the reaction vessel during reconstitution (avoid introducing air).
- If sodium pertechnetate Tc 99m must be diluted, use preservative-free sodium chloride injection only.
- Typical preparation procedure:
  - Place reaction vial in appropriate lead shield.
  - Swab the vial closure with germicide.
  - Inject sterile, additive-free sodium pertechnetate Tc 99m (1–5 mL; may contain up to 3700 MBq / 100 mCi Tc 99m) into the vial while maintaining nitrogen atmosphere.
  - Secure lead shield and gently swirl; allow to stand (approximately 15 minutes).
  - Record date/time of preparation and measured radioactivity.
  - Visually inspect solution; if not clear and free of particulate matter or discoloration, do not use.
  - Withdraw patient doses using sterile technique and appropriate shielding for administration.
  - Use prepared material within the stated interval for clinical use (see above: no more than 18 hours after formulation).

### Missed dose / repeat dosing
- An interval of at least 24 hours should be allowed before repeating the examination.

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to technetium tc 99m mebrofenin or any component of the preparation.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- **Allergic reactions**: Theoretical possibility of allergic or hypersensitivity reactions; consider risk in patients receiving multiple doses.
- Contents of radiopharmaceutical preparation are intended for preparation of technetium tc 99m mebrofenin only and not for direct administration of unprepared kit components.
- Delayed or non-visualization of the gallbladder can occur:
  - Immediately post-prandial or after prolonged fasting or parenteral feeding.
  - In functional biliary obstruction (e.g., chronic cholecystitis, pancreatitis).
  - In hepatocellular disease and juvenile hepatitis.
  - Administration of opioids such as meperidine or morphine may delay intestinal transit and can result in non-visualization.
  - Septic patients may have absent or delayed hepatobiliary clearance.
- Labeling/Preparation quality:
  - The technetium labeling reaction depends on reduced stannous ion; oxidants present in sodium pertechnetate Tc 99m will adversely affect labeling—do not use pertechnetate solutions known to contain oxidants.
- Radiation safety:
  - Radiopharmaceuticals should be used only by clinicians qualified in handling radionuclides.
  - Minimize radiation exposure to patients and occupational personnel consistent with proper patient management.
- Carcinogenesis, mutagenesis, impairment of fertility:
  - No long-term animal studies have been performed to evaluate carcinogenic potential or effects on fertility.
- Pregnancy:
  - Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. It is unknown whether technetium tc 99m mebrofenin can cause fetal harm. Administer to pregnant women only if expected benefit justifies potential risk to fetus.
- Lactation:
  - Technetium tc 99m is excreted in human milk; breastfeeding should be interrupted and formula feeding substituted following administration.
- Pediatric use:
  - Safety and effectiveness in individuals < 18 years have not been established.
- Monitoring requirements and clinical precautions:
  - Inspect prepared solution visually for clarity and particulate matter prior to administration.
  - Measure activity with an appropriate calibration system and document time and activity.
  - Interpret imaging results in the context of clinical status and other diagnostic data (delayed imaging/abnormal results may reflect non-biliary causes).
  - Consider serum bilirubin level as it affects biodistribution and excretion; elevated bilirubin increases renal excretion and may delay hepatobiliary transit.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Pharmacodynamic interactions:
  - Opioids (e.g., meperidine, morphine) may delay intestinal transit of the imaging agent and can result in nonvisualization of the gallbladder.
- Preparation/technical interactions:
  - Oxidizing agents present in the sodium pertechnetate Tc 99m supply can oxidize stannous ion and compromise radiolabeling efficiency—do not use pertechnetate containing oxidants.
- Other clinically significant interactions: Not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- Pregnancy:
  - Animal reproductive studies: Not conducted.
  - Potential for fetal harm: Unknown. Administer only if expected benefit justifies potential risk.
  - Jurisdictional pregnancy categories: Not specified in available sources.
- Breastfeeding:
  - Technetium tc 99m is excreted in human milk during lactation; recommended to substitute formula feeding for breast feeding after administration.
- Fertility:
  - No data from long-term animal studies regarding impairment of fertility; effect on human fertility: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
- Paediatric use:
  - Safety and effectiveness in children < 18 years: Not established.
- Elderly:
  - Specific dose adjustments or cautions in elderly patients: Not specified in available sources.
- Renal impairment:
  - Specific dosing adjustments by renal function/eGFR: Not specified in available sources.
  - Pharmacokinetic observations: In patients with elevated serum bilirubin (see hepatic impairment below), renal excretion of activity may increase.
- Hepatic impairment:
  - In jaundiced/severely hyperbilirubinemic patients:
    - Percent injected dose remaining in blood at 10 minutes may be approximately twice that in normals.
    - Hepatobiliary transit may be delayed and visualization times prolonged; image quality may be diminished.
    - In studies of patients with mean elevated bilirubin (~9.8 mg/dL; range reported), mean percent injected dose excreted in urine during first 3 hours was about 3% (range 0.2–11.5%); percent excreted in urine during 3–24 hours averaged 14.9% (range 0.4–34.8%).
  - Dosing modifications for hepatic impairment: Not specifically defined in available sources beyond the guidance to increase administered activity for patients with elevated bilirubin (see Dosage section).

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **technetium tc 99m mebrofenin** is a radiolabeled iminodiacetic acid derivative used for diagnostic hepatobiliary imaging; it has no known pharmacologic (therapeutic) action at recommended diagnostic doses.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Distribution and clearance:
  - Rapid clearance from circulation after intravenous administration.
  - Mean percent injected dose remaining in blood at 10 minutes in normal subjects: ~17%.
  - Hepatic uptake occurs rapidly: liver visualized by ~5 minutes; mean time to maximum liver uptake ~11 minutes post-injection.
  - Hepatic duct and gallbladder visualization typically by 10–15 minutes in subjects with normal hepatobiliary function.
  - Intestinal activity visualized typically by 30–60 minutes in subjects with normal function.
- Excretion:
  - In normal subjects, mean percent injected dose excreted in urine during first 3 hours: ~1% (range 0.4–2.0%).
  - In patients with elevated serum bilirubin, renal excretion is higher (see section 10).
- Physical characteristics of radionuclide:
  - Technetium-99m decays by isomeric transition with a physical half-life of 6.02 hours.
  - Principal useful photon energy: mean ~140.5 keV.
  - Specific gamma ray constant: 0.78 R/hour-millicurie at 1 cm.
  - First half-value layer in lead: 0.017 cm.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Diagnostic imaging properties derive from hepatic extraction and biliary excretion of the labeled compound enabling visualization of liver, biliary tree, gallbladder, and intestinal transit.

### Radiation dosimetry
- Estimated absorbed radiation doses for a 370 MBq (10 mCi) intravenous injection (values reported in source):
  - Total body: 2.0 mGy (rads 0.2) in normal subjects; 1.7 mGy (0.17 rad) in severely jaundiced patients.
  - Liver: 4.7 mGy (0.47 rad) normal; 8.1 mGy (0.81 rad) jaundiced.
  - Gallbladder wall: 13.7 mGy (1.37 rad) normal; 12.5 mGy (1.25 rad) jaundiced.
  - Small intestine: 29.9 mGy (2.99 rad) normal; 16.0 mGy (1.60 rad) jaundiced.
  - Upper large intestine wall: 47.4 mGy (4.74 rad) normal; 24.8 mGy (2.48 rad) jaundiced.
  - Lower large intestine wall: 36.4 mGy (3.64 rad) normal; 19.7 mGy (1.97 rad) jaundiced.
  - Kidney: 2.2 mGy (0.22 rad) normal; 1.9 mGy (0.19 rad) jaundiced.
  - Urinary bladder wall: 2.9 mGy (0.29 rad) normal; 24.2 mGy (2.42 rad) jaundiced.
  - Ovaries: 10.1 mGy (1.01 rad) normal; 6.4 mGy (0.64 rad) jaundiced.
  - Testes: 0.5 mGy (0.05 rad) normal; 1.1 mGy (0.11 rad) jaundiced.
  - Red marrow: 3.4 mGy (0.34 rad) normal; 2.5 mGy (0.25 rad) jaundiced.
- Dosimetry calculations reported make assumptions about percent hepatic uptake and routes of excretion and differ between normal and severely jaundiced scenarios.
