# Editorial Product Information: Triamcinolone Acetonide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the Australian source document was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Triamcinolone acetonide (a synthetic glucocorticoid).
- **Brand Name(s):** **ARCATUS** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** S01BA05 (Corticosteroids, plain).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injection suspension; each 0.1 mL dose contains 4 mg of triamcinolone acetonide [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Suprachoroidal injection via the **SCS Microinjector™** [Australian].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arctic Vision Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Macular Oedema:** Treatment of macular oedema associated with non-infectious uveitis [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Recommended Dose:** 4 mg (0.1 mL injection suspension).
- **Frequency:** A second 4 mg dose may be administered 12 weeks after the initial dose. This decision should be based on disease activity, assessed by visual acuity or anatomical parameters [Australian].
- **Limitations:** Safety and efficacy beyond two doses have not been evaluated [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Specialist Administration:** Must be performed by an ophthalmologist trained in the suprachoroidal injection method and the use of the **SCS Microinjector™**.
- **Preparation:** The vial must be shaken vigorously for 10 seconds before use to ensure a uniform suspension. If clumping or a granular appearance persists, the product should not be used.
- **Microinjector Components:** The kit includes a 30G x 900 µm needle and a 30G x 1100 µm needle. The shorter needle is standard, but the longer needle may be used at the clinician's discretion.
- **Procedure:** The injection is performed 4–4.5 mm posterior to the limbus. The needle is inserted perpendicular to the sclera until the hub creates a "dimple" on the ocular surface. The drug is then slowly injected over 5–10 seconds.
- **Single Use:** Designed for single use in one eye only. Any residue must be discarded.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Infections:** Active or suspected ocular or periocular infections, including viral (e.g., herpes simplex, vaccinia, varicella), mycobacterial, and fungal diseases [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to triamcinolone acetonide or any excipient.
- **Mechanical/Structural:** Presence of glaucoma or retinal detachment [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Ophthalmic Effects:** Intraocular injection may cause cataracts, increased intraocular pressure (IOP), endophthalmitis, or bleeding.
- **IOP Monitoring:** Elevated IOP may persist for up to 6 months. Patients with diabetes are at higher risk for corticosteroid-induced IOP elevation and cataracts. Post-procedure monitoring for optic nerve head perfusion and tonometry is essential.
- **Infection Risk:** Corticosteroids may mask or enhance secondary infections. Use with extreme caution in patients with a history of ocular herpes simplex; contraindicated in active cases.
- **Endocrine Effects:** Systemic absorption, though minimal, carries the risk of hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis suppression, Cushing’s syndrome, and hyperglycemia, particularly with chronic use.
- **Bilateral Treatment:** Concurrent treatment of both eyes has not been studied [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects observed in clinical trials (PEACHTREE study) for the 4 mg suprachoroidal injection [Australian]:

| Adverse Reaction | Frequency (ARCATUS N=96) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Increased IOP (non-acute)** | 14% |
| **Eye pain (non-acute)** | 12% |
| **Cataract** | 7% |
| **Increased IOP (acute)** | 6% |
| **Vitreous detachment** | 5% |
| **Headache (non-ocular)** | 5% |
| **Conjunctival haemorrhage** | 4% |
| **Injection site pain** | 4% |
| **Visual acuity reduced** | 4% |
| **Photophobia** | 3% |

*Note: Acute IOP elevation is defined as occurring on the day of injection and resolving the same day.*

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Specific Studies:** Interaction studies have not been conducted with this specific suprachoroidal formulation [Australian].
- **Systemic Interactions:** While systemic exposure is low, clinicians should consider potential interactions common to corticosteroids if the patient is on concurrent systemic steroid therapy.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. No adequate studies in pregnant women. Animal studies of other ocular corticosteroids have shown teratogenicity (cleft palate, skeletal malformations). Use only if the potential benefit outweighs the risk to the fetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if suprachoroidal administration results in detectable levels in human milk. Systemic corticosteroids can suppress growth in infants. Use with caution.
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in rat studies at oral doses up to 15 μg/kg/day [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in paediatric patients [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy have been observed between elderly and younger patients [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources; however, triamcinolone is primarily metabolised in the liver.
- **Thyroid Status:** Metabolic clearance is decreased in hypothyroid patients and increased in hyperthyroid patients; adjustments to dosage may be required if systemic absorption is significant [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Triamcinolone acetonide is a synthetic glucocorticoid receptor agonist. It modulates gene expression to exert potent anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive effects.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Reduces inflammation by inhibiting the release of inflammatory mediators and reducing capillary permeability.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Suprachoroidal injection provides higher concentrations in the sclera and choroid compared to intravitreal injection.
    - **Systemic Exposure:** Plasma concentrations following a 4 mg dose were generally <100 pg/mL in clinical trials.
    - **Metabolism/Elimination:** Metabolised mainly in the liver; excreted via urine [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **PEACHTREE Study:** A pivotal, 27-week, Phase 3 randomised, sham-controlled trial (n=160).
- **Primary Endpoint:** Proportion of patients gaining ≥15 letters in Best Corrected Visual Acuity (BCVA).
- **Results:** 47% of the triamcinolone group achieved the primary endpoint compared to 16% in the control group (p < 0.01) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25 °C. 
- **Handling:** Do not freeze or refrigerate. 
- **Light:** Protect the vial from light by storing it in the original carton.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **S4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
