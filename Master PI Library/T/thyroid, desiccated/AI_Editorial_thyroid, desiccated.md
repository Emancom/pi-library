# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine Injection, synthesised from official Product Information for use by healthcare professionals.
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP). It is a carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane.
- **Chemical Class:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for intravenous injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Administered every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Monitoring:** Obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to treatment. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine (target free carnitine levels: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are supranormal [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [US].
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate risks/benefits before re-administration [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine may increase the frequency or severity of seizures in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a prior history of seizure activity [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** In patients receiving concurrent **warfarin** therapy, international normalised ratio (INR) levels should be monitored closely following the initiation of levocarnitine or dose adjustments [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD)
The following events occurred at a frequency ≥5% in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials, though causality was not necessarily established [US]:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (5–21%), headache (3–37%), injection site reaction (27–38%), pain (21–35%), fever, chest pain, and flu syndrome.
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%), arrhythmia, and palpitations.
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), nausea (5–12%), vomiting (9–21%), and anorexia.
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%), insomnia, and depression.
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea, and rhinitis.
- **Metabolic/Nutritional:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hypervolemia, and peripheral edema.

### Other Reported Effects
- **General:** Transient nausea and vomiting, body odor, and gastritis [US].
- **Serious Effects:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in INR when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. Monitoring of INR is recommended [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine is not bound to plasma proteins, reducing the likelihood of protein-binding displacement interactions [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not specified; however, animal data exists]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after exogenous administration. Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of supplementation for the mother [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders are applicable to paediatric patients [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide), which are normally excreted in urine [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis trough concentrations from ~20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg three times weekly demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8 mg/mL [US].
