# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Class:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for both acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance haemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It may be administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). Administration should be via slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Dosing by Indication
- **Acute Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **ESRD Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine levels. Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

### Monitoring
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended. Monitoring parameters should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine levels: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].
- **ESRD:** Therapy initiation may be prompted by trough plasma concentrations below the normal range of 40 to 50 µmol/L [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- None specified in available sources [US].

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal oedema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, therapy must be discontinued immediately and appropriate medical management initiated [US].
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation Note):** While this PI concerns the injectable form, it notes that chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine (both oral and intravenous) has been associated with reports of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **INR Monitoring:** Increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) has been reported when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trials Experience (ESRD Patients)
The following adverse effects were reported in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in chronic haemodialysis patients at a frequency of ≥5% (regardless of causality) [US]:

| System Organ Class | Adverse Effect | Frequency (≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **General/Injection Site** | Injection site reaction, Pain, Asthenia, Fever, Flu syndrome, Chest pain, Abdominal pain | Very Common/Common |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, Hypotension, Tachycardia | Common |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Diarrhea, Vomiting, Nausea, Dyspepsia | Common |
| **Nervous System** | Headache, Dizziness, Paresthesia, Depression | Common |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, Cough increase, Dyspnea, Rhinitis | Common |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia, Hyperkalemia, Hypervolemia | Common |
| **Skin/Appendages** | Pruritus | Common |

### Serious and Notable Adverse Effects
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (postmarketing reports) [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis and laryngeal oedema [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent reports of body odour and gastritis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Concomitant use may lead to an increase in INR. Recommendation: Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified by letter, but labeled as follows]: Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. However, there are no adequate/well-controlled human studies. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine intake must be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing and safety in paediatric patients for metabolic disorders are generally consistent with adult recommendations (see Dosage and Administration) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the source document; however, clinical monitoring is advised as for all populations.
- **Renal Impairment:** For ESRD patients, use the specific dialysis-related dosing. The safety of high-dose *oral* carnitine in renal impairment is cautioned due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, where they undergo beta-oxidation to produce energy. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. The distribution half-life is approximately 0.585 hours [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and gamma-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Bioavailability:** Absolute bioavailability of oral formulations is low, approximately 15-16% [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Pharmacokinetic Study:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on dialysis receiving 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly showed that trough levocarnitine levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks of therapy.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses in dialysis patients confirmed that plasma carnitine concentrations increase in a dose-dependent manner over 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Note:** While levocarnitine effectively increases plasma carnitine levels in ESRD, its effects on specific clinical outcomes (signs/symptoms of deficiency) in this population have not been fully determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) in PVC bags [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources (US jurisdiction does not use the Australian S-scheduling system).
