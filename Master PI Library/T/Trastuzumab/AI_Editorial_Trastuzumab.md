# Editorial Product Information: Trastuzumab deruxtecan

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information documents from the following jurisdictions: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised clinical overview of **trastuzumab deruxtecan** for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to local regulations and the current approved PI for their region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Trastuzumab deruxtecan
- **Chemical Class:** HER2-targeted antibody-drug conjugate (ADC). It consists of a humanised anti-HER2 IgG1 monoclonal antibody (trastuzumab) covalently linked to deruxtecan (a topoisomerase I inhibitor, an exatecan derivative) via a tetrapeptide-based cleavable linker.
- **Brand Name:** **ENHERTU** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** L01FD03
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Lyophilised powder for injection (100 mg single-use vial). After reconstitution, the concentration is 20 mg/mL [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AstraZeneca Pty Ltd (under license from Daiichi Sankyo) [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Trastuzumab deruxtecan** is indicated as monotherapy for the following adult populations:

- **Metastatic Breast Cancer (HER2-positive):** Unresectable or metastatic disease in patients who have received a prior anti-HER2-based regimen (specifically trastuzumab and a taxane) or developed recurrence within six months of completing (neo)adjuvant therapy [Australian].
- **Metastatic Breast Cancer (HER2-low/ultralow):** Unresectable or metastatic HER2-low (IHC 1+ or IHC 2+/ISH-negative) or HER2-ultralow (IHC 0 with membrane staining) breast cancer. Requirements include:
    - Prior chemotherapy in the metastatic setting or recurrence within 6 months of adjuvant chemotherapy.
    - For HR-positive patients, they must have received and be ineligible for further endocrine therapy [Australian].
- **Gastric Cancer:** Locally advanced or metastatic HER2-positive gastric or gastroesophageal junction (GEJ) adenocarcinoma in patients who received a prior anti-HER2-based regimen [Provisional Approval; Australian].
- **Other Solid Tumours:** Unresectable or metastatic HER2-positive (IHC 3+) solid tumours in patients with no satisfactory alternative options who have received prior systemic treatment [Provisional Approval; Australian].
- **Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC):** Unresectable or metastatic NSCLC with activating HER2 (ERBB2) mutations in patients who received prior platinum-based chemotherapy [Provisional Approval; Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Breast Cancer, NSCLC, and Solid Tumours:** 5.4 mg/kg via IV infusion once every 3 weeks (21-day cycle) until disease progression or toxicity [Australian].
- **Gastric Cancer:** 6.4 mg/kg via IV infusion once every 3 weeks (21-day cycle) [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Do not substitute:** This product must not be substituted for or with trastuzumab or trastuzumab emtansine [Australian].
- **Infusion Rate:** Initial dose over 90 minutes. If well-tolerated, subsequent doses may be given over 30 minutes. Slow or interrupt the infusion if symptoms develop.
- **Premedication:** Highly emetogenic. Premedicate with a two- or three-drug regimen (e.g., 5-HT3 antagonist, dexamethasone, and/or NK1 receptor antagonist) [Australian].

### Dose Modifications
- **General Principle:** Do not re-escalate the dose after a reduction has been made.
- **Reduction Schedule:**
    - **5.4 mg/kg starting dose:** First reduction to 4.4 mg/kg; Second reduction to 3.2 mg/kg; thereafter discontinue.
    - **6.4 mg/kg starting dose:** First reduction to 5.4 mg/kg; Second reduction to 4.4 mg/kg; thereafter discontinue [Australian].
- **Toxicity Management:** Specific modifications are required for **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD)**, **Neutropenia**, and **Left Ventricular Ejection Fraction (LVEF)** decrease. Symptomatic ILD (Grade 2+) requires permanent discontinuation [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- None specified in available sources [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD)/Pneumonitis:** [Australian: Boxed warning-style priority]. Cases can be fatal. Monitor for cough, dyspnoea, and fever. Asymptomatic (Grade 1) cases require withholding treatment and starting corticosteroids. Symptomatic cases require permanent discontinuation.
- **Neutropenia:** Grade 3/4 neutropenia is common. Monitor CBC prior to each dose. Dose interruption or reduction may be required.
- **Left Ventricular Dysfunction:** LVEF decrease has been observed. Assess LVEF prior to initiation and at regular intervals. Permanently discontinue if LVEF <40% or if symptomatic congestive heart failure occurs [Australian].
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Can cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status before starting. Advise effective contraception (Females: 7 months post-dose; Males: 4 months post-dose) [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency and Severity
- **Very Common (>10%):** Nausea (approx. 74-80%), fatigue, vomiting, alopecia, neutropenia, anaemia, constipation, decreased appetite, diarrhoea, increased transaminases, musculoskeletal pain, leukopenia, and thrombocytopenia [Australian].
- **Common (1-10%):** Dry eye, blurred vision, abdominal pain, stomatitis, dyspnoea, cough, and headache.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:**
    - **ILD/Pneumonitis:** Occurred in 11.8% (5.4 mg/kg) to 16.8% (6.4 mg/kg) of patients. Fatalities were reported in 1.0-2.3% of trial participants [Australian].
    - **Febrile Neutropenia:** Reported in 1-2.6% of patients [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Deruxtecan (the released payload) is a substrate for **P-gp**, **OATP1B1**, **BCRP**, and **MRP1**.
- **Management:** Co-administration with ritonavir (dual OATP1B/CYP3A inhibitor) or itraconazole (strong CYP3A inhibitor) did not result in clinically meaningful changes in exposure. No dose adjustments are required when given with inhibitors of these transporters or enzymes [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** The payload does not significantly inhibit or induce major CYP450 enzymes [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category D]. There are no human data, but animal studies and the mechanism of action indicate a risk of fetal harm and teratogenicity.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if the drug is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for **7 months** after the final dose [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies suggest male reproductive impairment. Advise patients to seek counselling on sperm storage prior to treatment [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in patients under 18 years [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required for patients ≥65 years.
- **Renal Impairment:** No adjustment for mild or moderate impairment. Data for severe impairment (CLcr <30 mL/min) are limited, and no recommendation can be made [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for mild impairment. No data for moderate/severe impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Following binding to HER2 on tumour cells, the ADC is internalised. Intracellular cleavage of the linker by lysosomal enzymes releases the deruxtecan payload. This causes DNA damage and apoptosis. The "bystander effect" allows the payload to reach neighbouring cells due to its membrane permeability [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Distribution:** Central volume of distribution (Vc) is ~2.68 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Payload is metabolised mainly by CYP3A4.
    - **Elimination:** Clearance is ~0.4 L/day. Elimination half-life is approximately 5.4-5.7 days [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **DESTINY-Breast03:** Showed superior PFS (HR 0.28) compared to trastuzumab emtansine in HER2+ mBC.
- **DESTINY-Breast04:** Showed significant OS and PFS benefit in HER2-low mBC compared to physician’s choice of chemotherapy.
- **DESTINY-Breast06:** Investigated use in HER2-low and HER2-ultralow breast cancer with significant PFS benefit.
- **DESTINY-Gastric01:** Demonstrated improved ORR (40.5% vs 11.3%) and OS compared to standard chemotherapy in gastric cancer [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Vials:** Store in a refrigerator (2°C to 8°C). Do not freeze. Protect from light [Australian].
- **Reconstituted/Diluted Solution:** 
    - Reconstituted solution should be used immediately. Vials may be stored refrigerated (2°C to 8°C) for up to 24 hours.
    - Diluted solution in 5% dextrose may be stored at room temperature for up to 4 hours (including prep and infusion) or refrigerated (2°C to 8°C) for up to 24 hours. Protect from light [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** [Australian Schedule 4] (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **Provisional Registration:** Indications for Gastric cancer, Solid tumours (IHC 3+), and HER2-mutant NSCLC are approved via a provisional pathway depending on the submission of further confirmatory clinical data [Australian].
