# Editorial Product Information: Talquetamab

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration) and United States (Food and Drug Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of talquetamab, synthesising information from multiple regulatory authorities into a single reference for healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Talquetamab (identified as **talquetamab-tgvs** in the [US]). It is a humanised immunoglobulin G4-proline, alanine, alanine (IgG4-PAA)-based bispecific T-cell engaging antibody.
- **Brand Name:** **TALVEY**
- **ATC Code:** L01FX29
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - 3 mg/1.5 mL (2 mg/mL) single-dose vial.
    - 40 mg/1.0 mL (40 mg/mL) single-dose vial.
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Janssen-Cilag Pty Ltd [Australian]; Janssen Biotech, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Relapsed or Refractory Multiple Myeloma (RRMM):** Indicated as monotherapy for the treatment of adult patients with RRMM who have received at least four prior lines of therapy, including a proteasome inhibitor (PI), an immunomodulatory agent (IMiD), and an anti-CD38 monoclonal antibody.
- **Regulatory Status:** This indication has received **Provisional Approval** [Australian] and **Accelerated Approval** [US] based on response rate and durability of response. Continued approval may be contingent upon verification of clinical benefit in confirmatory trials.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
Talquetamab is administered subcutaneously on either a weekly or biweekly (every 2 weeks) schedule. Dosing is based on actual body weight.

### Step-up Dosing Schedules
To reduce the risk of Cytokine Release Syndrome (CRS), a step-up dosing schedule must be followed.

| Dosing Schedule | Step-up Dose 1 (0.01 mg/kg) | Step-up Dose 2 (0.06 mg/kg) | Step-up Dose 3 (0.4 mg/kg) | First Treatment Dose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Weekly** | Day 1 | Day 3 [Australian] or Day 4 [US] | N/A | Day 5 [Australian] or Day 7 [US] (0.4 mg/kg) |
| **Biweekly** | Day 1 | Day 3 [Australian] or Day 4 [US] | Day 5 [Australian] or Day 7 [US] (0.4 mg/kg) | Day 7 [Australian] or Day 10 [US] (0.8 mg/kg) |

*Subsequent doses:* 0.4 mg/kg weekly or 0.8 mg/kg every 2 weeks.

### Administration Instructions
- **Hospitalisation:** Patients should be hospitalised for 48 hours after all doses within the step-up dosing schedule [Australian, US].
- **Pretreatment Medications:** Administer 1–3 hours before each step-up dose:
    - Corticosteroid (Dexamethasone 16 mg IV/oral).
    - Antihistamine (Diphenhydramine 50 mg or equivalent).
    - Antipyretics (Paracetamol 500–1000 mg [Australian] or Acetaminophen 650–1000 mg [US]).
- **Site of Injection:** Subcutaneous tissue of the abdomen (preferred) or thigh.

### Restarting after Delay
If a dose is delayed, restart based on the duration of the delay and the last dose administered. Delays exceeding 28 days [US] or as specified in detailed regional tables [Australian] may require repeating the step-up phase.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None.** There are no contraindications listed in the available source documents, other than hypersensitivity to the active substance or excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning: CRS and Neurologic Toxicity
- **Cytokine Release Syndrome (CRS):** Can be life-threatening or fatal. Occurred in 76–77% of patients in clinical trials.
- **Neurologic Toxicity:** Includes Immune Effector Cell-Associated Neurotoxicity Syndrome (ICANS). Can be life-threatening or fatal. Occurred in 55% [US] or 29% [Australian] of patients.

### General Precautions
- **REMS Program [US]:** Due to risks of CRS and neurologic toxicity, **TALVEY** is available only through a restricted program called the **TECVAYLI** and **TALVEY** Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy (REMS).
- **Oral Toxicity and Weight Loss:** Dysgeusia, dry mouth, dysphagia, and stomatitis are very common. 62% of patients experienced weight loss [US]. Monitor weight and refer to nutritionists if necessary.
- **Infections:** Serious, including life-threatening or fatal infections (e.g., bacterial, COVID-19, sepsis), occurred in 16–19% of patients.
- **Cytopenias:** Grade 3 or 4 neutropenia and thrombocytopenia are common. Monitor CBC at baseline and during treatment.
- **Skin Toxicity:** Rash and other skin reactions (e.g., xerosis) occurred in 62% of patients. Use of topical and/or systemic steroids is recommended.
- **Hepatotoxicity [US]:** Monitor liver enzymes and bilirubin at baseline and during treatment.
- **Vaccines:** Avoid live viral vaccines for at least 4 weeks prior to, during, and for at least 4 weeks after treatment.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Summary
- **Very Common (>10%):** CRS (77%), Dysgeusia (72%), Hypogammaglobulinaemia (67%), Nail disorder (56%), Musculoskeletal pain (48%), Anaemia (47%), Fatigue (43%), Weight decreased (40%), Rash (39%), Xerosis (32%), Neutropenia (35%), Thrombocytopenia (30%), Upper respiratory tract infection (29%), Lymphopenia (27%), Diarrhoea (25%).
- **Common (1–10%):** ICANS (9.8% [Australian]), Sepsis (4.4%), Pneumonia (6.8%).

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Fatalities:** Reported in 3.2% of patients, primarily due to COVID-19, dyspnoea, and bacterial infections [US].
- **Discontinuation:** Most common cause for permanent discontinuation was ICANS (1.1%) and weight loss (0.9%).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No formal drug-drug interaction studies have been conducted.
- **CYP Enzyme Suppression:** Talquetamab causes cytokine release, which may suppress CYP450 enzymes. The risk is highest during the step-up phase and during CRS episodes. 
- **Management:** Monitor for toxicity or concentrations of drugs with narrow therapeutic indices that are CYP substrates (e.g., CYP3A4, CYP2C9). Adjust doses as needed [Australian, US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:**
    - **[Australian]: Category C.** Human IgG is known to cross the placenta; talquetamab may cause fetal harm.
    - **[US]:** Based on the mechanism of action, it may cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to initiation.
- **Contraception:** Females of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for 3 months after the last dose. [US] also recommends this for males with female partners of reproductive potential [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Discontinue breastfeeding during treatment and for at least 3 months after the last dose.
- **Fertility:** No data available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients aged 17 years and younger.
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed between patients 65–74 years and younger patients. However, those $\ge$ 75 years may experience higher rates of fatal adverse reactions [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is recommended for mild or moderate impairment. No data for severe renal impairment.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild or moderate impairment [Australian; US notes moderate impairment did not influence PK]. No data for severe hepatic impairment.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A bispecific T-cell engaging antibody that binds to the CD3 receptor on T cells and the G protein-coupled receptor family C group 5 member D (GPRC5D) on multiple myeloma cells. This leads to T-cell activation and subsequent lysis of GPRC5D-expressing malignant cells.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Bioavailability:** Approximately 59–62% after subcutaneous administration.
    - **Steady State:** Achieved at approximately 16 weeks.
    - **Elimination:** Clearance decreases over time. Median terminal half-life is 12.2 days at steady state.
    - **Metabolism:** Expected to be catabolized into small peptides.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **MonumenTAL-1 Study:** A single-arm, open-label trial in RRMM patients. 
    - **0.4 mg/kg Weekly (N=117):** Overall Response Rate (ORR) 71.8%; Median Duration of Response (DOR) 10.8 months [Australian].
    - **0.8 mg/kg Biweekly (N=113):** ORR 69.0%; Median DOR not estimable [Australian].
    - Results were consistent across subgroups, including those with prior CAR-T or bispecific antibody therapy.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C.
- **Protection:** Keep in original carton to protect from light.
- **Handling:** Do not freeze. Equilibrate to ambient temperature (15°C to 30°C) for at least 15 minutes before use.
- **Shelf Life (Prepared Syringe):** Administer immediately. If necessary, store refrigerated (2°C to 8°C) for up to 24 hours, followed by up to 24 hours at room temperature.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australian Poison Schedule:** S4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **US Legal Classification:** Rx only.
