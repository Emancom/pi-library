# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory information into a single, clinically focused reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of this report, only the United States source document was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative; (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn metabolic defects [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Severe Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the next 24 hours. Administration should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and never less frequently than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilized based on clinical requirements [US].
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Monitor weekly and monthly, targeting a plasma free carnitine concentration between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration Timing:** Administer as a slow bolus (2–3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Adjustments:** Dosage should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels (target: 40–50 µmol/L). Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of treatment [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known:** There are no listed contraindications for levocarnitine in the provided source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring:** In ESRD patients, chronic high-dose oral administration (not applicable to this IV formulation) is cautioned due to metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO); however, for IV use, monitoring should focus on plasma carnitine levels and clinical response [US].
- **Administration Safety:** Inspect parenteral products for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disorders.

| Frequency | System/Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site reaction (33–59%), Pain (30–49%), Headache (22%), Hypertension (20%), Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%), Nausea (8–12%), Pharyngitis (23%), Fever (7–12%) [US]. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Abdominal pain, Asthenia, Chest pain, Flu syndrome, Infection, Arrhythmia, Hypotension, Tachycardia, Hypercalcemia, Hyperkalemia, Peripheral edema, Dizziness, Insomnia, Dyspnea, Rhinitis, Pruritus [US]. |
| **Rare (<0.1%) / Post-marketing** | Seizures, Anaphylaxis, Laryngeal edema, Bronchospasm [US]. |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** 
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (regardless of prior history).
- **Immune:** Anaphylactic shock and severe respiratory distress.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned a letter in the text, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm.
    - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after administration.
- **Recommendation:** The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant should be weighed against the benefit of supplementation to the mother. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy are supported by use in metabolic disorders and ESRD; dosing is weight-based (see Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically in ESRD patients on dialysis. Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency, and chronic high doses may lead to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels) [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine clears toxic acyl-CoA compounds by forming acylcarnitines, which are rapidly excreted [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses post-dialysis increased mean plasma levocarnitine from 20 µmol/L (pre-dialysis) to troughs of 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed dose-dependent increases in trough concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks.
- **Note:** While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations is proven, the effect on specific clinical outcomes in ESRD patients has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservative [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction

- **Other Jurisdictions:** Not specified in available sources.
