# Editorial Product Information: tramadol + paracetamol

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **tramadol + paracetamol**
- Chemical class:
  - Tramadol: opioid agonist analgesic (centrally acting)
  - Paracetamol: non-opioid analgesic/antipyretic
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 3. Approved Indications
- Short-term management of severe pain when other treatment options (including non-opioid analgesics) have failed, are contraindicated, not tolerated or are otherwise inadequate to provide appropriate pain control.
- Use beyond short-term therapy or for chronic non-cancer pain is not routinely recommended; if long-term use is considered it should be part of a multidisciplinary, multimodal pain management plan and require regular reassessment.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adults and adolescents ≥ 12 years)
- Initial recommended dose: two tablets (equivalent to 75 mg tramadol hydrochloride + 650 mg paracetamol).
- Additional doses may be taken as needed, but do not exceed 8 tablets per day (equivalent to 300 mg tramadol + 2,600 mg paracetamol).
- Minimum dosing interval: not less than 6 hours.
- Patients weighing 37.5–50 kg: maximum 6 tablets per day.
- Patients weighing < 37.5 kg: not to receive tramadol + paracetamol.
- The lowest effective dose for analgesia should be used.

### Dosing by indication
- Short-term severe pain: use as above. No separate approved dosing regimens for specific pain syndromes are specified in available sources.

### Administration instructions
- Oral use only.
- Tablets must be swallowed whole with adequate fluid; must not be broken or chewed.

### Titration and duration
- Use for the shortest duration necessary. If repeated or long-term therapy is required, perform careful, regular monitoring and include treatment breaks where possible.
- When initiating opioid therapy, start at the lower end of recommended doses and titrate carefully to clinical effect.

### Missed dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
List of contraindications (all items present in available sources):
- Hypersensitivity to tramadol, paracetamol or any excipients of the product.
- Acute intoxication with alcohol, hypnotic drugs, centrally acting analgesics, opioids or psychotropic drugs.
- Concomitant use with monoamine oxidase (MAO) inhibitors or within two weeks of MAOI withdrawal.
- Severe hepatocellular insufficiency.
- Hepatic failure or decompensated active liver disease.
- Severe respiratory disease, acute respiratory disease or respiratory depression.
- Epilepsy not controlled by treatment.
- Use in patients weighing less than 37.5 kg (contraindicated by weight threshold).
- Concomitant use of opioid agonist–antagonists (e.g. buprenorphine, pentazocine) is not recommended (risk of reduced analgesia and precipitating withdrawal).

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed/Prominent warnings
- **Risk of hazardous and harmful use (abuse, misuse, addiction):** tramadol-containing products may be abused, misused and may lead to addiction, overdose and death. Assess risk prior to prescribing and monitor regularly.
- **Life‑threatening respiratory depression:** may occur at recommended doses, particularly at initiation or dose increases, and in vulnerable populations (elderly, frail, renal/hepatic impairment, respiratory disease).
- **Dangerous interactions with benzodiazepines and other CNS depressants, including alcohol:** concomitant use may cause profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma and death; use only if no alternatives and with lowest effective doses and close monitoring.

### General warnings and precautions
- Hazardous and harmful use: screen for personal/family history of substance abuse or mental illness; prescribe smallest appropriate quantity; counsel on safe storage and disposal; advise against sharing medication.
- Respiratory depression: monitor patients (especially during initiation and escalation). Avoid use in patients with severe respiratory disease.
- Concomitant CNS depressants: use caution or avoid concomitant prescribing where possible (see Section 8). Warn patients not to consume alcohol.
- Chronic non-cancer pain: routine long-term use not recommended; consider opioids only after non-pharmacologic and non-opioid options have been maximised and as part of multidisciplinary care; set realistic goals and stop if no benefit.
- Tolerance, dependence and withdrawal: tolerance and physical dependence can develop after days to weeks; do not cease abruptly — taper gradually. Withdrawal symptoms and their common manifestations are described under Adverse Effects.
- Accidental ingestion/exposure: can be fatal, particularly in children; advise safe storage and disposal.
- Hyperalgesia: opioid-induced hyperalgesia can occur (increased pain or diffuse sensitivity); consider dose reduction, tapering or opioid rotation.
- Ceasing opioids: individualise taper; generally reduce dose by 10–25% every 2–4 weeks, adjusting for withdrawal/pain; consider specialist referral or medication-assisted treatment for opioid use disorder.
- Maximum daily dose: do not exceed 8 tablets/day (300 mg tramadol + 2,600 mg paracetamol). Advise patients not to use additional paracetamol- or tramadol-containing products.
- Hepatic impairment: both active ingredients are extensively metabolised by the liver; elimination of tramadol delayed in hepatic impairment — consider prolonging dosing interval. Do not use in severe hepatic impairment. In chronic/active hepatic disease/parenchymal insufficiency, paracetamol dose should not exceed 3 g/day.
- Renal impairment: elimination of tramadol is delayed in renal insufficiency — consider prolonging dosing interval.
- Sleep-related breathing disorders: opioids can cause central sleep apnoea and sleep-related hypoxaemia; risk increases with dose; consider dose reduction if central sleep apnoea develops.
- Opioid-dependent patients: tramadol is not recommended as a substitute to treat opioid dependence and may precipitate withdrawal.
- Glutathione depletion / pyroglutamic acidosis: use with caution in patients with glutathione-depleted states (e.g. severe renal or hepatic impairment, sepsis, malnutrition); co-administration with isoxazolyl penicillins (e.g. flucloxacillin) may increase risk of pyroglutamic (5-oxoprolinemic) metabolic acidosis, particularly with prolonged high-dose paracetamol.
- Increased intracranial pressure/head trauma/shock/reduced consciousness: use with caution; tramadol-induced miosis may obscure intracranial pathology.
- Seizure risk: tramadol may provoke convulsions in susceptible patients or when co-administered with medications that lower seizure threshold (SSRIs, tricyclic antidepressants, antipsychotics, other centrally acting analgesics, local anaesthetics). Use only if compelling circumstances in patients with epilepsy or predisposition to seizures. Risk increases with higher than recommended tramadol doses.
- Use during anaesthesia: cases reported of increased intra-operative recall with tramadol during anaesthesia with enflurane and nitrous oxide; avoid use during light anaesthetic planes.
- Use in elderly: patients up to 75 years without manifest hepatic/renal impairment usually require no dose change. In those >75 years elimination may be prolonged; extend dosing interval as required.
- Paediatric use: safety and efficacy not established in children <12 years; not recommended in this population or in children <37.5 kg.
- Effects on laboratory tests: Not specified in available sources beyond clinical cautions (e.g. hepatic monitoring in liver disease).

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor for signs of respiratory depression, sedation, misuse/abuse, constipation and overdose.
- In patients with hepatic impairment or chronic liver disease monitor hepatic function; avoid exceeding paracetamol limits.
- Monitor seizure-prone patients closely if treated with tramadol.
- Monitor for signs of metabolic acidosis (including pyroglutamic acidosis) in at-risk patients receiving prolonged high-dose paracetamol.

## 7. Adverse Effects
- The available sources include multiple adverse reactions and symptom lists; frequency information for specific adverse events is not specified in the available sources. Where frequency is required it is stated as "Not specified in available sources."

- Serious adverse effects highlighted: respiratory depression (including fatal cases), severe hepatic injury/failure (with paracetamol overdose or concomitant paracetamol-containing products), seizures/convulsions, serotonin syndrome, severe hypersensitivity, addiction/abuse/overdose, pyroglutamic metabolic acidosis (rare), central sleep apnoea/hypoxaemia, coma, and death from misuse/overdose.

- Withdrawal symptoms (may occur on abrupt cessation or rapid dose reduction; described in available sources):

  - Less frequently reported with tramadol/paracetamol discontinuation: panic attacks, severe anxiety and paresthesias.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Concomitant use that is contraindicated:
  - Monoamine oxidase (MAO) inhibitors (non-selective and selective MAO inhibitors): risk of life‑threatening serotoninergic/central excitation syndrome; avoid use and allow at least a 2-week washout after MAOI discontinuation before starting tramadol.

- Clinically significant interactions (presented with management notes)
  - Serotonergic drugs (e.g. SSRIs, SNRIs, tricyclic antidepressants, mirtazapine): concomitant use may precipitate serotonin syndrome; monitor closely for signs of serotonin toxicity and manage by stopping serotonergic agents and providing supportive care as needed.
  - Benzodiazepines and other CNS depressants (including alcohol, gabapentinoids, sedatives, hypnotics, antipsychotics, centrally-acting antiemetics, antihistamines, cannabis, other opioid analgesics): combined use may cause profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma and death; reserve co-prescribing for patients without alternatives, use lowest effective doses and shortest duration, and monitor closely for respiratory depression and sedation. Counsel patients to avoid alcohol.
  - Carbamazepine and other enzyme inducers: may reduce plasma concentrations of tramadol and shorten its duration of effect; monitor efficacy and consider dose adjustment or alternative analgesia.
  - Opioid agonist–antagonists (e.g. buprenorphine, pentazocine): may decrease analgesic effect of tramadol by competitive receptor effects and may precipitate withdrawal in opioid-dependent patients; avoid concomitant use.
  - Anaesthetics / alcohol: high doses of tramadol with anaesthetic agents or alcohol may increase risk of respiratory depression; avoid combination where possible and monitor.
  - Isoxazolyl penicillins (e.g. flucloxacillin): caution advised because concomitant use with paracetamol may increase the risk of pyroglutamic metabolic acidosis (PGA/HAGMA), especially with prolonged high paracetamol doses or in patients with glutathione depletion.
  - Other interactions noted conceptually: many classes that lower seizure threshold (e.g. SSRIs, tricyclics, antipsychotics, other centrally acting analgesics) may increase risk of convulsions when used with tramadol—use caution.

- Pharmacokinetic interactions:
  - Tramadol and paracetamol are extensively metabolised by the liver; enzyme inducers (e.g. carbamazepine) may reduce tramadol exposure. Specific CYP enzymes, inhibitor interactions and quantitative adjustment guidance are not specified in the available sources.

- Pharmacodynamic interactions:
  - Additive CNS and respiratory depression with other depressant agents; additive serotonergic effects with serotonergic agents leading to serotonin syndrome; additive seizure risk with medicines that lower seizure threshold.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
- Paediatric use:
  - Not recommended in children < 12 years of age or in those weighing < 37.5 kg. Safety and efficacy not established in children < 12 years.
  - Adolescents ≥ 12 years may be treated according to adult dosing recommendations, with weight-based limitations as noted.
- Elderly:
  - No routine dose adjustment needed up to age 75 in the absence of hepatic or renal impairment. In patients over 75 years elimination may be prolonged—extend dosing interval as clinically required and monitor.
- Renal impairment:
  - Elimination of tramadol is delayed in renal insufficiency. Prolongation of dosing intervals should be considered; specific eGFR/stage-based dosing adjustments are not provided in the available sources.
- Hepatic impairment:
  - Both tramadol and paracetamol are extensively metabolised by the liver. In hepatic impairment tramadol elimination is delayed — consider prolonging dosing interval and individualising dose. Do not use in severe hepatic impairment or decompensated active liver disease. In patients with chronic/active hepatic disease (hepatocellular insufficiency), paracetamol daily dose should not exceed 3 g/day.
- Patients with glutathione depletion:
  - Use with caution in patients with conditions associated with glutathione depletion (severe renal or hepatic impairment, sepsis, malnutrition) due to increased risk of metabolic acidosis; monitor and avoid prolonged high-dose paracetamol where practicable.
