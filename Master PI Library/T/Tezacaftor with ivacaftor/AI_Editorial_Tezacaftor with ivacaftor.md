# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinical decisions should be made in conjunction with local regulatory guidelines.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP); a carrier molecule involved in mitochondrial fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn metabolic errors leading to secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Severe Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically range from 50 mg/kg/day or as clinically required. Maximum reported dose is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of treatment [US].

### General Instructions
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Monitor weekly and monthly, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Preparation:** Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known:** There are no listed contraindications for levocarnitine injection in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Discontinue immediately if a reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Carnitine Monitoring:** Therapy should be guided by plasma carnitine concentrations and overall clinical response [US].
- **Oral Formulation Caution:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been established in renal insufficiency. High oral doses in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disorders.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site reaction (33–59%), pain (30–49%), hypertension (18–21%), diarrhea (9–35%), headache (3–37%), pharyngitis (15–33%), nausea (5–12%), vomiting (9–21%) [US]. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Abdominal pain, fever, chest pain, asthenia, tachycardia, hypercalcemia, dizziness, dyspnea, pruritus [US]. |
| **Neurologic** | Seizures (frequency not specified) [US]. |

*Note: In ESRD trials, the frequency of some events (e.g., flu syndrome) was high in both placebo and treatment groups.*

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) has been reported when co-administered with levocarnitine. **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned; modern labeling style]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits using 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies in humans are unavailable. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine should be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Refer to the "Dosage and Administration" section; weight-based dosing (50 mg/kg) is used for metabolic disorders [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Not specifically detailed in provided sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **IV Administration:** Indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis (see Section 3).
    - **Oral Administration Caution:** Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to accumulation of TMA and TMAO [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US]. 
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with metabolic errors, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic acids by forming acylcarnitine complexes, which are rapidly eliminated [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L by week 8.
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** A study comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses three times weekly showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) when stored at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservative [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** 
    - **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription only) [US].
