# Editorial Product Information: Ticlopidine Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial product information has been synthesised from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This synthesis serves as a professional reference and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing and dispensing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of this editorial, only US-based documentation was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ticlopidine hydrochloride (a thienopyridine platelet aggregation inhibitor).
- **Brand Name(s):** Ticlopidine Hydrochloride Tablets USP (formerly marketed as Ticlid).
- **ATC Code:** B01AC05.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 250 mg film-coated, oval, white to off-white tablets [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Carilion Materials Management; Teva Pharmaceuticals USA [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Stroke Risk Reduction:** To reduce the risk of thrombotic stroke (fatal or nonfatal) in patients who have experienced stroke precursors (e.g., TIA, amaurosis fugax) or who have had a completed thrombotic stroke. Due to the risk of life-threatening hematological adverse reactions, use is reserved for patients who are intolerant/allergic to aspirin or have failed aspirin therapy [US].
- **Coronary Artery Stenting:** Used as adjunctive therapy with aspirin to reduce the incidence of subacute stent thrombosis in patients following successful coronary stent implantation [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:**
  - **Stroke:** 250 mg twice daily [US].
  - **Coronary Stenting:** 250 mg twice daily plus aspirin (100 mg BID to 325 mg daily) for up to 30 days following successful stent placement [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** Must be taken with food to maximise gastrointestinal tolerance and increase bioavailability (AUC increases by approximately 20% postprandially) [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specifically addressed in source; standard clinical practice suggests taking as soon as remembered unless the next dose is imminent.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to ticlopidine or any excipients [US].
- Presence of hematopoietic disorders (e.g., neutropenia, thrombocytopenia) [US].
- History of thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP) or aplastic anemia [US].
- Active pathological bleeding (e.g., bleeding peptic ulcer or intracranial hemorrhage) [US].
- Severe hepatic impairment [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [US]:** **Life-threatening hematological reactions.** Ticlopidine can cause severe neutropenia/agranulocytosis, TTP, and aplastic anemia. 
  - **Neutropenia:** Incidence of 2.4% in stroke trials (0.8% severe, <450/mm³). Peak incidence at 4–6 weeks.
  - **TTP:** Estimated incidence as high as 1 in 2,000 to 4,000 patients. Peak incidence at 3–4 weeks.
  - **Aplastic Anemia:** Estimated incidence of 1 in 4,000 to 8,000 patients. Peak incidence at 4–8 weeks.
- **Monitoring Requirements:** A Complete Blood Count (CBC) with differential and platelet count is mandatory every 2 weeks from the start of therapy through the end of the third month. Monitoring must continue for 2 weeks after early discontinuation due to the drug’s long half-life [US].
- **Haemorrhage:** Ticlopidine prolongs bleeding time. Use with caution in patients at risk of increased bleeding from trauma or surgery. Discontinue 10–14 days prior to elective surgery if antiplatelet effect is not desired [US].
- **Hepatic Effects:** Associated with elevations in alkaline phosphatase, bilirubin, and transaminases (typically within the first 4 months). Discontinue if significant hepatitis or jaundice occurs [US].
- **Cholesterol:** Therapy may increase serum total cholesterol and triglycerides by 8–10% within one month [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Approximately 60% of patients report adverse effects, with roughly 21% discontinuing therapy due to these events [US].

| Event | Ticlopidine Incidence (%) | Aspirin Incidence (%) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| Diarrhea | 12.5 | 5.2 |
| Nausea | 7.0 | 6.2 |
| Dyspepsia | 7.0 | 9.0 |
| Rash | 5.1 | 1.5 |
| GI Pain | 3.7 | 5.6 |
| Neutropenia | 2.4 | 0.8 |
| Purpura | 2.2 | 1.6 |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Agranulocytosis, pancytopenia, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, exfoliative dermatitis, anaphylaxis, and nephrotic syndrome [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
  - **Cimetidine:** Chronic administration reduces ticlopidine clearance by 50% [US].
  - **Antacids:** Reduce ticlopidine plasma levels by 18% [US].
  - **Theophylline:** Increases theophylline half-life from 8.6 to 12.2 hours; monitor theophylline levels [US].
  - **Antipyrine:** 30% increase in antipyrine half-life; expect similar effects on other drugs metabolised by hepatic microsomal enzymes [US].
  - **Phenytoin:** Rare reports of elevated phenytoin levels and toxicity; monitor phenytoin concentrations [US].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
  - **Aspirin/NSAIDs:** Ticlopidine potentiates the effect of aspirin on collagen-induced platelet aggregation. Long-term co-administration (beyond 30 days for stents) is not recommended [US].
  - **Digoxin:** Slight decrease (~15%) in digoxin levels; typically not clinically significant [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category B]. Animal studies showed no evidence of teratogenicity at doses many times the human clinical dose. However, no adequate studies exist in pregnant women. Use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Excreted in rat milk; human excretion is unknown. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in infants, ticlopidine is not recommended for nursing mothers [US].
- **Fertility:** No effect on fertility observed in rat models at oral doses up to 400 mg/kg/day [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clearance decreases with age; steady-state trough levels in patients ~70 years old are twice those of younger populations. No overall difference in safety/efficacy was found in clinical trials, but increased sensitivity in some individuals cannot be ruled out [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Plasma clearance decreases (37–52%) and AUC increases (28–60%) in mild-to-moderate impairment. Bleeding time may be significantly prolonged in moderately impaired patients [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Higher plasma concentrations observed in patients with advanced cirrhosis. Contraindicated in severe liver disease [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Irreversibly interferes with platelet membrane function by inhibiting ADP-induced platelet-fibrinogen binding and subsequent platelet-platelet interactions. This effect lasts for the remaining lifespan of the platelet [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** >80% absorbed; peak plasma levels in ~2 hours [US].
  - **Distribution:** 98% reversibly bound to plasma proteins (albumin and lipoproteins) [US].
  - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised by the liver. One of the many metabolites is responsible for activity, but it has not been fully isolated [US].
  - **Elimination:** 60% recovered in urine, 23% in feces. Terminal elimination half-life is 4–5 days at steady state [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **TASS (Ticlopidine Aspirin Stroke Study):** Comparison of ticlopidine (250 mg BID) vs. aspirin (650 mg BID) in 3,069 patients. Ticlopidine showed a 24% reduction in risk for fatal/nonfatal stroke (p=0.011) over 5 years [US].
- **CATS (Canadian American Ticlopidine Study):** Comparison of ticlopidine (250 mg BID) vs. placebo in 1,073 patients with a previous atherothrombotic stroke. Ticlopidine reduced the overall risk of stroke by 24% (p=0.017) [US].
- **STARS (Stent Anticoagulation Restenosis Study):** Demonstrated that ticlopidine + aspirin was superior to aspirin alone or aspirin + coumadin in reducing stent thrombosis (0.5% vs. 3.6% and 2.7% respectively; p<0.001) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container [US].
- **Note:** Standard manufacturer settings apply (controlled room temperature) although specific degree ranges were not detailed in the source document [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only (Prescription Only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources (N/A for US jurisdiction).
