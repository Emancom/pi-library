# Editorial Product Information: Tolterodine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based solely on the official Product Information (PI) from the **Australian (TGA)** regulatory authority. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, intended to provide a clear, clinically accurate reference for the use of tolterodine tartrate.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing in any particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tolterodine tartrate, a competitive muscarinic receptor antagonist.
- **Brand Names:** **Detrusitol** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** G04BD07.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Film-coated tablets:** 1 mg (white, round, biconvex, engraved with “TO”) [Australian].
    - **Film-coated tablets:** 2 mg (white, round, biconvex, engraved with “DT”) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Viatris Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Overactive Bladder:** Treatment of patients with overactive bladder manifesting as symptoms of urinary frequency, urgency, or urge incontinence, or any combination thereof [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dose:** The standard recommended dose is **2 mg twice daily**.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Depending on individual response and tolerability, the dose may be reduced to **1 mg twice daily**.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** In patients with significantly reduced hepatic function, the dose must not exceed **1 mg twice daily**.
- **Renal Impairment:** In patients with severely impaired renal function (GFR ≤ 30 mL/min), the dose must not exceed **1 mg twice daily**.
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets are administered orally. Food intake increases bioavailability but does not require dose adjustment.
- **Treatment Review:** The necessity for continued treatment should be evaluated after 6 months of therapy.

## 4. Contraindications
Tolterodine is contraindicated in patients with:
- Urinary retention.
- Gastric retention.
- Uncontrolled narrow-angle glaucoma.
- Myasthenia gravis.
- Severe ulcerative colitis or toxic megacolon.
- Known hypersensitivity to tolterodine or any excipients.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Bladder Outflow Obstruction:** Use with caution due to the risk of precipitating urinary retention.
- **Gastrointestinal Disorders:** Exercise caution in patients with obstructive GI disorders (e.g., pyloric stenosis), autonomic neuropathy, hiatus hernia, or conditions associated with decreased GI motility.
- **Cardiac Function (QT Prolongation):** Caution is advised in patients with risk factors for QT prolongation, including electrolyte imbalances (hypokalaemia, hypomagnesaemia, hypocalcaemia), bradycardia, congenital or acquired QT prolongation, or concurrent use of QT-prolonging medications. It should be used with caution in patients with significant pre-existing cardiac disease (e.g., heart failure, ischaemia).
- **Narrow-Angle Glaucoma:** Use with caution in patients receiving treatment for narrow-angle glaucoma.
- **Central Nervous System Effects:** Patients should be informed that tolterodine may cause dizziness or blurred vision, which may impair the ability to drive or operate machinery.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are primarily related to the antimuscarinic pharmacological action of the drug.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Dry mouth (up to 39.6% at 2mg dose), headache. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Constipation, abdominal pain, dyspepsia, nausea, diarrhea, dizziness, vertigo, xerophthalmia (dry eyes), abnormal vision (accommodation disturbances), fatigue, chest pain, dysuria, UTI. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Memory impairment, gastro-oesophageal reflux, flushed skin, oedema, palpitations, paraesthesia. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Tachycardia, cardiac failure, convulsions, hallucinations, anaphylactoid reactions (including angioedema). |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Angioedema and anaphylactoid reactions (rare); cardiac failure (rare); and worsening of dementia symptoms (confusion, disorientation, delusion) in patients taking cholinesterase inhibitors.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Concomitant use with **potent CYP3A4 inhibitors** (e.g., macrolide antibiotics like erythromycin/clarithromycin, azole antifungals like ketoconazole/itraconazole, and protease inhibitors) is **not recommended** as they significantly increase serum tolterodine concentrations [Australian].
- **CYP2D6 Inhibitors:** Fluoxetine (a potent CYP2D6 inhibitor) increases tolterodine AUC but does not require dose adjustment because the total active moiety (tolterodine + DD01 metabolite) remains relatively stable.
- **Anticholinergics:** Concurrent use of other drugs with anticholinergic properties may potentiate therapeutic effects and adverse reactions.
- **Muscarinic Agonists:** May reduce the therapeutic effect of tolterodine.
- **Prokinetics:** Tolterodine may decrease the effect of prokinetic agents such as metoclopramide or cisapride.
- **Warfarin/Oral Contraceptives:** No clinically significant interactions have been observed.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. Animal studies have shown embryotoxicity and fetal abnormalities at high doses. There are no adequate studies in pregnant women; use only if the potential benefit justifies the risk to the fetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if tolterodine is excreted in human milk. Administration is not recommended during nursing [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no adverse effects on fertility at exposures significantly higher than human therapeutic levels.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established in the paediatric population.
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness have been observed between older (up to 91 years) and younger patients; no dose adjustment is routinely required [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Limit dose to 1 mg twice daily in severe impairment (GFR ≤ 30 mL/min).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Limit dose to 1 mg twice daily in patients with significant hepatic dysfunction.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Tolterodine is a competitive, specific muscarinic receptor antagonist with a high affinity for receptors mediating bladder contraction and salivation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; peak serum concentrations in 1–2 hours. Bioavailability is increased by food.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised by the liver via **CYP2D6** to the pharmacologically active 5-hydroxymethyl metabolite (**DD01**). In "poor metabolisers" (devoid of CYP2D6), metabolism occurs via **CYP3A4**.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily excreted via hepatic metabolism; 77% recovered in urine (mostly as metabolites) and 17% in faeces. The half-life of tolterodine is approximately 2–3 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- In Phase III, 12-week controlled studies, tolterodine (2 mg twice daily) was significantly more effective than placebo in reducing the number of micturitions per 24 hours and increasing the mean volume voided per micturition. Pooled analysis also demonstrated a significant reduction in incontinence episodes compared to placebo. Therapeutic effects were maintained for up to 12 months in long-term follow-up.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C [Australian].
- Shelf life is specified on the external packaging.
