# Editorial Product Information: Tolazamide

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: US (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of tolazamide for use by healthcare professionals, synthesising information from official product documentation.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tolazamide (a sulfonylurea oral hypoglycemic agent).
- **Brand Names:** Available as generic formulations (e.g., Mutual Pharmaceutical Company, Inc.) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A10BB05.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Oral scored white tablets: 100 mg, 250 mg, and 500 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Mutual Pharmaceutical Company, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus:** Indicated as an adjunct to diet to lower blood glucose in patients with non-insulin-dependent diabetes mellitus (Type II) whose hyperglycemia cannot be adequately controlled by diet alone [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
There is no fixed dosage regimen. The dose must be individualised based on periodic blood and urinary glucose monitoring to identify the minimum effective dose [US].

### Dosing by Indication (Type 2 Diabetes)
- **Usual Starting Dose:** 100 mg to 250 mg daily, administered with breakfast or the first main meal [US].
- **Initial Dose by Glycemic Status:** 
    - Fasting blood glucose <200 mg/dL: 100 mg/day as a single dose [US].
    - Fasting blood glucose >200 mg/dL: 250 mg/day as a single dose [US].
- **Initial Dose (Vulnerable Populations):** 100 mg once daily for malnourished, underweight, or elderly patients [US].
- **Maintenance Dose:** Typically 100 mg to 1000 mg daily. The average range is 250 mg to 500 mg daily [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** 1000 mg per day. Doses higher than this are generally not associated with further clinical response [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Doses up to 500 mg/day should be administered as a single morning dose [US].
- Doses exceeding 500 mg/day should be divided and administered twice daily [US].

### Titration Schedule
- Adjustments should be made in increments of 100 mg to 250 mg at weekly intervals based on the patient's blood glucose response [US].

### Transfer from Other Therapies
- **From other sulfonylureas (except chlorpropamide):** No transition period or priming dose is usually required [US].
- **From chlorpropamide:** Exercise particular care to avoid hypoglycemia during the 1-2 week transition due to chlorpropamide's prolonged half-life [US].
- **From Insulin:**
    - Insulin <20 units: Substitute 100 mg tolazamide daily [US].
    - Insulin 20–40 units: Start 250 mg tolazamide daily as a single dose [US].
    - Insulin >40 units: Reduce insulin dose by 50% and start 250 mg tolazamide daily; adjust based on response [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity or allergy to tolazamide [US].
- Diabetic ketoacidosis (with or without coma); this condition requires insulin therapy [US].
- Type 1 diabetes mellitus, as sole therapy [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiovascular Mortality:** The administration of oral hypoglycemic drugs (specifically tolbutamide) has been reported in a long-term prospective study (UGDP) to be associated with a 2.5-fold increase in cardiovascular mortality compared to diet alone. This is considered a class-wide concern for sulfonylureas, including tolazamide [US].
- **Hypoglycemia:** All sulfonylureas can produce severe hypoglycemia. Risk is increased by renal/hepatic insufficiency, malnutrition, adrenal or pituitary insufficiency, and strenuous exercise [US].
- **Loss of Control of Blood Glucose:** Stressors such as fever, trauma, infection, or surgery may cause a loss of glycemic control, necessitating the temporary discontinuation of tolazamide and the administration of insulin [US].
- **Secondary Failure:** The effectiveness of the drug may decrease over time due to disease progression or diminished responsiveness [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- Periodic monitoring of blood and urine glucose [US].
- Periodic measurement of glycosylated hemoglobin (HbA1c) [US].
- Assessment of renal function, particularly in elderly patients [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Tolazamide is generally well-tolerated, with only 2.1% of patients in clinical trials discontinuing therapy due to side effects [US].

| System Organ Class | Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Common (1%) | Nausea, epigastric fullness, heartburn. |
| **Dermatologic** | Uncommon (0.4%) | Pruritus, erythema, urticaria, morbilliform or maculopapular eruptions. |
| **Hepatic** | Rare | Cholestatic jaundice (requires discontinuation). |
| **Metabolic** | Rare/Very Rare | Disulfiram-like reactions, hyponatremia/SIADH (class effect). |
| **Neurological** | Infrequent | Weakness, fatigue, dizziness, vertigo, malaise, headache. |
| **Hematologic** | Not specified* | Leukopenia, agranulocytosis, thrombocytopenia, hemolytic anemia, aplastic anemia, pancytopenia (reported with sulfonylureas). |

*\*Reported as class effects; specific frequencies for tolazamide were not listed in the source.*

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Potentiation of Hypoglycemic Action
The following may increase the risk of hypoglycemia:
- NSAIDs, salicylates, sulfonamides, chloramphenicol, probenecid, coumarins, MAOIs, and beta-adrenergic blockers [US].
- **Miconazole:** Oral miconazole significantly interacts with oral hypoglycemics to cause severe hypoglycemia [US].

### Antagonism of Hypoglycemic Action
The following may lead to loss of glycemic control (hyperglycemia):
- Thiazides and other diuretics, corticosteroids, phenothiazines, thyroid products, estrogens, oral contraceptives, phenytoin, nicotinic acid, sympathomimetics, calcium channel blockers, and isoniazid [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category C.** Animal studies show decreased litter size and increased perinatal mortality at 10 times the human dose. There are no adequate studies in pregnant women [US].
    - Tolazamide is not recommended for use during pregnancy; insulin is preferred for maintaining normal blood glucose [US].
    - If used, it must be discontinued at least two weeks before the expected delivery date to avoid prolonged neonatal hypoglycemia [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if tolazamide is excreted in human milk, though some sulfonylureas are. Due to the risk of infant hypoglycemia, a decision should be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of carcinogenicity was found in rat/mouse bioassays over 103 weeks [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in children have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased susceptibility to hypoglycemia, which may be difficult to recognise. Initial and maintenance dosing should be conservative. Assessment of renal function is essential [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Increased risk of elevated drug levels and diminished gluconeogenic capacity, leading to severe hypoglycemia. Dosing should be conservative [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Primarily stimulates insulin release from functioning pancreatic beta cells. Long-term administration may involve extrapancreatic effects and persists despite a decline in the insulin secretory response [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Produces a mild diuresis by enhancing renal free water clearance [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly and well absorbed from the GI tract.
    - **Distribution:** Peak serum concentrations occur at 3–4 hours. 
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolised into five major metabolites with varying hypoglycemic activity (0–70%).
    - **Elimination:** Average biological half-life is 7 hours. Approximately 85% is excreted in the urine and 7% in the feces. Most urinary excretion occurs within the first 24 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **UGDP Study:** A long-term prospective trial evaluating tolbutamide (1.5 g/day) showed a cardiovascular mortality rate 2.5 times higher than diet alone. While controversial, these findings form the basis for the class-wide cardiovascular warning for sulfonylureas [US].
- **Comparative Potency:** Tolazamide is approximately 5 times more potent than tolbutamide and equivalent in potency to chlorpropamide on a milligram basis [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F) [US].
- **Packaging:** Dispense in tight, light-resistant containers [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription only (Rx only).
