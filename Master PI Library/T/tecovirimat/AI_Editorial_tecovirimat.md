# Editorial Product Information: Tecovirimat

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of tecovirimat for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved PI for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tecovirimat monohydrate (an inhibitor of the orthopoxvirus VP37 envelope wrapping protein).
- **Brand Name:** **TPOXX** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Capsules:** 200 mg tecovirimat.
    - **Injection:** 200 mg/20 mL (10 mg/mL) solution for intravenous infusion [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral or Intravenous infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** SIGA Technologies, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Human Smallpox Disease:** Indicated for the treatment of smallpox caused by variola virus in adults and pediatric patients weighing at least 3 kg [US-approved].
- **Limitations of Use:**
    - Effectiveness has not been determined in humans; efficacy is based on animal models as human field trials are not feasible or ethical [US].
    - Efficacy may be reduced in immunocompromised patients [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (14-day duration)
Treatment should be initiated as soon as possible following diagnosis.

#### Oral Dosing (Capsules)
Must be administered within 30 minutes after a full meal containing moderate or high fat (~600 kcal, 25 g fat) [US].
- **13 kg to <25 kg:** 200 mg (1 capsule) every 12 hours.
- **25 kg to <40 kg:** 400 mg (2 capsules) every 12 hours.
- **40 kg to <120 kg:** 600 mg (3 capsules) every 12 hours.
- **≥120 kg:** 600 mg (3 capsules) every 8 hours.

#### Intravenous Dosing (6-hour infusion)
Infuse over 6 hours every 12 hours via an infusion pump [US].
- **3 kg to <35 kg:** 6 mg/kg.
- **35 kg to <120 kg:** 200 mg.
- **≥120 kg:** 300 mg.

### Administration Instructions
- **Alternative Oral Administration:** For patients weighing ≥13 kg who cannot swallow capsules, the capsule contents may be mixed with 30 mL of liquid (e.g., milk) or soft food (e.g., applesauce) and administered within 30 minutes [US].
- **IV to Oral Transition:** Patients ≥13 kg should switch to oral capsules as soon as oral therapy is tolerated to complete the 14-day course [US].
- **IV Preparation:** Must be diluted in two equal parts of 0.9% NaCl or 5% Dextrose. **Not for IV bolus injection** [US].

### Missed Dose
If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as possible if there are more than 8 hours remaining before the next scheduled dose. If less than 8 hours remain, skip the missed dose and resume the normal schedule [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Oral Capsules:** None [US].
- **Intravenous Injection:** Contraindicated in patients with severe renal impairment (CrCl <30 mL/min) due to the risk of accumulation of the excipient hydroxypropyl-β-cyclodextrin (HP-β-CD) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypoglycemia:** Co-administration with **repaglinide** may result in mild-to-moderate hypoglycemia. Blood glucose monitoring is required during co-administration [US].
- **Injection Excipient Risk (HP-β-CD):** 
    - **Renal Insufficiency:** Patients with mild (CrCl 60–89 mL/min) or moderate (CrCl 30–59 mL/min) renal impairment should be monitored closely for renal toxicity when receiving the IV formulation. Consider switching to oral therapy if renal toxicity is suspected [US].
    - **Pediatrics <2 years:** Limited data; higher exposure to HP-β-CD may occur due to renal immaturity. Monitor renal function closely after IV treatment [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Reactions
| Formulation | Adverse Reaction (Frequency) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Oral** | Headache (12%), Nausea (5%), Abdominal pain (2%), Vomiting (2%) [US] |
| **Intravenous** | Infusion site pain (73%), Swelling (39%), Erythema (23%), Extravasation (19%), Headache (15%) [US] |

### Less Common/Serious Reactions
- **Neurological:** EEG changes, migraine, disturbance in attention, abnormal dreams.
- **Psychiatric:** Depression, irritability, panic attack.
- **Laboratory:** Decreased hematocrit/hemoglobin, increased heart rate [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP Enzyme Effects:** Tecovirimat is a weak inducer of CYP3A and a weak inhibitor of CYP2C8 and CYP2C19 [US].
    - **Midazolam (CYP3A substrate):** Decreased midazolam exposure; monitor for effectiveness [US].
    - **Repaglinide (CYP2C8 substrate):** Increased repaglinide exposure; monitor for hypoglycemia [US].
- **Phosphate Binders:** Co-administration with binders like calcium acetate or sevelamer increases tecovirimat bioavailability. Monitor for adverse effects [US].
- **Transporters:** Tecovirimat inhibits BCRP *in vitro* [US].

### Vaccine Interactions
- **Smallpox Vaccine:** Tecovirimat may reduce the immune response to live smallpox vaccine (vaccinia virus). The clinical impact on vaccine efficacy is unknown [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** No human data available to evaluate drug-associated risks. Animal studies (mice and rabbits) showed no embryofetal developmental toxicity at high systemic exposures [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended in patients with smallpox to avoid variola virus transmission through direct contact. It is unknown if tecovirimat is excreted in human milk, though it was present in animal milk (milk-to-plasma ratio ~0.8) [US].
- **Fertility:** No human data. Reduced fertility associated with testicular toxicity was observed in male mice [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for patients weighing at least 3 kg. Dosing is based on weight-based simulations to match adult exposure [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies included limited subjects ≥65 years. No dose adjustment is recommended based on age [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Oral:** No adjustment required, including for patients with ESRD on hemodialysis [US].
    - **IV:** Contraindicated in severe impairment (CrCl <30 mL/min). Use with caution in mild-to-moderate impairment [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for Child-Pugh Class A, B, or C [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Tecovirimat inhibits the activity of the orthopoxvirus VP37 protein, preventing the formation of egress-competent enveloped virions, thereby blocking cell-to-cell and long-range viral dissemination [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Oral bioavailability increases by ~39% when taken with a fatty meal. Tmax occurs at approximately 6 hours [US].
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is 77–82%.
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolized via hydrolysis of the amide bond and glucuronidation (UGT1A1, UGT1A4).
    - **Elimination:** Primarily eliminated via metabolism. Half-life is approximately 19–21 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy Summary:** Due to ethical constraints, efficacy is established via the FDA "Animal Rule." Pivotal studies in cynomolgus macaques (lethal monkeypox challenge) and New Zealand White rabbits (lethal rabbitpox challenge) demonstrated statistically significant improvement in survival when treated for 14 days [US].
- **Human Safety:** Safety was evaluated in 359 healthy adult volunteers for the oral formulation and 26 for the IV formulation [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Capsules:** Store at 20°C to 25°C in the original bottle [US].
- **Injection (Vials):** Store refrigerated (2°C to 8°C). Do not freeze [US].
- **Diluted IV Solution:** Stable for 4 hours at room temperature or 24 hours if refrigerated [US].
