# Editorial Product Information: Temozolomide

## Editorial Note
- The information contained in this editorial was synthesized from the official Product Information (PI) document provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data.
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial summary and serves as a supplement to, not a substitute for, the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Temozolomide (an imidazotetrazine alkylating agent).
- **Brand Name(s):** APO-Temozolomide [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Oral capsules: 5 mg, 20 mg, 100 mg, 140 mg, 180 mg, and 250 mg [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Newly Diagnosed Glioblastoma Multiforme (GBM):** Used concomitantly with focal radiotherapy, followed by adjuvant monotherapy [Australian].
- **Recurrent High-Grade Glioma:** Treatment of recurrent glioblastoma multiforme or anaplastic astrocytoma following standard therapy [Australian].
- **Advanced Metastatic Malignant Melanoma:** First-line treatment [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Capsules must be swallowed whole with water and should not be opened or chewed.
- **Fasting Requirement:** Administer in a fasting state at least one hour before a meal.
- If vomiting occurs post-dose, a replacement dose should not be administered that day.
- **Anti-emetic Prophylaxis:** Recommended prior to the initial concomitant dose and strongly recommended during the adjuvant phase for GBM.

### Dosing: Newly Diagnosed GBM (Adults)
1.  **Concomitant Phase:** 75 mg/m² daily for 42 days (up to 49 days) alongside focal radiotherapy (60 Gy in 30 fractions).
2.  **Adjuvant Phase:** Begins four weeks after completing the concomitant phase.
    *   **Cycle 1:** 150 mg/m² daily for 5 days, followed by a 23-day rest.
    *   **Cycles 2–6:** Escalation to 200 mg/m² daily for 5 days if Cycle 1 toxicity was minimal (ANC ≥1.5 x 10⁹/L, platelets ≥100 x 10⁹/L, non-haematological toxicity ≤ Grade 2).

### Dosing: Recurrent Glioma or Metastatic Melanoma (Adults)
- **Chemotherapy-Naive Patients:** 200 mg/m² daily for 5 days of a 28-day cycle.
- **Previously Treated Patients:** Initial dose of 150 mg/m² daily for 5 days, escalating to 200 mg/m² in the second cycle if haematological targets are met.

### Dosing: Paediatric (Recurrent Glioma)
- **Children ≥ 3 years:** 200 mg/m² daily for 5 days of a 28-day cycle. If previously treated with chemotherapy, start at 150 mg/m² [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **temozolomide**, its excipients, or **dacarbazine (DTIC)** [Australian].
- Pregnancy [Australian].
- Breastfeeding [Australian].
- Severe myelosuppression [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Critical Warnings
- **Pneumocystis carinii pneumonia (PCP):** Patients on the 42-day concomitant GBM regimen are at high risk. Prophylaxis is mandatory for all patients during this phase and should continue until lymphocytopenia resolves (≤ Grade 1) [Australian].
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Rare cases of fatal hepatic failure have been reported. Perform baseline liver function tests (LFTs) before initiation. For the 42-day cycle, repeat LFTs midway. For all patients, check LFTs after each cycle [Australian].
- **HBV Reactivation:** Screening for Hepatitis B is required before treatment. Monitor patients with prior infection for signs of reactivation during and for several months after therapy [Australian].

### General Precautions
- **Myelosuppression:** Dose-limiting toxicity is typically haematological. Monitor CBC weekly during the concomitant phase and on Day 22 of each adjuvant/monotherapy cycle.
- **Handling:** As a cytotoxic agent, contact with the capsule contents must be avoided if a capsule is damaged.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Very Common (>10%)
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, constipation, anorexia, diarrhoea.
- **Neurological:** Fatigue, headache, somnolence, dizziness, paraesthesia.
- **Dermatological:** Alopecia, rash.
- **Haematological:** Thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, anaemia, leucopenia.

### Common (1–10%)
- Fever, pain, malaise, weight decrease, rigors.
- Dyspnoea.
- Pruritus, petechiae.
- Taste perversion, abdominal pain, dyspepsia.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Pancytopenia/Aplastic Anaemia:** May be prolonged and potentially fatal.
- **Secondary Malignancies:** Rare cases of myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS) and myeloid leukaemia.
- **Severe Skin Reactions:** Rare reports of Stevens-Johnson syndrome and toxic epidermal necrolysis.
- **Infections:** Opportunistic infections (e.g., CMV, PCP) and sepsis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Valproic Acid:** Associated with a small but statistically significant decrease in temozolomide clearance [Australian].
- **Ranitidine/H2-receptor Antagonists:** No clinically significant alteration in absorption or clearance [Australian].
- **Dexamethasone, Phenytoin, Carbamazepine:** No significant effect on temozolomide clearance noted [Australian].
- **Combined Alkylating Agents:** May increase the risk of myelosuppression.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Cytotoxic agents can cause fetal loss and defects. Effective contraception is required for females during and for 6 months post-therapy.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated; it is unknown if temozolomide is excreted in human milk [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Temozolomide has genotoxic effects. Men should not father a child during and for 3 months after the final dose and should seek advice on sperm cryopreservation [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for children ≥3 years for recurrent glioma. No experience in children <3 years. No clinical experience in melanoma for patients <18 years [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Patients >70 years appear to be at increased risk of neutropenia and thrombocytopenia [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No specific data available; however, based on pharmacokinetics, dose reductions are unlikely to be required. Exercise caution [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No specific data available. Based on pharmacokinetics, dose reductions are unlikely to be required, but caution is advised [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A prodrug that undergoes rapid non-enzymatic conversion at physiological pH to the active metabolite MTIC. Cytotoxicity is primarily due to alkylation at the O⁶ position of guanine, leading to DNA damage and aberrant repair.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; peak plasma levels in 0.5–1.5 hours. Food reduces Cmax by 33% and AUC by 9%.
    *   **Distribution:** Low protein binding (10–20%); crosses the blood-brain barrier.
    *   **Metabolism:** Approximately 5–10% recovered unchanged in urine; remainder excreted as AIC or polar metabolites.
    *   **Elimination:** Clearance is independent of age, renal/hepatic function, or tobacco use.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Newly Diagnosed GBM:** A pivotal trial of 573 patients compared TMZ + RT vs. RT alone. The TMZ + RT arm showed a statistically significant improvement in overall survival (Hazard Ratio 1.59). The 2-year survival probability was 26% for the TMZ arm vs. 10% for the RT-only arm [Australian].
- **Metastatic Melanoma:** A Phase III trial of 305 patients compared TMZ vs. dacarbazine (DTIC). Median progression-free survival was significantly longer for TMZ (1.9 vs. 1.5 months), though overall survival difference was not statistically significant (7.7 vs. 6.4 months) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C [Australian].
- Keep out of reach of children.
- Shelf life information is located on the outer packaging.
