# Editorial Product Information: Tedizolid Phosphate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical and regulatory data into a single, coherent reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the United States PI was available for review; therefore, all information is attributed to that region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tedizolid phosphate (a phosphate prodrug converted by phosphatases to the active moiety, tedizolid).
- **Chemical Class:** Oxazolidinone antibacterial.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Sivextro** [US].
- **ATC Code:** J01XX11.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Tablet:** 200 mg (yellow, film-coated, oval; debossed with "TZD" and "200") [US].
    - **Injection:** 200 mg sterile lyophilised powder in a single-dose vial for reconstitution [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral and Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Merck Sharp & Dohme LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Bacterial Skin and Skin Structure Infections (ABSSSI):** Indicated for the treatment of ABSSSI caused by susceptible isolates of the following Gram-positive microorganisms:
    - *Staphylococcus aureus* (including methicillin-resistant [MRSA] and methicillin-susceptible [MSSA] isolates).
    - *Streptococcus pyogenes*.
    - *Streptococcus agalactiae*.
    - *Streptococcus anginosus* Group (including *S. anginosus*, *S. intermedius*, and *S. constellatus*).
    - *Enterococcus faecalis*.
- **Patient Population:** Adults and paediatric patients at least 26 weeks gestational age and weighing at least 1 kg [US].
- **Antibiotic Stewardship:** To reduce the development of drug-resistant bacteria, **Sivextro** should only be used to treat or prevent infections proven or strongly suspected to be caused by susceptible bacteria [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 Adult Dosage
- **Standard Dose:** 200 mg once daily for six (6) days [US].
- **Routes:** Administered orally (with or without food) or via IV infusion over 1 hour [US].
- **Switching:** No dose adjustment is required when transitioning between IV and oral administration [US].

### 3.2 Paediatric Dosage
- **Patients weighing ≥35 kg:** 200 mg once daily for 6 days (Oral or IV) [US].
- **Patients weighing <35 kg:**
    - **Oral:** **Sivextro** tablets must **not** be administered to paediatric patients weighing less than 35 kg [US].
    - **Intravenous (IV):**
        - **1 kg to <2 kg:** 3 mg/kg twice daily for 6 days [US].
        - **2 kg to <35 kg:** Weight-banded dosing (6 mg to 60 mg) administered twice daily for 6 days [US].
- **Infusion Time:** All paediatric IV doses should be infused over 1 hour [US].

### 3.3 Administration and Missed Doses
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as possible, up to 8 hours before the next scheduled dose. If less than 8 hours remain, skip the missed dose and resume the normal schedule. Do not double the dose [US].
- **IV Preparation:** Reconstitute the 200 mg vial with 4 mL of Sterile Water for Injection. Gently swirl; avoid vigorous agitation. Dilute further into 250 mL (for adults/large peds) or a calculated volume (for smaller peds) of 0.9% Sodium Chloride or 5% Dextrose [US].
- **Compatibilities:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride and 5% Dextrose [US].
- **Incompatibilities:** Incompatible with solutions containing divalent cations (e.g., **Lactated Ringer’s Injection**, **Hartmann’s Solution**) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None specified in available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Spontaneous reports of serotonin syndrome have occurred with co-administration of oxazolidinones and serotonergic agents (SSRIs, SNRIs, TCAs, triptans, and opioids like meperidine). Monitor for symptoms (hyperthermia, agitation, tremors). Consider discontinuation if syndrome is suspected [US].
- **Neutropenia:** Safety and efficacy have not been adequately evaluated in patients with neutrophil counts <1000 cells/mm³. In animal models, antibacterial activity was reduced in the absence of granulocytes. Consider alternative therapy for neutropenic patients [US].
- **Clostridioides difficile-Associated Diarrhea (CDAD):** Reported for nearly all systemic antibacterials. Severity ranges from mild diarrhea to fatal colitis. Evaluate if diarrhea occurs, even up to two months post-treatment [US].
- **Myelosuppression:** While Phase 3 trials (6-day duration) showed minimal impact, 21-day studies in healthy adults suggested a dose- and duration-dependent effect on haematologic parameters. Thrombocytopenia has been reported in post-marketing use, typically with treatment exceeding 6 days [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### 6.1 Common Adverse Effects (≥2%)
| Population | Common Reactions |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Adults** | Nausea (7%), Headache (5%), Diarrhea (4%), Infusion/Injection-site reactions (4%), Vomiting (3%), Dizziness (2%) |
| **Paediatrics (12 to <18 yrs)** | Phlebitis, increased hepatic transaminases |
| **Paediatrics (<12 yrs)** | Infusion/Injection-site reactions, Vomiting |

### 6.2 Less Common and Serious Reactions
- **Hematologic:** Anemia, decreased white blood cell count, thrombocytopenia (post-marketing) [US].
- **Neurological:** Peripheral and optic neuropathy (observed with other oxazolidinones used >28 days; similar rates between tedizolid and linezolid in 6-day trials), VIIth nerve paralysis, paraesthesia [US].
- **Ocular:** Blurred vision, visual impairment, vitreous floaters [US].
- **Psychiatric:** Insomnia [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **BCRP Substrates:** Orally administered tedizolid phosphate inhibits Breast Cancer Resistance Protein (BCRP) in the intestine. It may increase plasma concentrations of oral BCRP substrates (e.g., **rosuvastatin**, **methotrexate**, **topotecan**). If possible, interrupt BCRP substrate use during the 6-day **Sivextro** course [US].
- **MAO Inhibition:** Tedizolid is a reversible inhibitor of monoamine oxidase (MAO) *in vitro*. However, no meaningful changes in blood pressure or heart rate were seen in studies with pseudoephedrine or tyramine [US].
- **CYP Enzymes:** No detectable induction or inhibition of major CYP450 enzymes [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not assigned a lettered category]. Animal studies in mice and rats showed developmental toxicities (reduced fetal weight, skeletal variations) at exposures 4–6 times the human dose. Data in humans are insufficient; use only if potential benefit justifies the risk [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Tedizolid is excreted in rat milk. It is unknown if it is excreted in human milk. Consider the developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding alongside the mother’s clinical need for the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility or reproductive performance were observed in male or female rats at exposures up to 4–5 times the human therapeutic level [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved from birth (at least 26 weeks gestational age and weighing ≥1 kg) [US]. 
- **Geriatric Use:** No overall differences in pharmacokinetics or safety compared to younger subjects; no dose adjustment required [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for patients with any degree of renal impairment or those on haemodialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for moderate (Child-Pugh Class B) or severe (Child-Pugh Class C) hepatic impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Binds to the 50S subunit of the bacterial ribosome, inhibiting protein synthesis.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Activity correlates best with the AUC/MIC ratio [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Oral bioavailability is approximately 91%. Peak plasma levels (Cmax) are reached in ~3 hours (oral) or at the end of a 1-hour infusion [US].
    - **Distribution:** Protein binding is 70–90%. Penetrates well into interstitial fluid of adipose and skeletal muscle tissue [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily mediated by multiple sulfotransferase (SULT) isoforms. Not a significant substrate for CYP450 enzymes [US].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily hepatic. 82% excreted in feces and 18% in urine, mostly as an inactive sulfate conjugate. Terminal half-life is ~12 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adult ABSSSI Trials:** Two Phase 3 multicentre, double-blind, non-inferiority trials compared **Sivextro** (200 mg once daily for 6 days) to linezolid (600 mg twice daily for 10 days). **Sivextro** demonstrated non-inferiority for early clinical response (48–72 hours) and investigator-assessed clinical success at the post-therapy evaluation (7–14 days post-treatment) [US].
- **Paediatric Trials:** Evaluated in randomized trials for patients aged 12 to <18 years and 4 months to <12 years, showing comparable success rates and safety profiles to adult populations [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Tablets and Injection:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Total time from reconstitution to administration should not exceed 24 hours at room temperature or refrigerated (2°C to 8°C) [US].
