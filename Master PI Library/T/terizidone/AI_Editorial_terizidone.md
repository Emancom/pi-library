# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial information was derived from the official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection, synthesised for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for jurisdiction-specific legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [United States].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [United States].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [United States].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Metabolic Disorders)
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [United States].
- **Loading Dose:** Often utilised in patients presenting with severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [United States].
- **Frequency:** Administered every 3 to 4 hours (q3h or q4h); frequency should never be less than every 6 hours (q6h) [United States].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [United States].

### Dosing in ESRD on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [United States].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [United States].
- **Adjustments:** Dose reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations [United States].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [United States].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Serious Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, often within minutes of administration. These are reported most frequently in patients with ESRD on dialysis. If a reaction occurs, treatment must be discontinued immediately [United States].

### General Precautions
- **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine has been associated with the occurrence of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been reported [United States].
- **Oral Formulation Caution:** The safety of oral levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [United States].

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Plasma Concentrations:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine levels before starting therapy. Subsequent weekly and monthly monitoring should be performed. The target plasma free carnitine concentration is between 35 and 60 µmol/L [United States].
- **Clinical Monitoring:** Regular assessment of blood chemistries, vital signs, and overall clinical condition is required [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are frequently confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic errors.

### Frequency $\ge$ 5% (regardless of causality in ESRD trials)
| Body System | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Injection site reaction (38%), Pain (35%), Headache (37% at 20mg/kg), Chest pain (15%), Asthenia (12%), Fever (12%), Flu syndrome (29%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (21%), Hypotension (19%), Tachycardia (9%) |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea (35%), Vomiting (21%), Nausea (12%), Abdominal pain (21% at 10mg/kg) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (27%), Cough increase (18%), Dyspnea (11%), Rhinitis (11%) |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness (18%), Paresthesia (12%) |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia (15%), Hypervolemia (12%) |

### Other Serious or Notable Effects
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [United States].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; body odor and gastritis [United States].
- **HCP Reporting:** Suspected adverse reactions should be reported to the manufacturer or the national regulatory authority (e.g., FDA MedWatch) [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. Monitoring of INR is recommended upon initiation or dose adjustment [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine is not bound to plasma proteins, reducing the likelihood of protein-binding displacement interactions [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [United States]: Not formally assigned a letter category in the current text, but reproduction studies in rats and rabbits (up to 3.8 times the human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after exogenous administration. In nursing mothers, the risk of excess carnitine intake by the infant must be weighed against the benefit of supplementation to the mother [United States].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal models [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy are established for the treatment of inborn errors of metabolism; dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg) [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources; however, dose selection should generally be cautious, reflecting the greater frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function [United States].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosing adjustments for ESRD are based on post-dialysis trough levels. Accumulation of metabolites is a concern with oral, but not intravenous, administration in this population [United States].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [United States].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound; mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [United States].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [United States].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Study:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis showed that trough concentrations reached 54–180 µmol/L (normal range 40–50 µmol/L) after one week of therapy. Trough levels remained stable or increased over the trial period [United States].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** In a study of patients receiving 10, 20, or 40 mg/kg, mean trough concentrations at 24 weeks were 148, 240, and 456 µmol/L, respectively [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing [United States].
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial, as the formulation contains no preservatives [United States].
