# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to local regulatory guidelines for specific prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (generic name); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Clear, colorless sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It should be administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
  - **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
  - **Dosing Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less frequently than every 6 hours.
  - **Dose Range:** Subsequent daily doses typically remain at 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered if clinically required.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
  - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
  - **Dose Adjustment:** Initiation may be prompted by subnormal trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels (below 40–50 µmol/L). Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) should be guided by trough levels and may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect parenterals visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial [US].
- **Monitoring:** Obtain plasma carnitine concentrations before starting therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in patients with ESRD on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and initiate medical treatment [US].
- **Seizures:** Post-marketing reports indicate seizures may occur in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** Chronic high-dose oral administration (not specifically IV) in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites: trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].
- **Monitoring:** Plasma carnitine levels should be monitored to ensure they remain within the therapeutic range (35–60 µmol/L) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic errors.

### Very Common (>10%) [US-based ESRD clinical trials]
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33–38%), Pain (21–35%), Flu syndrome (15–29%), Chest pain (6–15%), Headache (3–37%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), Hypotension (3–19%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), Vomiting (9–21%), Nausea (5–12%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), Cough increase (10–18%), Dyspnea (3–11%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), Hypervolemia (3–12%).

### Common (1–10%) [US-based ESRD clinical trials]
- **General:** Abdominal pain, Fever, Asthenia, Infection.
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, Palpitations, Arrhythmia, Atrial fibrillation.
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (10–18%), Paresthesia, Insomnia, Depression.
- **Musculoskeletal:** Leg cramps.
- **Skin:** Pruritus, Rash.
- **Special Senses:** Taste perversion, Amblyopia, Eye disorder.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Anaphylaxis and Severe Hypersensitivity:** Rare but potentially life-threatening [US].
- **Seizures:** Reported in patients with and without pre-existing conditions [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating treatment or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not formally assigned in the provided text, but follows the "clearly needed" standard]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine levels following administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant should be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg for metabolic disorders). See Section 3 for specific instructions [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the source document.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. Safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency; toxic metabolites (TMA/TMAO) may accumulate [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are quickly eliminated [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
  - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), the mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours and the mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels) [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Administration to ESRD patients on hemodialysis results in increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Bioavailability Study:** A study of 15 healthy male volunteers established that levocarnitine tablets and oral solution are bioequivalent. Absolute bioavailability (corrected for endogenous levels) was approximately 15% [US].
- **ESRD Studies:**
  - A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly showed that trough concentrations stabilized between 54–180 µmol/L after one week of therapy [US].
  - Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) demonstrated dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing.
- **Stability (Admixture):** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL, stored at 25°C [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only Medicine (Rx-only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in the provided US source.
