# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of clinical information regarding levocarnitine injection, designed for use by healthcare professionals as a concise reference.
- **Note:** This editorial synthesis is not a substitute for the official PI. Clinicians must consult the approved local product information and relevant clinical guidelines in their specific jurisdiction before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP). It is a carrier molecule involved in the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (quaternary ammonium compound).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
    - **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Maintenance:** Administer every 3 to 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours) via infusion or IV injection.
    - **Dose Range:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised as therapy requires.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Adjustment:** Dosing should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions to 5 mg/kg may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if concentrations are adequate.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have occurred, particularly in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and monitor if re-administration is attempted.
- **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine has been associated with reports of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed.
- **Monitoring:** 
    - For metabolic disorders: Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended.
    - For ESRD: Monitor pre-dialysis trough concentrations. Normal levels are 40–50 µmol/L.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology (e.g., ESRD). The following have been reported:
- **Common (>5% in clinical trials):**
    - **General:** Pain (30%), injection site reaction (33%), headache (22%), fever (7%), flu syndrome (25%), asthenia (9%), abdominal pain (9%).
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), chest pain (12%).
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%).
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%).
    - **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%).
- **Specific Clinical Effects:** 
    - **Body Odor:** Transient body odor may occur with high doses.
    - **Gastritis:** Reported infrequently.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels at treatment initiation and following any dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned by letter, but descriptive data provided]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows indicate levocarnitine concentrations in milk increase following administration. Weigh the benefits of maternal treatment against the potential risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies revealed no evidence of impaired fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is calculated by weight (50 mg/kg), which applies to the paediatric population.
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects over 65 to determine if they respond differently than younger subjects.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - Specifically indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD patients on dialysis.
    - Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency; chronic high doses of oral formulations in these patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours.
    - **Dialysis:** Levocarnitine is easily removed from plasma by hemodialysis.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times per week after dialysis significantly increased plasma levocarnitine. Pre-dialysis trough concentrations increased from ~20 µmol/L at baseline to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks of therapy.
- **Bioavailability:** Absolute bioavailability of oral formulations is low, approximately 15–16% [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C.
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL).

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
