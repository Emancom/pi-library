# Editorial Product Information: Orlistat

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis harmonises Product Information (PI) documents from the following jurisdictions:
    - **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**
    - **US (Food and Drug Administration)**
- The purpose of this document is to provide a unified, clinically accurate overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis for informational purposes and does not replace the official PI used for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Orlistat (a peripherally acting, reversible inhibitor of gastrointestinal lipases).
- **Brand Name:** **Xenical**.
- **ATC Code:** A08AB01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 120 mg hard gelatin capsules (turquoise, opaque).
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** 
    - **Australian:** Pharmaco (Australia) Ltd.
    - **US:** H2-Pharma LLC (Distributed); Cheplapharm Arzneimittel GmbH (Licensed).

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Obesity Management:** Indicated for the treatment of obese patients with a Body Mass Index (BMI) $\ge$ 30 kg/m², or overweight patients with a BMI $\ge$ 27 kg/m² in the presence of other risk factors (e.g., hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidaemia).
- **Dietary Context:** Must be used in conjunction with a mildly hypocaloric [Australian] or reduced-calorie [US] diet.
- **Weight Maintenance:** Indicated for weight loss and weight maintenance [US].
- **Weight Regain Prevention:** Indicated to reduce the risk of weight regain after prior weight loss [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** One 120 mg capsule taken three times daily with each main meal.
- **Administration Timing:** During the meal or up to one hour after the meal. 
- **Fat Requirement:** If a meal is missed or contains no fat, the dose may be omitted.
- **Dietary Recommendations:** Patients should follow a nutritionally balanced diet where approximately 30% of calories are derived from fat. Daily intake of fat, carbohydrate, and protein should be distributed over three main meals.
- **Nutritional Supplementation:** Because orlistat reduces the absorption of fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) and beta-carotene, patients should take a multivitamin supplement. 
    - **Timing:** At least 2 hours before or after the administration of orlistat, such as at bedtime.
- **Co-administration Adjustments:**
    - **Cyclosporine:** Administer cyclosporine 3 hours after orlistat [US].
    - **Levothyroxine:** Administer levothyroxine and orlistat at least 4 hours apart [US].
- **Titration:** Doses above 120 mg TID have not shown additional benefit.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian: Category B1; US: Category X]. Both jurisdictions list pregnancy as a contraindication due to the lack of benefit from weight loss in a pregnant woman.
- **Chronic Malabsorption Syndrome.**
- **Cholestasis.**
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to orlistat or any excipients.
- **Breastfeeding:** [Australian-specific contraindication].
- **Specific Conditions:** Pancreatic enzyme deficiency, chronic pancreatitis, or major gastrointestinal surgery [Australian-specific contraindication].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Liver Injury:** Rare post-marketing reports of severe liver injury with hepatocellular necrosis or acute hepatic failure, sometimes resulting in transplant or death. Discontinue if symptoms of hepatic dysfunction occur (e.g., jaundice, dark urine, pruritus).
- **Renal Effects:** May be associated with hyperoxaluria and oxalate nephropathy, which can lead to renal failure. Risk is higher in patients with existing renal impairment or volume depletion. Monitor renal function in at-risk patients.
- **Cholelithiasis:** Substantial weight loss can increase the risk of gallstones.
- **Organic Causes of Obesity:** Exclude organic causes (e.g., hypothyroidism) before prescribing.
- **Gastrointestinal Events:** GI side effects increase if orlistat is taken with a diet high in fat (>30% of calories).
- **Glycaemic Control:** Weight loss may improve metabolic control in Type 2 Diabetes; doses of hypoglycaemic medications (e.g., sulfonylureas, insulin) may require reduction.
- **Miscellaneous:** 
    - Cases of rectal bleeding have been reported; investigate severe or persistent symptoms [Australian].
    - Use with caution in patients with active peptic ulcer disease, bulimia, or laxative abuse [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are primarily gastrointestinal and related to the mechanism of action (fat malabsorption). These are typically mild, transient, and occur within the first 3 months.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Oily spotting (26.6%), flatus with discharge (23.9%), faecal urgency (22.1%), fatty/oily stool (20.0%), oily evacuation (11.9%), increased defecation (10.8%) [US stats for Year 1]. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Faecal incontinence (7.7%), abdominal pain/discomfort, nausea, headache, influenza, upper respiratory tract infection, anxiety, hypoglycaemia (in Type 2 Diabetics), soft stools, asthenia. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Dizziness, vertigo, paraesthesia, rectal haemorrhage, diverticulitis, cholelithiasis, hepatitis, allergic reactions (rash, pruritus). |
| **Very Rare (<0.1%)** | Pancreatitis, bullous eruptions, increases in transaminases/alkaline phosphatase. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Severe liver injury, oxalate nephropathy, and anaphylaxis have been reported.
- **Discontinuation:** In clinical trials, 8.8% of patients discontinued treatment due to adverse events (vs 5.0% for placebo), primarily due to GI effects.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Cyclosporine:** Reduction in plasma levels observed. Simultaneous administration is not recommended. [US: 3-hour separation required; Australian: more frequent monitoring of levels required].
- **Amiodarone:** Reduction in systemic exposure (25–30%) may occur. Monitor ECG and amiodarone levels.
- **Levothyroxine:** Reports of hypothyroidism or reduced control. [US: 4-hour separation required].
- **Warfarin/Anticoagulants:** Potential for decreased Vitamin K absorption. Monitor INR/prothrombin time closely.
- **Antiepileptic Drugs:** Reports of convulsions. Monitor for changes in seizure frequency or severity.
- **Antiretroviral HIV Drugs:** Potential for reduced absorption and loss of virological control. Monitor HIV RNA levels frequently.
- **Fat-soluble Vitamins:** Absorption of vitamins A, D, E, K and beta-carotene is significantly reduced.
- **Acarbose:** Lack of interaction studies; concomitant use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Alcohol:** No interaction found with ethanol [Australian/US].
- **Oral Contraceptives:** No direct pharmacokinetic interaction, but secondary failure may occur if severe diarrhoea is present. Use of additional contraception is recommended [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian:** Category B1. Contraindicated.
    - **US:** Category X. Contraindicated. Weight loss offers no potential benefit to a pregnant woman and may result in fetal harm.
- **Breastfeeding:** 
    - **Australian:** Contraindicated.
    - **US:** Caution should be exercised; it is not known if orlistat is excreted in human milk.
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility or reproduction seen in animal (rat) studies.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - **Australian:** Not recommended for patients under 18 years.
    - **US:** Safety and efficacy not established in children under 12. Approved for use in adolescents aged 12–16 years based on studies showing BMI reduction.
- **Elderly Use:** 
    - **Australian:** No dose adjustment required. 
    - **US:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of patients $\ge$ 65 years to determine if they respond differently.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required, but monitor for oxalate nephropathy.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment required.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A potent, specific, and reversible long-acting inhibitor of gastric and pancreatic lipases. It forms a covalent bond with the active serine site of the lipase in the stomach and small intestine, preventing the hydrolysis of dietary fat (triglycerides) into absorbable free fatty acids and monoglycerides.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** At therapeutic doses, orlistat inhibits approximately 30% of dietary fat absorption. Faecal fat increase is seen within 24–48 hours.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Systemic absorption is minimal (nearly non-measurable).
    - **Distribution:** >99% protein bound (lipoproteins and albumin).
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily occurs within the gastrointestinal wall. Two minor metabolites (M1 and M3) are pharmacologically inconsequential.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 97% of the dose is excreted in faeces (83% as unchanged drug). Renal excretion is <2%.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** Efficacy established in studies ranging from 1 to 4 years. 
- **XENDOS Study (4 years):** Showed that orlistat, combined with lifestyle changes, significantly reduced the risk of developing Type 2 Diabetes by 37.3% in obese patients compared to placebo.
- **Weight Loss Outcomes:** Pooled 1-year data showed that 57% of patients on 120 mg TID lost $\ge$ 5% of their body weight, compared to 31% of placebo patients.
- **Metabolic Improvements:** Associated with significant reductions in LDL-cholesterol, blood pressure, fasting glucose, and insulin levels.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Conditions:** Keep in the original carton (to protect from light and moisture [Australian]) and keep the bottle tightly closed [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:**
    - **Australian:** Schedule 3 (Pharmacist Only Medicine).
    - **US:** Rx-only (Prescription).
