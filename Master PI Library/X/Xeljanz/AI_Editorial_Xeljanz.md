# Editorial Product Information: Xeljanz (tofacitinib)

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (TGA), US (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of official Product Information (PI) from multiple regulatory authorities. It is designed to provide healthcare professionals with a unified clinical overview of tofacitinib.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial is for informational purposes only and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tofacitinib (as citrate).
- **Chemical Class:** Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitor.
- **Brand Names:** **Xeljanz**, **Xeljanz XR**.
- **ATC Code:** L04AA29.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Immediate-Release (IR) Tablets:** 5 mg, 10 mg.
    - **Extended-Release (XR) Tablets:** 11 mg, 22 mg [US-approved; 22 mg not approved in Australia].
    - **Oral Solution:** 1 mg/mL.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Sponsor/Manufacturer:** Pfizer.

## 2. Approved Indications
**Xeljanz/Xeljanz XR** is indicated for the treatment of:

- **Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA):** Adults with moderately to severely active RA who have had an inadequate response or intolerance to methotrexate [Australian] or one or more TNF blockers [US]. Used as monotherapy or in combination with methotrexate or other non-biologic DMARDs.
- **Psoriatic Arthritis (PsA):** Adults with active PsA who have had an inadequate response or intolerance to methotrexate/DMARDs [Australian] or one or more TNF blockers [US]. Used in combination with non-biologic DMARDs.
- **Ankylosing Spondylitis (AS):** Adults with active AS who have had an inadequate response or intolerance to conventional therapy (e.g., NSAIDs) [Australian] or one or more TNF blockers [US].
- **Ulcerative Colitis (UC):** Adults with moderately to severely active UC who have had an inadequate response or intolerance to conventional therapy or a biological therapy [Australian] or one or more TNF blockers [US].
- **Polyarticular Course Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis (pcJIA):** Patients 2 years of age and older with active pcJIA who have had an inadequate response or intolerance to one or more DMARDs [Australian] or TNF blockers [US].
- **Juvenile Psoriatic Arthritis (jPsA):** Patients 2 years of age and older with active jPsA [Australian-approved; US-approved for 2 years and older].

**Limitations of Use:** Use in combination with biological DMARDs or potent immunosuppressants (e.g., azathioprine, ciclosporin) is not recommended.

## 3. Dosage and Administration

### General Dosing (Adults)
- **RA, PsA, and AS:** 5 mg IR twice daily or 11 mg XR once daily.
- **Ulcerative Colitis:**
    - **Induction:** 10 mg IR twice daily (or 22 mg XR once daily [US]) for 8 weeks. May extend to 16 weeks if response is inadequate. Discontinue if benefit is not achieved by week 16.
    - **Maintenance:** 5 mg IR twice daily or 11 mg XR once daily. For refractory patients, 10 mg IR twice daily [Australian: if no cardiovascular/malignancy risk factors; US: limited to the shortest duration for those with loss of response].
- **Switching IR to XR:** Patients treated with 5 mg IR twice daily may switch to 11 mg XR once daily the following day.

### Paediatric Dosing (pcJIA and jPsA)
Dosed twice daily based on body weight:
- **10 kg to <20 kg:** 3.2 mg (3.2 mL oral solution).
- **20 kg to <40 kg:** 4 mg (4 mL oral solution).
- **≥40 kg:** 5 mg (5 mL oral solution or one 5 mg tablet).

### Administration Instructions
- Administer with or without food.
- **XR Tablets:** Must be swallowed whole. Do not crush, split, or chew. The inert tablet shell may occasionally be seen in the stool [US].
- **Oral Solution:** Use the provided press-in bottle adapter and oral dosing syringe. Discard any remaining solution 60 days after first opening the bottle.

### Missed Dose
- Do not double the dose to make up for a missed one. Take the next dose at the regular time.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to tofacitinib or any excipients.
- **Combination Therapy:** Concomitant use with biological DMARDs or potent immunosuppressants (e.g., azathioprine, tacrolimus, ciclosporin) [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C) [Australian].
- *Note: US Highlights state "None", but clinical restrictions are detailed in Warnings.*

## 5. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed Warnings [Harmonised]
**Xeljanz** therapy is associated with several serious risks. These risks are higher in patients 50 years and older with at least one cardiovascular risk factor (based on a large post-marketing study in RA):
- **Serious Infections:** Increased risk of bacterial, viral (including herpes zoster), fungal, and mycobacterial (TB) infections leading to hospitalisation or death.
- **Mortality:** Higher rate of all-cause mortality, including sudden cardiovascular death, compared to TNF blockers.
- **Malignancies:** Increased incidence of lymphomas and lung cancers (especially in smokers) compared to TNF blockers.
- **Major Adverse Cardiovascular Events (MACE):** Higher rate of MI, stroke, and cardiovascular death.
- **Thrombosis:** Serious and sometimes fatal pulmonary embolism, deep vein thrombosis, and arterial thrombosis have occurred.

### General Warnings
- **Gastrointestinal Perforation:** Use with caution in patients at risk (e.g., history of diverticulitis, concomitant NSAIDs/corticosteroids).
- **Laboratory Monitoring:**
    - **Lymphocytes:** Do not initiate if ALC <0.75 x 10⁹/L [Australian] or <500 cells/mm³ [US].
    - **Neutrophils:** Do not initiate if ANC <1.0 x 10⁹/L [Australian] or <1000 cells/mm³ [US].
    - **Haemoglobin:** Do not initiate if Hb <90 g/L [Australian] or <9 g/dL [US].
    - **Lipids:** Assess 4–8 weeks after initiation.
    - **Liver Enzymes:** Routine monitoring recommended; investigate increases ≥3x ULN.
- **Hypoglycaemia:** Reported in patients treated for diabetes; monitor blood glucose.
- **Vaccinations:** Avoid live vaccines concurrently. Bring all patients up to date with immunisations before starting therapy.

## 6. Adverse Effects

### Frequency Categories (RA, PsA, AS)
| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Upper respiratory tract infections [Australian], Nasopharyngitis [Australian] |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Headache, Diarrhoea, Hypertension, Nausea, Vomiting, Gastritis, Dyspepsia, Arthralgia, Rash, Pyrexia, Increased blood creatine phosphokinase |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Anaemia, Neutropenia, Lymphopenia, Dyslipidaemia, Dehydration, Insomnia, Paresthesia, Dyspnoea, Cough, Pruritus, Peripheral oedema |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Infections:** Pneumonia, herpes zoster, tuberculosis, opportunistic infections (e.g., cryptococcosis).
- **Vascular:** Venous thromboembolism (VTE) including PE and DVT.
- **Malignancy:** Lymphoma, non-melanoma skin cancer (NMSC), lung cancer.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Perforation.

## 7. Drug Interactions

### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole):** Increase tofacitinib exposure. Reduce dose by half.
- **Moderate CYP3A4 Inhibitors + Strong CYP2C19 Inhibitors (e.g., fluconazole):** Increase tofacitinib exposure. Reduce dose by half.
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., rifampicin):** Decrease tofacitinib exposure. Concomitant use is not recommended as it may result in loss of clinical response.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Immunosuppressants:** Combined use with biologics, azathioprine, or ciclosporin increases the risk of serious infection and is not recommended.
- **Heart Rate/PR Interval:** Caution with medicines that decrease heart rate or prolong the PR interval (e.g., beta-blockers, digoxin).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category D:** Tofacitinib has been shown to be teratogenic in rats and rabbits (skeletal malformations, post-implantation loss). Use is not recommended during pregnancy or in women attempting to become pregnant.
    - **US Recommendation:** Use only if potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus.
- **Contraception:** Women of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for a period after the last dose [Australian: duration based on PD effects; US: at least 4 weeks].
- **Breastfeeding:** Tofacitinib is present in human milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for at least 18 hours after the last dose of **Xeljanz** or 36 hours after **Xeljanz XR**.

## 9. Use in Special Populations

### Paediatric Use
- Approved for pcJIA and jPsA in patients ≥2 years. Safety and efficacy in children <2 years or for other indications (e.g., UC) have not been established.

### Elderly Use (≥65 years)
- Increased risk of serious infections, MACE, malignancies, and mortality. Use only if no suitable treatment alternatives are available.

### Renal Impairment
- **Mild (eGFR 51–80 mL/min):** No dose adjustment required.
- **Moderate/Severe (including Haemodialysis):** Reduce the total daily dose by half. 
    - If taking 5 mg bid, reduce to 5 mg qd.
    - If taking 10 mg bid (UC), reduce to 5 mg bid.
    - [US: Xeljanz XR is not recommended in moderate/severe renal impairment for UC maintenance].

### Hepatic Impairment
- **Mild (Child-Pugh A):** No dose adjustment required.
- **Moderate (Child-Pugh B):** Reduce the total daily dose by half (as per renal instructions).
- **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Use is contraindicated [Australian] or not recommended [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selectively inhibits JAK1 and JAK3, and to a lesser extent JAK2 and TyK2. This blocks signaling for several cytokines (IL-2, -4, -7, -9, -15, -21) integral to lymphocyte activation and function.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Bioavailability is 74%. Peak plasma levels reached in 0.5–1 hour (IR) or 4 hours (XR).
    - **Distribution:** 40% protein bound (predominantly to albumin).
    - **Metabolism:** ~70% hepatic metabolism (primarily via CYP3A4; minor CYP2C19).
    - **Elimination:** ~30% renal excretion of parent drug. Elimination half-life is ~3 hours (IR) or ~6 hours (XR).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **RA (ORAL Trials):** Demonstrated efficacy in ACR20/50/70 responses and HAQ-DI improvement across various populations (DMARD-IR, MTX-IR, TNF-IR).
- **PsA (OPAL Trials):** Efficacy shown in ACR20 and HAQ-DI at 3 months when used with non-biologic DMARDs.
- **UC (OCTAVE Trials):** Induction trials showed significant remission and mucosal healing at week 8 with 10 mg bid. Maintenance trials showed sustained remission at 52 weeks.
- **pcJIA (Study pcJIA-I):** Demonstrated significantly fewer disease flares at 44 weeks compared to placebo.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Tablets (IR/XR):** Store below 30°C [Australian] or 20°C–25°C [US].
- **Oral Solution:** Store below 25°C [Australian] or 20°C–25°C [US]. Protect from light. Discard 60 days after first opening.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Medicine).
- **United States:** Rx-only.
