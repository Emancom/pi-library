# Editorial Product Information: Latanoprost (Xalatan)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis harmonises Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdictions: 
    - **Australian (TGA)**: XALATAN (Latanoprost) Eye Drops.
    - **US (FDA)**: XALATAN (Latanoprost Ophthalmic Solution) 0.005%.
- **Purpose**: This document provides a unified, clinically accurate overview for healthcare professionals by integrating regulatory data from multiple regions.
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Always consult the current local regulatory documentation before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Latanoprost (a prostaglandin F2α analogue and selective prostanoid FP receptor agonist).
- **Brand Name**: **Xalatan**.
- **ATC Code**: S01EE01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength**: Sterile, isotonic ophthalmic solution containing 50 micrograms/mL (0.005%) of latanoprost.
    - **Australian**: 2.5 mL solution in a 5 mL polyethylene container (minimum 80 drops).
    - **US**: 2.5 mL solution in a 5 mL low-density polyethylene bottle with a turquoise screw cap.
- **Route of Administration**: Topical Ophthalmic.
- **Sponsor/Manufacturer**: 
    - **Australian**: Aspen Pharmacare Australia Pty Ltd.
    - **US**: Viatris Specialty LLC (Distributed); Made in Belgium.

## 2. Approved Indications
- Reduction of elevated intraocular pressure (IOP) in patients with:
    - Open-angle glaucoma.
    - Ocular hypertension.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing (including Elderly)**: One eye drop in the affected eye(s) once daily.
- **Optimal Timing**: Administration in the evening provides the greatest efficacy.
- **Administration Instructions**:
    - Systemic absorption may be reduced by applying pressure to the tear duct (nasolacrimal occlusion) immediately after instillation.
    - **Contact Lenses**: Must be removed prior to instillation. Lenses may be reinserted after 15 minutes.
    - **Multiple Medications**: If using other topical ophthalmic drugs, allow an interval of at least 5 minutes between administrations.
- **Important Limitation**: Dosage should not exceed once daily. More frequent administration has been shown to decrease the IOP-lowering effect and may cause paradoxical elevations in IOP.
- **Missed Dose**: Treatment should continue with the next scheduled dose as normal; do not double the dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to latanoprost or any excipients in the formulation.
- **US Specific**: Known hypersensitivity to benzalkonium chloride.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Pigmentation Changes**:
    - **Iris**: May gradually increase brown pigment, potentially causing permanent changes in eye colour. This is most common in mixed-coloured irides (e.g., blue-brown, green-brown). Onset usually occurs within 8 months.
    - **Eyelid and Eyelashes**: Darkening of the palpebral skin and increased pigmentation of eyelashes may occur; these changes are typically reversible upon discontinuation.
- **Eyelash Changes**: May gradually change eyelashes and vellus hair, including increased length, thickness, and number of lashes. These changes are usually reversible.
- **Intraocular Inflammation**: Use with caution in patients with a history of iritis or uveitis. Generally, avoid use in patients with active intraocular inflammation.
- **Macular Oedema**: Reported uncommonly, primarily in aphakic patients, pseudophakic patients with a torn posterior lens capsule, or those with known risk factors for cystoid macular oedema.
- **Herpetic Keratitis**: Use with caution in patients with a history of herpetic keratitis. Avoid use in active herpes simplex keratitis or recurrent keratitis specifically associated with prostaglandin analogues.
- **Bacterial Keratitis**: Risk of contamination if the dispensing tip contacts the eye or surrounding structures. Multi-dose containers must be handled with care to prevent serious ocular infection [US].
- **Asthma**: Limited experience in patients with severe or brittle asthma; treat with caution as asthma aggravation and dyspnoea have been reported [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are primarily ocular. Most common reactions (5–15% in clinical trials) include blurred vision, burning/stinging, conjunctival hyperemia, foreign body sensation, itching, increased iris pigmentation, and punctate keratitis.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (>10%) | Iris hyperpigmentation, eye irritation (burning, grittiness, itching, stinging, foreign body sensation), eyelash/vellus hair changes. |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Ocular hyperemia, punctate keratitis (mostly asymptomatic), blepharitis, eye pain, conjunctivitis, vision blurred, eyelid oedema, eyelid skin reaction, myalgia, arthralgia, headache, dizziness, rash. |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Iritis, uveitis, keratitis, macular oedema (including cystoid), photophobia, chest pain, asthma, dyspnoea. |
| **Rare** (<0.1%) | Corneal oedema, corneal erosion, periorbital oedema, deepening of the eyelid sulcus, trichiasis, acute asthma attacks, herpetic keratitis. |

- **Post-marketing reports**: Pseudophakic corneal calcification (in patients with significant corneal damage using phosphate-buffered drops), palpitations, unstable angina, and nausea/vomiting.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Prostaglandin Analogues**: Concomitant use of two or more prostaglandins or derivatives is not recommended; it may lead to paradoxical elevations in IOP.
- **Thimerosal**: In vitro studies show precipitation when mixed with latanoprost. If such products are used, separate administration by at least 5 minutes.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**:
    - **Australian Category B3**: Safety in human pregnancy not established. Use only if the benefit justifies the potential risk.
    - **US Data**: No adequate, well-controlled human studies. Animal studies (IV administration) showed embryofetal lethality, malformations (cleft palate, skeletal anomalies), and abortions at doses significantly higher than the maximum recommended human ophthalmic dose (RHOD).
- **Breastfeeding**: It is not known if latanoprost or its metabolites are excreted in human milk. Exercise caution and consider the clinical need for the mother versus potential risks to the infant.
- **Fertility**: No effects on male or female fertility were observed in rat studies at IV doses 811 times the RHOD.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and effectiveness have not been established; use is not recommended in children [Australian].
- **Elderly Use**: No overall differences in safety or efficacy compared to younger patients.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment**: Not specified in available sources (Note: Systemic metabolism is primarily hepatic via fatty acid β-oxidation).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Latanoprost is a selective prostanoid FP receptor agonist. It reduces IOP by increasing the outflow of aqueous humour, primarily via the uveoscleral pathway.
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: A prodrug absorbed through the cornea and hydrolysed by esterases to the biologically active latanoprost acid. Peak concentration in aqueous humour is reached in ~2 hours.
    - **Distribution**: Primarily distributed in the anterior segment, conjunctiva, and eyelids. Plasma protein binding is ~90%.
    - **Metabolism**: Practically no metabolism occurs in the eye; primary metabolism occurs in the liver to 1,2-dinor and 1,2,3,4-tetranor metabolites.
    - **Elimination**: Plasma half-life is ~17 minutes. Metabolites are primarily excreted in the urine (88% recovery after topical dose).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy**: In 6-month pivotal trials, latanoprost 0.005% once daily reduced IOP by 27–34% (6–8 mmHg reduction). This was shown to be equivalent to or, in some studies, statistically superior to timolol 0.5% twice daily.
- **Long-term Safety**: A 5-year study [US] (48-month study [Australian]) showed that while iris pigmentation may increase, it does not affect the incidence or severity of other adverse events. IOP reduction remained stable over 48 months.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened**: Store under refrigeration at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze. Protect from light.
- **Shipment**: During shipment, the bottle may be maintained at temperatures up to 40°C for a maximum of 8 days [US].
- **Opened (In-use)**:
    - **Australian**: Store below 25°C and use within 4 weeks.
    - **US**: Store at room temperature (up to 25°C) and use within 6 weeks.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australian**: Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **US**: Rx Only.
