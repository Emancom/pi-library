# Editorial Product Information: Xiaflex (collagenase clostridium histolyticum)

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: US (FDA).
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **Xiaflex** for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a clinically relevant format.
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference and is not a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Collagenase clostridium histolyticum. This is a combination of two microbial collagenases, AUX-I (Class I) and AUX-II (Class II), isolated from *Clostridium histolyticum*.
- **Brand Name:** **Xiaflex** [US].
- **ATC Code:** M09AB02.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Lyophilized powder for reconstitution. Each single-use vial contains 0.9 mg of collagenase clostridium histolyticum.
- **Route of Administration:** Intralesional injection only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Endo USA, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Dupuytren’s Contracture:** Treatment of adult patients with a palpable cord [US].
- **Peyronie’s Disease:** Treatment of adult men with a palpable plaque and curvature deformity of at least 30 degrees at the start of therapy [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- The standard dose for both indications is **0.58 mg** per injection [US].

### Indication-Specific Administration
- **Dupuytren’s Contracture:**
    - Reconstitution volume varies by joint type: 0.39 mL of diluent for metacarpophalangeal (MP) joints; 0.31 mL for proximal interphalangeal (PIP) joints [US].
    - Finger extension procedure: If the contracture persists, a manual extension procedure should be performed approximately 24 to 72 hours post-injection [US].
    - Treatment cycle: Up to 3 injections may be administered per cord at 4-week intervals. Up to two joints/cords in the same hand may be treated during a single visit [US].
- **Peyronie’s Disease:**
    - Reconstitution volume: 0.39 mL of diluent [US].
    - Treatment cycle: Consists of two injections (1 to 3 days apart) followed by a penile modeling procedure 1 to 3 days after the second injection [US].
    - Course: Up to 4 treatment cycles (8 injections total) may be administered at 6-week intervals. Treatment should cease if curvature improves to <15 degrees or if further treatment is not clinically indicated [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Must be administered by a healthcare professional experienced in hand procedures (Dupuytren’s) or male urological diseases (Peyronie’s) [US].
- For Peyronie's, the healthcare provider must have completed required training for the **Xiaflex** REMS program [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** History of hypersensitivity to **Xiaflex** or any other therapeutic collagenase [US].
- **Peyronie's Specific:** Treatment of plaques that involve the penile urethra, due to the risk of permanent injury to the structure [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning (US): Corporal Rupture (Penile Fracture) or Other Serious Penile Injury.**
    - In Peyronie’s disease trials, corporal rupture occurred in 0.5% of patients. Other serious injuries (severe hematoma) occurred in 3.7%.
    - **Xiaflex** is available for Peyronie's disease only through a restricted Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy (REMS) program [US].
- **Tendon Rupture/Ligament Damage:** Injection into or near tendons or ligaments in the hand can cause permanent injury. Avoid injection into these structures [US].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Anaphylaxis has been reported. Providers should be prepared to treat severe allergic reactions [US].
- **Bleeding Risk:** Use with caution in patients with coagulation disorders or those taking anticoagulants (except low-dose aspirin $\le$ 150 mg/day) within 7 days of injection [US].
- **Acute Post-Injection Back Pain:** Reported in Peyronie’s disease patients; typically resolves within 15–30 minutes [US].
- **Syncope and Presyncope:** Can occur following the procedure, often as a vasovagal response to pain [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Very Common (>10%)
- **Dupuytren’s:** Peripheral edema (mostly of the injected hand), contusion, injection site hemorrhage, injection site pain, lymphadenopathy, and pruritus [US].
- **Peyronie’s:** Penile hematoma, penile swelling, penile pain, and penile ecchymoses [US].

### Common (1–10%)
- **Dupuytren’s:** Tenderness, skin laceration, lymph node pain, erythema, and axillary pain [US].
- **Peyronie’s:** Blood blisters, painful erection, erectile dysfunction, skin discoloration, and localized edema [US].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Flexor tendon rupture (0.3% in Dupuytren's trials) [US].
- Corporal rupture and severe penile hematoma [US].
- Complex regional pain syndrome (CRPS) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Anticoagulants:** Concomitant use with anticoagulants (excluding low-dose aspirin) may increase the risk of injection site bleeding and hematoma. Use with caution [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category B]. There are no adequate studies in pregnant women. Animal data (rats) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm at exposures up to 11 times the maximum recommended human dose. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if collagenase clostridium histolyticum is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution when administering to nursing women [US].
- **Fertility:** No impairment observed in rat studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed between patients $\ge$ 65 years and younger patients [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** These proteinases hydrolyze collagen in its native triple-helical conformation. When injected into a Dupuytren's cord or Peyronie's plaque (composed mostly of Types I and III collagen), they cause enzymatic lysis, resulting in disruption of the cord or plaque [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Following intralesional injection, systemic absorption is minimal and short-lived. In Peyronie’s patients, low levels were quantifiable for only 30 minutes post-injection. There is no evidence of accumulation with sequential doses [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Synergistic hydrolysis of collagen by AUX-I and AUX-II has been demonstrated in vitro [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Dupuytren’s Contracture:** Two randomized, double-blind trials showed significant reduction in contracture (to $\le$ 5 degrees) 30 days after the last injection (up to 3 injections) compared to placebo (Study 1: 64% vs 7%; Study 2: 44% vs 5%) [US].
- **Peyronie’s Disease:** Two randomized, double-blind trials demonstrated a significant reduction in penile curvature deformity and a reduction in patient-reported "bother" scores compared to placebo at Week 52 [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F). Do not freeze [US].
- **Reconstituted Solution:** May be kept at room temperature (20°C to 25°C) for up to 1 hour or refrigerated (2°C to 8°C) for up to 4 hours prior to use [US].
