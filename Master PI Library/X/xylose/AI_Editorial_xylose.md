# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- At the time of writing, official PI documents for other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian, Japanese) were not provided for comparison. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a clinical synthesis and must not be used as a substitute for the official, legally approved PI in a specific jurisdiction when prescribing or dispensing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metabolic Disorders:** For the acute and chronic management of patients diagnosed with an inborn error of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Metabolic Disorders)
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg.
- **Method:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Frequency:** Often initiated with a loading dose, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (not to exceed 6-hour intervals) for the first 24 hours. 
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg/day, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg/day have been utilized.

### Dosing in ESRD on Hemodialysis
- **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow bolus (2–3 minutes) into the venous return line at the completion of each dialysis session.
- **Adjustments:** Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels.

### Monitoring and Instructions
- **Target Levels:** Plasma free carnitine concentrations should be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Pre-treatment:** It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels and monitor weekly/monthly thereafter, alongside blood chemistries and vital signs.
- **Preparation:** Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration before administration. The product is for single use only; discard unused portions.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** While the US PI states "None known" in the dedicated section, the Warnings section advises against use in patients with a history of severe hypersensitivity to levocarnitine.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and do not re-administer without a thorough risk-benefit assessment [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Levocarnitine may increase the frequency or severity of seizures in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a prior history of epilepsy.
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Form Nuance):** While the injection is used in ESRD, chronic high doses of **oral** levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions in ESRD patients are often confounded by the underlying pathology of renal failure.

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site reaction (33–59%), Pain (general), Hypertension (18–21%), Hypotension (3–19%), Headache, Nausea, Vomiting, Diarrhea (up to 35% at high doses), Pharyngitis, Dyspnea |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Abdominal pain, Fever, Chest pain, Asthenia, Tachycardia, Dizziness, Hypercalcemia, Hyperkalemia, Anemia, Cough increase |
| **Post-marketing/Rare** | Seizures, Anaphylaxis, Body odor (transient) |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels at the initiation of levocarnitine therapy or following dose adjustments in patients receiving oral anticoagulants [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not assigned a lettered category]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations following exogenous administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant should be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg) and applicable to paediatric patients. Specific age-range limitations are not specified in available sources.
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources; generally, dose selection should be cautious, reflecting the greater frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. The intravenous route avoids the accumulation of TMA/TMAO metabolites associated with high-dose oral administration in this population [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation and energy production.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with organic acidemias, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly cleared.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV post-dialysis increased pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** A study of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg in ESRD patients (n≈30 per group) demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks. Clinical outcome improvements related to signs and symptoms of deficiency in the ESRD population have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing.
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
