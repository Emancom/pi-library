# Editorial Product Information: Xenon Xe 129 Hyperpolarized

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of **Xenoview** (hyperpolarized xenon Xe 129) for use by healthcare professionals.
- This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference; it is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing and administration within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Hyperpolarized Xenon Xe 129.
- **Chemical Class:** Noble gas; hyperpolarized contrast agent.
- **Brand Name:** **Xenoview** [US].
- **ATC Code:** V08CX01 (Xenon (129Xe)).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Hyperpolarized gas for oral inhalation. The dosage is expressed as Dose Equivalent (DE). One **Xenoview** Dose Delivery Bag contains a minimum DE of 75 mL (typically targetted at 75 mL to 100 mL) in total gas volumes of 300 mL, 500 mL, 750 mL, or 1,000 mL [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral inhalation.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Polarean, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Lung Ventilation Evaluation:** Indicated for use with magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) to evaluate lung ventilation in adults and paediatric patients aged 6 years and older [US].
- **Limitations of Use:** **Xenoview** has not been evaluated for use with lung perfusion imaging [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** The recommended target dose for adults and paediatric patients ($\ge$ 6 years) is 75 mL to 100 mL DE. The entire contents of one Dose Delivery Bag must be inhaled [US].
- **Dose Equivalent (DE) Definition:** DE is the volume (mL) of 100% isotopically enriched, 100% hyperpolarized xenon Xe 129 that produces equivalent net magnetization [US].
- **Bag Size Selection:** The size of the Dose Delivery Bag (300 mL to 1,000 mL) should be selected based on the patient’s Total Lung Capacity (TLC) or Forced Vital Capacity (FVC). If these values are unknown, estimates should be based on sex and height using ATS-endorsed plethysmography equations [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Administer the dose within 5 minutes of measuring the DE [US].
    - Use a compatible MRI chest coil and scanner [US].
    - Position the patient supine. The patient should inhale the entire bag contents in a single breath and hold for up to 15 seconds during image acquisition [US].
    - Discard any bag not used within 60 minutes of filling [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None specified [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Supplemental Oxygen Interference:** Simultaneous administration of supplemental oxygen can degrade image quality due to the depolarization of the xenon gas. Patients should withhold supplemental oxygen for two breaths prior to inhalation and resume immediately after the imaging breath-hold [US].
- **Transient Hypoxia:** Inhalation of an anoxic gas like **Xenoview** may cause temporary hypoxemia. Clinicians should monitor oxygen saturation ($SpO_2$) and symptoms of hypoxemia, particularly in susceptible patients [US].
- **Image Quality:** If the patient cannot sufficiently inhale the gas or if the image quality is poor, the scan may be repeated with a new dose [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions observed in efficacy trials ($n=83$ adults) were generally mild and occurred in approximately 14% of patients [US].

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Oropharyngeal pain (4.8%), headache (2.4%), dizziness (2.4%) |
| **Pediatric specific (unspecified frequency)** | Blood oxygen desaturation, heart rate elevation, numbness, tingling, euphoria |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** A single case of a hypersensitivity reaction (transient pruritus and erythema) was reported in a patient with a history of multiple allergies [US].
- **Paediatric Note:** In a study of 23 paediatric patients, a mean $SpO_2$ decrease of 6% and a mean heart rate increase of 6.6 bpm were noted; both resolved within 2 minutes without intervention [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No formal drug interaction studies have been conducted. Given the minimal systemic absorption and rapid elimination, systemic interactions are unlikely [US].
- **Technical Interaction:** Supplemental oxygen is a known technical interactant that reduces image quality [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not formally assigned under current labeling rules, but maternal use is not expected to result in fetal exposure due to minimal systemic absorption [US].
    - **Animal Data:** No effects on fertility or pregnancy were observed in rats administered xenon gas mixtures at 80% concentration [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Systemic absorption is minimal; therefore, breastfeeding is not expected to result in infant exposure to the drug. There is no data regarding the presence of the gas in human milk or its effect on milk production [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness are established for patients aged 6 years and older. Use in patients under 6 years of age has not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies included 43% of patients aged 65 and older. No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed compared to younger adults [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources; however, given the pulmonary route of elimination, dose adjustments are likely unnecessary [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Hyperpolarized xenon Xe 129 nuclei are prepared such that their nuclear spins are aligned. When inhaled, these nuclei are detected by a specialized MRI coil, allowing for the visualization of the distribution of the gas within the ventilated spaces of the lung [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The MRI signal intensity is directly dependent on the volume of gas inhaled and the degree of hyperpolarization/isotopic enrichment [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Readily diffusible gas; minimal amounts enter the pulmonary vessels [US].
    - **Distribution:** Distributes into ventilated lung areas; small amounts may reach distal organs and fatty tissues where xenon solubility is higher [US].
    - **Elimination:** Eliminated via exhalation. Following a single breath-hold, the elimination half-life is approximately 14.3 to 14.5 seconds [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies (Study 1 and Study 2):** Two multi-center, randomized, open-label, cross-over trials compared **Xenoview** MRI to xenon Xe 133 scintigraphy.
    - **Study 1 (Lung Resection):** Evaluated 31 patients for predicted post-operative lung ventilation. The mean difference between **Xenoview** and scintigraphy was 1.4%, meeting equivalence criteria [US].
    - **Study 2 (Lung Transplant):** Evaluated 49 patients. The mean difference in the percentage of overall lung ventilation contributed by the right lung was 1.6% between the two modalities, also meeting equivalence criteria [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Xenon Xe 129 Gas Blend (Cylinder):** Store at 20°C to 25°C; excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C [US].
- **Xenoview (Dose Delivery Bag):** Store at 20°C to 25°C for no more than 5 minutes after final DE measurement. The bag must be discarded 60 minutes after filling [US].
