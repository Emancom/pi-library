# Editorial Product Information: Xenon Xe 129

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: United States (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Xenon Xe 129 for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a single clinical reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the United States (FDA) source was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Xenon Xe 129 (hyperpolarized).
- **Chemical Class:** Hyperpolarized contrast agent; noble gas.
- **Brand Name:** **XENOVIEW** [US].
- **ATC Code:** V08CX01 (Xenon (129Xe)) [International classification].
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Clear, colorless, odorless gas. The product is supplied as a Dose Equivalent (DE) volume of hyperpolarized xenon Xe 129 in a volume of 250 mL to 750 mL total xenon, contained within a Dose Delivery Bag (300 mL, 500 mL, 750 mL, or 1,000 mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral inhalation.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Polarean, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Lung Ventilation Evaluation:** Indicated for use with magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) to evaluate lung ventilation in adults and pediatric patients aged 6 years and older [US].
- **Limitations of Use:** The product has not been evaluated for use with lung perfusion imaging [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric ≥ 6 years):** The recommended target dose is a Dose Equivalent (DE) volume of 75 mL to 100 mL [US].
- **Dose Selection:** The size of the XENOVIEW Dose Delivery Bag is selected based on the patient’s Total Lung Capacity (TLC) or Forced Vital Capacity (FVC) [US]:
    - TLC <2.0 L (or FVC <1.5 L): 300 mL bag.
    - TLC 2.0 to <3.3 L (or FVC 1.5 to <2.5 L): 500 mL bag.
    - TLC 3.3 to <5.0 L (or FVC 2.5 to <3.8 L): 750 mL bag.
    - TLC ≥5.0 L (or FVC ≥3.8 L): 1,000 mL bag.
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Administer within 5 minutes of measuring the DE [US].
    - For patients on supplemental oxygen: Withhold oxygen for two breaths prior to inhalation and resume immediately after the imaging breath hold [US].
    - Inhalation: The patient should completely inhale the contents of the bag in a single breath and hold that breath for up to 15 seconds during image acquisition [US].
- **Image Acquisition:** Initiate MRI scanning immediately following inhalation [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None:** There are no known contraindications listed in available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Image Quality Degradation (Supplemental Oxygen):** Simultaneous administration of supplemental oxygen during inhalation can depolarize the gas, leading to degraded image quality [US].
- **Transient Hypoxia:** Inhaling an anoxic gas such as Xenon Xe 129 may cause temporary hypoxemia in susceptible individuals. Clinicians should monitor oxygen saturation (SpO2) and symptoms of hypoxemia in all patients [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions observed in clinical trials were generally transient.
- **Common (>1 patient in efficacy trials):**
    - Oropharyngeal pain
    - Headache
    - Dizziness [US]
- **Pediatric-Specific Reactions:** In published studies of patients aged 6 to 18, observed reactions included blood oxygen desaturation, heart rate elevation, numbness, tingling, and euphoria [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** A single case of hypersensitivity (transient pruritus and erythema) was reported in a patient with a history of multi-substance allergies [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Management Notes:** The primary interaction of concern is the physical/magnetic interaction with supplemental oxygen rather than a pharmacological drug-drug interaction [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** No letter category assigned.
    - **Risk Summary:** Systemic absorption following inhalation is minimal; maternal use is not expected to result in fetal exposure. Animal reproduction studies (rats) using xenon/oxygen mixtures showed no evidence of teratogenicity or adverse effects on embryo-fetal development [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Systemic absorption is minimal, and exposure to the breastfed infant is not expected. There is no data regarding the presence of Xenon Xe 129 in human milk or its effect on milk production [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats found no observed effects on fertility following repeated exposure to xenon gas mixtures [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Established in patients 6 years and older. Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients under 6 years of age [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies included 43% of patients aged 65 and over. No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed compared to younger adults [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources; however, given the minimal systemic absorption and rapid exhalation, significant impact is unlikely.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** When inhaled, hyperpolarized xenon Xe 129 nuclei are directly detected by a specific MRI coil. This allows for the visualisation of the gas distribution throughout the ventilated areas of the lung [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The MRI signal intensity depends on the volume inhaled, the degree of isotopic enrichment (>80% Xe 129), and the level of hyperpolarization [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption/Distribution:** Readily diffusible gas; a small amount enters pulmonary vessels and is distributed to distal organs, with higher solubility in fatty tissues [US].
    - **Elimination:** Rapidly eliminated via exhalation. Following a single breath hold, the elimination half-life is approximately 14.3 to 14.5 seconds [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies (Study 1 & 2):** Two multi-center, randomized, open-label, cross-over trials compared XENOVIEW MRI to xenon Xe 133 scintigraphy in patients with various pulmonary disorders (e.g., COPD, asthma, interstitial lung disease) [US].
- **Outcomes:** Both studies demonstrated that XENOVIEW MRI was equivalent to Xe 133 scintigraphy in estimating the percentage of lung ventilation contributed by specific lung zones or the right lung [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Gas Blend (Cylinder):** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Hyperpolarized Gas (Dose Delivery Bag):** Store at 20°C to 25°C for no more than 5 minutes after final DE measurement. Discard the bag if not used within 60 minutes of filling [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only [US].
