# Editorial Product Information: Enzalutamide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis harmonises Product Information (PI) documents from the following jurisdictions:
    - **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**
    - **US (Food and Drug Administration)**
- The purpose of this document is to provide a unified, clinically accurate overview of enzalutamide for use by healthcare professionals.
- This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a reference guide; it is not a substitute for the official, legally binding Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Enzalutamide.
- **Chemical Class:** Androgen receptor signalling inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** **Xtandi**.
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Soft Capsules:** 40 mg [Australian, US].
    - **Film-coated Tablets:** 40 mg and 80 mg [US-approved; not specified in Australian source].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Astellas Pharma.

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Castration-Resistant Prostate Cancer (CRPC):**
    - Treatment of patients with metastatic CRPC (mCRPC) [Australian, US].
    - Treatment of patients with non-metastatic CRPC (nmCRPC) [Australian, US].
- **Hormone-Sensitive / Castration-Sensitive Prostate Cancer:**
    - Treatment of patients with metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC) [Australian] or metastatic castration-sensitive prostate cancer (mCSPC) [US].
    - Treatment of patients with non-metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (nmHSPC) [Australian] or non-metastatic castration-sensitive prostate cancer (nmCSPC) [US] with biochemical recurrence (BCR) at high risk for metastasis.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** The standard recommended dose is **160 mg** (four 40 mg capsules/tablets or two 80 mg tablets) administered orally once daily.
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - May be taken with or without food at the same time each day. 
    - Capsules and tablets must be swallowed whole with water. Do not chew, dissolve, open, crush, or cut.
- **Concomitant Therapy:** 
    - Patients with CRPC or metastatic disease who have not been surgically castrated must continue medical castration with a gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) analog (e.g., LHRH analog).
    - Patients with nmHSPC/nmCSPC with high-risk BCR may be treated with or without a GnRH analog.
- **Biochemical Recurrence (BCR) Protocol:** 
    - Treatment may be suspended if PSA is undetectable (< 0.2 ng/mL) after 36 weeks of therapy. 
    - Therapy should be reinitiated when PSA reaches ≥ 2.0 ng/mL (post-radical prostatectomy) or ≥ 5.0 ng/mL (post-primary radiation).
- **Missed Dose:** Take the dose as soon as remembered on the same day. If a dose is missed for an entire day, resume the regular schedule the following day. Do not double the dose.
- **Dose Adjustments (Toxicity):** For Grade 3 or higher toxicity or intolerable adverse reactions, withhold dosing for one week or until symptoms improve to $\le$ Grade 2, then resume at the same dose or a reduced dose (120 mg or 80 mg).
- **Dose Adjustments (Drug Interactions):**
    - **Strong CYP2C8 Inhibitors:** Reduce enzalutamide dose to 80 mg once daily.
    - **Strong CYP3A4 Inducers:** Avoid co-administration. If unavoidable, increase the enzalutamide dose from 160 mg to 240 mg daily [US]; however, Australian sources state no dose adjustment is necessary.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to enzalutamide or any excipients.
- **Women who are or may become pregnant** [Australian]. (Note: US PI does not list this in the Contraindications section but includes a specific Warning for Embryo-Fetal Toxicity).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Seizure:** Occurred in 0.6% of patients in clinical trials. Predisposing factors include brain injury, stroke, brain tumours, or alcoholism. Permanently discontinue **Xtandi** if a seizure occurs.
- **Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES):** Rare reports of this reversible neurological disorder. Diagnosis requires MRI confirmation. Discontinue treatment if PRES develops.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Reactions including face, tongue, and pharyngeal oedema have been reported. Discontinue for serious reactions.
- **Ischemic Heart Disease:** Increased risk observed (3.5% vs 2% placebo). Optimize management of cardiovascular risk factors. Discontinue for Grade 3-4 events.
- **Falls and Fractures:** Falls occurred in 12% of treated patients vs 5.5% for placebo. Evaluate fracture risk and consider bone-targeted agents.
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Can cause fetal harm. Advise males with female partners of reproductive potential to use effective contraception.
- **Dysphagia/Choking:** Reported due to product size, particularly with the capsule formulation.
- **Laboratory Interference:** **Xtandi** can interfere with certain digoxin immunoassays (e.g., CMIA), leading to falsely elevated plasma levels.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects observed with a frequency $\ge$ 10% include:
- **Very Common (>10%):** Fatigue/Asthenic conditions, hot flush, hypertension, nausea, decreased appetite, diarrhoea, musculoskeletal pain, falls, and fractures.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Ischemic heart disease, seizure, and neutropenia (uncommon).
- **Post-marketing:** Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), and Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Strong CYP2C8 Inhibitors (e.g., gemfibrozil):** Increase enzalutamide exposure (AUC increased 2.2-fold). Reduce enzalutamide dose to 80 mg.
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., rifampin):** Decrease enzalutamide exposure (AUC decreased by 37%). Avoid use if possible.
- **Enzyme Induction:** Enzalutamide is a potent inducer of CYP3A4 and a moderate inducer of CYP2C9 and CYP2C19. It may reduce the efficacy of substrates including midazolam, warfarin, and omeprazole.
- **P-gp Substrates:** Enzalutamide may inhibit P-gp. Digoxin AUC increased by 33%. Use caution with narrow therapeutic index P-gp substrates (e.g., colchicine, dabigatran).
- **Warfarin:** Avoid co-administration if possible; if necessary, perform additional INR monitoring.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category X:** Contraindicated.
    - **US-listed Warning:** Embryo-fetal toxicity; not indicated for use in women.
- **Breastfeeding:** Not indicated for use in women. It is unknown if enzalutamide is excreted in human milk.
- **Contraception:** Male patients must use a condom during treatment and for 3 months following the final dose when engaging in sexual activity with a pregnant woman or a woman of childbearing potential.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies suggest male fertility may be impaired.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established; not recommended for use in patients < 18 years.
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness; no dose adjustment required.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild-to-moderate impairment ($\text{CrCL} \ge 30 \text{ mL/min}$). Not studied in severe renal impairment or end-stage renal disease.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild, moderate, or severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class A, B, or C).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Androgen receptor (AR) signalling inhibitor. It competitively inhibits androgen binding to ARs, inhibits AR nuclear translocation, and inhibits AR binding to DNA.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ is 1–2 hours. Oral absorption $\ge$ 84.2%.
    - **Distribution:** Apparent volume of distribution is 110 L. 97–98% protein bound (primarily albumin).
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised by CYP2C8 (primary) and CYP3A4 (minor) to an active metabolite, N-desmethyl enzalutamide.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life ($T_{1/2}$) is approximately 5.8 days. Excretion is primarily renal (71%).

## 11. Clinical Trials
Key pivotal trials include:
- **AFFIRM:** Metastatic CRPC post-docetaxel; demonstrated significant overall survival (OS) benefit (18.4 vs 13.6 months).
- **PREVAIL:** Chemotherapy-naïve metastatic CRPC; demonstrated OS benefit (35.3 vs 31.3 months) and radiographic progression-free survival (rPFS) benefit.
- **PROSPER:** Non-metastatic CRPC; demonstrated significant metastasis-free survival (MFS) benefit (36.6 vs 14.7 months).
- **ARCHES:** Metastatic HSPC/CSPC; showed significant rPFS benefit when added to ADT.
- **EMBARK:** Non-metastatic HSPC/CSPC with high-risk BCR; demonstrated significant MFS benefit for combination and monotherapy arms compared to placebo + ADT.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store at 20°C to 25°C [US] or below 25°C [Australian].
- **Handling:** Women who are or may become pregnant should not handle damaged or opened capsules/tablets without protection (e.g., gloves).

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **United States:** Rx Only.
