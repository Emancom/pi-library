# Editorial Product Information: Xermelo (Telotristat Ethyl)

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration) and US (Food and Drug Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the clinical and regulatory profile of Xermelo, intended for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidelines mandated for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Telotristat ethyl (present as telotristat etiprate).
- **Chemical Class:** Tryptophan hydroxylase inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** **Xermelo**.
- **ATC Code:** A16AX15 [Australian].
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 
    - **Film-coated tablets:** 250 mg telotristat ethyl (equivalent to 327.9 mg telotristat etiprate). 
    - **Physical description:** White to off-white, oval, debossed with ‘T-E’ on one side and ‘250’ on the reverse.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** 
    - Clinect Pty Ltd [Australian].
    - Lexicon Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Xermelo** is indicated for the treatment of carcinoid syndrome diarrhoea in combination with somatostatin analogue (SSA) therapy in adults whose condition is inadequately controlled by SSA therapy.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The recommended dose is 250 mg three times daily.
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Tablets must be taken with food [US]. 
    - **Xermelo** should be administered within 15 minutes before or within 1 hour after a meal or snack [Australian].
    - When used concurrently with short-acting octreotide, **Xermelo** should be administered at least 30 minutes prior to the octreotide dose.
- **Clinical Assessment:** Clinical response is typically observed within 12 weeks. Consideration should be given to the benefit of continuing therapy in patients who fail to respond within this timeframe [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Patients should take the next dose at the regular scheduled time. Do not administer a double dose to compensate for a missed one.
- **Discontinuation:** Therapy should be discontinued if severe constipation develops [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **General:** None listed [US].
- **Specific:** Hypersensitivity to the active substance or any of the excipients (including lactose) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Constipation:** **Xermelo** reduces bowel movement frequency. Patients must be monitored for signs of constipation and severe, persistent, or worsening abdominal pain. If severe constipation or severe abdominal pain develops, the drug should be discontinued. Caution is advised in patients with metastatic carcinoid tumours who may have impaired intestinal wall integrity [US].
- **Hepatic Enzyme Elevations:** Increased liver enzymes (ALT, ALP, AST, GGT) have been observed. Monitoring of hepatic enzymes is recommended prior to initiation and during therapy as clinically indicated. If liver injury is suspected, treatment should be interrupted. Therapy should not be resumed unless the injury can be attributed to another cause [Australian].
- **Depressive Disorders:** Depression, depressed mood, and decreased interest have been reported in clinical trials and post-marketing. Patients should be advised to report any such symptoms to their physician [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally mild to moderate in intensity. Abdominal pain was the most common reason for discontinuation (7.1%) [Australian].

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Abdominal pain, nausea, headache [Australian], fatigue [Australian], increased GGT [Australian]. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Decreased appetite, depression/depressed mood, flatulence, peripheral oedema, pyrexia, abdominal distension, constipation, increased ALT, increased AST, increased ALP. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Faecaloma [Australian], intestinal obstruction [Australian]. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** In extension studies using higher-than-recommended doses (500 mg TID), cases of serious constipation leading to hospitalisation, intestinal perforation, and intestinal obstruction were reported [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Short-acting Octreotide:** Concurrent administration significantly reduces the systemic exposure (Cmax and AUC) of telotristat ethyl and its active metabolite by approximately 80%. If required, administer short-acting octreotide at least 30 minutes after **Xermelo**.
- **CYP3A4 Substrates:** **Xermelo** may decrease the efficacy of CYP3A4 substrates (e.g., midazolam, simvastatin, felodipine, cyclosporin) by reducing their systemic exposure. In a study with midazolam, AUC was decreased by 48%. Monitor for suboptimal efficacy; dose increases of the substrate may be necessary [US].
- **CYP2B6 Substrates:** **Xermelo** may induce CYP2B6, potentially decreasing the exposure of substrates such as valproic acid, bupropion, or sertraline. Monitor for reduced efficacy [Australian].
- **Carboxylesterase 2 (CES2) Substrates:** Potential for altered exposure of CES2 substrates (e.g., prasugrel, irinotecan). Monitor efficacy and safety [Australian].
- **Transporters:** Telotristat ethyl is an inhibitor of P-gp and MRP-2. While a study with fexofenadine showed only a small (16%) increase in exposure, caution should be exercised with substrates of these transporters.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Bowel Motility Agents:** Re-evaluate the use of concomitant therapies that affect bowel motility if constipation develops [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category:** B3. 
    - **US Category:** Not lettered; risk summary provided. 
    - **Recommendation:** **Xermelo** is not recommended during pregnancy or in women of childbearing potential not using effective contraception. Animal studies in rabbits showed post-implantation loss and reduced foetal weight at doses providing >10 times clinical exposure [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** A risk to newborns/infants cannot be excluded. It is recommended that patients do not breastfeed during treatment. Infants of breastfeeding mothers who are taking **Xermelo** should be monitored for constipation [US].
- **Fertility:** No human data. No effects on fertility were observed in rat studies.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients <18 years of age have not been established.
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment is required for patients ≥65 years. Greater sensitivity in some older individuals cannot be ruled out [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for patients with mild, moderate, or severe renal impairment who are not on dialysis. Use is not recommended in end-stage renal disease requiring dialysis [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild (Child-Pugh A):** Consider reducing dose to 250 mg twice daily based on tolerability [Australian].
    - **Moderate (Child-Pugh B):** Consider reducing dose to 250 mg once daily based on tolerability [Australian].
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Use is not recommended [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Telotristat ethyl is a prodrug that is hydrolysed to its active metabolite, telotristat. Telotristat is an inhibitor of L-tryptophan hydroxylase (TPH1 and TPH2), the rate-limiting enzyme in serotonin biosynthesis. By inhibiting peripheral TPH1, it reduces serotonin production, thereby alleviating carcinoid syndrome symptoms.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed and converted to active metabolite. Peak plasma levels of the metabolite occur within 1–3 hours. Food increases systemic exposure.
    - **Distribution:** >99% bound to human plasma proteins.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily converted by carboxylesterases to telotristat. Not a substrate for CYP enzymes.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily faecal (92.8% over 240 hours); <1% excreted in urine. The terminal half-life is approximately 11 hours.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Treatment results in significant reductions in 24-hour urinary 5-hydroxyindoleacetic acid (u5-HIAA), a metabolite of serotonin.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **TELESTAR Study:** A 12-week, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (n=135) in patients with carcinoid syndrome and ≥4 bowel movements (BM) per day. **Xermelo** 250 mg TID significantly reduced the mean number of daily BMs compared to placebo (-1.4 vs -0.6; p<0.001). Durable response (≥30% BM reduction for ≥50% of the time) was achieved by 44% of the **Xermelo** group vs 20% for placebo.
- **TELECAST Study:** A similar 12-week trial in patients with carcinoid syndrome but <4 BMs per day (n=76). The primary endpoint was the percent change in u5-HIAA. **Xermelo** 250 mg TID showed a -33.2% change vs +97.7% for placebo. Stool consistency also improved.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C [Australian]. US PI specifies 25°C (excursions permitted to 15–30°C).
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging.
- **Packaging:** Supplied in PVC/PCTFE/PVC/Aluminium blister packs. US packaging is dispensed as a monthly case containing four weekly boxes, each with seven daily dose packs.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australian Classification:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Animal Remedy/Prescription Only Medicine).
- **US Classification:** Rx Only.
