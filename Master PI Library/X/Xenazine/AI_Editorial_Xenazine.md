# Editorial Product Information: Tetrabenazine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: **United States (FDA)**.
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised clinical editorial overview of tetrabenazine, synthesised for healthcare professionals. 
- Clinical Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for regional legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tetrabenazine (a vesicular monoamine transporter 2 [VMAT2] inhibitor).
- **Brand Names:** Xenazine [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available source.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Oral tablets: 12.5 mg (white, non-scored, cylindrical) and 25 mg (yellowish-buff, scored, cylindrical) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Lundbeck Pharmaceuticals LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chorea associated with Huntington’s Disease:** Indicated for the treatment of involuntary movements (chorea) specifically related to Huntington’s disease [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Individualisation of the dose is mandatory. Titration occurs over several weeks to identify the lowest effective, tolerated dose. The medication may be administered without regard to food [US].

### Titration Schedule
- **Week 1:** Initial dose of 12.5 mg once daily in the morning.
- **Week 2:** Increase to 25 mg daily (12.5 mg twice daily).
- **Weekly Titration:** Doses may be increased by 12.5 mg per day at weekly intervals.

### Maximum Doses by Metabolizer Status [US]
Genotyping for **CYP2D6** is required for patients requiring doses exceeding 50 mg/day.
- **Extensive/Intermediate Metabolizers (EMs/IMs):** Maximum daily dose of 100 mg; maximum single dose of 37.5 mg.
- **Poor Metabolizers (PMs):** Maximum daily dose of 50 mg; maximum single dose of 25 mg.

### Administration Instructions
- Daily doses of 37.5 mg to 50 mg should be administered in three divided doses.
- If treatment is interrupted for more than 5 days, the dose must be re-titrated upon resumption. For interruptions less than 5 days, maintenance therapy can be resumed without titration [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Suicidality and Depression:** Patients who are actively suicidal or those with untreated or inadequately treated depression [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated due to significantly increased exposure and prolonged half-life [US].
- **MAOIs:** Concomitant use with Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs), or within 14 days of discontinuing MAOI therapy [US].
- **Reserpine:** Use with reserpine or within 20 days of discontinuing reserpine [US].
- **Other VMAT2 Inhibitors:** Concomitant use with deutetrabenazine or valbenazine [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning: Depression and Suicidality [US]
Tetrabenazine increases the risk of depression and suicidal thoughts/behavior (suicidality) in patients with Huntington’s disease. Clinicians must balance this risk against the clinical need for chorea control. Patients should be monitored closely for new or worsening depression or unusual behavioral changes.

### General Warnings
- **Clinical Worsening:** Periodically re-evaluate the benefit-risk profile. Tetrabenazine may cause worsening in mood, cognition, rigidity, and functional capacity [US].
- **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** A potentially fatal complex involving hyperpyrexia, muscle rigidity, and autonomic instability. Discontinue immediately if NMS occurs [US].
- **Extrapyramidal Symptoms (EPS):** May cause akathisia, restlessness, agitation, and parkinsonism. Dose reduction or discontinuation may be necessary [US].
- **Sedation and Somnolence:** Common dose-limiting adverse effects. Patients should not operate heavy machinery until a stable maintenance dose is established [US].
- **QTc Prolongation:** Causes a small increase (approx. 8 msec). Avoid in patients with congenital long QT syndrome or a history of cardiac arrhythmias [US].
- **Hyperprolactinemia:** Elevates serum prolactin levels; clinical significance is unknown, but caution is advised in patients with breast cancer history [US].

| Anxiety | 15% | 3% |
| Nausea | 13% | 7% |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Suicidality/Depression:** Significant risk identified in clinical trials (19% vs 0% placebo).
- **Parkinsonism:** Bradykinesia and rigidity observed in 15% of patients [US].
- **Dysphagia:** May increase the risk of aspiration pneumonia [US].
- **Postmarketing Reports:** Tremor, confusion, worsening aggression, pneumonia, and skin rash [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Strong CYP2D6 Inhibitors (e.g., fluoxetine, paroxetine, quinidine):** Significantly increase exposure to active metabolites ($\alpha$-HTBZ and $\beta$-HTBZ). Daily dose should not exceed 50 mg and single dose should not exceed 25 mg [US].
- **Reserpine:** Irreversible VMAT2 binding may lead to major depletion of serotonin and norepinephrine. Wait 20 days after stopping reserpine before starting tetrabenazine [US].
- **MAOIs:** Risk of severe hypertensive crisis; wait 14 days between therapies [US].
- **Alcohol/Sedatives:** Additive effects on sedation and somnolence [US].
- **Neuroleptic Drugs:** Increased risk of NMS, parkinsonism, and akathisia [US].
- **QTc Prolonging Agents:** Avoid use with antipsychotics (e.g., haloperidol, ziprasidone) or antibiotics (e.g., moxifloxacin) [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Not classified by lettered category in the provided source. Animal data (rats) showed increased stillbirths and postnatal mortality at clinically relevant doses. Use only if potential benefit justifies potential risk to the fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** No data available regarding presence in human milk or effects on the infant. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug should be considered based on maternal need [US].
- **Fertility:** In animal studies, disrupted estrous cyclicity was observed in females; however, no effects on male fertility parameters were found [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Pharmacokinetics have not been formally studied in the elderly [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated. Exposure to tetrabenazine is 7- to 190-fold higher than in healthy subjects [US].
- **CYP2D6 Poor Metabolizers:** Require dose reduction (max 50 mg/day) due to 3- to 9-fold higher metabolite exposure [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Reversible depletion of monoamines (dopamine, serotonin, norepinephrine, and histamine) from nerve terminals by inhibiting the human vesicular monoamine transporter type 2 (VMAT2) [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** At least 75% absorbed; peak plasma concentrations of active metabolites reached in 1–1.5 hours [US].
    - **Distribution:** Rapidly distributed to the brain (highest in the striatum). Protein binding: 82–85% (parent), 59–68% (metabolites) [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Extensive hepatic metabolism via carbonyl reductase to active metabolites $\alpha$-HTBZ and $\beta$-HTBZ, which are subsequently O-dealkylated by CYP2D6 [US].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (75%). Half-lives: $\alpha$-HTBZ (7 hours), $\beta$-HTBZ (5 hours), and 9-desmethyl-β-DHTBZ (12 hours) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Trial (Study 1):** A 12-week, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study (n=84). 
- **Primary Outcome:** Change in Total Chorea Score (UHDRS). 
- **Results:** Tetrabenazine group showed a reduction of 5.0 units vs. 1.5 units for placebo ($p < 0.05$). Chorea scores returned to baseline one week after discontinuation [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions permitted to 15°–30°C (59°–86°F) [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Handling:** Dispense in a USP tight, light-resistant container [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription-only medication (Rx only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
