# Editorial Product Information: xanomeline + trospium

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **xanomeline + trospium**
- Chemical class:
  - xanomeline: muscarinic receptor agonist
  - trospium: antimuscarinic (antimuscarinic anticholinergic)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of schizophrenia in adults.  
  - If any jurisdictional differences in approved indications exist, they are not specified in the available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Recommended initiation and titration (general adult population):
  - Start: 50 mg xanomeline / 20 mg trospium orally twice daily for at least 2 days.
  - Then: increase to 100 mg / 20 mg orally twice daily for at least 5 days (Days 3–7).
  - Thereafter, dosage may be increased to 125 mg / 30 mg orally twice daily based on tolerability and clinical response.
  - Maximum recommended dose: 125 mg xanomeline / 30 mg trospium twice daily.

### Dosing by population
- Geriatric patients:
  - Recommended starting dose: 50 mg / 20 mg orally twice daily.
  - Consider slower titration.
  - Maximum recommended dose in geriatric patients: 100 mg / 20 mg twice daily.
- Renal impairment:
  - Not recommended in patients with moderate or severe renal impairment (estimated GFR < 60 mL/min). (See Warnings and Precautions.)
- Hepatic impairment:
  - Contraindicated in moderate and severe hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh B and C).
  - Not recommended in mild hepatic impairment.

### Administration instructions
- Administer at least 1 hour before a meal or at least 2 hours after a meal.
- Do not open the capsules.
- Instruct patients regarding the need to monitor and report urinary, hepatic or cardiovascular symptoms (see Warnings and Precautions).

### Titration schedules
- See General dosing above. Dose escalation beyond 100 mg/20 mg should be based on tolerability and clinical response and can be titrated to 125 mg/30 mg twice daily where tolerated.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources

## 5. Contraindications
List of all contraindications stated in available sources:
- Pre-existing urinary retention.
- Moderate hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh Class B).
- Severe hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh Class C).
- Gastric retention (e.g., known gastric outlet obstruction).
- History of hypersensitivity to xanomeline + trospium or to trospium chloride (angioedema and hypersensitivity reactions reported).
- Untreated narrow‑angle glaucoma.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- **Risk of urinary retention:** xanomeline + trospium can cause urinary retention. Patients with clinically significant bladder outlet obstruction (e.g., benign prostatic hyperplasia), diabetic cystopathy, and geriatric patients are at increased risk. The medicine is contraindicated in patients with pre-existing urinary retention and not recommended in moderate or severe renal impairment.
- **Hepatic impairment and hepatobiliary injury:** Patients with hepatic impairment have higher systemic exposure to xanomeline and may have increased adverse reactions. The drug is contraindicated in moderate and severe hepatic impairment and not recommended in mild hepatic impairment. Transient increases in liver enzymes consistent with biliary contraction/obstruction have been observed; evaluate liver enzymes and bilirubin prior to initiation and as clinically indicated. Discontinue if signs of substantial liver injury occur (e.g., jaundice, pruritus, ALT > 5× ULN or > 5× baseline).
- **Biliary disease:** Cases consistent with transient biliary obstruction (with rapid decline in enzymes) and possible gallstone passage were reported. Not recommended in active biliary disease such as symptomatic gallstones.
- **Decreased gastrointestinal motility / gastric retention:** Trospium has antimuscarinic effects that may reduce GI motility. Use with caution in patients with gastrointestinal obstructive disorders (e.g., ulcerative colitis, intestinal atony, myasthenia gravis) because of risk of gastric retention.
- **Angioedema / hypersensitivity:** Angioedema of face, lips, tongue and/or larynx has been reported, including occurrence after the first dose of trospium in one case. Angioedema with airway involvement can be life‑threatening. Discontinue and treat appropriately if suspected.
- **Narrow‑angle glaucoma:** Pupillary dilation from anticholinergic effects can precipitate angle closure in patients with anatomically narrow angles. Use only if benefits outweigh risks and with careful monitoring.
- **Increases in heart rate / cardiovascular effects:** Increases in heart rate have been observed (see Adverse Effects). Assess heart rate at baseline and as clinically indicated during treatment.
- **Anticholinergic adverse reactions and renal impairment:** Trospium is substantially renally excreted; systemic exposure increases with moderate/severe renal impairment, increasing anticholinergic adverse reactions (dry mouth, constipation, dyspepsia, urinary tract infection, urinary retention). Not recommended in eGFR <60 mL/min.
- **Central nervous system (CNS) effects:** Anticholinergic CNS effects (dizziness, confusion, hallucinations, somnolence) have been reported. Monitor, especially after initiation or dose increases. Advise patients not to drive or operate heavy machinery until they know how they are affected. Consider dose reduction or discontinuation if significant CNS effects occur.
- **Drug interaction–related precautions:**
  - CYP2D6 inhibitors: CYP2D6 contributes significantly to xanomeline metabolism. Concomitant strong CYP2D6 inhibitors may increase xanomeline exposure and adverse reactions—monitor for increased frequency/severity of adverse reactions.
  - Drugs eliminated by active tubular secretion: Concomitant use may increase plasma concentrations of trospium and/or the concomitant drug due to competition for active tubular secretion—monitor for increased adverse reactions from either agent.
  - Oral drugs that are sensitive CYP3A4 substrates: Xanomeline transiently inhibits CYP3A4 locally in the gut; concomitant oral sensitive CYP3A4 substrates may have increased systemic exposure—monitor for increased adverse reactions of the substrate.
  - Oral drugs that are P‑glycoprotein substrates: Xanomeline transiently inhibits P‑glycoprotein locally in the gut; concomitant orally administered P‑gp substrates may have increased exposure—monitor.
  - Other antimuscarinic drugs: additive anticholinergic adverse reactions may occur—monitor and manage accordingly.

### Monitoring requirements
- Assess liver enzymes (ALT, AST) and bilirubin prior to initiation and as clinically indicated during treatment.
- Assess heart rate at baseline and as clinically indicated.
- Monitor for signs/symptoms of urinary retention (urinary hesitancy, weak stream, incomplete bladder emptying, dysuria) and for urinary tract infection.
- Monitor for CNS adverse effects (confusion, hallucinations, somnolence) particularly after dose initiation or increase.
- Monitor for signs of angioedema and hypersensitivity.
- Monitor for interactions with drugs that are strong CYP2D6 inhibitors, drugs eliminated by tubular secretion, sensitive CYP3A4 substrates, and P‑gp substrates.

## 7. Adverse Effects
- The adverse drug reaction profile below includes events reported from clinical trials and postmarketing experience. Frequencies are derived from pooled 5‑week placebo‑controlled trials and are shown where available. Postmarketing events are listed as frequency unknown where exact incidence cannot be estimated.

### Summary of key adverse reactions
- Most common adverse reactions (≥5% and at least twice placebo): nausea, dyspepsia, constipation, vomiting, hypertension, abdominal pain, diarrhea, tachycardia, dizziness, gastroesophageal reflux disease.
- Other notable safety findings: elevations in liver enzymes (ALT/AST), urinary retention (including cases requiring catheterization), increases in heart rate on ambulatory monitoring, anticholinergic CNS effects (dizziness, confusion, hallucinations, somnolence), angioedema.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- CYP2D6 inhibitors:
  - Clinical implication: CYP2D6 contributes significantly to xanomeline metabolism. Strong CYP2D6 inhibitors may raise xanomeline plasma concentrations and increase adverse reactions.
  - Management: Monitor patients for increased frequency/severity of adverse reactions when coadministered with strong CYP2D6 inhibitors.
- Drugs eliminated by active tubular secretion:
  - Clinical implication: Trospium is substantially renally excreted by active tubular secretion. Co‑administration with drugs eliminated by active tubular secretion may increase plasma concentrations of trospium and/or the concomitant drug due to competition, increasing adverse reactions.
  - Management: Monitor for increased adverse reactions related to either drug; consider dose adjustment or alternative therapy as clinically indicated.
- Oral drugs that are sensitive CYP3A4 substrates:
  - Clinical implication: Xanomeline transiently inhibits intestinal CYP3A4 (local gut effect, not systemic), which can increase systemic exposure of orally administered sensitive CYP3A4 substrates.
  - Management: Monitor for increased or prolonged effects/adverse reactions of such substrates and manage accordingly.
- Oral drugs that are substrates of P‑glycoprotein:
  - Clinical implication: Xanomeline transiently inhibits intestinal P‑gp; coadministration with oral P‑gp substrates may increase exposure.
  - Management: Monitor for increased adverse reactions of P‑gp substrates.
- Other antimuscarinic (anticholinergic) drugs:
  - Clinical implication: Additive anticholinergic effects can increase risk of dry mouth, constipation, urinary retention, cognitive/central anticholinergic effects.
  - Management: Avoid or monitor closely when combining with other antimuscarinic agents; consider dose reduction or alternative agents.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Additive anticholinergic effects with other antimuscarinics or drugs with anticholinergic properties may increase CNS and peripheral anticholinergic adverse events.
- Concomitant medications affecting heart rate or blood pressure should be used with caution given observed increases in heart rate and reports of hypertension.

### Clinically significant interactions and management
- Strong CYP2D6 inhibitors: monitor for xanomeline-related adverse reactions; adjust therapy as needed.
- Drugs eliminated by active tubular secretion: monitor and manage for increased trospium and/or concomitant drug toxicity.
- Sensitive CYP3A4 oral substrates and P‑gp substrates: monitor for increased exposure/effects; consider alternatives if clinically significant.
- Combination with other anticholinergic agents: monitor for augmented anticholinergic adverse reactions; avoid combinations when possible.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **xanomeline**: muscarinic receptor agonist (central muscarinic agonist activity implicated in therapeutic effect in schizophrenia).
- **trospium**: antimuscarinic (anticholinergic) agent included to mitigate peripheral muscarinic adverse effects.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption:
  - Oral administration; bioavailability and specific PK parameters not fully detailed in the available sources.
  - Administration timing with meals affects absorption: give at least 1 hour before or at least 2 hours after a meal.
- Distribution:
  - Not specified in available sources.
- Metabolism:
  - Xanomeline: CYP2D6 contributes significantly to metabolism.
  - Xanomeline transiently inhibits intestinal CYP3A4 and intestinal P‑glycoprotein locally (not systemic inhibition).
- Elimination:
  - Trospium: substantially excreted by the kidneys via active tubular secretion; exposure increases with reduced renal function.
  - Hepatic impairment increases systemic exposure of xanomeline.
- Pharmacogenomics:
  - Not specified in available sources beyond CYP2D6 contribution to xanomeline metabolism.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Increases in heart rate observed: mean 24‑hour heart rate increase 9.8 bpm in a dedicated ABPM study subset; peak Day 8 increase 13.5 bpm (COBENFY) vs 4.0 bpm (placebo) in pooled studies; some attenuation over continued dosing.
- Anticholinergic effects (CNS and peripheral) attributable primarily to trospium component.

## 12. Clinical Trials
### Overview of clinical development and pivotal trials
- Total exposure across clinical development: 1,594 subjects exposed to one or more doses, including 1,135 adults with schizophrenia and 389 healthy subjects.
- Duration of exposure:
  - 347 COBENFY-treated patients had ≥6 months exposure.
  - 150 patients had ≥1 year of exposure (≥50 weeks) in open‑label studies.
- Pivotal efficacy/safety trials described:
  - Two pooled 5‑week, placebo‑controlled, flexible‑dose studies in 504 adult patients with schizophrenia:
    - Design: initial dose 50 mg/20 mg twice daily for first 2 days, then 100 mg/20 mg twice daily Days 3–7; Day 8 allowed titration to 125 mg/30 mg twice daily unless not tolerated; option to return to 100 mg/20 mg twice daily.
    - In these trials, 6% of COBENFY‑treated patients and 4% of placebo discontinued due to adverse reactions. Adverse reactions leading to discontinuation in ≥1% of COBENFY-treated patients included nausea (2%) and vomiting (1%).
    - Adverse reaction rates (COBENFY vs placebo) in pooled trials (N=251 vs N=253) included: nausea 19% vs 4%; dyspepsia 18% vs 5%; constipation 17% vs 7%; vomiting 15% vs 1%; hypertension 11% vs 2%; abdominal pain 8% vs 4%; diarrhea 6% vs 2%; tachycardia 5% vs 2%; dizziness 5% vs 2%; gastroesophageal reflux disease 5% vs <1%; dry mouth 4% vs 2%; somnolence 3% vs 2%; vision blurred 3% vs 0%; salivary hypersecretion 2% vs 0%; orthostatic hypotension 2% vs 1%; cough 2% vs 1%; extrapyramidal symptoms (non‑akathisia) 2% vs <1%.
  - Dedicated 8‑week ambulatory blood pressure monitoring (ABPM) study:
    - 133 patients with schizophrenia underwent 24‑hour ABPM; 95 patients had acceptable recordings at baseline and Week 8; mean 24‑hour heart rate increased by 9.8 bpm (95% CI 7.5, 12.2) from baseline to Week 8 in that subgroup.

### Important safety outcomes from trials
- Liver enzyme elevations:
  - In the 5‑week controlled trials: ALT or AST ≥3× ULN occurred in 2.8% (6/214) of COBENFY‑treated patients vs 0.4% (1/224) of placebo.
  - Overall, 25/1,594 (1.6%) subjects exposed had elevated liver enzymes; most elevations occurred within the first month and often resolved, though some required interruption.
- Urinary retention:
  - 0.8% in pooled 5‑week controlled trials vs 0.4% placebo.
  - 3.5% in long‑term open‑label studies.
  - Four subjects required Foley catheterization among the total exposed population; some required dose reduction or discontinuation.
