# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction:
    - **United States**: US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) – Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation).
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a clinical synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 
- **Note on Sources**: At the time of preparation, only the United States PI was available for review. Consequently, all clinical recommendations and regulatory statuses refer to the US context unless otherwise specified.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Levocarnitine (INN/USP)
- **Chemical Class**: Carrier molecule (amino acid derivative) involved in fatty acid metabolism.
- **Brand Name(s)**: Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code**: Not specified in available sources (Commonly A16AA01).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s)**: 
    - Sterile solution for intravenous injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration**: Intravenous (bolus or infusion).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism**: For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD)**: For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Administration**: Administer intravenously via slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].

### Dosing by Indication
- **Metabolic Disorders**:
    - Recommended dose: 50 mg/kg.
    - **Acute Crisis**: A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - Frequency: Administer every 3 or 4 hours (q3h or q4h), and never less frequently than every 6 hours (q6h).
    - Maintenance: Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been used [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis**:
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - Frequency: Administer after each dialysis session.
    - Administration Site: Inject into the venous return line [US].

### Monitoring and Adjustments
- **Baseline**: Obtain plasma carnitine concentrations before initiating parenteral therapy.
- **Maintenance Monitoring**: Perform weekly and monthly monitoring, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels.
- **Target Range**: Maintain plasma free carnitine concentrations between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Dose Adjustment (ESRD)**: Adjustments (e.g., downward to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) concentrations [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions**: Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of injection. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and monitor the patient for recurrence if re-administration is attempted [US].
- **Neurologic Reactions (Seizures)**: Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a known seizure history, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulation Caution)**: While the injection is used in ESRD, the safety of **oral** levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO), which are normally excreted in the urine [US].
- **Parenteral Inspection**: Inspect for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (1–10%) or Noted in Trials**:
    - **Gastrointestinal**: Transient nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, diarrhea, and gastritis [US].
    - **General**: Body odor, fever, headache, pain, and injection site reactions [US].
    - **Cardiovascular**: Hypertension (reported in ~20% of ESRD patients), hypotension, tachycardia, and chest pain [US].
    - **Respiratory**: Pharyngitis, cough increase, and dyspnea [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects**:
    - Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity.
    - Seizures.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin**: Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine.
- **Management**: Monitor INR levels in patients on warfarin therapy after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **US Classification**: No evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose. However, no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women exist. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: 
    - Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied.
    - Levocarnitine concentrations in milk increase in dairy cows following administration. 
    - Weigh the benefits to the mother against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility**: No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal reproductive studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Dosing and administration in children follow the same weight-based guidelines (50 mg/kg for metabolic disorders) as adults [US].
- **Elderly Use**: Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment**: 
    - Intravenous levocarnitine is specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. 
    - **Note**: Monitor for metabolite accumulation if transitioning to oral therapy (see Section 5) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with specific metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Protein Binding**: Not bound to plasma protein or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism**: Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) and γ-butyrobetaine [US].
    - **Elimination**: Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics**: In ESRD patients, IV administration increases plasma carnitine concentrations. Normal trough levels are 40–50 µmol/L; treatment aims to correct sub-normal levels (often <20 µmol/L in deficiency) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies**: A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis (20 mg/kg three times weekly) showed that trough concentrations stabilized or increased above normal ranges (54–180 µmol/L) within one week.
- **Dose-Ranging Studies**: Trials using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Clinical Outcome Note**: While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the effect on specific clinical outcomes (e.g., malaise, muscle weakness) has not been definitively determined in this population [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling**: Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].
- **Stability (Compatibility)**: Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature in Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s solution in concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL when stored in PVC bags [US].
