# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved labeling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/generic name). Levocarnitine is a carrier molecule essential for transporting long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [United States].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Clear, colorless sterile solution for intravenous injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [United States].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for both acute and chronic management of patients with inborn metabolic errors resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [United States].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Administration is strictly via the intravenous route [United States].
- Parenteral products must be visually inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use [United States].

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [United States].
- **Loading Doses:** In cases of severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 or 4 hours (and no less frequently than every 6 hours) over the first 24 hours [United States].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically remain in the 50 mg/kg range, though therapy may require titration. The highest recorded clinical dose is 300 mg/kg [United States].
- **Monitoring:** Pre-therapy plasma carnitine levels are recommended. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [United States].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Initial Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [United States].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Initiation is often prompted by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levels below 40–50 µmol/L. Adjustments (including downward titration to 5 mg/kg) may be implemented as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known:** There are no listed contraindications for levocarnitine injection in the available source [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Hypersensitivity Reactions
- Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in dialysis patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. Treatment should be discontinued immediately if a severe reaction occurs. Re-administration requires careful risk-benefit assessment and close monitoring [United States].

### Neurologic Precautions (Seizures)
- Seizures have been reported in patients with and without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed following oral or intravenous levocarnitine [United States].

### Renal Insufficiency and Oral Administration
- While the injection is used in dialysis, the safety of **oral** levocarnitine in renal impairment has not been established. Chronic high-dose oral use in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites: trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (ESRD Clinical Trials)
Based on placebo-controlled trials in chronic hemodialysis patients (incidence ≥5%):
- **Body as a Whole:** Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (21–35%), flu syndrome (15–29%), chest pain (6–15%), and headache (3–37%) [United States].
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), and tachycardia (5–9%) [United States].
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%), and abdominal pain (5–21%) [United States].
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), and dyspnea (3–11%) [United States].
- **Other:** Dizziness (10–18%), hypercalcemia (6–15%), and asthenia (8–12%) [United States].

### Postmarketing Experience
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent reports of gastritis and body odor [United States].
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (see Warnings and Precautions) [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended to monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Not specifically categorised by letter in recent documentation, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits using 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies in nursing mothers have not been conducted. While carnitine is a normal component of human milk, studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations after exogenous administration. The risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefit of supplementation for the mother [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg) as described in the Dosage and Administration section [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis (see Section 3). Caution is advised regarding the accumulation of metabolites if oral formulations are used in this population [United States].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [United States].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Levocarnitine alleviates metabolic abnormalities in patients with inborn errors (e.g., glutaric aciduria II, methyl malonic aciduria) by clearing toxic acylCoA compounds [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. 
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [United States].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Study:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg thrice weekly after dialysis showed that trough levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. 
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses confirmed a dose-dependent increase in trough levocarnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks. However, the effect of this increase on specific clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [United States].
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [United States].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription only (Rx-only).
