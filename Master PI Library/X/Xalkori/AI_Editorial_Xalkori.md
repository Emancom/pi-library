# Editorial Product Information: Crizotinib

## Editorial Note
- The PI documents from the following jurisdictions contributed to this editorial: **Australian (TGA)** and **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of official product information intended for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial overview and not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing in a specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Crizotinib (a tyrosine kinase inhibitor).
- **Brand Name:** **Xalkori**.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Capsules (Hard Gelatin):** 200 mg and 250 mg [Australian, US].
    - **Oral Pellets:** 20 mg, 50 mg, and 150 mg (supplied in shells to be opened) [US-approved; not specified in Australian sources].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer.

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC):** 
    - Treatment of patients with anaplastic lymphoma kinase (ALK)-positive advanced or metastatic NSCLC [Australian, US].
    - Treatment of patients with ROS1-positive advanced or metastatic NSCLC [Australian, US].
- **Anaplastic Large Cell Lymphoma (ALCL):** Pediatric patients (1 year and older) and young adults with relapsed or refractory systemic ALK-positive ALCL [US-approved; not Australian-approved].
- **Inflammatory Myofibroblastic Tumor (IMT):** Adult and pediatric patients (1 year and older) with unresectable, recurrent, or refractory ALK-positive IMT [US-approved; not Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Adult Patients (NSCLC and IMT):** 250 mg taken orally twice daily [Australian, US].
- **Pediatric and Young Adult Patients (ALCL and IMT):** 280 mg/m² orally twice daily, adjusted for body surface area (BSA) [US-approved].

### Administration Instructions
- **General:** May be taken with or without food. Capsules must be swallowed whole [Australian, US].
- **Pellets [US]:** Shells must be opened; pellets are emptied into the mouth or a dry dosing aid. Do not swallow the shell, and do not chew or crush the pellets.
- **Vomiting:** If vomiting occurs after a dose, do not take an additional dose; take the next dose at the regular time [US].

### Dose Modifications
Dose reductions for toxicity in adults follow a tiered approach:
1. **First reduction:** 200 mg BID.
2. **Second reduction:** 250 mg once daily.
3. **Discontinuation:** If unable to tolerate 250 mg once daily, permanently discontinue [Australian, US].

### Missed Dose
If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as remembered unless it is within 6 hours of the next scheduled dose. Do not double doses [Australian, US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to crizotinib or any excipients [Australian]. 
- **US labeling:** Lists "None" under the contraindications section of the Highlights.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Drug-induced hepatotoxicity with fatal outcomes (0.1%) has occurred. Monitor liver function tests (ALT, AST, Bilirubin) every 2 weeks for the first 2 months, then monthly [Australian, US].
- **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD)/Pneumonitis:** Severe, life-threatening, or fatal ILD/pneumonitis occurred in up to 2.9% of patients. Permanently discontinue for any grade of drug-related ILD/pneumonitis [Australian, US].
- **QT Interval Prolongation:** Crizotinib can prolong the QT interval. Avoid in patients with congenital long QT syndrome. Monitor ECGs and electrolytes in patients with heart failure, bradyarrhythmias, or those taking QT-prolonging medications [Australian, US].
- **Bradycardia:** Symptomatic bradycardia can occur (approx. 13%). Monitor heart rate and blood pressure regularly [Australian, US].
- **Severe Visual Loss:** Grade 4 visual field defects with vision loss reported (0.2%). Discontinue in patients with new onset of severe visual loss [Australian, US].
- **Gastrointestinal Toxicity [US]:** High incidence (up to 100% in ALCL) of nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea in pediatric and young adult patients. Use standard antiemetic and antidiarrheal prophylaxis.
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Can cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status before initiation [Australian, US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Strong inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, itraconazole) increase crizotinib exposure. Avoid concomitant use [Australian, US].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Strong inducers (e.g., rifampicin, St. John's wort) decrease crizotinib exposure. Avoid concomitant use [Australian, US].
- **CYP3A4 Substrates:** Crizotinib is a moderate inhibitor of CYP3A. Exercise caution with narrow therapeutic index substrates (e.g., alfentanil, ciclosporin, tacrolimus) [Australian, US].
- **P-glycoprotein (P-gp):** Crizotinib inhibits P-gp in vitro; may increase concentrations of P-gp substrates [Australian].
- **Bradycardic Agents:** Increased risk of symptomatic bradycardia when used with beta-blockers, verapamil, diltiazem, or digoxin [Australian].
- **Gastric pH Agents:** No clinically meaningful effect with proton-pump inhibitors (e.g., esomeprazole) [Australian, US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - [Australian: Category D] 
    - [US: Risk of embryo-fetal toxicity based on animal data]. 
    - Advise females to use effective contraception during treatment and for 45 days [US] or 90 days [Australian] after the final dose.
    - Advise males with female partners to use condoms during treatment and for 90 days after the final dose [Australian, US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety not established. Advise against breastfeeding during treatment and for 45 days after the final dose [US].
- **Fertility:** May impair male and female fertility based on nonclinical findings [Australian, US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - [US: Approved for systemic ALK-positive ALCL and IMT in patients 1 year and older].
    - [Australian: Safety and efficacy not established].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy compared to younger patients; no starting dose adjustment required [Australian, US].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - Mild/Moderate (CrCl 30–89 mL/min): No adjustment needed.
    - Severe (CrCl < 30 mL/min, not requiring dialysis): Reduce dose [Australian: 250 mg daily; US: 2nd dose reduction level].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - Mild: No adjustment needed.
    - Moderate: Reduce dose [Australian: 200 mg BID; US: 1st dose reduction level].
    - Severe: Reduce dose [Australian: max 250 mg daily; US: 2nd dose reduction level].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibitor of receptor tyrosine kinases including ALK, ROS1, Hepatocyte Growth Factor Receptor (HGFR, c-Met), and RON [Australian, US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Bioavailability 43%; Tmax 4–6 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein bound (91%); Vss 1772 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP3A4/5.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life 42 hours; excreted primarily in feces (63%) and urine (22%) [Australian, US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **PROFILE 1014:** Randomized trial in previously untreated ALK-positive NSCLC showing significantly longer Progression-Free Survival (PFS) with crizotinib (10.9 months) compared to chemotherapy (7.0 months).
- **PROFILE 1007:** Randomized trial in previously treated ALK-positive NSCLC showing crizotinib superior to chemotherapy in PFS (7.7 vs 3.0 months).
- **Study ADVL0912 [US]:** Single-arm trial in pediatric/young adult ALCL and pediatric IMT showing objective response rates (ORR) of 88% and 86%, respectively.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Australian:** Store below 30°C.
- **US:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C).

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australian:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Medicine).
- **US:** Rx-only.
