# Editorial Product Information: Lefamulin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial document was developed using official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of lefamulin product information intended for use by healthcare professionals. It aims to consolidate clinical data into a structured, easily accessible format.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labelling for definitive regional requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Lefamulin acetate (a semi-synthetic pleuromutilin antibacterial).
- **Brand Name:** **Xenleta** [US].
- **ATC Code:** J01XG01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Injection:** 150 mg of lefamulin in 15 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride (clear, colorless solution) in a single-dose vial [US].
    - **Tablets:** 600 mg of lefamulin (available as blue, oval, film-coated tablets; imprinted with ‘LEF 600’) [US].
- **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion and Oral (PO).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Nabriva Therapeutics US, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Community-Acquired Bacterial Pneumonia (CABP):** Indicated for the treatment of adults with CABP caused by susceptible microorganisms, including *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Staphylococcus aureus* (methicillin-susceptible isolates), *Haemophilus influenzae*, *Legionella pneumophila*, *Mycoplasma pneumoniae*, and *Chlamydophila pneumoniae* [US].
- **Usage Note:** To mitigate the development of drug-resistant bacteria, lefamulin should only be utilized for infections proven or strongly suspected to be bacterial.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing for CABP
| Route | Dosage | Duration |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Intravenous** | 150 mg every 12 hours (60-minute infusion) | 5 to 7 days |
| **Oral** | 600 mg every 12 hours | 5 days |

- **IV-to-Oral Switch:** Patients initiated on IV therapy may be switched to oral tablets (600 mg every 12 hours) to complete the treatment course [US].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as possible, provided the next scheduled dose is at least 8 hours away. If less than 8 hours remain, the missed dose should be skipped [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Oral:** Tablets must be swallowed whole with water (6 to 8 ounces). Administer at least 1 hour before or 2 hours after a meal. Do not crush or divide [US].
- **Intravenous:** Must be diluted in the supplied 250 mL citrate-buffered 0.9% sodium chloride diluent bag before infusion. Infuse over 60 minutes [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to lefamulin, other pleuromutilin class antibacterials, or any excipients [US].
- **Drug Interactions (Oral Formulation):** Concomitant use of **Xenleta** tablets with CYP3A substrates known to prolong the QT interval (e.g., pimozide) is contraindicated [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **QT Prolongation:** Lefamulin may prolong the QT interval. Use should be avoided in patients with known QT prolongation, ventricular arrhythmias (including torsades de pointes), or those taking Class IA or Class III antiarrhythmics. Caution is advised in patients with renal failure requiring dialysis or hepatic impairment due to increased risks of metabolic disturbances leading to QT prolongation [US].
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Animal data suggest lefamulin may cause fetal harm. Pregnancy status should be verified in females of reproductive potential prior to treatment [US].
- **Clostridioides difficile-associated Diarrhea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all systemic antibacterials. Evaluate patients if diarrhea occurs [US].
- **Monitoring:** ECG monitoring is recommended during treatment for patients predisposed to QT prolongation [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency of Adverse Reactions (Incidence ≥2%)
| Adverse Reaction | IV Lefamulin (N=273) | Oral Lefamulin (N=368) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Diarrhea** | <2% | 12% |
| **Administration site reactions** | 7% | N/A |
| **Nausea** | 3% | 5% |
| **Vomiting** | <2% | 3% |
| **Hepatic enzyme elevation** | 3% | 2% |
| **Hypokalemia** | 3% | <2% |
| **Insomnia** | 3% | <2% |
| **Headache** | 2% | <2% |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** QT prolongation, CDAD, and serious hypersensitivity reactions. In clinical trials, serious adverse reactions occurred in 5.6% of patients [US].
- **Less Common (<2%):** Anemia, thrombocytopenia, atrial fibrillation, palpitations, abdominal pain, and anxiety [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Strong/Moderate CYP3A or P-gp Inducers:** Concomitant use reduces lefamulin exposure. Avoid use unless the clinical benefit outweighs the risk; monitor for reduced efficacy [US].
- **Strong CYP3A or P-gp Inhibitors:** Avoid use with oral lefamulin as it significantly increases exposure [US].
- **Moderate CYP3A or P-gp Inhibitors:** Monitor for increased adverse effects when used with oral lefamulin [US].
- **CYP3A4 Substrates:** Oral lefamulin increases the exposure of sensitive CYP3A4 substrates (e.g., midazolam, simvastatin). Monitor for toxicities related to the substrate [US].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **QT Prolonging Drugs:** Avoid co-administration with Class IA (e.g., quinidine) or Class III (e.g., amiodarone) antiarrhythmics, antipsychotics, erythromycin, or moxifloxacin due to additive effects on the QT interval [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned under current PLLR labeling; however, warnings for fetal harm exist]. Animal studies showed post-implantation loss, stillbirths, and malformations (cleft palate, heart defects) at exposures below clinical levels.
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no human data on the presence of lefamulin in milk. Due to potential risks (e.g., QT prolongation), lactating women should pump and discard human milk during treatment and for **2 days** after the final dose [US].
- **Contraception:** Females of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for 2 days after the final dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients <18 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy were observed between patients ≥65 years and younger adults [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required, including for patients on hemodialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **IV Administration:** Reduce dose to 150 mg every **24 hours** in patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class C). No adjustment for Class A or B [US].
    - **Oral Administration:** Not recommended for patients with moderate (Child-Pugh Class B) or severe (Child-Pugh Class C) impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Systemic pleuromutilin that inhibits bacterial protein synthesis by binding to the 23S rRNA of the 50S subunit at the peptidyl transferase center (PTC). It exhibits an "induced fit" mechanism to prevent correct positioning of tRNA [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Bioavailability:** Approximately 25% (oral).
    - **Distribution:** High protein binding (94.8% to 97.1%).
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized by CYP3A4.
    - **Excretion:** Primarily via feces (77–88%) [US].
- **Microbiology:** Bactericidal against *S. pneumoniae*, *H. influenzae*, and *M. pneumoniae*; bacteriostatic against *S. aureus* and *S. pyogenes* [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** Lefamulin was evaluated in two multicenter, non-inferiority trials (Trial 1 and Trial 2) comparing it to moxifloxacin (± linezolid in Trial 1).
- **Trial 1 (IV-to-Oral Switch):** Early Clinical Response (ECR) was 87.3% for lefamulin vs. 90.2% for moxifloxacin (Treatment difference -2.9%) [US].
- **Trial 2 (Oral Only):** ECR was 90.8% for lefamulin vs. 90.8% for moxifloxacin (Treatment difference 0.1%) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Injection Vials:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F). Do not freeze [US].
- **Diluted IV Solution:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature or 48 hours when refrigerated [US].
- **Tablets:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Diluent Bags:** Store at 2°C to 25°C (36°F to 77°F) [US].
