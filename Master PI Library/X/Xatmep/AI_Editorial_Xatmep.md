# Editorial Product Information: Methotrexate (Oral Solution)

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of the product information for **Xatmep** (methotrexate) oral solution, synthesized for healthcare professionals. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the United States (FDA) source documentation was provided.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Methotrexate (as methotrexate sodium).
- **Chemical Class:** Folate analog metabolic inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** **Xatmep** [US].
- **ATC Code:** L01BA01 (Methotrexate).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Oral solution, 2.5 mg/mL (equivalent to 2.74 mg methotrexate sodium/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Azurity Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia (ALL):** Indicated for the treatment of paediatric patients as a component of a multi-phase, combination chemotherapy maintenance regimen [US-approved].
- **Polyarticular Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis (pJIA):** Indicated for the management of paediatric patients with active pJIA who have had an insufficient response to, or are intolerant of, first-line therapy (e.g., full-dose NSAIDs) [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Xatmep** is specifically indicated and studied for paediatric populations in the provided sources. Use of this specific oral solution in adults is not detailed in the available documentation [US].

### Dosing by Indication (Paediatric)
- **Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia (ALL):** The recommended starting dose is 20 mg/m² administered **once weekly**. Dosing must be adjusted based on periodic monitoring of absolute neutrophil count (ANC) and platelet count to manage haematological toxicity [US].
- **Polyarticular Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis (pJIA):** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/m² administered **once weekly**. Doses should be tailored to individual response. While doses up to 30 mg/m²/week are used, doses exceeding 20 mg/m²/week significantly increase the risk of serious toxicities, particularly bone marrow suppression [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Frequency:** **Once weekly only.** Mistaken daily administration has resulted in fatal toxicity [US].
- **Measurement:** Must be measured with an accurate milliliter measuring device. Household teaspoons are not acceptable [US].
- **Food Effect:** Administration with food does not affect the AUC but decreases Cmax by 50% and delays absorption [US].
- **Switching Routes:** For doses between 20–30 mg/m²/week, alternative routes (parenteral) may offer better absorption and fewer gastrointestinal side effects [US].

### Titration and Missed Doses
- **pJIA:** Therapeutic response typically begins within 3 to 6 weeks, with continued improvement for 12 weeks or longer [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Patients should contact their healthcare provider for instructions if a weekly dose is missed [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy in non-malignant disease:** Contraindicated in pregnant patients being treated for pJIA [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Severe hypersensitivity to methotrexate [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
- **Severe Toxic Reactions:** Can cause fatal toxicities including bone marrow suppression, serious infections, renal damage, gastrointestinal toxicity (perforation), hepatic toxicity (fibrosis/cirrhosis), pulmonary toxicity (interstitial pneumonitis), and severe dermatologic reactions.
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Can cause fetal death and congenital malformations.
- **Medication Errors:** Inadvertent daily administration of weekly doses can be fatal.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Secondary Malignancies:** Can occur at any dose. Lymphoproliferative disease may regress upon methotrexate withdrawal [US].
- **Immunisations:** May be ineffective. Live virus vaccines are not recommended due to the risk of disseminated infection [US].
- **Third-Space Accumulation:** Methotrexate exits slowly from accumulations (e.g., ascites, pleural effusions), prolonging terminal half-life. These should be evacuated prior to treatment [US].
- **Laboratory Monitoring:** 
    - Baseline: Haematology, hepatic, and renal function. 
    - Ongoing: Haematology at least monthly; renal and liver function every 1 to 2 months [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects
The most frequent reactions across indications include ulcerative stomatitis, leukopenia, nausea, and abdominal distress [US].

**Incidence in pJIA (5–20 mg/m²/week):**
| Adverse Reaction | Frequency (%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Elevated Liver Function Tests | 14% |
| Gastrointestinal Reactions (Nausea, Vomiting, Diarrhea) | 11% |
| Stomatitis | 2% |
| Leukopenia | 2% |
| Headache | 1.2% |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Haematologic:** Pancytopenia, aplastic anaemia, neutropenia.
- **Hepatobiliary:** Fatal liver failure, fibrosis, cirrhosis.
- **Respiratory:** Respiratory failure, interstitial obstructive pulmonary disease.
- **Dermatologic:** Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN).
- **Infections:** Opportunistic infections (e.g., *Pneumocystis jiroveci* pneumonia), Sepsis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Oral Antibiotics (Penicillins):** May reduce renal clearance, increasing methotrexate serum levels and toxicity [US].
- **Trimethoprim/Sulfamethoxazole:** Increases risk of bone marrow suppression [US].
- **Hepatotoxins:** Increased risk of hepatotoxicity when used with agents like azathioprine, retinoids, or sulfasalazine [US].
- **Probenecid:** Reduces renal elimination [US].
- **Nitrous Oxide:** Potentiates effects on folate-dependent pathways, increasing toxicity. Avoid simultaneous use [US].
- **NSAIDs, Aspirin, and Steroids:** May elevate and prolong serum methotrexate levels. Deaths from severe haematologic and GI toxicity have been reported with concomitant high-dose methotrexate [US].
- **Theophylline:** Methotrexate may decrease theophylline clearance [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - [US]: Classified as a teratogen. Contraindicated in pJIA. In neoplastic disease, use only if potential benefits outweigh risks to the fetus.
    - Pregnancy testing is required prior to initiation.
- **Breastfeeding:** Methotrexate is present in human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in the infant (e.g., myelosuppression), breastfeeding is not recommended during therapy [US].
- **Contraception:** 
    - Females: Use effective contraception during and for 6 months after the final dose [US].
    - Males: Use effective contraception during and for 3 months after the final dose [US].
- **Fertility:** May cause impairment of fertility, oligospermia, and menstrual dysfunction, which may be irreversible [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness established for ALL and pJIA [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed for this formulation in provided sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Elimination is reduced. Monitor closely; consider dose reduction or discontinuation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not studied; however, these patients are at increased risk for hepatotoxicity. Consider dose adjustments [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits dihydrofolic acid reductase, preventing the reduction of dihydrofolates to tetrahydrofolates. This interferes with DNA synthesis and cellular replication. In pJIA, the mechanism is unknown but may involve immune modulation [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Dose-dependent. Bioavailability is higher at lower doses. Peak concentration (Tmax) is 0.7 to 4 hours [US].
    - **Distribution:** Approximately 50% protein bound. Does not penetrate the blood-cerebrospinal fluid barrier in therapeutic oral doses [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Undergoes hepatic and intracellular metabolism to polyglutamated forms (active) and 7-hydroxymethotrexate [US].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal via glomerular filtration and active tubular secretion. Terminal half-life in children ranges from 0.7 to 5.8 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **pJIA Trial:** A 6-month, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of 127 paediatric patients showed that 10 mg/m² weekly provided significant clinical improvement compared to placebo (based on articular-severity scores and global assessments). A dose of 5 mg/m² was not significantly more effective than placebo [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Dispensing:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F). Keep the container tightly closed [US].
- **Post-Dispensing:** Patients may store at room temperature (20°C to 25°C) for up to 60 days. Discard any unused portion after 60 days. Avoid freezing and excessive heat [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** [US]: Rx-only.
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Special Handling:** Methotrexate is a cytotoxic drug. Follow applicable procedures for handling and disposal [US].
