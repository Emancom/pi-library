# Editorial Product Information: **Xembify** (Normal Immunoglobulin [Human] 20%)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis incorporates information from the following jurisdictions:
    - Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
    - US (Food and Drug Administration)
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically accurate overview for healthcare professionals. 
- This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Normal Immunoglobulin (Human) 20% solution. The US jurisdiction utilizes the suffix "human-klhw". 
- **Chemical Class:** Plasma-derived immunoglobulin G (IgG).
- **Brand Name:** **Xembify**
- **ATC Code:** J06BA01 (Immunoglobulins, normal human, for extravascular administration).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Solution for subcutaneous injection, 200 mg/mL (20%). 
    - Available in single-use vials: 1 g/5 mL, 2 g/10 mL, 4 g/20 mL, and 10 g/50 mL.
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** 
    - Grifols Australia Pty Ltd [Australian]
    - Grifols Therapeutics LLC [US]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Primary Immunodeficiency (PID):** Indicated for replacement therapy in adult and paediatric patients (2 years of age and older in the [US]; 0 to 18 years in [Australia]). This includes, but is not limited to, congenital agammaglobulinaemia, common variable immunodeficiency (CVID), Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome, and severe combined immunodeficiencies.
- **Symptomatic Hypogammaglobulinaemia:** Indicated for patients with secondary hypogammaglobulinaemia due to underlying disease or treatment [Australian-approved; not explicitly listed in the US indication].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
Doses must be individualised based on the patient’s clinical response and serum IgG trough levels.

### Switching from Intravenous Immunoglobulin (IVIG)
- **Initial Weekly Dose:**
    - [US]: Calculate the weekly equivalent of the previous IVIG dose and multiply by a dose adjustment factor of 1.37.
    - [Australian]: Divide the previous IVIG dose (in grams) by the number of weeks between doses to establish the initial weekly dose.
- **Timing:** Begin **Xembify** treatment one week after the patient's last IVIG infusion.

### Switching from Subcutaneous Immunoglobulin (SCIg)
- Patients should maintain the same weekly dose (in grams) as their previous SCIg treatment.

### Treatment-Naïve Patients
- [US]: Administer a loading dose of 150 mg/kg/day for 5 consecutive days. Maintenance dosing of 150 mg/kg/week should begin at Day 8.
- [Australian]: A loading dose of 0.2 to 0.5 g/kg (1 to 2.5 mL/kg) may be required, potentially divided over several days (max daily dose 0.1 to 0.15 g/kg).

### Dosing Frequency
- Can be administered at regular intervals from daily up to every two weeks (biweekly). 
- To calculate biweekly dosing, multiply the calculated weekly dose by 2.
- For frequent dosing (2–7 times per week), divide the weekly dose by the number of desired administration days.

### Administration Instructions
- **Sites:** Abdomen, thigh, upper arm, and lateral hip. Avoid bony areas, scars, or inflamed/infected skin. 
- **Simultaneous Sites:** Up to 6 sites may be infused simultaneously [US]. Sites should be at least 5 cm (2 inches) apart.
- **Infusion Rates:**
    - Initial rate: Do not exceed 25 mL/hour/site. 
    - Maintenance: If tolerated, may be increased to 35 mL/hour/site.
    - Paediatrics (<10 years): Maximum volume 25 mL/site; maximum rate 25 mL/hour/site.
- **Handling:** Allow solution to reach room temperature before use. Do not shake. Use within 2 hours if transferred to a siliconized syringe; otherwise, administer within 8 hours of opening.

## 4. Contraindications
- History of anaphylactic or severe systemic reactions to human immunoglobulin.
- Known hypersensitivity to any excipients, specifically polysorbate 80 [US].
- IgA-deficient patients with antibodies against IgA and a history of hypersensitivity to human immunoglobulin treatment.
- **Xembify** must not be administered intravascularly or intramuscularly [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning: Thrombosis [US]
- **Thrombosis** may occur with immune globulin products. Risk factors include advanced age, prolonged immobilisation, hypercoagulable conditions, history of venous/arterial thrombosis, use of estrogens, indwelling vascular catheters, hyperviscosity, and cardiovascular risk factors. 
- For patients at risk, administer at the minimum dose and infusion rate practicable. Ensure adequate hydration and monitor for signs of thrombosis and blood viscosity.

### General Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity:** Severe reactions, including anaphylaxis, can occur. If a reaction occurs, discontinue the infusion immediately. 
- **Renal Dysfunction/Failure:** Acute renal failure, acute tubular necrosis, and osmotic nephrosis have occurred with IG products. While **Xembify** is sucrose-free, monitor renal function (BUN/serum creatinine) in at-risk patients (e.g., age >65, diabetes, sepsis).
- **Aseptic Meningitis Syndrome (AMS):** May occur within hours to 2 days of treatment. Symptoms include severe headache, neck stiffness, and photophobia.
- **Hemolysis:** IgG products can contain blood group antibodies (e.g., A, B, D) that act as hemolysins. Monitor for clinical signs of hemolysis and hemolytic anaemia.
- **Transfusion-Related Acute Lung Injury (TRALI):** Characterised by severe respiratory distress and pulmonary edema, typically within 1–6 hours post-treatment.
- **Transmissible Agents:** As a plasma-derived product, the risk of transmitting infectious agents (e.g., viruses, vCJD) cannot be totally excluded, despite screening and inactivation steps.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions occur more frequently in treatment-naïve patients or those switching products.

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Local infusion site reactions (erythema, redness). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Infusion site pain, swelling, bruising, nodules, pruritus, induration, headache, cough, diarrhoea, nausea, arthralgia, back pain, pyrexia, fatigue, and rash. |
| **Uncommon/Rare (<1%)** | Migraine, dizziness, tachycardia, hypotension, dyspnea, abdominal pain, and lymphadenopathy. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, thrombosis, acute renal failure, AMS, and TRALI.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Live Attenuated Virus Vaccines:** Passively transferred antibodies may interfere with the efficacy of live vaccines (e.g., measles, mumps, rubella, varicella) for 6 weeks to 3 months. For measles, interference may persist for up to 1 year. Antibody status should be checked if vaccination is required shortly after treatment.
- **Laboratory Tests:** Passively transferred antibodies may lead to false-positive results in serological testing and the direct antiglobulin (Coombs') test.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - [Australian]: Safety not established in controlled trials. Use with caution. IgG is known to cross the placenta increasingly during the third trimester.
    - [US]: Should be given to a pregnant woman only if clearly indicated. No animal reproduction studies have been conducted.
- **Breastfeeding:** Immunoglobulins are excreted into human milk. The benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need for the mother and potential adverse effects on the infant.
- **Fertility:** Clinical experience suggests no harmful effects on fertility are expected.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Evaluated in patients aged 2–16 years. Safety and efficacy profiles were similar to adults. Data for children under 2 years of age are not available [US] or are limited [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Caution is advised in patients ≥65 years due to increased risk of thrombosis and renal complications. Use the minimum dose and rate practicable.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No specific dose adjustments are provided in source documents, but close monitoring of renal function is mandatory for at-risk patients. No specific considerations noted for hepatic impairment.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Supplies a broad spectrum of opsonising and neutralising IgG antibodies against bacterial, viral, and parasitic agents. It also contains antibodies capable of modulating immune cell activity.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Contains IgG subclasses in proportions similar to native human plasma (purity ≥98% IgG).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak serum levels (Cmax) are achieved approximately 3 days after SC administration.
    - **Distribution:** IgG and IgG-complexes are broken down by the reticuloendothelial system.
    - **Steady State:** Achieved after approximately 13–14 weeks of weekly SC dosing. 

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **North American Study:** 49 subjects with PID (aged 2–72 years). The annual rate of Serious Bacterial Infections (SBIs) was 0.05 per subject-year.
- **European/Australian Study:** 61 subjects with PID (aged 2–69 years). The annual rate of SBIs was 0.017 per subject-year. 
- In both trials, sustained IgG trough levels were maintained, and the majority of adverse events were mild-to-moderate local reactions.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Refrigeration:** Store at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze.
- **Room Temperature:** May be stored at temperatures not exceeding 25°C for up to 6 months. Once moved to room temperature, the product must be used or discarded; it should not be returned to the refrigerator.
- **Light Protection:** Keep the vial in the outer carton.
- **Shelf Life:** 3 years.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australian Classification:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **US Classification:** Rx-only.
