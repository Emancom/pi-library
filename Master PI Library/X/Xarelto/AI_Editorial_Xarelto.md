# Editorial Product Information: Xarelto (rivaroxaban)

## Editorial Note
- Jurisdictions included in this synthesis: Australian (TGA) and US (FDA).
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **Xarelto** product information, synthesized from multiple regulatory sources for the use of healthcare professionals. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Always consult the current local PI.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Rivaroxaban (a highly selective direct Factor Xa inhibitor).
- **Brand Name:** **Xarelto**.
- **ATC Code:** B01AF01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Film-coated tablets:** 2.5 mg, 10 mg, 15 mg, and 20 mg [Australian, US].
    - **Granules for oral suspension:** 1 mg/mL once reconstituted [US-approved; not specified in Australian source].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Sponsor/Manufacturer:** 
    - Bayer Australia Ltd [Australian].
    - Janssen Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Non-valvular Atrial Fibrillation (AF):** Reduction of the risk of stroke and systemic embolism in adult patients with at least one additional risk factor for stroke.
- **Treatment of Venous Thromboembolism (VTE):** Treatment of deep vein thrombosis (DVT) and pulmonary embolism (PE).
- **Prevention of Recurrent VTE:** Reduction in the risk of recurrent DVT and/or PE following initial treatment.
- **VTE Prophylaxis (Orthopedic Surgery):** Prophylaxis of DVT (which may lead to PE) in adult patients undergoing elective total hip or knee replacement surgery.
- **VTE Prophylaxis (Medical Patients):** Prophylaxis of VTE and VTE-related death in acutely ill medical patients at risk for thromboembolic complications but not at high risk of bleeding [US-approved; not specified in Australian source].
- **Coronary Artery Disease (CAD):** In combination with aspirin (75–100 mg), to reduce the risk of major cardiovascular events (CV death, MI, and stroke) [US: includes patients with CAD; Australian: includes CAD and/or PAD].
- **Peripheral Artery Disease (PAD):** In combination with aspirin, to reduce the risk of major thrombotic vascular events, including acute limb ischemia and major amputation [US: includes patients post-lower extremity revascularization; Australian: includes CAD and/or PAD].
- **Pediatric Use:** Treatment of VTE and reduction in the risk of recurrent VTE in pediatric patients from birth to < 18 years after at least 5 days of initial parenteral anticoagulation [US-approved; Australian: not recommended for use under 18 years].
- **Pediatric Thromboprophylaxis:** For patients ≥ 2 years with congenital heart disease post-Fontan procedure [US-approved; Australian: not recommended].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Non-valvular AF:** 20 mg once daily. [US: take with the evening meal; Australian: take with food].
- **DVT/PE Treatment:** 15 mg twice daily with food for the first 21 days, followed by 20 mg once daily with food.
- **Recurrence Prevention (DVT/PE):** 10 mg once daily with or without food, typically after at least 6 months of standard anticoagulation.
- **Ortho Prophylaxis:** 10 mg once daily with or without food. Initial dose 6–10 hours post-surgery once haemostasis is established. Duration: 5 weeks (hip) or 2 weeks [Australian] / 12 days [US] (knee).
- **CAD/PAD:** 2.5 mg twice daily with or without food, plus aspirin (75–100 mg) once daily.
- **Acutely Ill Medical [US]:** 10 mg once daily with or without food in hospital and post-discharge (total duration 31–39 days).

### Administration Instructions
- **Swallowing difficulties:** Tablets (all strengths) may be crushed and mixed with water or apple sauce [Australian, US].
- **Enteral feeding:** Crushed tablets can be administered via gastric/nasogastric tubes [Australian, US]. Avoid administration distal to the stomach (e.g., small intestine) as absorption is reduced [US, Australian].
- **Food effect:** 15 mg and 20 mg doses must be taken with food to ensure adequate absorption. 2.5 mg and 10 mg doses may be taken regardless of food.

### Missed Dose
- **Once daily (10, 15, or 20 mg):** Take immediately on the same day. Do not double the dose the next day.
- **Twice daily (15 mg):** Take immediately to ensure 30 mg intake. Two 15 mg tablets may be taken at once [Australian, US].
- **Twice daily (2.5 mg):** Skip the missed dose and take the next dose at the regular time. Do not double the dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Active pathological bleeding** (e.g., intracranial, gastrointestinal).
- **Severe hypersensitivity** to rivaroxaban or excipients.
- **Significant hepatic disease** associated with coagulopathy leading to clinically relevant bleeding risk (Child-Pugh B and C) [Australian-approved; US lists as a warning].
- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding** [Australian-approved; US lists as a warning].
- **Renal impairment (CrCl < 15 mL/min)** [Australian-approved; US lists as avoid use].
- **Concomitant treatment with strong inhibitors of both CYP3A4 and P-gp** (e.g., HIV protease inhibitors, systemic azole-antimycotics) [Australian-approved; US lists as a warning].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
- **Premature Discontinuation:** Discontinuing **Xarelto** in the absence of adequate alternative anticoagulation increases the risk of thrombotic events (e.g., stroke).
- **Spinal/Epidural Hematoma:** Neuraxial anesthesia or spinal puncture in patients treated with **Xarelto** can result in hematomas leading to long-term or permanent paralysis. Risk factors include indwelling catheters and concomitant use of drugs affecting haemostasis (NSAIDs, platelet inhibitors).

### General Warnings
- **Hemorrhagic Risk:** Like all anticoagulants, **Xarelto** increases the risk of serious or fatal bleeding. Promptly evaluate signs of blood loss.
- **Reversal of Effect:** A specific reversal agent for **Xarelto** is not specified in these sources [Note: While idarucizumab exists for dabigatran and andexanet alfa for Xa inhibitors, these specific PI documents state no specific agent is available]. Rivaroxaban is not dialyzable. 
- **Prosthetic Heart Valves:** Not recommended for use in patients with prosthetic heart valves, including those post-TAVR (GALILEO study).
- **Antiphospholipid Syndrome (APS):** Not recommended in patients with triple-positive APS due to increased rates of recurrent thrombotic events compared to VKA therapy.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Bleeding (various sites) [US, Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Anaemia, nausea, dizziness, headache, peripheral oedema, fever, gastrointestinal pain, dyspepsia, pruritus, rash, increase in transaminases [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Tachycardia, dry mouth, hepatic impairment, allergic dermatitis, syncope, renal impairment [Australian].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Intracranial haemorrhage, spinal/epidural haematoma, fatal bleeding, Stevens-Johnson syndrome/Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis [US, Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Strong CYP3A4 and P-gp Inhibitors:** (e.g., ketoconazole, ritonavir, itraconazole). Increase rivaroxaban exposure and bleeding risk. Avoid concomitant use [US] / Contraindicated [Australian].
- **Strong CYP3A4 and P-gp Inducers:** (e.g., rifampicin, phenytoin, carbamazepine, St. John's Wort). Decrease rivaroxaban exposure and increase thrombotic risk. Avoid concomitant use [US, Australian].
- **Anticoagulants and Antiplatelets:** Concomitant use with NSAIDs, aspirin, clopidogrel, or other anticoagulants increases bleeding risk.
- **SSRIs/SNRIs:** May increase bleeding risk due to effects on platelets.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category C.** 
    - **US:** No lettered category; however, it notes potential for pregnancy-related haemorrhage. **Xarelto** should only be used if the potential benefit justifies the risk to the mother and fetus. Contraindicated in pregnancy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Rivaroxaban is excreted in animal milk. Discontinue breastfeeding or the drug, taking into account the importance of the drug to the mother. Contraindicated [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - **US:** Approved for VTE treatment (birth to <18 years) and post-Fontan thromboprophylaxis (≥2 years). Specific weight-based dosing for suspension and tablets provided. 
    - **Australian:** Not recommended for use in children under 18 years due to insufficient safety and efficacy data.
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment based on age alone, but higher bleeding rates and declining renal function in the elderly require caution.
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Mild (CrCl 50–80 mL/min):** No dose adjustment.
    - **Moderate (CrCl 30–49 mL/min):** Adjust dose in AF (15 mg QD).
    - **Severe (CrCl 15–29 mL/min):** Use with caution.
    - **ESRD (CrCl < 15 mL/min):** Contraindicated [Australian]; Avoid use [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Avoid use in patients with Child-Pugh B or C or any hepatic disease associated with coagulopathy [US, Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Highly selective, competitive, direct inhibitor of Factor Xa. It inhibits free FXa and prothrombinase activity, thereby decreasing thrombin generation. No direct effect on platelets but inhibits thrombin-induced platelet aggregation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid; $C_{max}$ in 2–4 hours. High bioavailability (80–100%) for 2.5 mg and 10 mg. Bioavailability of 20 mg is 66% (fasted) and increases with food.
    - **Distribution:** High plasma protein binding (~92–95%), primarily to albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** ~2/3 undergoes metabolic degradation (CYP3A4, CYP2J2, and CYP-independent pathways).
    - **Elimination:** ~1/3 excreted unchanged in urine; remainder as metabolites in urine and faeces. Terminal half-life 5–9 hours (young) and 11–13 hours (elderly).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ROCKET AF:** Demonstrated non-inferiority to warfarin in stroke prevention in AF.
- **EINSTEIN DVT/PE:** Demonstrated non-inferiority to enoxaparin/VKA for VTE treatment.
- **RECORD 1, 2, 3:** Demonstrated superiority to enoxaparin in VTE prevention following major orthopaedic surgery.
- **COMPASS:** Demonstrated that rivaroxaban 2.5 mg BID plus aspirin was superior to aspirin alone in reducing CV events in patients with stable CAD/PAD.
- **EINSTEIN Junior [US]:** Demonstrated safety and efficacy in paediatric VTE treatment.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Tablets:** Store below 30°C [Australian]; Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Oral Suspension [US]:** Store reconstituted suspension at room temperature (20°C to 25°C) for up to 60 days. Do not freeze.
