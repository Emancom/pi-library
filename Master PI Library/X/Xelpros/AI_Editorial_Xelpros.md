# Editorial Product Information: Latanoprost (Ophthalmic Emulsion)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (Food and Drug Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Latanoprost (prostaglandin F$_{2\alpha}$ analogue).
- **Brand Names:** **XELPROS** [United States].
- **ATC Code:** S01EE01 (Latanoprost).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Ophthalmic emulsion containing latanoprost 50 mcg/mL (0.005%) [United States].
- **Route of Administration:** Topical ophthalmic.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sun Pharmaceutical Industries, Inc. [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- Reduction of elevated intraocular pressure (IOP) in patients with open-angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is one drop in the affected eye(s) once daily, administered in the evening [United States].
- **Frequency Limit:** Dosage must not exceed once daily. Frequent administration of prostaglandin analogues may reduce the IOP-lowering effect or cause a paradoxical increase in IOP [United States].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - If multiple topical ophthalmic drugs are used, they should be administered at least five minutes apart [United States].
    - Contact lenses should be removed before instillation and may be reinserted 15 minutes after administration [United States].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, treatment should proceed with the next scheduled dose as normal; do not double the dose [United States].
- **Onset and Duration:** IOP reduction begins approximately 3 to 4 hours post-administration, reaching maximum effect at 8 to 12 hours. Reduction is maintained for at least 24 hours [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to latanoprost or any other ingredients in the formulation (e.g., potassium sorbate, castor oil) [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Pigmentation:** Increased pigmentation of the iris, periorbital tissue (eyelid), and eyelashes may occur. Iris pigmentation change (increased brown melanin content) is likely permanent. While treatment can continue, patients should be monitored regularly [United States].
- **Eyelash Changes:** Treatment may gradually increase the length, thickness, number, and pigmentation of eyelashes. These changes are typically reversible upon discontinuation [United States].
- **Intraocular Inflammation:** Use with caution in patients with a history of intraocular inflammation (e.g., uveitis, iritis). Should generally be avoided in patients with active inflammation [United States].
- **Macular Edema:** Macular edema, including cystoid macular edema, has been reported. Use with caution in aphakic patients, pseudophakic patients with a torn posterior lens capsule, or those with known risk factors for macular edema [United States].
- **Herpetic Keratitis:** Reactivation of herpes simplex keratitis has been reported. Avoid use in cases of active infection and use with caution in patients with a history of herpetic keratitis [United States].
- **Bacterial Keratitis:** Risk of bacterial keratitis is associated with the contamination of multiple-dose containers, particularly in patients with concurrent corneal disease or epithelial disruption [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Eye pain or stinging (55%), ocular hyperemia (41%), conjunctival hyperemia (15%), eye discharge (12%), and growth of eyelashes (11%) [United States].
- **Common (1–10%):** Eyelash thickening (8%), ocular itching (5%), reduced visual acuity (4%), dry eye (3%), erythema of eyelid (3%), foreign body sensation (2%), and eyelid edema (2%) [United States].
- **Uncommon to Rare (<1%):** Punctate keratitis, eyelash discoloration, and conjunctival edema [United States].
- **Serious/Systemic Reactions (Post-marketing):**
    - **Respiratory:** Asthma, asthma exacerbation, dyspnea.
    - **Dermatologic:** Toxic epidermal necrolysis, pruritus, localized skin reactions.
    - **Ocular:** Corneal edema/erosions, iris cysts, deepening of the eyelid sulcus (periorbital changes), and pseudophakic macular edema.
    - **Cardiac:** Angina, palpitations, unstable angina.
    - **Neurological/General:** Dizziness, headache, and chest pain [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Thimerosal:** In vitro studies show that precipitation occurs when Xelpros is mixed with eye drops containing thimerosal. If used concurrently, drugs should be administered at least five minutes apart [United States].
- **Prostaglandin Analogues:** The combined use of two or more prostaglandins or prostaglandin analogues is not recommended, as it may decrease the IOP-lowering effect [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified by letter, but label notes there are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. 
    - **Animal Data:** Intravenous administration in rabbits and rats at doses significantly higher than the human ophthalmic dose resulted in embryofetal lethality, malformations (e.g., cleft palate, spina bifida), and spontaneous abortion [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if latanoprost or its metabolites are excreted in human milk. Caution should be exercised, balancing the benefits of breastfeeding with the clinical need for the drug [United States].
- **Fertility:** No effects on male or female fertility were observed in rat studies at high intravenous doses [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under the age of 18 have not been established [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness have been observed between elderly and younger patients [United States].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A prostanoid selective FP receptor agonist that reduces IOP by increasing the outflow of aqueous humor, primarily via the uveoscleral pathway [United States].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Maximum IOP reduction occurs 8–12 hours after topical application [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absorbed through the cornea as an isopropyl ester prodrug and hydrolyzed to the active acid form.
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is 0.16 ± 0.02 L/kg.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized by the liver via fatty acid $\beta$-oxidation after reaching systemic circulation.
    - **Elimination:** Rapid plasma clearance (t$_{1/2}$ = 17 min). Approximately 88% of a topical dose is recovered in the urine [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- In randomized, controlled trials of patients with open-angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension (baseline IOP 23–26 mmHg), **XELPROS** administered once daily in the evening reduced IOP by 6–8 mmHg [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store at 2°C to 25°C (36°F to 77°F). Protect from light.
- **Shipping:** During shipment, the bottle may be maintained at temperatures up to 40°C (104°F) for no more than 8 days.
- **Stability:** After opening, the product can be used until the expiration date stamped on the bottle [United States].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [United States].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
