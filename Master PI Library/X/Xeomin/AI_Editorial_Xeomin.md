# Editorial Product Information: Xeomin (incobotulinumtoxinA)

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA), US (Food and Drug Administration - FDA).
*   **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of the official Product Information from multiple regulatory authorities. It is designed to provide healthcare professionals with a coherent, clinically accurate overview.
*   **Disclaimer:** This editorial is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Prescribing Information (US) for specific jurisdictional use. Prescribers should always refer to the current approved documentation within their own region.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** incobotulinumtoxinA (purified Botulinum toxin type A, 150 kD, free from complexing proteins).
*   **Chemical Class:** Acetylcholine release inhibitor; neuromuscular blocking agent.
*   **Brand Name:** **Xeomin**
*   **ATC Code:** M03AX01
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    *   Lyophilized powder for solution for injection in single-dose vials.
    *   50 Units, 100 Units [Australian, US].
    *   200 Units [US-approved].
*   **Route of Administration:** Intramuscular or intraglandular (intra-salivary gland) injection.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Merz Pharmaceuticals GmbH.

## 2. Approved Indications
### Adult Indications
*   **Cervical Dystonia (Spasmodic Torticollis):** To decrease the severity of abnormal head position and neck pain.
*   **Blepharospasm:** Treatment of benign essential blepharospasm.
*   **Upper Limb Spasticity:** Treatment of increased muscle stiffness/tone in the upper limb.
*   **Lower Limb Spasticity:** Treatment of unilateral spasticity of the lower limb affecting the ankle joint [Australian-approved].
*   **Chronic Sialorrhea:** Treatment of chronic drooling due to neurological/neurodevelopmental disorders.
*   **Upper Facial Lines:** Temporary improvement in the appearance of moderate to severe:
    *   Glabellar frown lines (associated with corrugator and/or procerus muscle activity).
    *   Lateral periorbital lines (Crow's feet) [Australian]; also referred to as lateral canthal lines [US].
    *   Horizontal forehead lines (associated with frontalis muscle activity).

### Paediatric Indications (Aged 2 to 17 years)
*   **Chronic Sialorrhea:** Symptomatic treatment due to neurological/neurodevelopmental disorders.
*   **Upper Limb Spasticity:** Treatment of increased muscle tone [Australian, US].
*   **Lower Limb Spasticity:** Symptomatic treatment of lower limb spasticity [Australian-approved; not specified as a paediatric indication in US PI].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Principles
*   **NON-INTERCHANGEABILITY:** **Xeomin** units are specific to this preparation. They are not equivalent to, nor interchangeable with, other botulinum toxin products. Potency units cannot be converted using a specific ratio to other brands.
*   **Administration:** Must only be administered by medical practitioners with suitable qualifications and experience in botulinum toxin application.
*   **Reconstitution:** Reconstitute with sterile, preservative-free 0.9% sodium chloride. Gently inject diluent to avoid foam; discard if vacuum does not pull solvent into the vial.

### Selected Dosing by Indication (Adults)
*   **Chronic Sialorrhea:** 100 Units total per session, divided in a 3:2 ratio between parotid (30 U/side) and submandibular glands (20 U/side).
*   **Cervical Dystonia:** Recommended initial dose is 120 Units per session.
*   **Blepharospasm:** Initial dose of 1.25 to 2.5 Units per site [Australian] or 50 Units total (25 Units per eye) [US]. Total dose should not exceed 100 Units per session (50 Units per eye).
*   **Upper Limb Spasticity:** Up to 400 Units total, divided among affected muscles.
*   **Upper Facial Lines:**
    *   Glabellar lines: 20 Units total (4 Units across 5 sites).
    *   Crow’s feet: 24 Units total (12 Units per side).
    *   Horizontal forehead lines: 10 to 20 Units total.

### Paediatric Dosing
*   **Sialorrhea:** Body weight-adjusted dosing (range 20 U to 75 U total) for patients $\ge$ 12 kg. Ultrasound guidance is recommended.
*   **Upper Limb Spasticity:** 8 Units/kg (max 200 U) per single limb; 16 Units/kg (max 400 U) for both limbs.

## 4. Contraindications
*   Known hypersensitivity to incobotulinumtoxinA or any excipients (human albumin, sucrose).
*   Infection or inflammation at the proposed injection site(s).
*   Generalised disorders of muscle activity, such as myasthenia gravis or Lambert-Eaton Syndrome [Australian listed as Contraindication; US listed under Warnings/Precautions].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning [US]
**Distant Spread of Toxin Effect:** Post-marketing reports indicate that toxin effects may spread from the injection area to produce symptoms consistent with botulinum toxin effects (e.g., asthenia, muscle weakness, diplopia, ptosis, dysphagia, dysphonia, dysarthria, urinary incontinence, and breathing difficulties). These can be life-threatening or fatal. The risk is highest in children treated for spasticity but can occur in adults.

### Clinical Precautions
*   **Dysphagia and Breathing Difficulties:** Treatment of cervical dystonia may weaken accessory muscles of ventilation. Patients with pre-existing respiratory or swallowing disorders are at increased risk.
*   **Corneal Exposure:** Reduced blinking in blepharospasm treatment may lead to corneal ulceration. Ectropion should be avoided by not injecting the medial lower eyelid.
*   **Lack of Interchangeability:** Re-emphasized due to the risk of overdose or underdose.
*   **Antibody Formation:** Potential for neutralising antibodies may result in treatment failure. Dosing at the lowest effective dose with intervals $\ge$ 12 weeks is recommended to minimise risk.
*   **Ptosis:** In aesthetic use, avoid injection near the levator palpebrae superioris. Corrugator injections should be $\ge$ 1 cm above the bony supraorbital ridge.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Very Common (>10%)
*   **Cervical Dystonia:** Dysphagia (dose-dependent).
*   **Blepharospasm:** Eyelid ptosis, dry eye.
*   **Upper Facial Lines:** Headache.

### Common (1–10%)
*   **Sialorrhea:** Dry mouth, diarrhea, hypertension [US], tooth extraction.
*   **Upper Limb Spasticity:** Seizure [US], nasopharyngitis, dry mouth.
*   **General:** Injection site pain, bruising, haematoma, muscular weakness, neck pain (in dystonia).

### Serious Adverse Effects
*   **Spread of Toxin:** Symptoms of botulism (respiratory failure, severe dysphagia).
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, serum sickness, urticaria.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Aminoglycosides/Spectinomycin:** May potentiate neuromuscular blockade; use with caution.
*   **Muscle Relaxants:** Potentiation of effect if used concurrently or before/after **Xeomin**.
*   **Anticholinergics:** May increase the effect of the toxin, particularly in sialorrhea treatment [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:**
    *   [Australian]: **Category B3**. Animal studies show increased abortions and minor embryo-foetal developmental effects at maternally toxic doses. Use only if clearly necessary.
    *   [US]: Data are insufficient to inform a drug-associated risk of adverse developmental outcomes. Embryotoxic effects were seen in animal studies.
*   **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if incobotulinumtoxinA is excreted in human milk. Use is not recommended [Australian] or should be considered based on clinical need [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric:** Safety and efficacy not established for patients < 2 years. Not established for cervical dystonia or blepharospasm in paediatric patients [US].
*   **Elderly:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness compared to younger adults, though some studies suggest increased sensitivity to dysphagia and asthenia in those $\ge$ 65 years.
*   **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No specific data available; not specified in sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** IncobotulinumtoxinA binds to cholinergic nerve terminals, is internalised, and enzymatically cleaves SNAP-25. This prevents the release of acetylcholine, causing localized chemical denervation and muscle paralysis or reduced glandular secretion.
*   **Pharmacokinetics:** Undetectable in peripheral blood at recommended doses due to rapid, irreversible binding to nerve terminals.
*   **Pharmacodynamics:** Recovery of function typically occurs within 3–4 months as nerve terminals sprout and reconnect.

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Cervical Dystonia:** Phase 3 trials showed significant TWSTRS score improvement at Week 4 compared to placebo for both 120 U and 240 U doses.
*   **Blepharospasm:** Demonstrated significant JRS severity subscore improvement. Non-inferiority to onabotulinumtoxinA was shown at a 1:1 ratio.
*   **Sialorrhea:** Significant reduction in unstimulated Salivary Flow Rate (uSFR) and improvement in Global Impression of Change Scale (GICS).
*   **Aesthetics:** High response rates for glabellar lines (up to 60% composite success) and lateral periorbital lines.

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Unopened Vials:** Store below 25°C [US] or below 30°C [Australian]. Shelf life: 36 months.
*   **Reconstituted Solution:** Store in a refrigerator at 2°C to 8°C. Must be used within 24 hours.
*   **Disposal:** Unused vials/syringes should be autoclaved or inactivated with dilute sodium hypochlorite (bleach) or sodium hydroxide.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **Australian Classification:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
*   **US Classification:** Rx Only.
