# Editorial Product Information: Rifaximin

## Editorial Note
*   **Jurisdictions Contributed:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration) and US (Food and Drug Administration).
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of rifaximin product information from multiple regulatory authorities. It is intended as a comprehensive clinical reference for healthcare professionals.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory approved labelling for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Rifaximin, a non-aminoglycoside semi-synthetic, non-systemic antibiotic derived from rifamycin SV.
*   **Brand Name:** **Xifaxan**
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    *   **Film-coated tablets:** 200 mg (round, pink-colored, biconvex, debossed with "Sx") [US].
    *   **Film-coated tablets:** 550 mg (oval, pink-colored, biconvex, debossed with "rfx" [US] or marked "RX" [Australian]).
*   **Routes of Administration:** Oral.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** 
    *   Norgine Pty Ltd [Australian].
    *   Salix Pharmaceuticals, a division of Bausch Health US, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Hepatic Encephalopathy (HE):** Reduction in risk of overt HE recurrence in adults [Australian, US].
*   **Travelers’ Diarrhea (TD):** Treatment of TD caused by noninvasive strains of *Escherichia coli* in adults and pediatric patients 12 years of age and older [US-approved; not specified in Australian PI].
    *   *Limitation of Use:* Should not be used for diarrhea complicated by fever or blood in the stool, or diarrhea due to pathogens other than *E. coli*.
*   **Irritable Bowel Syndrome with Diarrhea (IBS-D):** Treatment of IBS-D in adults [US-approved; not specified in Australian PI].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
*   **Hepatic Encephalopathy:** 550 mg taken orally twice daily [Australian, US]. In clinical trials, 91% of patients used lactulose concomitantly.
*   **Travelers’ Diarrhea [US]:** 200 mg taken orally three times daily for 3 days.
*   **Irritable Bowel Syndrome with Diarrhea [US]:** 550 mg taken orally three times daily for 14 days. Patients experiencing recurrence can be retreated up to two times with the same 14-day regimen.

### Administration Instructions
*   May be administered with or without food [Australian, US].
*   Completion of the full course of therapy is essential to prevent the development of drug-resistant bacteria [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   Hypersensitivity to rifaximin, any rifamycin antimicrobial agents (e.g., rifampin, rifabutin), or any excipients [Australian, US].
*   Cases of intestinal obstruction [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Clostridium difficile-Associated Diarrhea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all antibacterial agents, including rifaximin, ranging from mild diarrhea to fatal colitis. Evaluate if diarrhea occurs during or after treatment (up to two months post-therapy) [Australian, US].
*   **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** SJS and TEN have been reported, particularly in patients with cirrhosis. Discontinue immediately at the first sign of a severe cutaneous reaction [Australian, US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution in patients with severe (Child-Pugh Class C) hepatic impairment due to increased systemic exposure [Australian, US].
*   **P-glycoprotein (P-gp) Inhibitors:** Concomitant use with potent P-gp inhibitors (e.g., cyclosporine) can substantially increase systemic exposure to rifaximin. Use with caution [Australian, US].
*   **Development of Resistant Bacteria:** Prolonged use may lead to resistant strains (e.g., *Staphylococcus aureus*). Strains resistant to rifaximin may also show resistance to rifampicin [Australian, US].
*   **Non-E. coli Diarrhea [US]:** Rifaximin is not effective for diarrhea due to *Campylobacter jejuni*, *Shigella* spp., or *Salmonella* spp.

### Other Common Reactions (≥2%)
*   **Travelers’ Diarrhea [US]:** Headache.
*   **IBS-D [US]:** Nausea, increased ALT.
*   **Hepatic Encephalopathy [Australian]:** Anaemia (7.9%), Nasopharyngitis (7.1%), Pyrexia (6.4%).

### Post-Marketing and Serious Effects
*   **Immune System:** Anaphylaxis, angioedema, hypersensitivity (reported as early as 15 minutes after dosing).
*   **Infections:** *C. difficile* colitis.
*   **Musculoskeletal:** Rhabdomyolysis (in patients with cirrhosis).
*   **Skin:** Exfoliative dermatitis, urticaria, flushing.
*   **Investigations:** International Normalised Ratio (INR) abnormalities.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **P-glycoprotein Inhibitors:** Co-administration with a single dose of cyclosporine (600 mg) resulted in an 83-fold and 124-fold increase in rifaximin mean $C_{max}$ and AUC, respectively. The clinical significance of this is unknown [Australian, US].
*   **Warfarin:** Both increases and decreases in INR (some with bleeding events) have been reported. Monitor INR carefully upon initiation or discontinuation of rifaximin; adjustments to oral anticoagulant doses may be necessary [Australian, US].
*   **CYP3A4 Substrates:** Rifaximin is a substrate of CYP3A4. While it does not appear to clinically inhibit CYP450 enzymes in healthy subjects, patients with hepatic impairment may experience decreased exposure to concomitant CYP3A4 substrates (e.g., oral contraceptives, antiepileptics) due to higher systemic rifaximin levels [Australian].
*   **Oral Contraceptives [US]:** A 7-day course of 550 mg TID rifaximin decreased the $C_{max}$ of ethinyl estradiol by 25% and norgestimate by 13%, though the clinical relevance is unknown.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:**
    *   **Australian Category B1:** Animal studies (rats/rabbits) showed no evidence of teratogenicity at doses up to 300–1000 mg/kg/day. 
    *   **US Perspective:** Animal data suggests potential fetal harm (teratogenic effects including cleft palate and skeletal malformations observed in rats and rabbits at doses equivalent to 0.7 to 33 times the human dose). Rifaximin should be used only if the potential benefit outweighs the risk to the fetus.
*   **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if rifaximin is excreted in human milk. A decision should be made whether to discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the mother's clinical need and the benefits of breastfeeding [Australian, US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:**
    *   **TD:** Safety/efficacy established for ages 12 and older [US].
    *   **HE/IBS-D:** Safety/efficacy not established for patients under 18 years [Australian, US].
*   **Elderly:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness observed, but greater sensitivity in some older individuals cannot be ruled out [Australian, US].
*   **Renal Impairment:** No clinical data available; pharmacokinetics have not been studied [Australian, US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Systemic exposure is significantly higher in patients with hepatic impairment (10-, 14-, and 21-fold higher in Child-Pugh A, B, and C, respectively). No dose adjustment is recommended as it acts locally, but caution is advised in Child-Pugh C [Australian, US].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits bacterial RNA synthesis by binding to the beta-subunit of bacterial DNA-dependent RNA polymerase. 
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption:** Poorly absorbed (<1%). Peak plasma concentrations are reached in approximately 1 hour. A high-fat meal increases AUC by 2-fold but is not clinically significant.
    *   **Distribution:** Moderately bound to plasma proteins (approx. 62–67%).
    *   **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized by CYP3A4.
    *   **Elimination:** Almost exclusively excreted in feces (96.6% of dose) as unchanged drug.

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Hepatic Encephalopathy:** A pivotal 6-month, double-blind trial (N=299) showed that rifaximin 550 mg BID significantly reduced the risk of HE breakthrough by 58% ($p < 0.0001$) compared to placebo. It also reduced the risk of HE-related hospitalizations by 50% ($p = 0.0129$) [Australian, US].
*   **IBS-D [US]:** Three trials showed rifaximin 550 mg TID for 14 days provided significant relief of IBS symptoms (abdominal pain and stool consistency) compared to placebo.
*   **Travelers’ Diarrhea [US]:** Two trials showed rifaximin 200 mg TID for 3 days significantly reduced the time to last unformed stool compared to placebo.

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian]. US labeling permits excursions between 15°C and 30°C.
*   **Container:** Packaged in PVC/PE/PVDC/Aluminium blisters [Australian] or HDPE bottles [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **Australian:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
*   **US:** Rx-only.
