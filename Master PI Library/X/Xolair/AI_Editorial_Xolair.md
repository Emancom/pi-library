# Editorial Product Information: Xolair (omalizumab)

## Editorial Note
- This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdictions: **Australian (TGA)** and **US (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this editorial is to provide healthcare professionals with a unified, clinically accurate overview of **omalizumab** by integrating data from major regulatory sources. 
- **Note:** This synthesis is an editorial reference and does not replace the official PI or prescribing requirements of any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Omalizumab (rch).
- **Chemical Class:** Recombinant DNA-derived humanised monoclonal antibody (IgG1 kappa) that selectively binds to human immunoglobulin E (IgE).
- **Brand Name:** **Xolair**.
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Solution for Injection (Prefilled Syringe):** 75 mg/0.5 mL, 150 mg/1 mL, 300 mg/2 mL. [Australian; US].
    - **Solution for Injection (Autoinjector/Prefilled Pen):** 75 mg/0.5 mL, 150 mg/1 mL, 300 mg/2 mL. [Australian: Prefilled pen; US: Autoinjector].
    - **Powder for Injection (Lyophilized):** 150 mg single-use vial [US; Australian]; 75 mg single-use vial [Australian-only presentation].
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) injection only.
- **Sponsor/Manufacturer:** Novartis Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Ltd [Australian]; Genentech, Inc./Novartis Pharmaceuticals Corp [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Moderate to Severe Persistent Asthma:** Add-on therapy for adults and pediatric patients (aged ≥6 years) with a positive skin test or *in vitro* reactivity to a perennial aeroallergen and symptoms inadequately controlled with inhaled corticosteroids.
- **Chronic Rhinosinusitis with Nasal Polyps (CRSwNP):** Add-on maintenance treatment for adults (aged ≥18 years) with an inadequate response to nasal corticosteroids.
- **Chronic Spontaneous Urticaria (CSU):** Treatment of adults and adolescents (aged ≥12 years) who remain symptomatic despite H1 antihistamine treatment.
- **IgE-Mediated Food Allergy:** For the reduction of allergic reactions (Type I), including anaphylaxis, that may occur with accidental exposure to one or more foods. Used as an adjunct to food allergen avoidance in adults and pediatric patients aged ≥1 year. **[US-approved; not specified in Australian source]**.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing Principles
- **Asthma, CRSwNP, and Food Allergy:** Dosing is based on baseline serum total IgE levels (IU/mL), measured before treatment initiation, and body weight (kg).
- **Measurement of IgE:** Total IgE levels increase during treatment and remain elevated for up to one year post-discontinuation; therefore, re-testing during treatment or within 12 months of stopping cannot be used for dose determination.
- **CSU:** Dosing is not dependent on IgE or body weight. The recommended dose is 150 mg or 300 mg every 4 weeks.

### Dosing by Indication
- **Asthma:** 75 mg to 375 mg SC every 2 or 4 weeks.
- **CRSwNP:** 75 mg to 600 mg SC every 2 or 4 weeks.
- **Food Allergy:** 75 mg to 600 mg SC every 2 or 4 weeks **[US]**.
- **CSU:** 300 mg SC every 4 weeks is recommended; some patients may be controlled at 150 mg every 4 weeks.

### Administration Instructions
- **Location:** Inject SC into the thigh or abdomen (avoiding 5 cm around the navel). The outer upper arm may be used if administered by a caregiver or healthcare professional.
- **Technique:** For doses >150 mg, divide the total dose across two or more injection sites (at least 1 inch [2.5 cm] apart).
- **Self-Administration:** Treatment must be initiated in a healthcare setting. Once the patient is established on therapy with no hypersensitivity reactions (typically after at least 3 doses), a healthcare provider may determine if self-injection by the patient or caregiver is appropriate.
- **Preparation:** Allow prefilled syringes/pens to reach room temperature (15–30 minutes [US]; 30–45 minutes [Australian/US alternative instructions]) before use.

## 4. Contraindications
- Severe hypersensitivity to **omalizumab** or any excipients in the formulation.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [US]: Anaphylaxis.** Anaphylaxis has been reported after the first dose and also beyond one year after starting treatment. Clinicians must initiate therapy in a healthcare setting prepared to manage life-threatening reactions and observe patients for an appropriate period post-injection.
- **Malignancy:** Malignant neoplasms were observed in 0.5% of **Xolair**-treated patients vs 0.2% of controls in clinical trials. Subsequent 5-year observational studies did not indicate a significantly increased risk, though a risk cannot be definitively ruled out.
- **Corticosteroid Reduction:** Do not discontinue systemic or inhaled corticosteroids abruptly upon starting **Xolair**. Tapering should be gradual and under direct medical supervision.
- **Eosinophilic Conditions:** In rare cases, patients with asthma may present with systemic eosinophilia or vasculitis consistent with Churg-Strauss syndrome, often associated with systemic corticosteroid reduction.
- **Thrombocytopenia:** Dose-related decreases in platelets were observed in non-human primates. While rare in humans, severe idiopathic thrombocytopenia has been reported post-marketing. [Australian: recommends a baseline platelet count and periodic monitoring].
- **Latex Sensitivity:** The removable needle cap of the prefilled syringe contains a derivative of natural rubber latex. The autoinjector is not made with natural rubber latex [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adults/Adolescents (Asthma) | Pediatric (6–<12 yrs) | CSU (Pooled) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (>10%) | Injection site reactions (45% [US]) | Pyrexia, Nasopharyngitis | Headache (12% at 150mg [Australian]) |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Arthralgia, pain, fatigue, dizziness | Headache, upper abdominal pain, epistaxis | Nasopharyngitis, sinusitis, arthralgia, nausea |
| **Uncommon/Rare** | Weight increase, syncope, flushing | Not specified | Alopecia, idiopathic severe thrombocytopenia |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, Arterial Thromboembolic Events (ATE) (numerical imbalance observed in observational studies involving stroke, MI, and unstable angina), and parasitic (helminth) infections.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- No formal drug interaction studies have been conducted.
- **Xolair** has been safely used with inhaled corticosteroids, beta-agonists, and oral/H1/H2 antihistamines.
- **Allergen Immunotherapy:** The concomitant use of **Xolair** and allergen immunotherapy has not been evaluated. **[US]**

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:**
    - **Australian Category:** B1 (Animal studies have not shown evidence of increased fetal damage).
    - **US Classification:** No letter category; data from the EXPECT registry of 250 pregnant women showed major congenital anomalies (8.1%) were similar to disease-matched controls (8.9%).
    - **Clinical Consideration:** Poorly controlled asthma in pregnancy increases risks of pre-eclampsia and prematurity. **Xolair** may be used if clinically needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Omalizumab is present in human milk. However, as an IgG molecule, it is expected to undergo GI proteolysis and have poor bioavailability if ingested by the infant. Use may be considered if clinically needed.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric:** Safety/efficacy established for:
    - Food Allergy: ≥1 year **[US]**.
    - Asthma: ≥6 years.
    - CSU: ≥12 years.
    - CRSwNP: Not established below 18 years.
- **Elderly:** Limited data in patients >65 years. No evidence suggests they require different dosing, but caution is advised.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No studies available. Administer with caution as **omalizumab** clearance may be affected.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits the binding of IgE to the high-affinity receptor (FcεRI) on mast cells, basophils, and dendritic cells. This leads to a downregulation of these receptors and reduces the release of inflammatory mediators (IL-4, IL-5, IL-13).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is ~62%. Peak serum concentrations reached in 6–10 days.
    - **Distribution:** Apparent volume of distribution is 78 ± 32 mL/kg (Asthma).
    - **Elimination:** Half-life averages 24–26 days. Clearance involves IgG processes and liver reticuloendothelial system degradation.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Asthma:** In a 28-week pivotal study, **Xolair** reduced the rate of asthma exacerbations by 74% compared to placebo when adjusted for baseline imbalances.
- **CRSwNP:** Significant improvements in Nasal Polyp Score (NPS) and Nasal Congestion Score (NCS) were observed as early as Week 4 and sustained through Week 24.
- **CSU:** In multiple Phase III trials, doses of 150 mg and 300 mg significantly reduced weekly itch severity scores by Week 12 compared to placebo.
- **Food Allergy [US]:** In the FA trial, 68% of **Xolair** patients were able to consume ≥600 mg of peanut protein without dose-limiting symptoms, vs 5% for placebo.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze. 
- **Shelf Life:** Expiry date is stated on the packaging.
- **In-use Stability:** 
    - **Prefilled Syringes/Pens:** Once removed from the refrigerator, may be kept at room temperature (up to 25°C) for a total of 48 hours. Protect from light.
    - **Reconstituted Solution:** [Australian: Use within 8 hours if stored at 2–8°C or 2 hours at room temp; US: Use within 8 hours at 2–8°C or 4 hours at room temp].
