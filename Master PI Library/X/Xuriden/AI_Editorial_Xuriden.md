# Editorial Product Information: Xuriden (uridine triacetate)

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Xuriden (uridine triacetate) for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises available regulatory information into a clinically focused format.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the United States (FDA) Product Information was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Uridine triacetate (an acetylated pyrimidine analog).
*   **Brand Name:** **Xuriden** [US].
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Dosage Form and Strength:** Oral granules; each single-use packet contains 2 grams of uridine triacetate (95% w/w) [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Oral.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** BTG International Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Hereditary Orotic Aciduria:** Indicated for the treatment of hereditary orotic aciduria in both adult and pediatric patients [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric)
*   **Starting Dosage:** 60 mg/kg administered once daily [US].
*   **Maximum Dosage:** 120 mg/kg once daily, not to exceed 8 grams per day [US].
*   **Dose Titration:** Increase the dose if efficacy is insufficient, evidenced by:
    *   Urinary orotic acid levels remaining above normal or increasing.
    *   Worsening of laboratory indices (e.g., RBC or WBC counts).
    *   Progression of clinical signs and symptoms [US].

### Administration Instructions
*   **Preparation:** Use a scale (accurate to 0.1 g) or a graduated teaspoon to measure the required dose. Unused granules in an opened packet must be discarded [US].
*   **Administration with Soft Food:**
    *   Mix granules with 3 to 4 ounces of applesauce, pudding, or yogurt.
    *   The mixture must be swallowed immediately without chewing.
    *   Follow with at least 4 ounces of water [US].
*   **Administration in Milk or Infant Formula:**
    *   For doses up to 2 grams (3/4 teaspoon), the granules may be mixed with 5 mL of milk or formula in an oral syringe.
    *   The mixture should be shaken gently and administered via the syringe between the cheek and gum [US].

### Missed Dose
*   Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
*   None [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
*   **General Precautions:** None specified in the provided clinical documentation [US].
*   **Monitoring:** Clinicians should monitor urinary orotic acid levels and hematologic parameters (e.g., MCV, neutrophil counts) to assess treatment efficacy and guide dose titration [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*   **Clinical Trial Experience:** In a clinical study of four patients with hereditary orotic aciduria, no adverse reactions were reported over a 24-month period [US].
*   **Serious Adverse Effects:** None reported in available clinical data [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
*   **Cytochrome P450 (CYP) Enzymes:** In vitro studies indicate that uridine triacetate and its metabolite uridine do not meaningfully inhibit CYP3A4, 1A2, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, or 2E1. Furthermore, they do not induce CYP1A2, 2B6, or 3A4 [US].
*   **Transporters:** Uridine triacetate is a weak substrate and an inhibitor of P-glycoprotein (P-gp). In vitro, it inhibited digoxin transport with an IC50 of 344 µM. A potential interaction with orally administered P-gp substrates cannot be ruled out due to high local concentrations in the gastrointestinal tract [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
*   **Risk Summary:** No human data are available to inform drug-associated risks. [US].
*   **Animal Data:** Oral administration in pregnant rats during organogenesis at doses up to 2.7 times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) showed no evidence of teratogenicity or fetal harm [US].

### Breastfeeding
*   **Risk Summary:** There are no data regarding the presence of uridine triacetate in human milk, its effects on the breastfed infant, or its impact on milk production. The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the maternal clinical need for the drug [US].

### Fertility
*   No impairment of fertility was observed in male or female rats at doses up to 2.7 times the MRHD [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness are established in pediatric patients. Clinical data include patients from 2 months to 12 years of age [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include patients aged 65 years and older. It is unknown if they respond differently than younger patients [US].
*   **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Uridine triacetate is an acetylated prodrug of uridine. Upon oral administration, it is deacetylated by non-specific esterases throughout the body, delivering uridine into the systemic circulation. In patients with hereditary orotic aciduria, it bypasses the genetic defect in uridine nucleotide synthesis (specifically UMP synthase deficiency), providing the necessary substrate for pyrimidine nucleotide production and reducing orotic acid overproduction via feedback inhibition [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Delivers 4- to 6-fold more systemic uridine than equimolar doses of oral uridine. Peak plasma concentrations (Tmax) are reached within 2 to 3 hours [US].
*   **Distribution:** Uridine is taken up by cells via specific nucleoside transporters and crosses the blood-brain barrier [US].
*   **Metabolism:** Uridine is metabolized via normal pyrimidine catabolic pathways [US].
*   **Elimination:** Uridine can be excreted via the kidneys. The plasma half-life ranges from 2 to 2.5 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
The efficacy of **Xuriden** was assessed in a 6-week open-label study involving four patients (ages 3 to 19). Three patients previously treated with uridine were switched to **Xuriden**, and one was treatment-naïve. 
*   **Primary Outcomes:** Hematologic parameters (neutrophil count, WBC count, or MCV) remained stable or improved over 24 months.
*   **Secondary Outcomes:** Urine orotic acid and orotidine levels remained stable or were reduced. Growth parameters (height and weight) remained stable [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Storage:** Store at controlled room temperature, 25°C (77°F). Excursions are permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
*   **Handling:** Discard any unused granules after measuring the prescribed dose [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription Only) [US].
