# Editorial Product Information: Xyrem (Sodium Oxybate)

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of **Xyrem** (sodium oxybate) oral solution, synthesized for healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is intended for informational purposes and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Sodium oxybate (the sodium salt of gamma-hydroxybutyrate, GHB).
- **Chemical Class:** Central Nervous System (CNS) depressant.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Xyrem** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Oral solution, 0.5 g/mL (equivalent to 0.413 g/mL of oxybate) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Jazz Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Narcolepsy-associated Cataplexy:** Indicated for the treatment of cataplexy in patients aged 7 years and older with narcolepsy [US].
- **Excessive Daytime Sleepiness (EDS):** Indicated for the treatment of EDS in patients aged 7 years and older with narcolepsy [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing
- **Initial Dose:** 4.5 g per night, administered in two equal divided doses of 2.25 g [US].
- **Titration:** Increase the total nightly dose by 1.5 g (0.75 g per dose) at weekly intervals based on efficacy and patient tolerance [US].
- **Maintenance Range:** 6 g to 9 g per night [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** 9 g per night. Doses exceeding this limit have not been adequately studied [US].

### Pediatric Dosing (7 Years and Older)
- **General Approach:** Dosing is weight-based.
- **Weight < 20 kg:** Clinical data are insufficient for specific dosing recommendations [US].
- **Weight 20 kg to < 30 kg:** Start $\le$ 2 g/night; Max 6 g/night [US].
- **Weight 30 kg to < 45 kg:** Start $\le$ 3 g/night; Max 7.5 g/night [US].
- **Weight $\ge$ 45 kg:** Start $\le$ 4.5 g/night; Max 9 g/night [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Preparation:** Both nightly doses must be prepared prior to bedtime. Each dose must be diluted with approximately 60 mL (1/4 cup) of water in the provided pharmacy containers [US].
- **Timing:** 
    - The first dose should be taken at bedtime, at least 2 hours after a meal [US].
    - The second dose should be taken 2.5 to 4 hours after the first dose [US].
- **Positioning:** Patients should take the dose while in bed and lie down immediately, as sleep onset can be rapid (often within 5–15 minutes) [US].
- **Missed Dose:** If the second dose is missed, it should be skipped. Two doses should never be taken at once [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Alcohol:** Use in combination with alcoholic beverages [US].
- **Sedative Hypnotics:** Concomitant use with sedative-hypnotic agents [US].
- **Succinic Semialdehyde Dehydrogenase (SSDH) Deficiency:** Patients with this rare inborn error of metabolism [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
- **CNS Depression:** **Xyrem** causes profound CNS depression and clinically significant respiratory depression.
- **Abuse and Misuse:** The active moiety (GHB) is associated with severe adverse outcomes, including seizure, coma, and death when misused. **Xyrem** is only available through a restricted Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy (REMS) program.

### General Precautions
- **Hazardous Activities:** Patients must not drive or operate heavy machinery for at least 6 hours after dosing [US].
- **Respiratory Depression:** Use with caution in patients with underlying respiratory impairment (e.g., sleep apnea, obesity, or COPD) [US].
- **Depression and Suicidality:** Therapy may emerge or worsen depressive symptoms and suicidal ideation. Monitor patients closely, especially those with a psychiatric history [US].
- **Behavioral Reactions:** May cause confusion, anxiety, hallucinations, or paranoia [US].
- **Parasomnias:** Sleepwalking and related confused nighttime behaviors have been reported and may lead to injury [US].
- **Sodium Loading:** High sodium content (e.g., 1640 mg at 9 g/night dose). Use with extreme caution in patients with heart failure, hypertension, or renal impairment [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Adult Frequency Data
- **Very Common (>10%):** Nausea, dizziness, vomiting, somnolence (at 9 g dose) [US].
- **Common (1–10%):** Enuresis, tremor, diarrhea, abdominal pain, pharyngolaryngeal pain, sleepwalking, and anxiety [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Respiratory depression, obtundation, severe depression, and psychosis [US].

### Pediatric Variations
- Common pediatric effects include nausea (20%), enuresis (19%), vomiting (18%), headache (17%), and weight decrease (13%) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Alcohol and CNS Depressants:** Synergy increases risk of fatal respiratory depression and profound sedation [US].
- **Divalproex Sodium:** Increases GHB exposure by approximately 25%. **Recommendation:** Reduce the initial **Xyrem** dose by at least 20% when starting divalproex [US].
- **Other Interactions:** No significant pharmacokinetic interactions were observed with modafinil, zolpidem, or duloxetine; however, pharmacodynamic monitoring for increased sedation is advised [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified in lettered format in provided source]. Animal studies indicate potential for increased stillbirths and decreased offspring viability. There are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use only if potential benefit justifies potential fetal risk [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** GHB is excreted in human milk. The effects on the breastfed infant and milk production are unknown. Monitor for sedation in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal data showed no adverse effects on fertility at doses equivalent to the maximum human dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established for ages 7 to 17 years. Not recommended for those under 7 years [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Limited data. Increased frequency of headaches and adverse reaction-related discontinuations compared to younger adults. Dose selection should be cautious [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No specific studies conducted; however, the high sodium content requires monitoring for fluid retention [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** GHB clearance is halved in cirrhotic patients. **Recommendation:** Reduce the starting dose by 50% [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** While the exact mechanism is unknown, therapeutic effects are hypothesized to be mediated through $GABA_B$ receptors at noradrenergic, dopaminergic, and thalamocortical neurons [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; absolute bioavailability is ~88%. High-fat meals significantly delay $T_{max}$ and reduce $C_{max}$ and AUC [US].
    - **Distribution:** Hydrophilic; <1% plasma protein binding [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized via the Krebs cycle into carbon dioxide and water. No active metabolites [US].
    - **Elimination:** Excreted almost entirely via expiration of $CO_2$. Mean terminal half-life is 0.5 to 1 hour [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adult Cataplexy (Trial N1, N2):** Demonstrated significant reductions in weekly cataplexy attacks at 6 g and 9 g doses compared to placebo. Randomized withdrawal trials confirmed long-term efficacy [US].
- **Adult EDS (Trial N3, N4):** Significant improvements in Epworth Sleepiness Scale (ESS) scores and Maintenance of Wakefulness Test (MWT) scores [US].
- **Pediatric (Trial N5):** A double-blind, randomized-withdrawal study in 106 patients (ages 7–17) showed significant maintenance of efficacy in both cataplexy and EDS [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F). Excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C [US].
- **Diluted Solution:** Must be consumed within 24 hours of preparation [US].
- **Disposal:** Unused solution should be emptied down the sink drain [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as a Schedule III Controlled Substance for the medical product; however, the active moiety (GHB) is Schedule I when used illicitly [US].
- **Other Jurisdictions:** Poison/Schedule classifications for Australia, Europe, UK, Canada, and Japan are not specified in available sources.
