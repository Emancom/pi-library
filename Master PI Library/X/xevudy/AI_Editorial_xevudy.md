# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction:
    - **United States (FDA)**: Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation)
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note**: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class**: Carrier molecule (amino acid derivative).
- **Brand Names**: Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code**: Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**: 
    - **Intravenous Injection**: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration**: Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors)**: Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD)**: Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders**:
    - **Recommended Dose**: 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose**: Often required in severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
    - **Frequency**: Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less frequently than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Maintenance**: Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg may be required [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis**:
    - **Starting Dose**: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
    - **Dose Adjustment**: Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels <40 to 50 µmol/L. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration before use [US].
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions**: Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizures**: Levocarnitine may increase the frequency or severity of seizures in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a prior history [US].
- **Monitoring**: 
    - In metabolic disorders, obtain a baseline plasma carnitine concentration. Monitor weekly/monthly; optimal free carnitine levels should be between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
    - Monitor blood chemistries and vital signs [US].
- **Oral Levocarnitine Warning**: The safety of **oral** levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common and Serious Reactions
- **Metabolic Disorders**: Transient nausea, vomiting, body odor, and gastritis have been observed [US].
- **ESRD/Hemodialysis (Frequency ≥5%)**:
    - **Body as a Whole**: Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, and pain [US].
    - **Cardiovascular**: Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia, palpitations, and arrhythmias [US].
    - **Digestive**: Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), anorexia, and constipation [US].
    - **Neurological**: Dizziness (13%), insomnia, paresthesia, and depression [US].
    - **Respiratory**: Pharyngitis (23%), cough (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis [US].
    - **Other**: Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia, anemia, and pruritus [US].

### Postmarketing Experience
- Seizures and hypersensitivity reactions (anaphylaxis) have been reported [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin**: Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concurrently with levocarnitine. It is recommended to monitor INR levels upon initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **US Category**: Not assigned a letter category in the current text, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm [US].
    - **Recommendation**: Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine in milk. Clinicians should weigh the benefits to the mother against the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant; discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].
- **Fertility**: No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: See section 3 for dosing. In premature infants and newborns, secondary deficiency is defined by concentrations below age-related normal levels [US].
- **Elderly Use**: Not specifically addressed in the available source [US].
- **Renal Impairment**: Specifically indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. Note the warning regarding the accumulation of toxic metabolites with **oral** formulations in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, facilitating energy production via oxidation [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics**: It promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Distribution**: Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Elimination**: Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].
    - **Bioavailability**: Absolute bioavailability of oral formulations is low (~15–16%) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Trials**: Two double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in ESRD patients on chronic hemodialysis demonstrated that levocarnitine injection (10, 20, and 40 mg/kg) significantly increases plasma carnitine concentrations. However, the effects on specific clinical outcomes in this population have not been fully determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Conditions**: Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing [US].
- **Stability**: Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
