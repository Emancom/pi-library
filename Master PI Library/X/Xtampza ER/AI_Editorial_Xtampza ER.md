# Editorial Product Information: Oxycodone Extended-Release (XTAMPZA ER)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing in any particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Oxycodone (as myristate), an opioid agonist [US].
- **Brand Name(s):** **XTAMPZA ER** [US].
- **ATC Code:** N02AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Extended-release capsules containing microspheres. Available in the following strengths (expressed as oxycodone base and its hydrochloride [HCl] equivalent):
    - 9 mg (equiv. 10 mg oxycodone HCl) [US]
    - 13.5 mg (equiv. 15 mg oxycodone HCl) [US]
    - 18 mg (equiv. 20 mg oxycodone HCl) [US]
    - 27 mg (equiv. 30 mg oxycodone HCl) [US]
    - 36 mg (equiv. 40 mg oxycodone HCl) [US]
- **Route of Administration:** Oral [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Collegium Pharmaceutical, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Pain Management:** Indicated for the management of severe and persistent pain that requires daily, around-the-clock, long-term opioid treatment and for which alternative treatment options (e.g., non-opioid analgesics or immediate-release opioids) are inadequate, not tolerated, or otherwise insufficient [US].
- **Limitations of Use:** Not indicated for use as an as-needed (prn) analgesic. Due to the risks of addiction, abuse, and misuse, it is reserved for patients for whom alternative options are ineffective [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** Dosage must be individualised based on pain severity, prior analgesic experience, and risk factors for addiction and abuse [US].
- **Initial Dosing (Opioid-Naïve/Non-Tolerant):** Initiate with 9 mg orally every 12 hours [US].
- **Opioid-Tolerant Patients:** Doses exceeding 36 mg (single dose) or 72 mg (total daily dose) are reserved for patients who have established tolerance to opioids of comparable potency (e.g., at least 60 mg oral morphine/day or 30 mg oral oxycodone HCl/day for ≥1 week) [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - **Food Requirement:** Must be taken **with food**. Patients should be instructed to take each dose with approximately the same amount of food to ensure consistent plasma levels [US].
    - **Swallowing Difficulties:** Capsules may be swallowed whole, or the contents (microspheres) may be sprinkled onto soft foods (e.g., applesauce, pudding, yogurt) or into a cup for direct oral administration. The microspheres must not be pre-mixed with liquids [US].
    - **Enteral Feeding:** Microspheres may be administered via gastrostomy or nasogastric tubes [US].
- **Titration:** Dosage may be adjusted every 1 to 2 days. Increments of 25% to 50% of the total daily dose are suggested when an increase is clinically indicated [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** Limited to 288 mg per day (equivalent to 320 mg oxycodone HCl) due to the safety of excipients [US].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, patients should take the next dose at the usual time; they should not double the dose [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Significant respiratory depression [US].
- Acute or severe bronchial asthma in an unmonitored setting or in the absence of resuscitative equipment [US].
- Known or suspected gastrointestinal obstruction, including paralytic ileus [US].
- Hypersensitivity to oxycodone (e.g., anaphylaxis) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings [US]:**
    - **Addiction, Abuse, and Misuse:** Risk of opioid use disorder, which can lead to overdose and death.
    - **Life-Threatening Respiratory Depression:** Serious or fatal respiratory depression may occur, particularly during initiation or following dose increases.
    - **Accidental Ingestion:** Even one dose can be fatal, especially in children.
    - **Neonatal Opioid Withdrawal Syndrome (NOWS):** Prolonged use during pregnancy can result in life-threatening withdrawal in the neonate.
    - **Cytochrome P450 3A4 Interaction:** Concomitant use with CYP3A4 inhibitors (or stopping inducers) can increase oxycodone levels, leading to fatal overdose.
    - **Risks from Concomitant Use with Benzodiazepines or Other CNS Depressants:** May result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death.
- **Opioid-Induced Hyperalgesia (OIH) and Allodynia:** Paradoxical increase in pain or sensitivity; may require dose reduction or opioid rotation [US].
- **Adrenal Insufficiency:** Reported with opioid use, typically after >1 month. Requires diagnostic testing and potential corticosteroid replacement [US].
- **Severe Hypotension:** Risk of orthostatic hypotension and syncope, particularly in patients with depleted blood volume or concurrent CNS depressant use [US].
- **Seizure Disorders:** May increase the frequency of seizures [US].
- **Withdrawal:** Avoid abrupt discontinuation in physically dependent patients; gradual tapering is essential [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Nausea (16.6%), headache (13.9%), constipation (13.0%) [US].
- **Common (1–10%):** Somnolence (8.8%), pruritus (7.4%), vomiting (6.4%), dizziness (5.7%), abdominal pain, diarrhea, fatigue, insomnia, hyperhidrosis [US].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Increased heart rate, lethargy, memory impairment, abnormal dreams, euphoric mood, restlessness, dyspnea, night sweats [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Respiratory depression, apnea, circulatory depression, hypotension, shock, and death [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CNS Depressants (including alcohol, benzodiazepines):** Additive pharmacological effect; increased risk of respiratory depression and death. Use should be reserved for cases where alternative options are inadequate [US].
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors (e.g., erythromycin, ketoconazole, ritonavir):** Increase oxycodone plasma concentrations. Monitor closely and consider dose reduction [US].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., rifampin, carbamazepine):** Decrease oxycodone concentrations, potentially leading to reduced efficacy or withdrawal [US].
- **Serotonergic Drugs:** Concomitant use may result in serotonin syndrome [US].
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Avoid use within 14 days of MAOI therapy due to risk of serotonin syndrome or opioid toxicity [US].
- **Mixed Agonist/Antagonists and Partial Agonists (e.g., buprenorphine, pentazocine):** May reduce analgesic effect or precipitate withdrawal [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not specified; modern labeling used]. Prolonged use during pregnancy can cause Neonatal Opioid Withdrawal Syndrome. Animal studies show potential neurobehavioral effects in offspring. Advise patients of the risk to the fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Oxycodone is present in breast milk. Monitor infants for excess sedation and respiratory depression. Breastfeeding is generally not recommended during treatment [US].
- **Fertility:** Chronic opioid use may cause reduced fertility in males and females; it is unknown if these effects are reversible [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 18 years have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clearance is slightly reduced; plasma concentrations are ~15% higher than in younger adults. Initiate at the low end of the dosing range and titrate slowly [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Plasma concentrations are significantly higher. Initiate at 1/3 to 1/2 the usual starting dose and titrate carefully [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Plasma concentrations are ~50% higher in patients with CrCl <60 mL/min. Follow a conservative approach to initiation and titration [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Full opioid agonist, relatively selective for the mu-opioid receptor [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Produces analgesia, respiratory depression, miosis, reduced GI motility, and peripheral vasodilation [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Bioavailability is significantly increased with food (Cmax increases 100-150% with high-fat meals). Not bioequivalent to other ER oxycodone products [US].
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is 2.6 L/kg; 45% protein bound [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP3A4 (to noroxycodone) and to a lesser extent via CYP2D6 (to oxymorphone) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Excreted primarily via the kidney. Apparent half-life is ~5.6 hours (fed) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Study (Chronic Lower Back Pain):** An enriched-enrollment, randomized-withdrawal study (N=740) demonstrated that patients treated with **XTAMPZA ER** (36–144 mg/day) achieved significantly greater pain reduction compared to placebo over a 12-week maintenance period [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F–86°F) [US].
- Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container with a child-resistant closure [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:**
    - **United States:** Schedule II (CII) Controlled Substance. Rx-only [US].
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** Not specified for other jurisdictions in available sources.
