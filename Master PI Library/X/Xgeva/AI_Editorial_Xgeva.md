# Editorial Product Information: Denosumab (Xgeva)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis harmonises Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdictions: **Australian (TGA)** and the **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a unified, clinically focused overview for healthcare professionals, integrating global regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labeling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Denosumab (rch), a fully human IgG2 monoclonal antibody produced in genetically engineered Chinese Hamster Ovary (CHO) cells.
- **Chemical Class:** RANK ligand (RANKL) inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** **Xgeva**.
- **ATC Code:** M05BX04.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Single-use vial:** 120 mg denosumab in 1.7 mL solution (70 mg/mL) [Australian, US].
    - **Pre-filled syringe (PFS):** 120 mg denosumab in 1.0 mL solution (120 mg/mL) [Australian-approved; not specified in US source].
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) injection only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Amgen Inc. (US); Amgen Australia Pty Ltd (Australian).

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Skeletal-Related Events (SRE) Prevention:** Indicated for the prevention of SREs (e.g., pathological fracture, radiation to bone, spinal cord compression, or surgery to bone) in patients with multiple myeloma and in patients with bone metastases from solid tumours.
- **Giant Cell Tumour of Bone (GCTB):** Treatment of adults and skeletally mature adolescents with GCTB that is unresectable or where surgical resection is likely to result in severe morbidity.
- **Hypercalcaemia of Malignancy (HCM):** Treatment of HCM that is refractory to intravenous bisphosphonate therapy.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Standard Dose:** 120 mg administered as a single subcutaneous injection once every 4 weeks.
- **Injection Sites:** Thigh, abdomen, or upper arm.
- **Supplementation:** All patients (unless hypercalcaemic) must receive daily supplementation with at least 500 mg calcium and 400 IU Vitamin D [Australian]. The US source notes administration of calcium and Vitamin D as necessary to treat or prevent hypocalcaemia.

### Dosing by Indication
- **Multiple Myeloma and Bone Metastases:** 120 mg once every 4 weeks.
- **GCTB and HCM:** 120 mg once every 4 weeks, with **additional loading doses** of 120 mg on Days 8 and 15 of the first month (initial 4-week treatment period).

### Administration Instructions
- **Preparation:** Visually inspect for particulate matter or discolouration. The solution is clear and colourless to pale yellow; it may contain trace translucent-to-white proteinaceous particles. Do not shake.
- **Temperature:** Allow the product to reach room temperature (up to 25°C) naturally (15–30 minutes) before injection.
- **Technique:** Inject slowly. The 120 mg/1.7 mL vial must be administered by a healthcare professional [US]. The Australian PI specifies that the 120 mg/1 mL PFS may be administered by a trained patient or caregiver, with the first dose supervised by a professional.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypocalcaemia:** Pre-existing [Australian] or severe untreated [US] hypocalcaemia.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known clinically significant hypersensitivity to denosumab or any component of the formulation, including CHO-derived proteins [Australian].
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian-listed contraindication; US-listed as a Warning].
- **Oral Health:** Unhealed lesions from dental or oral surgery [Australian-listed contraindication].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypocalcaemia:** Xgeva can cause severe symptomatic hypocalcaemia, including fatal cases. Pre-existing hypocalcaemia must be corrected prior to initiation. Monitor calcium levels, especially in the first weeks of therapy and in patients with renal impairment (CrCl < 30 mL/min or on dialysis).
- **Osteonecrosis of the Jaw (ONJ):** Occurs commonly. Risk increases with duration of exposure and invasive dental procedures. Perform an oral exam and ensure preventive dentistry is completed before starting therapy. Maintain good oral hygiene and avoid invasive dental procedures during treatment.
- **Atypical Femoral Fractures (AFF):** Reported with long-term use. Evaluate patients presenting with new or unusual thigh, hip, or groin pain.
- **Post-Discontinuation Risks:**
    - **Hypercalcaemia:** Reported weeks to months after stopping treatment in patients with GCTB and those with growing skeletons.
    - **Multiple Vertebral Fractures (MVF):** Risk of new vertebral fractures, not due to metastases, following discontinuation. Advise patients not to interrupt therapy without medical advice.
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** May cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status before initiation.
- **Excipient Precautions:**
    - **Fructose Intolerance:** Vials and PFS contain sorbitol; use is contraindicated in patients with rare hereditary fructose intolerance [Australian].
    - **Phenylketonuria (PKU):** The Australian PFS contains 6.1 mg phenylalanine; the vial does not.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect profiles vary slightly by indication and duration of exposure.

### Bone Metastases/Multiple Myeloma (Combined Incidence)
- **Very Common (>10%):** Fatigue/asthenia, nausea, anaemia, dyspnoea, diarrhoea, hypophosphataemia, constipation, upper respiratory tract infection, back pain, and headache.
- **Common (1–10%):** Hypocalcaemia (including severe cases), thrombocytopenia, peripheral oedema, rash, and ONJ.

### Giant Cell Tumour of Bone
- **Very Common (>10%):** Arthralgia, back pain, pain in extremity, fatigue, headache, nausea, and nasopharyngitis.
- **Common (1–10%):** ONJ (incidence may be higher with longer duration in GCTB cohorts, reaching ~7–12% in long-term follow-up).

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Severe symptomatic hypocalcaemia (including QT prolongation, tetany, and seizures).
- Osteonecrosis of the jaw.
- Atypical femoral fractures.
- Anaphylactic reactions.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Drug-Drug Interactions:** No formal studies have been conducted. Pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics are not altered by concomitant chemotherapy or hormone therapy.
- **Bisphosphonates:** Do not administer denosumab concomitantly with intravenous bisphosphonates.
- **Same Active Ingredient:** Patients receiving **Xgeva** should not receive **Prolia** (denosumab) or other denosumab-containing products.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category D [Australian]:** Contraindicated.
    - **Fetal Harm [US]:** Based on animal data, denosumab may cause fetal loss, impaired bone growth, and lymph node agenesis. 
    - **Contraception:** Females of reproductive potential should use highly effective contraception during treatment and for at least **5 months** after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if denosumab is excreted in human milk. A decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug, taking into account the importance of the drug to the mother.
- **Fertility:** No human data; animal studies showed no effect on fertility or reproductive organs at high doses.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in patients < 18 years, except in **skeletally mature adolescents** (aged 12–17) with GCTB. Use in patients with growing skeletons is generally not recommended due to the risk of impaired bone growth and severe hypercalcaemia upon discontinuation.
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy observed in patients ≥ 65 years. No dose adjustment required.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment necessary. However, patients with severe impairment (CrCl < 30 mL/min) or on dialysis are at significantly higher risk for hypocalcaemia.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Safety and efficacy not studied; however, as a monoclonal antibody, denosumab is not expected to be cleared via hepatic mechanisms.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Denosumab binds with high affinity to RANK ligand (RANKL), preventing it from activating its receptor, RANK, on the surface of osteoclasts. This leads to a reduction in osteoclast formation, function, and survival, thereby decreasing bone resorption and destruction.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Bioavailability ~62% after SC injection.
    - **Distribution:** Steady-state achieved by 6 months with 4-weekly dosing (~2-fold accumulation).
    - **Metabolism:** Likely degraded into small peptides and amino acids via standard immunoglobulin clearance pathways.
    - **Elimination:** Mean half-life of approximately 28 days.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Rapid reduction in bone resorption markers (e.g., uNTx/Cr) by ~80% within 1 week of administration.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **SRE Prevention:** Pivotal Phase 3 trials (breast cancer, prostate cancer, other solid tumours/myeloma) demonstrated that Xgeva was superior or non-inferior to zoledronic acid in delaying the time to the first on-study SRE.
- **Multiple Myeloma:** Xgeva was non-inferior to zoledronic acid in time to first SRE (Hazard Ratio 0.98).
- **GCTB:** Two Phase 2 trials showed objective tumour response (RECIST) of ~25% and significant reductions in the need for morbid surgical procedures.
- **HCM:** An open-label trial demonstrated that ~64% of patients achieved a corrected serum calcium ≤ 11.5 mg/dL within 10 days.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Refrigeration:** Store at 2°C to 8°C in the original carton to protect from light.
- **Excursions:** Once removed from the refrigerator, Xgeva may be kept at room temperature (up to 25°C) but must be used within **30 days**.
- **Prohibitions:** Do not freeze. Do not expose to temperatures above 25°C. Do not shake excessively.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** 
    - **Schedule 4 (Prescription Medicine) [Australian]**.
    - **Rx Only [US]**.
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription-only biological medicine.
