# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from official Product Information (PI) source documents provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for reference and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** A naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. This may be administered as a slow bolus injection over 2 to 3 minutes or via infusion. 
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses administered every 3 or 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours).
    - Subsequent daily maintenance doses typically remain in the 50 mg/kg range but may be adjusted based on clinical response. Doses as high as 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2-to-3-minute bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Titration:** Therapy initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels below the normal range (40–50 µmol/L). Dose adjustments (e.g., downward to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of treatment based on trough level monitoring [US].

### Monitoring
- Pre-treatment plasma carnitine levels should be obtained.
- Weekly and monthly monitoring of plasma carnitine (target free carnitine levels 35–60 µmol/L), blood chemistries, and vital signs is recommended [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in patients with ESRD on dialysis. These may occur within minutes of intravenous administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue the drug immediately and do not re-administer unless the benefits clearly outweigh the risks, and only under close monitoring [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine has been associated with reports of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** The safety of long-term high-dose **oral** levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD on dialysis has not been established; such use may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted renally [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. It is recommended that INR be monitored following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not specified in text]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies in nursing mothers are unavailable. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. The benefits of maternal supplementation must be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no impairment of fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use is established for metabolic disorders and ESRD; refer to standard mg/kg dosing in Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific clinical data for patients over 65 are not detailed in the source [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. However, the accumulation of metabolites (TMA/TMAO) is a concern with high-dose chronic oral administration in renal failure [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Information not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters that are rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), the terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Administration to ESRD patients results in increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Normalisation of carnitine levels may improve metabolic abnormalities associated with carnitine deficiency [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Data from two double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in chronic hemodialysis patients demonstrated that levocarnitine injection (10, 20, or 40 mg/kg) significantly increases pre-dialysis (trough) carnitine concentrations. While efficacy in increasing carnitine levels is proven, the direct impact on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing.
- Vials are for single use; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].
