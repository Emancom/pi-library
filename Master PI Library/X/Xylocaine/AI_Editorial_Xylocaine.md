# Editorial Product Information: Lidocaine with Adrenaline (Epinephrine)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) documents from the following jurisdictions:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: 2% Xylocaine Dental with Adrenaline (Epinephrine) 1:80,000.
    - **US (FDA)**: Xylocaine-MPF with Epinephrine 1:200,000 2%.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- This document is an editorial synthesis and must not be used as a substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidelines for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Lidocaine hydrochloride (previously known as Lignocaine in Australia) and Adrenaline (Epinephrine) acid tartrate/bitartrate. 
- **Chemical Class**: Amide-type local anaesthetic and sympathomimetic amine.
- **Brand Names**: **Xylocaine**, **Xylocaine-MPF** (Methyl Paraben Free).
- **ATC Code**: N01BB52 (Lidocaine, combinations).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**:
    - **Australian**: 2% (20 mg/mL) Lidocaine hydrochloride monohydrate with Adrenaline (Epinephrine) 1:80,000 (12.5 mcg/mL). Available in 2.2 mL standard and self-aspirating glass cartridges [Australian].
    - **US**: 2% (20 mg/mL) Lidocaine hydrochloride anhydrous with Epinephrine 1:200,000 (5 mcg/mL). Available in 20 mL single-dose vials [US].
- **Routes of Administration**: 
    - Infiltration and nerve block (including routine dental procedures) [Australian].
    - Infiltration, peripheral nerve block (e.g., brachial plexus, intercostal), and central neural techniques (lumbar and caudal epidural blocks) [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: 
    - Dentsply Sirona Pty Ltd [Australian].
    - HF Acquisition Co LLC, DBA HealthFirst (relabelled from Fresenius Kabi USA, LLC) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Dental Procedures**: Production of local anaesthesia in routine dental procedures and oral surgery via infiltration and nerve block techniques [Australian, US].
- **General Anaesthesia**: Production of local or regional anaesthesia by infiltration, peripheral nerve block (e.g., brachial plexus, intercostal), or central neural techniques (lumbar and caudal epidural blocks) [US-approved; not specified in Australian dental PI].
- **Haemostasis**: Recommended for oral surgery requiring prolonged duration of anaesthesia and haemostasis [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Principles
- Use the lowest concentration and smallest dose that achieves the desired effect to avoid high plasma levels and serious adverse effects.
- Individualise dosage based on the area to be anaesthetised, tissue vascularity, and the specific technique employed.

### Adult Dosing
- **Maximum Recommended Dose**: 
    - **With Adrenaline (Epinephrine)**: Should not exceed 7 mg/kg body weight. The total maximum dose is generally recommended not to exceed 500 mg [US] or as guided by the procedure [Australian].
    - **Without Adrenaline (Epinephrine)**: 4.5 mg/kg body weight, not to exceed a total of 300 mg [US].
- **Dental Dosing [Australian]**:
    - **Infiltration**: 1–2 mL (onset approx. 1.5 min; pulp duration 60 min).
    - **Nerve Block**: 1.5–5 mL (onset approx. 4 min; pulp duration 90 min).
- **Epidural Anaesthesia [US]**: Dosage varies by the number of dermatomes (generally 2–3 mL of the indicated concentration per dermatome).

### Administration Instructions
- **Aspiration**: Perform frequent aspirations before and during injection to avoid accidental intravascular delivery.
- **Injection Rate**: Administer slowly at spaced intervals.
- **Test Dose**: For epidural blocks, a test dose (e.g., 2–3 mL of 1.5% lidocaine with epinephrine) is recommended to monitor for signs of unintended intravascular or intrathecal injection [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to amide-type local anaesthetics or any excipients (e.g., sodium metabisulfite).
- **Presence of Inflammation/Sepsis**: Do not use local anaesthetic techniques when there is infection or inflammation in the proposed injection region [Australian].
- **Specific to Adrenaline (Epinephrine) Component [Australian]**:
    - Patients with known sensitivity to sympathomimetic amines.
    - Most patients with cerebral arteriosclerosis.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Resuscitative Readiness**: Ensure immediate availability of oxygen, resuscitative drugs, and equipment.
- **Methemoglobinemia**: Cases have been reported with local anaesthetic use. Risk factors include G6PD deficiency, congenital methemoglobinemia, or concurrent exposure to oxidizing agents [US].
- **Intra-articular Infusions**: Not approved for this use; post-marketing reports of chondrolysis (permanent joint damage) have occurred following continuous infusion [US].

### Specific Precautions
- **Cardiovascular Disease**: Use with extreme caution in patients with severe bradycardia, cardiac conduction disturbances, heart block, or untreated hypertension [Australian, US].
- **Hepatic/Renal Disease**: Lidocaine is metabolised by the liver; use with caution in patients with hepatic impairment due to the risk of toxic accumulation [Australian, US].
- **Sulfite Sensitivity**: Products containing adrenaline (epinephrine) contain sodium metabisulfite, which may cause anaphylactic symptoms or severe asthmatic episodes in susceptible individuals [Australian, US].
- **Malignant Hyperthermia**: Use with caution in patients with a genetic predisposition; while not confirmed to trigger this reaction, a standard management protocol should be available [Australian, US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are rare at dental doses but can occur due to high plasma levels from excessive dosage or inadvertent intravascular injection.

| System | Frequency/Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **CNS** | **Excitatory**: Nervousness, dizziness, blurred/double vision, tremors, tinnitus, disorientation. **Depressant**: Drowsiness, convulsions, unconsciousness, respiratory depression/arrest. |
| **Cardiovascular** | Bradycardia, hypotension, myocardial depression, cardiovascular collapse, cardiac arrest. |
| **Allergic** | Allergic dermatitis, bronchospasm, urticaria, oedema, or anaphylaxis (very rare). |
| **Neurological** | Persistent numbness, paraesthesia, or sensory disturbances following nerve blocks. |
| **Hematologic** | Methemoglobinemia [US]. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Antiarrhythmics (e.g., Mexiletine, Amiodarone)**: Additive cardiac effects; potential for seizures or severe bradycardia with amiodarone [Australian].
- **Tricyclic Antidepressants/MAOIs**: Administration with adrenaline (epinephrine) may cause severe, prolonged hypertension [Australian, US].
- **Non-selective Beta-blockers (e.g., Propranolol)**: May enhance the pressor effect of adrenaline, leading to severe hypertension and bradycardia [Australian].
- **Oxytocic Drugs (Ergot-type)**: Concurrent use with adrenaline may result in severe, persistent hypertension [Australian, US].
- **Inhalation Anaesthetics (e.g., Halothane)**: May sensitise the heart to adrenaline, causing serious arrhythmias [Australian].
- **Anticonvulsants (e.g., Phenytoin)**: May enhance lidocaine metabolism and have additive cardiac depressant effects [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**:
    - **Australian Category A**: Extensively used in dental procedures during pregnancy with no reports of ill effects on the mother or fetus.
    - **US Category B**: Reproduction studies in rats (6.6x human dose) showed no evidence of harm. Use during early pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Labor and Delivery**: Local anaesthetics cross the placenta. Paracervical block may cause fetal bradycardia and acidosis. Maternal hypotension may occur due to vasodilation [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: Lidocaine passes into breast milk in small amounts; unlikely to lead to significant accumulation in the infant. Caution is advised [Australian, US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Doses must be reduced commensurate with age, body weight, and physical condition [Australian, US]. For children >3 years with normal development, doses of 1.5–2 mg/lb (approx. 3.3–4.4 mg/kg) have been suggested [US].
- **Elderly Use**: May require dose reductions due to potential for decreased cardiac output or impaired hepatic/renal function [Australian, US].
- **Debilitated/Acutely Ill Patients**: Dose reductions are required based on physical status [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Lidocaine stabilises the neuronal membrane by inhibiting ionic fluxes (sodium channels) required for the initiation and conduction of nerve impulses.
- **Pharmacodynamics**: Adrenaline (epinephrine) is a vasoconstrictor that reduces the rate of absorption from the injection site, increasing the duration of action and providing haemostasis.
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Dependent on site and presence of vasoconstrictor.
    - **Distribution**: Crosses the blood-brain and placental barriers.
    - **Metabolism**: Rapidly metabolised in the liver via oxidative N-dealkylation.
    - **Elimination**: Approximately 90% excreted as metabolites via the kidneys; <10% as unchanged drug. Elimination half-life is approx. 1.5–2 hours [US] or 1.8 hours [Australian] in healthy adults.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No specific pivotal trial data provided in the sources; clinical use is established based on standard textbook techniques and long-term clinical experience.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Australian (Dental Cartridges)**: Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze). Protect from light. Once removed from refrigeration for use, store below 25°C and use within 4 weeks. Do not return to the refrigerator.
- **US (MPF Vials)**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Protect from light.
- **General**: Discard unused portions of single-dose vials or cartridges. Do not use if the solution is discoloured, pinkish, or contains a precipitate.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australian**: Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **US**: Rx Only.
