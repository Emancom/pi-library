# Editorial Product Information: Levalbuterol Tartrate

## Editorial Note
- **Jurisdictional Sources:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of the official Product Information for levalbuterol tartrate. It is designed to provide healthcare professionals with a coherent, clinically accurate overview of the drug’s regulatory and clinical profile.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial summary for informational purposes and does not replace the official, legally binding Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levalbuterol tartrate (the (R)-enantiomer of racemic albuterol).
- **Chemical Class:** Relatively selective beta₂-adrenergic receptor agonist.
- **Brand Names:** **Xopenex HFA** [US].
- **ATC Code:** R03AC18.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Pressurized Metered-Dose Inhaler (MDI):** Each actuation delivers 59 mcg of levalbuterol tartrate (equivalent to 45 mcg of levalbuterol free base) from the actuator mouthpiece. Supplied as a 15 g canister containing 200 actuations [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral inhalation.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Lupin Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Bronchospasm:** Treatment or prevention of bronchospasm in adults, adolescents, and children aged 4 years and older with reversible obstructive airway disease [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Pediatrics $\ge$ 4 years):**
    - Standard dose: 2 inhalations (90 mcg levalbuterol base) every 4 to 6 hours.
    - Maintenance: In some patients, 1 inhalation (45 mcg base) every 4 hours may be sufficient [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - **Priming:** The inhaler must be primed before the first use and if the device has not been used for more than 3 days. To prime, release 4 test sprays into the air, away from the face [US].
    - **Technique:** Shake well before each use. A wait of approximately 1 minute is recommended between inhalations.
    - **Maintenance:** The plastic actuator should be washed with warm water and air-dried at least once a week to prevent medication build-up and blockage [US].
- **Clinical Monitoring:** The need for more frequent dosing than recommended may indicate a deterioration of asthma control and requires urgent clinical reevaluation [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known history of hypersensitivity to levalbuterol, racemic albuterol, or any component of the formulation. Observed reactions include urticaria, angioedema, rash, bronchospasm, anaphylaxis, and oropharyngeal edema [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Paradoxical Bronchospasm:** Like other inhaled beta-agonists, levalbuterol can produce life-threatening paradoxical bronchospasm, often occurring with the first use of a new canister. If this occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate alternative therapy [US].
- **Asthma Deterioration:** Levalbuterol should not be the sole treatment for patients with persistent asthma. Consideration should be given to adding anti-inflammatory agents (e.g., corticosteroids) [US].
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** Can produce clinically significant effects on heart rate and blood pressure. Use with caution in patients with underlying cardiovascular disorders, including coronary insufficiency, cardiac arrhythmias, and hypertension [US].
- **Excessive Use:** Fatalities have been reported in association with excessive use of inhaled sympathomimetic drugs [US].
- **Immediate Hypersensitivity:** Rare cases of anaphylaxis have been reported [US].
- **Coexisting Conditions:** Use with caution in patients with convulsive disorders, hyperthyroidism, or diabetes mellitus. Large doses of intravenous racemic albuterol have been noted to aggravate pre-existing diabetes and ketoacidosis [US].
- **Hypokalemia:** May produce significant but typically transient hypokalemia (via intracellular shunting) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are categorized by frequency based on clinical trial data for adults and children $\ge$ 4 years.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common ($\ge$ 2%)** | Accidental injury, bronchitis, dizziness, pain, pharyngitis, rhinitis, vomiting [US]. |
| **Post-marketing/Rare** | Angioedema, anaphylaxis, arrhythmias (atrial fibrillation, supraventricular tachycardia, extrasystoles), metabolic acidosis, GERD, dyspnea, tremor, and syncope [US]. |

**Serious Adverse Effects:**
- Sudden, life-threatening paradoxical bronchospasm.
- Cardiovascular instability (tachycardia, hypotension, or hypertension).
- Severe hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Short-acting Sympathomimetics:** Avoid concomitant use of other short-acting aerosol bronchodilators to prevent deleterious cardiovascular effects [US].
- **Beta-blockers:** May block the pulmonary effect of levalbuterol and induce severe bronchospasm. Use with extreme caution; if required, cardioselective beta-blockers may be considered under supervision [US].
- **Diuretics:** Potential for worsening of ECG changes and/or hypokalemia when co-administered with non-potassium-sparing diuretics (loop or thiazide) [US].
- **Digoxin:** May decrease serum digoxin levels (up to 16%–22% based on racemic albuterol studies). Monitor levels in patients receiving chronic digoxin [US].
- **MAOIs and Tricyclic Antidepressants:** Use with extreme caution (or within 2 weeks of discontinuation) as these may potentiate the effect of levalbuterol on the vascular system [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Risk Summary:** There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Animal data for levalbuterol HCl showed no teratogenicity at 750 times the maximum recommended human daily inhalation dose (MRHDID). However, racemic albuterol was teratogenic in mice and rabbits at doses near the human range [US].
    - **Labor and Delivery:** Use should be restricted to cases where benefits clearly outweigh risks due to the potential for beta-agonists to interfere with uterine contractility [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no available data on the presence of levalbuterol in human milk or its effects on milk production or the breastfed infant [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - **$\ge$ 4 years:** Safety and efficacy established [US].
    - **< 4 years:** Not indicated. Clinical trials in this age group failed to show a significant difference from placebo in efficacy and showed an increased incidence of asthma-related adverse reactions [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects $\ge$ 65 years to determine if they respond differently. Dosing should be cautious, typically starting at the low end of the range [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Albuterol is substantially excreted by the kidney. Use with caution in patients with impaired renal function; monitoring may be appropriate [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** The effect of hepatic impairment on levalbuterol pharmacokinetics has not been evaluated [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Activation of beta₂-adrenergic receptors on airway smooth muscle leads to the activation of adenylate cyclase and an increase in intracellular cyclic AMP (cAMP). This inhibits the phosphorylation of myosin and lowers intracellular calcium, resulting in smooth muscle relaxation throughout the airways [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Levalbuterol is the (R)-enantiomer of albuterol; the (S)-enantiomer is considered pharmacologically inactive regarding bronchodilation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Mean peak plasma concentration ($C_{max}$) is approximately 199 pg/mL in adults after a 90 mcg dose.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized by SULT1A3 (sulfotransferase) in the gastrointestinal tract.
    - **Elimination:** Primary route is renal excretion (80% to 100%). The elimination half-life of the propellant (HFA-134a) is 5 to 7 minutes in humans [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adults/Adolescents ($\ge$ 12 years):** Two 8-week trials in 748 patients showed 90 mcg of levalbuterol produced significantly greater improvement in FEV₁ than placebo. Median time to onset of a 15% increase in FEV₁ was 5.5 to 10.2 minutes; duration of effect was 3 to 4 hours (up to 6 hours in some) [US].
- **Pediatrics (4–11 years):** A 4-week trial in 150 children showed significant FEV₁ improvement consistent with adult studies. Median time to onset was 4.5 minutes [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store between 20°C and 25°C (68°F and 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Store with the actuator mouthpiece down. Protect from freezing and direct sunlight.
- **Warning:** Contents under pressure. Do not puncture or incinerate. Exposure to temperatures above 120°F (49°C) may cause bursting [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison/Legal Classification:** 
    - **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription only) [US].
- **Dose Indicator:** The inhaler features a dose indicator that counts down every 10 actuations. It turns red when 20 actuations remain. The device should be discarded when the indicator reaches "0" [US].
