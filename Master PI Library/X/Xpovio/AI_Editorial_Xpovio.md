# Editorial Product Information: Selinexor

## Editorial Note
- **Jurisdictions Contributing to this Editorial:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration) and United States (Food and Drug Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the Product Information for selinexor. It is intended as a clinically accurate reference for healthcare professionals to compare and integrate global regulatory data.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is for informational purposes for healthcare professionals and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Selinexor (INN), a reversible covalent selective inhibitor of nuclear export (SINE) compound.
- **Brand Name:** **Xpovio**
- **ATC Code:** L01XX66 [Not explicitly in provided sources, but consistent with chemical class]
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Film-coated tablets:** 20 mg [Australian].
    - **Film-coated tablets:** 10 mg, 20 mg, 40 mg, 50 mg, 60 mg, and 80 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Antengene (Aus) Pty Ltd [Australian]; Karyopharm Therapeutics Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Xpovio** is indicated for the treatment of adult patients with multiple myeloma (MM) in the following settings:
- **In combination with bortezomib and dexamethasone (SVd):** For patients who have received at least one prior therapy.
- **In combination with dexamethasone (Sd):** 
    - For relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma (RRMM) in patients who have received at least three prior therapies and whose disease is refractory to at least one proteasome inhibitor (PI), at least one immunomodulatory medicinal product (IMiD), and an anti-CD38 monoclonal antibody (mAb) [Australian-approved].
    - For RRMM in patients who have received at least four prior therapies and whose disease is refractory to at least two PIs, at least two IMiDs, and an anti-CD38 mAb [US-approved].

*Note: Accelerated approval for diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) was removed from US labeling in April 2026 [US].*

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Treatment must be initiated and monitored by physicians experienced in managing multiple myeloma.
- **SVd Combination:** The recommended starting dose of **Xpovio** is 100 mg orally once weekly. 
    - [Australian]: Administered on Days 1, 8, 15, 22, and 29 of a 35-day cycle.
    - [US]: Administered once weekly until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.
- **Sd Combination:** The recommended starting dose is 80 mg orally on Days 1 and 3 of each week (160 mg total weekly).

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets must be swallowed whole with water. Do not crush, chew, break, or divide [Australian, US].
- May be taken with or without food [Australian].
- Administer at approximately the same time of day [US].

### Concomitant Medications
- **Antiemetics:** Prophylactic 5-HT3 antagonists and/or other anti-nausea agents should be provided prior to and during treatment [Australian, US].
- **Hydration:** Maintain adequate fluid and caloric intake; consider IV hydration for patients at risk of dehydration [Australian, US].

### Missed Dose and Vomiting
- If a dose is missed or delayed, patients should take the next dose on the next regularly scheduled day. 
- If vomiting occurs after administration, the dose should not be repeated; the patient should wait for the next scheduled dose [Australian, US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Hypersensitivity to selinexor or any excipients [Australian].
- **None listed:** No contraindications specified in US labeling [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Thrombocytopenia:** A primary cause of dose modification. Can be life-threatening or fatal. Monitor platelet counts at baseline, during treatment, and as clinically indicated (more frequently during the first 2-3 months). Manage with dose interruption, reduction, and/or platelet transfusions [Australian, US].
- **Neutropenia:** Can be life-threatening and increase infection risk. Monitor absolute neutrophil counts (ANC) at baseline and throughout treatment. Manage with dose modification and/or growth factors (e.g., G-CSF) [Australian, US].
- **Gastrointestinal Toxicity:** Severe nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea may occur, leading to dehydration and weight loss. Prophylactic antiemetics are required [Australian, US].
- **Hyponatremia:** Can be severe or life-threatening. Monitor serum sodium at baseline and frequently during the first two months. Correct for hyperglycemia and high paraprotein levels. Manage with salt tablets, IV saline, and/or dose modification [Australian, US].
- **Neurological Toxicity:** Includes dizziness, confusion, delirium, syncope, and decreased alertness. Avoid situations where dizziness or falls are hazardous; avoid driving until symptoms resolve [Australian, US].
- **Serious Infection:** Fatal infections (e.g., pneumonia, sepsis) have occurred, including atypical fungal and viral infections. Monitor for signs of infection and treat promptly [Australian, US].
- **Cataracts:** New onset or exacerbation may occur, often requiring surgical removal. Monitor for visual changes and perform ophthalmologic evaluations as indicated [Australian, US].
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Can cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status in females of reproductive potential before initiation. Advise effective contraception for both sexes [Australian, US].
- **Tumour Lysis Syndrome (TLS):** Reported in patients receiving therapy. Monitor high-risk patients closely and treat per clinical guidelines [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Reactions (≥20%)
- **SVd Combination:** Fatigue, nausea, decreased appetite, diarrhea, peripheral neuropathy, upper respiratory tract infection, weight decrease, cataract, vomiting [Australian, US].
- **Sd Combination:** Nausea, thrombocytopenia, fatigue, anaemia, decreased appetite, weight decrease, diarrhoea, vomiting, hyponatremia, neutropenia, leukopenia [Australian, US].

### Serious and Fatal Reactions
- **Serious AEs:** Reported in 52% (SVd) to 58% (Sd) of patients [Australian, US].
- **Fatal AEs:** Occurred in approximately 6% (SVd) to 9% (Sd) of patients within 30 days of last treatment [Australian, US].
- Common fatal events include pneumonia and sepsis.

### Laboratory Abnormalities (Grade 3-4, ≥15%)
- Thrombocytopenia, lymphopenia, anaemia, and hypophosphatemia [Australian, US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Interactions:** Selinexor is a substrate of CYP3A4. Concomitant use with strong CYP3A4 inducers (e.g., carbamazepine) may reduce selinexor exposure [Australian]. No clinically significant difference was observed with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., clarithromycin) [US].
- **Paracetamol/Acetaminophen:** No clinically significant PK effect when co-administered up to 1000 mg daily [Australian, US].
- **Transporter Systems:** Selinexor inhibits OATP1B3 in vitro, but not OATP1B1, OAT1, OAT3, OCT1, OCT2, MATE1, or MATE2-K [Australian, US]. It is not a substrate of P-gp or BCRP.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - [Australian]: **Category D**. Selinexor is not recommended during pregnancy or in women of childbearing potential not using contraception.
    - [US]: Advise females of the potential risk to a fetus. Verify pregnancy status before initiation.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if selinexor or metabolites are excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding should be discontinued during treatment and for **one week** after the final dose [Australian, US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies suggest impairment of male and female fertility (reduced sperm, single-cell necrosis in testes, and decreased ovarian follicles) [Australian, US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients <18 years of age [Australian, US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in effectiveness were observed between patients ≥65 years and younger patients. However, patients ≥75 years had higher incidences of serious AEs and discontinuations [Australian, US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is recommended for patients with mild, moderate, or severe renal impairment. Selinexor has not been studied in patients requiring dialysis [Australian, US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild to Moderate:** No dose adjustment required [Australian, US].
    - **Severe (Total bilirubin >3x ULN):** Reduce the starting dose to 80 mg once weekly (SVd) or 100 mg once weekly (Sd) [US; Australian PI states data is unknown for severe impairment].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selinexor reversibly inhibits exportin 1 (XPO1), blocking the nuclear export of tumour suppressor proteins (e.g., p53, p21, FOXO), growth regulator proteins, and oncogenic mRNAs. This leads to nuclear accumulation of these proteins, cell cycle arrest, and apoptosis in cancer cells.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** $C_{max}$ is reached within 4 hours. A high-fat meal does not clinically affect PK [Australian, US].
    - **Distribution:** 95% plasma protein bound. Apparent volume of distribution ($V_d/F$) is 133 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolised by CYP3A4, UGTs, and GSTs.
    - **Excretion:** Mean half-life ($t_{1/2}$) is 6 to 8 hours. Total clearance is 18.6 L/h.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **BOSTON Trial (Phase 3):** Evaluated SVd vs. Vd (Bortezomib/Dexamethasone) in MM after 1-3 prior therapies. SVd demonstrated a statistically significant improvement in Progression-Free Survival (PFS): Median 13.9 months vs. 9.5 months (HR 0.70, p=0.0075) [Australian, US].
- **STORM Trial (Phase 2):** Evaluated Sd in RRMM. In the "penta-refractory" subgroup, the Overall Response Rate (ORR) was 25.3% to 26% with a median duration of response (DOR) of 3.8 to 4.4 months [Australian, US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at or below 30°C (86°F) [Australian, US].
- **Packaging:** Tablets are supplied in PVC/PCTFE/PVC-aluminium blisters [Australian] or child-resistant blister packs [US].
