# Editorial Product Information: Xospata (Gilteritinib)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis has been compiled using official Product Information (PI) documents from the following jurisdictions: **Australian (TGA)** and **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements from multiple regions into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory guidelines for definitive prescribing authority.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Gilteritinib (present as gilteritinib fumarate).
- **Chemical Class:** Tyrosine kinase inhibitor; FMS-like tyrosine kinase 3 (FLT3) inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** **XOSPATA**
- **ATC Code:** L01EX13
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Film-coated tablets containing 40 mg of gilteritinib (equivalent to 44.2 mg gilteritinib fumarate).
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Astellas Pharma.

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Relapsed or Refractory Acute Myeloid Leukaemia (AML):** Indicated for the treatment of adult patients with relapsed or refractory AML possessing a FMS-like tyrosine kinase 3 (FLT3) mutation.
    - [US]: The mutation must be detected by an FDA-approved test.
    - [Australian]: Mutation types specifically mentioned include internal tandem duplication (ITD) or tyrosine kinase domain (TKD).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Recommended Starting Dose:** 120 mg (three 40 mg tablets) administered orally once daily.
- **Dose Escalation:** In the absence of a response (failure to achieve composite complete remission) after 4 weeks of treatment, the dose may be increased to 200 mg once daily if tolerated [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Tablets should be swallowed whole with water and must not be broken, crushed, or chewed.
    - May be taken with or without food.
    - Administration should occur at approximately the same time each day.
- **Treatment Duration:** Treatment should continue until the patient no longer derives clinical benefit or until unacceptable toxicity occurs. Clinical response may be delayed; continuation for up to 6 months is recommended to allow for response [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:**
    - [Australian]: Administer as soon as possible on the same day. Return to the normal schedule the following day.
    - [US]: Administer as soon as possible on the same day, provided it is at least 12 hours before the next scheduled dose.
    - If vomiting occurs after a dose, do not take an additional dose; resume the normal schedule the following day.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to the active substance (gilteritinib) or any of the excipients (e.g., mannitol, hyprolose, magnesium stearate, hypromellose, talc, macrogol 8000, titanium dioxide, iron oxide yellow).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Differentiation Syndrome [US]:** Patients treated with **XOSPATA** have experienced differentiation syndrome, which can be fatal if untreated. Symptoms include fever, dyspnoea, pleural/pericardial effusion, pulmonary oedema, hypotension, rapid weight gain, and renal dysfunction. If suspected, initiate high-dose corticosteroids (e.g., dexamethasone 10 mg IV every 12 hours) and hemodynamic monitoring.
- **Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES):** Rare, reversible neurological disorder presenting with seizures, headache, visual disturbances, or altered mental status. If suspected, confirm via MRI and discontinue **XOSPATA**.
- **Prolonged QT Interval:** Gilteritinib is associated with concentration-related QTc prolongation. 
    - **Monitoring:** Perform ECGs prior to initiation, on Days 8 and 15 of Cycle 1, and monthly for at least the following two [US] or three [Australian] months. 
    - **Correction:** Correct hypokalaemia and hypomagnesaemia prior to and during treatment.
    - **Management:** Interrupt treatment if QTcF >500 msec.
- **Pancreatitis:** Reports of pancreatitis have occurred. Monitor for abdominal pain and interrupt/reduce dose if confirmed.
- **Embryofetal Toxicity:** Can cause fetal harm. Advise of potential risks and require effective contraception (see Section 8).

## 6. Adverse Effects
The safety profile is based on clinical trials involving patients with relapsed or refractory AML receiving 120 mg daily.

### Adverse Reaction Frequency
| Frequency | Adverse Reactions |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | ALT/AST increased, alkaline phosphatase increased, creatine phosphokinase increased, diarrhoea, fatigue, nausea, constipation, cough, peripheral oedema, dyspnoea, dizziness, hypotension, pain in extremity, arthralgia, myalgia, headache, rash. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Acute kidney injury, differentiation syndrome, anaphylactic reaction, pericardial effusion, heart failure, pericarditis, malaise, musculoskeletal pain. |
| **Uncommon (<1%)** | Posterior reversible encephalopathy syndrome (PRES). |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** The most frequent serious reactions include acute kidney injury (6.6%), diarrhoea (4.7%), and differentiation syndrome (2.2% to 3%). Fatalities occurred in approximately 2% of patients, primarily due to cardiac arrest, differentiation syndrome, or pancreatitis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Effect of Other Drugs on XOSPATA:**
    - **Strong CYP3A/P-gp Inducers:** Avoid concomitant use (e.g., rifampicin, phenytoin, St. John’s Wort) as they significantly decrease gilteritinib exposure (~70% reduction in AUC).
    - **Strong CYP3A/P-gp Inhibitors:** Use with caution (e.g., voriconazole, itraconazole). These may increase gilteritinib exposure (~2.2-fold increase in AUC). Monitor more frequently for adverse reactions.
- **Effect of XOSPATA on Other Drugs:**
    - **5HT2B or Sigma Nonspecific Receptors:** Gilteritinib may reduce the effects of drugs targeting these receptors (e.g., escitalopram, fluoxetine). Avoid use unless essential.
    - **Transporters (P-gp, BCRP, OCT1):** Gilteritinib may increase the exposure of substrates for these transporters (e.g., digoxin, dabigatran, metformin). Caution and dose reduction of the substrate may be required.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category D:** Gilteritinib can cause fetal harm based on animal data (teratogenicity and embryofetal lethality at subclinical exposures).
    - **Contraception:** Females of reproductive potential require a pregnancy test within 7 days of starting. Effective contraception is required during treatment and for **6 months** after the final dose. 
    - **Male Patients:** Those with female partners of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for **4 months** after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if gilteritinib is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for **2 months** after the final dose.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed impaired fertility in males (testicular degeneration/necrosis). Human impact is unknown.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children under 18 years have not been established. [Australian] notes it should not be used in infants under 6 months due to potential 5HT2B-related cardiac development risks.
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment is required for patients ≥65 years. No overall differences in safety or efficacy have been observed compared to younger patients.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for mild (Child-Pugh A) or moderate (Child-Pugh B) impairment. Not studied in severe (Child-Pugh C) impairment.
- **Renal Impairment:** No adjustment for mild or moderate impairment. [Australian] suggests monitoring for toxicity in severe renal impairment or end-stage renal disease, as exposure may be increased (1.5-fold).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Gilteritinib inhibits FLT3 receptor signalling and proliferation. It targets FLT3-ITD and FLT3-TKD mutations and has also shown activity against AXL receptors.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Near-complete (>90%) inhibition of FLT3 phosphorylation is achieved within 24 hours of the first dose.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Median $T_{max}$ is 4–6 hours. A high-fat meal decreases $C_{max}$ by ~26% and delays $T_{max}$ by 2 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Extensive tissue distribution; ~90% plasma protein bound (primarily to albumin).
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised via CYP3A4.
    - **Elimination:** Half-life is approximately 113 hours. Excreted primarily in faeces (64.5%) and urine (16.4%).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ADMIRAL Trial:** A Phase 3, open-label, randomised trial comparing **XOSPATA** (120 mg/day) to salvage chemotherapy (MEC, FLAG-Ida, LoDAC, or azacitidine) in patients with FLT3-mutated R/R AML.
- **Primary Outcome:** Median Overall Survival (OS) was significantly longer with **XOSPATA** (9.3 months) compared to chemotherapy (5.6 months) [HR 0.637; p=0.0004].
- **Response Rates:** The rate of complete remission (CR) or CR with partial haematologic recovery (CRh) was 34% for **XOSPATA** vs 15.3% for chemotherapy.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Handling:** Keep in the original container to protect from light, moisture, and humidity.
- **Shelf Life:** Noted on the packaging; in Australia, refer to the ARTG public summary.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **United States:** Rx-only.
