# Editorial Product Information: Xyntha (Moroctocog alfa)

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration) and US (Food and Drug Administration).
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the Product Information (PI) for **Xyntha**, intended for use by healthcare professionals as a consolidated clinical reference.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Moroctocog alfa (recombinant coagulation factor VIII, B-domain deleted).
- **Brand Names:** **Xyntha**, **Xyntha Solofuse** [US, Australian].
- **ATC Code:** B02BD02.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Lyophilised Powder for Injection (Vial):** 250 IU, 500 IU, 1000 IU, 2000 IU. Supplied with a prefilled diluent syringe (4 mL 0.9% Sodium Chloride) and vial adapter [Australian, US].
    - **Prefilled Dual-Chamber Syringe (Solofuse):** 250 IU, 500 IU, 1000 IU, 2000 IU, 3000 IU. Contains lyophilised powder in one chamber and 4 mL 0.9% Sodium Chloride diluent in the other [Australian, US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) injection/infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian]; Wyeth BioPharma Division of Wyeth Pharmaceuticals LLC, a subsidiary of Pfizer Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Xyntha** is indicated in adults and children with haemophilia A (congenital factor VIII deficiency) for:
- Control and prevention of haemorrhagic episodes (on-demand treatment).
- Perioperative management (prevention and control of bleeding in surgical settings).
- Routine prophylaxis to prevent or reduce the frequency of spontaneous bleeding episodes.

*Note: **Xyntha** does not contain von Willebrand factor and is not indicated for the treatment of von Willebrand’s disease.*

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing Principles
Treatment should be initiated under the supervision of a physician experienced in haemophilia management. Dosage must be individualised based on the patient's clinical condition, weight, and the severity/location of the haemorrhage.

The calculation of the required dosage is based on the empirical finding that 1 IU of factor VIII per kg body weight raises the plasma factor VIII activity by approximately 2 IU/dL.

**Calculation Formula:**
$Required\ units\ (IU) = body\ weight\ (kg) \times desired\ factor\ VIII\ rise\ (\%\ or\ IU/dL) \times 0.5\ (IU/kg\ per\ IU/dL)$

### Dosing by Indication
- **Minor Bleeding (e.g., early haemarthrosis, oral bleeds):** Maintain Factor VIII levels at 20–40%. Repeat every 12–24 hours for at least 1 day until resolved.
- **Moderate Bleeding (e.g., muscle haemorrhage, minor operations):** Maintain Factor VIII levels at 30–60%. Repeat every 12–24 hours for 3–4 days or until adequate wound healing.
- **Major Bleeding (e.g., gastrointestinal, intracranial, major operations):** Maintain Factor VIII levels at 60–100%. Repeat every 8–24 hours until the threat is resolved or until adequate wound healing is achieved [US].

### Routine Prophylaxis
- **Adults and Adolescents ($\ge 12$ years):** The recommended starting regimen is 30 IU/kg administered 3 times weekly [US]. 
- **Children ($<12$ years):** The recommended starting regimen is 25 IU/kg administered every other day [US]. Shorter dosage intervals or higher doses may be required to account for higher clearance in this age group [Australian, US].
- **General Range [Australian]:** 10 to 50 IU/kg given at least twice a week.

### Administration Instructions
- Administer via IV injection over several minutes after reconstitution.
- The rate of administration should be determined by the patient’s comfort level.
- **Xyntha** must be reconstituted only with the provided 0.9% Sodium Chloride diluent.
- Once reconstituted, the solution should be used immediately or within 3 hours. Do not refrigerate after reconstitution [Australian, US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known history of life-threatening immediate hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis, to **Xyntha**, its components, or hamster proteins [US, Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity:** Allergic-type reactions, including anaphylaxis, are possible. Patients should be alert for early signs such as hives, chest tightness, wheezing, and hypotension. **Xyntha** contains trace amounts of hamster protein; hypersensitivity may develop [Australian, US].
- **Neutralising Antibodies (Inhibitors):** The development of factor VIII inhibitors (neutralising antibodies) has been reported. Patients should be monitored via clinical observation and Bethesda Unit (BU) assays. If expected plasma factor VIII levels are not attained or bleeding is not controlled, an inhibitor assay should be performed [Australian, US].
- **Assay Discrepancy:** Factor VIII activity monitoring using the chromogenic substrate assay typically yields results approximately 100% higher than the one-stage clotting assay. It is recommended to use the one-stage clotting assay for clinical monitoring to avoid under-dosing [Australian].
- **Inadequate Therapeutic Response:** Reports of less than expected therapeutic effect (without inhibitor development) have occurred in both prophylaxis and on-demand settings [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Periodic monitoring of factor VIII levels is required, especially for surgical interventions.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Distribution [Australian data]
| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common ($\ge 10\%$)** | Headache, arthralgia, pyrexia, cough |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Dizziness, decreased appetite, diarrhoea, vomiting, nausea, abdominal pain, urticaria, rash, pruritus, myalgia, chills, catheter site related reaction, factor VIII inhibition (PUPs) |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Anaphylactic reaction, neuropathy peripheral, somnolence, dysgeusia, hypotension, tachycardia, palpitations, dyspnoea, hyperhidrosis, injection site reaction/pain/inflammation, liver function test abnormal, factor VIII inhibition (PTPs) |
| **Rare/Very Rare** | Muscle weakness, feeling cold, blurred vision |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and the development of high-titre inhibitors.
- **Immunogenicity:** In clinical trials of 167 Previously Treated Patients (PTPs), 4 subjects (2.4%) developed factor VIII inhibitors [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- No formal drug interaction studies have been conducted.
- No known interactions with other recombinant factor VIII products [Australian].
- **Xyntha** should not be mixed with other medicinal products or infusion solutions [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category:** B2 (Drugs taken by only a limited number of pregnant women without evidence of malformation/harm; animal studies inadequate). 
    - **US Classification:** Narrative risk summary; it is unknown if **Xyntha** can cause fetal harm or affect reproduction capacity. Use only if clearly indicated [US, Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is not known whether **Xyntha** is excreted into human milk. Caution should be exercised, and the benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need for the mother and potential risks to the infant [Australian, US].
- **Fertility:** No data available regarding the effect on fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for all ages, including newborns. Children generally exhibit shorter half-lives, larger volumes of distribution, and lower recovery levels compared to adults. Higher or more frequent dosing is often required in patients $<12$ years [Australian, US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects $\ge 65$ years to determine if they respond differently. Dose selection should be cautious, typically starting at the low end of the range [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specifically studied in clinical trials; no dose adjustment provided [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Xyntha** is a glycoprotein that replaces the missing coagulation factor VIII required for effective haemostasis. It acts as a cofactor for activated factor IX, accelerating the conversion of factor X to activated factor X, ultimately leading to the formation of a fibrin clot.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Administration of **Xyntha** increases plasma factor VIII levels and can temporarily correct the coagulation defect. It normalises the activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT).
- **Pharmacokinetics (Adults/Adolescents $\ge 12$ years):**
    - **Mean Half-life ($t_{1/2}$):** 11.2 $\pm$ 5.0 hours.
    - **Clearance:** 4.51 $\pm$ 2.23 mL/hr/kg.
    - **Vss:** 66.1 $\pm$ 33.0 mL/kg.
    - **Incremental Recovery:** 2.15 $\pm$ 0.44 IU/dL per IU/kg.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Prophylaxis and On-demand:** A pivotal study in 94 PTPs with moderately severe to severe haemophilia A demonstrated that **Xyntha** effectively prevented and controlled bleeding. The median annualised bleeding rate (ABR) during routine prophylaxis was 1.9 compared to 31 IU/kg for on-demand treatment [Australian, US].
- **Surgical Prophylaxis:** A study in 30 PTPs undergoing major surgery showed that **Xyntha** provided effective haemostasis. Investigators rated efficacy as "excellent" or "good" for all surgical procedures [Australian, US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Packaged Product:** Store and transport refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze. 
- **Room Temperature Storage:** May be stored at room temperature (up to 25°C) for a single period of up to 3 months. The date of removal from refrigeration must be recorded on the carton. After room temperature storage, the product must be used or discarded; do not return to refrigeration [Australian, US].
- **Light Protection:** Avoid prolonged exposure to light.
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Use within 3 hours.
