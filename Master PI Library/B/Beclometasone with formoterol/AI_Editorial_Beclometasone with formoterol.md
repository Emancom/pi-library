# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical and regulatory data to support evidence-based practice.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or prescribing information required in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (L-carnitine).
*   **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/amino acid derivative; (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
*   **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [United States].
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injection, solution: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [United States].
*   **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with an inborn error of metabolism which results in secondary carnitine deficiency [United States].
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 General Dosing
*   **Metabolic Disorders:**
    *   **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [United States].
    *   **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    *   **Frequency:** Administered every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [United States].
    *   **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically in the range of 50 mg/kg, though higher doses may be required. The maximum recorded dose is 300 mg/kg [United States].
*   **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    *   **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [United States].
    *   **Administration:** Slow 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    *   **Dose Adjustments:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels below 40–50 µmol/L. Adjustments (including downward titration to 5 mg/kg) should be guided by trough levels and may occur as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [United States].

### 3.2 Administration Instructions
*   Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
*   **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable when mixed in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL. Mixed solutions are stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
*   None known [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occurred within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue the drug and initiate appropriate medical management [United States].
*   **Seizures:** Reports of seizures have occurred in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a known seizure history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [United States].
*   **Renal Impairment (Oral vs. IV):** While the IV formulation is used in ESRD, the safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites: trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [United States].
*   **Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### 6.1 Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported with a frequency ≥5% in placebo-controlled trials, regardless of causality, include:
*   **General:** Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, and pain [United States].
*   **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (reported in up to 21% of patients), hypotension, tachycardia, and palpitations [United States].
*   **Digestive:** Diarrhea (up to 35% in high-dose groups), nausea, vomiting, and dyspepsia [United States].
*   **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (up to 27%), cough, and dyspnea [United States].
*   **Neurologic:** Dizziness (up to 18%), insomnia, and paresthesia [United States].

### 6.2 General Side Effects
*   **Common:** Transient nausea and vomiting.
*   **Uncommon:** Gastritis and body odor (the latter is often associated with metabolite accumulation) [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine.
*   **Management:** It is recommended to monitor INR levels in patients on warfarin therapy both after initiation of levocarnitine and after any dose adjustments [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [United States: Category not specified]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [United States].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies in nursing mothers have not been conducted. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased milk concentrations of levocarnitine following administration. The risk of excess carnitine intake by the infant must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [United States].
*   **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders apply to paediatric patients; refer to the Dosage and Administration section.
*   **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources.
*   **Renal Impairment:** IV levocarnitine is indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. Dose titration is based on trough plasma concentrations.
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### 10.1 Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [United States].

### 10.2 Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
*   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [United States].
*   **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly after dialysis increased mean trough levocarnitine levels from 21.3 µmol/L (baseline) to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks [United States].
*   **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed a dose-proportional increase in trough concentrations at 12 and 24 weeks. While carnitine levels increased, the impact on specific clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
*   **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
*   **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [United States].
