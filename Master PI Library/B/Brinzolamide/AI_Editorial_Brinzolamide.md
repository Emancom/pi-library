# Editorial Product Information: Brinzolamide and Timolol Maleate

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from the following jurisdictional Product Information (PI) source:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: Australian Product Information – AZARGA (Brinzolamide and Timolol Maleate) Eye Drops.
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of the clinical properties and regulatory requirements for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and is not intended to replace the official PI for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to the local approved documentation for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients**: Brinzolamide (carbonic anhydrase inhibitor) and Timolol maleate (non-selective beta-adrenergic receptor blocker).
- **Brand Names**: Azarga [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: S01ED51.
- **Dosage Form and Strength**: Ophthalmic suspension (eye drops) containing brinzolamide 10 mg/mL (1%) and timolol 5 mg/mL (0.5%) [Australian]. 
- **Route of Administration**: Topical ophthalmic.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Novartis Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Intraocular Pressure (IOP) Reduction**: Indicated for the reduction of elevated IOP in patients diagnosed with open-angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension where monotherapy with either agent provides insufficient clinical response [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult)**: The standard regimen is one drop instilled into the conjunctival sac of the affected eye(s) twice daily [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions**:
    - The bottle must be shaken well before use.
    - To minimize systemic absorption and enhance local activity, patients should perform nasolacrimal occlusion and close the eyelid for approximately two minutes immediately after instillation.
    - If multiple topical ophthalmic agents are being used, a minimum 5-minute interval should be maintained between administrations.
    - To prevent contamination, the dropper tip should not contact the eye or any external surface.
- **Switching Therapy**: When replacing a different antiglaucoma agent with this combination, the previous agent should be discontinued and the combination started the following day [Australian].
- **Missed Dose**: Treatment should continue with the next scheduled dose as planned; the dose should not exceed one drop twice daily in the affected eye(s).
- **Contact Lenses**: Azarga contains benzalkonium chloride, which may discolour soft contact lenses. Lenses must be removed before instillation and may be reinserted after 15 minutes [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity**: Known history of hypersensitivity to brinzolamide, other sulfonamides, timolol, other beta-blockers, or any excipient.
- **Respiratory Disorders**: Reactive airway disease, including bronchial asthma (current or history) and severe chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD).
- **Cardiac Disorders**: Sinus bradycardia, second or third-degree atrioventricular block, overt cardiac failure, cardiogenic shock, sino-atrial block, and sick sinus syndrome.
- **Other Conditions**: 
    - Severe allergic rhinitis and bronchial hyperreactivity.
    - Hyperchloraemic acidosis.
    - Severe renal impairment [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Systemic Absorption**: Like all topical ophthalmic agents, systemic absorption occurs. The brinzolamide component is a sulfonamide; serious reactions (e.g., Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis) can occur. Treatment should be discontinued at the first sign of a skin rash or hypersensitivity [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular/Respiratory Safety**: Timolol may cause cardiovascular and pulmonary reactions similar to systemic beta-blockers. Use with caution in patients with coronary heart disease, Prinzmetal’s angina, or hypotension. 
- **Masking of Symptoms**: Beta-blockers may mask symptoms of acute hypoglycemia in diabetic patients and signs of hyperthyroidism.
- **Ocular Effects**: 
    - Use is not recommended in patients with narrow-angle glaucoma.
    - Caution is advised in patients with compromised corneas (e.g., low endothelial cell count, diabetes mellitus) as carbonic anhydrase inhibitors can affect corneal hydration and lead to corneal decompensation or oedema.
- **Surgical Anaesthesia**: The anaesthesiologist should be informed if a patient is using timolol, as it may block systemic beta-agonist effects (e.g., of adrenaline).
- **Anaphylactic Reactions**: Patients with a history of severe atopy/anaphylaxis may be more reactive to repeated allergen challenges and may be less responsive to standard doses of adrenaline [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Clinical trial data (394 patients) indicates the most frequent reaction is transient blurred vision (3.6%) [Australian].

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Dysgeusia (bitter/unusual taste), blurred vision, eye pain, eye irritation, foreign body sensation, decreased heart rate. |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Decreased WBC count, insomnia, corneal erosion, punctate keratitis, photophobia, dry eye, eye discharge, hypotension, COPD, cough, rhinorrhoea, lichen planus, malaise, haematuria. |
| **Rare** (0.01-0.1%) | Eyelid margin crusting, oropharyngeal pain. |

**Serious/Post-Marketing Effects**: Anaphylactic shock, Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), palpitations, tinnitus, hallucination, depression, asthma, dyspnoea, and visual impairment [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Carbonic Anhydrase Inhibitors (CAIs)**: Concomitant use with oral CAIs is not recommended due to potential additive systemic effects.
- **Beta-blockers/Calcium Channel Blockers**: Potential for additive effects leading to hypotension and/or marked bradycardia when used with oral calcium channel blockers, beta-blockers, antiarrhythmics (e.g., amiodarone), or digitalis glycosides.
- **CYP Enzyme Interactions**: 
    - Brinzolamide is metabolized by CYP3A4; caution is advised with CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, ritonavir).
    - Timolol is metabolized by CYP2D6; potent inhibitors (e.g., quinidine, cimetidine) may increase timolol plasma levels and potentiate systemic beta-blockade.
- **Other Interactions**: 
    - Beta-blockers may potentiate the hypertensive reaction to sudden withdrawal of clonidine.
    - Mydriasis has been reported when used concomitantly with adrenaline.
    - Potential interaction with NSAIDs and salicylates due to the CAI component [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: **Category C** [Australian]. There are no adequate data in pregnant women. Animal studies show reproductive toxicity at high systemic doses. Use only if the benefit justifies the risk to the foetus. If used until delivery, monitor the neonate for signs of beta-blockade.
- **Breastfeeding**: Brinzolamide is excreted in animal milk; it is unknown if it is transferred to human milk. Timolol is detectable in human milk. A decision should be made to either discontinue breastfeeding or the medication, based on the importance of the drug to the mother [Australian].
- **Fertility**: No human data available; animal studies showed no adverse effects on fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Not recommended for use in children under 18 years of age due to lack of safety and efficacy data [Australian].
- **Elderly Use**: No dose modifications are required for the elderly.
- **Renal Impairment**: No dose adjustment for mild to moderate impairment. **Contraindicated** in patients with severe renal impairment (CrCl < 30 mL/min) or hyperchloraemic acidosis.
- **Hepatic Impairment**: No studies conducted in this population; however, no dose adjustment is considered necessary [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**:
    - **Brinzolamide**: Potent inhibitor of carbonic anhydrase II (CA-II) in the ciliary processes, reducing bicarbonate ion formation and subsequently reducing sodium and fluid transport, which lowers aqueous humour secretion.
    - **Timolol**: Non-selective beta-blocker that reduces IOP primarily by decreasing aqueous humour formation.
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Both agents are absorbed systemically via the cornea. Steady-state timolol levels are lower than with timolol 0.5% monotherapy.
    - **Distribution**: Brinzolamide has a high affinity for CA-II and is sequestered in red blood cells (RBCs), leading to a long half-life in whole blood.
    - **Metabolism**: Brinzolamide is metabolized primarily via CYP3A4 to N-desethyl brinzolamide. Timolol is metabolized primarily via CYP2D6.
    - **Elimination**: Brinzolamide and metabolites are mainly eliminated renally (~60%). Timolol and metabolites are also primarily excreted by the kidneys [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **12-Month Study (n=437)**: Azarga demonstrated non-inferiority to the fixed combination of dorzolamide 2% + timolol 0.5% in reducing mean IOP (reduction of 7 to 9 mmHg).
- **6-Month Study (n=523)**: Azarga showed a statistically superior reduction in mean IOP (8 to 9 mmHg) compared to both brinzolamide 1% and timolol 0.5% monotherapies. 
- **Ocular Comfort Study (n=96)**: Azarga was found to have superior ocular comfort (less burning and stinging) compared to the dorzolamide/timolol fixed combination [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage**: Store below 25°C. Refrigeration is permitted. Do not freeze.
- **Shelf Life**: Discard the container 4 weeks after initial opening [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule**: **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
