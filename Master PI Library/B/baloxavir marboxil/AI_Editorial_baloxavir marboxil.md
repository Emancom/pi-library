# Editorial Product Information: baloxavir marboxil

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **baloxavir marboxil**
- Chemical class: Cap‑dependent endonuclease inhibitor (selective influenza antiviral prodrug)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral
- Excipients with known effect (clinical relevance): contains lactose (amounts vary by strength) — consider in patients with lactose intolerance or known excipient hypersensitivity.

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of last revision/update of Australian product information: 21 December 2023 [Australian].
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources.
- Dates of last revision/update for other jurisdictions: Not specified in available sources.
- Withdrawal, suspension or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of uncomplicated influenza in patients aged 12 years and older who have been symptomatic for no more than 48 hours and who are:
  - otherwise healthy, or
  - at high risk of developing influenza complications.
- Post‑exposure prophylaxis of influenza in patients aged 12 years and older following close contact with an individual who has confirmed influenza.
- Note: Routine prophylaxis via vaccination is preferred. Where jurisdictional approvals differ, see regional notes: Not specified in available sources for other jurisdictional indication differences.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General
- A single oral dose should be administered within 48 hours of symptom onset for treatment, or as soon as possible within 48 hours following close contact with a confirmed influenza case for post‑exposure prophylaxis.
- May be taken with or without food.
- Avoid co‑administration with calcium‑fortified beverages, polyvalent cation‑containing laxatives, antacids, or oral supplements containing iron, zinc, selenium, calcium or magnesium. Where possible, avoid co‑administration with dairy products.
- Do not co‑administer with polyvalent cation products that may decrease systemic concentrations.

### Recommended single dose for adults and adolescents (≥ 12 years)
- Body weight 40 kg to < 80 kg: 40 mg (single oral dose)
- Body weight ≥ 80 kg: 80 mg (single oral dose)

### Dose adjustments and modifications
- No routine dose reductions are recommended.
- Hepatic impairment: No dose adjustment required for mild (Child‑Pugh A) or moderate (Child‑Pugh B) hepatic impairment. Severe hepatic impairment not studied.
- Renal impairment: Safety and efficacy not studied; change in dose is not required based on available data.
- Elderly: No dose adjustment recommended.
- Paediatric populations: Safety and efficacy not established in patients < 12 years of age or in those weighing < 40 kg. For patients ≥ 12 years and ≥ 40 kg follow the adult/adolescent dosing above.

### Administration instructions
- Single oral dose only (no scheduled repeated dosing for a treatment course).
- Avoid concurrent ingestion of polyvalent cation‑containing products and certain supplements as above.

### Titration / missed dose
- Titration schedules: Not applicable.
- Missed dose instructions: Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **baloxavir marboxil** or to any of the excipients.
- Jurisdictional variations or additional contraindications: Not specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity:** Hypersensitivity reactions including anaphylaxis, facial or throat swelling, skin eruptions and urticaria have been reported post‑marketing. If a hypersensitivity reaction is suspected, discontinue use and institute appropriate treatment.
- **Viral resistance:** Influenza viruses can develop reduced susceptibility (notably substitutions at PA position I38T/M/F/N/S) associated with reduced baloxavir sensitivity; this may diminish clinical benefit. Consider local/regional influenza susceptibility data when selecting therapy.
- **Use in hepatic impairment:** Safety and efficacy in severe hepatic impairment not studied; exercise caution.
- **Use in renal impairment:** Safety and efficacy in patients with renal impairment not studied.
- **Vaccination interactions:** Concurrent use with intranasal live attenuated influenza vaccine (LAIV) has not been evaluated; antiviral drugs may inhibit viral replication of LAIV and reduce vaccine effectiveness. Interactions with inactivated influenza vaccines have not been evaluated.
- **Effects on laboratory tests:** No data available in the provided sources.
- **Overdose:** No specific antidote; manage supportively. Baloxavir is unlikely to be significantly removed by dialysis due to high serum protein binding.
- **QTc:** At twice the expected exposure from recommended dosing, no QTc prolongation was observed.

Monitoring requirements
- Monitor for signs of hypersensitivity and severe gastrointestinal events (see Adverse Effects).
- Monitor clinical response and consider virological testing if treatment failure or clinical deterioration occurs, particularly where resistance is suspected.

## 7. Adverse Effects

The safety profile is derived from pooled clinical data and post‑marketing reports. Frequency categories follow: very common (>10%), common (1–10%), uncommon (0.1–1%), rare (<0.1%), very rare (<0.01%), and not known (cannot be estimated from available data). Where event frequencies were provided in trials, those percentages are shown.

### Summary from clinical trials (acute uncomplicated influenza trials; adults/adolescents)
- Overall safety population: data from 2483 subjects; 1640 received **baloxavir marboxil** in placebo‑controlled studies.
- In treatment trials (recommended dose), the most commonly reported adverse events (≥ 1%) were:

| System organ class / Adverse reaction | Frequency (trial data) |

| Gastrointestinal disorders: Diarrhoea | Common — 3% |
| Respiratory, thoracic and mediastinal disorders: Bronchitis | Common — 3% |
| Gastrointestinal disorders: Nausea | Common — 2% |
| Respiratory, thoracic and mediastinal disorders: Sinusitis | Common — 2% |
| Nervous system disorders: Headache | Common — 1% |

- Prophylaxis study (post‑exposure): Nasopharyngitis was the most frequent event (6.4% in treated subjects) — Common.

### Post‑marketing adverse reactions (frequency cannot be reliably estimated)
- Immune system disorders:
  - Anaphylaxis, anaphylactic reactions, hypersensitivity — Not known (post‑marketing reports)
- Skin and subcutaneous tissue disorders:
  - Angioedema, urticaria, rash — Not known (post‑marketing reports)
- Gastrointestinal disorders:
  - Vomiting — Not known (post‑marketing reports)
  - Bloody diarrhoea — Not known (post‑marketing reports)
  - Melaena — Not known (post‑marketing reports)
  - Ischemic colitis — Not known (post‑marketing reports)

Serious adverse effects highlighted
- Severe hypersensitivity reactions including anaphylaxis and angioedema — reported post‑marketing and may be life‑threatening.
- Ischemic colitis and other severe gastrointestinal bleeding events (bloody diarrhoea, melaena) — reported post‑marketing.

Notes
- Because post‑marketing reports are voluntary, true incidence cannot be established from these data.
- The clinical trial frequencies above compare close to or equal to placebo for many events; however, all events listed should be considered potential adverse reactions.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Clinically important interactions and management
- Polyvalent cation‑containing products (including calcium‑fortified beverages, polyvalent cation laxatives, antacids, and oral supplements containing iron, zinc, selenium, calcium, magnesium): May decrease plasma concentrations of active drug; avoid co‑administration and separate dosing where possible.
- Dairy products: Avoid concurrent administration where possible.

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- P‑glycoprotein (P‑gp):
  - **baloxavir marboxil** and baloxavir are substrates for P‑gp.
  - Itraconazole (P‑gp inhibitor) increased baloxavir Cmax by ~1.33‑fold and AUC by ~1.23‑fold — increases not considered clinically meaningful.
  - A single 80 mg dose of baloxavir did not affect digoxin pharmacokinetics (digoxin is a P‑gp substrate) — not expected to affect co‑administered P‑gp substrates.
- UGT / Probenecid:
  - Probenecid decreased baloxavir Cmax by 21% and AUC by 25% — decreases not considered clinically meaningful.
- CYP and UGT enzymes:
  - In vitro at clinically relevant concentrations, baloxavir marboxil and baloxavir did not inhibit CYP1A2, CYP2B6, CYP2C8, CYP2C9, CYP2C19, CYP2D6, CYP3A4, or the tested UGT isozymes, and did not cause significant induction of CYP1A2, CYP2B6, or CYP3A4 — no expected clinically relevant interactions via these pathways.
  - A single 40 mg dose did not affect midazolam pharmacokinetics (CYP3A4 substrate) — not expected to affect CYP3A substrates.
- Transporters:
  - In vitro, baloxavir marboxil inhibited P‑gp; baloxavir inhibited BCRP (breast cancer resistance protein).
  - Single 80 mg dose decreased rosuvastatin (a BCRP substrate) Cmax and AUC by 18% and 17%, respectively — not considered clinically meaningful.
  - Baloxavir is not expected to be an in vivo inhibitor of OATP1B1, OATP1B3, OCT1, OCT2, OAT3, MATE1, or MATE2K based on in vitro data.
- Dialysis: Baloxavir is unlikely to be significantly removed by dialysis due to high serum protein binding.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Concomitant administration with intranasal live attenuated influenza vaccine (LAIV) has not been evaluated; antiviral therapy may reduce replication of LAIV and thereby reduce vaccine effectiveness — avoid concurrent administration of LAIV and antiviral therapy where possible.
- Interactions with inactivated influenza vaccines have not been evaluated.

### Regional interaction notes
- No additional jurisdiction‑specific interaction restrictions were specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Australian pregnancy category: **Category B3** [Australian].
- Human data: No adequate, well‑controlled studies in pregnant women; potential risk unknown. Avoid use during pregnancy unless potential benefit justifies potential risk to the fetus.
- Animal data: Baloxavir crosses the placenta in rats. No malformations observed in rat or rabbit embryofetal studies at tested doses. In rabbits, high doses produced maternal toxicity with two miscarriages and an increased incidence of a skeletal variation (cervical rib) at 1000 mg/kg/day; lower doses were without adverse effects in rabbits. Pre‑ and postnatal studies in rats showed no drug‑related adverse findings up to highest tested dose.

### Breastfeeding
- It is not known whether baloxavir marboxil or baloxavir are excreted in human milk. At 1 mg/kg, baloxavir or metabolites were detected in milk of lactating rats.
- Recommendation: Weigh potential benefits of therapy for the mother against potential risks to the infant; consider discontinuing breastfeeding or the drug as clinically appropriate.

### Fertility
- No effects on fertility observed in male and female rats at doses up to 1000 mg/kg/day (approx. 2× human exposure based on AUC at the maximum recommended human dose).

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Safety and efficacy not established in patients under 12 years of age or in those weighing < 40 kg. For patients ≥ 12 years and ≥ 40 kg follow adult/adolescent dosing (40 mg if 40–<80 kg; 80 mg if ≥ 80 kg).

### Elderly
- No dose adjustment recommended for geriatric patients. Safety and efficacy established in patients aged ≥ 65 years and weighing at least 40 kg.

### Renal impairment
- Safety and efficacy have not been studied in patients with renal impairment. Available data indicate no change in dosing is required; however, use in renal impairment has not been formally evaluated.

### Hepatic impairment
- Mild (Child‑Pugh A) and moderate (Child‑Pugh B) hepatic impairment: no dose adjustment required.
- Severe hepatic impairment: not studied; use with caution.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **baloxavir marboxil** is a prodrug that is rapidly converted by hydrolysis to the active moiety, baloxavir. Baloxavir inhibits the cap‑dependent endonuclease (CEN) activity within the polymerase acidic (PA) subunit of influenza viral RNA polymerase, thereby blocking viral mRNA transcription and inhibiting influenza virus replication.

### Pharmacodynamics
- In enzyme inhibition assays, the IC50 of baloxavir for influenza A viruses ranged approximately 1.4–3.1 nmol/L and for influenza B viruses approximately 4.5–8.9 nmol/L.
- Cell‑based assays (MDCK cells) demonstrated median EC50 values of:
  - A/H1N1: ~0.73 nmol/L (range 0.20–1.85)
  - A/H3N2: ~0.83 nmol/L (range 0.35–2.63)
  - Type B viruses: ~5.97 nmol/L (range 2.67–14.23)
- Baloxavir shows potent in vitro and in vivo antiviral activity against influenza A and B strains, including many neuraminidase inhibitor‑resistant strains.
- Viruses with PA/I38 substitutions demonstrate reduced susceptibility; the clinical significance of these findings is uncertain.
- At twice the expected clinical exposure, no QTc prolongation was observed.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Conversion: Baloxavir marboxil is a prodrug hydrolysed to active baloxavir.
- Protein binding / dialysis: High serum protein binding; baloxavir is unlikely to be significantly removed by dialysis.
- Transporter and enzyme pathways:
  - Substrate for P‑gp.
  - Baloxavir inhibits BCRP in vitro; marboxil inhibits P‑gp in vitro.
  - Not expected to be an in vivo inhibitor of major hepatic uptake transporters (OATP1B1/1B3) or renal transporters (OCT1/2, OAT3, MATE1/2K) based on in vitro data.
- Specific numeric PK parameters (Tmax, half‑life, absolute bioavailability, clearance) and detailed PK in special populations beyond what is summarized above: Not specified in available sources.

## 12. Clinical Trials
### Key pivotal trials and outcomes (summary)
- CAPSTONE‑1 (Study 1601T0831): Randomized, double‑blind, multicentre, placebo‑ and active‑controlled study in otherwise healthy adolescents and adults (≥ 12 to ≤ 64 years) with confirmed influenza, comparing single‑dose **baloxavir marboxil** (weight‑based 40/80 mg) vs placebo and oseltamivir (active control).
  - Primary efficacy endpoint: time to alleviation of influenza symptoms (composite of cough, sore throat, headache, nasal congestion, feverishness or chills, muscle/joint pain, fatigue).
  - Results: Median time to alleviation of symptoms was shorter with **baloxavir marboxil** versus placebo (median ~53.7 hours vs ~80.2 hours; difference approximately −26.5 hours; p < 0.0001).
  - Comparison with oseltamivir: No statistically significant difference in time to alleviation of symptoms between **baloxavir marboxil** and oseltamivir (median ~53.5 h vs ~53.8 h).
  - Fever resolution occurred more rapidly with **baloxavir marboxil** compared with placebo (median ~24.5 h vs ~42.0 h); no difference versus oseltamivir.
  - Virology: Rapid reduction in viral titres with **baloxavir marboxil**; median time to cessation of viral shedding by virus titre approximately 24 hours for baloxavir vs 72 hours for oseltamivir and 96 hours for placebo in the study.
- CAPSTONE‑2 and other studies: Additional randomized trials included high‑risk patient cohorts and prophylaxis (BLOCKSTONE) studies. Pooled clinical trial experience included 2483 subjects across trials, with 1640 subjects receiving **baloxavir marboxil** in three placebo‑controlled studies; safety profile in high‑risk patients was similar to otherwise healthy patients.
- Resistance monitoring in trials:
  - Treatment‑emergent PA protein substitutions associated with reduced susceptibility (notably I38 variants) were observed with variable frequencies in pooled trial data:
    - Influenza A/H1N1: 3.7% (5/135)
    - Influenza A/H3N2: 10.1% (49/486)
    - Influenza B: 0.9% (2/228)
  - In the prophylaxis study, PA/I38T/M substitutions were detected in 10 of 374 treated subjects (2.7%).
- Clinical relevance of treatment‑emergent reduced susceptibility is unclear and should be considered when assessing therapeutic response.
