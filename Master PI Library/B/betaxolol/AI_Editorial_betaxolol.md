# Editorial Product Information: Betaxolol Hydrochloride (Ophthalmic)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a single, coherent reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI. Clinicians should consult the current approved PI in their specific region before prescribing or dispensing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Betaxolol hydrochloride (a cardioselective beta-1 adrenergic receptor inhibitor).
- **Brand Name(s):** **BETOPTIC S** [US].
- **ATC Code:** S01ED02.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Ophthalmic suspension: 0.25% (equivalent to 2.5 mg/mL betaxolol base) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Topical ophthalmic use.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Novartis Pharmaceuticals Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Elevated Intraocular Pressure (IOP):** Indicated for the reduction of IOP in patients with chronic open-angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** Instill one drop in the affected eye(s) twice daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Shake well before using [US].
    - To prevent contamination, do not touch the dropper tip to the eye or any other surface [US].
    - If using multiple topical ophthalmic medications, allow at least 10 minutes between the instillation of **BETOPTIC S** and other agents [US].
- **Titration:** In some patients, the IOP-lowering response may require several weeks to stabilise; careful monitoring is recommended during initiation [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to any component of the formulation [US].
- Sinus bradycardia [US].
- Greater than a first-degree atrioventricular (AV) block [US].
- Overt cardiac failure [US].
- Cardiogenic shock [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Systemic Absorption:** Topically applied betaxolol is absorbed systemically. The same adverse reactions associated with systemic beta-blockers may occur, including severe respiratory reactions (bronchospasm in asthmatic patients) and cardiac failure/death [US].
- **Cardiac Failure:** Discontinue at the first signs of cardiac failure. Exercise caution in patients with a history of heart block or cardiac failure [US].
- **Diabetes Mellitus:** Beta-blockers may mask signs of acute hypoglycemia (e.g., tachycardia). Use with caution in diabetic patients, especially those with labile diabetes [US].
- **Thyrotoxicosis:** May mask clinical signs of hyperthyroidism (e.g., tachycardia). Avoid abrupt withdrawal, as it may precipitate a thyroid storm [US].
- **Muscle Weakness:** Potential to potentiate muscle weakness consistent with myasthenic symptoms (e.g., diplopia, ptosis, generalized weakness) [US].
- **Surgical Anesthesia:** Gradual withdrawal prior to major surgery may be considered, as beta-blockade impairs the heart's ability to respond to reflex stimuli; however, effects can be reversed with adrenergic agonists during surgery if necessary [US].
- **Respiratory Disease:** Use with caution in patients with excessive restriction of pulmonary function. While ophthalmic betaxolol has shown minimal effect on pulmonary function tests in some studies, asthmatic attacks and pulmonary distress have been reported [US].
- **Atopy/Anaphylaxis:** Patients with a history of severe anaphylaxis may be more reactive to allergens while on beta-blockers and may be unresponsive to standard doses of epinephrine [US].
- **Angle-Closure Glaucoma:** Should not be used alone; treatment requires constricting the pupil to reopen the angle, and betaxolol has little to no effect on the pupil [US].
- **Vascular Insufficiency:** Use with caution in patients with Raynaud’s phenomenon or cerebral vascular insufficiency [US].
- **Contact Lens Wear:** The preservative benzalkonium chloride may be absorbed by soft contact lenses. Lenses should be removed prior to instillation and may be reinserted 15 minutes post-dose [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequencies are based on clinical trial data [US].

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Transient ocular discomfort |
| **Common/Uncommon** | **Ocular:** Blurred vision, corneal punctate keratitis, tearing, photophobia, itching, dryness, erythema, ocular pain, decreased visual acuity, crusty lashes. |
| **Systemic (Rare)** | **Cardiovascular:** Bradycardia, heart block, congestive failure. <br> **Pulmonary:** Dyspnea, bronchospasm, asthma, respiratory failure. <br> **CNS:** Insomnia, dizziness, vertigo, headache, depression, lethargy. <br> **Other:** Hives, toxic epidermal necrolysis, alopecia, glossitis, taste/smell perversion. |

- **Additional Potential Reactions:** Ocular edema, anisocoria, and decreased corneal sensitivity have been reported with other betaxolol formulations [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Oral Beta-Blockers:** Potential for additive effects on IOP or systemic beta-blockade [US].
- **Catecholamine-depleting drugs (e.g., Reserpine):** Risk of hypotension and/or bradycardia leading to vertigo or syncope [US].
- **Calcium Antagonists, Antiarrhythmics (e.g., Amiodarone), and Digitalis:** Potential for additive effects resulting in hypotension and marked bradycardia [US].
- **Adrenergic Psychotropic Drugs:** Caution is advised due to potential pharmacodynamic interactions [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned a letter, but labeled as "use only if potential benefit justifies potential risk."
    - **Data:** No adequate human studies. Animal studies (oral administration) showed embryo-fetal lethality and developmental abnormalities at doses 778 to 4982 times the maximum human ophthalmic dose [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Oral beta-blockers are excreted in human milk. It is unknown if topical ophthalmic administration results in measurable levels in milk. Consider the mother's clinical need vs. potential risks to the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on rat fertility were observed at oral doses up to 256 mg/kg/day [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have been demonstrated in pediatric patients in a 3-month trial; the adverse effect profile was comparable to adults [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness compared to younger patients [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A cardioselective (beta-1) receptor inhibitor. It reduces IOP by decreasing aqueous humor production, as demonstrated by tonography and fluorophotometry [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:**
    - Onset: Within 30 minutes [US].
    - Peak effect: Approximately 2 hours [US].
    - Duration: A single dose provides IOP reduction for 12 hours [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Systemic absorption occurs following topical application. Specific distribution/metabolism parameters for the ophthalmic suspension are not detailed in the source [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Equivalence:** Controlled double-masked studies established that **BETOPTIC S** (0.25% suspension) is clinically equivalent in magnitude and duration of IOP reduction to betaxolol 0.5% solution [US].
- **Pulmonary Safety:** In a challenge study of nine patients with reactive airway disease, ophthalmic betaxolol 1% did not significantly affect FEV1 or FVC compared to placebo and did not inhibit the action of isoproterenol [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store upright at 2°C to 25°C (36°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Stability:** Use until the expiration date on the bottle [US].
- **Handling:** Shake well before use. Keep bottle closed when not in use [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription Drug).
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
