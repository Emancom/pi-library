# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document is intended as a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, providing a concise summary of the clinical profile of levocarnitine.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official, current Product Information approved in a specific jurisdiction for prescribing purposes.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (naturally occurring carrier molecule).
- **Brand Names:** Not specified as a brand name in the source; provided as Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg, administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose may be given, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
    - Frequency: Every 3 to 4 hours, and no less than every 6 hours [US].
    - Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg or as clinically required; the maximum recorded dose is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - Administration: Slow 2–3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - Adjustments: Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occur within minutes of IV administration. Discontinue treatment immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Levocarnitine may cause seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a known seizure history, an increase in frequency or severity has been reported [US].
- **Monitoring:** 
    - Obtain plasma carnitine concentrations before starting parenteral therapy [US].
    - Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Caution):** While not specific to the IV form, high doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD on dialysis may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **General:** Transient nausea and vomiting have been observed. Body odor and gastritis are reported less frequently [US].
- **Common (≥5% Frequency in ESRD Clinical Trials, regardless of causality) [US]:**
    - **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (9%), accidental injury (10%), asthenia (9%), chest pain (12%), fever (7%), headache (22%), injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%).
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%).
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%).
    - **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%), hypervolemia (5%).
    - **Nervous:** Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), paresthesia (5%).
    - **Respiratory:** Cough increased (9%), dyspnea (7%), pharyngitis (23%), rhinitis (9%).
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, and seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred. It is recommended to monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned by letter, but label states use only if clearly needed]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women exist [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies in nursing mothers have not been conducted. Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk; studies in cows show increased milk concentrations following exogenous administration. Risks to the child must be weighed against maternal benefits [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders applies to pediatric patients. Safety and efficacy in ESRD pediatric patients are not specifically detailed beyond general dosing guidelines [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used to treat carnitine deficiency in ESRD patients on dialysis. Note: Oral administration is cautioned in this group due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production in all tissues except the brain [US]. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and [³H]-γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Administration to ESRD patients results in increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic/Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials in ESRD patients (N=12 to N=30 per group) demonstrated that doses of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg significantly increased trough levocarnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks compared to baseline [US].
- **Clinical Outcomes:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the effects on clinical outcomes in this specific population have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat. Vials are single-dose and contain no preservative; discard any unused portion [US].
