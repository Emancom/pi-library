# Editorial Product Information: Bosutinib

## Editorial Note
- This harmonised editorial overview was developed using the following jurisdiction-specific Product Information (PI): 
    - **US (FDA):** Bosutinib tablets, film-coated (Dr. Reddy’s Laboratories Inc.), revised 04/2026.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a clinical synthesis of available regulatory information for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s current approved labeling.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** **Bosutinib** (as monohydrate)
- **Chemical Class:** Kinase inhibitor (specifically a tyrosine kinase inhibitor)
- **Brand Names:** **Bosulif** (referenced as the innovator product) [US]
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - 400 mg film-coated tablets: Orange, oval, biconvex, debossed with “400” on one side and “B” on the other [US].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Dr. Reddy's Laboratories Inc. [US]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Newly-Diagnosed Chronic Phase (CP) Ph+ CML:** Treatment of adult patients with newly-diagnosed Philadelphia chromosome-positive (Ph+) chronic myelogenous leukemia (CML) [US].
- **Resistant or Intolerant Ph+ CML:** Treatment of adult patients with chronic, accelerated (AP), or blast phase (BP) Ph+ CML with resistance or intolerance to prior therapy [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Newly-Diagnosed CP Ph+ CML:** 400 mg orally once daily with food [US].
- **Resistant/Intolerant CP, AP, or BP Ph+ CML:** 500 mg orally once daily with food [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets must be swallowed whole; do not cut, crush, break, or chew [US].
- Administration must occur with food [US].
- Therapy should continue until disease progression or patient intolerance [US].

### Titration/Dose Escalation
- Incremental increases of 100 mg daily (up to a maximum of 600 mg/day) may be considered in patients who have not achieved complete hematologic, cytogenetic, or molecular response, provided they have not experienced Grade 3 or higher adverse reactions [US].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- If a dose is missed by more than 12 hours, the patient should skip that dose and resume the usual prescribed dose the following day. No double doses should be taken [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known history of hypersensitivity to **bosutinib**. Reported reactions include anaphylaxis [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Gastrointestinal Toxicity:** Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, and abdominal pain are frequent. Diarrhea usually occurs early (median onset 2–4 days). Manage with antidiarrheals, antiemetics, and fluid replacement. Withhold, reduce dose, or discontinue based on severity [US].
- **Myelosuppression:** Thrombocytopenia, anemia, and neutropenia occur. Monitor Complete Blood Count (CBC) weekly for the first month and monthly thereafter [US].
- **Hepatic Toxicity:** Elevations in ALT, AST, and bilirubin have been observed. Monitor liver enzymes monthly for the first 3 months and then as clinically indicated. Specific discontinuation is required for Hy’s Law cases (ALT/AST ≥3x ULN with bilirubin >2x ULN and alkaline phosphatase <2x ULN) [US].
- **Cardiovascular Toxicity:** Includes cardiac failure, left ventricular dysfunction, and cardiac ischemic events. Risk is higher in older patients or those with pre-existing risk factors (e.g., diabetes, hypertension). Monitor for symptoms and treat promptly [US].
- **Fluid Retention:** May manifest as pericardial effusion, pleural effusion, pulmonary edema, or peripheral edema. Monitor and manage with standard care (e.g., diuretics) [US].
- **Renal Toxicity:** A decline in eGFR is common during therapy. Monitor renal function at baseline and throughout treatment [US].
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Can cause fetal harm based on animal data. Verify pregnancy status before starting treatment [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency and Clinical Presentation
| System Organ Class | Very Common (>10%) | Common (1–10%) or Noted Serious |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Diarrhea (up to 85%), Abdominal pain, Nausea, Vomiting | Constipation, Gastritis, Pancreatitis |
| **Hepatobiliary** | Hepatic dysfunction (elevated ALT/AST) | Drug-induced liver injury |
| **Skin/Subcutaneous** | Rash (up to 44%), Pruritus | Stevens-Johnson syndrome, Erythema multiforme |
| **General/Infections** | Fatigue, Pyrexia, Respiratory tract infection | Pneumonia, Influenza, Pain |
| **Nervous System** | Headache, Dizziness | Dysgeusia |
| **Respiratory** | Cough, Dysnea | Pleural effusion, Pulmonary hypertension |
| **Vascular** | Hypertension | Cardiac failure, Ischemia |

### Common Laboratory Abnormalities (≥20%)
- **Hematology:** Decreased hemoglobin, lymphocytes, platelets, white blood cells, and absolute neutrophil count [US].
- **Biochemistry:** Increased creatinine, ALT, AST, glucose, urate, alkaline phosphatase, lipase, amylase, and creatine kinase; decreased calcium and phosphorus [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Strong/Moderate CYP3A Inhibitors:** Avoid concomitant use (e.g., ketoconazole, ritonavir). These significantly increase **bosutinib** plasma concentrations, raising toxicity risk [US].
- **Strong CYP3A Inducers:** Avoid concomitant use (e.g., rifampin, phenytoin, St. John’s wort). These significantly decrease **bosutinib** concentrations, potentially reducing efficacy [US].
- **Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs):** Avoid concomitant use. **Bosutinib** solubility is pH-dependent; PPIs reduce its absorption. Use short-acting antacids or H2 blockers, separated from the **bosutinib** dose by more than 2 hours [US].
- **P-gp Substrates:** No clinically significant interaction with P-gp substrates (e.g., dabigatran) has been observed [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned/Letter-less labeling]. Based on animal studies at 1.2 times human exposure, **bosutinib** can cause structural abnormalities, embryo-fetal mortality, and growth alterations. Females of reproductive potential must use effective contraception during treatment and for 2 weeks after the final dose [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Due to the risk of serious adverse reactions in the infant, breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for 2 weeks after the last dose [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies indicate potential for reduced fertility in both males and females [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in patients <1 year of age for CP-CML, or in any pediatric age group for AP- or BP-CML. Note: Some pediatric information is subject to marketing exclusivity for the innovator product [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in efficacy/safety compared to younger patients, though greater sensitivity in some older individuals cannot be ruled out [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Reduce starting dose based on CrCl [US]:
    - **CrCl 30–50 mL/min:** 300 mg (Newly diagnosed); 400 mg (Resistant/Intolerant).
    - **CrCl <30 mL/min:** 200 mg (Newly diagnosed); 300 mg (Resistant/Intolerant).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Reduce starting dose to 200 mg daily for patients with any degree of impairment (Child-Pugh A, B, or C) [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI). It inhibits the BCR-ABL kinase and Src-family kinases (Src, Lyn, Hck). It is active against 16 of 18 imatinib-resistant forms of BCR-ABL but notably does **not** inhibit T315I or V299L mutations [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is ~34%. Tmax occurs at ~6 hours. Food (specifically high-fat) increases Cmax and AUC by ~1.6 to 1.8-fold [US].
    - **Distribution:** Large apparent volume of distribution (6,080 L); highly protein bound (94–96%) [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized by CYP3A4 [US].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is ~22.5 hours. Excretion is primarily fecal (91.3%) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **BFORE Trial (Newly-Diagnosed CP CML):** A Phase 3 study comparing **bosutinib** 400 mg vs. imatinib 400 mg. **Bosutinib** demonstrated a significantly higher Major Molecular Response (MMR) at 12 months (47% vs. 37%) and Complete Cytogenetic Response (CCyR) (77% vs. 66%) [US].
- **Study 200 (Resistant/Intolerant CML):** A Phase 1/2 study in patients previously treated with imatinib (and potentially other TKIs). MCyR at 24 weeks was 40.1% in those previously treated with imatinib only [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Avoid touching or handling crushed or broken tablets. Dispose of in accordance with local hazardous drug/anticancer drug requirements [US].
