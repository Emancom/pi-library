# Editorial Product Information: Brexpiprazole

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **brexpiprazole** product information, synthesized for use by healthcare professionals. It integrates clinical data, regulatory requirements, and pharmacological profiles.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for legal and regulatory compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** **Brexpiprazole**
- **Chemical Class:** Atypical antipsychotic; Serotonin-dopamine activity modulator (SDAM).
- **Brand Name(s):** **REXULTI** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N05AX16.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Film-coated tablets: 0.25 mg, 0.5 mg, 1 mg, 2 mg, 3 mg, and 4 mg [Australian]. 
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Lundbeck Australia Pty Ltd and Otsuka Australia Pharmaceutical Pty. Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Schizophrenia:** Treatment of schizophrenia in adult patients [Australian].
- **Agitation in Alzheimer’s Dementia (AAD):** Treatment of agitation associated with Alzheimer’s dementia in adult patients who have not responded to non-pharmacological interventions [Australian].
  - *Note:* Not indicated for "as needed" (PRN) use [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
**Brexpiprazole** should be administered once daily, with or without food [Australian].

### Schizophrenia
- **Starting Dose:** 1 mg once daily on Days 1 to 4.
- **Titration:** Increase to 2 mg once daily on Day 5, then to 4 mg on Day 8 based on clinical response.
- **Target Range:** 2 mg to 4 mg once daily.
- **Maximum Dose:** 4 mg once daily [Australian].

### Agitation Associated with Alzheimer’s Dementia
- **Starting Dose:** 0.5 mg once daily on Days 1 to 7.
- **Titration:** 1 mg once daily on Days 8 to 14, then 2 mg once daily on Day 15.
- **Target Range:** 2 mg to 3 mg once daily.
- **Dose Escalation:** After at least 28 days at 2 mg, the dose may be increased to 3 mg if clinically warranted.
- **Maximum Dose:** 3 mg once daily [Australian].
- **Treatment Duration:** Efficacy beyond 12 weeks has not been established in placebo-controlled trials [Australian].

### Administration and Missed Doses
- Tablets must be swallowed whole; they have not been evaluated for administration via enteral tubes or in patients with dysphagia [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Take as soon as possible unless close to the next scheduled dose. Do not double the dose [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **brexpiprazole** or any of its excipients (e.g., lactose) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Increased Mortality in Elderly Patients with Dementia-Related Psychosis:** Antipsychotic use in this population is associated with an increased risk of death. **Brexpiprazole** is not approved for dementia-related psychosis or agitation caused by conditions other than Alzheimer's Disease (e.g., acute delirium) [Australian].
- **Cerebrovascular Adverse Reactions:** Increased incidence of stroke and TIA, including fatalities, has been observed in elderly patients with dementia [Australian].
- **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** Potentially fatal; requires immediate discontinuation of all antipsychotics and intensive symptomatic monitoring [Australian].
- **Tardive Dyskinesia:** A potentially irreversible syndrome of involuntary movements. Risk increases with duration of treatment and total cumulative dose [Australian].
- **Metabolic Changes:** 
    - **Hyperglycaemia/Diabetes:** Regular monitoring of blood glucose is required, especially in patients with risk factors.
    - **Dyslipidaemia:** Undesirable lipid alterations may occur.
    - **Weight Gain:** Clinical monitoring of weight is recommended [Australian].
- **Impulse Control Disorders:** Post-marketing reports indicate a risk of pathological gambling and other compulsive behaviours (e.g., binge eating, hypersexuality) [Australian].
- **Orthostatic Hypotension and Syncope:** Risk is highest during initial titration or dose escalation. Monitor vital signs in patients with known cardiovascular or cerebrovascular disease [Australian].
- **Leukopenia, Neutropenia, and Agranulocytosis:** Monitor Complete Blood Count (CBC) in patients with a history of low White Blood Cell (WBC) counts. Discontinue if absolute neutrophil count (ANC) is <1000/mm³ [Australian].
- **Seizures:** Use with caution in patients with a history of seizures or conditions that lower the seizure threshold [Australian].
- **Lactose Content:** Tablets contain lactose; not recommended for patients with galactose intolerance or glucose-galactose malabsorption [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Schizophrenia (Acute Therapy)
| Adverse Reaction | Brexpiprazole (All Doses) | Placebo |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Akathisia** | 5.5% | 4.6% |
| **Weight Increased** | 3.9% | 1.6% |
| **Dyspepsia** | 3.2% | 1.9% |
| **Diarrhoea** | 2.9% | 1.9% |
| **Tremor** | 2.5% | 0.5% |
| **Sedation** | 2.1% | 0.8% |

### Agitation in Alzheimer’s Dementia
Common reactions (≥2% and > placebo) include:
- **Nervous System:** Somnolence (3.4%), dizziness, akathisia.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhoea (2.0%), dry mouth (1.4%).
- **Infections:** Urinary tract infection (2.6%), bronchitis, cystitis.
- **Other:** Insomnia (3.7%), weight increase (1.1%), fatigue (1.2%) [Australian].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- NMS, tardive dyskinesia, severe hyperglycaemia, and venous thromboembolism (VTE) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
**Brexpiprazole** is primarily metabolised by CYP3A4 and CYP2D6 [Australian].

| Scenario | Dose Adjustment for Brexpiprazole |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Strong CYP2D6 Inhibitors** (e.g., quinidine) | Administer 1/2 the usual dose |
| **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors** (e.g., ketoconazole) | Administer 1/2 the usual dose |
| **CYP2D6 PM* receiving Strong/Mod CYP3A4 Inhibitors** | Administer 1/4 the usual dose |
| **Strong CYP3A4 + Strong CYP2D6 Inhibitors** | Administer 1/4 the usual dose |
| **Strong CYP3A4 Inducers** (e.g., rifampicin) | Double the usual dose (over 1-2 weeks) |

*\*PM = Poor Metabolisers*

- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Use caution with other CNS-active drugs, alcohol, and medications known to prolong the QT interval or cause electrolyte imbalances [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Neonates exposed to antipsychotics during the third trimester are at risk for extrapyramidal and/or withdrawal symptoms. Use only if potential benefits outweigh risks to the fetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** Excreted in rat milk; human excretion is unknown. A decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the mother's clinical need [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed diestrus and decreased fertility in females at high doses; testicular lesions were noted in males at exposures ≥6-fold clinical levels [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients <18 years have not been evaluated [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in schizophrenia patients ≥65 or AAD patients ≥90. Use caution due to potential for decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** For moderate, severe, or end-stage renal impairment (CrCl <60 mL/min), the maximum dose is 3 mg (Schizophrenia) or 2 mg (AAD) [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** For moderate to severe impairment (Child-Pugh ≥7), the maximum dose is 3 mg (Schizophrenia) or 2 mg (AAD) [Australian].
- **CYP2D6 Poor Metabolisers:** Dose reduction to 1/2 the usual maintenance dose is required [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Partial agonist at dopamine D2, D3, and serotonin 5-HT1A receptors; antagonist at 5-HT2A, 5-HT2B, 5-HT7, and noradrenergic alpha-1A/1B/1D and alpha-2C receptors [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Well absorbed; absolute bioavailability is 95.1%. Tmax is ~4 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (>99%). Vd ~1.56 L/kg.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily CYP3A4 and CYP2D6. Major metabolite DM-3411 is not considered to contribute to therapeutic effects.
    - **Elimination:** Half-life is ~91 hours. Excretion: 25% in urine, 46% in faeces [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Schizophrenia:** Two 6-week acute trials and one long-term maintenance trial demonstrated significant improvement in PANSS total scores and CGI-S compared to placebo. Maintenance therapy significantly delayed time to relapse (Hazard Ratio 3.42) [Australian].
- **AAD Agitation:** Two 12-week trials (Trial 4 and 5) demonstrated significant improvement in the Cohen-Mansfield Agitation Inventory (CMAI) total score for 2 mg and 3 mg doses compared to placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Packaging:** PVC/Aluminium foil blisters [Australian].
