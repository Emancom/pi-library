# Editorial Product Information: Bivalirudin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical data into a structured reference format.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory guidelines for the most current information.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Bivalirudin (as trifluoroacetate).
- **Chemical Class:** Synthetic, 20-amino acid residue peptide; specific and reversible direct thrombin inhibitor (DTI).
- **Brand Name(s):** BIVALIRUDIN ARX [Australian].
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Lyophilised powder for injection (250 mg per single-use glass vial).
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Bivalirudin** is indicated for use as an anticoagulant in the following settings:
- **Acute Coronary Syndromes (ACS):** Treatment of patients with moderate-to-high risk unstable angina (UA) or non-ST segment elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI) undergoing early invasive management [Australian].
- **Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI):** Patients undergoing PCI [Australian].

**Usage Note:** Bivalirudin is intended for use with aspirin (300-325 mg daily) and may be used with a P2Y12 antagonist (e.g., clopidogrel) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Bivalirudin must be administered via an IV line and is **not** intended for intramuscular use. It is intended for use with aspirin.

### Acute Coronary Syndromes (ACS)
- **Starting Dose:** IV bolus of 0.1 mg/kg followed by an infusion of 0.25 mg/kg/hour.
- **Medical Management:** Infusion may continue for up to 72 hours.
- **Transition to PCI:** If the patient proceeds to PCI, an additional bolus of 0.5 mg/kg should be given at initiation, and the infusion rate increased to 1.75 mg/kg/hour for the duration of the procedure. 
- **Post-PCI:** The infusion may be reduced back to 0.25 mg/kg/hour for 4 to 12 hours as clinically necessary.

### Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI)
- **Standard Dosing:** IV bolus of 0.75 mg/kg immediately followed by an IV infusion of 1.75 mg/kg/hour for the duration of the procedure (or up to 4 hours post-PCI).
- **STEMI Patients:** In primary PCI for ST-elevation MI (STEMI), the 1.75 mg/kg/hour infusion should be continued for up to 4 hours post-procedure [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Reconstitution:** Add 5 mL of Water for Injections (WFI) to a 250 mg vial. Gently swirl until dissolved.
- **Dilution:** Further dilute the reconstituted solution in 5% Glucose or 0.9% Sodium Chloride to a final concentration of 5 mg/mL (total volume of 50 mL).
- **Weight-Based Adjustment:** Doses must be adjusted according to the patient's weight.

## 4. Contraindications
- Active bleeding or increased risk of bleeding due to haemostasis/coagulation disorders.
- Severe uncontrolled hypertension.
- Subacute bacterial endocarditis.
- Hypersensitivity to bivalirudin or its components.
- **Renal Function:** Severe renal impairment (GFR < 30 mL/min) and dialysis-dependent patients [Australian].
- Acute gastric or duodenal ulcer.
- Cerebral haemorrhage.
- Severe cerebro-spinal trauma.
- Diabetic or haemorrhagic retinopathy.
- Proximal use of spinal/epidural anaesthesia.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Haemorrhage:** Patients must be monitored closely for signs of bleeding, especially when combined with other anticoagulants. Unexplained drops in blood pressure or haemoglobin suggest a haemorrhagic event. 
- **Antidote:** There is no known antidote; however, the anticoagulant effect wears off quickly (half-life of 35–40 minutes) [Australian].
- **Acute Stent Thrombosis:** Increased risk has been observed in STEMI patients undergoing primary PCI, typically within the first 4 hours post-procedure. Monitoring in a facility capable of managing ischaemic complications for at least 24 hours is required.
- **Gamma Brachytherapy:** Not recommended due to an increased risk of thrombus formation, including fatal outcomes [Australian].
- **Cardiac Surgery:** Avoid techniques that allow stagnant blood in grafts or bypass circuits, as local bivalirudin levels may decrease due to protease metabolism, leading to clot formation.
- **Immunogenicity:** Allergic reactions (urticaria, anaphylaxis) have been reported uncommonly. Caution is advised in patients previously treated with lepirudin who developed antibodies.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Bleeding Frequency
Bleeding is the most common adverse effect. In clinical trials (ACUITY and REPLACE-2), minor bleeding was very common (≥10%) and major bleeding was common (1–10%) [Australian].

### Common Adverse Events (≥1%)
| System Organ Class | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Vascular** | Minor bleeding, Major bleeding, Hypotension |
| **Nervous System** | Headache, Insomnia |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea, Vomiting, Abdominal pain |
| **Musculoskeletal** | Back pain, Chest pain |
| **General** | Pyrexia, Injection site pain |
| **Cardiac** | Atrial fibrillation, Angina pectoris, Bradycardia |

### Serious Adverse Effects (Post-Marketing)
- Anaphylactic shock (fatal outcomes reported).
- Intracranial, pulmonary, and fatal haemorrhage.
- Cardiac tamponade, ventricular fibrillation, and myocardial infarction.
- Compartment syndrome and catheter thrombosis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Heparin:** Bivalirudin can be started 30 minutes after stopping unfractionated heparin or 8 hours after stopping low molecular weight heparin (LMWH) [Australian].
- **Anticoagulants/Platelet Inhibitors:** Combined use (e.g., warfarin, thrombolytics) increases bleeding risk. 
- **Laboratory Tests (INR):** Bivalirudin increases INR; INR monitoring may not be reliable for warfarin dosing until bivalirudin is discontinued and levels return to baseline.
- **Physical Incompatibilities:** **Do not** administer in the same IV line with: alteplase, amiodarone HCl, amphotericin B, chlorpromazine HCl, diazepam, prochlorperazine edisylate, reteplase, streptokinase, or vancomycin HCl.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category C. Anticoagulants can cause placental haemorrhage. Bivalirudin should only be used in pregnancy if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if bivalirudin is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution when administering to nursing women.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies (rats) showed no effect on fertility at clinical doses, but decreased implantations occurred at maternally toxic doses (3x MRCD).

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Higher incidence of bleeding events. Monitor renal function closely and adjust dose based on GFR.
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **ACS:** No dose adjustment required.
    - **PCI:** Dose reduction may be necessary. Monitor Activated Clotting Time (ACT).
    - **Contraindication:** GFR < 30 mL/min or dialysis.
    - **GFR Calculation:** $\text{GFR (mL/sec)} = \frac{(140 - \text{age}) \times \text{weight (kg)}}{48,869 \times \text{serum creatinine (mmol/L)}}$ (Multiply by 0.85 for females).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Metabolism is limited; no dose adjustment required.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Direct, reversible inhibition of thrombin (Factor IIa). It binds to both the catalytic site and the anion-binding exosite of free and clot-bound thrombin.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Prolongs ACT, aPTT, TT, and PT in a concentration-dependent manner. Immediate onset; coagulation times return to baseline approx. 2 hours after cessation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Rapidly distributes to plasma and interstitial fluid; does not bind to plasma proteins.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily by plasma proteases.
    - **Elimination:** Mean half-life is 25 minutes (normal renal function). Approx. 20% cleared renally; 25% cleared via haemodialysis.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ACUITY (ACS):** Bivalirudin monotherapy was superior to heparin/enoxaparin + GPIIb/IIIa inhibitor regarding major bleeding and net clinical outcomes at 30 days.
- **REPLACE-2 (PCI):** Bivalirudin with provisional GPIIb/IIIa inhibitor was non-inferior to heparin + planned GPIIb/IIIa inhibitor.
- **HORIZONS (STEMI):** In primary PCI, bivalirudin significantly reduced major bleeding and all-cause mortality compared to heparin + GPIIb/IIIa inhibitor at 30 days, despite a higher rate of early (<24h) stent thrombosis.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store below 25°C in the original container.
- **Reconstituted/Diluted Solution:** Stable at room temperature (25°C) for up to 24 hours. May be stored refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C for up to 24 hours. Do not freeze.
