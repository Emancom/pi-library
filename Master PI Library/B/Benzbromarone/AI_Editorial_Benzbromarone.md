# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the Product Information for levocarnitine injection. It is designed as a structured clinical reference for healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial is a clinical synthesis and does not replace the official Product Information approved by regulatory authorities in specific jurisdictions. Clinicians should consult the local approved PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN). 
- **Chemical Class:** A naturally occurring carrier molecule (quaternary ammonium compound) essential for fatty acid metabolism.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from congenital metabolic disorders [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
  - The standard recommended dose is 50 mg/kg, administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
  - Severe metabolic crisis may require a loading dose followed by equivalent doses every 3 or 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours) [US].
  - Subsequent daily maintenance doses are generally 50 mg/kg, though titration up to 300 mg/kg may be required based on clinical response [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:**
  - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
  - **Dose Adjustments:** Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma carnitine concentrations [US].

### Administration and Monitoring
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Regular weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations [US].
- **Target Range:** Plasma free carnitine levels should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Inspection:** Parenteral products must be visually inspected for discoloration or particulate matter prior to use [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known according to available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. Treatment must be discontinued immediately upon signs of severe hypersensitivity [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients with or without a history of seizure activity. In those with pre-existing epilepsy, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** While the injection is used in ESRD, the safety of **oral** levocarnitine in patients with severe renal impairment has not been established; chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** In patients receiving concurrent warfarin therapy, International Normalised Ratio (INR) levels must be monitored regularly, as increases have been reported following the initiation of levocarnitine or dose adjustments [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects
- **General:** Transient nausea and vomiting are common. Body odor and gastritis are reported less frequently [US].
- **Dialysis-Specific Events (≥5% frequency):** In clinical trials of ESRD patients, the following were reported regardless of causality [US]:
  - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%), palpitations (3–8%).
  - **Neurological:** Headache (3–37%), dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%).
  - **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (9–35%), abdominal pain (5–21%), vomiting (9–21%).
  - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%).
  - **Other:** Injection site reactions (27–38%), chest pain (6–15%), fever (5–12%).

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Anaphylaxis and severe respiratory distress (bronchospasm/laryngeal edema) [US].
- Seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Concurrent use may lead to an increased INR and risk of bleeding. Clinical monitoring of anticoagulation status is essential during levocarnitine therapy [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine is not protein-bound, suggesting a low potential for interactions involving protein-binding displacement [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **Category:** Not specified by letter category in source, but reproductive studies in animals (rats/rabbits) at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm [US]. 
  - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly required, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. The benefits of maternal therapy must be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No mutagenic effects were observed in standard tests; however, long-term carcinogenic potential has not been evaluated in animals [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders apply to paediatric patients. Specific safety and efficacy profiles for other indications in children are not detailed in the source [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trial data did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65 and over to determine if they respond differently than younger subjects [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD patients on dialysis. The drug is easily removed by hemodialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US]. It also aids in removing toxic acyl-CoA esters by forming acylcarnitines, which are then excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound; volume of distribution is not specified [US].
  - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in faeces) [US].
  - **Elimination:** Following IV administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly showed that trough levocarnitine levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L by week 8 [US].
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses in dialysis patients confirmed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat. Use only clear, colorless solutions [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8 mg/mL [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservative [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources (refer to local jurisdiction) [US].
