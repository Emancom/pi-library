# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information documents contributed to this editorial: United States (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview designed for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises available regulatory data into a single clinical reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [United States].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [United States].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Metabolic Errors:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [United States].
- **Carnitine Deficiency in End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing maintenance dialysis [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [United States].
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 to 4 hours; never less frequently than every 6 hours [United States].
- **Dose Range:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [United States].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [United States].
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow (2 to 3 minute) bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [United States].
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [United States].

### General Administration Notes
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Monitor weekly and monthly, including blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [United States].
- **Inspection:** Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration before use. Discard any unused portion as the product contains no preservatives [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and do not re-administer without careful risk-benefit assessment and close monitoring [United States].
- **Seizure Risk:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [United States].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** While this editorial focuses on the injectable form, clinicians should note that chronic high-dose *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [United States].
- **Monitoring:** Trough carnitine levels should be monitored to ensure they remain within the normal range (40–50 µmol/L) [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (ESRD Clinical Trials)
The following events occurred at a frequency of ≥5% in ESRD patients, regardless of causality [United States]:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), chest pain (12%), flu syndrome (25%), infection (15%), headache (22%), abdominal pain (9%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%).
- **Neurological:** Dizziness (13%), paresthesia (5%).

### Other Reported Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis and transient nausea/vomiting have been observed.
- **Body Odor:** A "fishy" body odor may occur during therapy [United States].
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (see Section 5) [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels at treatment initiation and following any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [United States: Category not explicitly assigned in modern format, but reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm]. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the maternal benefit [United States].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders apply to paediatric patients; safety and efficacy have been established for this population [United States].
- **Geriatric Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily used in ESRD patients on dialysis. Dose reductions to 5 mg/kg may be required based on trough plasma concentrations [United States].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [United States].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** It promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin; mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [United States].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In clinical trials of ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis, mean pre-dialysis (trough) levocarnitine concentrations increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While levocarnitine levels effectively increase, the impact on specific clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [United States].
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [United States].

- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [United States].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources (refer to local jurisdiction).
