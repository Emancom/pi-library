## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of HIV‑1 infection in adults and paediatric patients weighing at least 25 kg who are:
  - Antiretroviral therapy (ART)-naïve; or
  - Virologically suppressed (HIV‑1 RNA < 50 copies/mL) on a stable antiretroviral regimen at the start of therapy, with no history of treatment failure and no known substitutions associated with resistance to bictegravir or tenofovir.

(Note: No additional regional indication differences are specified in the available sources.)

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult and paediatric ≥25 kg)
- One oral tablet once daily. (Tablet strength composition in sources: bictegravir 50 mg, emtricitabine 200 mg, tenofovir alafenamide 25 mg equivalent.)

### Administration instructions
- May be administered with or without food.
- For patients on chronic haemodialysis: administer the daily dose after completion of haemodialysis on dialysis days.

### Renal/hepatic special-population initiation limits (see Section 10 for details)
- Initiation not recommended in patients with estimated creatinine clearance ≥ 15 and < 30 mL/min, or < 15 mL/min who are not receiving chronic haemodialysis.

### Titration schedules
- Not specified in available sources.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to bictegravir, emtricitabine, tenofovir (tenofovir alafenamide), or any excipients.
- Coadministration with dofetilide (contraindicated due to risk of increased dofetilide concentrations and serious or life‑threatening events).
- Coadministration with rifampicin (contraindicated due to significant reduction in bictegravir exposure, risking loss of therapeutic effect and resistance).

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Patients may continue to develop opportunistic infections and other complications of HIV despite treatment; continue close clinical follow-up by clinicians experienced in HIV care.
- Effective viral suppression reduces but does not eliminate the risk of HIV transmission; inform patients BIC+FTC+TAF is not a cure.

### Hepatitis B virus (HBV) co‑infection
- Discontinuation of therapy may lead to severe acute exacerbations of hepatitis B because of the emtricitabine and tenofovir components. Patients with HIV/HBV co‑infection who stop therapy should be monitored clinically and by laboratory testing for several months; consider initiating or continuing anti‑HBV therapy if appropriate, particularly in patients with advanced liver disease or cirrhosis.

### Use with other antiretroviral products
- Should not be coadministered with products containing any of the same components (bictegravir, emtricitabine, tenofovir alafenamide), nor with products containing lamivudine or tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF). Should not be administered with adefovir dipivoxil.

### Immune reconstitution syndrome
- Immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome (IRIS) has been reported with combination antiretroviral therapy, including emtricitabine. Monitor for inflammatory reactions after initiating therapy, particularly in patients with advanced immunodeficiency.

### Lactic acidosis and severe hepatomegaly with steatosis
- Lactic acidosis and severe hepatomegaly with steatosis, including fatal cases, have been reported with nucleoside analogues; suspend therapy if clinical or laboratory findings suggestive of lactic acidosis or marked hepatotoxicity occur.

### Renal impairment and renal tubular dysfunction
- Postmarketing reports with tenofovir alafenamide–containing products include acute renal failure, proximal renal tubulopathy (PRT), and Fanconi syndrome. Many reports had confounding factors but tenofovir-related renal events remain possible.
- Patients with impaired renal function or those taking concomitant nephrotoxic agents (including NSAIDs) may be at increased risk.
- Before initiation and periodically during therapy, assess:
  - Serum creatinine and estimated creatinine clearance,
  - Urine glucose and urine protein in all patients,
  - Serum phosphorus in patients with chronic kidney disease.
- Discontinue therapy in patients who develop clinically significant declines in renal function or evidence of Fanconi syndrome.

### Hepatic impairment
- No dose adjustment required in mild (Child‑Pugh A) or moderate (Child‑Pugh B) hepatic impairment.
- Safety/PK not studied in severe hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh C); use not recommended unless benefits outweigh risks.

### Antacid and polyvalent cation interaction
- Under fasted conditions, simultaneous administration with magnesium/aluminium‑containing antacids substantially decreases bictegravir exposure; avoid simultaneous dosing.
  - If administered fasted: give antacid at least 2 hours before or 6 hours after bictegravir + emtricitabine + tenofovir.
  - If taken with food, simultaneous administration with magnesium/aluminium‑containing antacids is acceptable.
- Calcium, iron, and other polyvalent cations can reduce bictegravir exposure under fasted conditions; effects are attenuated with food. See Section 8 for interaction magnitudes and dosing guidance.

### Effects on laboratory tests
- Not specified in available sources.

### Monitoring requirements (summary)
- Baseline and periodic: renal function (serum creatinine, estimated creatinine clearance), urine protein and glucose; serum phosphorus in CKD; clinical and laboratory monitoring for hepatitis B if HBV co‑infection or if therapy is discontinued; monitor for signs of IRIS and lactic acidosis/hepatotoxicity.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic mechanisms (summary)
- Bictegravir:
  - In vitro inhibits OCT2 and MATE1; clinically, coadministration with metformin did not result in a clinically significant increase in metformin exposure in interaction studies.
  - Bictegravir is a substrate of CYP3A and UGT1A1; primarily eliminated by hepatic metabolism via CYP3A and UGT1A1.
  - Bictegravir is a substrate of P‑gp and BCRP in vitro, but these transporters are not clinically significant determinants of bictegravir disposition based on interaction studies.
  - Bictegravir is not an inhibitor or inducer of CYP3A in vivo.
- Emtricitabine:
  - Low potential for CYP‑mediated interactions.
  - Eliminated primarily by the kidney via glomerular filtration and active tubular secretion; drugs that reduce renal function or compete for tubular secretion may increase emtricitabine concentrations.
- Tenofovir alafenamide (TAF):
  - Substrate of P‑gp and BCRP; drugs that strongly affect these transporters may change TAF absorption.
  - TAF is not an inhibitor or inducer of CYP3A in vivo.

### Clinically significant interactions and management
- Contraindicated combinations:
  - Dofetilide: coadministration contraindicated (risk of increased dofetilide concentrations).
  - Rifampicin: coadministration contraindicated (substantially decreases bictegravir exposures; risk of loss of efficacy/resistance).
- Agents that decrease bictegravir and/or TAF exposure (avoid or use alternatives where indicated):
  - Potent inducers of both CYP3A and UGT1A1 (e.g., rifampicin, rifapentine, rifabutin): may significantly decrease bictegravir and TAF exposures; rifampicin is contraindicated; others are expected to decrease exposures and risk loss of efficacy.
  - Anticonvulsants that induce metabolizing enzymes (carbamazepine, oxcarbazepine, phenobarbital, phenytoin): decrease bictegravir and TAF concentrations; consider alternative anticonvulsants.
  - Carbamazepine: shown to reduce TAF exposure (~0.46 AUC for TAF in an interaction study).
- Agents that increase bictegravir exposure:
  - Potent inhibitors of both CYP3A and UGT1A1 (e.g., atazanavir ± cobicistat) may significantly increase bictegravir exposure; coadministration not recommended.
  - Cobicistat‑boosted protease inhibitors (e.g., darunavir + cobicistat) increased bictegravir exposure in studies.
- Polyvalent cations / antacids:
  - Maximum‑strength aluminium/magnesium antacid given simultaneously in fasted state markedly reduces bictegravir exposure (BIC AUC and Cmax reductions); dosing separation recommended (≥2 hours before or 6 hours after) when fasted. If bictegravir + emtricitabine + tenofovir is taken with food, simultaneous administration with magnesium/aluminium antacids is acceptable.
  - Calcium carbonate, ferrous fumarate and other cations reduce bictegravir exposure when given fasted; food attenuates the effect.
- Interactions with direct-acting antivirals for HCV:
  - Certain HCV regimens (e.g., ledipasvir/sofosbuvir; sofosbuvir/velpatasvir/voxilaprevir) have shown variable effects on bictegravir and TAF exposures; consult product‑specific guidance and consider monitoring.
- Metformin:
  - Bictegravir inhibits OCT2/MATE1 in vitro; coadministration did not result in a clinically significant metformin exposure increase in interaction studies, but modest increases were observed (study results showed increases in metformin AUC up to ~1.39). Monitor for metformin adverse effects and consider risk/benefit.
- Hormonal contraceptives:
  - Small increases in ethinyl estradiol and progestin exposures were observed; interaction magnitude not clinically significant in studies reported.
- Other interactions:
  - TAF exposure increased with some hepatically active antivirals (e.g., studies showed increases with sofosbuvir/velpatasvir/voxilaprevir combinations).
  - Drugs that inhibit P‑gp and BCRP may increase TAF absorption and exposure.
  - Drugs that decrease renal function can increase emtricitabine exposure.

### Interaction studies and magnitude (selected study findings)
- Bictegravir exposure decreased markedly with rifampicin (AUC ~25% of control in a single‑dose study) and with rifabutin (moderate decrease).
- Bictegravir exposure increased substantially with atazanavir (with or without cobicistat) and with darunavir + cobicistat in interaction studies.
- Polyvalent antacids given simultaneously in fasted state reduced bictegravir AUC to ~21% and Cmax to ~20% in one study; food ameliorates this effect.
- TAF exposures decreased with carbamazepine (TAF AUC ~46% of control) and increased with some HCV antiviral regimens (TAF AUC increases reported with sofosbuvir/velpatasvir/voxilaprevir).

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Approved for paediatric patients weighing at least 25 kg (same one tablet once daily dose). No data to recommend dosing for patients weighing < 25 kg.

### Elderly use
- No dose adjustment required for elderly patients. Clinical trial exposure included 111 patients aged 65 years and over; no differences in safety or efficacy observed compared with younger patients.

### Renal impairment
- No dose adjustment required in adult patients with estimated creatinine clearance ≥ 30 mL/min.
- Use in end‑stage renal disease (ESRD) on chronic haemodialysis: studies of emtricitabine + tenofovir alafenamide (with elvitegravir + cobicistat) reported similar safety in patients on chronic haemodialysis; a small group switched to bictegravir + emtricitabine + tenofovir showed no additional adverse reactions in the study population. For patients on haemodialysis, administer dose after dialysis on dialysis days.
- Initiation not recommended in patients with estimated creatinine clearance ≥ 15 and < 30 mL/min, or in patients with < 15 mL/min who are not receiving chronic haemodialysis, since safety has not been established in these ranges.
- No data to make dose recommendations in paediatric patients with renal impairment.

### Hepatic impairment
- No dose adjustment required for mild (Child‑Pugh A) or moderate (Child‑Pugh B) hepatic impairment.
- Not studied in severe hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh C); safety not established.

### Pharmacokinetics (selected data from interaction and PK descriptions)
- Bictegravir:
  - Primarily metabolised via hepatic CYP3A and UGT1A1 pathways.
  - In vitro substrate of P‑gp and BCRP; transporter contribution to in vivo disposition appears limited based on interaction data.
  - In vitro inhibitor of OCT2 and MATE1; clinical metformin interaction not judged clinically significant in studied conditions.

  - Eliminated primarily by the kidneys through glomerular filtration and active tubular secretion; low potential for CYP‑mediated interactions.
- Tenofovir alafenamide (TAF):
  - Substrate of P‑gp and BCRP; absorption altered by strong modulators of these transporters.
  - Not an inhibitor or inducer of CYP3A in vivo.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Open-label study GS‑US‑292‑0112 (Study 0112): Evaluated emtricitabine + tenofovir alafenamide (components of the combination) in combination with elvitegravir + cobicistat in 248 HIV‑1 infected adults with mild to moderate renal impairment (eGFR 30–69 mL/min); included treatment‑naïve and virologically suppressed patients. Safety profile of FTC + TAF was similar to that observed in patients with normal renal function through 144 weeks.
- Open‑label study GS‑US‑292‑1825 (Study 1825): Evaluated FTC + TAF with elvitegravir + cobicistat in 55 virologically suppressed HIV‑1 infected patients with ESRD on chronic haemodialysis for 96 weeks. In an extension, 10 patients switched to bictegravir + emtricitabine + tenofovir for 48 weeks; safety profile in haemodialysis patients was similar to patients with normal renal function in the study.
- Clinical trials included 111 patients aged 65 years or older who received bictegravir + emtricitabine + tenofovir; no differences in safety or efficacy were observed compared with younger patients.
- Numerous dedicated drug–drug interaction studies were performed in healthy volunteers to characterise effects of concomitant medications on bictegravir, emtricitabine, and tenofovir alafenamide exposures and vice versa (selected interaction data are summarised in Sections 8 and 6).
