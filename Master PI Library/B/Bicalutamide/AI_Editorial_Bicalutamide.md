# Editorial Product Information: Bicalutamide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: APO-Bicalutamide 50 mg (Bicalutamide) Tablets.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a concise, professional reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical guide; it is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Bicalutamide (a non-steroidal anti-androgen).
- **Chemical Class:** Propionanilide derivative.
- **Brand Names:** APO-Bicalutamide [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L02BB03.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 50 mg film-coated tablets.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Advanced Prostate Cancer:** Treatment of advanced prostate cancer in combination with luteinising hormone-releasing hormone (LHRH) agonist therapy [Australian].
- **Prevention of Disease Flare:** Management and prevention of the initial disease flare-up associated with the introduction of LHRH agonist therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Adult Dosing (including elderly):** One 50 mg tablet administered once daily [Australian].
- **Timing:** Therapy should be initiated concurrently with the start of LHRH agonist treatment [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets are for oral use and may be taken with or without food [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Females:** Bicalutamide is contraindicated in women [Australian].
- **Paediatrics:** Contraindicated for use in children [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to the active ingredient or any excipient (e.g., lactose) [Australian].
- **Drug-Drug Combinations:** Co-administration with terfenadine, astemizole, or cisapride is strictly contraindicated [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Bicalutamide is extensively metabolised in the liver. Severe liver injury, including rare cases of death or hospitalisation, has been reported. 
    - *Monitoring:* Periodic liver function tests (LFTs) should be conducted, particularly during the first 6 months of therapy.
    - *Action:* Discontinue immediately if jaundice develops or if alanine aminotransferase (ALT) levels exceed 2x the upper limit of normal (ULN) [Australian].
- **QT/QTc Interval Prolongation:** Androgen deprivation therapy may prolong the QT interval. Caution is advised in patients with a history of heart failure, electrolyte abnormalities, or those taking medications known to prolong the QT interval [Australian].
- **Hyperglycaemia:** Reduced glucose tolerance and loss of glycaemic control in diabetic patients have been observed in males receiving LHRH agonists. Monitoring of blood glucose is recommended [Australian].
- **Metastatic Monotherapy:** Bicalutamide should **not** be used as monotherapy for metastatic prostate cancer, as it has been associated with reduced survival compared to surgical castration [Australian].
- **Haemostatic Monitoring:** Potentiation of coumarin anticoagulants (e.g., warfarin) may occur; close monitoring of PT/INR is required [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Coumarin Anticoagulants (e.g., Warfarin):** Bicalutamide can displace warfarin from protein binding sites. PT/INR must be closely monitored, and anticoagulant doses adjusted accordingly [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Substrates:** Bicalutamide inhibits CYP3A4. Caution and potential dose monitoring are required for drugs with a narrow therapeutic index metabolised by this enzyme (e.g., cyclosporin, calcium channel blockers) [Australian].
- **Oxidation Inhibitors:** Theoretical interactions with cimetidine and ketoconazole may increase bicalutamide plasma concentrations [Australian].
- **Contraindicated Interactions:** Terfenadine, astemizole, and cisapride (due to potential for serious cardiac events) [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category D. Bicalutamide may cause foetal harm when administered to pregnant women. It is contraindicated in females [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated in breastfeeding mothers [Australian].
- **Fertility:** May lead to inhibition of spermatogenesis and morphological changes in spermatozoa. Male patients with female partners of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during and for 130 days after stopping therapy [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established; contraindicated in children [Australian].
- **Elderly:** No dosage adjustment is required for the elderly [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is necessary [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - *Mild:* No adjustment needed.
    - *Moderate to Severe:* Use with caution. Bicalutamide may accumulate due to slowed elimination; a lower or less frequent dose may be considered [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bicalutamide is a competitive androgen receptor antagonist. It binds to the receptors in target tissues without activating gene expression, thereby inhibiting the stimulus for growth in androgen-dependent prostatic tumours [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - *Absorption:* Well absorbed orally; unaffected by food.
    - *Distribution:* Highly protein-bound (96% for racemate).
    - *Metabolism:* Extensively metabolised in the liver via oxidation and glucuronidation. The (R)-enantiomer is the active form.
    - *Elimination:* Metabolites are excreted via kidneys and bile. The (R)-enantiomer has a long plasma elimination half-life of approximately one week, leading to a 10-fold accumulation during daily dosing [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Combination Therapy Study:** A large multicentre trial of 813 patients compared bicalutamide (50 mg/day) plus LHRH agonist to flutamide (250 mg TID) plus LHRH agonist. Bicalutamide therapy demonstrated a statistically significant improvement in time to treatment failure (p=0.005) compared to the flutamide combination [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Moisture:** Protect from moisture [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only.
