# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **United States (FDA)** for levocarnitine injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation).
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically focused overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local regulatory requirements for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP); a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acids.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative [(R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt].
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Clear, colorless sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It may be administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).

### Administration Instructions
- **ESRD Administration:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often given, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours (q3h or q4h) and never less frequently than every 6 hours (q6h) [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

### Monitoring and Titration
- **Plasma Monitoring:** Trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels should be obtained before initiating therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended.
- **Target Range:** Maintain plasma free carnitine concentrations between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Titration:** In ESRD patients, downward dose adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None Known:** There are currently no known contraindications for levocarnitine injection [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These can occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** In patients with severely compromised renal function, chronic administration of high oral doses may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology (e.g., ESRD). The following frequencies are based on clinical trials in dialysis patients [US]:

| Frequency | System Organ Class | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | General | Injection site reaction (33–59%), Pain (30–49%), Headache (22%), Fever (12%) |
| | Cardiovascular | Hypertension (18–21%), Hypotension (14–19%) |
| | Gastrointestinal | Diarrhea (16–35%), Vomiting (15–21%), Nausea (8–12%) |
| | Respiratory | Pharyngitis (15–33%), Cough (10–18%), Dyspnea (3–19%) |
| | Metabolic | Hypervolemia (3–17%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Cardiovascular | Tachycardia (5–9%), Chest pain (6–15%), Palpitations (3–8%) |

| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Neurological | Seizures [US Postmarketing] |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and other severe hypersensitivity reactions [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not assigned under current labeling, but reproductive data provided]. Animal studies (rats/rabbits) using up to 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies in nursing mothers are unavailable. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after administration. **Management:** Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of supplementation for the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See Section 3 (Dosage and Administration) for metabolic disorder dosing, which applies to paediatric patients.
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65 and over to determine if they respond differently than younger subjects [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety of oral levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. For IV use in ESRD, see specific dosing in Section 3.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x weekly), mean pre-dialysis concentrations rose from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses in ESRD patients showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Conditions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only. NDC 36000-368-10 (carton of 10 vials) [US].
