# Editorial Product Information: Budesonide and Formoterol Fumarate Dihydrate

## Editorial Note
- **Jurisdictions Contributed:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data from the provided Australian Product Information for the fixed-dose combination of budesonide and formoterol.
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial reference and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within specific legal jurisdictions.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Budesonide (glucocorticosteroid) and Formoterol fumarate dihydrate (Long-Acting Beta-2 Agonist; LABA).
- **Brand Name:** **BiResp Spiromax** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** R03AK07.
- **Dosage Form and Strengths:** Multi-dose, inspiratory flow-driven dry powder inhaler (Spiromax). Available in two metered strengths [Australian]:
    - 200/6 µg (Delivering 160 µg budesonide / 4.5 µg formoterol to the patient).
    - 400/12 µg (Delivering 320 µg budesonide / 9 µg formoterol to the patient).
- **Route of Administration:** Oral inhalation.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Teva Pharma Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Asthma:** Indicated for the treatment of asthma in adults (18 years and older) to achieve control, including symptom relief and reduction of exacerbation risk [Australian].
- **Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD):** Indicated for symptomatic treatment in adults with moderate to severe COPD (FEV1 ≤50% predicted normal) who experience frequent symptoms despite regular long-acting bronchodilator therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Asthma (Adults ≥18 years)
Three treatment approaches are recognised [Australian]:
1.  **Anti-inflammatory Reliever Therapy:** For mild disease. Patients take **BiResp Spiromax** 200/6 as needed for symptom relief and to prevent allergen- or exercise-induced bronchoconstriction.
2.  **Anti-inflammatory Reliever plus Maintenance Therapy:** Patients take a daily maintenance dose plus as-needed inhalations for relief. 
    - *Note:* The 400/12 strength is **not** approved for this regimen [Australian].
3.  **Fixed-dose Maintenance Therapy:** Patients take a fixed daily dose with a separate short-acting bronchodilator for rescue use.

**Dosing Specifics:**
- **Reliever Dosing (200/6 strength):** 1 inhalation as needed. If symptoms persist after a few minutes, a second inhalation may be taken. Max 6 inhalations per single occasion. Total daily dose >8 is not normally needed, though up to 12 can be used temporarily [Australian].
- **Maintenance Dosing (200/6):** 1-2 inhalations twice daily.
- **Maintenance Dosing (400/12):** 2 inhalations twice daily (Max 1600/48 µg per day).

### COPD (Adults)
- **200/6 strength:** 2 inhalations twice daily (Max 800/24 µg per day) [Australian].
- **400/12 strength:** 1 inhalation twice daily (Max 800/24 µg per day) [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Patients should not shake the inhaler before use.
- The device is breath-actuated; patients must inhale forcefully and deeply.
- **Mouth Rinsing:** Patients must rinse their mouth with water after inhalation to minimize the risk of oropharyngeal candidiasis.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to budesonide, formoterol, or the excipient lactose (which may contain milk protein residue) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Acute Deterioration:** Not for initiation of therapy in acute asthma attacks or for the initiation of bronchodilator therapy in COPD. Sudden worsening requires urgent medical assessment.
- **Systemic Corticosteroid Effects:** Potential effects include HPA axis suppression, reduction in bone mineral density, cataracts, glaucoma, and very rare psychiatric symptoms (e.g., depression, aggression in children).
- **Pneumonia in COPD:** An increased incidence of pneumonia has been observed in COPD patients receiving inhaled corticosteroids (ICS). Physicians should remain vigilant as clinical features overlap with COPD exacerbations [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** Formoterol (a beta-2 agonist) may induce QTc interval prolongation, palpitations, and tachycardia. Use with caution in patients with severe hypertension, aneurysms, or ischaemic heart disease.
- **Infection/Tuberculosis:** Use with caution in patients with active or quiescent pulmonary tuberculosis or fungal/viral respiratory infections.
- **Metabolic Effects:** Potential for hypokalaemia (enhanced by hypoxia in acute attacks) and hyperglycaemia (initial blood glucose monitoring recommended for diabetic patients).

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are typical of beta-agonist and corticosteroid therapies.
- **Common (1–10%):** Palpitations, oropharyngeal candidiasis, headache, tremor, mild throat irritation, coughing, and hoarseness.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Tachycardia, blurred vision, nausea, diarrhoea, weight gain, muscle cramps, dizziness, and anxiety.
- **Rare (0.01–0.1%):** Immediate/delayed hypersensitivity (e.g., urticaria, angioedema), cardiac arrhythmias (atrial fibrillation), and hypokalaemia.
- **Very Rare (<0.01%):** Angina pectoris, Cushing’s syndrome, growth retardation, and paradoxical bronchospasm.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic (CYP3A4):** Potent inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, ritonavir, cobicistat) can significantly increase systemic budesonide exposure. Avoid combination unless benefits outweigh risks; if used, monitor for systemic effects.
- **Beta-Blockers:** Beta-adrenergic blockers (including eye drops) can inhibit the effect of formoterol and may induce bronchospasm. Avoid use in asthma patients unless essential.
- **Other Sympathomimetics:** Cumulative effects with other beta-adrenergic stimulants.
- **QT-Prolonging Drugs:** Caution with MAOIs, tricyclic antidepressants, quinidine, and phenothiazines due to increased risk of ventricular arrhythmias.
- **Digitalis Glycosides:** Hypokalaemia may increase digitalis toxicity.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B3]. No clinical data available for exposed pregnancies. Budesonide has shown no adverse effects on the fetus in large studies, but formoterol has shown reproductive toxicity in high-dose animal studies. Use only if benefits outweigh risks; the lowest effective dose should be used.
- **Breastfeeding:** Budesonide is excreted in breast milk in small amounts. It is unknown if formoterol is excreted in human milk. Use only after weighing maternal benefit against infant risk.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** **BiResp Spiromax** is NOT indicated for children and adolescents under 18 years [Australian].
- **Elderly:** No special dosing requirements [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No data available for the combination product.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No data available. However, since both actives are eliminated via hepatic metabolism, increased exposure is expected in patients with severe liver disease.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Budesonide:** Potent glucocorticoid with local anti-inflammatory action in the airways.
    - **Formoterol:** Potent selective beta-2 agonist that induces rapid (within 1-3 minutes) and long-acting (at least 12 hours) bronchial smooth muscle relaxation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Bioavailability is approximately 40% for budesonide and 46% for formoterol (higher lung deposition) [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Budesonide undergoes extensive (~90%) first-pass hepatic metabolism via CYP3A4. Formoterol is metabolised via conjugation to inactive glucuronides.
    - **Elimination:** Budesonide metabolites are excreted in urine. Formoterol has a terminal half-life of ~17 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **SYGMA 1 & 2:** Demonstrated that budesonide/formoterol used as needed was superior to SABA alone in reducing severe exacerbations (64% reduction) and was non-inferior to maintenance budesonide in reducing exacerbation rates in mild asthma [Australian].
- **COPD Studies (629 & 670):** Showed significant improvement in FEV1 and reduction in severe exacerbation frequency compared to placebo or formoterol monotherapy.
- **Spiromax Device:** Studies confirmed that patients with asthma and COPD can generate sufficient inspiratory flow (>60 L/min) to deliver the therapeutic dose.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Handling:** Replace cap firmly after each use.
- **Shelf Life:** Use within 6 months of removing the inhaler from the foil wrapping [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4) [Australian].
