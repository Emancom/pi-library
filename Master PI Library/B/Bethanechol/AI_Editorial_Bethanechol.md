# Editorial Product Information: Bethanechol

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesises information from the following jurisdictional Product Information (PI) documents:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: Urocarb (bethanechol chloride) Tablets.
- **Purpose**: This is a harmonised editorial overview designed for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer**: This document is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, legally approved Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Bethanechol chloride.
- **Chemical Class**: Synthetic ester; quaternary ammonium parasympathomimetic agent.
- **Brand Names**: **Urocarb** [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: N07AB02 (Not explicitly stated in source; provided for clinical reference).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**: 
    - **Tablets**: 10 mg (round, light pink, scored) [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration**: Oral or sublingual [Australian].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Mayne Pharma International Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Urinary Retention**: Treatment of acute post-operative and postpartum non-obstructive urinary retention [Australian].
- **Neurogenic Atony**: Management of neurogenic atony of the urinary bladder with retention [Australian].

*Editorial Note: Bethanechol does not exhibit clinically significant nicotinic stimulant activity at therapeutic doses [Australian].*

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Standard Regimen**: 10 mg to 30 mg administered three to four times daily. This range typically meets most clinical requirements [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Route**: May be administered orally or sublingually [Australian].
- **Timing**: It is preferable to administer the drug on an empty stomach. Administration shortly after food intake may induce nausea and vomiting [Australian].

### Clinical Response Timing
- **Onset of Action**: Effects may appear within 30 minutes, but typically occur within 60 to 90 minutes [Australian].
- **Duration**: Effects generally persist for approximately one hour [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
Bethanechol is contraindicated in patients with the following conditions:
- **Hypersensitivity**: Known allergy to bethanechol chloride or any tablet excipients (e.g., lactose, wheat starch) [Australian].
- **Respiratory**: Latent or active bronchial asthma, due to the risk of precipitated asthmatic attacks or bronchospasm [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular**: Pronounced bradycardia; vasomotor instability; hypotension; or coronary artery disease/occlusion (as the drug may reduce coronary blood flow) [Australian].
- **Endocrine**: Hyperthyroidism (increased risk of atrial fibrillation) [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal**: Peptic ulcer (risk of symptom aggravation via increased acid secretion and motility); recent gastrointestinal surgery (anastomosis or resection); or gastrointestinal obstruction [Australian].
- **Urinary**: Urinary tract obstruction [Australian].
- **Neurological**: Epilepsy or Parkinsonism [Australian].
- **Integrity of Tissues**: Conditions where the strength or integrity of the bladder or gastrointestinal wall is questionable (e.g., acute inflammation) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings**: None listed in available sources.
- **Bacteriuria**: In cases of urinary retention, if the bladder contracts against a non-relaxed sphincter, urine may be forced into the kidney pelvis. If bacteriuria is present, this carries a risk of reflux infection [Australian].
- **Postural Hypotension**: Patients should be cautioned regarding the risk of orthostatic hypotension and advised to move slowly when transitioning from a sitting or lying position [Australian].
- **General Monitoring**: Clinical monitoring for cholinergic overstimulation is advised.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are reported as rare following oral administration, though frequency increases with higher dosages [Australian].

| System Organ Class | Adverse Reactions |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Abdominal cramps/discomfort, colicky pain, nausea, belching, diarrhoea, borborygmi, salivation. |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypotension (fall in blood pressure) with reflex tachycardia, vasomotor response. |
| **Respiratory** | Bronchial constriction, asthmatic attacks. |
| **Nervous System** | Headache; Seizures (causal relationship not established). |
| **Skin/Appendages** | Flushing (feeling of warmth/heat about the face), sweating. |
| **Special Senses** | Lacrimation (tearing), miosis (pupillary constriction). |
| **Renal** | Urinary urgency. |
| **General** | Malaise. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Cholinergic Agents**: Concurrent use with other cholinergics or cholinesterase inhibitors may result in additive effects and increased toxicity [Australian].
- **Sympathomimetics**: Bethanechol may offset the therapeutic effects of sympathomimetic drugs [Australian].
- **Anticholinergics**: Drugs such as quinidine or procainamide may oppose the pharmacological actions of bethanechol [Australian].
- **Ganglionic Blocking Agents**: **Critical Precaution.** Concurrent use may result in a severe/critical fall in blood pressure, often preceded by intense abdominal symptoms [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: **Australian Category B2.** Adequate and well-controlled human studies have not been performed, and animal data is unavailable. Due to the drug's potent excitatory effect on smooth muscle, it should be avoided during pregnancy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding**: No data available in the source document regarding excretion in human milk or effects on the infant [Australian].
- **Fertility**: No data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: No data available [Australian].
- **Elderly Use**: No data available [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment**: Not specified in available sources (General note: Choline esters are rapidly eliminated by the kidneys) [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: A quaternary ammonium parasympathomimetic that acts directly on muscarinic receptors. Unlike acetylcholine, it is not inactivated by cholinesterase, resulting in a more prolonged duration of action. It stimulates the detrusor muscle of the bladder and increases tone/peristalsis in the GI tract [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics**: 
    - **Absorption**: Poorly absorbed from the GI tract [Australian]. 
    - **Distribution**: Does not cross the blood-brain barrier at standard doses [Australian].
    - **Metabolism**: Metabolic rate is undetermined, but the drug is resistant to cholinesterase hydrolysis [Australian].
    - **Elimination**: Rapidly eliminated by the kidneys [Australian].
