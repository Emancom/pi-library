# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection, synthesised for healthcare professionals. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of preparation, only the United States (FDA) source documentation was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule involved in fatty acid metabolism.
- **Chemical Name:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors):** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **Carnitine Deficiency in ESRD:** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease (ESRD) undergoing hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion. 
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Administer intravenously.
- For metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 or 4 hours (not less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- Parenteral products should be visually inspected for particulates or discoloration prior to use [US].

### Monitoring and Titration
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Therapy should be guided by weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].
- **ESRD:** Dose adjustments may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations below 40–50 µmol/L. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough levels [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If severe reactions occur, discontinue immediately and do not re-administer without careful risk-benefit assessment [US].
- **Oral Formulation Caution:** Safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been established in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) normally cleared by the kidneys [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Periodic monitoring of plasma carnitine levels and blood chemistry is essential to guide dosing [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic errors.

### Very Common (>10%)
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (21–35%), flu syndrome (15–29%), chest pain (6–15%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%).
- **Nervous/Respiratory:** Dizziness (10–18%), headache (3–37%), pharyngitis (15–27%), cough (10–18%).

### Common (1–10%)
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hyperkalemia (6%), hypervolemia (3–12%).
- **Other:** Abdominal pain, asthenia, fever, infection, tachycardia, dyspepsia, anemia, weight changes, and paresthesia [US].

### Serious and Post-marketing Effects
- **Neurologic:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Body Odor:** Transient reports of body odor have occurred with levocarnitine use [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating treatment or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not categorized by letter]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies in nursing mothers have not been conducted. Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk; studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations following exogenous administration. The risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the maternal benefit [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impairment found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders follows the 50 mg/kg guideline. Safety/efficacy in ESRD pediatric patients is not specifically detailed beyond general dosing recommendations [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specific dosing for ESRD is established (see Section 3). Note the risk of metabolite accumulation with oral (but not IV) forms [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also facilitates the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Levocarnitine alleviates metabolic abnormalities in inborn errors (e.g., glutaric aciduria II, methyl malonic aciduria) by clearing toxic acylCoA compounds [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (n=12) receiving 20 mg/kg post-dialysis, mean pre-dialysis carnitine levels rose from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While the drug effectively increases carnitine concentrations, the impact on specific clinical outcomes in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation is preservative-free [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
