# Unified Product Information: Burosumab (Crysvita)

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** Crysvita  
**Active Ingredient:** Burosumab

Burosumab is a recombinant human monoclonal IgG1 antibody directed against fibroblast growth factor 23 (FGF23). It is produced using recombinant DNA technology in Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) mammalian cell culture. The solution is sterile, preservative-free, and intended for subcutaneous injection.

## 2. Approved Indications
Crysvita is indicated for the treatment of:
*   **X-linked Hypophosphatemia (XLH):** For use in adults and pediatric patients aged 6 months and older [FDA].
*   **Tumor-Induced Osteomalacia (TIO):** For the treatment of FGF23-related hypophosphatemia associated with phosphaturic mesenchymal tumors that cannot be curatively resected or localized. Approved for adults and pediatric patients aged 1 year and older [TGA].

## 3. Contraindications
Burosumab is contraindicated in the following scenarios:
*   **Concurrent Therapy:** In combination with oral phosphate supplements or active vitamin D analogues (e.g., calcitriol).
*   **Baseline Phosphate Levels:** When fasting serum phosphate is already within or above the normal reference range for the patient's age.
*   **Renal Status:** In patients with severe renal impairment or end-stage renal disease (ESRD), as these conditions inherently disrupt mineral metabolism.

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Ectopic Mineralization and Nephrocalcinosis
Excessive serum phosphate levels increase the risk of ectopic mineralization. In XLH patients, nephrocalcinosis has been observed, particularly when burosumab is combined with prohibited phosphate/vitamin D therapy. Monitoring via renal ultrasonography is recommended at the start of treatment, every 6 months for the first year, and annually thereafter [TGA].

### Hyperphosphatemia
Fasting serum phosphate must be monitored regularly. If levels exceed the upper limit of the normal range, dose interruption or reduction is required to mitigate the risk of ectopic mineralization.

### Hypercalcemia and Hyperparathyroidism
Increases in serum calcium and parathyroid hormone (PTH) have been reported. Patients with risk factors such as dehydration or existing hyperparathyroidism should be monitored closely. If moderate to severe hypercalcemia occurs, burosumab should be withheld until levels are managed.

### Injection Site Reactions
Local reactions (e.g., erythema, pruritus, swelling) occur in a significant portion of patients. Therapy should be discontinued if severe local reactions occur.

### Spinal Stenosis
In adult XLH patients, spinal stenosis and cord compression have been reported. While common in the XLH population, clinicians should remain vigilant for new or worsening neurological symptoms.

## 5. Dosage and Administration
### General Requirements
*   **Washout Period:** Discontinue oral phosphate and active vitamin D analogues at least one week prior to initiating burosumab.
*   **Administration:** Subcutaneous injection only, performed by a healthcare professional. Injection sites should be rotated (upper arm, abdomen, buttock, or thigh).
*   **Maximum Volume:** 1.5 mL per injection site. If the total dose exceeds this, split the volume across multiple sites.

### X-linked Hypophosphatemia (XLH)
*   **Pediatric (6 months to <10 kg):** Starting dose is 1 mg/kg every 2 weeks [FDA].
*   **Pediatric ($\ge$ 10 kg):** Starting dose is 0.8 mg/kg every 2 weeks, rounded to the nearest 10 mg (Min: 10 mg; Max: 90 mg).
*   **Adults:** 1 mg/kg every 4 weeks, rounded to the nearest 10 mg (Max: 90 mg).

### Tumor-Induced Osteomalacia (TIO)
*   **Pediatric (1–12 years):** Starting dose of 0.4 mg/kg every 2 weeks (Max: 90 mg) [TGA].
*   **Adolescents (13–17 years):** Starting dose of 0.3 mg/kg every 2 weeks (Max: 180 mg) [TGA].
*   **Adults:** Starting dose is 0.5 mg/kg every 4 weeks [FDA]. *Note: The TGA suggests a more conservative start of 0.3 mg/kg.* Dose may be titrated up to 2.0 mg/kg (Max: 180 mg) administered every 2 weeks if necessary to achieve target phosphate levels.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Pediatric Patients
The most frequent adverse reactions ($>10\%$) include:
*   Pyrexia, injection site reactions, and cough.
*   Vomiting, headache, and pain in extremities.
*   Dental issues, including tooth abscess and dental caries.
*   Decreased Vitamin D levels and rash.

### Adult Patients
Commonly reported reactions include:
*   Back pain, headache, and dizziness.
*   Tooth infections.
*   **Restless Legs Syndrome (RLS):** Approximately 12% of adult XLH patients reported new or worsening RLS.
*   Muscle spasms and constipation.

### Immunogenicity
Anti-drug antibodies (ADA) were detected in less than 10% of patients across clinical trials. The presence of ADAs did not correlate with a loss of efficacy or significant changes in the safety profile.

## 7. Drug Interactions
No formal pharmacokinetic drug interaction studies have been conducted.
*   **Contraindicated:** Oral phosphate and active vitamin D analogues.
*   **Cautionary:** Use of calcimimetics should be approached with caution, as their effect on calcium receptors may interact with the mineral-regulating effects of burosumab. Close monitoring of serum calcium is advised [TGA].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
There are no adequate data on the use of burosumab in pregnant women. Animal studies in cynomolgus monkeys using doses 15 times higher than the human maximum showed increased fetal loss and preterm birth, likely secondary to maternal hyperphosphatemia and placental mineralization. Burosumab is not recommended during pregnancy.

### Breastfeeding
It is unknown if burosumab is excreted in human milk. Because maternal IgG is known to be present in breast milk, the developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the mother’s clinical need for the drug and any potential risks to the infant.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Following subcutaneous injection, peak serum concentrations ($T_{max}$) are reached between 7 and 13 days.
*   **Distribution:** The apparent volume of distribution is approximately 7.35 to 8 liters.
*   **Elimination:** Burosumab exhibits a half-life of approximately 18 to 19 days. Clearance is body-weight dependent.
*   **Metabolism:** As a monoclonal antibody, burosumab is expected to be degraded into small peptides and amino acids via catabolic pathways.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Burosumab binds to and inhibits the biological activity of FGF23. In XLH and TIO, excess FGF23 levels suppress the kidney's ability to reabsorb phosphate and inhibit the production of 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D. By neutralizing FGF23, burosumab restores renal tubular reabsorption of phosphate and increases the serum concentration of active Vitamin D, thereby improving bone mineralization.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store in a refrigerator at $2^\circ\text{C}$ to $8^\circ\text{C}$ ($36^\circ\text{F}$ to $46^\circ\text{F}$).
*   **Protection:** Keep the vial in the original carton to protect from light.
*   **Handling:** Do not freeze. Do not shake the vial before use. Discard any unused portion as the vials are for single use only.
