# Editorial Product Information: Bismuth subcitrate potassium, Metronidazole, and Tetracycline hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of the combination product containing bismuth subcitrate potassium, metronidazole, and tetracycline hydrochloride. It is designed for use by healthcare professionals as a clinically accurate synthesis of available regulatory information.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Prescribing Information required for clinical practice in a specific jurisdiction. Always consult the current local PI when prescribing or dispensing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Bismuth subcitrate potassium, Metronidazole (nitroimidazole antimicrobial), and Tetracycline hydrochloride (tetracycline class antimicrobial).
- **Brand Names:** **PYLERA** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
  - **Oral Capsule:** Each capsule contains 140 mg bismuth subcitrate potassium, 125 mg metronidazole, and 125 mg tetracycline hydrochloride [US]. 
  - *Note: The tetracycline component is encapsulated within a smaller internal capsule to prevent contact with the bismuth component [US].*
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Distributed by H2-Pharma, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Helicobacter pylori Eradication:** Indicated for use in a triple-combination regimen with omeprazole to treat patients with *H. pylori* infection and active or historical (within 5 years) duodenal ulcer disease [US].
- **Antibacterial Stewardship:** Should be reserved for infections proven or strongly suspected to be caused by susceptible bacteria to mitigate the development of drug resistance [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Adult Dosing:** The standard regimen consists of three (3) capsules administered four times daily for a duration of 10 days [US].
- **Co-administration:** Must be taken with omeprazole 20 mg twice daily (after the morning and evening meals) for the 10-day duration [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
  - Capsules should be swallowed whole with a full glass of water (approx. 240 mL) [US].
  - Administration after meals and at bedtime is recommended to reduce the risk of oesophageal irritation and ulceration associated with tetracycline [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Patients should continue the normal schedule until all medication is consumed. Do not double the dose. If more than four doses are missed, the prescriber should be contacted [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy:** Absolutely contraindicated due to risks of fetal harm [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with severe renal impairment [US].
- **Coadministration with Disulfiram:** Contraindicated in patients who have taken disulfiram within the previous two weeks [US].
- **Alcohol Consumption:** Contraindicated during therapy and for at least three days following completion (includes products containing propylene glycol) [US].
- **Cockayne Syndrome:** Contraindicated due to risk of fatal hepatotoxicity associated with metronidazole [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to bismuth subcitrate potassium, metronidazole (or other nitroimidazoles), or tetracycline [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning (Potential Carcinogenicity):** Metronidazole has demonstrated carcinogenic activity in mice and rats. Human relevance is currently unknown [US].
- **Fetal and Maternal Toxicity:** Tetracycline may cause permanent tooth discoloration and inhibit skeletal development if used during pregnancy. High-dose IV tetracycline has been linked to maternal hepatotoxicity and stillbirth [US].
- **Tooth Discoloration:** Use during tooth development (last half of pregnancy through age 8) may cause permanent yellow-gray-brown discoloration and enamel hypoplasia [US].
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Risk of SJS, TEN, DRESS, and AGEP. Discontinue immediately at the first sign of a skin rash [US].
- **Central Nervous System (CNS) Effects:**
  - **Metronidazole:** Risk of encephalopathy, seizures, aseptic meningitis, and peripheral neuropathy [US].
  - **Tetracycline:** Risk of intracranial hypertension (pseudotumor cerebri); monitor overweight women of childbearing age specifically [US].
  - **Bismuth:** Potential for neurotoxicity with excessive doses [US].
- **Photosensitivity:** Tetracycline may cause exaggerated sunburn; patients should avoid sun and UV exposure [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not recommended for patients with severe hepatic impairment [US].
- **Darkening of Stool/Tongue:** Bismuth may cause harmless, temporary blackening of the tongue or stool (not to be confused with melaena) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Anticoagulants:** Both metronidazole and tetracycline can potentiate the effects of warfarin. Monitor INR and prothrombin time closely [US].
- **Lithium:** Metronidazole may increase serum lithium levels, leading to toxicity. Monitor lithium and creatinine levels daily for several days [US].
- **Oral Contraceptives:** Tetracycline may reduce the efficacy of hormonal contraceptives; advise secondary birth control [US].
- **Antacids, Dairy, and Cations:** Absorption of the combination product is significantly reduced by antacids (Al, Ca, Mg), iron, zinc, sodium bicarbonate, and dairy products. Do not take concomitantly [US].
- **Busulfan:** Metronidazole increases plasma busulfan levels. Avoid use or monitor levels closely [US].
- **CYP450 Interactions:** CYP inhibitors (e.g., cimetidine) may prolong metronidazole half-life; CYP inducers (e.g., phenytoin, phenobarbital) may accelerate elimination [US].
- **QT Prolongation:** Caution when used with other drugs known to prolong the QT interval [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Contraindicated]. Exposure during the 2nd and 3rd trimesters is associated with permanent tooth discoloration and impaired bone growth [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Metronidazole and tetracycline are excreted in human milk. Due to potential tumorigenicity risks (from metronidazole), mothers should pump and discard milk during therapy and for 2 days after the final dose [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies show metronidazole may cause reversible infertility in males (decreased spermatogenesis) [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety not established in children. Specifically contraindicated in children under 8 years of age due to tetracycline-induced tooth discoloration and enamel hypoplasia [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No significant differences in response compared to younger patients, but increased monitoring for renal and hepatic function is advised [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Tetracycline may increase BUN (antianabolic effect). Contraindicated in patients with severe renal impairment [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Metronidazole clearance is reduced in liver disease. Use with caution in mild-to-moderate cases; not recommended in severe (Child-Pugh C) impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
  - **Tetracycline:** Inhibits bacterial protein synthesis by binding to the 30S ribosomal subunit [US].
  - **Metronidazole:** Disrupts DNA and inhibits nucleic acid synthesis in anaerobic environments via free radical formation [US].
  - **Bismuth:** Antibacterial action against *H. pylori* is not fully understood, but it exerts local antimicrobial effects [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Metronidazole is well-absorbed (peak 1–2h). Tetracycline absorption is 60–90% but reduced by food/cations. Bismuth absorption is low but increased by omeprazole [US].
  - **Distribution:** Bismuth is >90% protein bound. Metronidazole and tetracycline distribute widely into tissues and cross the placenta [US].
  - **Elimination:** Bismuth has a long half-life (approx. 5 days) and accumulates in tissues. Metronidazole half-life is 8 hours (primarily urinary). Tetracycline is excreted in urine and feces [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **North American Pivotal Study:** An open-label, randomized trial compared **PYLERA** plus omeprazole (OBMT) to a control of omeprazole, amoxicillin, and clarithromycin (OAC). 
- **Outcomes:** The OBMT regimen achieved an *H. pylori* eradication rate of 92.5% (Per Protocol) compared to 85.7% for the OAC control [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at controlled room temperature, 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Packaging:** Supplied in a 10-day Therapy PAK containing 120 capsules (10 blister cards with 12 capsules each) [US].
