# Editorial Product Information: Bevacizumab

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis was developed using official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **bevacizumab** clinical data for use by healthcare professionals. It is designed to consolidate regulatory information into a single, structured reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource only. It is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of this writing, only the Australian PI was provided for synthesis; therefore, regional variations for other jurisdictions (e.g., US, European, Canadian) are noted as "not specified in available sources" unless otherwise known from the provided text.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Bevacizumab (rch), a recombinant humanised monoclonal antibody.
- **Chemical Class:** Antineoplastic agent; Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor (VEGF) inhibitor.
- **Brand Names:** **ABEVMY** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01FG01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Sterile, preservative-free, non-pyrogenic concentrate for intravenous (IV) infusion (25 mg/mL).
    - 100 mg in 4 mL single-dose vial [Australian].
    - 400 mg in 16 mL single-dose vial [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous infusion only. **Do not** administer as an IV push or bolus. Not formulated for intravitreal use [Australian].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Maxx Pharma Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
[Note: Indications listed below are currently Australian-approved; status in other jurisdictions is not specified in available sources.]

- **Metastatic Colorectal Cancer (mCRC):** In combination with fluoropyrimidine-based chemotherapy.
- **Metastatic Breast Cancer (mBC):** First-line treatment in combination with paclitaxel for patients where anthracycline-based therapy is contraindicated.
- **Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC):** First-line treatment of unresectable advanced, metastatic, or recurrent non-squamous NSCLC in combination with carboplatin and paclitaxel.
- **Renal Cell Cancer (RCC):** Treatment of advanced and/or metastatic RCC in combination with interferon alfa-2a.
- **Grade IV Glioma:** Monotherapy for patients with relapse or disease progression following standard therapy, including chemotherapy.
- **Epithelial Ovarian, Fallopian Tube or Primary Peritoneal Cancer:**
    - First-line treatment (advanced FIGO stages IIIB, IIIC, and IV) in combination with carboplatin and paclitaxel.
    - Recurrent platinum-sensitive disease (first recurrence) in combination with carboplatin/paclitaxel or carboplatin/gemcitabine in patients not previously treated with VEGF inhibitors.
    - Recurrent platinum-resistant disease in combination with paclitaxel, topotecan, or pegylated liposomal doxorubicin (limited to patients who received $\le$ 2 prior chemotherapy regimens and no prior anti-angiogenic therapy).
- **Cervical Cancer:** In combination with paclitaxel and cisplatin; or paclitaxel and topotecan in patients where cisplatin is not tolerated or indicated.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Treatment should be continued until progression of the underlying disease or unacceptable toxicity.
- **mCRC:** 5 mg/kg or 7.5 mg/kg every 2 or 3 weeks (first-line); 10 mg/kg or 15 mg/kg every 2 or 3 weeks (second-line).
- **mBC and Grade IV Glioma:** 10 mg/kg every 2 weeks or 15 mg/kg every 3 weeks.
- **NSCLC:** 15 mg/kg every 3 weeks in combination with carboplatin/paclitaxel for up to 6 cycles, followed by bevacizumab monotherapy.
- **RCC:** 10 mg/kg every 2 weeks in combination with interferon alfa-2a.
- **Cervical Cancer:** 15 mg/kg every 3 weeks.
- **Ovarian Cancer:** 10 mg/kg every 2 weeks or 15 mg/kg every 3 weeks depending on the chemotherapy backbone and platinum sensitivity.

### Administration Instructions
- **Preparation:** Aseptic dilution in 0.9% sodium chloride is required. The final concentration should range between 1.4 and 16.5 mg/mL [Australian].
- **Incompatibilities:** Do not mix or administer with dextrose or glucose solutions.
- **Infusion Rate:**
    - Initial dose: Over 90 minutes.
    - Second dose: Over 60 minutes (if the first was well tolerated).
    - Subsequent doses: Over 30 minutes (if the 60-minute infusion was well tolerated).

### Dose Modifications
- **Dose Reduction:** Not recommended for adverse reactions. Therapy should either be discontinued or temporarily suspended as clinically indicated.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to bevacizumab, Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cell products, or other recombinant human or humanised antibodies [Australian].
- Not specified in available sources for other jurisdictions.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Gastrointestinal (GI) Perforation and Fistulae:** Increased risk of serious, sometimes fatal, GI perforation. Permanently discontinue if perforation occurs. Increased risk of GI-vaginal fistulae in cervical cancer patients [Australian].
- **Wound Healing:** May adversely affect wound healing. Do not initiate therapy for at least 28 days following major surgery or until the wound is fully healed. Withhold bevacizumab for elective surgery [Australian].
- **Haemorrhage:** Increased risk of tumour-associated haemorrhage and minor mucocutaneous bleeding (e.g., epistaxis). Serious pulmonary haemorrhage/haemoptysis has been observed in NSCLC patients. Do not treat patients with recent major haemoptysis [Australian].
- **Arterial Thromboembolic Events (ATE):** Increased risk of CVA, TIA, and MI. Permanently discontinue in patients who develop ATE. Risk is higher in patients >65 years or with a history of ATE/diabetes.
- **Hypertension:** Dose-dependent increased incidence. Pre-existing hypertension must be controlled before starting therapy. Monitor blood pressure during treatment.
- **Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES):** Rare neurological disorder. Symptoms include seizures, headache, and visual disturbance. Discontinue bevacizumab and manage hypertension if PRES occurs.
- **Proteinuria:** Increased risk, potentially dose-dependent. Monitor with dipstick urinalysis. Permanently discontinue for Grade 4 proteinuria (nephrotic syndrome).
- **Congestive Heart Failure (CHF):** Use with caution in patients with cardiovascular disease. Increased risk observed in patients with prior anthracycline or chest wall radiation exposure.
- **Osteonecrosis of the Jaw (ONJ):** Reported in cancer patients, often in association with bisphosphonate use. Consider dental examination prior to treatment.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Categorisation
- **Very Common (>10%):** Hypertension, fatigue, asthenia, diarrhoea, abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, stomatitis, constipation, pyrexia, dyspnoea, epistaxis, rhinitis, headache, dysgeusia, peripheral sensory neuropathy, lacrimation increased, proteinuria, and dry skin [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Sepsis, abscess, cellulitis, febrile neutropenia, anaemia, dehydration, hyponatraemia, CVA, syncope, somnolence, CHF, supraventricular tachycardia, arterial thromboembolic events, pulmonary embolism, hypoxia, GI perforation, ileus, proctalgia, and palmar-plantar erythrodysaesthesia syndrome [Australian].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- GI perforation, fistulae, wound healing complications, serious haemorrhage (especially pulmonary), ATE, hypertensive encephalopathy, and PRES.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetics:** No clinically relevant interaction was observed between co-administered chemotherapy (e.g., irinotecan, oxaliplatin, capecitabine, interferon alfa-2a) and bevacizumab clearance [Australian].
- **Sunitinib Malate:** Combination with sunitinib in RCC resulted in reports of microangiopathic haemolytic anaemia (MAHA); this combination is not recommended [Australian].
- **Radiotherapy:** Safety and efficacy of concomitant bevacizumab and radiotherapy have not been established.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category D [Australian]:** Likely to inhibit angiogenesis in the foetus; potential for adverse pregnancy outcomes. Must not be used during pregnancy.
    - Contraception must be used during treatment and for at least 6 months following the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Immunoglobulins are excreted in milk, though bevacizumab-specific data is absent. Advise discontinuation of breastfeeding during and for at least 6 months after treatment.
- **Fertility:** May impair female fertility. Ovarian failure was reported in 39% of bevacizumab-treated patients vs 2.6% in control [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not approved for use in patients <18 years. Efficacy not demonstrated in trials for high-grade glioma or soft tissue sarcoma. Osteonecrosis at sites other than the jaw has been observed in this population.
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required. Patients >65 years have a higher risk of ATE, Grade 3-4 leucopenia, and thrombocytopenia.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Safety and efficacy have not been studied; these organs are not major routes for bevacizumab metabolism or excretion.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A recombinant humanised monoclonal antibody that binds to and neutralises the biological activity of human VEGF. This inhibits VEGF binding to its receptors (Flt-1 and KDR) on endothelial cells, reducing tumour vascularisation and growth.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Central volume ($V_c$) is 2.73 L (females) and 3.28 L (males).
    - **Metabolism:** Expected to be degraded by proteolysis, similar to native IgG.
    - **Elimination:** Clearance is ~0.188 L/day (females) to 0.220 L/day (males). The terminal half-life is approximately 18 to 20 days.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **mCRC (Study AVF2107g):** Addition of bevacizumab to IFL chemotherapy significantly increased median overall survival (20.3 vs 15.6 months; $p = 0.00004$) and progression-free survival (10.6 vs 6.2 months; $p < 0.0001$).
- **mBC (Study E2100):** Bevacizumab + paclitaxel significantly improved PFS compared to paclitaxel alone (11.4 vs 5.8 months; $p < 0.0001$).
- **NSCLC (Study E4599):** Addition of bevacizumab to PC chemotherapy improved median OS (12.3 vs 10.3 months; $p = 0.003$).
- **Grade IV Glioma (Study AVF3708g):** Demonstrated a 6-month PFS of 42.6% and an objective response rate of 28.2% as assessed by independent review.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store at 2°C to 8°C. Refrigerate; do not freeze. Protect from light.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging.
- **In-use Stability:** After dilution in 0.9% NaCl, chemical/physical stability is maintained for 48 hours at 2°C to 30°C. Microbiological stability is maintained for 24 hours at 2°C to 8°C [Australian].
