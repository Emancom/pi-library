# Editorial Product Information: Bromazepam

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical and regulatory data into a concise reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidelines required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Bromazepam (a benzodiazepine derivative).
- **Brand Names:** Lexotan [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N05BA08.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Tablets:** 3 mg (pale red, cylindrical, marked "/3" and break mark) and 6 mg (greenish-grey, cylindrical, marked "/6" and break mark) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pharmaco (Australia) Ltd under license from CHEPLAPHARM Arzneimittel GmbH [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Anxiety Disorders:** Symptomatic relief of tension, anxiety, and agitation [Australian].
- **Clinical Note:** Anxiety or tension associated with the ordinary stresses of daily life generally does not warrant treatment with an anxiolytic [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** Dosage must be individualised. Therapy should commence at the lowest effective dose and be titrated upward as needed.
- **Ambulatory Patients:** Typically 3 mg administered two or three times daily. For low total daily doses (3–6 mg), the entire dose may be administered in the evening to facilitate sleep [Australian].
- **Severe/Hospitalised Cases:** 6 mg to 12 mg administered two or three times daily [Australian].
- **Maximum Dose:** 60 mg daily [Australian].
- **Administration:** Doses should preferably be taken on an empty stomach, as the impact of food on absorption is not fully characterised [Australian].
- **Duration of Treatment:** Use should be restricted to the shortest period possible, generally 2–4 weeks. A tapering period of 6–8 weeks may be required following treatment [Australian].
- **Discontinuation:** Abrupt withdrawal must be avoided. A gradual, individualised tapering schedule is mandatory to prevent withdrawal symptoms [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to benzodiazepines or any excipients (e.g., lactose) [Australian].
- Severe respiratory insufficiency or chronic obstructive airways disease with incipient respiratory failure [Australian].
- Severe hepatic impairment (risk of precipitating hepatic encephalopathy) [Australian].
- Sleep apnoea syndrome [Australian].
- Myasthenia gravis [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Alcohol and CNS Depressants:** Concomitant use must be avoided. Interaction may result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, cardiovascular collapse, coma, or death [Australian].
- **Dependence and Abuse:** Use may lead to physical and psychological dependence. Risk increases with higher doses, prolonged duration, and in patients with a history of alcohol or drug abuse [Australian].
- **Withdrawal Symptoms:** May occur following even one week of therapy. Symptoms range from headache and insomnia to major syndromes involving convulsions, hallucinations, and delirium [Australian].
- **Amnesia:** Anterograde amnesia may occur at therapeutic doses, with increased risk at higher dosages [Australian].
- **Psychiatric/Paradoxical Reactions:** Reactions including restlessness, agitation, rage, hallucinations, and inappropriate behaviour are known to occur. Discontinue use if these appear; they are more frequent in children and the elderly [Australian].
- **Depression/Psychosis:** Not recommended as primary therapy. May unmask pre-existing depression or increase suicidal tendencies [Australian].
- **Glaucoma:** Use with caution in acute narrow-angle glaucoma due to potential atropine-like side effects [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Most adverse effects involve the central nervous system. Drowsiness and ataxia often diminish with continued use.

### Adverse Events (Frequency $\ge$ 1%)
| Adverse Event | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- |
| Drowsiness | 32% |
| Ataxia | 13% |
| Dizziness | 7.6% |
| Behavioural disorders | 4% |
| Minor lab elevations (Bilirubin, AST, ALT, SAP, BUN) | 3% each |
| Speech disorders | 2% |
| Sleep disorders, Confusion, Headache, Depression, Nausea, Vomiting, GI disturbances | 1% each |

### Post-Marketing and Serious Effects
- **Psychiatric:** Disorientation, libido changes, emotional disturbances, and paradoxical reactions (aggression, nightmares) [Australian].
- **Nervous System:** Seizures, tremors, incontinence, and decreased alertness [Australian].
- **General:** Fatigue, muscle weakness, and skin reactions (pruritus/rash) [Australian].
- **Serious:** Cardiac failure, cardiac arrest, hypotension, tachycardia, and respiratory depression [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic (Metabolic):**
    - **CYP450 Inhibitors:** Drugs that inhibit hepatic microsomal oxidation (e.g., azole antifungals, macrolides, HIV protease inhibitors) may increase bromazepam levels.
    - **Cimetidine:** May reduce clearance by 50%, necessitating dose reduction.
    - **Fluvoxamine:** Increases bromazepam exposure (AUC) by 2.4-fold and doubles elimination half-life [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamic:**
    - **CNS Depressants:** Additive effects with barbiturates, antidepressants, hypnotics, phenothiazines, and narcotic analgesics (which may also enhance euphoria/dependence) [Australian].
    - **Anticonvulsants:** May alter serum concentrations of either agent; frequent monitoring is required [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category C [Australian]:** Benzodiazepines cross the placenta. Use in late pregnancy or during labour may cause neonatal hypotonia, hypothermia, and respiratory depression. Chronic use may lead to neonatal physical dependence and withdrawal [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Bromazepam passes into breast milk. Nursing mothers should not take this medication [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal data (rats) showed no effect on fertility at doses up to 50 mg/kg/day [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended due to insufficient safety and efficacy data [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Lower doses are required. Elderly patients are more susceptible to sedation, ataxia, and confusion, significantly increasing the risk of falls and fractures. Clearance in patients over 60 years may be reduced by 50% [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution; dose reduction may be necessary [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe impairment. Use the lowest possible dose in mild-to-moderate impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A benzodiazepine that selectively reduces tension and anxiety at low doses. Higher doses induce sedative and muscle-relaxant properties [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Almost completely absorbed (fasting state); Bioavailability is 60%. Peak plasma levels (Tmax) are reached in 0.5–4 hours [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** 70% protein bound; Volume of distribution is approximately 50 L [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Extensive hepatic metabolism via hydroxylation. Primary metabolites are 3-hydroxy-bromazepam and 2-(2-amino-5-bromo-3-hydroxybenzoyl) pyridine [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Half-life is approximately 17 hours (range 11–22 hours). Less than 2% is excreted unchanged in the urine [Australian].
