# Editorial Product Information: bacillus calmette-guerin

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **bacillus calmette-guerin**
- Chemical class: Live, attenuated strain of Mycobacterium bovis (BCG)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intravesical

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment and prophylaxis of carcinoma in situ (CIS) of the urinary bladder.
- Prophylaxis of primary or recurrent stage Ta and/or T1 papillary bladder tumors following
  complete transurethral resection (TUR).
- Limitations: Not recommended for stage TaG1 papillary tumors unless judged to be at high
  risk of recurrence. Not indicated for papillary tumors of stages higher than T1.
- Regional differences: Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Standard intravesical dose: 1 vial reconstituted and suspended in 50 mL preservative-free
  sterile saline for a single instillation. The reconstituted vial corresponds to approximately
  50 mg wet weight and contains about 1 to 8 × 10^8 colony forming units (CFU).

### Dosing by indication
- Carcinoma in situ (CIS): Induction weekly for at least 6 weeks followed by maintenance once
  monthly thereafter for up to 12 months in most protocols; some patients received longer
  maintenance in clinical studies.
- Ta/T1 papillary tumors (high-risk protocols used in clinical trials): Induction weekly for
  6 weeks, then instillations at weeks 8 and 12, then monthly to complete 1 year of treatment
  in certain trial regimens. (See clinical trials section for study-specific schedules.)

### Administration instructions
- Administer intravesically only. Do not inject subcutaneously or intravenously.
- Retain intravesical instillate for approximately 2 hours, then void. Patients should void while
  seated to avoid splashing.
- For the 6 hours after treatment, voided urine should be disinfected for 15 minutes with an
  equal volume of household bleach before flushing. Patients should be advised to increase
  fluid intake to assist bladder clearance.
- Reconstitution and preparation must use aseptic technique. To avoid cross-contamination,
  parenteral drugs should not be prepared in the same area where bacillus calmette-guerin is
  reconstituted; a separate preparation area is recommended. Treat all materials and waste in
  contact with the product as biohazardous.
- Care should be taken not to traumatize the urinary tract or introduce contaminants into the
  urinary system during catheterization.

### Titration schedules
- Not specified in available sources.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known congenital or acquired immunodeficiency (e.g., AIDS, leukemia, lymphoma).
- Concurrent cancer therapy causing immunosuppression (e.g., cytotoxic drugs, radiation) or
  concomitant immunosuppressive therapy (e.g., systemic corticosteroids) that result in
  immunosuppression.
- Active tuberculosis.
- Presence of concurrent febrile illness, urinary tract infection, or gross hematuria until these
  conditions have resolved.
- Recent traumatic urinary tract manipulation: instillation should be postponed for 7–14 days
  following transurethral resection (TUR), biopsy, or traumatic catheterization.
- Patients at increased risk for dissemination of infection (documented immunosuppression or
  other conditions at increased risk for BCG infection).
- Additional jurisdiction-specific contraindications: Not specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed/Critical Warnings
- **Contains live, attenuated mycobacteria.** Prepare, handle, and dispose of as biohazard
  material. Health-care personnel can be infected through accidental needle sticks or skin
  lacerations during preparation. BCG is capable of dissemination after intravesical
  administration and fatal infections have been reported.

### General warnings and precautions
- Bacillus calmette-guerin is infectious; personnel handling the product should be familiar
  with prevention, diagnosis, and treatment of BCG-related complications and have access to
  infectious disease consultation.
- Avoid preparation of other parenteral drugs in areas where BCG is reconstituted to reduce
  nosocomial transmission risk.
- Do not administer if the urinary tract is actively bleeding or mucosa is traumatized; such
  conditions may promote systemic infection.
- Postpone intravesical instillation for at least 7–14 days following biopsy, TUR, or traumatic
  catheterization.
- Administer with caution in persons at high risk for HIV infection.
- Antimicrobial therapy for other infections may interfere with the effectiveness of intravesical
  BCG; intravesical instillations should be postponed during antibiotic therapy.
- Small bladder capacity has been associated with increased risk of severe local reactions;
  consider this when selecting patients.
- Tuberculin (PPD) sensitivity may develop; obtain baseline tuberculin reactivity prior to
  initiation.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor for fever, chills, malaise, and other systemic symptoms after each instillation.
- Persistent abnormalities of liver function tests may suggest systemic BCG infection;
  monitor liver function tests in the setting of systemic symptoms.
- Have appropriate mycobacterial culture media available when evaluating suspected
  infection; negative cultures do not exclude infection.
- Monitor for local genitourinary signs (dysuria, frequency, hematuria,
  epididymitis/prostatitis/orchitis).

### Management of serious complications
- Persistent fever (≥38.5°C / 101.3°F), febrile episodes lasting more than 72 hours, systemic
  manifestations that increase in intensity with repeated instillations, or persistent abnormal
  liver function tests suggest systemic BCG infection and warrant diagnostic evaluation and
  empirical antimycobacterial therapy.
- For suspected systemic BCG infection, discontinue BCG and begin at least two
  antimycobacterial agents while diagnostic work-up (including appropriate cultures) is
  conducted. Consult an infectious disease specialist experienced in mycobacterial
  infections.
- Bacillus calmette-guerin strains are typically sensitive to isoniazid, rifampin, and ethambutol;
  they are not sensitive to pyrazinamide.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Antimicrobials: Concurrent systemic antimicrobial therapy for other infections may interfere
  with the efficacy of intravesical bacillus calmette-guerin; intravesical instillations should be
  postponed during antimicrobial therapy.
- Immunosuppressants / cytotoxic therapy / radiation: Concomitant use of agents or therapies
  that suppress the immune response (including systemic corticosteroids, cytotoxic drugs,
  radiation) may interfere with development of the immune response and should not be used
  in combination with bacillus calmette-guerin.
- Antituberculous agents: Bacillus calmette-guerin strains are generally sensitive to isoniazid,
  rifampin, and ethambutol; they are not sensitive to pyrazinamide. Use of antituberculous
  agents may be required for treatment of systemic BCG infection. Antituberculous drugs
  should not be used prophylactically to prevent local irritative urinary adverse effects of
  intravesical BCG.
- Other clinically significant interactions: Not specified in available sources.
- Regional interaction notes: Not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted with bacillus calmette-guerin.
- It is not known whether intravesical bacillus calmette-guerin can cause fetal harm when
  administered to a pregnant woman or affect reproductive capacity.
- Recommendation: Should not be given to a pregnant woman except when clearly needed.
  Women should be advised not to become pregnant while on therapy.

### Breastfeeding
- It is not known whether bacillus calmette-guerin is excreted in human milk.
- Given the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants from exposure to
  bacillus calmette-guerin, a decision should be made to discontinue breastfeeding or to
  discontinue therapy, weighing the clinical importance of the drug to the mother.

### Fertility
- Impairment of fertility: Not evaluated / Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Safety and effectiveness for the treatment of superficial bladder cancer in pediatric patients
  have not been established.

### Elderly use
- Clinical studies included many older patients (mean age in trials approximately 66–69 years).
  No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed between older and younger
  subjects, but greater sensitivity in some older individuals cannot be ruled out.

### Renal impairment
- Dosing adjustments by degree of renal impairment are not specified in available sources.

### Hepatic impairment
- Dosing adjustments by severity of hepatic impairment are not specified in available sources.
  Persistent abnormalities of liver function tests may indicate systemic BCG infection;
  hepatitis and hepatic granuloma have been reported in association with systemic
  dissemination.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Intravesical bacillus calmette-guerin induces a local granulomatous inflammatory reaction in
  the bladder. The precise antitumour mechanism is not fully established but is immune-
  mediated.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: Not specified in available sources.
- Distribution: Not specified in available sources.
- Metabolism: Not specified in available sources.
- Elimination: Not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Induces tuberculin sensitivity (PPD conversion can occur).
- Local immune activation and granulomatous inflammation at the site of administration.

## 12. Clinical Trials
### Carcinoma in situ (CIS)
- Data were pooled from multiple investigational studies using an induction plus maintenance
  schedule.
- Typical regimen in these studies: intravesical administration once weekly for at least 6 weeks
  (induction) followed by once-monthly maintenance thereafter for up to 12 months; some
  patients received longer maintenance.
- Evaluable population: 119 patients (from pooled studies). Reported response (1987
  analysis): Complete Histological Response (CR) in 54/119 (46%); Complete Clinical
  Response without cytology (CRNC) in 36/119 (30%); overall response rate 90/119 (76%).
- A follow-up update (median follow-up 47 months) estimated median duration of response
  ≥4 years. Among responders, cystectomy incidence was 11% with median time to
  cystectomy exceeding 74 months.
- Safety: Serious disseminated infections and deaths have been reported in association with
  intravesical therapy; monitor and manage as described above.

### Ta/T1 papillary bladder cancer
- SWOG study (randomized, phase III; study 8795): Compared intravesical bacillus
  calmette-guerin versus intravesical mitomycin C in patients at high risk of recurrence
  (treatment initiated 1–2 weeks after TUR). Regimen for BCG in this trial: weekly for
  6 weeks, then at 8 and 12 weeks, then monthly to complete 1 year.
  - Patients evaluable: 191 in BCG arm, 186 in mitomycin C arm.
  - Estimated disease-free survival at 2 years: BCG 57% (95% CI 50–65%) vs mitomycin C
    45% (95% CI 38–53%). The difference was statistically significant (log-rank P=0.03).
  - No significant differences were observed in time to progression, tumor invasion, or
    overall survival.
  - Toxicity: Local and systemic toxicity were more frequent and often more severe with BCG
    compared with mitomycin C.
- Nijmegen study (randomized): Compared BCG substrains and mitomycin C; in this study
  maintenance BCG was not routinely used. Estimated 2-year disease-free survival: BCG
  (TICE substrain) 53% vs another BCG substrain 62% vs mitomycin C 64%; differences
  among the three arms were not statistically significant (P=0.08).
- Overall: Intravesical bacillus calmette-guerin demonstrated efficacy in reducing recurrence
  in CIS and in certain Ta/T1 populations in randomized and non-randomized studies;
  toxicity profile includes more frequent local and systemic effects compared with mitomycin
  C in trials that compared them.
