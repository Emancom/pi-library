# Editorial Product Information: Bumetanide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **bumetanide** intended for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not replace the official, legally binding Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Bumetanide (a metanilamide derivative).
- **Brand Names:** Burinex [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** C03CA02.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Oral tablet: 1 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AFT Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Bumetanide** is indicated for the management of oedema associated with the following conditions [Australian]:
- Congestive heart failure.
- Hepatic disease.
- Renal disease (including nephrotic syndrome).
- Acute pulmonary oedema.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Initial Dose:** 1 mg daily, administered as a single dose in the morning or early evening [Australian].
- **Refractory Cases:** If the initial response is insufficient, subsequent doses may be administered at 4- to 5-hour intervals [Australian].
- **Maximum Daily Dose:** 10 mg [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Intermittent Scheduling:** To enhance safety and efficacy in long-term control of oedema, an intermittent schedule is recommended (e.g., dosing on alternate days or for 3–4 consecutive days followed by a 1–2 day rest period) [Australian].

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Anuria:** While usable in some renal insufficiency, **bumetanide** must be discontinued if there is a marked increase in BUN/creatinine or development of oliguria during treatment of progressive renal disease [Australian].
- **Hepatic Encephalopathy:** Including hepatic coma [Australian].
- **Severe Electrolyte Depletion** [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to **bumetanide** or any excipients. (Note: Cross-sensitivity with frusemide is reported to be low) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Volume and Electrolyte Imbalance:** Excessive dosing may lead to profound dehydration, blood volume reduction, and circulatory collapse. This carries a risk of vascular thrombosis and embolism, particularly in elderly patients [Australian].
- **Laboratory Monitoring:** Regular monitoring of serum potassium is essential, especially in patients also taking digitalis, those with hepatic cirrhosis/ascites, or those with a history of ventricular arrhythmias. Periodic assessment of sodium, chloride, calcium, bicarbonate, and magnesium is advised [Australian].
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Life-threatening reactions, including Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), have been reported. **Bumetanide** must be permanently discontinued if these signs appear [Australian].
- **Hypomagnesaemia:** Risk is increased when co-administered with Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs) [Australian].
- **Metabolic Effects:** May increase serum uric acid (potential for gout) and may affect glucose metabolism; monitoring of blood and urine glucose is recommended in diabetic or pre-diabetic patients [Australian].
- **Genito-urinary:** Use with caution in patients with urinary tract obstruction or prostatic hypertrophy [Australian].
- **Sulphonamide Allergy:** Patients allergic to sulphonamides may exhibit hypersensitivity to **bumetanide** [Australian].
- **Athletes:** **Bumetanide** is a prohibited substance in competitive sports (doping) [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often manifestations of the drug's pharmacological activity (diuresis).

### Frequency Data (Clinical Trials)
| System Organ Class | Common (≥ 1%) | Uncommon (0.5% to < 1%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Metabolism/Nutrition** | Electrolyte imbalance (hypokalaemia, hyponatraemia, hypochloraemia, hyperkalaemia) | Gout |
| **Nervous System** | Dizziness, headache, encephalopathy (in pre-existing liver disease) | — |
| **Vascular** | Hypotension | — |
| **Respiratory** | Dyspnoea | Cough |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea, abdominal pain | Vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation |
| **Musculoskeletal** | Muscle cramps (may be severe at high doses) | Arthralgia, musculoskeletal pain |
| **Renal/Urinary** | Micturition disorder | — |
| **General** | Peripheral oedema | Malaise, weakness, thirst, chest pain |

### Serious and Other Notable Effects
- **Laboratory Abnormalities:** Hyperuricaemia (18.4%), hypochloremia (14.9%), hypokalaemia (14.7%), and azotemia (10.6%) [Australian].
- **Post-Marketing Reports:** Bone marrow failure, pancytopenia, thrombocytopenia, leucopenia, liver damage, and idiosyncratic reactions such as gynaecomastia [Australian].
- **Ototoxicity:** While potentially less ototoxic than frusemide, hearing disturbances have been reported, particularly in patients with renal impairment [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Lithium:** Diuretics reduce lithium clearance; concurrent use is not recommended due to risk of lithium toxicity [Australian].
- **Probenecid:** Inhibits the diuretic and natriuretic effects of **bumetanide** [Australian].
- **Antihypertensives:** Potentiation of effect may occur; first-dose hypotension is possible with tricyclic antidepressants [Australian].
- **Digitalis Glycosides:** Hypokalaemia increases the risk of digitalis toxicity and myocardial sensitivity to toxins [Australian].
- **Ototoxic/Nephrotoxic Drugs:** Avoid co-administration with aminoglycosides or other known nephrotoxins, as **bumetanide** may potentiate their toxicity [Australian].
- **NSAIDs:** These agents inhibit the diuretic effect of **bumetanide** and may increase the risk of renal failure [Australian].
- **Antiarrhythmics:** Concomitant use with Class III agents may increase the risk of QT prolongation and torsades de pointes due to electrolyte imbalances [Australian].
- **Proton Pump Inhibitors:** May exacerbate hypomagnesaemia [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. **Bumetanide** may cause harmful pharmacological effects (electrolyte disturbances, neonatal thrombocytopenia). Use only if benefits outweigh potential risks to the foetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Excretion in breast milk is unknown. Use is not recommended unless expected benefits outweigh risks [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No clinical data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children under 18 years of age have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of circulatory collapse, thrombosis, and embolism due to profound diuresis [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Caution is advised in severe or progressive impairment. Monitor for increasing BUN/creatinine [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Exercise extreme caution in severe impairment or cirrhosis; sudden electrolyte shifts may precipitate hepatic coma [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
**Bumetanide** is a potent loop diuretic that inhibits sodium and chloride reabsorption in the ascending limb of the loop of Henle. It is approximately 40 times more potent than frusemide on a weight-for-weight basis (1 mg bumetanide $\approx$ 40 mg frusemide) [Australian].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Rapidly and almost completely absorbed following oral administration [Australian].
- **Onset of Action:** Diuresis begins within 30 minutes; peak effect occurs between 1 and 2 hours [Australian].
- **Duration:** 3–4 hours at usual doses (1–2 mg); up to 4–6 hours at higher doses [Australian].
- **Distribution:** Apparent volume of distribution is ~25 L; protein binding is ~95% [Australian].
- **Metabolism:** Oxidation of the N-butyl side chain to alcohol metabolites, excreted as glucuronides [Australian].
- **Excretion:** 75–80% excreted in urine (half as unchanged drug); 10% in faeces. Plasma half-life is 60–90 minutes [Australian].
