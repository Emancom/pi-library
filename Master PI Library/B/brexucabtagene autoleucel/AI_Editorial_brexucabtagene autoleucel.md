**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been compiled by integrating clinical data, safety warnings, and administration protocols from the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). Where regulatory guidance differs, the most conservative or comprehensive recommendations have been prioritized to ensure maximum patient safety.

# Product Information: TECARTUS (brexucabtagene autoleucel)

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** TECARTUS
**Active Ingredient:** Brexucabtagene autoleucel

TECARTUS is a CD19-directed, genetically modified autologous T-cell immunotherapy. It consists of the patient’s own T cells, which have been harvested via leukapheresis and engineered ex vivo using a retroviral vector. These cells express a chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) that includes a murine anti-CD19 single-chain variable fragment (scFv) coupled with CD28 and CD3-zeta co-stimulatory domains.

## 2. Approved Indications
TECARTUS is indicated for the treatment of:
*   **Mantle Cell Lymphoma (MCL):** Adult patients with relapsed or refractory MCL. Australian guidance specifies that patients should have received two or more lines of systemic therapy, including a Bruton’s tyrosine kinase (BTK) inhibitor [TGA].
*   **Acute Lymphoblastic Leukaemia (ALL):** Adult patients (18 years and older) with relapsed or refractory B-cell precursor ALL.

## 3. Contraindications
TECARTUS is contraindicated in patients with a known history of hypersensitivity to the active substance or any of the excipients, including dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) and gentamicin (residual). Clinicians must also consider the contraindications associated with the required lymphodepleting chemotherapy (cyclophosphamide and fludarabine).

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Cytokine Release Syndrome (CRS)
CRS is a common and potentially fatal systemic inflammatory response. In clinical trials, CRS occurred in approximately 91-93% of patients.
*   **Management:** Do not administer to patients with active infections or inflammatory disorders. A minimum of two doses of tocilizumab must be available on-site prior to infusion, though Australian guidance recommends at least four doses be accessible to ensure management of severe cases [TGA, FDA].
*   **Monitoring:** Monitor patients daily for at least 7 days post-infusion in a qualified clinical setting for fever, hypoxia, and hypotension.

### Neurologic Toxicities (ICANS)
Immune Effector Cell-Associated Neurotoxicity Syndrome (ICANS) can be life-threatening or fatal. It may occur concurrently with CRS, after its resolution, or in its absence.
*   **Symptoms:** Includes encephalopathy, seizures, aphasia, and tremors.
*   **Monitoring:** Perform daily neurological evaluations. Patients should be monitored for at least 4 weeks post-infusion for signs of delayed neurotoxicity.

### Secondary Malignancies
Patients treated with TECARTUS may develop secondary malignancies. T-cell malignancies have been reported following treatment with CD19-directed CAR T-cell therapies [FDA]. Patients should be monitored lifelong for the occurrence of new neoplasms.

### Driving and Hazardous Activities
Due to the risk of sudden alterations in consciousness or seizures, patients must be advised to refrain from driving or operating heavy machinery. The restricted period is **8 weeks** following infusion [TGA], though US guidance suggests a minimum of 2 weeks [FDA]. The more conservative 8-week restriction is recommended.

### Other Precautions
*   **Serious Infections:** Severe, opportunistic, and fatal infections (including HBV reactivation) have occurred. Screening for HIV, HBV, and HCV is required before cell collection.
*   **Prolonged Cytopenias:** Patients may exhibit Grade 3 or higher neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, and anemia for several weeks following chemotherapy and TECARTUS infusion.
*   **Hypogammaglobulinaemia:** B-cell aplasia leading to low immunoglobulin levels is expected. Monitor levels and provide replacement therapy as indicated.
*   **Tumour Lysis Syndrome (TLS):** Prophylactic measures for TLS should be implemented in patients with high tumor burden.

## 5. Dosage and Administration
TECARTUS is for **autologous use only** and must be administered via intravenous infusion in a qualified healthcare facility.

### Target Dosage
*   **Mantle Cell Lymphoma:** 2 × 10⁶ CAR-positive viable T cells per kg of body weight (Maximum: 2 × 10⁸ cells).
*   **Acute Lymphoblastic Leukaemia:** 1 × 10⁶ CAR-positive viable T cells per kg of body weight (Maximum: 1 × 10⁸ cells).

### Treatment Schedule
1.  **Lymphodepletion:** Administer cyclophosphamide (500 mg/m²) and fludarabine (30 mg/m² or 25 mg/m² depending on indication) intravenously for 3 days prior to infusion.
2.  **Premedication:** Administer paracetamol (500–1000 mg) and diphenhydramine (12.5–25 mg) approximately 30–60 minutes before infusion. Avoid prophylactic systemic steroids.
3.  **Infusion:** Thaw TECARTUS at 37°C. Infuse the entire contents of the bag within 30 minutes. **Do not use a leukodepleting filter.**

## 6. Adverse Effects
The safety profile is characterized by high rates of immune-mediated reactions.
*   **Very Common (≥20%):** Pyrexia, CRS, hypotension, encephalopathy, fatigue, tachycardia, hypoxia, chills, cough, headache, nausea, and tremors.
*   **Laboratory Abnormalities:** Neutropenia, leukopenia, lymphopenia, and thrombocytopenia are nearly universal (often >90% of patients).
*   **Serious Reactions:** Encephalopathy, septic shock, and respiratory failure. Hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis/macrophage activation syndrome (HLH/MAS) has also been reported [FDA].

## 7. Drug Interactions
No formal interaction studies have been conducted.
*   **Vaccines:** The safety of live viral vaccines has not been studied. Vaccination with live vaccines is not recommended for at least 6 weeks prior to the start of lymphodepletion and should be avoided until immune recovery is complete.
*   **Corticosteroids:** Use of systemic corticosteroids as a premedication or for other conditions may interfere with the activity of TECARTUS.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy (Category C):** There are no data on the use of TECARTUS in pregnant women. It is not recommended for use during pregnancy. Women of childbearing potential should undergo pregnancy testing before starting treatment.
*   **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if TECARTUS is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding women should be advised of the potential risks to the infant.
*   **Fertility:** There are no data regarding the effect of TECARTUS on male or female fertility.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
Following infusion, TECARTUS cells undergo rapid expansion, typically peaking in the blood within 7 to 15 days. The cells then gradually decline; however, they may remain detectable for several months. In MCL patients, higher peak levels (Cmax) and greater exposure (AUC) were positively associated with objective clinical responses. Pharmacokinetics were not significantly impacted by age (within the adult range) or gender.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Brexucabtagene autoleucel binds to CD19-expressing target cells (both malignant and normal B cells). Upon engagement, the CD28 and CD3-zeta signaling domains activate the CAR T cells, triggering proliferation, cytokine secretion, and the direct cytotoxic killing of CD19-positive cells.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Primary Storage:** TECARTUS must be stored in the vapor phase of liquid nitrogen (at or below -120°C).
*   **Temporary Storage:** The product may be stored at -80°C (± 10°C) for a single period of up to 90 days [TGA, FDA]. Once moved to -80°C, it must not be returned to liquid nitrogen storage.
*   **Thawed Product:** Once thawed, the product must be administered within 30 minutes, though it can remain stable at room temperature (20°C to 25°C) for up to 3 hours. Do not refreeze.
