# Editorial Product Information: Buprenorphine (Transdermal)

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdiction: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the approved Product Information for healthcare professionals. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial overview and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. This synthesis is based on available documentation from the Australian TGA; information regarding other jurisdictions was not provided for this review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Buprenorphine (a partial opioid agonist)
- **Chemical Class**: Thebaine derivative; psychotropic substance under international convention.
- **Brand Name**: B-PATCH [Australian]
- **ATC Code**: N02AE01 [Not explicitly stated in source, though standard for buprenorphine]
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**: Transdermal drug delivery system (patch) available in seven strengths: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, and 40 micrograms/hour [Australian].
- **Route of Administration**: Transdermal
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: AU Pharma Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
Buprenorphine transdermal patches are indicated for the management of severe pain under the following conditions [Australian]:
- Other treatment options (non-opioid analgesics or other opioids) have failed, are contraindicated, not tolerated, or are otherwise inappropriate.
- The pain is classified as opioid-responsive.
- The patient requires daily, continuous, long-term analgesic treatment.

**Important limitations**:
- Not indicated for "as-needed" (PRN) analgesia.
- Use in chronic non-cancer pain is restricted to exceptional circumstances [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Initial Dose**: 5 micrograms/hour is the recommended starting dose for all patients, particularly opioid-naïve individuals [Australian].
- **Maximum Dose**: 40 micrograms/hour. Total dose should not exceed this limit [Australian].

### Titration and Maintenance
- **Initial Titration**: The dose may be adjusted every 3 days (72 hours) during the initial phase.
- **Maintenance**: Once a stable dose is reached, the 7-day dosing interval must be maintained.
- **Dose Adjustment**: Based on the need for supplemental (PRN) analgesia and patient response.

### Administration Instructions
- **Application**: Apply to non-irritated, intact skin on the upper outer arm, upper chest, upper back, or side of the chest.
- **Wear Time**: Each patch is worn continuously for 7 days.
- **Site Rotation**: Do not reuse the same skin site for 3 to 4 weeks [Australian].
- **Application Technique**: Press firmly with the palm for 30 seconds. If the edges peel, they may be secured with surgical tape.
- **External Heat**: Patients must avoid exposing the application site to direct heat (heating pads, electric blankets, saunas, etc.), as this significantly increases buprenorphine absorption [Australian].

### Discontinuation
- Do not abruptly discontinue in physically dependent patients.
- Use a gradual downward titration (e.g., reducing the dose by 10% to 25% every 2 to 4 weeks) to prevent withdrawal symptoms [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to buprenorphine or patch components.
- **Pregnancy** [Australian].
- Myasthenia gravis and delirium tremens.
- Severely impaired respiratory function, acute respiratory depression, or severe respiratory disease.
- Concurrent use of non-selective monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs) or use within 14 days of stopping MAOIs [Australian].
- Management of opioid dependence or withdrawal.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [Australian]
- **Hazardous and Harmful Use**: Potential for abuse, misuse, and addiction, which can lead to overdose and death.
- **Life-threatening Respiratory Depression**: Serious or fatal respiratory depression may occur; monitor closely during initiation and dose increases.
- **Concomitant CNS Depressants**: Use with benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, or alcohol may result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death.

### General Precautions
- **QT Prolongation**: Dosages of 40 mcg/hr have been associated with a mean QTc prolongation of 5.9 ms. Use caution in patients with congenital long QT syndrome or those taking Class IA or Class III anti-arrhythmics [Australian].
- **Serotonin Syndrome**: Risk is increased when used with SSRIs, SNRIs, or tricyclic antidepressants.
- **Skin Reactions**: Both irritant and allergic contact dermatitis (Type IV hypersensitivity) may occur. Allergic reactions may be delayed by months and require discontinuation [Australian].
- **Febrile Illness**: Severe fever may increase absorption; monitor for increased opioid effects.
- **Head Injury**: Use with caution in patients with increased intracranial pressure or reduced consciousness.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally typical of opioid analgesics, though constipation may be less frequent than with full mu-agonists.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Constipation, nausea, vomiting, dizziness, headache, somnolence, application site reactions (erythema, pruritus). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Anorexia, anxiety, confusion, insomnia, dyspnoea, abdominal pain, diarrhoea, dyspepsia, rash, sweating, asthenic conditions. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Palpitations, tachycardia, tinnitus, blurred vision, flatulence, urinary retention, affect lability, agitation, hypotension. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Respiratory depression, ileus, biliary colic, anaphylactic reactions, sexual dysfunction. |

**Note**: In clinical trials, females reported nausea, vomiting, dizziness, and headache 10% to 15% more frequently than males [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic (CYP3A4)**: Buprenorphine is a CYP3A4 substrate. Inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, ritonavir) may increase levels/toxicity. Inducers (e.g., rifampicin, phenytoin) may reduce efficacy [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamic**:
    - **CNS Depressants**: Alcohol, benzodiazepines, and other sedatives increase the risk of fatal respiratory depression.
    - **Serotonergic Agents**: SSRIs/SNRIs increase the risk of Serotonin Syndrome.
    - **MAOIs**: Non-selective MAOIs are contraindicated.
    - **Anticholinergics**: Increased risk of urinary retention and severe constipation.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: **Contraindicated** [Australian]. Buprenorphine crosses the placenta and may cause respiratory depression in the neonate. Chronic use may lead to Neonatal Opioid Withdrawal Syndrome.
- **Breastfeeding**: Not recommended. Buprenorphine passes into breast milk and has the potential to inhibit lactation [Australian].
- **Fertility**: No adverse effects on fertility were seen in animal studies at doses 70 times the human exposure [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and efficacy not established in patients under 18 years; use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Elderly Use**: No specific dose adjustment is required, but increased caution is advised due to potential sensitivity to respiratory depression.
- **Renal Impairment**: No dosage adjustment required [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: No adjustment for mild-to-moderate impairment. Use with caution (or avoid) in severe hepatic impairment, as buprenorphine may accumulate [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Partial agonist at the mu-opioid receptor; antagonist at the kappa-opioid receptor.
- **Pharmacodynamics**: Exhibits a "ceiling effect" for analgesia and respiratory depression compared to full mu-agonists. Binds/dissociates from mu-receptors slowly, leading to prolonged action.
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Median time to detectable concentrations is 17 hours. Bioavailability is 13–15% relative to IV [Australian].
    - **Distribution**: 96% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism**: Primarily hepatic via CYP3A4 to norbuprenorphine (active metabolite) and UGT1A1/1A3 to buprenorphine 3-O-glucuronide.
    - **Elimination**: Excreted in faeces within seven days. Terminal half-life after patch removal is approximately 12 hours (range 10–24 hours).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study BUPN.CLIN0001**: An equivalence study (n=238) comparing buprenorphine patches (5, 10, 20 mg) to sublingual tablets in osteoarthritis. Both arms showed similar reductions in pain scores (approx. 3 units on an 11-point scale) [Australian].
- **Study BP98-1201**: A double-blind trial (n=270) in moderate-to-severe back pain comparing patches to hydrocodone/paracetamol. Buprenorphine patches were found equally effective in pain relief and patient satisfaction [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Handling**: Used patches must be folded with the adhesive sides together and disposed of safely, out of reach of children.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule**: Schedule 8 (S8) - Controlled Drug [Australian].
