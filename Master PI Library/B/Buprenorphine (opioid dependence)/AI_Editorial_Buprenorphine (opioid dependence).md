# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for information purposes and is not a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
  - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn errors of metabolism [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. This may be administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
  - **Severe Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
  - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically remain in the range of 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised based on clinical requirement [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
  - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
  - **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations.
- **Frequency:** Weekly and monthly monitoring is advised. 
- **Target:** Normal plasma free carnitine levels are generally considered to be between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- None listed [US].

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **Thromboembolic Risk (Oral context):** While not specifically highlighted for the injection, the document notes that high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of metabolites like trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

- Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
  - **Management:** Monitor INR levels at the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **Category:** Not explicitly assigned a letter grade in the source, but described as "Category C" context (animal studies show no harm at 3.8x human dose, but no adequate human studies exist).
  - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation has not been specifically studied in nursing mothers. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation for the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal reproductive studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is calculated by weight (50 mg/kg). Specific age-range safety profiles beyond weight-based dosing are not detailed [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
  - **ESRD:** Levocarnitine is specifically indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD patients on dialysis.
  - **Non-dialysis Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production [US].

### Pharmacodynamics
In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly cleared [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Following intravenous administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Trials:** Two double-blind, placebo-controlled trials were conducted in chronic hemodialysis patients. These trials demonstrated that levocarnitine injection (10, 20, and 40 mg/kg) significantly increased plasma carnitine concentrations. However, the effect on specific clinical outcomes (signs and symptoms of deficiency) in this population was not determined during these studies [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Conditions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s in concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL, stored in PVC bags [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** Rx-only [US].
