# Editorial Product Information: Belantamab Mafodotin

**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been synthesized from the official regulatory filings of the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). Where clinical guidelines or approved indications differ between these regions, both sets of information are provided with the contributing agency noted in brackets.

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** BLENREP  
**Active Ingredient:** Belantamab mafodotin (belantamab mafodotin-blmf [FDA])

Belantamab mafodotin is an antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) consisting of a humanized monoclonal IgG1κ antibody targeting B-cell maturation antigen (BCMA). The antibody is conjugated to the microtubule inhibitor maleimidocaproyl monomethyl auristatin F (mcMMAF).

---

## 2. Approved Indications
Belantamab mafodotin is indicated for the treatment of adults with relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma:

*   **In combination with bortezomib and dexamethasone:**
    *   For patients who have received at least one prior therapy [TGA].
    *   For patients who have received at least two prior lines of therapy, including an immunomodulatory agent and a proteasome inhibitor [FDA].
*   **In combination with pomalidomide and dexamethasone:**
    *   For patients who have received at least one prior therapy, including lenalidomide [TGA].

---

## 3. Contraindications
*   Hypersensitivity to belantamab mafodotin or any of the excipients (citric acid monohydrate, edetate disodium, polysorbate 80, sodium citrate dihydrate, and trehalose dihydrate).

---

## 4. Warnings and Precautions

### Ocular Toxicity
**Boxed Warning [FDA]:** Belantamab mafodotin causes significant changes to the corneal epithelium. Reported events include severe visual impairment, corneal ulcers (including infectious keratitis), blurred vision, and ocular dryness. 

*   **Monitoring:** Ophthalmic examinations (visual acuity and slit-lamp exams) must be conducted at baseline and prior to every dose. 
*   **Frequency Adjustment:** If no corneal changes are observed by the sixth dose, the frequency of examinations may be reduced to every three months [TGA].
*   **Restricted Access:** In the United States, this drug is only available through a restricted Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy (REMS) program [FDA].
*   **Supportive Care:** Patients should use preservative-free artificial tears at least four times daily during treatment. Contact lenses should be avoided unless directed by an eye specialist.

### Thrombocytopenia
High rates of thrombocytopenia have been reported. Clinicians must obtain a complete blood count at baseline and monitor periodically throughout treatment. Dosage delays or reductions are required based on the severity of the count and the presence of bleeding.

### Infusion-Related Reactions (IRRs)
IRRs are common, though usually Grade 1 or 2. If a Grade 2 or higher reaction occurs, the infusion should be slowed or interrupted. Premedication is required for subsequent doses if a Grade 2 or 3 reaction is experienced.

### Pneumonitis and Infections
Non-infectious pneumonitis, including fatal cases, has been observed. Treatment should be withheld for suspected Grade 3+ pneumonitis. Additionally, severe infections such as pneumonia and COVID-19 have occurred frequently in combination trials.

### Hepatitis B Reactivation
As a B-cell directed therapy, there is a risk of Hepatitis B Virus (HBV) reactivation. Patients with positive HBV serology should be monitored for clinical and laboratory signs of reactivation.

## 5. Dosage and Administration

### Recommended Dosing Schedules
*   **With Bortezomib/Dexamethasone (BVd):** 2.5 mg/kg intravenously every 3 weeks. [FDA notes this combination is for the first 8 cycles, followed by belantamab mafodotin 2.5 mg/kg as a single agent].
*   **With Pomalidomide/Dexamethasone (BPd) [TGA]:** 2.5 mg/kg for Cycle 1, followed by 1.9 mg/kg every 4 weeks from Cycle 2 onwards.

### Preparation
1.  **Reconstitution:** Reconstitute the 70 mg vial with 1.4 mL (or the 100 mg vial with 2 mL [TGA]) of Sterile Water for Injection to achieve a 50 mg/mL concentration. Swirl gently; do not shake.
2.  **Dilution:** Withdraw the required volume and add to an infusion bag containing 250 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride. The bag must be made of PVC or polyolefin.
3.  **Administration:** Infuse over approximately 30 minutes. A 0.2-micron polyethersulfone (PES) filter is recommended if filtration is used.

### Dose Modifications for Ocular Toxicity
Dosage must not be re-escalated after a reduction for ocular toxicity.
*   **Mild (Grade 1):** Continue at the current dose.
*   **Moderate (Grade 2):** Withhold until improvement to Grade 1 or better; resume at Reduced Dose Level 1 (1.9 mg/kg every 3 weeks).
*   **Severe (Grade 3):** Withhold until improvement to Grade 1 or better; resume at Reduced Dose Level 1 or 2 (1.9 mg/kg every 8 weeks) depending on the regimen.
*   **Corneal Ulcer (Grade 4):** Consider permanent discontinuation.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most common adverse reactions (reported in ≥20% of patients) across monotherapy and combination trials include:
*   **Ocular:** Keratopathy (corneal epithelium changes), decreased visual acuity, blurred vision, dry eye, and photophobia.
*   **Hematologic:** Thrombocytopenia, anemia, neutropenia, and lymphopenia.
*   **Systemic:** Fatigue, pyrexia, and infusion-related reactions.
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea and nausea.
*   **Infections:** Upper respiratory tract infections, pneumonia, and COVID-19.
*   **Laboratory Changes:** Increased aspartate aminotransferase (AST) and gamma-glutamyl transferase (GGT).

---

## 7. Drug Interactions
No formal clinical drug-drug interaction studies have been conducted. In vitro data indicates that the cys-mcMMAF component is a substrate of OATP1B1 and OATP1B3, as well as several multidrug resistance-associated proteins (MRP1, 2, and 3). However, clinical population pharmacokinetic analyses suggest that co-administration with bortezomib, pomalidomide, or dexamethasone does not significantly alter the pharmacokinetics of belantamab mafodotin.

---

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

### Pregnancy
**Category D [TGA].** Based on its mechanism of action (genotoxic microtubule inhibition), belantamab mafodotin can cause fetal harm. There are no data on use in pregnant women. 
*   **Pregnancy Testing:** Verify pregnancy status in females of reproductive potential prior to starting treatment.
*   **Contraception:** Females should use effective contraception during treatment and for 4 months after the final dose. Males with female partners of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for 6 months after the final dose.

### Breastfeeding
It is unknown if the drug is excreted in human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in the infant, patients should be advised to cease breastfeeding during treatment and for 3 months following the last dose.

---

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** The steady-state volume of distribution is approximately 10.8 L.
*   **Elimination:** The initial systemic clearance is 0.901 L/day, which decreases to 0.605 L/day at steady state. The elimination half-life is 13 days after the first dose and 17 days at steady state.
*   **Special Populations:** No clinically significant differences in pharmacokinetics were observed based on age, sex, race, or body weight (37–170 kg). No dose adjustment is required for patients with mild or moderate renal impairment, or mild hepatic impairment. Data are insufficient for severe renal or hepatic impairment.

---

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Belantamab mafodotin is a BCMA-directed ADC. Upon binding to BCMA on the surface of myeloma cells, the conjugate is internalized. Proteolytic cleavage releases cys-mcMMAF, which disrupts the microtubule network, leading to cell cycle arrest and apoptosis. The afucosylated antibody component also triggers tumor cell death via antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) and antibody-dependent cellular phagocytosis (ADCP).

---

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Vials:** Store in original packaging to protect from light. Refrigerate at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze.
*   **Reconstituted Solution:** May be stored for up to 4 hours at room temperature (20°C to 25°C) or in the refrigerator (2°C to 8°C).
*   **Diluted Solution:** May be refrigerated (2°C to 8°C) for up to 24 hours. Once at room temperature, it must be administered within 6 hours (including infusion time). Do not freeze or shake.
