# Editorial Product Information: Bulevirtide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) documentation from the following jurisdiction:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: HEPCLUDEX (bulevirtide) Product Information.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer**: This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidance required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory approved labelling for the most current information.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Bulevirtide acetate (a synthesized, N-terminally myristoylated lipopeptide consisting of 47 amino acids).
- **Chemical Class**: Hepatitis B virus (HBV) and Hepatitis D virus (HDV) entry inhibitor.
- **Brand Name(s)**: Hepcludex [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s)**: 
    - 2 mg lyophilized powder for injection in a single-use vial [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration**: Subcutaneous injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Gilead Sciences [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Hepatitis Delta Virus (HDV) Infection**: Indicated for the treatment of chronic HDV infection in adults with compensated liver disease [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult)**: The recommended dose is 2 mg administered once daily [Australian].
- **Duration of Treatment**: The optimal duration is currently unknown. Treatment should continue as long as clinical benefit is observed. Discontinuation may be considered upon sustained (6 months) HBsAg seroconversion [Australian].
- **Concomitant Therapy**: Underlying HBV infection should be managed simultaneously according to clinical appropriateness [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions**:
    - Administered via subcutaneous injection into the upper thigh or lower abdomen.
    - Injection sites should be rotated with each dose.
    - **Reconstitution**: Aseptically reconstitute the 2 mg vial with 1 mL of Sterile Water for Injection. Gently tap and roll the vial (up to 3 minutes) until the powder is dissolved. Do not shake. The solution should be clear and free of particles [Australian].
- **Missed Dose Instructions**: If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as possible on the same day. If it is nearly time for the next scheduled dose, the missed dose should be skipped. Do not double the dose [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- None identified in current official documentation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatitis Exacerbation Post-Discontinuation**: Severe acute exacerbations of HDV and HBV may occur following treatment cessation. The risk is heightened in patients with cirrhosis, potentially leading to hepatic decompensation. 
    - **Monitoring**: Close clinical and laboratory monitoring of hepatic function is required for at least six months after stopping bulevirtide. Antiviral therapy may need to be resumed if flares occur [Australian].
- **Dizziness**: Dizziness has been reported; patients should be advised of this regarding their ability to drive or operate machinery [Australian].
- **Monitoring Requirements**: Regular assessment of liver function and viral markers is essential.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reaction frequencies are based on pooled data from Phase 2 and Phase 3 clinical trials (MYR203, MYR301) [Australian].

| System Organ Class | Adverse Reaction | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hepatobiliary** | Total bile salts increased | Very Common (≥10%) |
| **Nervous System** | Headache | Very Common (≥10%) |
| **Skin/Subcutaneous** | Pruritus | Very Common (≥10%) |
| **General/Site** | Injection site reactions (erythema, pain, swelling, etc.) | Very Common (≥10%) |
| **General/Site** | Fatigue, Influenza-like illness | Common (1–10%) |
| **Nervous System** | Dizziness | Common (1–10%) |
| **Blood/Lymphatic** | Eosinophilia | Common (1–10%) |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea | Common (1–10%) |
| **Musculoskeletal** | Arthralgia | Common (1–10%) |

- **Bile Salt Elevations**: Asymptomatic increases in total bile salts are very common and relate to the mechanism of action (NTCP inhibition). These typically resolve upon treatment discontinuation. Elevations may be more pronounced in patients with renal impairment [Australian].
- **Postmarketing Experience**: Hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis, have been identified during post-approval use [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Profile**: Bulevirtide is a linear peptide expected to be catabolized into amino acids by peptidases; no active metabolites are expected [Australian].
- **Transporter Interactions**: 
    - **OATP1B1/1B3**: In vitro studies show inhibition of these transporters (IC50 0.5 µM and 8.7 µM, respectively). However, at the 2 mg dose, no clinically relevant interactions with OATP1B1/3 substrates are expected [Australian].
    - No clinically relevant interactions are predicted for efflux transporters (MDR1, BCRP, BSEP, MATE1/2K) or other uptake transporters (OATP2B1, OAT1/3, OCT1/2) [Australian].
- **CYP Enzymes**: Bulevirtide does not inhibit or induce major CYP450 enzymes (1A2, 2A6, 2B6, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 3A4) [Australian].
- **Tenofovir (TDF)**: No significant pharmacokinetic interaction was observed when co-administered [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **Category B1 [Australian]**: Animal studies (rats/rabbits) showed no adverse effects on embryofetal development at exposures significantly higher than the human dose (12- to 124-fold). 
    - Human data are lacking. Use only if potential benefits outweigh potential fetal risks [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding**: It is unknown if bulevirtide is excreted in human milk. A clinical decision should be made to either discontinue breastfeeding or the drug, considering the benefit of nursing to the infant and therapy to the mother [Australian].
- **Fertility**: No effects on fertility or early embryonic development were observed in animal studies [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients under 18 years of age [Australian].
- **Elderly Use**: No data are available for patients over 65 years; no specific dosing recommendations are provided [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment**:
    - **Mild (CrCl 60–89 mL/min)**: No dose adjustment required.
    - **Moderate to Severe (CrCl < 60 mL/min)**: Not evaluated; use with caution [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment**:
    - **Mild (Child-Pugh A)**: No dose adjustment required.
    - **Moderate to Severe (Child-Pugh B/C)**: Not evaluated; the safety/efficacy in decompensated liver disease is unknown [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Bulevirtide binds to and inactivates the Sodium/Bile acid Cotransporter Polypeptide (NTCP), the essential entry receptor for HBV and HDV on hepatocytes, thereby blocking viral entry and intrahepatic spread [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Peak plasma concentration (Tmax) occurs between 0.5 to 3 hours. Bioavailability is non-linear and estimated at 48–57% for higher doses (5–10 mg) [Australian].
    - **Distribution**: Highly protein-bound (>99.9%). Mean apparent volume of distribution is approximately 133 L [Australian].
    - **Elimination**: Apparent systemic clearance is 12.8 L/h. Terminal half-life (t1/2) is 3 to 7 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies (MYR301, MYR202, MYR203)**:
    - **MYR301 (Phase 3)**: Evaluated 2 mg bulevirtide vs. delayed treatment. At Week 48, the combined response (undetectable HDV RNA or ≥ 2 log10 decrease and ALT normalisation) was achieved by 45% of the bulevirtide group vs. 2% in the delayed treatment group [Australian].
    - **Resistance**: No amino acid substitutions associated with reduced susceptibility or virologic resistance have been identified through Week 48 of treatment [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials**: Store in a refrigerator at 2°C to 8°C. Keep in the original carton to protect from light [Australian].
- **Reconstituted Solution**: Chemical and physical stability demonstrated for 2 hours at room temperature (up to 25°C). Microbiologically, immediate use is recommended [Australian].
- **Shelf Life**: Refer to the product packaging for the specific expiry date [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule**: Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
