# Editorial Product Information: Box Jellyfish Antivenom (Ovine)

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information provided by the following regulatory authority: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview intended for healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) approved in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians must consult the local PI for definitive prescribing and legal requirements within their region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Box jellyfish antivenom (ovine).
- **Chemical Class:** Antivenom (purified antibodies/fragments derived from immunised sheep).
- **Brand Name(s):** Box Jellyfish Antivenom [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Solution for Injection:** Each vial contains 20,000 units of antivenom [Australian]. 
    - **Excipients:** Phenol (2.2 mg), sodium chloride (8 mg), and water for injections (to 1 mL). Total plasma protein of ovine origin is $\le$ 100 mg per mL [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (preferred) or Intramuscular (if IV access is not practical) [Australian].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Seqirus Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Systemic Envenoming:** For the treatment of patients exhibiting manifestations of systemic poisoning from the box jellyfish (*Chironex fleckeri*) [Australian].
- **Local Involvement:** For patients with extensive local involvement causing extreme, intractable pain that is unresponsive to standard analgesic therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
The dose is identical for both adults and children [Australian].
- **Intravenous (Preferred):** 1 vial (20,000 units) [Australian].
- **Intramuscular (Alternate):** 3 vials (60,000 units) if intravenous administration is not practical [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **IV Administration:** Administer slowly by intravenous infusion after dilution.
    - **Dilution:** Standard dilution is 1 in 10 using Hartmann’s solution or 0.9% w/v Sodium Chloride. A 1 in 5 dilution may be used for patients at risk of fluid overload [Australian].
- **IM Administration:** Administer undiluted at three separate injection sites [Australian].
- **Critical Care Requirement:** Ideally, antivenom should be administered in an intensive care facility. In severe cases involving respiratory or cardiac arrest, resuscitation and first aid must be initiated immediately, with antivenom administered as soon as possible once resuscitation has commenced [Australian].

### Monitoring and Titration
- **Duration:** Patients must be monitored for a minimum of 6 hours following the conclusion of the infusion [Australian].
- **Adequacy of Dose:** The goal is to neutralise the venom. If the clinical response is inadequate, additional antivenom may be required [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Absolute Contraindications:** None [Australian].
- **Relative Contraindications:** The product should not be used without clear evidence of systemic envenoming or intractable pain [Australian]. Refer to Section 5 for precautions in patients with known allergies.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Anaphylaxis and Severe Allergic Reactions:** As an ovine-derived product, severe allergic reactions, including anaphylactic shock, may occur. Adrenaline must be prepared and ready for immediate use prior to administration [Australian].
- **Premedication:** The use of prophylactic adrenaline (0.25 mL of 1:1,000 subcutaneously) and intravenous antihistamines is controversial and not universally recommended [Australian].
- **Skin Testing:** Skin testing to predict allergic reactions is unreliable and should not be performed [Australian].
- **Serum Sickness:** Delayed serum sickness is common following the administration of large volumes of foreign protein. Symptoms include fever, rash, arthralgia, and lymphadenopathy, occurring days to weeks post-treatment [Australian].
- **Infectious Agents:** Because this product is prepared from animal plasma, the transmission of infectious agents cannot be entirely excluded, though there are no historical records of viral transmission with this product [Australian].
- **Fluid Overload:** Care should be taken during dilution, particularly in patients sensitive to volume [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse event frequencies are defined as Common ($\ge$ 1/100 to < 1/10).

| System Organ Class | Adverse Effect | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Immune System Disorders** | Allergic reactions, anaphylactic shock, delayed serum sickness | Common |
| **Skin and Subcutaneous Tissue** | Urticaria, rash | Common |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and serum sickness (which may rarely involve arthritis, nephritis, neuropathy, or vasculitis) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** No specific data available [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Safety in pregnant women has not been established. Clinical decision-making should involve weighing the risks of untreated envenoming against the potential risks of the antivenom [Australian]. (Australian Category: Not specified in source text).
- **Breastfeeding:** No information is available regarding use during lactation. The benefits of treatment should be weighed against the potential risks [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is the same as for adults [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No specific data available [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** The antivenom consists of specific antibodies that bind to and neutralise the toxins in the venom of *Chironex fleckeri* [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Box jellyfish venom contains toxins that target the myocardium and respiratory neuromuscular mechanisms, and cause dermatonecrosis. Antivenom administration can diminish pain and skin wheals within minutes if given IV soon after the sting [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** No specific information is available regarding the absorption, distribution, metabolism, or excretion of this antivenom [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Summary:** No pivotal clinical trial data are described in the source documentation [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store between 2°C and 8°C (Refrigerate) [Australian].
- **Handling:** Protect from light. Do not freeze [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging. The product contains no antimicrobial preservative and is for single-use only; discard any residue [Australian].
