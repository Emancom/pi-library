# Editorial Product Information: Berotralstat

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial synthesis: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview designed for healthcare professionals to provide a single, coherent reference regarding the clinical use of berotralstat.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, legally approved PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the local approved labelling for the specific country in which they practice.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Berotralstat hydrochloride (a plasma kallikrein inhibitor).
- **Brand Name:** **ORLADEYO** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Capsules:** 110 mg and 150 mg [US].
    - **Oral Pellets:** 72 mg, 96 mg, 108 mg, and 132 mg in unit-dose packets [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** BioCryst Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hereditary Angioedema (HAE) Prophylaxis:** Indicated for routine prophylaxis to prevent attacks of HAE in adult and pediatric patients aged 2 years and older [US-approved].
- **Limitations of Use:** Berotralstat should not be used for the treatment of acute HAE attacks [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Adults and Pediatrics (12 years and older):** 150 mg once daily taken orally with food [US].
- **Pediatrics (2 to less than 12 years):** Dosing is based on body weight using oral pellets [US]:
    - **12 kg to < 24 kg:** 72 mg once daily.
    - **24 kg to < 32 kg:** 96 mg once daily.
    - **32 kg to < 40 kg:** 108 mg once daily.
    - **$\ge$ 40 kg:** 132 mg once daily.

### Administration Instructions
- **Capsules:** Take once daily with food [US].
- **Oral Pellets:** 
    - A meal should be consumed immediately before or after dosing [US].
    - Do not chew or crush pellets as this disrupts the taste-masking film coating, resulting in a bitter taste [US].
    - **Direct Administration:** Pour packet contents directly into the mouth and swallow immediately with a non-acidic liquid (e.g., water or milk) [US].
    - **Food Sprinkle:** Sprinkle over approximately 1 tablespoon (15 mL) of soft, non-acidic food (e.g., pudding, mashed potatoes, applesauce is excluded) and consume within 10 minutes [US]. 
    - **Restriction:** Do not use acidic foods (e.g., yogurt, applesauce) as they may dissolve the film coating [US].

### Dosing Modifications
- **Persistent GI Adverse Reactions:** For patients $\ge$ 12 years, consider reducing the dose to 110 mg once daily if gastrointestinal reactions persist [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- None identified in the provided source material [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **QTc Interval Prolongation:** Berotralstat may cause concentration-dependent prolongation of the QTc interval at doses higher than recommended (e.g., > 150 mg). Patients should be cautioned not to exceed the prescribed once-daily dose [US].
- **Acute HAE Attacks:** The drug is not established as a treatment for acute attacks. Standard rescue medications should remain available [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions typically occur early after treatment initiation, become less frequent over time, and are generally self-resolving [US].

### Common Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Reaction [US] |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Abdominal pain (23% at 150mg), Vomiting (15%), Diarrhea (15%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Back pain (10%), Gastroesophageal reflux disease (5%), Headache (9%), Fatigue (6%), Flatulence (6%) |

### Serious/Specific Adverse Effects
- **Hepatic Effects:** Asymptomatic transaminase elevations (ALT >8x ULN and AST >3x ULN) have been reported, leading to discontinuation in some cases [US].
- **Dermatologic:** Maculopapular rash occurred in <1% of patients [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **P-gp Inducers:** Avoid concomitant use (e.g., rifampin, St. John’s wort), as these may decrease berotralstat plasma concentrations and reduce efficacy [US].
- **CYP3A4 and CYP2D6 Substrates:** Berotralstat is a moderate inhibitor of CYP3A4 and CYP2D6. Concomitant use increases the exposure of substrates. Close monitoring or dose adjustment of the substrate is recommended, especially for drugs with a narrow therapeutic index [US].
- **P-gp Substrates:** Berotralstat is a P-gp inhibitor. Monitor or modify the dosage of P-gp substrates (e.g., digoxin) when used concomitantly [US].
- **Desogestrel:** Concomitant use increases exposure to etonogestrel (active metabolite), potentially increasing the risk of adverse reactions [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified by letter, but the risk summary indicates insufficient data in humans. Animal studies (rats/rabbits) at doses up to 10 and 2 times the maximum recommended human daily dose (MRHDD) showed no evidence of structural alterations or developmental toxicity [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** No data is available regarding the presence of berotralstat in human milk. A risk-benefit assessment considering the mother's clinical need and potential effects on the infant is recommended [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats showed no effect on male or female fertility at doses approximately 2 times the MRHDD [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy are established for patients aged 2 years and older [US]. Safety has not been established in children under 2 years [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies included a limited number of patients $\ge$ 65 years. No overall differences in safety or efficacy were observed compared to younger patients [US].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Mild to Moderate:** No dose adjustment required [US].
    - **Severe:** Avoid use in pediatric patients 2 to <12 years. No dose adjustment required for patients $\ge$ 12 years [US].
    - **End-Stage Renal Disease (eGFR <15 mL/min):** Not recommended [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild (Child-Pugh A):** No dose adjustment required [US].
    - **Moderate/Severe (Child-Pugh B or C):** For patients $\ge$ 12 years, reduce dose to 110 mg once daily. For patients 2 to <12 years, avoid use [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Berotralstat is a plasma kallikrein inhibitor. By inhibiting the proteolytic activity of plasma kallikrein, it prevents the cleavage of high-molecular-weight kininogen (HMWK), thereby reducing the generation of bradykinin—the primary mediator of swelling and pain in HAE [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Concentration-dependent inhibition of plasma kallikrein was demonstrated in patients with HAE [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Median $T_{max}$ is 5 hours when taken with food. $C_{max}$ and AUC increase more than dose-proportionally [US].
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is approximately 99% [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolized by CYP2D6 and CYP3A4 with low turnover *in vitro* [US].
    - **Elimination:** Median half-life is approximately 93 hours. Primary excretion route is faecal (79%), with minimal urinary excretion (9%) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Trial 1 (APeX-2):** A 24-week, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 120 patients (aged $\ge$ 12). Berotralstat 150 mg QD resulted in a 44.2% reduction in the HAE attack rate compared to placebo ($p < 0.001$). Reductions were sustained through 48 weeks in the open-label portion [US].
- **Trial 3 (APeX-S):** A multicenter, single-arm, open-label study in 29 pediatric patients (aged 2 to <12). Pharmacokinetic and safety data supported the weight-based dosing for this population, with no new safety signals identified [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Pellets should be discarded if not consumed within 10 minutes of being sprinkled on food [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription-only medicine (Rx only) [US].
