## 3. Approved Indications
- Prophylactic management of asthma (maintenance therapy to control and prevent symptoms).
- Not indicated for immediate relief of acute bronchospasm or status asthmaticus.

(If any jurisdiction-specific additional indications were present, they are not specified in the available sources.)

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General dosing (adults)
- For mild to moderate asthma: 50 µg to 200 µg twice daily.
- For more severe asthma: doses up to 400 µg twice daily.
- Maximum recommended total daily dose: 800 µg.

### Paediatric dosing
- Children aged five years and over: recommended dose 50 µg twice daily.
- Safety and efficacy in children under five years: not established.

### Transfer / titration guidance
- Use regularly for maintenance even when asymptomatic; reduce gradually to the lowest effective dose once control is achieved.
- Doses can be titrated by switching between 50 µg and 100 µg actuations as required.
- When transferring from other inhaled corticosteroids, consider current effective dose and clinical state; symptomatic patients may require higher starting dose. A conversion table (approximate equivalence) is provided below:

  - CFC-beclometasone dipropionate 200–250 µg/day → beclometasone dipropionate 100 µg/day  
  - CFC-beclometasone dipropionate 400–500 µg/day → beclometasone dipropionate 200 µg/day  
  - CFC-beclometasone dipropionate 800–1000 µg/day → beclometasone dipropionate 400 µg/day  
  - CFC-beclometasone dipropionate 1200–1500 µg/day → beclometasone dipropionate 600 µg/day  
  - CFC-beclometasone dipropionate 1600–2000 µg/day → beclometasone dipropionate 800 µg/day

  - Budesonide (dry powder inhaler) equivalents: 200 → 200 µg; 400 → 400 µg; 800 → 800 µg; 1200 → 1200 µg; 1600–2000 → 1600–2000 µg  
  - Fluticasone (pressurised MDI) equivalents (approx): 100 → 100 µg; 200–250 → 200 µg; 400–500 → 400 µg; 600–750 → 600 µg; 1000 → 1000 µg

  (These conversions reflect comparative dosing information presented in the source and should be applied clinically with judgment.)

### Administration instructions
- Instruct patients on correct inhaler technique and adherence; poor technique reduces benefit.
- For patients unable to coordinate actuation with inhalation, use a breath-actuated device or substitute an appropriate breath-actuated inhaler.
- Patients should be advised to rinse their mouth with water after inhalation to reduce risk of oropharyngeal candidiasis and hoarseness.
- The formulation described delivers an extra-fine aerosol (very small particle size) with greater lung deposition and less oropharyngeal deposition than older CFC suspension formulations.
- Use of a spacer: a compatible spacer (specified in sources as AeroChamber Plus) may reduce oropharyngeal deposition without reducing lung deposition for the described formulation. If a different spacer or a different formulation is used, monitor patients for clinical loss of control.
- Patients using a spacer should breathe in and out after each actuation into the spacer with minimal delay. Clean spacer before first use and at least monthly (wash in warm water and detergent, allow to air dry; do not dry with cloth or rinse residual detergent away as per source guidance).

### Missed dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- **Not for relief of acute bronchospasm**. Patients requiring immediate relief should use a short-acting bronchodilator and seek medical review if control worsens.
- Asthma control should be assessed regularly (symptoms and lung function). Increasing use of β2-agonists may indicate worsening asthma and need for re-assessment and possible increase in anti-inflammatory therapy.
- Systemic effects of inhaled corticosteroids can occur at high doses or with prolonged use, including suppression of the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal (HPA) axis, Cushing’s syndrome, reduction in bone mineral density, growth retardation in children and adolescents, cataract and glaucoma, and psychological/behavioural effects (e.g., psychomotor hyperactivity, sleep disturbances, anxiety, depression, aggression). Titrate to the lowest effective dose.
- Monitor patients transferred from systemic corticosteroids closely for adrenal insufficiency; recovery of the HPA axis may be slow. Continue appropriate management of systemic steroid withdrawal and consider providing oral steroid cover for periods of stress or intercurrent illness where necessary.
- During transfer from systemic steroids, do not discontinue systemic therapy abruptly; withdrawal should be gradual and under supervision. Monitor for signs of adrenal insufficiency and, if present, consider resuming systemic steroids.
- Caution in patients with active or latent pulmonary tuberculosis; the effect on recurrent lung infections is not known.
- Propellant effects: the formulation described contains a hydrofluoroalkane propellant (norflurane). Animal data showed narcosis and increased sensitivity to arrhythmogenic effects of adrenaline at high exposure concentrations. Avoid excessive use because of potential hazards from the propellant in addition to corticosteroid overdosage.
- Visual disturbance: report of blurred vision and other visual disturbances have occurred with systemic and topical corticosteroids; consider ophthalmologic evaluation for cataract, glaucoma, or central serous chorioretinopathy if visual symptoms occur.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor growth in children and adolescents regularly; consider dose reduction to the minimum effective dose.
- Monitor adrenal function where clinically indicated (e.g., patients transferred from systemic steroids, prolonged high-dose therapy).
- Monitor for signs of systemic corticosteroid effects: Cushingoid features, bone mineral density changes, ocular effects.
- Monitor asthma control (symptoms, use of short-acting bronchodilators, lung function tests).

### Specific clinical precautions
- Use caution and monitor patients during periods of stress (surgery, severe infection).
- If paradoxical bronchospasm occurs, discontinue beclometasone dipropionate immediately and institute alternative therapy; treat acute bronchospasm with a short-acting bronchodilator.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- No clinically significant drug interactions have been associated with therapeutic doses of beclometasone dipropionate in the available sources.
- Propellant-related interaction: animal data indicate norflurane (HFA propellant) at high exposures can increase susceptibility to adrenaline-induced arrhythmia; avoid excessive inhaler use. (Clinical significance at therapeutic use was not demonstrated in available sources.)
- Management notes: monitor for additive systemic corticosteroid effects when co-administered with other corticosteroids (systemic or topical) and adjust therapy accordingly.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

### Pregnancy
- [Australian] Pregnancy category: B3.
- There is inadequate clinical evidence of safety in pregnancy. Animal studies with systemic administration at relatively high doses caused fetal abnormalities (including growth retardation and cleft palate) and inhalational studies with the norflurane-containing formulation in rats caused fetal growth retardation and adrenal changes.
- Recommendation: avoid use in pregnancy unless the expected maternal benefit outweighs potential risk to the fetus.
- For jurisdictions without specific category information in the available sources: Not specified in available sources.

### Breastfeeding
- Beclometasone dipropionate is probably excreted in human milk; however, inhaled doses are expected to result in low milk levels. Studies in lactating animals were not described in the available sources.
- Clinical decision: weigh potential benefit to the mother against possible risk to the infant.

### Fertility
- Potential effects on fertility have not been adequately investigated in animal studies.

## 11. Pharmacology

### Mechanism of action
- **Beclometasone dipropionate** is a synthetic glucocorticoid with anti-inflammatory activity. Inhaled therapy is intended to deliver topical glucocorticoid effects in the bronchial tree, inhibiting inflammatory cells and mediator release relevant to asthma pathogenesis.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Particle characteristics (as described for the formulation in the sources): extra-fine aerosol with Mass Median Aerodynamic Diameter (MMAD) approximately 0.8–1.2 µm, resulting in greater deposition in central and peripheral airways and reduced oropharyngeal deposition compared with older chlorofluorocarbon (CFC) suspension formulations (CFC-BDP MMAD ~3.5–4 µm) and dry powder formulations (MMAD ~10 µm).
- Radiolabelled deposition studies (formulation described) demonstrated >55% of dose ex-actuator deposited in the lungs and <35% in the oropharynx; by contrast, CFC suspension formulations deposited approximately 4–7% in the lungs and >90% in oropharynx.
- Systemic exposure and effect on HPA axis: in clinical pharmacodynamic studies, mean 24-hour urinary free cortisol and plasma cortisol remained within normal ranges across doses up to 800 µg/day in trial subjects; however, dose-related reduction in urinary cortisol correlated with beclometasone dose. The lowest dose producing HPA suppression, bone mineral density effects, or growth retardation has not been established.
- Absorption and elimination specifics (bioavailability, Tmax, half-life, clearance): Not specified in available sources beyond statements about rapid elimination of the propellant and plasma cortisol/ACTH test results.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Anti-inflammatory effects reduce airway inflammation, leading to improved control of asthma symptoms and pulmonary function. Clinical equivalence to older formulations was demonstrated at lower total daily doses with this extra-fine formulation (approximate conversion ratio cited below).

## 12. Clinical Trials

### Pharmacodynamic study (steroid-naïve subjects)
- 43 steroid-naïve asthmatics received placebo, 200, 400, or 800 µg/day of the extra-fine beclometasone formulation, or 800 µg/day of CFC-BDP for 14 days. Results: dose-dependent reductions in 24-hour urinary free cortisol and correlation between dose and plasma beclomethasone; mean 24h-UFC remained within normal range for all dosing regimens. 800 µg/day produced similar reductions in urinary free cortisol as 800 µg/day of CFC-BDP.

### Short-term efficacy/safety trials
- Two 12-week trials in patients with symptomatic moderate (n=347) and symptomatic moderately severe (n=233) asthma monitored plasma cortisol as a safety parameter; mean changes and number of patients below normal reference were similar between placebo, 800 µg/day beclometasone, and 800 µg/day CFC-BDP in these studies.

### Long-term comparative trial
- A 12-month study (n≈473) compared beclometasone extra-fine formulation at doses 200–800 µg/day with CFC-BDP at doses 400–1600 µg/day. Adrenal function assessed by plasma cortisol and cosyntropin stimulation showed no differences in mean plasma cortisol levels or clinically significant cortisol changes between groups.

### Efficacy vs older formulations
- Comparative clinical evidence indicates that equivalent pulmonary function and symptomatic control can be achieved with the extra-fine formulation at lower total daily doses compared with CFC suspension beclometasone; clinical equivalence was demonstrated when given at an approximate dose ratio of 2.5:1 (CFC-BDP:extra-fine formulation).

### Adverse event data
- Multiple-dose studies (6–12 weeks) and 12-month multicentre trials reported adverse events and frequencies as summarized in Section 7 (Adverse Effects). Tables in the original clinical reports provided trial-specific incidence; notable trial findings include inhalation site sensations, dysphonia, taste alterations, cough, pharyngitis, and headache among the most commonly reported events (generally in the low single-digit percentages).
