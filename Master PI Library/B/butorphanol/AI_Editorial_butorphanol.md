# Editorial Product Information: Butorphanol Tartrate

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of butorphanol tartrate nasal spray for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical and regulatory data to support safe prescribing and administration.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is intended for informational purposes and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Butorphanol tartrate; a synthetically derived opioid agonist-antagonist of the phenanthrene series.
- **Brand Names:** Not specified in available sources (Repackaged by Bryant Ranch Prepack).
- **ATC Code:** N02AF01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 
    - Nasal Spray, USP: 10 mg/mL aqueous solution in a 2.5 mL metered-dose bottle [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intranasal.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Apotex Inc. (Manufacturer); Bryant Ranch Prepack (Repackager) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Pain Management:** Indicated for the management of pain severe enough to require an opioid analgesic and for which alternative treatment options (e.g., non-opioid analgesics) are inadequate or not tolerated [US-approved].
- **Limitations of Use:** Due to risks of addiction, abuse, and misuse, butorphanol should not be used for extended periods unless pain remains severe and alternatives continue to be inadequate [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Standard Initial Dose:** 1 mg (one spray in one nostril).
- **Titration:** If adequate analgesia is not achieved within 60 to 90 minutes, an additional 1 mg dose may be administered.
- **Maintenance:** The 1 mg (initial) or 2 mg (titrated) dose sequence may be repeated every 3 to 4 hours as required for pain [US].

### Dosing by Indication
- **Severe Pain:** An initial dose of 2 mg (one spray in each nostril) may be used if the patient is able to remain recumbent (due to potential for high incidences of somnolence/dizziness). In this case, additional doses should not be given for 3 to 4 hours [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Priming:** The pump must be primed before initial use (up to 7–8 strokes until a fine spray appears). If the unit is not used for 48 hours or longer, it must be reprimed (1–2 strokes) [US].
- **Technique:** Patients should blow their nose gently, insert the tip approximately 1 cm into one nostril, close the other nostril, and pump the unit firmly while sniffing gently.

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Patients should take the next dose at the usual time; they should not take more than the prescribed dose [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Significant respiratory depression [US].
- Acute or severe bronchial asthma in an unmonitored setting or in the absence of resuscitative equipment [US].
- Known or suspected gastrointestinal obstruction, including paralytic ileus [US].
- Hypersensitivity to butorphanol tartrate, the preservative benzethonium chloride, or any other formulation excipients [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
- **Addiction, Abuse, and Misuse:** Butorphanol exposes users to the risks of opioid addiction, which can lead to overdose and death. Assess risk prior to prescribing and monitor regularly.
- **Life-Threatening Respiratory Depression:** Serious or fatal respiratory depression may occur, particularly during initiation or following dose increases.
- **Accidental Exposure:** Accidental ingestion or inhalation of even one dose, especially by children, can result in fatal overdose.
- **Neonatal Opioid Withdrawal Syndrome (NOWS):** Prolonged use during pregnancy can result in life-threatening withdrawal in the neonate.
- **Cytochrome P450 3A4 Interactions:** Concomitant use with CYP3A4 inhibitors or discontinuation of CYP3A4 inducers may increase butorphanol levels, potentially causing fatal respiratory depression.
- **Risks from Concomitant Use with Benzodiazepines or CNS Depressants:** Concomitant use may result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death.

### General Precautions
- **Opioid-Induced Hyperalgesia (OIH) and Allodynia:** Opioids may paradoxically cause increased pain sensitivity. This requires dose reduction or opioid rotation [US].
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Reported when used with serotonergic drugs. Symptoms include mental status changes, autonomic instability, and neuromuscular hyperactivity [US].
- **Adrenal Insufficiency:** Reported with opioid use, usually following more than one month of treatment [US].
- **Increased Intracranial Pressure:** Use with caution in patients with head injury or brain tumours; $CO_2$ retention can further increase intracranial pressure [US].
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** Butorphanol increases cardiac workload (pulmonary artery pressure, vascular resistance). Use with caution in acute myocardial infarction or ventricular dysfunction [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience
The following frequencies are based on short-term and long-term trials [US]:

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Somnolence (49%), Dizziness (23%), Nasal congestion (13%*), Insomnia (11%*) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Nausea/Vomiting (8%), Asthenia/Lethargy, Headache, Sensation of heat, Pain, Hypertension, Hypotension, Constipation, Dry mouth, Anxiety, Euphoria, Blurred vision, Nasal irritation, Sweating, Pruritus |
| **Uncommon (<1%)** | Syncope, Hallucinations, Agitation, Dysphoria, Hostility, Withdrawal symptoms, Rash/Hives, Impaired urination |
*\*Reported frequently in long-term trials.*

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Life-threatening respiratory depression.
- Severe hypertension (rarely reported).
- Anaphylaxis (reported with formulation ingredients).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CNS Depressants:** Alcohol, benzodiazepines, and other sedatives increase the risk of respiratory depression and death [US].
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors/Inducers:** Macrolides (e.g., erythromycin), azole antifungals, and protease inhibitors may increase butorphanol levels. Rifampin and phenytoin may decrease efficacy or precipitate withdrawal [US].
- **Serotonergic Drugs:** SSRIs, SNRIs, triptans, and MAOIs increase the risk of Serotonin Syndrome [US].
- **Nasal Vasoconstrictors:** Oxymetazoline does not affect the fraction of butorphanol absorbed but slows the rate of absorption; a slower onset of action may occur [US].
- **Cimetidine:** No significant pharmacokinetic interaction observed [US].
- **Sumatriptan:** Analgesic effect may be diminished if administered within 1 minute of nasal sumatriptan; no significant effect if separated by 30 minutes [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category C [US]:** There are no adequate/controlled studies in pregnant women before 37 weeks gestation. Animal studies showed increased stillbirths and post-implantation loss at high doses.
    - **Labor/Delivery:** Not recommended. Opioids cross the placenta and may cause neonatal respiratory depression.
- **Breastfeeding:** Butorphanol is excreted in human milk. Following IM administration (2 mg QID), the infant dose is estimated at 4 mcg/L, which is considered clinically insignificant; however, infants should be monitored for sedation and respiratory depression [US].
- **Fertility:** Chronic opioid use may cause reduced fertility in both sexes due to effects on the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in patients <18 years; use is not recommended [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Patients $\ge$65 years have increased sensitivity and a 25% increase in half-life (to >6 hours). Initial dosing should be limited to 1 mg, with a minimum 6-hour interval between repeat dose sequences [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** In patients with $CrCl <30$ mL/min, the half-life is doubled. Use caution and limit dosing to 1 mg with a minimum 6-hour interval [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Half-life is tripled in patients with hepatic impairment. Use caution; limit dosing to 1 mg with a minimum 6-hour interval [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Partial agonist at the mu ($\mu$) opioid receptor and a full agonist at the kappa ($\kappa$) opioid receptor. Analgesia is the principal therapeutic effect.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** 
    - **Onset:** Within 15 minutes for nasal administration.
    - **Peak Activity:** 1 to 2 hours.
    - **Duration:** 4 to 5 hours.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is 60–70%.
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is 305–901 L; protein binding is ~80%. Crosses the blood-brain and placental barriers.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively hepatic. Major metabolite is hydroxybutorphanol; minor metabolite is norbutorphanol.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (70–80%), with ~15% in faeces.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Postoperative Pain:** 1 mg and 2 mg nasal doses provided analgesia similar to 37.5 mg and 75 mg IM meperidine, respectively. Median duration was 2.5 hours (1 mg) and 3.5 hours (2 mg).
- **Migraine Headache:** Two 1 mg doses (1 hour apart) were compared to 10 mg IM methadone; butorphanol provided significant relief within 15 minutes with a median duration of 6 hours.
- **Dose-Response:** In migraine trials, a 2 mg initial dose provided similar activity to a 1 mg dose but was associated with a higher incidence of adverse effects (nausea, vomiting, dizziness) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Butorphanol is an open delivery system; avoid aerosolization during priming to prevent healthcare worker exposure.
- **Disposal:** Dispose of unused units by unscrewing the cap, rinsing the bottle, and placing parts in a waste container out of reach of children [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule/Legal Classification:** 
    - **United States:** Schedule IV (CIV) controlled substance; Rx-only.
