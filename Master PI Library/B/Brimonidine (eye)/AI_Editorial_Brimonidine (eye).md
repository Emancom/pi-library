# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: US (Food and Drug Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical and regulatory information to facilitate safe and effective prescribing.
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) mandated for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corp) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (over 2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** In acute metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Titration:** Adjustments should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine levels. Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of treatment [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) when mixed in PVC bags with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. Treatment must be discontinued immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In those with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels and monitor weekly/monthly. Free carnitine levels should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** The safety/efficacy of oral levocarnitine has not been established in renal impairment. High oral doses in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO) normally excreted in urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology (e.g., ESRD symptoms).
- **Very Common (>10%):** Injection site reaction (33–59%), pain (21–49%), headache (3–37%), hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), nausea (5–12%), vomiting (9–21%), pharyngitis (15–27%), and dizziness (10–18%) [US].
- **Common (1–10%):** Abdominal pain, fever, chest pain, flu syndrome, diarrhea, dyspepsia, hypercalcemia, and anemia [US].
- **Other Notable Effects:** 
    - Transient nausea and vomiting.
    - Body odor (less frequent) [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, and seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels at therapy initiation and following any dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specifically lettered, but follows standard risk text]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits using doses up to 3.8 times the human surface area-based dose showed no evidence of fetal harm or impaired fertility. Use during pregnancy only if clearly required [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations after exogenous administration. Weigh the benefits of maternal treatment against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No mutagenic effects were found in standard tests (Salmonella, yeast). Long-term animal carcinogenicity studies have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg); see Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. Monitoring of trough levels is required to prevent over-accumulation. Oral formulations should be avoided in severe renal impairment [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This process provides substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production in all tissues except the brain (primarily skeletal and cardiac muscle) [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Levocarnitine can promote the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated. This helps alleviate autointoxication from acyl-CoA compounds in patients with inborn metabolic errors [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is approximately 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis (trough) carnitine levels from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L. Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) showed dose-proportional increases in trough concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Note:** While levocarnitine effectively increases plasma carnitine levels in ESRD patients, the definitive impact on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, arrhythmias) has not been fully determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the product contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources (refer to local jurisdiction) [US].
