# Editorial Product Information: Basiliximab

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a clinical reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for full prescribing details and legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Basiliximab (chimeric murine/human monoclonal antibody of the IgG1κ subclass).
- **Brand Name:** **Simulect** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Powder for injection: 20 mg vial (sterile, freeze-dried).
    - Available as a vial only or a composite pack containing a 5 mL ampoule of water for injections [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) injection or infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Novartis Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Renal Transplantation:** Prophylaxis of acute organ rejection in patients undergoing renal transplantation. It is intended for use as part of an immunosuppressive regimen [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Standard Regimen:** Total dose of 40 mg, administered in two separate 20 mg doses.
- **Timing:** 
    - **First Dose:** Administer within 2 hours prior to transplantation surgery.
    - **Second Dose:** Administer 4 days after transplantation.
- **Clinical Prerequisite:** Basiliximab must only be administered if it is certain the patient will receive the graft and concurrent immunosuppression.
- **Withholding Treatment:** The second dose must be withheld if the patient experiences graft loss or severe hypersensitivity reactions to the first dose [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Reconstitution:** Add 5 mL of sterile water for injections to the 20 mg vial. Shake gently to dissolve.
- **Infusion:** May be diluted to ≥50 mL with 0.9% sodium chloride or 5% dextrose and administered over 20–30 minutes.
- **Bolus Injection:** May be administered as a bolus injection.
- **Compatibility:** Due to lack of compatibility data, always administer via a separate infusion line.
- **Storage After Reconstitution:** Use immediately if possible; otherwise, store at 2–8°C for up to 24 hours [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to **basiliximab** or any excipient in the formulation (potassium phosphate, sodium phosphate, sodium chloride, sucrose, mannitol, and glycine) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Expert Supervision:** Should only be prescribed by physicians experienced in post-transplant immunosuppressive therapy.
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Severe acute hypersensitivity (within 24 hours), including anaphylactoid reactions, may occur on initial exposure or re-exposure. Symptoms include rash, urticaria, hypotension, tachycardia, dyspnoea, pulmonary oedema, and capillary leak syndrome. Facilities must be equipped with resuscitation equipment (e.g., adrenaline). Discontinue permanently if a severe reaction occurs.
- **Risk Subgroup:** Patients whose initial immunosuppression was discontinued prematurely (e.g., due to abandoned transplant) may be at increased risk of hypersensitivity upon re-exposure for a subsequent transplant.
- **Malignancy and Infection:** As with all immunosuppressants, there is an increased risk of lymphoproliferative disorders (LPD), other malignancies, and opportunistic infections (e.g., CMV). Clinical trials showed no significant increase in these risks when adding basiliximab to standard regimens.
- **Vaccination:** Live vaccines should be avoided in immunosuppressed patients. Inactivated vaccines may be used, though the immune response may be diminished.
- **Cytokine Release Syndrome:** Suspected cases have been reported during post-marketing surveillance [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Basiliximab does not appear to increase the background rate of adverse events associated with organ transplantation and concurrent immunosuppressant use. In clinical trials, the pattern of events was similar between basiliximab and placebo.

### Adult Experience (≥20% frequency in trials)
| System Organ Class | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Constipation, nausea, abdominal pain, vomiting, diarrhoea |
| **Urinary** | Urinary tract infection |
| **Body as a whole** | Pain, fever |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, peripheral/general oedema |
| **Central Nervous System** | Headache, insomnia, tremor |
| **Haematological** | Anaemia |
| **Metabolic** | Hyperkalaemia, hypercholesterolaemia |
| **Respiratory** | Upper respiratory tract infection |
| **Skin/Surgical** | Surgical wound complications |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Cardiac (Non-Renal Use):** In small heart transplant trials (an unapproved indication), serious events including cardiac arrest (2.2%) and atrial flutter (1.9%) occurred more frequently with basiliximab than other induction agents [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis and capillary leak syndrome (Rare/Post-marketing).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Metabolic Interactions:** As an immunoglobulin, no cytochrome P450-mediated interactions are expected.
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **Azathioprine:** Addition of azathioprine to a regimen of cyclosporine and corticosteroids reduced basiliximab clearance by ~22%.
    - **Mycophenolate Mofetil (MMF):** Addition of MMF to a similar regimen reduced basiliximab clearance by ~51%.
- **Management:** Despite these clearance changes, no increase in adverse events or infections was observed in clinical trials, and no dose adjustment is required [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category D.** Basiliximab has potentially hazardous immunosuppressive effects. Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during treatment and for **4 months** after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if basiliximab is excreted in human milk. Due to potential risks to the neonate, breastfeeding is not recommended for **4 months** following the last dose [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - **Weight <35 kg:** Total dose of 20 mg (two doses of 10 mg).
    - **Weight ≥35 kg:** Total dose of 40 mg (two doses of 20 mg).
    - Clearance is reduced by ~50% in children (1–11 years) compared to adults.
- **Elderly Use:** Data are limited; however, no evidence suggests that patients >65 years require different dosing or experience different safety profiles than younger adults [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No specific dose adjustments are provided in the available source.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Basiliximab binds specifically to the **CD25 antigen** (IL-2 receptor alpha-chain) on the surface of activated T-lymphocytes. This prevents IL-2 from binding to its receptor, thereby inhibiting T-cell proliferation, the key signal for cellular transplant rejection.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Complete blocking of the IL-2 receptor is maintained while serum levels exceed **0.2 µg/mL**. Receptor expression typically returns to pre-therapy levels within 1–2 weeks after concentrations fall below this threshold.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Cmax (20 mg dose):** 7.1 ± 5.1 mg/L.
    - **Volume of Distribution (Vd):** 8.6 ± 4.1 L.
    - **Terminal Half-life:** 7.2 ± 3.2 days.
    - **Clearance:** 41 ± 19 mL/hr. Clearance is not significantly affected by age (20–69 years), gender, or race [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Dual Therapy Studies (B201 & B352):** Randomised, double-blind trials comparing basiliximab to placebo in combination with cyclosporine and corticosteroids. Basiliximab significantly reduced the incidence of biopsy-confirmed acute rejection at 6 and 12 months post-transplant (30–33% vs 44–46% for placebo).
- **Triple Therapy Studies (CHI INT 10 & 11):** Adding basiliximab to regimens including azathioprine or MMF also showed reductions in acute rejection, though statistical significance varied across studies [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vial Storage:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze) [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging. In Australia, specific shelf-life data is held on the ARTG public summary [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
