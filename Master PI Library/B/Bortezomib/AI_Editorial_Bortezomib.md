# Editorial Product Information: Bortezomib

## Editorial Note
- This editorial harmonises product information for **bortezomib**. The primary source document utilised is the Australian (TGA) Product Information for **BORTEZOM**. 
- Reference is also made to specific regional findings (e.g., Japanese data) as noted within the primary source. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Bortezomib, a modified dipeptidyl boronic acid.
- **Chemical Class:** Reversible inhibitor of the 26S proteasome.
- **Brand Names:** **BORTEZOM** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01XG01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile lyophilised powder for injection; 3.5 mg single-dose vial (contains 35 mg mannitol) [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) or Subcutaneous (SC).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pharmacor Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Multiple Myeloma (MM) - Previously Untreated:**
    - In combination with melphalan and prednisone for patients ineligible for high-dose chemotherapy [Australian].
    - As part of combination induction therapy prior to high-dose chemotherapy with autologous stem cell rescue for patients under 65 years [Australian].
- **Multiple Myeloma (MM) - Relapsed/Refractory:**
    - Monotherapy or in combination with dexamethasone for patients who have received at least one prior therapy and have progressive disease [Australian].
- **Mantle Cell Lymphoma (MCL) - Previously Untreated:**
    - In combination with rituximab, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, and prednisone for adult patients [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** The standard recommended dose is 1.3 mg/m² body surface area.
- **Administration:**
    - **IV:** Reconstituted to 1 mg/mL; administered as a 3-5 second bolus injection [Australian].
    - **SC:** Reconstituted to 2.5 mg/mL; injected into the thighs or abdomen. Injection sites must be rotated [Australian].
- **Indication-Specific Regimens:**
    - **MM (Untreated, Non-transplant):** Twice weekly (Days 1, 4, 8, 11, 22, 25, 29, 32) for four 6-week cycles, followed by once weekly (Days 1, 8, 22, 29) for five 6-week cycles [Australian].
    - **MM (Relapsed):** Twice weekly for 2 weeks (Days 1, 4, 8, 11) followed by a 10-day rest period (3-week cycle).
    - **Mantle Cell Lymphoma:** 1.3 mg/m² twice weekly for two weeks (Days 1, 4, 8, 11) followed by a 10-day rest (3-week cycle). Six cycles are typically recommended [Australian].
- **Dose Modifications:** Therapy must be withheld if Grade 3 non-haematological or Grade 4 haematological toxicities occur. Upon resolution, reinitiate at a 25% dose reduction (e.g., 1.3 mg/m² to 1.0 mg/m²; 1.0 mg/m² to 0.7 mg/m²) [Australian].
- **Safety Note:** At least 72 hours must elapse between consecutive doses of **bortezomib**.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to **bortezomib**, boron, or the excipient mannitol [Australian].
- **Bortezomib** is strictly contraindicated for intrathecal administration.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **FATAL ERROR POTENTIAL:** **Bortezomib** is for IV or SC use only. Inadvertent intrathecal administration has resulted in death [Australian].
- **Peripheral Neuropathy (PN):** Predominantly sensory; however, severe motor neuropathy has been reported. Patients with pre-existing PN should be monitored closely. SC administration may reduce the incidence of PN compared to IV [Australian].
- **Hypotension:** Orthostatic and postural hypotension are common. Use caution in patients with a history of syncope or those taking antihypertensives [Australian].
- **Cardiac Disorders:** Acute development or exacerbation of congestive heart failure and decreased LVEF have occurred. Monitor patients with cardiac risk factors [Australian].
- **Pulmonary Disorders:** Rare cases of pneumonitis, interstitial pneumonia, and ARDS (some fatal). A higher proportion of these events has been reported in Japan [Japanese].
- **Thrombocytopenia/Neutropenia:** Cyclical patterns of decrease and recovery are typical. Monitor CBC prior to each dose. Hold dose if platelets are <25,000/µL [Australian].
- **Tumour Lysis Syndrome:** High risk in patients with high tumour burden.
- **HBV Reactivation:** Screening for Hepatitis B is required before initiating therapy in combination with rituximab [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Nausea, diarrhoea, constipation, vomiting, fatigue, pyrexia, thrombocytopenia, anaemia, neutropenia, peripheral neuropathy (including sensory), headache, paraesthesia, appetite decreased, dyspnoea, and rash [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Hypotension, dizziness, dysgeusia, vision blurred, vertigo, abdominal pain, dyspepsia, stomatitis, myalgia, arthralgia, and bone pain [Australian].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:**
    - Heart failure, pulmonary hypertension, and cardiac arrest [Australian].
    - Reversible Posterior Leukoencephalopathy Syndrome (RPLS/PRES).
    - Thrombotic Microangiopathy (TTP/HUS).
    - Acute liver failure (rare).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Co-administration with potent inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, ritonavir) can increase **bortezomib** exposure by approximately 35%. Monitor for toxicity [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Co-administration with potent inducers (e.g., rifampicin, St John’s Wort, carbamazepine) may reduce efficacy by up to 45%. Use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Hypoglycaemics:** Both hypo- and hyperglycaemia have been reported in diabetic patients. Glucose monitoring and dose adjustment of antidiabetic agents may be necessary [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Potential for foetal harm. Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during treatment.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if **bortezomib** is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding is advised against due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in infants [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies suggest potential effects on both male and female fertility (degenerative changes in testes and ovaries) [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in children [Australian].
- **Elderly:** No specific dose adjustments are required; however, clinical trials show consistent efficacy across age groups (including those ≥65 years) [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Pharmacokinetics are not significantly influenced by mild to moderate impairment. Dosing in severe renal impairment (CrCl < 20 mL/min/1.73m²) is not fully established. Administer after dialysis as the procedure may reduce drug concentrations [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Mild impairment does not require adjustment. Patients with moderate to severe impairment should start at a reduced dose of 0.7 mg/m² for the first cycle, with subsequent adjustments based on tolerance [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Reversible inhibitor of the chymotrypsin-like activity of the 26S proteasome. This prevents the degradation of ubiquitinated proteins, disrupting signalling cascades and leading to cell death in cancer cells.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Widely distributed to peripheral tissues. Plasma protein binding is approximately 83%.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily oxidative metabolism via CYP3A4, 2C19, and 1A2. Deboronation is the major metabolic pathway.
    - **Elimination:** Elimination half-life ranges from 40 to 193 hours following multiple dosing. Clearance is more rapid after the first dose [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **APEX Study:** Demonstrated superiority of **bortezomib** over high-dose dexamethasone in relapsed MM, showing significantly longer time to progression (TTP) and improved overall survival (OS) [Australian].
- **VISTA Study:** Confirmed efficacy of **bortezomib** + melphalan + prednisone (VcMP) in untreated MM patients ineligible for transplant, showing superior TTP and OS compared to MP alone [Australian].
- **LYM-3002:** Showed a 59% improvement in progression-free survival (PFS) for the VcR-CAP regimen over R-CHOP in untreated Mantle Cell Lymphoma [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Vials:** Store below 25°C. Keep in the original carton to protect from light [Australian].
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Contains no antimicrobial preservative. Use immediately. If storage is necessary, the solution is stable for up to 24 hours at 2°C–8°C [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **S4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
