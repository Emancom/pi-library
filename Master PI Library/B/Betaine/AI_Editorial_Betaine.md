# Editorial Product Information: Betaine Anhydrous

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical data into a single, concise reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific prescribing requirements in their region. Information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., US, European, Canadian) was not available in the provided source material.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Betaine anhydrous (also known as trimethylglycine).
- **Chemical Class:** A metabolite of choline; methyl group donor.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Cystadane** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** White, granular oral powder. 1 g of powder contains 1 g of betaine anhydrous (100% w/w) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Recordati Rare Diseases Australia Pty. Ltd. [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Betaine anhydrous** is indicated as an adjunctive treatment for homocystinuria. It is used to reduce elevated blood homocysteine levels in patients of all ages with:
- Cystathionine beta-synthase (CBS) deficiency.
- 5, 10-methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase (MTHFR) deficiency.
- Cobalamin cofactor metabolism defect (cbl defect).

Additionally, it is indicated to increase methionine and S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) levels in patients with MTHFR deficiency and cbl defects [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Paediatrics)
- **Standard Dose:** 6 grams per day, administered in divided doses of 3 grams twice daily [Australian].
- **Maximum Dose:** Up to 20 grams per day may be required in some patients to achieve homocysteine control [Australian].

### Paediatric-Specific Dosing
- **Children < 3 years:** Start at 100 mg/kg/day. Increase the dose weekly by 100 mg/kg increments until the target response is reached [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- The powder must be measured using the provided scoops:
    - **Green scoop:** 100 mg
    - **Blue scoop:** 150 mg
    - **Pink scoop:** 1 g
- Measurements should be "heaped" and then leveled off with a flat surface (e.g., the back of a knife).
- Dissolve the measured dose in 120–180 mL of water and ingest immediately [Australian].

### Monitoring and Titration
- Dosage should be titrated until plasma homocysteine is undetectable or present only in minimal amounts.
- Response typically occurs within one week; steady state is usually reached within one month [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Hypermethioninemia
- Patients with **CBS deficiency** may experience significantly elevated plasma methionine levels due to the remethylation of homocysteine. 
- **Cerebral oedema** has been reported in a small number of patients treated with betaine, secondary to severe hypermethioninemia.
- **Monitoring Requirement:** Plasma methionine concentrations must be monitored in CBS-deficient patients. Levels should be maintained below 1,000 µmol/L via dietary restriction and, if necessary, dose reduction of betaine [Australian].

### General Precautions
- Always ensure the container cap is replaced tightly after use to protect the powder from moisture [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally described as minimal.

### Gastrointestinal
- **Common (estimated):** Nausea, diarrhoea, and general gastrointestinal distress [Australian].

### Neurological/Metabolic
- **Rare:** Cerebral oedema, specifically in patients with CBS deficiency who develop severe hypermethioninemia [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** None known [Australian].
- **Concomitant Therapy:** Betaine can be safely administered alongside other treatments for homocystinuria, including folate, vitamin B6 (pyridoxine), and vitamin B12 (cobalamin) [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category C**. Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. It is unknown if betaine causes foetal harm or impacts reproductive capacity. Use during pregnancy only if clearly required [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if betaine is excreted in human milk, though its precursor, choline, is found in high levels. Exercise caution when administering to nursing mothers [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Long-term studies evaluating the effects on fertility have not been conducted [Australian].

## 10. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Most clinical experience involves paediatric patients. The drug has been used successfully in infants (within the first months of life) at doses of 6 g/day or more without adverse effects, though titration is preferred [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Betaine acts as a methyl group donor in the remethylation of homocysteine to methionine. This pathway bypasses the primary defective enzymes (such as CBS) to reduce toxic homocysteine levels [Australian].

### Pharmacodynamics
- Reduces plasma homocysteine to approximately 20–30% of pre-treatment levels.
- In MTHFR and cbl defects, it increases plasma methionine and SAM, which may help prevent or treat demyelination [Australian].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption/Distribution:** Specific PK studies are not available [Australian].
- **Onset of Action:** Several days [Australian].
- **Steady State:** Achieved within several weeks [Australian].
- **Tolerance:** No evidence of tolerance has been observed after long-term use [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Data indicates that homocysteine plasma levels decreased in 98% of patients.
- Clinical improvement was noted in 77% of patients, and disease progression was halted in an additional 21%.
- Many responders had previously failed therapy with B6, B12, or folate [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store below 25 °C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].
- **Special Handling:** Keep container tightly closed; dispose of unused medicine via a pharmacy [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** Unscheduled [Australian].
