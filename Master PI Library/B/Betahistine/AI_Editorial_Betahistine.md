# Editorial Product Information: Betahistine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating available regulatory data into a single, clinically focused reference.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Betahistine dihydrochloride (an analogue of histamine).
- **Brand Name(s):** APO-BETAHISTINE [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 16 mg tablets (white to almost white, round, flat bevelled edged, embossed "B16", scored) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Meniere’s Syndrome:** Indicated for the management of the core symptoms of Meniere’s Syndrome, including:
    - Vertigo (accompanied by nausea and vomiting)
    - Hearing loss (hardness of hearing)
    - Tinnitus [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:** The recommended starting dose is 8 mg to 16 mg administered three times daily.
- **Maximum Daily Dose:** 48 mg [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets may be taken with or without food. To mitigate potential gastrointestinal upset, it is recommended that the medication be taken with meals.
- **Titration and Response:** Dosage should be individualised based on patient response. Symptomatic improvement may be noted within a few days, though in some cases, it may take several weeks [Australian].
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to betahistine dihydrochloride or any excipients (including lactose).
- **Phaeochromocytoma:** Contraindicated in patients with this condition [Australian].
- **Peptic Ulcer Disease:** Contraindicated in patients with active peptic ulcer or a history of the condition [Australian].
- **Pregnancy and Lactation:** Contraindicated for use during pregnancy and while breastfeeding [Australian].
- **Paediatrics:** Contraindicated in children under 18 years of age [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [Australian].
- **Bronchial Asthma:** Patients with a history of bronchial asthma require careful clinical monitoring during therapy due to the potential for bronchospasm [Australian].
- **Antihistamine Use:** Caution is advised when co-administering with antihistamines (see Section 7).
- **Driving and Operating Machinery:** While clinical studies in healthy volunteers showed negligible effects on driving ability, Meniere’s Syndrome itself can negatively impact the ability to drive or operate machinery [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are primarily associated with the gastrointestinal tract, nervous system, and skin.

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Nausea, dyspepsia, headache. |
| **Rare (0.1–1%)** | Vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal distension, bloating, epigastric pain; various rashes, pruritus, urticaria; dizziness; tiredness and malaise. |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Convulsions, somnolence, confusion, hallucinations; vasodilation, postural hypotension, tachycardia; dyspnoea, asthma, bronchospasm. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:**
    - **Immune System:** Anaphylaxis and angioneurotic oedema have been reported.
    - **Skin:** A single case of Stevens-Johnson syndrome has been documented [Australian].
    - **Neurological:** Convulsions and hallucinations (usually in patients with confounding factors).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic (MAOIs):** *In vitro* data suggest that drugs inhibiting monoamine oxidase (MAO), particularly MAO-B (e.g., selegiline), may inhibit the metabolism of betahistine. Caution is recommended during concomitant use [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamic (Antihistamines):** A theoretical antagonism exists between betahistine and antihistamines; however, no such interactions have been clinically reported in the available source [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category: B2.** 
    - Betahistine **must not be used** during pregnancy as there are insufficient data to evaluate potential harmful effects [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Betahistine **must not be used** during lactation [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal data regarding the effects of betahistine on fertility are not available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use is contraindicated in children under 18 years due to a lack of clinical experience and safety data [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific data not available; use with caution [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Specific data/dose adjustments not available [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Specific data/dose adjustments not available [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** The exact mechanism remains unknown. Animal studies suggest betahistine improves microcirculation in the inner ear (stria vascularis) by relaxing precapillary sphincters.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Betahistine acts as a weak H1 receptor agonist and a considerable H3 receptor antagonist in the central and autonomic nervous systems. It has shown a dose-dependent inhibitory effect on spike generation in vestibular nuclei in animal models [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly and completely absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract.
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly metabolised into its primary metabolite, 2-pyridylacetic acid.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 90% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The plasma half-life is approximately 3.4 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Summary:** Specific pivotal trial data were not provided in the source document.
- **Driving Study:** A study of 12 healthy volunteers indicated that betahistine had no or negligible effects on the ability to drive compared to placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Conditions:** Store in the original container to protect from light/moisture.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (S4) – Prescription Only Medicine [Australian].
- **Registration Number:** AUST R 217105 [Australian].
