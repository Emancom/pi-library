# Editorial Product Information: brompheniramine + pseudoephedrine + dextromethorphan

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **brompheniramine + pseudoephedrine + dextromethorphan**
- Chemical class:
  - Brompheniramine: H1-antihistamine (alkylamine class; anticholinergic, sedative properties)
  - Pseudoephedrine: sympathomimetic nasal decongestant (acts on α-sympathetic receptors)
  - Dextromethorphan: centrally acting antitussive
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 3. Approved Indications
- For relief of cough and upper respiratory symptoms, including nasal congestion, associated with allergy or the common cold. [US-approved]

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (oral)
- Adults and pediatric patients 12 years of age and over: 10 mL every 4 hours.
- Children 6 to under 12 years of age: 5 mL every 4 hours.
- Children 2 to under 6 years of age: 2.5 mL every 4 hours.
- Infants 6 months to under 2 years of age: dosage to be established by a physician.
- Do not exceed 6 doses during a 24-hour period.

### Dosing by indication
- Specific dosing by indication is not differentiated in available sources; the dosing above applies to symptomatic relief of cough and upper respiratory symptoms associated with allergy or common cold. Not specified in available sources.

### Administration instructions
- Administer orally at the recommended intervals.
- Patients should be cautioned about activities requiring mental alertness (see Warnings and Precautions).

### Titration schedules

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to brompheniramine, pseudoephedrine, dextromethorphan, or any component of the product.
- Do not use in the newborn or in premature infants.
- Do not use in nursing mothers. **(Contraindicated in nursing mothers per US labeling.)**
- Do not use in patients with severe hypertension or severe coronary artery disease.
- Do not use dextromethorphan in patients receiving monoamine oxidase (MAO) inhibitors. (Co‑administration should be avoided.)
- Antihistamines should not be used to treat lower respiratory tract conditions including asthma.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- None specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Especially in infants and small children, antihistamines in overdose may cause hallucinations, convulsions, and death.
- Antihistamines may diminish mental alertness; patients should be warned about engaging in activities requiring mental alertness, such as driving or operating dangerous machinery.
- In young children, antihistamines may cause paradoxical excitation.
- Use with caution in patients with a history of bronchial asthma, narrow-angle glaucoma, gastrointestinal obstruction, or urinary bladder neck obstruction (primarily related to the antihistamine/anticholinergic component).
- Use with caution in patients with diabetes, hypertension, heart disease, or thyroid disease (primarily related to sympathomimetic effects of pseudoephedrine).

### Monitoring requirements
- Specific monitoring parameters and schedules are not specified in available sources. Clinicians should monitor clinically relevant vital signs (e.g., blood pressure, heart rate) and symptoms as indicated based on the patient’s comorbidities and concurrent medications.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Avoid coadministration with MAO inhibitors because of risk of severe reactions (hyperpyrexia, hypotension, death) and potentiation of anticholinergic and sympathomimetic effects.
- Antihistamine components may potentiate CNS depression when used with alcohol or other CNS depressants.
- Sympathomimetic component may reduce the effectiveness of antihypertensive agents.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Major interactions
- Monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs)
  - Concomitant use of MAOIs and products containing dextromethorphan has been associated with hyperpyrexia, hypotension, and death. MAOIs also prolong and intensify anticholinergic effects of antihistamines and may potentiate the sympathomimetic effects of pseudoephedrine.
  - Concomitant administration should be avoided. (Also listed under Contraindications.)

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- CNS depressants (e.g., alcohol, hypnotics, sedatives, tranquilizers, antianxiety agents): additive sedative effects with the antihistamine component; patients should be warned about combined use.
- Antihypertensive drugs: sympathomimetic component (pseudoephedrine) may reduce antihypertensive efficacy.

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Specific pharmacokinetic interactions (e.g., CYP-based interactions) are not specified in available sources.

### Management notes
- In overdose presenting with CNS depression due to dextromethorphan, naloxone hydrochloride (0.005 mg/kg IV noted in source material) may be of value in reversing CNS depression.
- Hypertensive responses and tachycardia should be treated appropriately per standard clinical care.
- For convulsive seizures or CNS hyperactivity in overdose, intravenous short‑acting barbiturates may be indicated.
- Oxygen, intravenous fluids and other supportive measures should be used as indicated.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Pregnancy classification (US): Pregnancy Category C.
- Animal reproduction studies for the combination have not been conducted. It is not known whether the combination causes fetal harm when administered to a pregnant woman or affects reproductive capacity. The combination should be given to a pregnant woman only if clearly needed.
- Component data: Reproduction studies of brompheniramine (single component) in rats and mice at doses up to 16 times the maximum human doses showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm.

### Breastfeeding
- The product is contraindicated in nursing mothers due to higher risk of intolerance of antihistamines in small infants, particularly newborns and premature infants.

### Fertility
- Combination-specific fertility data are not available. Component data for brompheniramine: reproduction studies up to 16× human dose in animals showed no evidence of impaired fertility.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Safety and effectiveness in pediatric patients below the age of 6 months have not been established.
- Dosing by age: see Section 4 (Adults and pediatric patients 12 years and over; children 6–<12; children 2–<6; infants 6 months–<2 years: physician to establish dosing).
- Infants and small children are at higher risk of severe adverse effects (hallucinations, convulsions, death) with antihistamine overdose.

### Elderly use
- Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65 and over to determine whether they respond differently from younger subjects.
- Antihistamines are more likely to produce dizziness, sedation, and hypotension in elderly patients. The elderly are also more likely to experience adverse reactions to sympathomimetics.
- Dose selection for elderly patients should be cautious, usually starting at the low end of the dosing range, accounting for decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function and concomitant disease or other drug therapy.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Brompheniramine: H1‑receptor antagonist (antihistamine) of the alkylamine class; competes with histamine for receptor sites on effector cells and has anticholinergic (drying) and sedative effects. Antagonizes allergic responses in nasal tissue (vasodilation, increased vascular permeability, increased mucus secretion).
- Pseudoephedrine: sympathomimetic that acts on sympathetic nerve endings and smooth muscle; nasal decongestant effect mediated via α‑adrenergic receptor–mediated vasoconstriction of dilated nasal arterioles.
- Dextromethorphan: centrally acting antitussive that elevates the cough threshold; lacks analgesic or addictive properties.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Brompheniramine: well absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract; peak plasma concentration after a single 4 mg oral dose occurs at approximately 5 hours. Urinary excretion is the primary route of elimination, mainly as metabolites; liver is assumed the main site of metabolism.
- Pseudoephedrine: following oral administration, effects observed within ~30 minutes with peak activity at approximately 1 hour.
- Dextromethorphan: onset of antitussive action occurs in 15–30 minutes after administration and has a prolonged duration of action.
- Detailed parameters for distribution, protein binding, metabolic pathways, elimination half‑lives, and renal/hepatic clearance for the combination are not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Combined pharmacologic effects reflect H1 antagonism (antiallergic, anticholinergic, sedative), α‑adrenergic vasoconstriction (decongestant), and central antitussive effects.
