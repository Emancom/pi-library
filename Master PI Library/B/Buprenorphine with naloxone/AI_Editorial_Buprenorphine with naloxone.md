# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the **United States (FDA)**. Information from other major regulatory authorities (e.g., Australian TGA, European EMA) was not available in the provided source material for this synthesis.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acids.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Administration:** Administered intravenously via slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion [US].

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg.
- **Loading Dose:** Often administered in patients with severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Should be administered every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
- **Titration:** Subsequent daily doses are typically in the range of 50 mg/kg; however, doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered if clinically required [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration Timing:** Administered into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
- **Monitoring/Adjustment:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels <40–50 µmol/L. Dose adjustments (e.g., downward to 5 mg/kg) should be guided by trough concentrations and may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Monitoring
- Plasma carnitine concentrations should be obtained prior to therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- None specified in available sources.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These can occur within minutes of administration. Treatment should be discontinued immediately upon a severe reaction [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Formulation Caution:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- Regular monitoring of plasma carnitine levels and INR (in patients on warfarin) is required [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported at a frequency ≥5% in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials (regardless of causality) include [US]:

| Body System | Adverse Event | Frequency (10–40 mg/kg) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Injection site reaction, Pain, Flu syndrome, Headache, Chest pain, Fever | 7%–33% |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, Hypotension, Tachycardia | 6%–20% |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, Vomiting, Nausea, Abdominal pain | 8%–16% |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, Cough increase, Rhinitis, Dyspnea | 7%–23% |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, Paresthesia | 5%–13% |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, Hyperkalemia | 6%–9% |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis and laryngeal edema [US].
- **Digestive:** Transient nausea and vomiting; gastritis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed with concurrent use. **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified by letter, but noted as used only if clearly needed]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased carnitine concentrations in milk. The risks to the infant from excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefits to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is based on mg/kg body weight (see Section 3).
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in provided sources; however, general caution regarding renal function is prudent given renal excretion [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. Note the specific warning against high-dose **oral** levocarnitine due to metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine from 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L at baseline to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While increases in carnitine levels are proven, the effect on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservative [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Legal Classification:** Human Prescription Drug [US].
