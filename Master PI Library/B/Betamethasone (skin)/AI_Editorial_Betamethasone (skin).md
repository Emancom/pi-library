# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory information into a single, clinically actionable resource.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP); Chemical Class: Amino acid derivative / Carrier molecule.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):**
    - **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from congenital metabolic defects [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose may be given, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the next 24 hours (administered every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours) [US].
    - Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
    - Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - **Dose Adjustment:** Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect parenteral products visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

### Monitoring
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels before starting therapy.
- Monitor weekly and monthly, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target free carnitine range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at this time [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in dialysis patients. Some reactions occurred within minutes of intravenous administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate appropriate medical care [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a known seizure disorder, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been reported [US].
- **General Monitoring:** Chronic administration of high oral doses in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide); however, this is primarily a concern with oral formulations rather than the intravenous route [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Anaphylaxis/Hypersensitivity:** [US].
- **Seizures:** Reported in post-marketing experience [US].

### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported with a frequency ≥5% in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials (regardless of causality) [US]:

| Body System | Adverse Event | Levocarnitine (All Doses) | Placebo |

| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain, Asthenia, Chest pain, Fever, Headache, Infection, Injection site reaction, Pain | Common (7–33%) | 5–59% |

- **Other Reactions:** Transient nausea and vomiting, and body odor have been observed [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Clinical Note:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients on anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. However, there are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine intake must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother. Consideration should be given to discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing guidelines for metabolic disorders apply to paediatric patients (see Section 3) [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is specifically indicated for patients with ESRD on dialysis. Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrates for oxidation and subsequent ATP production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Levocarnitine clears accumulated acyl-CoA compounds (which can disrupt intermediary metabolism and cause life-threatening acidosis) by forming acylcarnitine, which is then rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following a 20 mg/kg IV bolus, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Study:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg IV three times per week showed that trough (pre-dialysis) levels increased from a baseline of 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks of therapy [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing.
- **Stability:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) when mixed in PVC bags with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].
