# Editorial Product Information: Bilastine

## Editorial Note
- The primary source for this editorial is the Australian Product Information (TGA). 
- **Note:** At the time of this synthesis, official Product Information documents from other jurisdictions (e.g., US FDA, European EMA, Health Canada, Japan PMDA) were not provided for comparison. Therefore, clinical recommendations and regulatory classifications are currently based on the **Australian** context.
- This document serves as a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Bilastine
- **Chemical Class:** Second-generation, non-sedating, long-acting histamine H1-receptor antagonist.
- **Brand Names:** **Allertine**, **Allertine Meltlets** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - Oral Tablet: 20 mg bilastine (oval, biconvex, scored white tablets) [Australian].
    - Orally Disintegrating Tablet (ODT): 20 mg bilastine (round, flat, white, engraved "20" on one side) [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** A. Menarini Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Allergic Rhinoconjunctivitis:** Symptomatic treatment of seasonal allergic rhinitis (SAR) and perennial allergic rhinitis (PAR) [Australian-approved].
- **Urticaria:** Symptomatic treatment of urticaria [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Adolescents ≥12 years):** The standard dose is 20 mg (one tablet or one ODT) once daily [Australian].
- **Dosing by Indication:**
    - **Allergic Rhinoconjunctivitis:** Limit treatment to the period of allergen exposure. For SAR, therapy may be discontinued upon symptom resolution and reinitiated upon recurrence. For PAR, continuous treatment may be maintained during exposure periods [Australian].
    - **Urticaria:** Duration of treatment depends on the clinical course and severity of symptoms [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Bilastine must be taken in a fasted state to ensure adequate bioavailability. 
    - Administer at least **1 hour before or 2 hours after** the intake of food or fruit juice [Australian].
    - **Standard Tablets:** Swallow whole with a glass of water [Australian].
    - **Meltlets (ODT):** Place in the mouth to disperse rapidly in saliva, then swallow. May be followed by water if preferred [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to bilastine or to any excipients included in the formulation (e.g., sucralose in Meltlets) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [Australian].
- **General Warnings:**
    - **CNS Effects:** While clinical trials showed somnolence rates similar to placebo, individual responses vary. Patients should determine their own response to bilastine before driving or operating machinery [Australian].
    - **Cardiac Safety:** Post-marketing reports have included rare instances of QT interval prolongation, though clinical studies at 10 times the therapeutic dose did not show significant effects [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment & Transporter Inhibitors:** Use caution when co-administering bilastine with P-glycoprotein (P-gp) inhibitors (e.g., cyclosporine, diltiazem) in patients with moderate or severe renal impairment, as this may significantly increase plasma levels and adverse reaction risks [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** The product is subject to additional monitoring in Australia [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The overall incidence of adverse events (AEs) is comparable to placebo (12.7% vs. 12.8%).

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Headache (15.61%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Somnolence (4.05%), Abdominal pain (2.65%), Dizziness (1.77%), Fatigue (1.40%), Nasopharyngitis (1.25%), Diarrhoea (1.18%), Pharyngolaryngeal pain (1.10%) |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Anxiety, insomnia, tinnitus, vertigo, right bundle branch block, sinus arrhythmia, QT prolongation, dyspnoea, nasal dryness, dry mouth, gastritis, pruritus, weight gain, increased liver enzymes (ALT, AST, GGT) |
| **Post-Marketing (Rare)** | Palpitations, tachycardia, hypersensitivity (anaphylaxis, angioedema), vomiting |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Food and Fruit Juice:** Concomitant intake reduces bilastine bioavailability by 25% to 30%. Grapefruit juice specifically inhibits OATP1A2-mediated uptake [Australian].
- **P-gp Inhibitors:** Co-administration with ketoconazole or erythromycin increases bilastine AUC 2-fold and Cmax 2-3 fold. Diltiazem increases Cmax by 50% [Australian].
- **OATP1A2 Substrates/Inhibitors:** Agents like ritonavir or rifampicin may decrease bilastine plasma concentrations [Australian].
- **Lorazepam:** Bilastine 20 mg does not potentiate the CNS depressant effects of lorazepam [Australian].
- **Alcohol:** No significant difference in psychomotor performance observed when bilastine is taken with alcohol [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B3.** 
    - Data in pregnant women are limited. Animal studies showed no effects on embryofetal development except at maternotoxic doses (rabbits). As a precaution, avoid use during pregnancy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** 
    - Excretion in human milk is unknown. In rats, milk concentrations were approximately half of maternal plasma levels. A risk-benefit assessment is required [Australian].
- **Fertility:** 
    - No effects on rat reproductive organs. In dogs, high doses (380x clinical exposure) showed reduced sperm motility and morphology, with no effect at 130x clinical exposure [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - Safety and efficacy not established in children <12 years. No data available for children ≤2 years [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** 
    - No dosage adjustment required. No significant PK or safety differences observed in patients >65 years compared to younger adults [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - No dosage adjustment required. However, exposure increases with the degree of impairment (AUC increase of ~130% in severe impairment). See **Section 5** regarding P-gp inhibitors [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - No dosage adjustment required. Bilastine is not metabolised and is primarily eliminated unchanged; hepatic dysfunction is not expected to significantly alter exposure [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Highly selective, peripheral H1-receptor antagonist. It lacks affinity for muscarinic receptors [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid; Tmax ~1.3 hours. Mean bioavailability is 61%.
    - **Distribution:** 84–90% plasma protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Negligible metabolism in humans; not a substrate for or inhibitor of CYP450 enzymes.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 95% excreted unchanged (28.3% in urine, 66.5% in faeces). Elimination half-life is ~14.5 hours [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** 20 mg dose provides 24-hour inhibition of wheal and flare reactions. Onset of action occurs within 1 hour [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Allergic Rhinoconjunctivitis (SAR/PAR):** Trials in over 2,000 patients demonstrated that bilastine 20 mg daily significantly reduced Total Symptom Scores (TSS) compared to placebo. Efficacy was comparable to cetirizine, desloratadine, and fexofenadine, with sustained 24-hour action [Australian].
- **Urticaria:** In a 28-day study of Chronic Idiopathic Urticaria (CIU), bilastine 20 mg significantly reduced TSS (wheal and itch scores) compared to placebo, showing efficacy similar to levocetirizine [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 2 (Pharmacy Medicine) [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Over-the-counter (OTC) availability in specific pack sizes/jurisdictions may vary; refer to local regulations.
