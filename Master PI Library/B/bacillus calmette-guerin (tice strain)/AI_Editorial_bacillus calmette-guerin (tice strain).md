# Editorial Product Information: bacillus calmette-guerin (tice strain)

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **bacillus calmette-guerin (tice strain)**

- Route(s) of administration: Percutaneous (multiple puncture device)

## 3. Approved Indications
- Prevention of tuberculosis in persons not previously infected with Mycobacterium tuberculosis who are at high risk for exposure.
- Recommended use situations described (regional recommendation):
  - Infants and children with negative tuberculin skin tests who are at high risk of intimate and prolonged exposure to persistently untreated or ineffectively treated infectious pulmonary TB and cannot be removed from the source or placed on long-term preventive therapy. [US]
  - Infants and children continuously exposed to persons with infectious pulmonary TB with bacilli resistant to isoniazid and rifampin, when the child cannot be separated from the infectious patient. [US]
  - Health care workers (HCWs) in high-risk settings may be considered for vaccination on an individual basis when (a) a high percentage of TB patients are infected with strains resistant to both isoniazid and rifampin, (b) transmission of such drug-resistant strains to HCWs is likely, and (c) comprehensive TB infection control measures have been implemented but have not succeeded. Vaccination should not be mandatory for employment or assignment. HCWs should be counselled regarding risks/benefits of vaccination versus preventive therapy. [US]
- Note: BCG vaccination is not routinely recommended for HCWs in low-risk settings. [US]

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (preparation)
- Reconstitution:
  - Adult primary preparation: add 1 mL Sterile Water for Injection (4–25°C) to one single‑dose vial of bacillus calmette-guerin (tice strain).
  - Pediatric preparation: add 2 mL Sterile Water for Injection (4–25°C) to one single‑dose vial of bacillus calmette-guerin (tice strain).
- The suspension should be gently swirled to obtain a homogeneous suspension; avoid forceful agitation to prevent clumping.

### Dose (administration)
- Administration route: **Percutaneous** using a sterile multiple‑puncture device. A separate sterile multiple‑puncture device must be used for each patient and discarded after use.
- DO NOT INJECT INTRAVENOUSLY, SUBCUTANEOUSLY, INTRAMUSCULARLY, OR INTRADERMALLY.

### Dosing by indication
- Specific numeric schedules (e.g., age-specific schedules, revaccination intervals) are not specified in available sources; see local/regional immunization guidance for programmatic schedules.
- Vaccination is recommended only for individuals who are tuberculin (PPD) negative on recent testing (5 TU).

### Administration instructions (precautions during preparation and administration)
- Use aseptic technique for preparation. To avoid cross-contamination, do not prepare parenteral drugs in areas where the vaccine is handled.
- Use personal protective equipment as appropriate during preparation (gloves; mask and gown if not prepared in a biocontainment hood).
- All equipment, supplies and receptacles contacting the vaccine should be handled and disposed of as biohazardous.
- After vaccination the site does not require dressing; it is recommended to keep the site loosely covered and dry for 24 hours and the site clean until local reaction resolves.
- Inform patients/caregivers about expected local lesion development (see Adverse Effects).

## 5. Contraindications
List of contraindications from available sources (do not administer in these situations):
- Known allergy to any component of bacillus calmette-guerin (tice strain) or history of anaphylactic/allergic reaction to a prior dose.
- Any persons with impaired immunologic responses, including but not limited to:
  - HIV infection (all ages) — vaccination should not be given to HIV‑infected persons.
  - Congenital immunodeficiency (examples given: chronic granulomatous disease, interferon gamma receptor deficiency).
  - Leukemia, lymphoma, generalized malignancy.
  - Immunosuppression due to steroids, alkylating agents, antimetabolites, or radiation.
- Severe immune deficiency syndromes (general statement).
- Infants, children, or adults with known immunocompromise.
- Children with a family history of immunodeficiency disease should not be vaccinated; if inadvertent vaccination occurs, consult an infectious disease specialist and consider anti-tuberculous therapy if clinically indicated.
- Not for treatment of active tuberculosis disease.

(Where regional differences were stated in sources, they are presented as general contraindications above.)

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources as a boxed warning; however, the most serious known complication is disseminated BCG infection which can be fatal, primarily in immunocompromised individuals — see Serious Warnings below.

### Serious warnings and critical precautions (highlighted)
- **Do not administer by intravenous, subcutaneous, intramuscular, or intradermal injection. Percutaneous application only.**
- **Disseminated BCG infection (including fatal cases) has occurred, primarily among immunocompromised persons.** If systemic infection is suspected, initiate diagnostic work-up and empiric antimycobacterial therapy promptly in consultation with an infectious disease specialist.
- BCG strains are resistant to pyrazinamide; pyrazinamide should not be used to treat BCG infections.

### General warnings and precautions
- Vaccination should not be attempted in individuals with severe immune deficiency disease.
- Use caution in persons at high risk for HIV infection; evaluate immunization history and individual risk/benefit.
- Vaccinate only tuberculin-negative individuals (5 TU).
- After vaccination, distinguishing post‑vaccination tuberculin reaction from new infection can be difficult; a sharp rise in tuberculin reactivity since the last test (outside the immediate post‑vaccination period) should prompt investigation.
- Epinephrine injection (1:1000) should be immediately available for treatment of anaphylaxis.
- To avoid interference with immune response, antimicrobial or immunosuppressive agents should be administered only under medical supervision; live vaccines given within 30 days of each other may impair immune response — whenever possible administer live vaccines at least 30 days apart if not given the same day.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor local injection site for expected lesion progression and for signs of ulceration, regional adenitis, or suppuration.
- Monitor for systemic signs of BCG infection (fever ≥103°F / 39.4°C, persistent local inflammation beyond 2–3 days, systemic febrile illness).
- If systemic BCG infection is suspected, obtain cultures and initiate multi‑agent antimycobacterial therapy while diagnostic evaluation proceeds.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Persons preparing the vaccine should avoid contact with broken skin and avoid inhalation of the organism (use hood or mask/gown as required).
- Parenteral medications should not be prepared in the area where the vaccine is handled to avoid cross-contamination.
- Patients should be counselled that vaccine contains live organisms and that transmission is possible although vaccine organisms do not survive long in a dry state.
- Caution when vaccinating individuals likely to develop keloids — may have more visible scarring.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Antimicrobials and immunosuppressive agents:
  - Antimicrobial or immunosuppressive therapy may interfere with development of vaccine‑induced immunity. Use under medical supervision only and consider timing relative to vaccination.
- Live vaccines:
  - Because bacillus calmette-guerin is a live vaccine, immune response could be impaired if administered within 30 days of another live vaccine. Whenever feasible, live vaccines given on different days should be separated by at least 30 days. (Sources note lack of conclusive evidence for all vaccine combinations but recommend the precaution.)
- Treatment of suspected systemic BCG infection:
  - BCG strains are resistant to pyrazinamide; pyrazinamide should not be used for BCG infections.
  - If systemic BCG infection is suspected, initiate two or more antimycobacterial agents while diagnostic evaluation is performed; consult an infectious disease expert.
- Regional variations or additional interaction notes: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Pediatric dosing preparation: add 2 mL Sterile Water for Injection to one single‑dose vial for pediatric use.
- Vaccine efficacy and safety in infants:
  - A prospective trial described infants vaccinated with the strain showing a 75% reduction in tuberculosis cases and an 83% reduction in TB‑attributable deaths in the vaccinated group versus controls in one long‑term follow‑up study.
  - After a single vaccination, 99.3% of infants became PPD positive; 84.2% remained PPD positive after 8 years in that study.
- Contraindications: Do not vaccinate infants known to be HIV‑infected or immunocompromised; children with family history of immunodeficiency should not be vaccinated unless evaluated.
- Further pediatric schedule details and revaccination recommendations: Not specified in available sources (see local immunization program guidance).

### Elderly
- Clinical data in ≥65 years: Not sufficient numbers to determine differences in response. Other reported clinical experience has not identified differences between elderly and younger patients.
- Precaution: An intact immune system is required; evaluate immune status before vaccinating elderly patients.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **bacillus calmette-guerin (tice strain)** is an attenuated, live culture preparation of Mycobacterium bovis intended to induce protective immune responses against Mycobacterium tuberculosis. Conversion to tuberculin skin test (PPD) positivity after vaccination has been used as a surrogate marker of vaccine potency and immunogenic response in studies.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: Not applicable / not specified in available sources.
- Distribution: Not specified in available sources.
- Metabolism: Not specified in available sources.
- Elimination: Not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- PPD (tuberculin) conversion rates reported in studies:
  - In infants after single vaccination, 99.3% became PPD positive; 84.2% remained positive after 8 years in one study.
  - In adult volunteer studies, conversion rates of approximately 92% and 95% were observed at 8 weeks.
- Vaccine potency determination in manufacturing: in vitro colony counts from serial dilution assays and intradermal guinea pig testing are used as indirect measures of potency.
- Composition notes (manufacturing medium components): The strain is grown in a medium containing glycerin, asparagine, citric acid, potassium phosphate, magnesium sulfate, and iron ammonium citrate; lactose is present in the final preparation prior to freeze‑drying. (These are manufacturing details provided in available sources.)

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Key trial summaries described in available sources:
  - Long-term infant prospective study (Rosenthal et al.): 1,716 vaccinated infants and 1,665 non‑vaccinated controls born at a single institution and followed for 12–23 years. Findings included:
    - Tuberculosis cases: 17 in vaccinated group (0.43/1,000/yr) vs 65 in controls (1.7/1,000/yr) — a 75% reduction (p<0.001).
    - Deaths due to tuberculosis: 1 in vaccinated group vs 6 in controls — an 83% reduction.
    - In sibling-pair subanalysis, 8 of 790 vaccinated developed TB versus 30 of 945 controls (p<0.001).
    - Among children ≤2 years in the control group there were 13 nonfatal TB cases and 3 deaths from TB <2.5 years (all miliary TB with meningitis); none of these nonfatal cases occurred in vaccinated children in that reported cohort.
    - PPD conversion: following single vaccination 99.3% converted to PPD positive; 84.2% remained positive at 8 years.
  - Adult/volunteer conversion studies (vaccine potency/immunogenicity):
    - Study A: 26 tuberculin‑negative subjects vaccinated; 24 returned for follow-up at 8 weeks; 22 converted to PPD positive (conversion rate 92%; average induration 15.5 mm).
    - Study B: 22 volunteers aged 18–40 (non‑HCWs, not foreign‑born, HIV negative), vaccinated; at 8 weeks 21 of 22 converted to PPD positive (conversion rate 95%).
- Other clinical trial details (design, statistical methods, additional endpoints) beyond the above are not specified in available sources.
