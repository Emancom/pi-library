# Editorial Product Information: Black Snake Antivenom

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been derived from the official Product Information (PI) document provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a concise format.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Black snake antivenom (equine) [Australian].
- **Chemical Class:** Purified equine immunoglobulins (plasma proteins) [Australian].
- **Brand Name(s):** Black Snake Antivenom [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J06AA (Antivenoms - proposed).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Concentrated injection for intravenous use. Each vial contains 18,000 units of antivenom (standardised to neutralise the average yield of king brown snake venom) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous infusion [Australian].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Seqirus Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Systemic Envenoming:** Treatment of patients exhibiting manifestations of systemic envenoming following a bite by a king brown snake (*Pseudechis australis*), also known as the mulga snake [Australian].
- **Note on Genus Pseudechis:** While effective against other members of the genus *Pseudechis* (e.g., red-bellied or common black snake), **Tiger Snake Antivenom** is the preferred treatment for these species due to lower injection volumes and equine protein content [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric):** The initial dose is one vial (18,000 units). The dosage is identical for adults and children [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Administer via slow intravenous infusion after dilution [Australian].
    - **Dilution:** Recommended 1 in 10 dilution with Hartmann’s Solution or 0.9% w/v sodium chloride. A 1 in 5 dilution may be used for patients at risk of fluid overload [Australian].
    - **Speed:** Use immediately after dilution. Do not store diluted antivenom [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Patients must be monitored for a minimum of 6 hours post-administration [Australian].
- **Additional Dosing:** Deterioration in clinical condition may indicate inadequate neutralisation, requiring further antivenom [Australian].
- **Route Restriction:** Must not be administered via the intramuscular route [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Absolute Contraindications:** There are no absolute contraindications [Australian].
- **Clinical Threshold:** The product should not be used unless there is clear evidence of systemic envenoming with potential for serious toxic effects [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Setting:** Administration should occur in an intensive care setting or where resuscitation facilities are immediately available [Australian].
- **Anaphylaxis and Hypersensitivity:** As a horse-serum-derived product, severe allergic reactions, including fatal anaphylaxis, may occur. Adrenaline must be prepared and ready for use prior to administration [Australian].
- **Atopy and Cross-reactivity:** Patients with a history of horse allergy or animal atopy (e.g., sheep, dog, cat) are at increased risk. Skin testing is not recommended as results are unsatisfactory [Australian].
- **First Aid Management:** Do not remove the splint and pressure bandage until antivenom is available, as removal can precipitate sudden systemic envenoming [Australian].
- **Observation Period:** Suspected bite victims should be observed for at least 12 hours after the bite or bandage removal [Australian].
- **Infectious Agents:** Because this product is prepared from animal plasma, the risk of transmitting infective agents cannot be totally excluded, though no cases of viral transmission have been recorded for this product [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions identified during post-approval use are categorised by frequency:

| Frequency | System Organ Class | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Common** (≥1/100 to <1/10) | Immune System | Allergic reactions, anaphylactic shock, delayed serum sickness |
| | Nervous System | Headache |
| | Skin/Subcutaneous | Urticaria, rash |
| | General | Pyrexia, chills |
| **Uncommon** (≥1/1,000 to <1/100) | Gastrointestinal | Abdominal pain, vomiting, nausea, diarrhoea |
| | Musculoskeletal | Myalgia |
| | General | Local injection site reactions, chest pain |

- **Serum Sickness:** Delayed serum sickness (fever, rash, arthralgia) occurs in at least 5% of patients receiving horse serum for the first time. Onset may be 12 hours to several weeks post-treatment [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Premedication Controversy:** The use of prophylactic adrenaline and antihistamines to reduce anaphylaxis risk is controversial [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category: Not specified]. Information is limited and inconclusive. Healthcare providers should weigh the risks of untreated envenoming against the potential risks of antivenom administration [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** No information available. Use should be based on a risk-benefit assessment [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Children may become critically ill more rapidly than adults and may require more antivenom. Dosing is the same as for adults [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources; however, patients must be monitored for renal impairment as a complication of envenoming [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Contains specific antibodies that neutralise the toxins in the venom of the king brown (mulga) snake [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** King brown snake venom contains neurotoxins, myolytic toxins, and anticoagulant toxins. Notably, it does not typically cause the afibrinogenaemic coagulopathy common with other Australian snake venoms [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** No specific information available on absorption, distribution, metabolism, or excretion [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No clinical trial data provided in the source documentation [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store between 2°C and 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze) [Australian].
- **Light:** Protect the vial from light [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging. Use diluted product immediately [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4) [Australian].
- **Other:** The vial and components do not contain natural rubber latex [Australian].
