# Editorial Product Information: Bexarotene

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of bexarotene oral capsules, intended for use by healthcare professionals as a clinically accurate reference. 
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial is a synthesis of available regulatory information and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Bexarotene (a member of the retinoid class that selectively activates retinoid X receptors).
- **Chemical Class:** Retinoid X receptor (RXR) selective retinoid.
- **Brand Names:** Available as generic formulations (e.g., manufactured for AvKARE) [US].
- **ATC Code:** L01XF03
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Oral Capsules:** 75 mg white, oblong soft gelatin capsules (imprinted with "A125") [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AvKARE [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Cutaneous T-cell Lymphoma (CTCL):** Indicated for the treatment of cutaneous manifestations in patients who have proven refractory to at least one prior systemic therapy [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** 
    - **Recommended Initial Dose:** 300 mg/m²/day [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Administer as a single daily oral dose.
    - **Must be taken with a meal** to optimize absorption [US].
- **Dose Titration and Modification:** 
    - **Reduction:** If necessitated by toxicity, the dose may be reduced to 200 mg/m²/day, then to 100 mg/m²/day, or temporarily suspended [US].
    - **Escalation:** If there is no tumor response after 8 weeks and the initial 300 mg/m²/day dose is well tolerated, the dose may be increased to 400 mg/m²/day with close monitoring [US].
- **Duration of Therapy:** Should be continued as long as the patient derives clinical benefit. In clinical trials, the drug was administered for up to 97 weeks [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy:** Bexarotene is contraindicated in females who are pregnant or who intend to become pregnant, due to significant teratogenic risk [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Contraindicated in patients with a known serious hypersensitivity to bexarotene or any component of the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning (Birth Defects):** Bexarotene is a retinoid associated with birth defects in humans. It must not be administered to pregnant women [US].
- **Hyperlipidemia:** Substantial elevations in fasting triglycerides and cholesterol occur in most patients. Triglycerides should be normalized prior to initiation and maintained below 400 mg/dL to reduce the risk of pancreatitis [US].
- **Pancreatitis:** Acute pancreatitis (including fatal cases) has been reported, typically associated with marked hypertriglyceridemia. Interrupt therapy if pancreatitis is suspected [US].
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Elevations in LFTs (AST, ALT, bilirubin) are common. Interrupt or discontinue if tests exceed 3x the upper limit of normal (ULN) [US].
- **Hypothyroidism:** Bexarotene induces primary hypothyroidism (reversible reduction in TSH and T4) in approximately half of all patients. Replacement therapy is often required [US].
- **Neutropenia:** Leukopenia and neutropenia (NCI Grade 3/4) may occur, usually 4–8 weeks after initiation [US].
- **Cataracts:** New or worsening cataracts have been observed in clinical and preclinical studies. Patients experiencing visual changes require ophthalmologic evaluation [US].
- **Photosensitivity:** Retinoids may increase sensitivity to sunlight and artificial UV light; patients should minimize exposure [US].
- **Vitamin A Supplementation:** Patients should limit Vitamin A intake to $\le$ 15,000 IU/day to avoid additive toxic effects [US].
- **Hypoglycemia Risk:** Bexarotene may enhance the action of insulin or sulfonylureas, potentially leading to hypoglycemia in patients with diabetes [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are dose-dependent and occur more frequently at doses $>300$ mg/m²/day.

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction [US] |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Hypertriglyceridemia (79%), Hypercholesterolemia (32%), Hypothyroidism (29%), Headache (30%), Asthenia (20%), Leukopenia (17%), Rash (17%), Nausea (16%), Infection (13%), Peripheral edema (13%), Abdominal pain (11%), Dry skin (10%). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Diarrhea, Vomiting, Anorexia, Fever, Chills, Back pain, Flu syndrome, Alopecia, Anemia. |
| **Serious/NCI Grade 3/4** | Hypertriglyceridemia (28%), Hypercholesterolemia (25%), Neutropenia (12% Grade 3, 4% Grade 4). |

- **Rare/Other:** Fatalities have occurred due to acute pancreatitis, liver failure, and subdural hematoma [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Gemfibrozil:** Concomitant use results in significant increases in bexarotene plasma concentrations. **Co-administration is not recommended** [US].
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors/Inducers:** Bexarotene is metabolized by CYP3A4. Strong inducers (e.g., rifampin, phenytoin) may decrease concentrations. Ketoconazole did not significantly alter concentrations in trials, but caution is advised [US].
- **Effect of Bexarotene on Other Drugs:** 
    - **Hormonal Contraceptives:** Bexarotene may induce CYP3A4, potentially reducing the efficacy of systemic hormonal contraceptives. **Non-hormonal contraception is strongly recommended** [US].
    - **Atorvastatin:** Exposure (AUC) to atorvastatin may be decreased by 50% [US].
    - **Tamoxifen:** Plasma concentrations may be reduced by approximately 35% [US].
- **Laboratory Test Interference:** CA125 assay values in ovarian cancer patients may be increased [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned but carries a Boxed Warning]. Highly teratogenic. 
    - **Pregnancy Testing:** Females of reproductive potential must have a negative pregnancy test (sensitivity $\ge$ 50 mIU/L) within one week prior to therapy and monthly thereafter [US].
    - **Contraception (Females):** Must use two reliable forms of contraception (one being non-hormonal) starting one month prior to therapy, during therapy, and for one month following discontinuation [US].
    - **Contraception (Males):** Males with pregnant partners or partners of reproductive potential must use condoms during treatment and for one month after the final dose [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants, breastfeeding must be discontinued during treatment [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy were observed in patients $\ge$ 60 years old compared to younger patients, though greater sensitivity in some individuals cannot be ruled out [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No formal studies have been conducted. Urinary elimination is a minor pathway ($<1\%$), but protein binding changes in renal failure may alter pharmacokinetics [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No formal studies have been conducted. Hepatic impairment is expected to significantly decrease clearance; patients should be monitored closely for toxicity [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bexarotene selectively binds and activates retinoid X receptor subtypes (RXR$\alpha$, RXR$\beta$, RXR$\gamma$). These receptors act as transcription factors regulating genes involved in cellular differentiation and proliferation. The exact mechanism in CTCL is unknown [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ is approximately 2 hours. Bioavailability is significantly increased with a fat-containing meal [US].
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound ($>99\%$) [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolized by CYP3A4 into four primary metabolites (6- and 7-hydroxy-bexarotene and 6- and 7-oxo-bexarotene) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily hepatobiliary; renal excretion is $<1\%$. Terminal half-life is approximately 7 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** Two multicenter, open-label, historically controlled trials (n=152) evaluated patients with CTCL refractory to prior systemic therapy.
- **Outcomes:** At an initial dose of 300 mg/m²/day, the overall objective response rate was approximately 32% (1.6% complete clinical response, 30% partial response). Responses were observed as early as 4 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at $2^\circ$C to $25^\circ$C ($36^\circ$F to $77^\circ$F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from light, heat, and humidity. Avoid high temperatures after the bottle is opened [US].
