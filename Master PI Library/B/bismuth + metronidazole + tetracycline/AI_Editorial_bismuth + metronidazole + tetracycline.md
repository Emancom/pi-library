# Editorial Product Information: bismuth + metronidazole + tetracycline

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **bismuth + metronidazole + tetracycline**
- Chemical class:
  - Bismuth subcitrate potassium: bismuth salt
  - Metronidazole: nitroimidazole antimicrobial
  - Tetracycline hydrochloride: tetracycline-class antimicrobial
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 2006.
- Most recent PI revision date reported: April 2025.
- Recent PI changes noted (dates reported in source):
  - Warnings and Precautions, Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions updated March 2025.
  - Removal of a contraindication referencing methoxyflurane (March 2025).
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.
- If other jurisdictional registration dates or PI revision dates are not provided above: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Eradication of Helicobacter pylori infection in patients with active duodenal ulcer disease or history of duodenal ulcer disease (within the past 5 years) — to be used in combination with a proton pump inhibitor (omeprazole 20 mg twice daily as per the dosing regimen described).  
  - Regional note: This indication is described in the available sources; where regional differences in approval exist, specific regional approval status beyond the above is not specified in available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General adult dosing
- Standard adult dosing regimen (as described in available sources):
  - Take three capsules of **bismuth + metronidazole + tetracycline** four times daily (after morning meal, after lunch, after evening meal, and at bedtime) for 10 days.
  - Concurrent proton pump inhibitor: omeprazole 20 mg orally twice daily (after the morning and evening meals) for 10 days.
- Strengths per administered unit (as stated in sources):
  - Each administered capsule contains: 140 mg bismuth subcitrate potassium, 125 mg metronidazole, and 125 mg tetracycline hydrochloride.
### Administration instructions
- Swallow capsules whole with a full glass (8 ounces) of water. Adequate fluid intake is particularly recommended with the bedtime dose to reduce the risk of esophageal irritation/ulceration from tetracycline.
- Avoid taking antacids, multivitamins, or dairy products concomitantly with doses, since these may reduce absorption.
### Missed dose
- If a single dose is missed, continue the normal dosing schedule; do not double doses.
- If more than four doses are missed, contact the prescriber for further instructions.
### Titration schedules
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
List includes all contraindications reported in the available sources:
- Concomitant or recent (within two weeks) use of disulfiram (risk of psychotic reactions with metronidazole).
- Consumption of alcoholic beverages or products containing propylene glycol during therapy and for at least 3 days after completion (risk of disulfiram-like reaction with metronidazole).
- Cockayne syndrome (reported severe irreversible hepatotoxicity/acute liver failure after metronidazole initiation in patients with Cockayne syndrome).
- Severe renal impairment (contraindicated due to risk of tetracycline-related antianabolic effects, azotemia, hyperphosphatemia, acidosis and higher serum concentrations).
- Pregnancy (contraindicated) — see Section 9.
- Known hypersensitivity to any component: bismuth subcitrate potassium, metronidazole or nitroimidazole derivatives, tetracycline or tetracycline-class antibiotics.
- Additional region-specific contraindications: Not specified beyond the above in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warning
- **Potential for carcinogenicity** [US]: Metronidazole has produced tumors in mice and rats in carcinogenicity studies; it is unknown whether metronidazole is associated with carcinogenicity in humans. This boxed warning is present in the available sources.
### General warnings and precautions (all items are drawn from available sources)
- Fetal toxicity: Tetracycline-class drugs can cause permanent tooth discoloration and may impair bone development if used during the second and third trimesters. Use in pregnancy is contraindicated; counsel patients accordingly.
- Maternal hepatotoxicity: High doses of intravenous tetracycline have been associated with rare but serious maternal hepatotoxicity; such events can lead to stillbirth or premature birth.
- Tooth enamel discoloration and hypoplasia: Use during tooth development (last half of pregnancy, infancy, and childhood to age 8) may cause permanent discoloration and enamel hypoplasia; avoid use in children <8 years unless no alternatives.
- Severe cutaneous adverse reactions (SCARs): Metronidazole has been associated with SJS, TEN, DRESS, and AGEP. Tetracycline has been associated with fixed drug eruptions including generalized bullous fixed drug eruption. Discontinue immediately if severe skin reactions occur.
- Central and peripheral nervous system effects:
  - Metronidazole: convulsive seizures, encephalopathy (including cerebellar dysfunction with MRI lesions), aseptic meningitis, peripheral neuropathy (including optic neuropathy). CNS findings are often reversible after discontinuation, but monitor closely and discontinue if new neurologic signs develop.
  - Tetracycline: intracranial hypertension/pseudotumor cerebri — presents with headache, visual disturbances, papilledema; may persist after discontinuation and can lead to permanent visual loss; concomitant isotretinoin should be avoided.
  - Bismuth-containing products: neurotoxicity has been reported with excessive bismuth exposure; effects have been reversible on discontinuation.
- Development of microbial overgrowth/superinfection: Oral candidiasis and other nonsusceptible organism overgrowth may occur; treat appropriately and discontinue therapy if serious superinfection develops.
- Photosensitivity: Tetracycline-associated photosensitivity (exaggerated sunburn) — advise avoidance of direct sunlight/UV; discontinue at first sign of erythema.
- Darkening of the tongue and/or black stool: Bismuth may cause harmless reversible darkening of tongue and stools; distinguish from melena.
- Blood dyscrasias: Metronidazole may cause mild leukopenia; use with caution in patients with history/evidence of blood dyscrasia; obtain baseline and follow-up leukocyte counts as appropriate.
- Hepatic impairment: Metronidazole is metabolized more slowly in hepatic impairment with potential accumulation. Patients with mild–moderate hepatic impairment should be monitored; not recommended in severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C).
- Laboratory test interference:
  - Bismuth may interfere with radiographic imaging of the GI tract (bismuth absorbs x-rays).
  - Metronidazole may interfere with enzymatic laboratory assays that rely on NAD+/NADH (e.g., AST, ALT, LDH, triglycerides, hexokinase glucose), potentially yielding erroneous results.
- Development of drug-resistant bacteria: Use only for indicated infections; inappropriate use contributes to resistance.
### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor for signs of SCARs and discontinue immediately if suspected.
- Monitor neurologic status; discontinue if new or worsening neurologic symptoms occur.
- Monitor prothrombin time/INR when used concomitantly with warfarin or other coumarin anticoagulants.
- Monitor serum lithium and serum creatinine when used in patients on lithium (see Drug Interactions).
- Monitor for signs of superinfection (fungal or resistant organisms).
- Monitor hepatic function (AST/ALT) and hematologic parameters (CBC) as clinically indicated.
- Monitor busulfan plasma concentrations if coadministered and adjust dosing due to increased busulfan exposure.
### Specific clinical precautions
- Avoid alcohol and propylene glycol–containing products during therapy and for at least 3 days after therapy completion due to risk of a disulfiram-like reaction.
- Advise women of childbearing potential that oral contraceptive efficacy may be reduced; use alternative or additional contraception during therapy.
- Avoid concomitant use with disulfiram and with lithium without close monitoring.
- Avoid concomitant administration with antacids, multivitamins, and dairy products that interfere with tetracycline absorption around dosing times.

## 7. Adverse Effects
- Adverse reaction data below include both clinical trial and postmarketing reports cited in the available sources. Frequencies are provided where reported in the available sources; for many reactions only occurrence was documented without a quantified frequency.

### Adverse reactions observed in clinical trials (North American randomized, open-label, active-controlled trial)
- Population: adults age 18–75; 147 patients received bismuth + metronidazole + tetracycline plus omeprazole (treatment group), 152 patients received control regimen.
- Overall adverse reaction reporting: 58% in treatment group vs 59% in control group.
- Adverse reactions with incidence ≥5% (treatment group):
  - Abnormal feces: 15.6% (very common)
  - Nausea: 8.2% (common)
  - Diarrhea: 6.8% (common)

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Disulfiram: Concurrent use or use within two weeks is contraindicated (risk of psychotic reactions with metronidazole). [Contraindication]
- Alcohol and propylene glycol-containing products: Concomitant use is contraindicated during therapy and for at least 3 days after therapy due to risk of disulfiram-like reactions (abdominal cramps, nausea, vomiting, headache, flushing).
- Oral contraceptives: Efficacy may be decreased (tetracycline interaction); breakthrough bleeding has been reported. Advise alternative or additional contraception during therapy.
- Anticoagulants (warfarin and other oral coumarins): Metronidazole can potentiate anticoagulant effect leading to prolonged prothrombin time/INR; tetracycline may depress prothrombin activity. Closely monitor coagulation tests and clinical signs of bleeding when coadministered.
- Lithium: Metronidazole can increase serum lithium concentrations and precipitate lithium toxicity. Monitor serum lithium and serum creatinine closely (daily for several days after starting therapy) and observe for signs of lithium toxicity.
- Antacids, multivitamins, and dairy products: These decrease absorption of tetracycline component; avoid concomitant ingestion with doses.
- Busulfan: Metronidazole has been reported to increase busulfan concentrations leading to increased risk of serious busulfan toxicity. Avoid coadministration if possible; if necessary, monitor busulfan plasma concentrations and signs of toxicity and adjust busulfan dose accordingly.
- CYP enzyme inhibitors and inducers: Metronidazole’s half-life may be prolonged or shortened by concomitant CYP inhibitors or inducers; use caution and monitor for altered effects or increased toxicity of metronidazole or concomitant medications.
- Drugs that prolong QT interval: QT prolongation has been reported with metronidazole, particularly when combined with other QT-prolonging drugs — use caution.
- Other interactions: Metronidazole may interfere with certain laboratory assays (see Section 6).

- Regional interaction notes: Specific regional differences in listed interactions were not provided beyond the interactions above. If region-specific differences exist in other sources, they are not specified in the available sources.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Pregnancy status: **Contraindicated in pregnancy** per available sources.
- Rationale:
  - Tetracycline-class drugs: risk of permanent tooth discoloration in fetus when used during second and third trimester and possible inhibition of bone growth.
  - Maternal risks: high-dose IV tetracycline has been associated with rare but serious maternal hepatotoxicity that can result in stillbirth or premature birth.
- Pregnancy classification systems (regional) from sources:
  - Specific jurisdictional pregnancy category labels (e.g., Australian categories, US letter categories) are not explicitly provided in available sources beyond the contraindication statement. If region-specific pregnancy category labeling exists, it is not specified in the available sources.
### Breastfeeding
- Recommendation from available sources: A woman should pump and discard human milk during the course of therapy and for 2 days after therapy ends.
- Rationale: Metronidazole and tetracycline components are excreted in human milk and may have effects in the nursing infant; pump-and-discard is recommended as a precaution.
### Fertility
- Fertility data: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Contraindication/limitation: Do not use in children less than 8 years of age because tetracycline may cause permanent tooth discoloration and enamel hypoplasia during tooth development (last half of pregnancy, infancy, and childhood to age 8).
- Dosing in children ≥8 years: Not specified in available sources.
### Elderly use
- Specific dosing adjustments for elderly: Not specified in available sources.
- Caution: As older patients may have decreased hepatic or renal function, monitor for accumulation and adverse effects (general principle present but specific guidance not provided).
### Renal impairment
- Severe renal impairment: Contraindicated.
- Rationale: Tetracycline antianabolic effects may increase BUN; higher serum tetracycline concentrations may lead to azotemia, hyperphosphatemia, and acidosis.
- Mild to moderate renal impairment: Specific dosing adjustments not specified in available sources.
### Hepatic impairment
- Metronidazole accumulates in hepatic impairment due to slower metabolism.
- Patients with mild to moderate hepatic impairment: monitor for metronidazole-associated adverse events.
- Severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C): Not recommended.
### Other special populations
- Cockayne syndrome: Contraindicated due to reports of severe irreversible hepatotoxicity/acute liver failure after metronidazole initiation.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Metronidazole: nitroimidazole antimicrobial (active against anaerobic bacteria and protozoa).
- Tetracycline hydrochloride: tetracycline-class antimicrobial (inhibits bacterial protein synthesis).
- Bismuth subcitrate potassium: bismuth salt included as part of combination therapy for H. pylori eradication; specific detailed mechanism of bismuth component is not specified in available sources.
- Overall combination: used to eradicate Helicobacter pylori when administered with a proton pump inhibitor (omeprazole).
### Pharmacokinetics
- Metronidazole: metabolized by the liver; patients with hepatic impairment have slower metabolism and resultant accumulation. Specific absorption, distribution, metabolic pathways, half-lives, and elimination parameters are not fully specified in available sources.
- Bismuth: can absorb x-rays and may interfere with radiographic imaging of the GI tract. Further PK details not specified in available sources.
- Tetracycline: specific pharmacokinetic parameters for the combination formulation are not specified in available sources.
### Pharmacodynamics
- Not specified in detail in available sources beyond antimicrobial classes and clinical effect of H. pylori eradication.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Key trial described in available sources:
  - Design: Open-label, randomized, active-controlled clinical trial conducted in North America comparing omeprazole + bismuth/metronidazole/tetracycline (treatment group) versus omeprazole + amoxicillin + clarithromycin (control group).
  - Population: 147 patients received the bismuth + metronidazole + tetracycline regimen plus omeprazole; 152 received the control regimen. Age range 18–75 years; 59% male; 59% Caucasian.
  - Treatment duration: 10 days.
  - Safety outcomes: Adverse drug reactions reported in 58% of treatment group and 59% of control group. Common adverse reactions (≥5%) in the treatment group included abnormal feces (15.6%), nausea (8.2%), diarrhea (6.8%), and headache (5.4%).
  - Efficacy outcomes (eradication rates, primary endpoint details): Specific eradication rate data and primary efficacy results are not specified in available sources.
- Additional trials and long-term outcome data: Not specified in available sources.
