# Editorial Product Information: Benzphetamine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonized editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- This document serves as an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the United States Product Information was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Benzphetamine hydrochloride (a sympathomimetic amine).
- **Chemical Class:** Anorectic agent; amphetamine-related stimulant.
- **Brand Name(s):** Not specified in available sources (Generic manufacturer: Heritage Pharmaceuticals Inc. d/b/a Avet Pharmaceuticals Inc.) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Oral Tablet:** 50 mg benzphetamine hydrochloride (peach, round, biconvex, film-coated, debossed "952" and scored) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Avet Pharmaceuticals Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Exogenous Obesity:** Indicated as a short-term (a few weeks) adjunct in a weight reduction regimen based on caloric restriction for patients with an initial Body Mass Index (BMI) of 30 kg/m² or higher [US].
- **Clinical Criteria:** For use in patients who have failed to respond to appropriate weight-reducing regimens (diet and/or exercise) alone [US].
- **Usage Limitation:** Approved for use as monotherapy only [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Adult Dosing:**
    - **Suggested Range:** 25 mg to 50 mg, one to three times daily [US].
    - **Initiation:** Treatment should begin with 25 mg to 50 mg once daily, with subsequent adjustments in dose or frequency based on patient response [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - A single daily dose is preferably administered in the mid-morning or mid-afternoon, depending on the patient's eating habits [US].
    - Avoid late afternoon administration in some patients to prevent sleep disturbances [US].
- **Treatment Duration:** Limited to "a few weeks" [US].
- **Discontinuation:** If a patient does not achieve satisfactory weight loss (e.g., at least 4 pounds) within the first four weeks of treatment, the drug should be discontinued [US]. If tolerance to the anorectic effect develops, the dose should not be increased; rather, the drug should be stopped [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Cardiovascular Disease:** Advanced arteriosclerosis, symptomatic cardiovascular disease, and moderate to severe hypertension [US].
- **Endocrine/Ocular:** Hyperthyroidism and glaucoma [US].
- **Psychological/Behavioral:** Agitated states or a history of drug abuse [US].
- **Drug Interactions:** Concomitant use with other CNS stimulants or use within 14 days of administering monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs) due to the risk of hypertensive crisis [US].
- **Pregnancy:** Benzphetamine is contraindicated in women who are or may become pregnant [US].
- **Sensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity or idiosyncrasy to sympathomimetic amines [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Combination Therapy:** Must not be used in combination with other anorectic agents, including OTC and herbal products [US].
- **Pulmonary Hypertension:** The use of anorectic agents is associated with an increased risk of pulmonary hypertension, a rare but often fatal disorder. Risk increases significantly with use exceeding three months [US]. Immediately discontinue if dyspnea, angina pectoris, syncope, or lower extremity edema occur [US].
- **Valvular Heart Disease:** While not specifically reported with benzphetamine monotherapy, serious valvular heart disease has been reported with other anorectic agents. Evaluation for pre-existing heart disease is recommended prior to initiation [US].
- **CNS Effects:** May impair the ability to operate machinery or drive motor vehicles [US].
- **Diabetes Mellitus:** Insulin requirements may be altered due to the drug and accompanying dietary restrictions [US].
- **Psychological Effects:** Use with caution in patients with even mild hypertension; psychological disturbances may occur during restrictive dieting while on anorectics [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Note: Frequencies are not specified in the source document.*

- **Cardiovascular:** Palpitation, tachycardia, elevated blood pressure. Chronic amphetamine use has been linked to cardiomyopathy and ischemic cardiac events [US].
- **Central Nervous System:** Overstimulation, restlessness, dizziness, insomnia, tremor, sweating, headache. Psychotic episodes (rare at recommended doses) and depression following withdrawal have been reported [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Xerostomia (dry mouth), unpleasant taste, nausea, diarrhea, and other disturbances [US].
- **Allergic:** Urticaria and other skin-related allergic reactions [US].
- **Endocrine:** Changes in libido [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **MAOIs:** Risk of hypertensive crisis if used within 14 days [US].
- **CNS Stimulants:** Avoid concomitant use [US].
- **Antihypertensives:** Benzphetamine may decrease the hypotensive effect of these agents [US].
- **Tricyclic Antidepressants (TCAs):** May enhance the effects of amphetamine-related drugs [US].
- **Urinary Alkalinizing Agents:** Increase blood levels and decrease excretion of the drug [US].
- **Urinary Acidifying Agents:** Decrease blood levels and increase excretion of the drug [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated [US]. Benzphetamine may cause fetal harm. Amphetamines are known to be teratogenic and embryotoxic in animal models at high doses [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Amphetamines are excreted in human milk. Mothers are advised to refrain from nursing while taking the medication [US].
- **Fertility:** Studies evaluating the impairment of fertility have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established. Use is not recommended in individuals under 17 years of age [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient subjects aged 65+ to establish safety/efficacy. Dosing should be cautious, generally starting at the low end of the range to account for decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Specific dosing adjustments not provided; however, caution is advised in the elderly due to potential organ dysfunction [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A sympathomimetic amine that stimulates the central nervous system and elevates blood pressure. The primary mechanism in obesity is likely appetite suppression, though metabolic or other CNS effects may contribute [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Human pharmacokinetic data are not available for benzphetamine [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Tachyphylaxis and tolerance typically develop with this class of drugs [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Summary:** Short-term clinical trials indicate that obese adult subjects treated with anorectic drugs and dietary management lose more weight on average than those treated with placebo and diet. The magnitude of additional weight loss is typically only a fraction of a pound per week, with the highest rate occurring in the first few weeks of therapy [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Dispense in tight, light-resistant containers [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as a Schedule III (CIII) controlled substance under the Controlled Substance Act due to the potential for abuse and dependence [US].
