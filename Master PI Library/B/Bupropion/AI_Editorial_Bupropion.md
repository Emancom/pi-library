# Editorial Product Information: Naltrexone Hydrochloride / Bupropion Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of the fixed-dose combination of **naltrexone** and **bupropion** for healthcare professionals.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory guidance for region-specific legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Naltrexone hydrochloride (mu-opioid antagonist) and Bupropion hydrochloride (aminoketone antidepressant; weak inhibitor of neuronal dopamine and norepinephrine reuptake).
- **Brand Name(s):** **CONTRAVE** 8/90 [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A08AA62.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Extended-release (modified-release) trilayer tablets containing 8 mg naltrexone hydrochloride and 90 mg bupropion hydrochloride [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** iNova Pharmaceuticals (Australia) Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Weight Management:** Indicated as an adjunct to a reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity for chronic weight management in adults (≥18 years) with an initial Body Mass Index (BMI) of:
    - ≥ 30 kg/m² (obesity), or
    - ≥ 27 kg/m² to < 30 kg/m² (overweight) in the presence of at least one weight-related co-morbidity (e.g., type 2 diabetes, dyslipidaemia, or controlled hypertension) [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
The dose must be escalated over a 4-week period to improve gastrointestinal tolerability. The tablets must be swallowed whole (not cut, chewed, or crushed) and should preferably be taken with food [Australian].

### Titration Schedule
| Week | Morning Dose | Evening Dose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Week 1** | 1 tablet | None |
| **Week 2** | 1 tablet | 1 tablet |
| **Week 3** | 2 tablets | 1 tablet |
| **Week 4 - Onward** | 2 tablets | 2 tablets |

- **Maximum Daily Dose:** Two tablets twice daily (total 32 mg naltrexone / 360 mg bupropion).
- **Discontinuation Rule:** Evaluate response after 16 weeks. If a patient has not lost at least 5% of their initial body weight, treatment should be discontinued [Australian].

### Missed Dose
If a dose is missed, the patient should wait and take the next prescribed dose at the usual time; an additional dose should not be taken [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to bupropion, naltrexone, or any excipients.
- Uncontrolled hypertension.
- Current seizure disorder or a history of seizures.
- Known central nervous system (CNS) tumour.
- Acute withdrawal from alcohol or benzodiazepines.
- History of bipolar disorder.
- Current or previous diagnosis of bulimia or anorexia nervosa.
- Concomitant use of other products containing bupropion or naltrexone.
- Opioid dependence: Includes patients currently dependent on chronic opioids, those treated with opioid agonists (e.g., methadone, buprenorphine), or those in acute opioid withdrawal.
- Concomitant use of Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs): At least 14 days must elapse between MAOI discontinuation and starting treatment.
- Severe hepatic impairment [Australian].
- End-stage renal failure [Australian].
- Pregnancy [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Suicidality:** Bupropion is an antidepressant. While clinical trials in obesity did not show increased suicide risk, post-marketing reports include suicidal ideation. Monitor all patients for clinical worsening or unusual changes in behaviour, particularly those <25 years of age [Australian].
- **Seizures:** Bupropion carries a dose-related risk of seizures (approx. 0.1% for 300 mg SR). Risk is increased by head trauma, excessive alcohol use, addiction to stimulants, and drugs that lower the seizure threshold. Discontinue permanently if a seizure occurs [Australian].
- **Opioid Overdose Risk:** Patients may attempt to overcome the naltrexone-induced blockade by taking large amounts of opioids, which can lead to fatal respiratory arrest or circulatory collapse [Australian].
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) and Acute Generalised Exanthematous Pustulosis (AGEP) have been reported. Discontinue immediately at the first sign of a skin rash [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** Can increase blood pressure and heart rate. Monitor these parameters before and during treatment. Avoid use in patients with a recent history of MI, unstable heart disease, or NYHA Class III/IV heart failure [Australian].
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Naltrexone can cause hepatocellular injury. Monitor for signs of acute hepatitis and discontinue if suspected [Australian].
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Reported particularly when co-administered with SSRIs, SNRIs, or opioids [Australian].
- **Brugada Syndrome:** Bupropion may unmask this condition; caution is advised in patients with a family history of cardiac arrest [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Discontinuation:** 23.8% of patients in clinical trials discontinued due to adverse events (vs 11.9% for placebo).
- **Very Common (>10%):** Nausea (31.8%), constipation (18.1%), vomiting (9.9%), dizziness, headache.
- **Common (1–10%):** Dry mouth, insomnia, palpitations, tinnitus, vertigo, upper abdominal pain, tremor, dysgeusia, hyperhidrosis, pruritus, and alopecia.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Tachycardia, cholecystitis, hepatic enzyme increase, dehydration, urticaria, abnormal dreams, and micturition urgency.
- **Rare/Not Known:** Serotonin syndrome, SJS, AGEP, angioedema, seizure, and syncope [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **MAOIs:** Contraindicated; risk of hypertensive reactions.
- **Opioids:** Naltrexone blocks opioid receptors. Stop **CONTRAVE** at least 3 days before elective surgery requiring opioids.
- **CYP2D6 Substrates:** Bupropion and its metabolite hydroxybupropion are strong inhibitors of CYP2D6. Use caution and lower starting doses for drugs like metoprolol, desipramine, venlafaxine, haloperidol, and flecainide [Australian].
- **CYP2B6 Inhibitors/Inducers:** Bupropion is metabolised by CYP2B6. 
    - Inhibitors (e.g., ticlopidine, clopidogrel) increase bupropion exposure (do not exceed 2 tablets/day).
    - Inducers (e.g., ritonavir, lopinavir, efavirenz) reduce efficacy; concomitant use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Digoxin:** Co-administration may decrease plasma digoxin levels.
- **Dopaminergic Drugs:** Use caution with levodopa or amantadine due to risk of CNS toxicity (ataxia, dizziness, agitation).
- **Alcohol:** Minimise or avoid; reports of reduced alcohol tolerance and neuropsychiatric events.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Contraindicated.** Weight loss offers no benefit to a pregnant woman and may cause foetal harm [Australian Category B3].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. Naltrexone, bupropion, and their metabolites are excreted in human milk [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal data showed decreased pregnancy rates in rats at high doses; human relevance is unknown.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for those <18 years of age; safety and efficacy not established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Use with caution in patients >65 years; not recommended in those >75 years due to increased sensitivity to CNS effects and potential for reduced renal function [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - Mild: No dose adjustment.
    - Moderate/Severe: Maximum recommended daily dose is 2 tablets (one morning, one evening).
    - End-stage: Contraindicated [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - Severe: Contraindicated.
    - Mild/Moderate: Not recommended [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bupropion stimulates pro-opiomelanocortin (POMC) neurons in the hypothalamus to release $\alpha$-MSH, which binds to MC4 receptors to reduce appetite. $\alpha$-MSH release is normally inhibited by $\beta$-endorphin; naltrexone blocks this inhibitory feedback, thereby amplifying bupropion's effects on the reward system (mesolimbic dopamine) and energy balance [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Both components are >90% absorbed, but naltrexone has a high first-pass effect (5-6% bioavailability). High-fat meals significantly increase exposure.
    - **Distribution:** Low protein binding for naltrexone (21%); higher for bupropion (84%).
    - **Metabolism:** Naltrexone to 6-beta-naltrexol (active). Bupropion to hydroxybupropion, threohydrobupropion, and erythrohydrobupropion (via CYP2B6 and other pathways).
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal. Half-life: Naltrexone ~5 hours; Bupropion ~21 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **NB-301, 302, 303, 304:** Four Phase 3 trials (n=4,536) showed significant weight loss compared to placebo. 
- **Results:** Mean weight loss in NB-301 was -5.4% (vs -1.3% placebo). In subjects completing 56 weeks, weight loss was -8.1%.
- **Responders:** 50.8% of patients lost ≥5% body weight by Week 16. These "Week 16 Responders" achieved an average loss of 11.3% at one year [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Handling:** Do not freeze [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging.
