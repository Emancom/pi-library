# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**. 
- Information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian, Japanese) was not provided in the source material; where relevant, these are marked as "Not specified in available sources."
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a clinical synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not explicitly in source, but standard classification).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US]; Manufactured by Baxter Pharmaceuticals India Private Ltd.

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Metabolic):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **ESRD-Related Carnitine Deficiency:** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease (ESRD) undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - Administration: Slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection or by infusion.
    - Loading doses may be used in severe metabolic crisis, followed by equivalent doses over 24 hours.
    - Frequency: Every 3 or 4 hours (q3h or q4h), and never less than every 6 hours (q6h).
    - Maximum reported dose: 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - Administration: Slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - Dose adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations (normal range: 40 µmol/L to 50 µmol/L). Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

### Monitoring
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations.
- Perform weekly and monthly monitoring including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, particularly in ESRD patients on dialysis. Reactions can occur within minutes of IV administration. Discontinue treatment immediately upon occurrence of a severe reaction [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Levocarnitine (oral or IV) has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been reported [US].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** While this document covers the injection, it is noted that chronic high-dose oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **General/Common:** Transient nausea and vomiting. Less frequent effects include body odor and gastritis [US].
- **ESRD Clinical Trial Data (≥5% Incidence):**
    - **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (5–21%), headache (3–37%), injection site reaction (27–38%), pain (21–35%), fever (5–12%).
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), chest pain (6–15%), tachycardia (5–9%).
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%).
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%).
    - **Neurological:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%).
- **Postmarketing Experience:** Seizures and serious hypersensitivity reactions (anaphylaxis) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concurrently with levocarnitine. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating treatment or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned (Source: [US] FDA). Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate human studies exist. Use only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations after exogenous administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant should be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Distribution half-life: 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Elimination:** Mean terminal elimination half-life: 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours [US].
    - **Bioavailability (Oral):** 15.1% to 15.9% [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg post-dialysis), trough levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels. Note: While carnitine levels increased, the effect on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
