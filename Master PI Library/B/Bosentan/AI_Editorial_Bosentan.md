# Editorial Product Information: Bosentan

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document provided by the following jurisdiction:
    - **Australian** (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically accurate editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Important:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing within any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Bosentan (as monohydrate)
- **Chemical Class:** Endothelin receptor antagonist (ERA); pyrimidine derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Bosentan Cipla** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** C02KX01
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - 62.5 mg film-coated tablets: Cream to pale yellow, circular, biconvex, debossed with “62.5”.
    - 125 mg film-coated tablets: Cream to pale yellow, oblong, biconvex, debossed with “125”. [Australian]
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Cipla Australia Pty Ltd. [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
**Bosentan** is indicated for the treatment of:
- Idiopathic pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH)
- Familial PAH
- PAH associated with scleroderma (connective tissue disease)
- PAH associated with congenital systemic-to-pulmonary shunts, including Eisenmenger’s physiology.
- **Severity Specification:** Indicated for patients with WHO functional Class II, III, or IV symptoms [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Adult Dosing
- **Initiation:** 62.5 mg twice daily for 4 weeks.
- **Maintenance:** Increase to 125 mg twice daily. Doses exceeding 125 mg twice daily have not demonstrated additional benefit but increase the risk of hepatotoxicity.
- **Administration:** Administer morning and evening, with or without food.

### Titration and Discontinuation
- **Up-titration:** Transition from starting to maintenance dose occurs after the first 4 weeks.
- **Discontinuation:** To avoid clinical deterioration, consider a gradual dose reduction (e.g., 62.5 mg twice daily for 3 to 7 days) while intensifying monitoring.

### Paediatric Dosing (Aged 3 years and over)
Dosing is based on body weight [Australian]:
| Body Weight | Starting Dose (First 4 weeks) | Maintenance Dose (Week 5 onwards) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 10 to 20 kg | 31.25 mg once daily | 31.25 mg twice daily |
| >20 to 40 kg | 31.25 mg twice daily | 62.5 mg twice daily |
| >40 kg | 62.5 mg twice daily | 125 mg twice daily |

- **Low Body Weight (>12 years):** For patients <40 kg but >12 years old, the recommended dose is 62.5 mg twice daily.

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy:** Categorically contraindicated due to teratogenicity [Australian Category X].
- **Hepatic Function:** Moderate to severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class B or C) or baseline aminotransferases (ALT/AST) >3 x upper limit of normal (ULN).
- **Concomitant Medications:** 
    - **Cyclosporine A** (markedly increases bosentan plasma levels).
    - **Glibenclamide** (increases risk of liver enzyme elevations).
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to bosentan or any excipients (e.g., DRESS, anaphylaxis, angioedema).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- **Teratogenicity:** **Bosentan** is expected to cause major birth defects. Pregnancy must be excluded before and during treatment [Australian].
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Rare cases of hepatic failure and cirrhosis have been reported. Liver function must be monitored monthly.

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Liver Function:** Measure AST/ALT levels prior to initiation and monthly thereafter.
- **Pregnancy Testing:** Monthly testing for females of childbearing potential.
- **Haematology:** Check haemoglobin/haematocrit at 1 and 3 months post-initiation, then every 3 months.

### Clinical Precautions
- **Fluid Retention:** Monitor for weight gain and peripheral oedema, particularly in patients with pre-existing left ventricular dysfunction. Diuretics may be required.
- **Pulmonary Veno-Occlusive Disease (PVOD):** If pulmonary oedema occurs, consider the possibility of associated PVOD.
- **Anaemia:** Exercise caution in patients with baseline haemoglobin >30% below the lower limit of normal.
- **Hypotension:** Close monitoring is required for patients with pre-existing hypotension (systolic BP <85 mmHg).

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Data
- **Very Common (>10%):** Headache (11.1%), Abnormal liver function tests (post-marketing).
- **Common (1–10%):** Nasopharyngitis, URTI, peripheral oedema, anaemia, flushing, hypotension, palpitations, dyspepsia/diarrhoea, pruritus/erythema.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, leukopenia, jaundice, hepatitis.
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Liver failure, hepatic cirrhosis, angioedema, anaphylaxis, DRESS.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Dose-related elevations in aminotransferases are typically reversible upon dose reduction or interruption but can progress to liver failure.
- **Haematological:** Clinically significant decreases in haemoglobin and haematocrit (57% of patients experienced a decrease ≥1 g/dL) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP3A4 and CYP2C9 Inducer:** **Bosentan** induces these enzymes and may decrease the plasma concentration of drugs like simvastatin, sildenafil, and hormonal contraceptives.
- **CYP3A4 and CYP2C9 Substrate:** Inhibitors of these enzymes (e.g., ketoconazole, fluconazole, ritonavir) may increase **bosentan** exposure.

### Specific Management Notes
- **Cyclosporine A:** Increases **bosentan** levels by 30-fold initially; **bosentan** decreases cyclosporine levels by 50%. Co-administration is contraindicated.
- **Hormonal Contraceptives:** Efficacy is reduced. Patients must use additional or alternative non-hormonal contraception.
- **Sildenafil:** Bosentan reduces sildenafil AUC by 63%; sildenafil increases bosentan AUC by 50%. Caution is advised.
- **Warfarin:** **Bosentan** may slightly decrease warfarin levels, but no routine dose adjustment is required; however, intensified INR monitoring is recommended.
- **Rifampicin:** Potent induction may lead to significantly reduced **bosentan** efficacy.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category X]. Highly teratogenic. Pregnancy must be prevented during treatment and for at least 3 months after stopping **bosentan**. Prescribers must ensure reliable, non-hormonal-only contraception (double barrier recommended).
- **Breastfeeding:** **Bosentan** has been detected in human milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies show testicular tubular atrophy. While human studies did not show a systematic effect on sperm count, a long-term impact on male fertility (especially in children) cannot be excluded.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Weight-based dosing established for children ≥3 years [Australian]. Limited data for infants <3 years.
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies were insufficient to determine if those >65 respond differently. Caution is advised in dose selection due to increased frequency of comorbidities.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required. Exposure to metabolites increases 2-fold in severe impairment, but this is not deemed clinically significant.
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild (Child-Pugh A):** No dose adjustment, but increased risk of hepatotoxicity.
    - **Moderate to Severe (Child-Pugh B/C):** Contraindicated.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Dual endothelin receptor antagonist. It competitively binds to both $ET_A$ and $ET_B$ receptors in the vascular endothelium and smooth muscle, blocking the potent vasoconstrictive and proliferative effects of endothelin-1.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** 41% bioavailability; peak plasma levels reached in 3–5 hours.
    - **Distribution:** >98% protein bound (mainly albumin).
    - **Metabolism:** Hepatic via CYP3A4 and CYP2C9. Three metabolites identified (one is active).
    - **Elimination:** Biliary excretion; <3% recovered in urine. Terminal half-life is approximately 5.4 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **BREATHE-1:** A pivotal study in WHO Class III/IV PAH showing a placebo-subtracted 6-minute walk distance (6MWT) increase of 44 metres at 16 weeks.
- **EARLY Study:** Focused on WHO Class II PAH. It demonstrated a 22.6% reduction in pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR), although the 6MWT improvement did not reach statistical significance.
- **BREATHE-5:** Conducted in Eisenmenger’s physiology; **bosentan** increased oxygen saturation by 1% and improved 6MWT by 53 metres without worsening hypoxaemia.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Handling:** Protect from moisture [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to expiry date on packaging.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** [Australian: Schedule 4 - Prescription Only Medicine].
- **Dispensing:** Should be administered under the supervision of a physician experienced in PAH management.
