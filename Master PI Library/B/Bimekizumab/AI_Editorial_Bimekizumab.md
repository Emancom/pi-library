# Editorial Product Information: Bimekizumab

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (TGA).
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of the official Product Information (PI) for bimekizumab, designed as a coherent reference for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not replace the official PI approved for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current documentation for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Bimekizumab (recombinant humanized IgG1/κ monoclonal antibody).
- **Chemical Class:** Interleukin-17A (IL-17A) and Interleukin-17F (IL-17F) inhibitor.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Bimzelx** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L04AC21.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Solution for injection: 160 mg in 1 mL (pre-filled syringe or pen).
    - Solution for injection: 320 mg in 2 mL (pre-filled syringe or pen).
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Subcutaneous injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** UCB Pharma [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Plaque Psoriasis:** Treatment of moderate to severe plaque psoriasis in adults who are candidates for systemic therapy or phototherapy [Australian-approved].
- **Psoriatic Arthritis:** Treatment of active psoriatic arthritis in adults with inadequate response or intolerance to previous disease-modifying antirheumatic drug (DMARD) therapy [Australian-approved].
- **Non-radiographic Axial Spondyloarthritis (nr-axSpA):** Treatment of active nr-axSpA in adults with objective signs of inflammation (elevated CRP and/or MRI) who have had an inadequate response or intolerance to NSAIDs [Australian-approved].
- **Ankylosing Spondylitis (AS):** Treatment of active AS in adults with inadequate response or intolerance to conventional therapy [Australian-approved].
- **Hidradenitis Suppurativa (HS):** Treatment of moderate to severe HS in adults with an inadequate response to conventional systemic HS therapy [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Plaque Psoriasis:** 320 mg (two 160 mg injections or one 320 mg injection) at Weeks 0, 4, 8, 12, 16, and every 8 weeks thereafter. For patients ≥120 kg with moderate to severe psoriasis, 320 mg every 4 weeks after Week 16 may be considered.
- **Psoriatic Arthritis:** 160 mg every 4 weeks. If coexistent moderate to severe plaque psoriasis is present, the psoriasis dosing (320 mg every 8 weeks after Week 16) is recommended. If joint response is insufficient at this dose, 160 mg every 4 weeks can be considered.
- **Axial Spondyloarthritis (nr-axSpA and AS):** 160 mg every 4 weeks.
- **Hidradenitis Suppurativa:** 320 mg every 4 weeks. For more rapid disease control, 320 mg every 2 weeks up to Week 16, followed by 320 mg every 4 weeks, may be considered.

### Administration Instructions
- Administer via subcutaneous injection to the thigh, abdomen, or upper arm.
- Rotate injection sites; avoid areas of active psoriasis, or skin that is tender, bruised, indurated, or erythematous.
- For a 320 mg dose, two separate 160 mg injections should be administered at different sites.

### Missed Doses
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to the active substance or any excipients (glycine, sodium acetate trihydrate, acetic acid, polysorbate 80).
- Simultaneous administration of live vaccines.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Infections:** **Bimzelx** increases the risk of infections, particularly upper respiratory tract infections and mucocutaneous candidiasis (oral, vulvovaginal, and oesophageal). Avoid initiation in patients with clinically important active infections. Use caution in patients with chronic or recurrent infections.
- **Tuberculosis (TB):** Evaluate patients for TB before starting treatment. Do not administer to patients with active TB. Monitor for signs of TB during therapy. Consider anti-TB therapy for patients with a history of latent or active TB without confirmed adequate treatment.
- **Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD):** New-onset or exacerbations of IBD have been reported. Monitor patients closely; use caution in patients with existing IBD.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Serious hypersensitivity reactions have occurred. Discontinue immediately if a reaction is suspected.
- **Vaccinations:** Complete all age-appropriate immunizations prior to treatment. Avoid live vaccines. Non-live vaccines may be administered; a single 320 mg dose did not affect the response to the seasonal influenza vaccine when given two weeks prior.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Very Common (>10%)
- Upper respiratory tract infections (most commonly nasopharyngitis).

### Common (1–10%)
- **Fungal Infections:** Oral candidiasis, Oropharyngeal candidiasis, Vulvovaginal candidiasis [in females], Tinea pedis.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastroenteritis, Diarrhoea.
- **Nervous System:** Headache.
- **Skin:** Acne, Folliculitis, Rash, Dermatitis contact, Eczema, Pruritus.
- **General:** Fatigue, Injection site reactions (erythema, pain, swelling), Pyrexia.
- **Other:** Urinary tract infection, Arthralgia.

### Uncommon (0.1–1%)
- Uveitis, Conjunctivitis, Neutropenia, Vulvovaginal mycotic infection, Oral herpes.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Serious Infections:** Reported in approximately 0.3% of patients in short-term studies and 1.5% in long-term psoriasis studies.
- **Neutropenia:** Grade 3/4 neutropenia was observed in 1% of patients in long-term studies.
- **Hepatic Transaminase Elevations:** Increases >3x ULN were reported, particularly in psoriatic arthritis and HS studies; most cases were transient and resolved without treatment discontinuation.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP450 Substrates:** The clearance of cytokines (IL-17A/F) by bimekizumab may normalise CYP450 enzyme levels. Monitor drugs with a narrow therapeutic index (e.g., warfarin) when starting or stopping **Bimzelx**.
- **cDMARDs:** Concomitant use of methotrexate or prior biologic exposure does not clinically impact bimekizumab clearance.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. There are no adequate studies in pregnant women. Animal data showed no developmental toxicity, but bimekizumab is known to cross the placenta. Use only if potential benefits outweigh risks.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if bimekizumab is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution; a decision should be made to either discontinue breastfeeding or the drug, considering the benefit of the drug to the mother.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no harmful effects on reproductive organs or fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children and adolescents under 18 years have not been established.
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment is required for patients ≥65 years.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specifically studied; however, dose adjustments are not considered necessary as monoclonal antibodies are not primarily cleared by these organs.
- **Body Weight:** Patients ≥120 kg may have lower drug exposure. In plaque psoriasis, Q4W maintenance dosing may be considered for this group after the initial 16 weeks.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bimekizumab is a humanized IgG1/κ monoclonal antibody that selectively binds to and neutralises IL-17A, IL-17F, and IL-17AF cytokines. These cytokines drive chronic inflammation in tissues related to psoriasis and spondyloarthritis. Dual inhibition suppresses inflammatory gene expression more effectively than IL-17A inhibition alone.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is approximately 70.1%. Peak plasma concentration is reached in 3–4 days.
    - **Distribution:** Steady-state volume of distribution is 11.2 L in psoriasis patients.
    - **Metabolism:** Degraded into small peptides and amino acids via catabolic pathways.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is 23 days (20 days in HS patients). Steady-state is reached at Week 16 with Q4W dosing.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Plaque Psoriasis (BE VIVID, BE READY, BE SURE):** Bimekizumab demonstrated superior PASI 90 and IGA 0/1 responses at Week 16 compared to placebo, ustekinumab, and adalimumab. Long-term efficacy was maintained through 4 years in open-label extensions.
- **Psoriatic Arthritis (BE OPTIMAL, BE COMPLETE):** Significant improvements in ACR 50 and physical function (HAQ-DI) were observed at Week 16 in both biologic-naïve and TNF-inhibitor-experienced patients.
- **Axial Spondyloarthritis (BE MOBILE 1 & 2):** Bimekizumab achieved significant ASAS 40 responses at Week 16 in both nr-axSpA and AS, with sustained improvements through Week 52.
- **Hidradenitis Suppurativa (BE HEARD I & II):** Higher HiSCR50 and HiSCR75 response rates were achieved at Week 16 compared to placebo, with significant reductions in abscess/nodule counts.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store at 2°C to 8°C (refrigerate). Do not freeze.
- Keep in the original carton to protect from light.
- **Room Temperature Excursion:** May be stored at room temperature (up to 25°C) for a single period of up to 30 days. Discard if not used within this 30-day period.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
