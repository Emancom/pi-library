# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine (Intravenous)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data and regulatory guidance into a single, coherent reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource only. It is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing in any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (also known as L-carnitine).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative [(R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt].
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Generic), Carnitor [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance haemodialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Doses should be administered every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
- **Monitoring:** Subsequent daily doses should be adjusted based on clinical response and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### General Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature (25°C) when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occur within minutes of IV administration. Discontinue treatment immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of plasma carnitine, blood chemistries, and vital signs is recommended for patients with metabolic disorders [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Levocarnitine):** While this PI covers the injection, it notes that high-dose oral levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency and may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect profiles differ significantly based on the underlying pathology (ESRD vs. metabolic disorders).

### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
In double-blind, placebo-controlled trials, the following events occurred at a frequency ≥5% [US]:
- **Very Common (>10%):**
    - **General:** Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (30–35%), headache (22%), chest pain (12–15%), flu syndrome (25–29%), accidental injury (10–12%).
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (14–19% [Note: Placebo rate was 19%]).
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhea (10–35%), vomiting (15–21%), nausea (5–12%).
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%).
    - **Nervous:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%).
- **Common (1–10%):** Abdominal pain, asthenia, fever, tachycardia, palpitations, anemia, hypercalcemia, peripheral edema, weight changes, depression, insomnia, pruritus, rhinitis, and eye disorders.

### Postmarketing Experience
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; body odor and gastritis (less frequent).
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (regardless of prior history).
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified, but text describes no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose]. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Not specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after administration. Risks of excess intake by the infant must be weighed against maternal benefit; consideration may be given to discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal reproductive studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders (50 mg/kg) is applicable to paediatric patients. In premature infants and newborns, secondary carnitine deficiency is defined by plasma concentrations below age-related normals [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed; however, general caution regarding renal function is prudent given the renal clearance of the drug.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for ESRD patients. Dose adjustments in ESRD are based on pre-dialysis trough concentrations. High doses of oral metabolites (TMA/TMAO) may accumulate in renal failure [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with organic acidemias, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following a 20 mg/kg IV bolus, the mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine over 24 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic/Dose-Ranging Studies:** In ESRD patients, doses of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg after dialysis were shown to increase trough levocarnitine concentrations from baseline (approx. 21 µmol/L) to supra-normal levels (ranging from 116 to 456 µmol/L) after 12–24 weeks of therapy.
- **Clinical Outcomes:** While IV levocarnitine effectively increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the effects on specific clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, cardiomyopathy) in this population have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C.
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial; the formulation contains no preservative [US].
