# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the following jurisdiction:
    - **United States (FDA)**: Product Information for Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation).
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals based on the available regulatory documentation.
- **Note**: This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Levocarnitine ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
- **Chemical Class**: Carrier molecule / amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Names**: Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code**: A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength**: Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL).
- **Route of Administration**: Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism**: For the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD)**: For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose**: 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Loading Dose**: In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency**: Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours (q3h or q4h) and must not be administered less frequently than every 6 hours (q6h).
- **Maximum Dose**: The highest recorded dose administered is 300 mg/kg.
- **Monitoring**: It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is advised.

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose**: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg of dry body weight.
- **Administration**: Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustment**: Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are within or above the normal range (40–50 µmol/L).

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity**: None known at the time of publication, though hypersensitivity reactions have been reported post-administration [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions**: Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have occurred, particularly in dialysis patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If severe, discontinue immediately and do not re-administer without careful risk-benefit analysis [US].
- **Seizure Activity**: Levocarnitine (both oral and IV) has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with a history, there may be an increase in seizure frequency or severity [US].
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulations)**: Safety of high-dose *oral* levocarnitine has not been established in patients with severe renal impairment or ESRD, as toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) may accumulate due to impaired urinary excretion [US].
- **Monitoring**: INR should be monitored in patients receiving concurrent warfarin therapy.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects
The following effects have been observed, though frequencies are often confounded by underlying pathology in ESRD populations:
- **Gastrointestinal**: Transient nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, diarrhea, and gastritis [US].
- **General**: Body odor (associated with high doses) [US].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Neurologic**: Seizures [US].
- **Immunologic**: Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin**: Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly. Frequent monitoring of INR is recommended upon initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: [US: Category not assigned via letter system]. Animal studies (rats/rabbits) at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate human studies exist. Use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: Not specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations. The risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefit of maternal supplementation. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].
- **Fertility**: No mutagenic effects were observed in *Salmonella*, *S. cerevisiae*, or *S. pombe* [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg for metabolic disorders); specific age-related safety profiles were not further delineated beyond newborn/premature definitions of deficiency [US].
- **Elderly Use**: Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment**: IV administration is indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD. However, oral levocarnitine should be avoided in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production.
- **Pharmacodynamics**: It promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted.
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Distribution**: Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism**: Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and [3H]-γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination**: Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Studies**: In ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis trough concentrations from ~20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Clinical Outcome Note**: While IV levocarnitine effectively increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, its effect on specific clinical outcomes (signs and symptoms of deficiency) in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Handling**: Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability**: Compatible with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL. Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature [US].
- **Preservatives**: Contains no preservatives; discard any unused portion [US].
