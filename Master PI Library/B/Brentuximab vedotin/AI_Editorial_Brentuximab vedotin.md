# Editorial Product Information: Brentuximab Vedotin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on Product Information (PI) documents from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically accurate editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the Australian PI was provided for synthesis; thus, regional variations are limited to this source.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Brentuximab vedotin, a CD30-directed antibody-drug conjugate (ADC).
- **Brand Name:** **Adcetris** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Powder for injection; 50 mg of brentuximab vedotin per single-use vial. Following reconstitution, the solution contains 5 mg/mL [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Takeda Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
### Hodgkin Lymphoma (HL)
- **Previously Untreated CD30+ Stage III or IV HL:** In combination with doxorubicin, vinblastine, and dacarbazine (AVD) [Australian].
- **Previously Untreated CD30+ Stage IIB (with large mediastinal mass/extranodal disease), Stage III, or Stage IV HL:** In combination with etoposide, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, dacarbazine, and dexamethasone (BrECADD) [Australian].
- **High-Risk Relapse/Progression Post-ASCT:** For CD30+ HL patients at increased risk following autologous stem cell transplant [Australian].
- **Relapsed or Refractory (R/R) CD30+ HL:** 
    - Following ASCT, or 
    - Following at least two prior therapies when ASCT or multi-agent chemotherapy is not a viable option [Australian].

### Peripheral T-cell Lymphoma (PTCL)
- **Previously Untreated CD30+ PTCL:** In combination with cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, and prednisone (CHP) [Australian].
- **Relapsed or Refractory Systemic Anaplastic Large Cell Lymphoma (sALCL):** Monotherapy [Australian].

### Cutaneous T-cell Lymphoma (CTCL)
- **CD30+ CTCL:** For patients who have received at least one prior systemic therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing Principles
- **Weight-Based Calculation:** Dosing is based on actual body weight. For patients weighing >100 kg, the dose calculation must be capped at 100 kg (maximum dose based on 1.8 mg/kg is 180 mg) [Australian].
- **Administration:** Infuse intravenously over 30 minutes. Do **not** administer as an IV push or bolus [Australian].

### Indication-Specific Dosing
- **Untreated HL (A+AVD):** 1.2 mg/kg on Days 1 and 15 of each 28-day cycle for 6 cycles [Australian].
- **Untreated HL (BrECADD):** 1.8 mg/kg every 3 weeks for up to 6 cycles [Australian].
- **Untreated PTCL (A+CHP):** 1.8 mg/kg every 3 weeks for 6 to 8 cycles [Australian].
- **R/R HL, Post-ASCT HL, R/R sALCL, and CTCL:** 1.8 mg/kg every 3 weeks. For R/R indications, treat until 8–16 cycles are completed [Australian].

### Prophylaxis Requirements
- **G-CSF:** Recommended as primary prophylaxis starting from the first dose for all patients receiving A+AVD or A+CHP. For BrECADD, G-CSF must be initiated on Day 5 of each cycle [Australian].
- **Antibiotics/Steroids (BrECADD):** Antibiotic prophylaxis is required 3 times weekly throughout treatment. Dexamethasone pretreatment for 4 days before Cycle 1 is recommended for patients >40 years [Australian].

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to brentuximab vedotin or any excipients [Australian].
- **Bleomycin Co-administration:** Combined use with bleomycin is strictly contraindicated due to a high incidence of pulmonary toxicity [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy (PML):** John Cunningham virus (JCV) reactivation resulting in fatal PML has been reported. Monitor for new neurological, cognitive, or behavioral symptoms. Permanently discontinue if PML is confirmed [Australian].
- **Pulmonary Toxicity:** Cases of pneumonitis, interstitial lung disease, and ARDS have occurred. Hold treatment for new or worsening symptoms and consider corticosteroids [Australian].
- **Peripheral Neuropathy (PN):** Both sensory and motor neuropathy may occur; typically cumulative. Monitor and manage with dose delays, reductions, or discontinuation [Australian].
- **Hematological Toxicities:** Severe and prolonged (≥1 week) Grade 3/4 neutropenia and thrombocytopenia can occur. Monitor CBC prior to every dose [Australian].
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Monitor liver function tests (LFTs) before and during treatment. Pre-existing liver disease increases risk [Australian].
- **Serious Infections:** Opportunistic infections (e.g., Pneumocystis jiroveci pneumonia, CMV reactivation) have been reported [Australian].
- **Infusion-Related Reactions (IRR):** Anaphylaxis and IRRs occur. Pre-medicate with paracetamol, antihistamines, and corticosteroids if a prior reaction occurred [Australian].
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** SJS, TEN, and DRESS have been reported. Discontinue treatment if SCARs occur [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal Complications:** Serious events including perforation, ileus, and neutropenic colitis have been reported, particularly in patients with GI involvement by lymphoma [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Table (Representative Data)
| System Organ Class | Very Common (>10%) | Common (1–10%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Infections** | Upper respiratory tract infection, general infections | Herpes zoster, pneumonia, oral candidiasis |
| **Blood** | Neutropenia, Anaemia | Thrombocytopenia, Febrile neutropenia |
| **Nervous System** | Peripheral sensory/motor neuropathy, Dizziness | Demyelinating polyneuropathy (Uncommon) |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea, Diarrhoea, Vomiting, Constipation, Abdominal pain | Stomatitis |
| **General** | Fatigue, Pyrexia | Chills, Infusion-related reactions |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy (PML), acute pancreatitis (sometimes fatal), Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), and Tumour Lysis Syndrome (TLS) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Bleomycin:** Contraindicated due to pulmonary toxicity [Australian].
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors (e.g., Ketoconazole):** Co-administration increased MMAE (active metabolite) exposure by ~73%. Monitor closely for neutropenia [Australian].
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., Rifampicin):** Co-administration reduced MMAE exposure by ~31% [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Substrates:** Brentuximab vedotin is not expected to alter the metabolism of drugs processed by CYP3A4 (e.g., midazolam) [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Can cause fetal harm based on animal data (embryofetal toxicities, malformations).
    - **Contraception:** Women of childbearing potential must use two effective methods during and for 6 months after treatment [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Data regarding excretion in human milk is unavailable. A decision should be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug, weighing the benefit to the mother against the risk to the infant [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies show potential for male reproductive impairment. Men are advised not to father a child during treatment and for 6 months post-dose; sperm cryopreservation should be considered before starting therapy [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients <18 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** 
    - No adjustment required for CTCL patients ≥65 years. 
    - Safety and efficacy not established in patients >60 years for the BrECADD regimen [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Mild/Moderate:** No specific adjustment in monotherapy.
    - **Severe (CrCl <30 mL/min):** Consider a starting dose of 1.2 mg/kg every 3 weeks in monotherapy. Avoid combination therapy in severe renal impairment [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** MMAE exposure increases ~2.3-fold. 
    - **Mild (Child-Pugh A):** 1.2 mg/kg every 3 weeks (monotherapy) or 0.9 mg/kg (A+AVD).
    - **Moderate/Severe (Child-Pugh B/C):** Avoid combination therapy. In monotherapy, use 1.2 mg/kg with extreme caution [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** An ADC comprising a chimeric IgG1 antibody (cAC10) specific for CD30, a protease-cleavable linker, and the microtubule-disrupting agent monomethyl auristatin E (MMAE). Upon binding CD30, the complex is internalized and MMAE is released, leading to cell cycle arrest and apoptosis [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** MMAE is 68–82% protein bound. Volume of distribution for ADC is ~6–10 L.
    - **Metabolism:** MMAE is a substrate of CYP3A4.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily via faeces (~72% of recovered MMAE). The half-life of ADC is ~4–6 days [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study C25003 (ECHELON-1):** Randomised trial in untreated Stage III/IV HL. A+AVD showed a significant improvement in modified PFS vs. ABVD (HR 0.77; p=0.035) [Australian].
- **Study HD21:** Comparison of BrECADD vs. eBEACOPP in untreated HL. BrECADD met superiority for reduced treatment-related morbidity and non-inferiority for PFS [Australian].
- **Study SGN35-014 (ECHELON-2):** Randomised trial in untreated PTCL. A+CHP demonstrated superior PFS and OS compared to CHOP (HR 0.71 for PFS) [Australian].
- **Study C25001 (ALCANZA):** Phase 3 trial in CD30+ CTCL. Adcetris showed significantly higher ORR4 (56.3% vs. 12.5%) compared to physician’s choice (methotrexate or bexarotene) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate). Do not freeze. Keep in the original carton to protect from light [Australian].
- **Reconstituted/Diluted Solution:** Chemical and physical stability demonstrated for 24 hours at 2°C to 8°C. From a microbiological standpoint, use immediately [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4) [Australian].
