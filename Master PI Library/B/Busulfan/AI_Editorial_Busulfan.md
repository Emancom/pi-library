# Editorial Product Information: Busulfan

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a structured format.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes for healthcare professionals. It does not replace the official PI or local clinical guidelines and should not be used as a substitute for the official prescribing information in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Busulfan (a bifunctional alkylating agent).
- **Chemical Class:** Methanesulfonate (cytotoxic agent).
- **Brand Names:** **Busulfan Accord** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01AB01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Concentrated injection for infusion; 60 mg/10 mL (6 mg/mL) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion via central venous catheter only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Accord Healthcare Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **HSCT Conditioning:** Indicated for use as part of a conditioning regimen (in combination with cyclophosphamide, melphalan, or fludarabine) prior to conventional haematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Myeloablative HSCT:** 3.2 mg/kg/day administered for four consecutive days (Total dose: 12.8 mg/kg).
- **Administration Schedule:** May be given as a single daily 3-hour infusion, or in divided doses every 12 hours (8 doses total) or every 6 hours (16 doses total) [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing (0 to 17 years)
Dosing is weight-based to achieve consistent exposure [Australian]:
- **< 9 kg:** 4.0 mg/kg/day
- **9 to < 16 kg:** 4.8 mg/kg/day
- **16 to 23 kg:** 4.4 mg/kg/day
- **> 23 to 34 kg:** 3.8 mg/kg/day
- **> 34 kg:** 3.2 mg/kg/day

### Administration Instructions
- **Dilution Required:** Must be diluted to approximately 0.5 mg/mL using 0.9% sodium chloride or 5% glucose.
- **Infusion Method:** Administer over 2 to 3 hours via central venous catheter. Do **not** administer via rapid IV, bolus, or peripheral injection.
- **Incompatibility:** Do **not** use infusion components containing polycarbonate [Australian].

### Mandatory Pre-medication
- **Anticonvulsants:** To prevent seizures, administer anticonvulsant prophylaxis (e.g., phenytoin or benzodiazepines) starting 12 hours prior to busulfan and continuing 24 hours after the final dose.
- **Antiemetics:** Administer prior to the first dose and continue on a fixed schedule [Australian].

### Therapeutic Drug Monitoring (TDM)
- TDM is recommended to adjust subsequent doses and achieve a target AUC of 900 to 1500 µMol-minute. Blood sampling should occur at the completion of infusion and at 1, 2, and 4 hours post-infusion [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to busulfan or any excipients (e.g., dimethylacetamide, macrogol 400).
- Pregnancy [Australian].
- Breastfeeding [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Myelosuppression:** Profound and universal. Severe granulocytopenia, thrombocytopenia, and anaemia occur in nearly all patients. Frequent CBC monitoring is required until recovery [Australian].
- **Hepatic Veno-Occlusive Disease (HVOD):** A major risk. Monitor serum transaminases, alkaline phosphatase, and bilirubin daily until day 28 post-transplant. Risk increases with prior radiation or extensive chemotherapy [Australian].
- **Seizures:** High-dose therapy is associated with central nervous system toxicity. Prophylactic anticonvulsants are required [Australian].
- **Pulmonary Toxicity:** May cause interstitial pulmonary fibrosis (busulfan lung). Caution in patients with a history of mediastinal or pulmonary radiation [Australian].
- **Cardiac Toxicity:** Cardiac tamponade has been reported in paediatric patients (primarily those with thalassaemia receiving oral formulations) [Australian].
- **Fanconi Anaemia:** Use with extreme caution as these cells are hypersensitive to cross-linking agents [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Leukopenia, neutropenia, anaemia, thrombocytopenia, pancytopenia, HVOD, nausea, vomiting, stomatitis, diarrhoea, abdominal pain, fever, chills, insomnia, headache, tachycardia, hypertension, rash, and increased LFTs. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Confusion, pneumonia, arrhythmia, atrial fibrillation, cardiomegaly, respiratory failure, alveolar haemorrhage, oesophagitis, erythema, and haematuria. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Delirium, hallucinations, encephalopathy, cerebral haemorrhage, seizures, bradycardia, femoral artery thrombosis, and gastrointestinal haemorrhage. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Profound myeloablation, HVOD, and fatal interstitial pulmonary fibrosis [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Itraconazole:** Reduces busulfan clearance by approximately 20-25%; monitor for toxicity.
- **Metronidazole:** Increases plasma levels of busulfan, increasing the risk of toxicity.
- **Phenytoin:** Increases busulfan clearance via induction of glutathione-S-transferase (GST).
- **Paracetamol:** May decrease glutathione levels, potentially reducing busulfan clearance if used within 72 hours of administration.
- **Deferasirox:** Observed to decrease busulfan clearance; discontinue iron chelators well in advance of conditioning.
- **Cyclophosphamide/Melphalan:** To reduce toxicity, busulfan should be completed at least 24 hours before the administration of these agents [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Busulfan is a known teratogen. It is contraindicated. Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during and for 6 months after treatment.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. It is unknown if busulfan or its solvent (DMA) is excreted in human milk.
- **Fertility:** Can cause irreversible infertility in both sexes. Ovarian suppression/amenorrhoea is common in females; azoospermia and testicular atrophy occur in males. Cryo-conservation of sperm is recommended prior to treatment [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for use from birth to 17 years. Use weight-based dosing (see Section 3). High concentrations of the solvent DMA in this formulation may pose unique risks to children [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Data are limited for patients >60 years. Safe use has been documented in patients >50 years, particularly in reduced-intensity regimens [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specifically studied; however, as only 30% is excreted renally, dose modification is generally not recommended, though caution is advised [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specifically studied. Use with extreme caution in patients with pre-existing impairment due to the high risk of HVOD [Australian].
- **Obese Patients:** In adults, dosing should be based on **Adjusted Ideal Body Weight (AIBW)** [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A bifunctional alkylating agent. It releases methanesulphonate groups to produce carbonium ions that alkylate DNA, leading to DNA cross-linking and cytotoxicity.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Profoundly myeloablative.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** 7% reversible protein binding; 32% irreversible binding to albumin. Crosses the blood-brain barrier (levels comparable to plasma).
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised in the liver via conjugation with glutathione (GST-mediated).
    - **Excretion:** Approximately 30% excreted in urine over 48 hours; negligible faecal elimination.
    - **Half-life:** Terminal half-life is approximately 2.8 to 3.9 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adult Data:** Based on trials OMC-BUS 3 and 4. Busulfan + cyclophosphamide achieved 100% myeloablation with median engraftment by day 10–13. One-year survival rates were 67–70%.
- **Paediatric Data:** Trial F60002 IN 1 01 showed 100% myeloablation with a weight-based dosing regimen. Two-year survival was approximately 77–79% [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Vials:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (refrigerate). Do **not** freeze.
- **Diluted Solution:** Chemical and physical stability demonstrated for 8 hours at 20°C–25°C. To reduce microbiological hazard, use as soon as possible; if necessary, store at 2°C–8°C for no more than 15 hours [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
