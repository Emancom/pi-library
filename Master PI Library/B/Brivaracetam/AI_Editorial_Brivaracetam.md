# Editorial Product Information: Brivaracetam

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following jurisdiction's Product Information:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: Briviact (Brivaracetam) Solution for Injection.
- Information from other major jurisdictions (e.g., US, European, Canadian, Japanese) was not provided for this specific editorial and is therefore noted as "Not specified in available sources" where applicable.
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a synthesis for clinical reference and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Brivaracetam (chemical class: pyrrolidone derivative).
- **Brand Names**: **Briviact** [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: N03AX23.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**: 
    - Solution for injection: 50 mg brivaracetam per 5 mL (10 mg/mL) [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration**: Intravenous (IV). (Oral formulations are mentioned as interchangeable but specific PI data for oral forms was not provided).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: UCB Pharma [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Adjunctive Therapy**: Indicated as add-on treatment for partial-onset seizures, with or without secondary generalisation, in patients aged 4 years and older with epilepsy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Effective Dose Range**: 50 mg to 200 mg per day.
- **Initial Dose**: May be initiated at 50 mg/day or 100 mg/day based on clinical assessment.
- **Titration**: Dose adjustments may be made in increments of 50 mg/day every 2 weeks based on clinical response and tolerability.
- **Frequency**: Total daily dose is administered in two equally divided doses (morning and evening).

### Administration Instructions
- **IV Administration**: May be administered as an intravenous bolus without dilution or as a 15-minute intravenous infusion.
- **Compatibility**: Physically compatible with Sodium Chloride 0.9%, Glucose 5%, and Lactated Ringer’s solution. Also compatible with several co-administered compounds including lacosamide, valproate, phenytoin, and midazolam [Australian].
- **Switching**: Patients may be converted from oral to IV (and vice versa) directly without dose adjustment, maintaining the same total daily dose and frequency.
- **Duration**: Clinical experience with twice-daily IV administration is limited to 4 days [Australian].

### Missed Dose and Discontinuation
- **Missed Dose**: Take a single dose as soon as remembered.
- **Discontinuation**: Should be withdrawn gradually to minimise the risk of rebound seizures.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity**: Known hypersensitivity to **brivaracetam**, other pyrrolidone derivatives, or any of the inactive excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Suicidal Behaviour and Ideation**: Antiepileptic drugs (AEDs) increase the risk of suicidal thoughts or behaviour. Patients should be monitored for the emergence or worsening of depression or unusual changes in mood. This risk has been observed as early as one week after starting treatment [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions**: Bronchospasm and angioedema have been reported. If these occur, the drug must be discontinued immediately.
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs)**: Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN) have been reported. Treatment should be withdrawn immediately upon the first appearance of signs suggestive of these reactions [Australian].
- **Neurological Effects**: Treatment is associated with somnolence, fatigue, and dizziness, which may impair the ability to drive or operate machinery.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally mild to moderate in intensity. Somnolence and fatigue show a dose-related increase in frequency.

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Somnolence (14.3%), Dizziness (11.0%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Fatigue, irritability, nausea, vomiting, constipation, nasopharyngitis, insomnia, anxiety, depression, headache, and decreased appetite |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Neutropenia, aggression, agitation, psychotic disorder, and suicidal ideation (0.3% in adults) |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Not specified in available sources |

- **IV Specific Effects**: Infusion site pain was reported in 2.8% of patients [Australian].
- **Paediatric Considerations**: Behavioural adverse reactions and psychomotor hyperactivity (4.7%) are more common in children than in adults. Suicidal ideation was reported in 4.7% of paediatric patients [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Rifampicin**: Co-administration with this strong enzyme inducer reduces **brivaracetam** plasma concentrations by approximately 45%. A dose increase of **brivaracetam** may be required when starting rifampicin [Australian].
- **Carbamazepine**: **Brivaracetam** is a moderate reversible inhibitor of epoxide hydrolase, which can increase levels of carbamazepine-epoxide (an active metabolite). Dose reduction of carbamazepine should be considered if tolerability issues arise.
- **Phenytoin**: High doses of **brivaracetam** (400 mg/day) may increase phenytoin plasma levels by up to 20% [Australian].
- **Oral Contraceptives**: No significant interaction at 100 mg/day; however, at 400 mg/day, estrogen and progestin AUCs may be reduced by 27% and 23%, respectively.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Alcohol**: **Brivaracetam** may increase the effects of alcohol on psychomotor function and attention.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **Australian Category B3**: Animal studies show some embryo-fetal toxicity (increased post-implantation loss) at maternotoxic doses. There are no adequate data in pregnant women. Use is not recommended unless clinically necessary (benefit to mother outweighs risk to fetus).
- **Breastfeeding**: **Brivaracetam** is excreted in human breast milk. A decision must be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the importance of the medication to the mother.
- **Fertility**: No human data available. No adverse effects on fertility were seen in animal (rat) studies.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**:
    - **$\ge$ 50 kg**: Adult dosing applies.
    - **4 years to < 50 kg**: Initial dose 1 mg/kg/day (may start at 2 mg/kg/day). Maintenance 2 mg/kg/day. Effective range 1–4 mg/kg/day.
    - **< 4 years**: Safety and efficacy not established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use**: No dose adjustment is generally necessary for patients $\ge$ 65 years.
- **Renal Impairment**: No dose adjustment required. However, it is not recommended for patients with end-stage renal disease (ESRD) undergoing dialysis due to lack of data [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: 
    - **Adults**: Consider a starting dose of 50 mg/day. Maximum daily dose should not exceed 150 mg (divided BID) for all stages of impairment (Child-Pugh A, B, and C).
    - **Paediatrics (< 50 kg)**: Starting dose 1 mg/kg/day; maximum dose 3 mg/kg/day [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: **Brivaracetam** displays a high and selective affinity for synaptic vesicle protein 2A (SV2A) in the brain. This binding is the primary mechanism for its anticonvulsant activity.
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Rapid and complete after oral intake; dose-proportional (10–600 mg).
    - **Distribution**: Weakly plasma protein bound ($\le$ 20%). Volume of distribution is 0.5 L/kg.
    - **Metabolism**: Primarily via hydrolysis of the amide moiety (by amidase) and secondarily via hydroxylation (CYP2C19). Metabolites are not pharmacologically active.
    - **Elimination**: >95% excreted in urine (less than 10% as unchanged drug). Terminal half-life is approximately 9 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy**: Established in three fixed-dose, randomised, placebo-controlled multicenter studies (N=1558). 
- **Design**: 8-week baseline followed by a 12-week treatment period. 
- **Outcomes**: Statistically significant reductions in partial-onset seizure frequency (per 7 or 28 days) were observed at doses of 100 mg/day and 200 mg/day compared to placebo. 50% responder rates for 100 mg/day ranged from 36.0% to 38.9% across studies [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials**: Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life**: 4 years.
- **Diluted Solution**: Chemically stable for 24 hours at 25°C in PVC/polyolefin bags. However, for microbiological reasons, use immediately or store at room temperature for no more than 4 hours after dilution.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australian Classification**: Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4).
- **Other Jurisdictions**: Not specified in available sources.
