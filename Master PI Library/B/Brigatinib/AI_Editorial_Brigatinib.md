# Editorial Product Information: Brigatinib

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a structured reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or prescribing information required for practice within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Brigatinib (tyrosine kinase inhibitor).
- **Brand Name:** **ALUNBRIG** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01ED04.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Film-coated tablets:** 30 mg, 90 mg, and 180 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Takeda Pharmaceuticals [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **ALK-positive Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC):** Treatment of adult patients with anaplastic lymphoma kinase (ALK)-positive locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC [Australian]. 
- *Note:* Validated ALK assay testing is required to confirm ALK-positive status prior to treatment initiation.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Starting Dose:** 90 mg once daily for the first 7 days (lead-in period).
- **Maintenance Dose:** 180 mg once daily, provided the starting dose is tolerated.
- **Duration:** Continue treatment as long as clinical benefit is observed.

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets should be swallowed whole with water. Do not crush or chew.
- May be administered with or without food [Australian].

### Missed Doses and Vomiting
- If a dose is missed or if vomiting occurs after administration, an additional dose should **not** be taken. The next dose should be taken at the regularly scheduled time.

### Dose Titration and Modifications
If treatment is interrupted for 14 days or longer for reasons other than adverse reactions, therapy must be re-initiated at 90 mg once daily for 7 days before escalating to the previously tolerated dose [Australian].

**Standard Dose Reduction Levels:**
- **Initial 180 mg/day dose:** Reduce to 120 mg -> 90 mg -> 60 mg -> Permanently discontinue if 60 mg is not tolerated.
- **Initial 90 mg/day dose (lead-in):** Reduce to 60 mg -> Permanently discontinue if 60 mg is not tolerated.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to brigatinib or any of the tablet excipients (including lactose monohydrate) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Pulmonary Adverse Reactions:** Severe, life-threatening, or fatal Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD)/pneumonitis can occur, typically within the first 7 days of treatment. Risk factors include increased age and a short interval (<7 days) between crizotinib discontinuation and brigatinib initiation. Monitor for new or worsening respiratory symptoms (dyspnoea, cough).
- **Hypertension:** Blood pressure should be monitored regularly. Treatment should be withheld for Grade 3 hypertension and permanently discontinued for Grade 4 or recurrent Grade 3 reactions.
- **Bradycardia:** Heart rate and blood pressure monitoring is required. Use caution when co-administering with other bradycardic agents.
- **Visual Disturbance:** Patients should report any new visual symptoms. Severe cases may require ophthalmic evaluation and dose reduction.
- **Creatine Phosphokinase (CPK) Elevation:** Patients should report unexplained muscle pain or weakness. Monitor CPK levels regularly.
- **Pancreatic Enzyme Elevation:** Regular monitoring of lipase and amylase is required.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Monitor hepatic enzymes (ALT, AST) and bilirubin prior to initiation, every 2 weeks for the first 3 months, and periodically thereafter.
- **Hyperglycaemia:** Assess fasting serum glucose before initiation and monitor periodically. Anti-hyperglycaemic therapy should be initiated or optimised as needed.
- **Photosensitivity:** Patients should avoid prolonged sun exposure and use SPF ≥30 protection for at least 5 days after treatment discontinuation.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Summary (180 mg Regimen)
| Frequency | Adverse Reactions |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Increased AST (68%), increased CPK (64%), hyperglycaemia (61%), increased lipase (54%), hyperinsulinaemia (53%), diarrhoea (49%), increased ALT (49%), increased amylase (47%), anaemia (47%), nausea (40%), fatigue (40%), hypophosphataemia (39%), decreased lymphocyte count (39%), cough (38%), rash (37%), headache (33%), hypertension (30%), vomiting (26%). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Pneumonia, insomnia, memory impairment, dysgeusia, palpitations, dry mouth, photosensitivity, musculoskeletal chest pain, weight decreased. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Neutrophil count decreased, dizziness, stomatitis. |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Pneumonitis:** Reported in 5.5% of patients [Australian].
- **Pneumonia:** Reported in 6.9% of patients [Australian].
- **Grade 3-4 Reactions:** Most common include increased CPK (18%), increased lipase (15%), and hypertension (11%).

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP3A Inhibitors:** Strong CYP3A inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, ritonavir) increase brigatinib concentrations. Avoid co-administration; if unavoidable, reduce brigatinib dose by approximately 50%.
- **CYP3A Inducers:** Strong CYP3A inducers (e.g., rifampicin, St. John’s Wort) significantly decrease brigatinib concentrations and should be avoided. Moderate inducers may decrease exposure by 50%; if unavoidable, the brigatinib dose may be increased in 30 mg increments after 7 days, up to double the original dose.
- **Transporter Substrates:** Brigatinib may increase concentrations of substrates for P-gp (e.g., digoxin, dabigatran), BCRP (e.g., methotrexate), OCT1, MATE1, and MATE2K.
- **CYP3A Substrates:** Brigatinib induces CYP3A4 and may reduce the plasma concentrations of sensitive CYP3A substrates (e.g., hormonal contraceptives, midazolam).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Brigatinib may cause fetal harm based on animal data (skeletal and visceral malformations). It should not be used unless clinical benefit to the mother outweighs the risk to the fetus.
- **Contraception:** Females of childbearing potential must use effective non-hormonal contraception during treatment and for **4 months** after the final dose. Males with female partners of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during treatment and for **3 months** after the final dose [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Avoid breastfeeding during treatment. It is unknown if brigatinib is excreted in human milk.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies indicate potential for reduced male fertility; effects in monkeys were reversible, but effects in rats (testicular tubular degeneration) were not [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients <18 years have not been established.
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required for patients ≥65 years. Limited data available for patients >85 years.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - Mild/Moderate (eGFR ≥30 mL/min/1.73 m²): No adjustment.
    - **Severe (eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m²):** Reduce dose by approximately 50% (e.g., 180 mg to 90 mg) [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - Mild/Moderate (Child-Pugh A or B): No adjustment.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Reduce dose by approximately 40% (e.g., 180 mg to 120 mg) [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Tyrosine kinase inhibitor targeting ALK, ROS1, and insulin-like growth factor 1 receptor (IGF-1R). It inhibits ALK autophosphorylation and ALK-mediated signaling (STAT3). It is active against multiple mutant forms of EML4-ALK associated with resistance to other inhibitors (e.g., G1202R).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Tmax 1–4 hours. Steady-state reached within 1.9–2.4 ratio accumulation.
    - **Distribution:** 91% plasma protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by CYP2C8 and CYP3A4.
    - **Elimination:** Mean plasma half-life of 25 hours. 65% excreted in faeces; 25% in urine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ALTA 1L (Study 301):** Phase 3 trial in ALK-inhibitor-naive patients. Brigatinib (180 mg regimen) showed superior Progression-Free Survival (PFS) compared to crizotinib (Median PFS 24 months vs 11 months; HR 0.49). Intracranial Objective Response Rate (ORR) was 78% for brigatinib vs 26% for crizotinib in patients with measurable brain metastases [Australian].
- **ALTA (Study 201):** Phase 2 trial in patients who progressed on crizotinib. The 180 mg regimen (with 7-day lead-in) showed an ORR of 55.5% and a median PFS of 15.6 months [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C.
- **Packaging:** Keep in original container (blister pack or bottle).

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4). Subject to additional monitoring (Black Triangle Scheme).
