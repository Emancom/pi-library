# Editorial Product Information: Bromhexine Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australia (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of bromhexine hydrochloride for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing or dispensing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for the most current legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Bromhexine hydrochloride (a synthetic derivative of the herbal active ingredient vasicine).
- **Chemical Class:** Mucolytic agent.
- **Brand Names:** **Bisolvon Chesty** (Oral Liquid, Kids Oral Liquid, Forte Oral Liquid, Forte Tablets) [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** R05CB02.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths [Australian]:**
    - **Oral Liquid:** 4 mg/5 mL.
    - **Kids Oral Liquid:** 4 mg/5 mL.
    - **Forte Oral Liquid:** 8 mg/5 mL.
    - **Forte Tablets:** 8 mg.
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sanofi Consumer Healthcare [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Mucolytic Therapy:** Indicated to facilitate the breakdown of mucus and assist in clearing the chest in conditions characterized by excessive mucus secretions. These include the common cold, influenza, and various respiratory tract infections [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Children ≥12 years)
- **Standard Dose:** 8 mg (10 mL of 4 mg/5 mL liquid, 5 mL of 8 mg/5 mL liquid, or one 8 mg tablet) three times daily.
- **Initial Intensive Dose:** May be increased to 16 mg three times daily for the first seven days of treatment [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing (Children 6–11 years)
- **Standard Dose:** 8 mg (10 mL of 4 mg/5 mL liquid, 5 mL of 8 mg/5 mL liquid, or one 8 mg tablet) three times daily [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Method:** For oral use.
- **Clinical Advice:** Patients should be advised to expect an increase in mucus flow. If symptoms do not improve rapidly, medical advice should be sought. In the presence of infection, bromhexine should be used in conjunction with appropriate antibiotic therapy [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to bromhexine hydrochloride or any excipients in the specific formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Severe Skin Reactions:** Very rare reports of Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS) and Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN) have been associated with bromhexine. If new skin or mucosal lesions appear, treatment must be discontinued immediately as a precaution [Australian].
- **Gastric Ulceration:** Use with caution in patients with a history of or active gastric ulceration [Australian].
- **Hepatic and Renal Impairment:** Caution is advised in patients with severe hepatic disease or severe renal failure due to potential accumulation of the drug or its metabolites [Australian].
- **Excipient Considerations [Australian]:**
    - **Maltitol/Sorbitol:** Liquid formulations contain maltitol and sorbitol (up to 2.4 g sorbitol per max daily dose). May have a laxative effect and are contraindicated in patients with hereditary fructose intolerance.
    - **Lactose:** Tablets contain lactose (444 mg per max daily dose); avoid in patients with hereditary galactose intolerance (e.g., galactosaemia).

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by system organ class. Frequencies are not explicitly quantified in the source but are generally described as mild or rare.

- **Immune System:** Anaphylactic reactions (including shock), angioedema, and other hypersensitivity reactions.
- **Skin and Subcutaneous Tissue:** Rash, urticaria, pruritus.
- **Respiratory:** Bronchospasm.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, and upper abdominal pain.
- **Nervous System:** Headache, dizziness, sweating.
- **Hepatic:** Transient elevations in serum aminotransferase levels [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Antibiotics:** Bromhexine administration increases the concentrations of **amoxicillin**, **erythromycin**, and **oxytetracycline** in sputum and bronchopulmonary secretions [Australian].
- **Anticoagulants/Digoxin:** No specific interaction studies have been performed; however, long-term marketing data suggest no substantial interaction potential [Australian].
- **General:** No clinically relevant unfavourable interactions have been reported with other medicinal products [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy [Australian Category A]:** Bromhexine has been taken by a large number of pregnant women without observed increases in malformations or harmful effects on the foetus. It crosses the placental barrier. While animal studies show no ill effects, use is generally not recommended during the first trimester.
- **Breastfeeding:** Bromhexine is excreted in human milk. Although adverse effects on infants are not expected, use in breastfeeding mothers is not recommended [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Data not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Contraindicated in children under 6 years of age. Use in children 6–11 years should only occur under the advice of a healthcare professional [Australian].
- **Elderly:** No specific pharmacokinetic data available; however, extensive clinical experience has not identified unique safety concerns in this population [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution in severe renal failure. Accumulation of metabolites may occur [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution in severe hepatic disease due to reduced clearance of the parent compound [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bromhexine is a mucolytic that reduces mucus viscosity by activating ciliated epithelium and enhancing mucociliary clearance [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Preclinical data indicate an increase in the proportion of thin, watery bronchial secretions. It exhibits secretolytic and secretomotor effects, facilitating expectoration [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics [Australian]:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; linear kinetics (8–32 mg). Bioavailability is 22–27% due to extensive first-pass metabolism. Food increases plasma concentrations.
    - **Distribution:** Widely distributed (Vss up to 1209 L). 95% protein bound. Crosses the blood-brain barrier; small amounts cross the placenta.
    - **Metabolism:** Almost completely metabolised to various hydroxylated metabolites, including **ambroxol** (an active metabolite).
    - **Elimination:** High extraction ratio (843–1073 mL/min). Primarily excreted in urine (97.4%). Terminal half-life ranges from 6.6 to 31.4 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Summary:** Preclinical and clinical studies have demonstrated that bromhexine increases the volume of bronchial secretions and facilitates expectoration. It has also been shown to enhance the penetration of certain antibiotics into lung tissue [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Container [Australian]:** Oral liquids are supplied in bottles; tablets are supplied in Al/PVC/PVDC blisters.
- **Disposal:** Unused medicine should be disposed of via local pharmacy waste programs [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Schedule 2 (Pharmacy Medicine) [Australian].
