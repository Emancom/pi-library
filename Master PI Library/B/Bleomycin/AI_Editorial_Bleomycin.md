## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Bleomycin (as sulfate). It is an antineoplastic antibiotic consisting of a mixture of glycopeptides isolated from *Streptomyces verticillus*, primarily bleomycin A2 and B2.
- **Brand Names:** Blenamax [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01DC01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Lyophilised powder for injection, 15,000 IU per vial [Australian]. 
- **Route of Administration:** Intramuscular (IM), Intravenous (IV), Subcutaneous (SC), Intra-arterial, and Intra-pleural.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aspen Pharma Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Bleomycin is indicated for palliative and/or adjuvant treatment (to surgery or radiation) for the following neoplasms [Australian]:
- **Squamous Cell Carcinoma (SCC):** Skin, head and neck, oesophagus, penis, larynx, and uterine cervix.
- **Lymphomas:** Hodgkin’s disease, Non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma, and mycosis fungoides.
- **Germ Cell Tumours:** Choriocarcinoma and embryonal cell carcinoma of the testis.
- **Malignant Pleural Effusion:** Used as a sclerosing agent for the treatment of malignant pleural effusion.

*Note: SCC of the bronchus typically shows infrequent response [Australian].*

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Adult Dosing
- **Standard Regimen:** 10,000 to 20,000 IU/m² (10–20 units USP/m²) administered weekly or twice weekly.
- **Alternative Regimen:** 15,000 IU daily for 7 days, followed by three weeks off-treatment; repeat for three cycles to reach a total dose of ~300,000 IU.
- **Cumulative Dose Limit:** Total doses exceeding 300,000 IU (300 units USP) must be administered with extreme caution due to the risk of pulmonary toxicity.

### Indication-Specific Instructions
- **Lymphoma:** Due to the risk of anaphylactoid reactions, a **test dose** of 1,000 to 5,000 IU (1–5 units USP) is required for the first two treatments. If no reaction occurs within 4–6 hours, the remainder of the dose may be given.
- **Malignant Pleural Effusion:** A single dose of 60,000 IU (60 units USP) administered via bolus intrapleural injection.
- **Intra-arterial Administration:** 30,000 to 60,000 IU once or twice weekly until a total dose of 300,000 IU is reached.

### Administration & Reconstitution
- **IM/SC:** Dissolve vial in 1–5 mL sterile water or 0.9% saline.
- **IV/Intra-arterial:** Dissolve in 5–10 mL diluent and administer slowly over 10 minutes.
- **Intra-pleural:** Dissolve 60,000 IU in 50–100 mL 0.9% saline; administer through thoracostomy tube after drainage.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to bleomycin sulfate [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Pulmonary Toxicity:** The most serious adverse effect, manifesting as subacute or chronic pneumonitis that may progress to fatal interstitial fibrosis. Risk increases with age (>70 years), cumulative doses (>400,000 IU), and prior/concurrent radiotherapy or oxygen therapy.
- **Anaesthesia Risk:** Patients treated with bleomycin are at risk for post-operative adult respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) 6–12 months after therapy. Recommendations include maintaining low inspiratory oxygen concentrations (below 0.25) and careful fluid management during surgery.
- **Idiosyncratic Reactions:** Approximately 1% of patients (up to 5% in lymphoma patients) may experience an anaphylactoid reaction (hypotension, confusion, fever, wheezing). This usually occurs after the first or second dose.
- **Monitoring Requirements:**
    - Chest X-rays every 1–2 weeks during and up to 4 weeks after treatment.
    - Monthly monitoring of Pulmonary Diffusion Capacity for Carbon Monoxide (DLCO); discontinue if DLCO falls below 30–35% of pre-treatment value.
    - Frequent observation for fine rales (earliest sign of toxicity).

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** 
    - Mucocutaneous reactions (approx. 50%), including hyperpigmentation, hyperkeratosis, alopecia, and stomatitis.
    - Fever, chills, and nausea.
- **Common (1–10%):**
    - Pulmonary toxicity (approx. 10%).
    - Idiosyncratic/anaphylactoid reactions (approx. 1%).
- **Serious Adverse Effects:**
    - **Pulmonary:** Fatal interstitial fibrosis.
    - **Vascular:** Raynaud’s phenomenon, myocardial infarction, cerebrovascular accident, and thrombotic microangiopathy.
    - **Other:** Hepatic and renal toxicity, CNS toxicity (disorientation, aggression).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Digoxin:** Bleomycin may reduce serum levels and efficacy of digoxin due to intestinal mucosa alterations affecting absorption [Australian].
- **Phenytoin:** Serum concentrations of phenytoin may be decreased due to reduced absorption or increased metabolism [Australian].
- **Incompatibilities:** Bleomycin is physically incompatible with amino acids, aminophylline, ascorbic acid, dexamethasone, frusemide, hyoscine butylbromide, and riboflavine [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category D. Bleomycin is teratogenic in animals and is not recommended for use in pregnant women unless the benefit outweighs the potential risk to the foetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if bleomycin is excreted in breast milk. Due to potential harm to the newborn, breastfeeding is not recommended.
- **Fertility:** The effects of bleomycin on human fertility have not been established; however, it is mutagenic in test systems [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No information available in the provided sources.
- **Elderly Use:** Patients over 70 years require close observation due to a heightened risk of pulmonary toxicity.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dose reductions of 40% to 75% are recommended when creatinine clearance (CrCl) is $\le$ 40 mL/minute.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution; deterioration in liver function tests has been reported infrequently.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** An antineoplastic antibiotic that produces single and double-strand breaks in DNA, inhibiting DNA synthesis and cell division. It is most effective in the G2 and M phases of the cell cycle.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** ~45% systemic absorption after intra-pleural administration. Peak serum levels occur 30 minutes after IM injection.
    - **Distribution:** High concentrations found in skin, lungs, kidneys, and lymphatic system; very low levels in brain/CSF.
    - **Metabolism:** Inactivated by bleomycin-inactivating enzyme, found primarily in the liver but absent in skin and lungs.
    - **Excretion:** 60–70% excreted in urine as active drug. Terminal half-life is ~115 minutes (in normal renal function).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vial Storage:** Store at 2–8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze). Protect from light.
- **Shelf Life:** 3 years [Australian].
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Stable for 24 hours at 25°C, but recommended for immediate use to avoid microbiological contamination. If necessary, store at 2–8°C for no more than 24 hours.
