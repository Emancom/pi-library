# Editorial Product Information: Bupivacaine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of bupivacaine for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, dosing recommendations, and safety profiles.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing within a particular region. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Bupivacaine hydrochloride monohydrate.
- **Chemical Class:** Amide-type local anaesthetic.
- **ATC Code:** N01BB01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Australian:** 0.25% (50 mg/20 mL) and 0.5% (50 mg/10 mL or 100 mg/20 mL) solutions for injection. Available in glass vials, **Steriamp** plastic ampoules, and **Steriluer** plastic ampoules.
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Local infiltration, peripheral nerve block, and epidural/caudal block. **Note:** Not for intravenous administration under any circumstances.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Bupivacaine is indicated for the production of local or regional anaesthesia and analgesia in the following settings:
- **Surgical Anaesthesia:**
    - Epidural block for abdominal, pelvic, and lower limb surgery (including Caesarean section).
    - Field block, including minor and major nerve blocks and infiltration.
- **Analgesia:**
    - Continuous epidural infusion or intermittent bolus administration for the management of postoperative or labour pain.
    - Field block for minor nerve blocks and infiltration.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Principles
Dosage must be individualised based on the area to be anaesthetised, tissue vascularity, the number of neuronal segments involved, depth of anaesthesia required, and the patient's physical condition. The lowest effective dose should always be employed.

### Adult Dosing Guidelines (70 kg Patient)
- **Lumbar Epidural (Surgery):** 75–150 mg (15–30 mL of 0.5% solution).
- **Thoracic Epidural (Surgery):** 12.5–50 mg (5–15 mL of 0.25% or 5–10 mL of 0.5% solution).
- **Caudal Epidural (Surgery):** 37.5–125 mg.
- **Local Infiltration:** Up to 150 mg.
- **Brachial Plexus Block:** 100–150 mg (20–30 mL of 0.5% solution).
- **Analgesia (Labour/Postoperative):** 
    - Lumbar bolus: 15–60 mg.
    - Continuous infusion (Lumbar): 12.5–18.75 mg/hour.
    - Continuous infusion (Thoracic): 6.25–12.5 mg/hour.

### Maximum Dose
- Experience suggests 400 mg administered over 24 hours is well tolerated in healthy adults. 
- Individual doses should generally not exceed 2 mg/kg [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Test Dose:** For epidural procedures, a 3–5 mL test dose (preferably containing adrenaline) is required to detect inadvertent intravascular or subarachnoid injection.
- **Aspiration:** Frequent aspiration is mandatory before and during the injection of the total dose.
- **Injection Rate:** The main dose should be injected slowly (25–50 mg/min) while maintaining verbal contact with the patient.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to local anaesthetics of the amide type.
- Infection in the region of proposed injection or septicaemia.
- **Obstetrical paracervical block.**
- **Intravenous regional anaesthesia (Bier’s block).**
- Epidural or spinal anaesthesia in patients with **uncorrected hypotension**.
- General contraindications related to epidural anaesthesia must be observed regardless of the agent used.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Emergency Preparedness:** Use is mandatory only where oxygen, resuscitative equipment, and emergency drugs are immediately available.
- **Cardiovascular Risk:** Major blocks can cause hypotension and bradycardia. Cardiac arrest and death have been reported; resuscitation can be difficult.
- **CNS Toxicity:** Clinicians must monitor for early signs of toxicity, including restlessness, tinnitus, dizziness, blurred vision, or tremors.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Prolonged use or repeated injections have been associated with drug-induced liver injury (DILI). Monitor liver function; discontinue if dysfunction occurs [Australian].
- **Anticoagulation:** Patients on low molecular weight heparins or heparinoids are at increased risk of spinal/epidural haematoma and subsequent permanent paralysis.
- **Chondrolysis:** Intra-articular continuous infusion is an unapproved use and has been linked to post-marketing reports of chondrolysis. Bupivacaine should not be used for this purpose.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are rare in the absence of overdose or accidental intravascular injection.
- **CNS Effects:** Excitatory (nervousness, euphoria, twitching, convulsions) or depressant (drowsiness, respiratory arrest).
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** Bradycardia, hypotension, ventricular arrhythmias, and cardiovascular collapse.
- **Neurologic:** Persistent motor or sensory deficits, urinary retention, and rarely, paraplegia or arachnoiditis.
- **Allergic:** Rare cutaneous lesions, urticaria, or anaphylactoid reactions.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Sudden cardiovascular collapse, cardiac arrest, and total spinal block.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Anti-arrhythmic Drugs:** Caution is advised when used with Class III agents (e.g., amiodarone) or other amide-type local anaesthetics (e.g., lidocaine, mexiletine), as cardiac effects may be additive.
- **Anticoagulants/NSAIDs:** Increased risk of spinal haematoma during neuraxial blockade.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category A]. Bupivacaine crosses the placenta; however, it has been used effectively for obstetrical analgesia without evidence of ill effects at recommended doses. Use cautiously in pregnancy for non-labour indications as well-controlled studies are lacking.
- **Labour/Delivery:** Fetal bradycardia/tachycardia may occur, particularly with paracervical block.
- **Breastfeeding:** Bupivacaine enters breast milk in amounts unlikely to cause significant accumulation in the infant. It is generally considered compatible with breastfeeding.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatrics:** Limited experience in children under 12. Dosing should be weight-based (up to 2 mg/kg).
- **Elderly:** Reduced doses are required to commensurate with physical condition and age.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Bupivacaine is metabolised in the liver. Use with caution in patients with severe hepatic disease due to the risk of accumulation.
- **Renal Impairment:** Caution is required in severe renal dysfunction as acidosis and reduced plasma protein may increase the risk of systemic toxicity.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Reversibly blocks impulse propagation along nerve fibres by preventing the inward movement of sodium ions through sodium channels in the nerve membrane.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Provides a long duration of action (2–5 hours for epidural; up to 12 hours for peripheral blocks).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rate depends on dose, route, and vascularity. Peak plasma levels are typically reached within 30–45 minutes.
    - **Distribution:** 96% protein-bound, primarily to $\alpha_1$-acid glycoprotein.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 6% excreted unchanged in urine; terminal half-life is 2.7 hours in adults.

- Solutions showing discolouration should not be used.
