# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **US (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a coherent reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing in any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) sterile solution in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion. 
    - Severe metabolic crisis may require a loading dose, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - Subsequent daily maintenance doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - Administration: Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - Dose adjustments (e.g., reduction to 5 mg/kg) should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine concentrations, which should ideally be between 40 µmol/L and 50 µmol/L [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

### Compatibility
- Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Hypersensitivity Reactions
- **Serious Reactions:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occurred within minutes of administration [US].
- **Management:** Discontinue treatment immediately and initiate appropriate medical care. If re-administration is considered, patients must be monitored closely for reoccurrence of symptoms [US].

### Neurologic Precautions
- **Seizures:** Reports of seizures have occurred in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed following oral or intravenous levocarnitine [US].

### General Precautions
- **Oral Formulation Restriction:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. High doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].
- **Monitoring:** For parenteral therapy, it is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target range 35–60 µmol/L) is advised [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels regularly after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category C]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. However, there are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. In dairy cows, milk carnitine concentrations increase following exogenous administration. Weigh the benefits of maternal supplementation against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no evidence of impaired fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders are applicable to paediatric patients.
- **Geriatric Use:** Not specifically addressed in the source document.
- **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD patients on dialysis. Note the caution regarding toxic metabolite accumulation with **oral** formulations in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters that are rapidly excreted [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Bioavailability:** Absolute bioavailability of oral formulations is approximately 15–16% [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Dosing Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (n=12), 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased pre-dialysis trough levels from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses demonstrated dose-proportionate increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks. While carnitine levels increased, effects on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population were not determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing [US].
- **Vial Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard unused portion [US].
