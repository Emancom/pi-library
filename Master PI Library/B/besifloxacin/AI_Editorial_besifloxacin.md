# Editorial Product Information: Besifloxacin

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s current approved PI for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Besifloxacin hydrochloride (an 8-chloro fluoroquinolone anti-infective).
- **Brand Name:** Besivance [US].
- **ATC Code:** S01AE08 (Fluoroquinolones).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile ophthalmic suspension containing 6 mg/mL (0.6%) of besifloxacin [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Topical ophthalmic use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Bausch & Lomb Americas Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Bacterial Conjunctivitis:** Indicated for the treatment of bacterial conjunctivitis caused by susceptible isolates of the following organisms [US]:
    - **Gram-positive bacteria:** *Aerococcus viridans*, CDC coryneform group G, *Corynebacterium pseudodiphtheriticum*, *Corynebacterium striatum*, *Staphylococcus aureus*, *Staphylococcus epidermidis*, *Staphylococcus hominis*, *Staphylococcus lugdunensis*, *Staphylococcus warneri*, *Streptococcus mitis* group, *Streptococcus oralis*, *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Streptococcus salivarius*.
    - **Gram-negative bacteria:** *Haemophilus influenzae*, *Moraxella catarrhalis*, *Moraxella lacunata*, *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*.
    - *Note: Efficacy for several organisms (marked with asterisks in the PI) was established in fewer than 10 infections.*

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Children $\ge$1 year):**
    - Instill one drop into the affected eye(s) three times daily.
    - Doses should be administered 4 to 12 hours apart for a total of 7 days [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - The closed bottle should be inverted and shaken once prior to use to ensure proper suspension of the drug [US].
    - Patients should be advised to avoid contaminating the applicator tip with material from the eye, fingers, or other sources [US].
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- None listed in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Not for Injection:** Besifloxacin is for topical ophthalmic use only and must not be injected subconjunctivally or directly into the anterior chamber of the eye [US].
- **Growth of Resistant Organisms:** Prolonged use of any anti-infective may result in the overgrowth of non-susceptible organisms, including fungi. If a superinfection occurs, treatment should be discontinued and alternative therapy initiated [US].
- **Clinical Monitoring:** When clinically indicated, patients should be examined using magnification (e.g., slit-lamp biomicroscopy) and fluorescein staining [US].
- **Avoidance of Contact Lenses:** Patients are advised not to wear contact lenses if they exhibit signs or symptoms of bacterial conjunctivitis or during the course of therapy with besifloxacin [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following adverse reactions were identified in clinical trials involving approximately 1,000 patients [US]:

- **Common (1–10%):**
    - Conjunctival redness (approximately 2%).
- **Uncommon (1–2%):**
    - Blurred vision.
    - Eye pain.
    - Eye irritation.
    - Eye pruritus.
    - Headache.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Specific drug interaction studies have not been conducted with besifloxacin ophthalmic suspension [US].
- **Clinical Note:** Given the low systemic plasma concentrations observed following topical ocular administration ($< 1.3$ ng/mL), systemic drug-drug interactions are unlikely [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned a lettered category in the provided source; however, the PI notes there are no available human data to inform drug-associated risks. 
    - **Risk Summary:** Systemic exposure following ocular administration is low. Animal studies (oral administration in rats) did not show adverse embryofetal effects at clinically relevant systemic exposures. Maternal toxicity and fetal growth delays were only observed at doses exceeding 11,600 times the human ophthalmic dose [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on the presence of besifloxacin in human milk or its effects on milk production/breastfed infants. Because systemic exposure is low, it is unknown if measurable levels would appear in breast milk. The benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the mother's clinical need [US].
- **Fertility:** Oral doses up to 500 mg/kg/day in rats (26,500 times the human ophthalmic dose) did not impair male or female fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have been established in children aged 1 year and older. Safety and efficacy in infants below 1 year of age have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness have been observed between elderly and younger patients [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources; however, low systemic exposure suggests no dose adjustment is required for topical use.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Besifloxacin is a fluoroquinolone that inhibits bacterial DNA gyrase and Topoisomerase IV. DNA gyrase is essential for replication, transcription, and repair; Topoisomerase IV is essential for partitioning chromosomal DNA during cell division.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The drug is bactericidal, with minimum bactericidal concentrations (MBCs) generally within one dilution of minimum inhibitory concentrations (MICs) [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** In patients with bacterial conjunctivitis dosed TID for 6 days, the maximum plasma concentration ($C_{max}$) was consistently $< 1.3$ ng/mL. The mean $C_{max}$ was approximately 0.43 ng/mL on Day 6.
    - **Elimination:** The average elimination half-life in plasma is estimated to be 7 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Study Summary:** In a randomised, double-masked, vehicle-controlled multicentre trial of patients aged 1–98 years, besifloxacin was superior to its vehicle. 
- **Outcomes:** 
    - **Clinical Resolution:** 45% for besifloxacin vs. 33% for vehicle.
    - **Microbiological Eradication:** 91% for besifloxacin vs. 60% for vehicle [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 15°C to 25°C (59°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from light [US].
- **Packaging:** Supplied in a 5 mL white low-density polyethylene (LDPE) bottle with a controlled dropper tip and a beige polypropylene cap [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** Not specified in available sources for other jurisdictions.
