# Editorial Product Information: Baloxavir Marboxil

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidance required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Baloxavir marboxil.
- **Chemical Class:** Cap-dependent endonuclease (CEN) inhibitor; polymerase acidic (PA) subunit inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** **Xofluza** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 20 mg, 40 mg, and 80 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Roche Products Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Treatment of Influenza:** Indicated for the treatment of acute, uncomplicated influenza in patients aged 12 years and older who have been symptomatic for no more than 48 hours and who are otherwise healthy or at high risk of developing influenza-related complications [Australian].
- **Prophylaxis of Influenza:** Indicated for post-exposure prophylaxis in patients aged 12 years and older following close contact with an individual with confirmed influenza [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Adolescents ≥ 12 years)
Baloxavir is administered as a **single oral dose**. The dose is determined by the patient's body weight:
- **40 kg to < 80 kg:** 40 mg as a single dose.
- **≥ 80 kg:** 80 mg as a single dose.

### Administration Instructions
- May be taken with or without food.
- **Co-administration Caution:** Avoid taking baloxavir with polyvalent cation-containing products (e.g., antacids, laxatives, or oral supplements containing calcium, iron, magnesium, selenium, or zinc).
- Avoid taking with calcium-fortified beverages or dairy products where possible [Australian].

### Timing of Dose
- **Treatment:** Within 48 hours of the onset of influenza symptoms.
- **Prophylaxis:** As soon as possible within 48 hours following exposure.

### Missed Dose
- Not applicable as this is a single-dose regimen.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to baloxavir marboxil or any of the excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Viral Resistance:** Influenza viruses evolve; factor in current drug susceptibility patterns (e.g., from WHO or local health authorities) when deciding on treatment. Resistance (PA/I38T/M/F/N/S mutations) has been observed in clinical trials [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Post-marketing reports include anaphylaxis, angioedema, urticaria, and rash. Appropriate medical treatment must be available if an allergic-type reaction is suspected.
- **Severe Hepatic/Renal Impairment:** Safety and efficacy have not been studied in patients with severe hepatic or renal impairment [Australian].
- **Bacterial Infections:** Baloxavir is not effective against bacterial infections; treat secondary bacterial complications as appropriate.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The overall safety profile is based on data from over 2,400 subjects. In clinical trials, the incidence of adverse events was similar between baloxavir and placebo.

### Clinical Trial Adverse Events (≥ 1% frequency)
| Adverse Event | Baloxavir (N=1440) | Placebo (N=1136) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Diarrhoea | 3% | 4% |
| Bronchitis | 3% | 4% |
| Nausea | 2% | 3% |
| Sinusitis | 2% | 3% |
| Headache | 1% | 1% |

### Post-Marketing Experience (Frequency not established)
- **Immune System:** Anaphylaxis, anaphylactic reactions.
- **Skin:** Angioedema, urticaria, rash.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Vomiting, bloody diarrhoea, melaena, ischemic colitis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Polyvalent Cations:** Baloxavir may chelate with polyvalent cations. Co-administration with antacids, laxatives, or supplements containing calcium, magnesium, iron, or zinc significantly decreases plasma concentrations.
- **P-glycoprotein (P-gp):** Baloxavir marboxil and its metabolite are substrates for P-gp. Inhibitors like itraconazole may increase exposure, though not to a clinically meaningful level.
- **CYP/UGT:** No clinically significant interactions are expected with CYP450 or UGT substrates, inhibitors, or inducers.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Live Attenuated Influenza Vaccine (LAIV):** Antiviral drugs may inhibit the replication of the vaccine virus, potentially reducing LAIV efficacy. Concurrent use has not been evaluated. Interactions with inactivated vaccines have not been studied [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B3.**
    - There are no adequate studies in pregnant women. Animal studies (rats/rabbits) did not show malformations at high doses, but skeletal variations and miscarriages were noted in rabbits at maternally toxic doses. Avoid use unless the potential benefit justifies the risk to the fetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if baloxavir is excreted in human milk. A decision should be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug, weighing the benefit to the mother against the potential risk to the infant [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No effects on fertility were observed in animal models (rats) [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients < 12 years or weighing < 40 kg have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment is required for patients ≥ 65 years [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for patients with creatinine clearance ≥ 50 mL/min. Not studied in severe impairment or ESRD [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for mild (Child-Pugh A) to moderate (Child-Pugh B) impairment. Not studied in severe impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Baloxavir marboxil is a prodrug converted to baloxavir. It inhibits the cap-dependent endonuclease (CEN) within the PA subunit of the viral RNA polymerase, blocking the "cap-snatching" process and preventing viral mRNA transcription and replication.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Tmax is ~4 hours (fasted). Food reduces Cmax by 48% and AUC by 37%, though this was not considered clinically relevant in trials.
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (~93-94%). Volume of distribution is large (~1180 L in Caucasians).
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily converted to baloxavir by arylacetamide deacetylase. Further metabolism via UGT1A3 (primary) and CYP3A4 (minor).
    - **Elimination:** Primarily faecal (80.1%). Terminal half-life is approximately 79.1 hours (Caucasians) to 93.9 hours (Japanese).
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Baloxavir does not prolong the QTc interval at twice the expected exposure.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **CAPSTONE-1 (Healthy Patients):** Single-dose baloxavir significantly reduced the time to alleviation of symptoms compared to placebo (53.7 hours vs 80.2 hours; p < 0.0001). Efficacy was similar to oseltamivir.
- **CAPSTONE-2 (High-Risk Patients):** In patients at high risk of complications (e.g., elderly, chronic lung/heart disease), baloxavir significantly reduced symptom improvement time compared to placebo (73.2 hours vs 102.3 hours; p < 0.0001).
- **BLOCKSTONE (Prophylaxis):** In household contacts of index patients, the proportion of subjects developing laboratory-confirmed clinical influenza was significantly lower with baloxavir (1.9%) compared to placebo (13.6%).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Handling:** Keep in the original carton to protect from light and moisture.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date (EXP) on the packaging.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 – Prescription Only Medicine [Australian].
