# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not intended to replace the official PI used for prescribing in specific jurisdictions. Clinicians should consult local regulatory guidance and the manufacturer’s current official PI for the relevant region before making treatment decisions.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP), a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport. 
- **Chemical Name:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection containing 200 mg/mL (1 g per 5 mL vial) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** Indicated for both acute and chronic management of patients diagnosed with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) Carnitine Deficiency:** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing hemodialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Administer exclusively via the intravenous route. 
- Parenteral products must be inspected for discoloration or particulate matter prior to administration.

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (over 2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Acute Management:** In cases of severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose may be given, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours. Doses should not be spaced further than every 6 hours.
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though titration may be necessary based on clinical response. 
- **Maximum Reported Dose:** 300 mg/kg.
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended.

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Initial Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
- **Administration Timing:** Administer as a slow 2-to-3-minute bolus into the venous return line immediately following each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels below 40–50 µmol/L. Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be performed as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known:** There are currently no listed contraindications for levocarnitine injection in the provided source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Hypersensitivity Reactions
- **Serious Reactions:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, and laryngeal edema have been reported, primarily in dialysis patients.
- **Onset:** Reactions may occur within minutes of intravenous administration.
- **Management:** If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate medical intervention. Re-administration should only be considered after a risk-benefit analysis and under close monitoring.

### Seizure Activity
- Levocarnitine may increase the frequency or severity of seizures in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a prior history.

### General Precautions
- **Oral vs. IV in Renal Impairment:** The safety of **oral** levocarnitine in patients with renal impairment or ESRD has not been established. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

- **Postmarketing Reports:** Seizures and serious hypersensitivity reactions (anaphylaxis) have been identified during post-approval use. 
- **Other Effects:** Transient nausea, vomiting, and "fishy" body odor have been reported.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate that levocarnitine may increase the International Normalised Ratio (INR) in patients stabilized on warfarin. 
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving concomitant anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Not formally categorized by letter in the source, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits using doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of fetal harm or impaired fertility. Use during pregnancy only if clearly required [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies on levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers are unavailable. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations following exogenous administration. The benefit to the mother must be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for paediatric patients is included under general metabolic disorder guidelines (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific dose adjustments for the elderly are not detailed in the available source [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically in ESRD patients on dialysis (see Section 3). The oral formulation is cautioned against in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Administration to ESRD patients on hemodialysis results in an immediate increase in plasma carnitine concentrations.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg thrice weekly after dialysis), mean trough levocarnitine levels increased from 21.3 µmol/L at baseline to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. 
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks.
- **Note:** While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations is proven, the direct effect on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, cardiomyopathy) in the dialysis population has not been definitively determined in the provided source.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL).

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources for other jurisdictions.
