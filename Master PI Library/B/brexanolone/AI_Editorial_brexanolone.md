# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical and regulatory information into a single reference.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved labeling.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
*   **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/amino acid derivative.
*   **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
*   **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Clear, colorless sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
*   **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion. 
    *   *Loading Dose:* In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. 
    *   *Frequency:* Should be administered every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    *   *Titration:* Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been used [US].
*   **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    *   *Administration:* Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    *   *Titration:* Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
*   Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration before use.
*   **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   **None Known:** There are no listed contraindications for levocarnitine injection in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
*   None specified in available sources.

### General Warnings and Precautions
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in dialysis patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued and appropriate medical treatment initiated [US].
*   **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation Caveat):** The safety of *oral* levocarnitine has not been established in patients with renal impairment. High oral doses in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in urine [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
*   **Plasma Levels:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations before starting therapy.
*   **Periodic Monitoring:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is advised [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting are common. Gastritis and body odor occur less frequently.
*   **ESRD Specific (Frequency ≥5%):** In clinical trials of dialysis patients, reported events (regardless of causality) included:
    *   **General:** Injection site reaction (33–59%), pain (30–49%), flu syndrome (25–40%), headache (3–37%), chest pain (6–15%), fever (5–12%), and asthenia (8–12%).
    *   **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (14–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%).
    *   **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), abdominal pain (5–21%).
    *   **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–33%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–19%).
    *   **Neurological:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%) [US].

### Serious Adverse Effects
*   **Neurologic:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis and laryngeal edema [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
*   **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
*   **Jurisdictional Category:** Not explicitly categorised by letter [US].
*   **Clinical Data:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. There are no adequate/controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].

### Breastfeeding
*   **Safety Information:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations of levocarnitine following administration.
*   **Recommendation:** Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of maternal supplementation. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].

### Fertility
*   No impairment of fertility was observed in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is weight-based (see Section 3) [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Renal Impairment:** Used therapeutically in ESRD for carnitine deficiency. Note the accumulation of metabolites with oral dosing in this population (see Section 5) [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance essential for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production in tissues (excluding the brain) [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours.
*   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
*   **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly after dialysis increased mean plasma concentrations from 20 µmol/L (pre-dialysis) to steady-state trough levels of 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
*   **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses in dialysis patients showed dose-proportional increases in trough levocarnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks of therapy [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C.
*   **Conditions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing.
*   **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of a vial as the formulation is preservative-free [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only (Rx-only) [US].
*   **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
