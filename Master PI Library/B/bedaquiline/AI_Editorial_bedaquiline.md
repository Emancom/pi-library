# Editorial Product Information: Bedaquiline

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) provided by the **United States (FDA)**.
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview intended for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing in any particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Bedaquiline fumarate.
*   **Chemical Class:** Diarylquinoline antimycobacterial.
*   **Brand Name(s):** **SIRTURO** [US].
*   **ATC Code:** J04AK05.
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    *   20 mg functionally scored tablets (uncoated, white/off-white, oblong, debossed with "2" and "0") [US].
    *   100 mg tablets (uncoated, white/off-white, round, biconvex, debossed with "T" over "207" on one side and "100" on the other) [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Oral.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Janssen Products, LP [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Multi-Drug Resistant Tuberculosis (MDR-TB):** Indicated as part of combination therapy in adult and pediatric patients (2 years and older, weighing at least 8 kg) with pulmonary tuberculosis due to *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* resistant to at least rifampin and isoniazid [US-approved].
*   **Limitations of Use:** Not indicated for latent infection, drug-sensitive TB, extra-pulmonary TB, or infections caused by non-tuberculous mycobacteria [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
*   **Directly Observed Therapy (DOT):** Must be administered under DOT to ensure compliance.
*   **Food Requirement:** Always administer with food to enhance bioavailability.
*   **Combination Therapy:** Use only in combination with at least three (if susceptibility is proven) or four (if results are unavailable) other antimycobacterial drugs.

### Adult Dosing (≥18 years)
*   **Weeks 1–2:** 400 mg (4 x 100 mg or 20 x 20 mg tablets) once daily.
*   **Weeks 3–24:** 200 mg (2 x 100 mg or 10 x 20 mg tablets) three times per week, with at least 48 hours between doses.

### Pediatric Dosing (2 to <18 years)
Dosing is weight-based for the 24-week regimen:
*   **8 kg to <10 kg:** 80 mg daily (Weeks 1–2); then 40 mg three times per week (Weeks 3–24).
*   **10 kg to <15 kg:** 120 mg daily (Weeks 1–2); then 60 mg three times per week (Weeks 3–24).
*   **15 kg to <30 kg:** 200 mg daily (Weeks 1–2); then 100 mg three times per week (Weeks 3–24).
*   **≥30 kg:** 400 mg daily (Weeks 1–2); then 200 mg three times per week (Weeks 3–24).

### Administration Instructions
*   **100 mg Tablets:** Swallow whole with water.
*   **20 mg Tablets:** May be swallowed whole, split at the score line, or dispersed in water. For patients with swallowing difficulties, the dispersion may be mixed with soft foods (e.g., yogurt, apple sauce) or administered via a feeding tube (≥8 French).

### Missed Dose Instructions
*   **Weeks 1–2:** Skip the missed dose; do not double the dose.
*   **Week 3 onwards:** Take the missed dose as soon as possible, then resume the 3x/week schedule. Ensure at least 24 hours between intakes and do not exceed the total weekly dose.

## 4. Contraindications
*   No contraindications were specified in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Boxed Warning: QTc Prolongation [US]:** Bedaquiline prolongs the QTc interval. Co-administration with other QTc-prolonging drugs (e.g., clofazimine, fluoroquinolones) may have additive effects. ECG and electrolyte monitoring (potassium, calcium, magnesium) are required at baseline and periodically. Discontinue if significant ventricular arrhythmia or QTc >500 ms (confirmed) occurs.
*   **Mortality Imbalance:** Clinical trials showed an unexplained increased risk of death in the bedaquiline-treated group (11.4%) compared to placebo (2.5%) in Study 1 [US].
*   **Hepatotoxicity:** Liver enzyme elevations and hepatitis may occur. Monitor ALT, AST, alkaline phosphatase, and bilirubin at baseline, monthly, and as needed. Avoid alcohol. Discontinue if transaminase elevations are >8x ULN or >5x ULN persisting beyond two weeks, or if accompanied by jaundice or total bilirubin >2x ULN.
*   **Drug Resistance:** To prevent resistance development, only use in appropriate combination regimens.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Adult Experience
| Adverse Reaction | Frequency (%) in Study 1 (N=79) | Frequency (%) in Study 4 (N=211) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| Nausea | 38% | 54% |
| Arthralgia | 33% | 44% |
| Headache | 28% | 17% |
| QTc Prolongation | Not Specified | 61% |
| Vomiting | Not Specified | 53% |
| Hemoptysis | 18% | <5% |
| Chest Pain | 11% | 16% |

### Pediatric Experience
*   **12 to <18 years:** Very common reactions include arthralgia (40%), nausea (13%), and abdominal pain (13%).
*   **5 to <12 years:** Liver enzyme elevation (33%) is common and may lead to treatment discontinuation.
*   **2 to <5 years:** Vomiting (20%) is common.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Strong or moderate inducers (e.g., rifampin, efavirenz) significantly decrease bedaquiline exposure and should be avoided [US].
*   **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Strong inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole) increase bedaquiline systemic exposure and the risk of adverse effects. Monitor closely if co-administration is necessary.
*   **Lopinavir/Ritonavir:** Increases bedaquiline exposure by approximately 22%; monitor for toxicity.
*   **QTc-Prolonging Drugs:** Exercise extreme caution when used with clofazimine, as additive QTc effects have been observed.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   **Category:** Not specified by letter; however, animal data (rats/rabbits) showed no evidence of fetal harm at 6x human clinical exposure. Human data are insufficient to evaluate drug-associated risks [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. Bedaquiline is excreted in human milk; concentrations are 19–29 times higher in milk than in maternal plasma. Due to the long half-life, breastfeeding should be avoided during treatment and for **27.5 months** after the final dose [US].
*   **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in animal models.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatrics:** Approved for ages 2 years and older (≥8 kg). Safety/efficacy not established for children <2 years or <8 kg [US].
*   **Elderly:** Limited data for patients ≥65 years; use with caution.
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment needed for mild-to-moderate impairment (Child-Pugh A or B). Use with caution and increased monitoring in severe impairment [US].
*   **Renal Impairment:** No adjustment for mild-to-moderate impairment. Use with caution in severe renal impairment or end-stage renal disease requiring dialysis.

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits mycobacterial ATP (adenosine 5’-triphosphate) synthase, specifically binding to subunit c of the enzyme, essential for energy generation in *M. tuberculosis*.
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ is ~5 hours. Bioavailability increases 2-fold with a high-fat meal.
    *   **Distribution:** High protein binding (>99.9%).
    *   **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized via CYP3A4 to the M2 metabolite (less active, but contributes to QTc prolongation).
    *   **Elimination:** Primarily fecal excretion. Terminal half-life is approximately **5.5 months**.
*   **Microbiology:** Exhibits bactericidal activity. Resistance can occur via mutations in the *atpE* gene or the *Rv0678* gene (efflux pump).

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Study 1:** A randomized, placebo-controlled trial in adults showed bedaquiline significantly reduced the median time to sputum culture conversion (83 days vs. 125 days) [US].
*   **Study 4 (STREAM Trial):** Demonstrated that a bedaquiline-containing 40-week all-oral regimen was non-inferior to a standard 40-week regimen containing an injectable agent, with favorable outcomes in 82.7% of patients [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F–86°F).
*   **Handling:** Protect from light and moisture. Keep in the original container with the desiccant.
*   **Dispensing:** If 100 mg tablets are dispensed outside the original container, use a tight, light-resistant container with an expiration date not exceeding 3 months [US].

*   **Classification:** Prescription-only (Rx-only) [US].
