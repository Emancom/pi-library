# Editorial Product Information: budesonide + formoterol

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **budesonide + formoterol**
- Chemical class:
  - Budesonide: inhaled corticosteroid (glucocorticoid)
  - Formoterol: long‑acting β2‑adrenoceptor agonist (LABA)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Inhalation (dry powder inhaler / breath‑actuated inhalation device)
- Do not include brand or trade names, tablet physical descriptions, or schedule listings.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Asthma (adults ≥18 years): to achieve overall asthma control including symptom relief and reduction in risk of exacerbations. Treatment approaches described include:
  - Anti‑inflammatory reliever therapy (as‑needed use for symptom relief and prevention of known triggers)
  - Anti‑inflammatory reliever plus maintenance therapy
  - Maintenance therapy (fixed dose) with a separate short‑acting bronchodilator as reliever
- Not indicated in children and adolescents under 18 years for the product strengths described where a lower strength (e.g. 100/6 µg) is not available [Australian].
- Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) (adults): symptomatic treatment of moderate to severe COPD (FEV1 ≤50% predicted) in adults with frequent symptoms despite prior long‑acting bronchodilator use and/or a history of recurrent exacerbations. Not indicated for initiation of bronchodilator therapy in COPD.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General principles
- Use the lowest effective maintenance dose to achieve and maintain control.
- When used as maintenance therapy, advise patients to continue maintenance doses even if asymptomatic.
- If discontinuing long‑term therapy, taper maintenance dose rather than stopping abruptly. Complete withdrawal of inhaled corticosteroid should only be considered in exceptional circumstances (e.g., to confirm diagnosis).
- If patients experience progressive or sudden deterioration, urgent medical assessment is required; combination ICS/LABA inhaler alone is insufficient treatment for severe exacerbations—consider systemic corticosteroids and/or other acute measures.

### Adult dosing by regimen/indication
- Anti‑inflammatory reliever therapy (adults ≥18 years)
  - Use the lower strength combination inhaler (200/6 µg metered dose; corresponding delivered dose specified by product) as needed for symptom relief and for prevention of known triggers (e.g., allergen/exercise) when discussed with clinician.
  - Typical as‑needed dosing: 1 inhalation in response to symptoms; if symptoms persist after a few minutes, one additional inhalation may be taken.
  - Do not take more than 6 inhalations on a single occasion.
  - Total daily use: normally not more than 8 inhalations; up to 12 inhalations per day may be used temporarily.
  - If a three‑day period of deteriorating symptoms occurs despite as‑needed use, reassess for alternative explanations and consider escalation of therapy.

- Anti‑inflammatory reliever plus maintenance therapy (adults ≥18 years)
  - Reliever: 1 inhalation of the 200/6 µg product as needed for symptoms (same limits as above).
  - Maintenance: commonly 2 inhalations per day of the 200/6 µg product, given either as one inhalation morning and evening or two inhalations together (morning or evening) depending on patient needs.
  - For some patients, maintenance of 2 inhalations twice daily may be appropriate. Titrate to the lowest effective maintenance dose.
  - The higher‑strength product (400/12 µg) should not be used as the reliever in the anti‑inflammatory reliever plus maintenance regimen.

- Maintenance (fixed‑dose) therapy (adults ≥18 years)
  - 200/6 µg strength: 1–2 inhalations twice daily. Maximum recommended daily maintenance dose: 4 inhalations/day (equivalent to 800 µg budesonide / 24 µg formoterol per day).
  - 400/12 µg strength (for adults requiring higher maintenance dose): 2 inhalations twice daily (maximum recommended daily maintenance dose: 4 inhalations/day, equivalent to 1600 µg budesonide / 48 µg formoterol). When control is achieved, reduce dose (for example to 1 inhalation twice daily).
  - When used as fixed maintenance, patients should have a separate short‑acting bronchodilator available for relief of acute symptoms.

- COPD (adults)
  - 200/6 µg strength: 2 inhalations twice daily (maximum recommended daily dose: 4 inhalations = 800 µg budesonide / 24 µg formoterol).
  - 400/12 µg strength: 1 inhalation twice daily (maximum recommended daily dose: 2 inhalations = 800 µg budesonide / 24 µg formoterol).

### Administration instructions (device‑specific)
- The device is breath‑actuated and inspiratory flow‑driven. Patients must inhale forcefully and deeply through the mouthpiece to deliver the dose.
- Recommended user steps (paraphrased):
  - Open: open the mouthpiece cover fully until it clicks.
  - Breathe: place mouthpiece between teeth and form a tight lip seal; inhale forcefully and deeply through the mouthpiece; remove device and hold breath for about 10 seconds or as long as comfortable.
  - Close: breathe out gently away from the device and close the mouthpiece cover.
- Do not shake the inhaler before use. Do not exhale into the device. Do not block air vents when preparing to inhale.
- Patients should rinse their mouth with water after inhalation.
- Patients may notice a taste related to the lactose excipient; lactose may contain milk protein residues.

### Titration
- Maintenance dose should be titrated to the lowest dose that maintains asthma control.
- If transferring from oral corticosteroids, do not initiate inhaled steroid component as the sole therapy for steroid withdrawal without careful consideration; assess adrenal function where prior systemic steroid exposure may have suppressed the HPA axis.

### Missed dose

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to budesonide, formoterol or lactose (including lactose that may contain milk protein residues).
- No other absolute contraindications specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- **Asthma/COPD management**: Treatment should conform to physician recommendation and national treatment guidelines. Patients with asthma should have a personal asthma action plan.
- **Reliever availability**: Ensure patients always have appropriate reliever therapy available:
  - For anti‑inflammatory reliever plus maintenance regimens, the combination inhaler used as reliever should be available at all times.
  - For fixed‑dose maintenance regimens, a separate short‑acting bronchodilator should be available for symptom relief.
- **Exacerbations**: Sudden or progressive deterioration of asthma or COPD can be life‑threatening. Urgent assessment is required. Consider systemic corticosteroids and/or antibiotics for severe exacerbations. A combination ICS/LABA inhaler alone is not sufficient for management of severe exacerbations.
- **Withdrawal**: When discontinuing long‑term therapy, taper rather than stop abruptly to reduce risk of adrenal insufficiency.
- **Oral corticosteroid exposure**: Do not use this fixed combination to initiate inhaled steroids in patients being transferred directly from oral steroids without appropriate clinical supervision; prior systemic steroid use may have suppressed adrenal function—exercise caution.
- **Potential systemic corticosteroid effects**: At higher than recommended doses, inhaled corticosteroids may cause Cushing’s syndrome, Cushingoid features, HPA axis suppression, decreased bone mineral density, cataract, glaucoma, blurred vision, growth retardation in children/adolescents, increased susceptibility to infections, and impaired stress response. Very rare cases of clinically relevant adrenal dysfunction have been reported at recommended doses.
  - Monitor for adrenal suppression in patients on prolonged high doses and in patients receiving concomitant potent CYP3A4 inhibitors.
  - Consider additional systemic glucocorticoid coverage during periods of stress (e.g., surgery, severe infection) for patients with possible adrenal suppression.
- **Bone health**: High doses of corticosteroids can affect bone mass. Long‑term adult studies cited did not show negative effects at recommended doses, but the lowest dose affecting bone mass is not established. Interpret bone mineral density in growing children with caution.
- **Growth**: Limited long‑term data suggest most children eventually reach expected adult height, but a small transient reduction in growth (approx. 1 cm) has been observed; height monitoring is recommended in children receiving inhaled corticosteroids.
- **Visual disturbances**: Reported with systemic and topical corticosteroid use. Consider ophthalmologic evaluation for blurred vision or other visual changes (possible cataract, glaucoma, or central serous chorioretinopathy).
- **Infections and tuberculosis**: High doses of glucocorticoids may mask or exacerbate infections. Exercise caution in patients with active or latent pulmonary tuberculosis and in those with fungal, bacterial or viral respiratory infections.
- **Sensitivity to sympathomimetic amines**: Use formoterol with caution in patients with increased susceptibility (e.g., inadequately controlled hyperthyroidism).
- **Cardiovascular effects**:
  - β2‑agonists have arrhythmogenic potential. Caution in patients with pre‑existing cardiovascular disease (ischaemic heart disease, tachyarrhythmias, severe hypertension, hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy, aneurysm, severe heart failure, idiopathic subvalvular aortic stenosis).
  - Formoterol may prolong the QTc interval and can cause tachycardia, arrhythmias and myocardial effects at high doses. Use with caution in patients with known QTc prolongation or who are receiving concomitant therapies that prolong QTc.
- **Hypokalaemia**: High doses of β2‑agonists may cause hypokalaemia via intracellular redistribution of potassium; this may be potentiated by concomitant treatments (xanthine derivatives, mineralocorticoids, diuretics) and by hypoxia during acute exacerbation. Monitor serum potassium where clinically indicated (e.g., patients on digoxin, diuretics, or with severe exacerbations).
- **Diabetes**: β2‑agonists may increase blood glucose. Monitor blood glucose control when initiating therapy in diabetic patients.
- **Phaeochromocytoma**: Administer with caution.
- **Paradoxical bronchospasm**: Rarely, immediate paradoxical bronchospasm can occur after dosing. Discontinue drug and treat immediately if this occurs.
- **Pneumonia (COPD)**: In patients with COPD treated with inhaled corticosteroids, an increased incidence of pneumonia (including hospitalisation) has been observed. Risk factors include current smoking, older age, low BMI, and severe COPD. Maintain vigilance for pneumonia because symptoms may overlap with COPD exacerbations.
- **Hepatic impairment**: Budesonide and formoterol are primarily eliminated via hepatic metabolism; increased systemic exposure is expected in patients with severe liver disease—use with caution.
- **Renal impairment**: No clinical data available; effect on pharmacokinetics not specified.
- **Elderly**: No special dosing requirements specified.
- **Paediatric use**: This product is not indicated for use in children and adolescents under 18 years when the lower pediatric strength is not available in this product formulation [Australian].
- **Laboratory tests**: No data available regarding specific laboratory test interferences.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Budesonide is primarily metabolised by CYP3A4. Potent CYP3A4 inhibitors can increase systemic exposure to budesonide and thereby increase the risk of systemic corticosteroid adverse effects.
  - Examples of potent CYP3A4 inhibitors listed: ketoconazole, itraconazole, voriconazole, posaconazole, clarithromycin, telithromycin, nefazodone, ritonavir and HIV protease inhibitors. Co‑treatment with cobicistat‑containing products is also expected to increase systemic exposure and risk.
  - Co‑administration with potent CYP3A4 inhibitors should be avoided unless the clinical benefit outweighs the increased risk; if co‑administration is necessary, monitor for systemic corticosteroid side effects and consider alternative arrangements.
  - Use of the maintenance‑and‑reliever regimen with the 200/6 µg product is **not recommended** in patients receiving potent CYP3A4 inhibitors [Australian].

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- β‑receptor blocking agents (including eye drops), particularly non‑selective blockers, may antagonise the effects of formoterol and may increase airway resistance; their use in asthma patients is not recommended.
- Other sympathomimetic agents / β‑adrenergic stimulants (e.g., ephedrine) may have additive bronchodilator effects and should not be given concomitantly with formoterol; avoid formoterol in patients who have received large doses of sympathomimetics.
- Drugs that may potentiate hypokalaemia (xanthine derivatives, mineralocorticoids, diuretics) can increase the hypokalaemic effect of β2‑agonists; monitor serum potassium where indicated, especially during acute exacerbations.
- Patients receiving digoxin are particularly sensitive to hypokalaemia which may increase the risk of digitalis‑induced arrhythmias; monitor potassium and digoxin levels as clinically indicated.
- Caution with concomitant therapies that prolong QTc interval due to potential additive effect (formoterol may prolong QTc) — monitor ECG where clinically warranted.

### Management notes
- Avoid or use with caution potent CYP3A4 inhibitors; if unavoidable, monitor closely for systemic corticosteroid effects and consider dose adjustments or alternative therapy.
- Avoid concurrent non‑selective β‑blockers in asthma patients; selective β1‑blockers may be used with caution if essential and under close supervision.
- Monitor serum potassium and ECG in patients at risk of hypokalaemia or with significant cardiac disease.
- Monitor blood glucose in diabetic patients when initiating or changing dose.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Budesonide: inhaled corticosteroid with anti‑inflammatory effects on the airways via glucocorticoid receptor agonism, reducing airway inflammation and hyperresponsiveness.
- Formoterol: long‑acting β2‑adrenoceptor agonist causing bronchodilation by relaxation of bronchial smooth muscle through β2 receptor activation. The combination provides both controller (ICS) and bronchodilator (LABA) effects; when used as an anti‑inflammatory reliever, the LABA component provides rapid relief of bronchoconstriction while the ICS component provides anti‑inflammatory benefit.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: Not specified in available sources for systemic absorption parameters.
- Distribution: Not specified in available sources.
- Metabolism:
  - Budesonide: primarily metabolised via cytochrome P450 3A4 (CYP3A4).
  - Formoterol: metabolised primarily in the liver (specific enzymes not detailed in available sources).
- Elimination: Both drugs are primarily eliminated by hepatic metabolism; increased systemic exposure is expected in severe liver disease. Specific half‑lives, clearance and renal excretion details are not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Formoterol exhibits rapid onset bronchodilation and can cause cardiovascular effects (e.g., tachycardia, arrhythmias) particularly at high doses; may prolong QTc interval.
- Budesonide exerts glucocorticoid effects locally and, at high systemic exposure, can produce systemic corticosteroid effects (HPA suppression, Cushingoid features).

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Peak inspiratory flow rate studies: Moderate and severe asthmatic patients were shown to be able to generate sufficient inspiratory flow for delivery from the breath‑actuated dry powder device (device‑specific clinical trial details not provided).
- Bone mineral density / growth studies:
  - Long‑term adult follow‑up studies (3–6 years) with budesonide at recommended doses did not demonstrate a negative effect on bone mass compared with placebo, including in patients at high risk for osteoporosis (study details not specified).
  - In children (5–16 years) three medium to long‑term studies (12 months–6 years) did not show changes in bone mineral density with budesonide (189–1322 µg/day) compared with controls (details not specified).
  - A randomized 18‑month pediatric study (n = 176; ages 5–10 years) reported a statistically significant reduction in bone mineral density (−0.11 g/cm2; p = 0.023) in the budesonide group compared with a comparator group; dosing regimen in that study: 400 µg twice daily for 1 month, 200 µg twice daily for 5 months, 100 µg twice daily for 12 months.
- COPD / pneumonia observations:
  - Clinical data have shown an increased incidence of pneumonia (including hospitalisation) in COPD patients receiving inhaled corticosteroids; some evidence suggests increased risk with higher steroid dose though results vary between studies. Specific trial names, designs and numerical results are not specified in available sources.
