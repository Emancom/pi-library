# Editorial Product Information: Baclofen

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial synthesis of the Product Information for **baclofen**, intended for use by healthcare professionals. 
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information approved in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for prescribing within their region.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Baclofen (a derivative of gamma-aminobutyric acid [GABA])
*   **Chemical Class:** Antispastic agent, GABA-beta agonist
*   **Brand Names:** APO-Baclofen [Australian]
*   **ATC Code:** M03BX01
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    *   Oral tablets: 10 mg and 25 mg [Australian]
*   **Route of Administration:** Oral
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Multiple Sclerosis:** Suppression of voluntary muscle spasm [Australian].
*   **Spinal Lesions:** Treatment of hypertonus, spasticity, and dyssynergic bladder dysfunction resulting from spinal lesions of traumatic, infectious, degenerative, neoplastic, or unknown origin [Australian].
*   **Note on Limitations:** Baclofen is not recommended for use in Parkinson's disease, rheumatoid disorders, cerebral palsy, or spasticity resulting from stroke [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
*   **General Dosing (Adult):** Initial therapy should commence at low doses, typically in a hospital setting, and be titrated upward until the optimal response is achieved (balancing spasm reduction against the maintenance of enough muscle tone for functional movement) [Australian].
*   **Administration Instructions:** Tablets should be taken during meals with a glass of water, divided into at least three daily doses [Australian].
*   **Titration Schedule:** 
    *   **Initial:** 5 mg three times daily for 3 days.
    *   **Increments:** Increase by 5 mg per dose (15 mg/day total increase) every three days until the desired effect is achieved. 
    *   **Standard Range:** 30 mg to 75 mg daily.
    *   **Maximum:** Up to 100 mg daily may be required for hospitalized patients [Australian].
*   **Discontinuation:** Unless in an emergency (overdose or serious adverse effect), treatment must be tapered gradually over 1 to 2 weeks to avoid withdrawal symptoms [Australian].
*   **Evaluation:** If no clinical benefit is observed within 6 to 8 weeks of reaching maximum dosage, continuation should be reassessed [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
*   Known hypersensitivity to **baclofen** or any of the inactive excipients (e.g., lactose monohydrate) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Abrupt Withdrawal:** Sudden cessation of therapy can lead to anxiety, delirium, hallucinations, psychosis, seizures (status epilepticus), tachycardia, hyperthermia, and rebound spasticity. Neonatal withdrawal has been reported following intrauterine exposure [Australian].
*   **Psychiatric and CNS Disorders:** Use with caution in patients with psychotic disorders, schizophrenia, or depressive/manic states, as **baclofen** may exacerbate these conditions. Monitor closely for suicidal ideation or behavior [Australian].
*   **Neurological Effects:** Encephalopathy (somnolence, confusion, myoclonus, coma) has been reported; therapy should be discontinued if these signs occur [Australian].
*   **Epilepsy:** Use with caution; the convulsion threshold may be lowered, though treatment may continue if adequate anticonvulsant therapy is maintained [Australian].
*   **Posture and Balance:** Use caution when spasticity is necessary to maintain upright posture or balance in locomotion [Australian].
*   **Misuse and Dependence:** Monitor patients with a history of substance abuse for drug-seeking behavior or dose escalation [Australian].
*   **Concomitant Conditions:** Use with caution in patients with peptic ulcers, cerebrovascular disease, porphyria, or diabetes mellitus (may increase blood glucose) [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions occur primarily at the start of treatment or following rapid dose escalation.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Nausea, sedation, somnolence |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Confusion, hallucinations, depression, insomnia, euphoria, nightmares; lightheadedness, dizziness, ataxia, tremor, nystagmus, tinnitus; headache; accommodation disorders, visual disturbances; hypotension; respiratory depression; vomiting, constipation, diarrhea, dry mouth; hyperhidrosis, rash; muscular weakness, myalgia; dysuria, urinary frequency, enuresis; fatigue; decreased cardiac output |
| **Rare (0.1–1%)** | Arrhythmias, palpitations, chest pain; dyspnea, ankle edema; colicky abdominal pain, anorexia; paraesthesia, dysarthria, dysgeusia, syncope, dyskinesia, coma; urinary retention, nocturia, hematuria; erectile dysfunction |
| **Very Rare (<0.1%)** | Hypothermia |
| **Not Known** | Hypersensitivity, bradycardia, urticaria, alopecia, sexual dysfunction, drug withdrawal syndrome, facial/peripheral edema, increased blood glucose |

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    *   **CNS Depressants:** Alcohol, other muscle relaxants (e.g., tizanidine), and synthetic opiates increase sedation and respiratory depression risks [Australian].
    *   **Antihypertensives:** Potential for increased hypotensive effects; dosage adjustment of antihypertensive agents may be necessary [Australian].
    *   **Levodopa/Carbidopa:** Concomitant use may result in mental confusion, hallucinations, and worsening of Parkinsonian symptoms [Australian].
    *   **Tricyclic Antidepressants:** May potentiate **baclofen** effects, leading to significant muscular hypotonia [Australian].
    *   **MAOIs:** May increase CNS depression and hypotensive risks [Australian].
    *   **Lithium:** May aggravate hyperkinetic symptoms [Australian].
*   **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    *   **Renal Impairment Agents:** Drugs that significantly impact renal function may reduce **baclofen** excretion, increasing toxicity risk [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B3]. **Baclofen** crosses the placental barrier. Animal studies show increased omphaloceles and skeletal ossification issues at maternotoxic doses. Use only if benefits outweigh risks to the fetus. Monitor neonates for withdrawal symptoms [Australian].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Found in very small quantities in breast milk. Due to limited data, use caution when treating lactating women [Australian].
*   **Fertility:** No human data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Use with extreme caution in children under 16 years due to limited data. Tablets are not suitable for children weighing less than 33 kg [Australian].
*   **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of CNS toxicity and renal impairment; requires a cautious dosage schedule and close surveillance [Australian].
*   **Renal Impairment:** Primarily eliminated via kidneys. A lower dose (approximately 5 mg daily) is advised. End-stage renal failure patients should only receive the drug if benefits outweigh risks and must be monitored for early signs of toxicity (e.g., somnolence) [Australian].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Partially metabolized in the liver; monitor liver function (AST, alkaline phosphatase) periodically [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** **Baclofen** is a GABA-beta receptor agonist. It depresses monosynaptic and polysynaptic reflex transmission in the spinal cord by inhibiting the release of excitatory amino acids (glutamate and aspartate). It does not affect neuromuscular transmission [Australian].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    *   **Absorption:** Rapid and complete; bioavailability 70–80%. Peak plasma levels reached in 1–2 hours.
    *   **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is 0.7 L/kg; protein binding is approximately 30%. CSF concentrations are ~8.5 times lower than plasma.
    *   **Metabolism:** ~15% metabolized in the liver to an inactive metabolite.
    *   **Elimination:** Primarily renal (75% as unchanged drug); elimination half-life is 2 to 6.8 hours [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   Store below 25°C. Protect from light and moisture [Australian].
*   **Shelf Life:** Consult packaging for expiry; refer to the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG) summary for registered shelf life [Australian].
