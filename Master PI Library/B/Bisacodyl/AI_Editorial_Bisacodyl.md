# Editorial Product Information: Bisacodyl

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial was developed using official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **bisacodyl** for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory guidance into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Bisacodyl (chemical class: diphenylmethane derivative).
- **Brand Names:** **Bisalax** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A06AB02.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Enema:** 10 mg bisacodyl per 5 mL tube [Australian].
    - **Tablets:** Mentioned as a secondary dosage form for combination therapy; typically 5 mg (strength not explicitly specified in the primary enema document) [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Rectal (enema), Oral (tablets).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Orion Pharma (Aus) Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Constipation:** Management of constipation associated with bed confinement, dietary changes, or travel; also indicated for constipation related to serious illness, metabolic disorders, or circulatory disturbances [Australian].
- **Chronic Constipation and Bowel Re-training:** Occasional use in chronic cases and as an aid to re-establish normal bowel hygiene [Australian].
- **Periprocedural Use:** Preparation for radiography, sigmoidoscopy, proctoscopy, or surgery (e.g., colostomy irrigation or preoperative preparation) [Australian].
- **Obstetric Use:** Antepartum and postpartum constipation; preparation for delivery [Australian].
- **Postoperative Care:** Management of constipation following surgery, including cases involving haemorrhoids or anal fissures [Australian].
- **Barium Contrast Clearance:** Management of constipation following barium contrast medium administration [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Acute Constipation:** One enema initially, followed by 1 or 2 tablets in the evening and one enema the following morning for approximately three days [Australian].
- **Elderly Patients:** One enema in the morning on days when defecation is desired [Australian].
- **Chronic Constipation/Bowel Retraining:** Gradually increase the interval between doses as colonic tone improves [Australian].

### Periprocedural Dosing
- **Radiography (Abdominal X-ray):** The day prior, take 2 tablets at lunch and 2 tablets in the evening. Administer one enema 1–2 hours before the procedure. Fasting should occur after tablet administration to prevent caecal re-accumulation [Australian].
- **Sigmoidoscopy/Proctoscopy:** Tablets the night before and one enema one hour before the examination [Australian].
- **Colostomies:** Two tablets the night before; one enema inserted into the colostomy opening the morning of the procedure [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Enema:** Pull off the plastic cap, insert the nozzle into the rectum, and squeeze the tube. The nozzle may be coated with a lubricant to assist insertion [Australian].
- **Patient Advice:** If bowel habits change persistently, or if rectal bleeding occurs, patients should consult a physician. Use should not exceed one week without medical supervision [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Acute Gastrointestinal Conditions:** Symptoms suggestive of appendicitis (cramps, colic, nausea, vomiting), inflammatory bowel disease, ileus, intestinal obstruction, or undiagnosed abdominal pain [Australian].
- **Metabolic/Systemic Conditions:** Dehydration and hypokalaemia [Australian].
- **Local Rectal Conditions:** Anal fissures, ulcerative proctitis with mucosal damage, and ulcerated haemorrhoids [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to **bisacodyl** or any excipients (e.g., macrogols) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
- **Prolonged Use:** Long-term use may lead to laxative dependence, chronic constipation, and loss of normal bowel function. It may result in "cathartic colon," characterised by atony and dilatation [Australian].
- **Electrolyte Imbalance:** Continued use can cause significant loss of electrolytes, particularly potassium, which may lead to cardiac dysfunction, dehydration, and protein-losing enteropathy [Australian].
- **Syncope:** Cases of syncope have been reported, potentially related to defecation syncope or a vasovagal response to abdominal pain [Australian].
- **General Precaution:** Rectal bleeding or failure to have a bowel movement after use may indicate a serious condition and requires medical evaluation [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally rare and well-tolerated when used as directed.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhoea, local rectal irritation, proctitis, tenesmus, nausea, abdominal pain, cramping, vomiting, and epigastralgia [Australian]. 
- **Hypersensitivity:** Rare cases of mild transient skin reactions, angioedema, and anaphylactoid reactions [Australian].
- **Nervous System:** Syncope [Australian].
- **Clinical Management:** Colic associated with use should be treated with atropine derivatives [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Diuretics and Systemic Corticosteroids:** Concomitant use may increase the risk of electrolyte imbalance, particularly if **bisacodyl** is taken in excessive doses [Australian].
- **Digoxin and Controlled-Release Formulations:** Increased intestinal motility may reduce the bioavailability of drugs primarily absorbed in the small intestine [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category A (Drugs which have been taken by a large number of pregnant women and women of childbearing age without any proven increase in the frequency of malformations or other direct or indirect harmful effects on the fetus having been observed) [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Small quantities are excreted in breast milk. There is no contraindication for use by nursing mothers, but it should only be used when clearly indicated [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Data not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Should not be used frequently without medical supervision. Tablets are not recommended for children under 6 years [Australian].
    - **Preparation for X-ray (<1 yr):** Half enema the night before, half enema the morning of the exam.
    - **Preparation for X-ray (1–3 yrs):** One enema the night before, one enema the morning of the exam.
    - **Preparation for X-ray (3–6 yrs):** One enema the afternoon before, one enema the morning of the exam.
    - **Preparation for X-ray (6–12 yrs):** One tablet at 10 am and one enema the afternoon before.
    - **Constipation (<3 yrs):** Half enema (insert only half the point of the tube).
    - **Constipation (3–6 yrs):** One enema as required.
    - **Constipation (6–12 yrs):** One tablet in the evening and one enema in the morning [Australian].
- **Elderly:** See Section 3 for dosing. Use with caution due to potential for electrolyte disturbances [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Bisacodyl** is a contact laxative that acts locally in the colon. It stimulates sensory nerve endings in the colonic mucosa to induce parasympathetic reflexes, resulting in increased peristalsis. It may be effective in patients with spinal cord damage or ganglionic blockade [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Rectal administration typically produces an effect within 5 to 15 minutes [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Approximately 5% of an oral dose is absorbed [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Absorbed drug is converted to the glucuronide [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Excreted primarily in the faeces; partially in the urine as a glucuronide [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store between 2°C and 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze) [Australian].
- **Handling:** Allow the enema to reach room temperature before administration [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].
