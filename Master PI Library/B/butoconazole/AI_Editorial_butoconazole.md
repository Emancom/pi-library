# Editorial Product Information: Butoconazole Nitrate

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (Food and Drug Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the Product Information (PI) for butoconazole nitrate vaginal cream. It is designed as a structured reference for healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information. Clinicians should consult the current approved PI in their specific region before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Butoconazole nitrate (an imidazole derivative antifungal)
- **Brand Names:** Gynazole•1 [US]
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in provided sources
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Vaginal cream, 2% (USP). Each 5 g applicatorful contains approximately 100 mg of butoconazole nitrate [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Vaginal use only
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Padagis Israel Pharmaceuticals Ltd [US]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Vulvovaginal Candidiasis (VVC):** Indicated for the local treatment of vulvovaginal fungal infections caused by *Candida* species. Diagnosis should be confirmed via KOH smears and/or fungal cultures [US].
- **Clinical Note:** Safety and efficacy have been established in non-pregnant women; however, use in pregnancy requires specific clinical justification (see Section 8) [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** A single-dose regimen consisting of one prefilled applicator (approximately 5 g of cream) administered intravaginally [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - The applicator is designed for single-use only.
    - Do not warm the applicator before use.
    - The patient should pull the ring to fully extend the plunger, insert the applicator gently into the vagina as far as it will comfortably go, and depress the plunger to release the cream [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Not applicable for this single-dose treatment.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known history of hypersensitivity to butoconazole nitrate or any of the inactive ingredients in the formulation (e.g., mineral oil, parabens) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Interaction with Latex/Rubber:** The vaginal cream contains mineral oil, which may weaken latex or rubber products such as condoms or vaginal contraceptive diaphragms. Use of these barrier methods within 72 hours of treatment is not recommended [US].
- **Differential Diagnosis:** If clinical symptoms persist, diagnostic tests (KOH smear/culture) should be repeated to rule out other pathogens or conditions predisposing the patient to recurrent infections [US].
- **HIV Risk Assessment:** Recurrent vaginal yeast infections, particularly those difficult to eradicate, may be an early indicator of infection with human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) in at-risk women [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
In controlled clinical trials involving 314 patients, approximately 5.7% reported complaints.
- **Common (1–10%):** 
    - Vulvar/vaginal burning, itching, soreness, and swelling.
    - Pelvic or abdominal pain or cramping.
- **Serious/Treatment-Related:** 
    - Approximately 1% of complaints were considered treatment-related by investigators.
    - Discontinuation due to adverse events occurred in approximately 1.6% of the trial population [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Physical Interactions:** Significant interaction with latex/rubber (see Section 5) [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Not specified in provided sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category C:** Use during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus. In US labeling, it is recommended that use during pregnancy occur only under the supervision of a physician [US].
    - **Animal Data:** Intravaginal administration in rats (6 mg/kg/day) showed increased fetal resorption and decreased litter size without teratogenicity. High oral doses in rats (≥100 mg/kg/day) resulted in fetal malformations (cleft palate, abdominal wall defects), though maternal stress was noted at these levels [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if butoconazole is excreted in human milk. Caution is advised when administering to nursing women [US].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in rabbits or rats at oral doses up to 30 mg/kg/day and 100 mg/kg/day, respectively [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in children have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in provided sources.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in provided sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** An imidazole derivative that inhibits steroid synthesis in fungi. It primarily inhibits the conversion of lanosterol to ergosterol, leading to altered fungal cell membrane lipid composition, increased permeability, osmotic disruption, and growth inhibition [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** On average, 1.7% (range 1.3–2.2%) of the dose is absorbed following vaginal administration.
    - **Distribution/Metabolism:** Peak plasma levels (13.6–18.6 ng radioequivalents/mL) of the parent drug and metabolites are reached between 12 and 24 hours post-administration [US].
- **Microbiology:** Fungicidal activity *in vitro* against *Candida* spp. Clinical efficacy is primarily established against *Candida albicans* [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Vulvovaginal Candidiasis:** Two studies compared 2% butoconazole cream (single dose) to 500 mg clotrimazole vaginal tablets. 
- **Outcomes (30-day follow-up):**
    - **Therapeutic Cure:** 67% (Butoconazole) vs. 61% (Clotrimazole).
    - **Clinical Cure:** 81% (Butoconazole) vs. 80% (Clotrimazole).
    - **Mycologic Eradication:** 74% (Butoconazole) vs. 66% (Clotrimazole) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F).
- **Excursions:** Permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F–86°F).
- **Special Handling:** Avoid heat above 30°C (86°F) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only (Rx Only) [US].
