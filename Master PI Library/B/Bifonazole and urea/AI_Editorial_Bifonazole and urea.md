# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine Injection for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data from official product labeling.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements. 

*Note: While the request context mentioned "Bifonazole and urea," the provided source documentation pertains exclusively to Levocarnitine Injection. This editorial is based on the provided clinical data for Levocarnitine.*

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Name:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection solution: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - Administration: Slow intravenous bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or infusion.
    - Acute Crisis: A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and not less than every 6 hours.
    - Maintenance: Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilized based on clinical requirement [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** 
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - Administration: Slow intravenous bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - Dose Adjustment: Adjustments should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if concentrations are supra-normal [US].

### Monitoring
- Plasma free carnitine concentrations should be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L.
- Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma concentrations is recommended for patients with metabolic disorders [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known based on current clinical documentation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported. These often occur within minutes of administration, primarily in ESRD patients. Treatment must be discontinued immediately upon signs of hypersensitivity [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in both patients with and without pre-existing seizure disorders. In those with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].

### Specific Precautions
- **Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. Monitoring of INR is recommended upon initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not explicitly letter-categorized in source; however, reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg); see Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primary use includes ESRD patients on dialysis. High-dose oral therapy is cautioned against in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance essential for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing the substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly cleared [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
- **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours.
- **Half-life:** Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, intravenous doses of 20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis resulted in pre-dialysis (trough) concentrations increasing from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg showed dose-dependent increases in trough concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks. While carnitine levels increased, the effect on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives.
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
