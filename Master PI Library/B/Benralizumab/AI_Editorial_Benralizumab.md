# Editorial Product Information: Benralizumab

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information from Australia (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of benralizumab for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Benralizumab (a humanised, afucosylated, IgG1 kappa-class monoclonal antibody).
- **Chemical Class:** Interleukin-5 receptor alpha (IL-5Rα) antagonist.
- **Brand Name(s):** FASENRA, FASENRA PEN [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** R03DX10.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
  - Solution for injection: 30 mg in 1 mL (30 mg/mL).
  - Available in single-use prefilled syringes or prefilled pens (FASENRA PEN).
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AstraZeneca Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Severe Eosinophilic Asthma:** Add-on maintenance treatment in patients aged 12 years and older with severe eosinophilic asthma (defined by blood eosinophil count ≥300 cells/µL, or ≥150 cells/µL if receiving oral corticosteroids) [Australian-approved].
- **Eosinophilic Granulomatosis with Polyangiitis (EGPA):** Add-on treatment for adult patients (18 years and older) with relapsing or refractory EGPA [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Severe Eosinophilic Asthma (Adults and Adolescents ≥12 years):** The recommended dose is 30 mg via subcutaneous injection every 4 weeks for the first three doses, followed by a maintenance dose of 30 mg every 8 weeks thereafter [Australian].
- **EGPA (Adults ≥18 years):** The recommended dose is 30 mg via subcutaneous injection every 4 weeks [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Administer into the thigh or abdomen. The upper arm may be used if administered by a caregiver.
- Do not inject into areas of tender, bruised, erythematous, or hardened skin.
- Prior to use, allow the device to reach room temperature (approximately 30 minutes).
- Visually inspect for particulate matter or discolouration; the solution should be clear to opalescent, colourless to yellow, and may contain translucent/white particles.
- Do not shake.

### Missed Dose
- If a dose is missed, resume dosing as soon as possible on the indicated regimen. Do not administer a double dose [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to benralizumab or any of its excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Acute Exacerbations:** Benralizumab must not be used to treat acute asthma symptoms or exacerbations [Australian].
- **Corticosteroid Reduction:** Do not discontinue systemic corticosteroids abruptly upon starting benralizumab. Reductions in dose should be gradual and supervised by a physician [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Anaphylaxis, angioedema, and urticaria have been reported. These may occur within hours or days of administration. In the event of a reaction, discontinue the drug and initiate appropriate therapy [Australian].
- **Parasitic (Helminth) Infections:** Eosinophils are involved in the immune response to helminths. Pre-existing infections should be treated before starting benralizumab. If a patient becomes infected during treatment and does not respond to anti-helminthics, benralizumab should be discontinued until the infection resolves [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Benralizumab is not cleared by Cytochrome P450 enzymes, efflux pumps, or protein-binding mechanisms. It is not expected to affect the pharmacokinetics of co-administered medications [Australian].
- **Vaccination:** In a small study, humoral antibody responses to seasonal influenza vaccination were not impaired by benralizumab [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **Australian Category B1:** (Drugs which have been taken by only a limited number of pregnant women and women of childbearing age, without an increase in the frequency of malformation or other direct or indirect harmful effects on the human fetus having been observed). 
  - Monoclonal antibodies (IgG) are transported across the placenta, particularly in the third trimester. Use is preferable to avoid during pregnancy unless the benefit to the mother outweighs the risk to the fetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if benralizumab is excreted in human milk. A risk to the breastfed child cannot be excluded. Decisions should consider the benefits of breastfeeding versus the clinical need for the drug in the mother [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal data (monkeys) showed no impairment of surrogate fertility parameters at doses >400 times the human exposure [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
  - **Asthma:** Safety and efficacy not established in children <12 years [Australian].
  - **EGPA:** Safety and efficacy not established in children and adolescents <18 years [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment is required for patients ≥65 years [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for mild to moderate renal impairment (eGFR 30–89 mL/min/1.73m²). Data are limited for eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73m², though the drug is not renally cleared [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required. Monoclonal antibodies are not primarily cleared via the hepatic pathway [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Benralizumab binds with high affinity (16 pM) to the alpha subunit of the human IL-5 receptor (IL-5Rα). This receptor is expressed on eosinophils and basophils. The antibody is afucosylated, which increases its affinity for FcγRIII receptors on effector cells (like Natural Killer cells), leading to apoptosis of target cells through enhanced antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC) [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Reduces blood eosinophils to a median count of 0 cells/µL within 24 hours (asthma) or 1 week (EGPA) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Bioavailability:** ~59%.
  - **Half-life:** ~15.5 days.
  - **Metabolism:** Degraded by proteolytic enzymes widely distributed in the body.
  - **Clearance:** 0.29 L/day; exhibits linear pharmacokinetics [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Asthma (SIROCCO/CALIMA):** Pivotal Phase 3 trials showed a 51% (SIROCCO) and 28% (CALIMA) reduction in the annual rate of exacerbations vs placebo in patients with ≥300 cells/µL eosinophils. Significant improvements in FEV1 (0.116 L to 0.159 L) were also noted [Australian].
- **Oral Corticosteroid (OCS) Reduction (ZONDA):** Patients on benralizumab achieved a median 75% reduction in daily OCS dose compared to 25% with placebo [Australian].
- **EGPA (MANDARA):** A 52-week non-inferiority trial compared benralizumab 30 mg every 4 weeks to mepolizumab 300 mg every 4 weeks. Benralizumab demonstrated non-inferiority in remission rates (58% vs 57% for mepolizumab) and allowed 41% of patients to completely taper off OCS compared to 26% in the mepolizumab group [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate). Do not freeze.
- Store in the original package to protect from light.
- **Room Temperature Stability:** May be kept at up to 25°C for a maximum of 14 days. If not used within 14 days of removal from the refrigerator, the product must be discarded [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4) [Australian].
