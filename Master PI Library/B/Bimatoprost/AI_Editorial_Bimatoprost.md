# Editorial Product Information: Bimatoprost

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **bimatoprost** for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, regulatory warnings, and administration guidelines into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI used for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for final prescribing decisions.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: **Bimatoprost** (a synthetic prostamide analogue).
- **Chemical Class**: Prostamide.
- **Brand Names**: Bimatoprost Sandoz [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: S01EE03.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**:
    - Ophthalmic solution: 300 µg/mL (0.3 mg/mL) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration**: Topical ophthalmic.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Sandoz Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Reduction of Elevated Intraocular Pressure (IOP)**: Indicated for patients with open-angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension. It may be used as first-line therapy, monotherapy, or as adjunctive therapy to topical beta-blockers [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults)**: One drop in the affected eye(s) once daily, administered in the evening [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions**:
    - Do not exceed once-daily dosing, as more frequent administration may reduce the IOP-lowering effect [Australian].
    - To minimise systemic absorption, apply pressure to the tear duct (nasolacrimal occlusion) immediately following instillation [Australian].
    - If using multiple topical ophthalmic medications, allow at least 5 minutes between the administration of different products [Australian].
- **Contact Lenses**: **Bimatoprost** solutions containing benzalkonium chloride may be absorbed by and discolour soft contact lenses. Lenses must be removed prior to instillation and may be reinserted 15 minutes after administration [Australian].
- **Missed Dose**: Not explicitly detailed; however, the instruction to maintain once-daily evening dosing applies [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity**: Known hypersensitivity to **bimatoprost** or any excipient in the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings**: None specified in available sources.
- **Pigmentation and Eyelash Changes**: Treatment may cause permanent increased brownish iris pigmentation. It may also cause reversible darkening of the eyelid skin and increased eyelash growth (length, thickness, and number). These changes may lead to a difference in appearance between eyes if only one eye is treated [Australian].
- **Prostaglandin Analogue Periorbitopathy (PAP)**: May induce periorbital lipodystrophic changes, including deepening of the eyelid sulcus, ptosis, and enophthalmos. These changes are typically mild and may be partially or fully reversible upon discontinuation [Australian].
- **Ocular Inflammation**: Use with caution in patients with active intraocular inflammation (e.g., uveitis) or a history of significant ocular viral infections (e.g., herpes simplex) [Australian].
- **Macular Oedema**: Use with caution in aphakic patients, pseudophakic patients with a torn posterior lens capsule, or those with known risk factors for macular oedema (e.g., diabetic retinopathy, retinal vein occlusion) [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular/Respiratory**: Use with caution in patients with a history of heart block (more severe than first degree), uncontrolled congestive heart failure, low blood pressure, or low heart rate. Use with caution in patients with compromised respiratory function (e.g., asthma, COPD) [Australian].
- **Bacterial Keratitis**: Risk of contamination exists with multi-dose containers; patients should avoid letting the dropper tip contact the eye or surrounding structures [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%)**: Conjunctival hyperemia (up to 42%), growth of eyelashes (up to 36%), ocular pruritus (up to 14%) [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%)**:
    - **Ocular**: Allergic conjunctivitis, asthenopia, blepharitis, eye discharge, eyelid erythema/pruritus, eye pain, foreign body sensation, increased iris pigmentation, lacrimation, ocular burning/irritation, superficial punctate keratitis, visual disturbance/blurred vision [Australian].
    - **Systemic**: Headache, asthenia, skin hyperpigmentation [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%)**: Blepharospasm, eyelid oedema, iritis, retinal haemorrhage, depression, vertigo, hirsutism [Australian].
- **Post-marketing Experience (Frequency not estimated)**: Macular oedema, nausea, dizziness, hypertension, asthma exacerbation, and hypersensitivity reactions [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions**: No drug-drug interactions are anticipated in humans due to extremely low systemic concentrations (<0.2 ng/mL) following ocular dosing [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions**: 
    - **Beta-blockers**: No evidence of interaction when used concomitantly with various ophthalmic beta-blocking agents [Australian].
    - **Prostaglandin Analogues**: Concomitant use of two or more prostaglandin analogues may paradoxically reduce the IOP-lowering effect [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: **Category B3** [Australian]. Animal studies show embryo/foetal toxicity and increased abortion at high oral doses. There are no adequate studies in pregnant women; use only if clearly necessary [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding**: **Bimatoprost** is excreted in rat milk. It is unknown if it is excreted in human milk. Nursing women should stop breastfeeding while using this medication [Australian].
- **Fertility**: No effects on fertility observed in rat studies at doses up to 0.6 mg/kg/day [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and effectiveness in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use**: No dosage adjustment is necessary for elderly patients [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment**: Not studied; use with caution [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Not studied; use with caution [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: **Bimatoprost** is a prostamide analogue that reduces IOP by increasing the outflow of aqueous humour through both the trabecular meshwork and the uveoscleral pathways [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics**: IOP reduction begins approximately 4 hours after administration, peaks at 8 to 12 hours, and is maintained for at least 24 hours [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Penetrates the human cornea and sclera. Peak blood concentrations are reached within 10 minutes and fall below detection limits within 1.5 hours [Australian].
    - **Distribution**: Moderately distributed into tissues (Vd 0.67 L/kg). Plasma protein binding is ~90% [Australian].
    - **Metabolism**: Undergoes oxidation, N-deethylation, and glucuronidation to form various metabolites [Australian].
    - **Elimination**: Primarily renal (67%); 25% via faeces. The elimination half-life is approximately 45 minutes after intravenous administration [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Monotherapy**: Two Phase 3 trials (n=1198) compared **bimatoprost** 0.03% (once or twice daily) to timolol 0.5% (twice daily). Once-daily **bimatoprost** was superior to timolol, with mean IOP reductions ranging from 6.88 to 8.69 mmHg. Twice-daily dosing was less effective than once-daily dosing [Australian].
- **Adjunctive Therapy**: Two 3-month trials (n=722) evaluated **bimatoprost** added to beta-blockers. **Bimatoprost** once daily provided additional IOP reduction (6.03 to 7.95 mmHg) and was superior to vehicle/beta-blocker combinations [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature**: Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life**: 36 months unopened; discard contents 4 weeks after opening the bottle [Australian].
- **Handling**: Keep the container tightly closed and avoid touching the dropper tip to any surface [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule**: **S4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
