# Editorial Product Information: Bisoprolol

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information provided by the **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data, dosing protocols, and safety information.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for full prescribing details and legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Bisoprolol fumarate.
- **Chemical Class:** $\beta_1$-selective adrenoceptor blocking agent (without intrinsic sympathomimetic or relevant membrane stabilising activity).
- **Brand Names (Australian):** APO-Bisoprolol.
- **ATC Code:** C07AB07 [Not explicitly specified in source].
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Australian:** Oral tablets available in 1.25 mg, 2.5 mg, 3.75 mg, 5 mg, and 10 mg strengths.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Sponsor (Australian):** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd.

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Stable Chronic Heart Failure (Moderate to Severe):** For use in addition to ACE inhibitors and diuretics, and optionally cardiac glycosides [Australian].

*Editorial Note: While bisoprolol is commonly used for hypertension and angina globally, the provided source document is specific to the treatment of chronic heart failure.*

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Treatment must be initiated in patients who are stable (without acute failure) and under the supervision of a physician experienced in managing chronic heart failure. 

### Titration Schedule
A gradual up-titration is mandatory to ensure tolerability [Australian]:
- **Week 1:** 1.25 mg once daily.
- **Week 2:** 2.5 mg once daily.
- **Week 3:** 3.75 mg once daily.
- **Weeks 4–7:** 5 mg once daily.
- **Weeks 8–11:** 7.5 mg once daily.
- **Maintenance:** 10 mg once daily (Maximum recommended dose).

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets should be taken in the morning.
- May be taken with or without food.
- Tablets must be swallowed whole with liquid and not chewed.

### Treatment Modification
If the maximum dose is not tolerated, gradual dose reduction should be considered. In cases of transient worsening of heart failure, hypotension, or bradycardia, clinicians should reconsider the dosages of concomitant medications or consider a temporary dose reduction/discontinuation of bisoprolol.

### Missed Dose Instructions
Not specified in available source.

## 4. Contraindications
Bisoprolol is contraindicated in patients with [Australian]:
- Acute heart failure or episodes of heart failure decompensation requiring IV inotropic therapy.
- Cardiogenic shock.
- Second or third-degree AV block (in patients without a functional pacemaker).
- Sick sinus syndrome or sinoatrial block.
- Bradycardia (heart rate <60 beats/min) prior to initiating therapy.
- Hypotension (systolic blood pressure <100 mmHg).
- Severe bronchial asthma or severe chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD).
- Late stages of peripheral arterial occlusive disease or Raynaud's syndrome.
- Untreated phaeochromocytoma (must only be administered after $\alpha$-receptor blockade).
- Metabolic acidosis.
- Hypersensitivity to bisoprolol or any tablet excipients.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Abrupt Withdrawal:** Beta-blocker therapy should not be discontinued abruptly, particularly in patients with ischaemic heart disease. Sudden cessation can lead to severe exacerbation of angina, myocardial infarction, or ventricular arrhythmias. Withdrawal should be gradual (8–14 days).
- **Monitoring:** Regular monitoring of vital signs (heart rate, blood pressure) and symptoms of worsening heart failure is required, especially during the titration phase.
- **General Anaesthesia:** The anaesthetist must be informed of $\beta$-blockade due to the risk of interactions (e.g., severe bradyarrhythmias, hypotension). Continued maintenance is generally recommended peri-operatively.
- **Respiratory Disease:** While $\beta_1$-selective, bisoprolol should be used with caution in patients with airway obstruction. Concomitant bronchodilator therapy ($\beta_2$-stimulants) may be required.
- **Diabetes Mellitus:** Bisoprolol may mask premonitory symptoms of acute hypoglycaemia (especially tachycardia).
- **Thyrotoxicosis:** Adrenergic symptoms of hyperthyroidism may be masked.
- **Psoriasis:** Use only after careful benefit-risk assessment, as $\beta$-blockers may provoke or worsen psoriasis.
- **Allergic Reactions:** Sensitivity to allergens and the severity of anaphylactic reactions may be increased. Adrenaline (epinephrine) response may be attenuated.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequencies are based on the CIBIS II trial and post-marketing data [Australian].

| Frequency | System Organ Class / Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (>10%) | Bradycardia. |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Dizziness, headache, worsening of heart failure, feeling of coldness or numbness in extremities, hypotension, gastrointestinal complaints (nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation), asthenia, fatigue. |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | AV-conduction disturbances, bronchospasm (in patients with asthma/obstructive disease), muscular weakness, cramps, orthostatic hypotension, sleep disorders, depression. |
| **Rare** (<0.1%) | Increased triglycerides, increased liver enzymes (ALT, AST), hepatitis, reduced tear flow (caution with contact lenses), hearing disorders, allergic rhinitis, hypersensitivity reactions (itching, flush, rash, angioedema), potency disorders, nightmares, hallucinations, syncope. |
| **Very Rare** (<0.01%) | Conjunctivitis, alopecia, provoke or worsen psoriasis. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Combinations Not Recommended
- **Calcium Antagonists (Verapamil/Diltiazem type):** Risk of profound hypotension, AV block, and negative inotropy.
- **Class I Antiarrhythmics:** May potentiate effects on AV conduction time and negative inotropy.
- **Centrally Acting Antihypertensives (e.g., Clonidine):** May worsen heart failure; abrupt withdrawal increases risk of rebound hypertension.

### Combinations to be Used with Caution
- **Dihydropyridine Calcium Antagonists (e.g., Amlodipine):** Increased risk of hypotension and further deterioration of pump function.
- **Class III Antiarrhythmics (e.g., Amiodarone):** Potentiation of AV conduction effects.
- **Insulin and Oral Antidiabetics:** Intensification of hypoglycaemic effect; masking of symptoms.
- **Digitalis Glycosides:** Reduction of heart rate; increased AV conduction time.
- **NSAIDs:** May reduce the hypotensive effect of bisoprolol.
- **$\beta$-Sympathomimetics (e.g., Isoprenaline):** Mutual reduction of effect.
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** May increase bisoprolol plasma concentrations by up to a factor of 2.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category C.** $\beta$-blockers may reduce placental perfusion, leading to growth retardation, intrauterine death, or early labour. Bisoprolol should not be used unless clearly necessary. If used, the newborn must be monitored for hypoglycaemia and bradycardia (usually occurring within the first 3 days).
- **Breastfeeding:** Excretion in human milk is unknown. Safety in infants has not been established. Breastfeeding is **not recommended** [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No effect on fertility was observed in rat studies at doses up to 150 mg/kg/day.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No paediatric experience exists; use is **not recommended** for children [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment is generally required based on age. However, caution is advised in patients >80 years due to limited clinical data.
- **Renal Impairment:** Clearance is balanced between the liver and kidney. In patients with a creatinine clearance of approximately 28 mL/min, no dose adjustment is normally required up to 10 mg/day. Caution is advised in heart failure patients with significant renal impairment.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No clinically relevant pharmacokinetic differences were found between patients with normal vs. impaired hepatic function. However, dose reduction may be required in heart failure patients with concomitant liver disease.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Highly $\beta_1$-selective adrenoceptor blocker. At therapeutic doses, it lacks significant $\beta_2$-receptor activity, thus having minimal effect on airway resistance and metabolic regulation.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Reduces heart rate (negative chronotropy) and cardiac output. Increases peripheral and pulmonary arterial resistance in the short term due to reflex autonomic changes.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** >90% absorbed; bioavailability ~85-90% due to low first-pass metabolism (10-15%). Not affected by food.
    - **Distribution:** Extensive; volume of distribution 3.5 L/kg. Plasma protein binding ~35%.
    - **Metabolism:** "Balanced clearance" (50% renal, 50% hepatic). Primarily metabolised via **CYP3A4** (~95%), with a minor contribution from **CYP2D6**.
    - **Elimination:** Half-life of 10–12 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **CIBIS II Trial:** A pivotal study involving 2,647 patients with NYHA Class III or IV heart failure (Ejection Fraction $\le$35%).
- **Outcomes:** Bisoprolol reduced total mortality from 17.3% to 11.8% (relative risk reduction of 34%). Sudden death was reduced by 44%. There was a 36% relative reduction in heart failure episodes requiring hospitalisation.
