# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising available clinical data into a concise, structured format.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not explicitly stated in source, provided for clinical context).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing maintenance haemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Administration:** Delivered as a slow 2- to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Administered every 3 to 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** Highest recorded dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Haemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Slow 2- to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].
- **Monitoring:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Contraindications:** None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal oedema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, treatment must be discontinued immediately [US].
- **Neurologic Effects (Seizures):** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** Chronic administration of high oral doses in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites: trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5% in ESRD clinical trials)
Adverse events reported regardless of causality in ESRD patients include:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (5–21%), chest pain (6–15%), fever (5–12%), flu syndrome (15–29%), headache (3–37%), infection (10–24%), injection site reaction (27–38%), pain (21–35%) [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%) [US].
- **Digestive:** Diarrhoea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%) [US].
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%) [US].
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%) [US].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity [US].
- Seizures [US].

### Other Effects
- Transient nausea, vomiting, gastritis, and body odour have been observed [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalised Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Clinical Management:** Monitor INR levels at therapy initiation and following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned under current labeling]. Reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. Risks of excess intake in the infant should be weighed against maternal benefits [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg); safety and efficacy in children are consistent with adult profiles [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the source document; general dosing principles apply.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD. Use with caution regarding the accumulation of metabolites (TMA/TMAO) associated with high-dose oral therapy in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation and energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses post-dialysis significantly increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations from a baseline of ~20 µmol/L (pre-dialysis) to trough levels between 54 and 180 µmol/L after three doses [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg three times weekly showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s when stored in PVC bags at room temperature (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
