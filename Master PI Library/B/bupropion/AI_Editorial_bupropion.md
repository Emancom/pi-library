# Editorial Product Information: bupropion

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **bupropion**
- Chemical class: aminoketone antidepressant
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 1985 (as stated in available sources).
- Date of last revision/update of source PI provided: Revised 11/2025 (as stated in available sources).
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.
- Other jurisdictional registration dates: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of major depressive disorder (MDD) in adults.
  - Efficacy was established across two 4-week controlled inpatient trials and one 6-week controlled outpatient trial in adult subjects with MDD. Maintenance of antidepressant response for up to 44 weeks following 8 weeks of acute treatment was also demonstrated in a placebo-controlled trial (see Clinical Trials).
- Other indications (including for smoking cessation) are not specified as approved indications in the available source PI. Where the sources discuss other clinical contexts (e.g., neuropsychiatric events during smoking cessation), those appear as safety information rather than approved indications.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Initiate at 150 mg once daily in the morning.
- After 3 days, the dose may be increased to the target dose of 300 mg/day, given as 150 mg twice daily with an interval of at least 8 hours between doses.
- Maximum recommended daily dose: 400 mg/day (200 mg twice daily), considered only for patients in whom no clinical improvement is noted after several weeks at 300 mg/day.
- Do not exceed 200 mg in any single dose, to avoid high peak concentrations of bupropion and/or its metabolites.

### Dosing by special conditions / organ impairment
- Hepatic impairment:
  - Moderate to severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh score 7–15): maximum dose 100 mg/day or 150 mg every other day.
  - Mild hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh score 5–6): consider reducing dose and/or dosing frequency.
- Renal impairment:
  - Consider reducing dose and/or dosing frequency in patients with renal impairment (GFR < 90 mL/min).

### Administration instructions
- Swallow tablets whole; do not crush, divide, or chew.
- May be taken with or without food.
- Increase dose gradually to reduce seizure risk.
- Periodically reassess dose and need for maintenance treatment.

### Titration schedules
- Start at 150 mg once daily for 3 days, then increase to 150 mg twice daily (300 mg/day) if tolerated. Escalate to a maximum of 200 mg twice daily (400 mg/day) only if clinical response is inadequate after several weeks at 300 mg/day and tolerability allows.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
List includes all contraindications stated in the available source PI:
- History of seizure disorder.
- Current or prior diagnosis of bulimia nervosa or anorexia nervosa.
- Patients in whom alcohol, benzodiazepines, barbiturates, or antiepileptic drugs are being abruptly discontinued, owing to the associated increase in seizure risk.
- Concurrent or recent use of monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs) intended to treat psychiatric disorders: bupropion must not be used with such MAOIs or started within 14 days of stopping them. Likewise, an MAOI intended to treat psychiatric disorders must not be started within 14 days of stopping bupropion.
- Concurrent treatment with linezolid or intravenous methylene blue (reversible MAOIs): bupropion must not be started in patients receiving these agents.
- Known hypersensitivity to bupropion or any ingredient of the formulation.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warning
- **Suicidal thoughts and behaviors:** Antidepressants increased the risk of suicidal thinking and behavior in children, adolescents, and young adults in short-term trials. In patients of all ages who are started on antidepressant therapy, monitor closely for worsening and for the emergence of suicidal thoughts and behaviors. Families and caregivers should be advised of the need for close observation and communication with the prescriber.
  - Note from source: short-term trials did not show an increased risk in subjects over age 24; a reduction in risk was observed in subjects aged 65 and older.

### General warnings and precautions
- **Neuropsychiatric adverse events during smoking cessation:** Postmarketing reports have included mood changes (including depression and mania), psychosis, hallucinations, paranoia, delusions, homicidal ideation, aggression, hostility, agitation, anxiety, panic, suicidal ideation, suicide attempt, and completed suicide. Patients attempting to quit smoking with bupropion should be observed for the emergence of such symptoms and instructed to stop the drug and contact a healthcare provider if these reactions occur.
- **Seizure risk:** The risk is dose-related. Minimize risk through gradual dose escalation and by not exceeding the maximum recommended daily dose (400 mg/day total; 200 mg in any single dose). Bupropion must be discontinued if a seizure occurs.
- **Hypertension:** Bupropion can increase blood pressure. Blood pressure should be monitored before initiating treatment and periodically throughout treatment.
- **Activation of mania/hypomania:** Screen patients for bipolar disorder prior to initiating treatment and monitor for signs of mania or hypomania during therapy.
- **Psychosis and other serious neuropsychiatric reactions:** Instruct patients to contact a healthcare professional if such reactions occur.
- **Angle-closure glaucoma:** Antidepressant use has been associated with angle-closure glaucoma in patients with anatomically narrow angles. Appropriate evaluation and management are advised.
- **Hypersensitivity reactions:** Clinically significant hypersensitivity reactions have been reported (see Contraindications).

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor for emergence or worsening of suicidal thoughts or behavior, particularly in children, adolescents, and young adults.
- Monitor blood pressure prior to starting bupropion and periodically during therapy.
- Monitor for neuropsychiatric symptoms in patients using bupropion during smoking cessation attempts.
- Discontinue bupropion if a seizure occurs.
- Monitor digoxin levels if bupropion is coadministered with digoxin (see Drug Interactions).
- If urgent treatment with linezolid or intravenous methylene blue is required in a patient receiving bupropion, stop bupropion promptly and monitor the patient for 2 weeks or until 24 hours after the last dose of linezolid or intravenous methylene blue, whichever comes first. Bupropion may be resumed 24 hours after the last dose of linezolid or intravenous methylene blue.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Concomitant use with drugs that lower the seizure threshold requires caution and careful dose selection.
- Concomitant use with dopaminergic drugs (levodopa, amantadine) can produce CNS toxicity.
- Coadministration with CYP2B6 inducers may reduce bupropion exposure; dose adjustment of bupropion based on clinical response may be necessary, but the maximum recommended dose must not be exceeded.
- Bupropion inhibits CYP2D6 and can increase exposure of CYP2D6 substrates when coadministered; dose reductions of the concomitant drug may be necessary.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- **CYP2B6 inducers** (e.g., ritonavir, lopinavir, efavirenz, carbamazepine, phenobarbital, phenytoin): these agents may reduce bupropion exposure. A dose increase of bupropion based on clinical response may be required, but the total daily dose must not exceed the maximum recommended amount.
- **Bupropion as a CYP2D6 inhibitor:** bupropion inhibits CYP2D6 and can increase plasma concentrations of drugs metabolized by this enzyme, including certain antidepressants (venlafaxine, nortriptyline, imipramine, desipramine, paroxetine, fluoxetine, sertraline), antipsychotics (haloperidol, risperidone, thioridazine), beta-blockers (metoprolol), and Class 1C antiarrhythmics (propafenone, flecainide). Consider dose reduction of the concomitant CYP2D6 substrate when used with bupropion.
- **Digoxin:** bupropion may decrease plasma digoxin levels; digoxin levels and clinical response should be monitored.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- **Drugs that lower the seizure threshold:** concomitant use increases seizure risk. Use bupropion with caution in patients receiving such agents.
- **Dopaminergic drugs (levodopa, amantadine):** concomitant use can lead to CNS toxicity; exercise caution.
- **Monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs):** increased risk of hypertensive reactions. MAOIs intended to treat psychiatric disorders must not be used with bupropion or started within 14 days of stopping bupropion. Bupropion must not be started within 14 days of stopping an MAOI intended to treat psychiatric disorders.
  - Bupropion must not be started in a patient being treated with linezolid or intravenous methylene blue. If urgent treatment with linezolid or intravenous methylene blue is required in a patient already receiving bupropion, bupropion should be stopped promptly. The patient should be monitored for 2 weeks or until 24 hours after the last dose of linezolid or intravenous methylene blue, whichever comes first. Bupropion may be resumed 24 hours after the last dose of these agents. The interaction risk associated with non-intravenous routes or very low doses of methylene blue is unclear; clinicians should remain aware of the possibility of an interaction with such use.

### Drug–laboratory test interactions
- Bupropion may produce false-positive results on urine screening tests for amphetamines.

### Management notes
- Adjust bupropion dose when coadministered with CYP2B6 inducers based on clinical response; the maximum recommended daily dose must not be exceeded.
- Consider dose reduction of concomitant CYP2D6 substrates when clinically indicated.
- Monitor blood pressure before initiating bupropion and periodically during treatment.
- Exercise caution and monitor for seizure activity when bupropion is administered with drugs that lower the seizure threshold.
