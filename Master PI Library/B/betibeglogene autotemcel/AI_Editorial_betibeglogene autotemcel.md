# Editorial Product Information: betibeglogene autotemcel

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active ingredient (INN/generic name):** betibeglogene autotemcel
- **Chemical class:** Gene therapy product; autologous CD34+ haematopoietic stem
  cell (HSC)-enriched cell population transduced ex vivo with a lentiviral vector
  encoding the βA-T87Q-globin gene
- **ATC code:** Not assigned at time of approval
- **Route(s) of administration:** Intravenous infusion (single dose)

## 2. Regulatory History
- **FDA (United States) approval date:** 17 August 2022
- **Date of last revision/update (FDA PI):** Refer to current FDA label for most
  recent revision date
- **Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history:** Not specified in available
  sources at time of preparation

## 3. Approved Indications
- **FDA (United States):** betibeglogene autotemcel is indicated for the treatment
  of adult and paediatric patients 12 years of age and older with
  β-thalassemia who require regular red blood cell (RBC) transfusions.

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General dosing
- betibeglogene autotemcel is a one-time, single-dose gene therapy.
- The minimum recommended dose is ≥5.0 × 10⁶ CD34+ cells/kg body weight.
- The target dose is ≥8.0 × 10⁶ CD34+ cells/kg.
- The product is patient-specific (autologous) and must not be administered to
  any patient other than the patient for whom it was manufactured.

### Administration instructions
- Administer by intravenous infusion following myeloablative conditioning with
  busulfan.
- Confirm the patient's identity before administration.
- Pre-medicate as directed per institutional protocols prior to infusion.
- Administer through a central venous catheter.
- Do not use a leukocyte-depleting filter.
- Infuse the entire contents of each bag; multiple bags may be required.

### Prior conditioning regimen
- Myeloablative conditioning with busulfan is required before infusion of
  betibeglogene autotemcel. Busulfan dosing and monitoring (e.g., therapeutic
  drug monitoring) should follow current institutional practices and the busulfan
  prescribing information.

### Titration schedules
- Not applicable — betibeglogene autotemcel is a single administration.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not applicable — betibeglogene autotemcel is a single administration.

## 5. Contraindications
- No contraindications are listed in the FDA prescribing information.
- Note: Patients with certain conditions may not be eligible candidates; refer
  to full prescribing information and the REMS program criteria.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed warnings
1. **Hematologic malignancy:** Cases of hematologic malignancy, including acute
   myeloid leukaemia (AML) and myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS), have been
   reported in patients treated with betibeglogene autotemcel. Insertional
   oncogenesis is a potential risk of all lentiviral gene therapy products.
   Monitor patients for hematologic malignancy with complete blood counts and,
   if clinically indicated, bone marrow assessments for at least 15 years after
   treatment through the long-term follow-up programme.

2. **Insertional oncogenesis:** Integration of the lentiviral vector into the
   genome creates the potential for insertional oncogenesis. Patients must be
   enrolled in a long-term follow-up study to monitor for delayed adverse events,
   including genotoxicity and insertional oncogenesis.

### General warnings and precautions

- **Veno-occlusive disease (VOD)/Sinusoidal obstruction syndrome (SOS):**
  VOD/SOS has been reported in patients who received myeloablative conditioning
  prior to treatment with betibeglogene autotemcel. VOD/SOS can be severe and
  fatal. Monitor patients for signs and symptoms of VOD/SOS following
  conditioning. Refer to the busulfan prescribing information for additional
  information.

- **Delayed or failed engraftment:** Patients who do not achieve adequate
  engraftment may require subsequent haematopoietic stem cell transplantation
  from a donor (allogeneic transplant). Adequate stem cell collection should be
  confirmed prior to myeloablative conditioning. A back-up autologous cell
  collection should be cryopreserved prior to treatment.

- **Infusion-related reactions:** Hypersensitivity reactions, including
  anaphylaxis, are possible. Appropriate medical support measures should be
  available during administration.

- **REMS programme:** betibeglogene autotemcel is available only through a
  restricted programme under a Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy (REMS)
  due to the risk of hematologic malignancy.

- **Antiretroviral therapy:** Certain antiretroviral agents may inhibit
  lentiviral transduction during the manufacturing process. Patients receiving
  antiretroviral therapy should discontinue such therapy before and during the
  cell collection period, as directed.

### Monitoring requirements
- Complete blood counts: regularly post-infusion to monitor engraftment and
  haematopoietic recovery.
- Haemoglobin and HbAT87Q levels: to assess therapeutic response.
- Liver function tests: monitor for VOD/SOS post-conditioning.
- Long-term follow-up for insertional oncogenesis and haematologic malignancy:
  minimum 15 years post-infusion.
- Bone marrow assessments: if clinically indicated for malignancy surveillance.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Back-up autologous haematopoietic stem cells must be collected and
  cryopreserved prior to myeloablative conditioning as a contingency in case
  of engraftment failure.
- Iron overload management should be continued as clinically appropriate
  post-infusion.

## 8. Drug Interactions

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Not applicable in the traditional pharmacokinetic sense; betibeglogene
  autotemcel is a cell-based gene therapy and does not undergo conventional
  absorption, distribution, metabolism, or elimination.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- **Busulfan (conditioning agent):** Myeloablative conditioning with busulfan
  is required for engraftment. Busulfan toxicity (including VOD/SOS) is additive
  with the preparative regimen. Therapeutic drug monitoring of busulfan is
  recommended.

### Clinically significant interactions
- **Antiretroviral agents:** Antiretroviral drugs (e.g., HIV antiretrovirals) may
  interfere with lentiviral vector transduction during the ex vivo manufacturing
  process. Patients on antiretroviral therapy must discontinue such treatment
  prior to haematopoietic stem cell collection. Management: discontinue
  antiretroviral therapy before collection; resume after infusion as clinically
  appropriate in consultation with an infectious disease specialist.
- **Hydroxyurea:** Patients receiving hydroxyurea for β-thalassemia management
  should follow washout guidance prior to stem cell mobilisation. Refer to full
  prescribing information for guidance.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

- **Pregnancy category/system:** No FDA pregnancy category assigned (post-2015
  labelling format); pregnancy subsection within labelling.
- **Pregnancy safety:** There are no adequate and well-controlled studies of
  betibeglogene autotemcel in pregnant women. It is not known whether the drug
  can cause fetal harm when administered to a pregnant woman. Females of
  reproductive potential should use effective contraception before undergoing
  the myeloablative conditioning regimen and during the period recommended in
  the busulfan prescribing information.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is not known whether betibeglogene autotemcel or its
  components are present in human milk. The effects on the breastfed infant or
  on milk production are unknown. Breastfeeding is not recommended during and
  after treatment, in line with guidance in the conditioning regimen prescribing
  information.
- **Fertility:** Myeloablative conditioning with busulfan may impair fertility.
  Males and females of reproductive potential should be counselled regarding
  options for fertility preservation prior to treatment.

## 10. Use in Special Populations

- **Paediatric use:** betibeglogene autotemcel is approved by the FDA for
  patients 12 years of age and older. Safety and efficacy in patients younger
  than 12 years have not been established.
- **Elderly use:** Clinical studies of betibeglogene autotemcel did not include
  sufficient numbers of patients aged 65 years and older to determine whether
  they respond differently from younger patients. Use in elderly patients should
  be approached with caution given the tolerability of myeloablative
  conditioning.
- **Renal impairment:** No specific dose adjustment recommendations are provided
  in the FDA PI. Renal function should be considered when planning the
  conditioning regimen.
- **Hepatic impairment:** No specific dose adjustment recommendations are
  provided in the FDA PI. Hepatic function should be assessed prior to
  conditioning, given the risk of VOD/SOS.

## 11. Pharmacology

### Mechanism of action
betibeglogene autotemcel is an autologous gene therapy consisting of
haematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (HSPCs) derived from the patient's
own CD34+ cells, which are transduced ex vivo using a replication-incompetent
lentiviral vector (BB305) encoding the βA-T87Q-globin gene. After infusion
into the patient following myeloablative conditioning, the transduced cells
engraft in the bone marrow and produce red blood cells expressing
βA-T87Q-globin, leading to the production of functional haemoglobin
(HbAT87Q). HbAT87Q has anti-sickling properties due to the T87Q amino acid
substitution, contributes to total haemoglobin production, and reduces or
eliminates the need for chronic RBC transfusions in patients with
β-thalassemia.

### Pharmacokinetics

- **Absorption:** Not applicable — betibeglogene autotemcel is administered
  directly as an intravenous infusion of transduced cells.
- **Distribution:** Transduced CD34+ cells engraft in the bone marrow following
  myeloablative conditioning and differentiate into haematopoietic lineages.
- **Metabolism:** Not applicable in the conventional pharmacokinetic sense.
  The integrated βA-T87Q-globin gene is transcribed and translated as part of
  normal cellular processes.
- **Elimination:** Not applicable. The engrafted cells are expected to persist
  long-term; the duration of gene expression is intended to be lifelong.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Following successful engraftment, production of HbAT87Q-containing
  haemoglobin increases over weeks to months post-infusion.
- Patients achieving transfusion independence demonstrate total haemoglobin
  levels ≥9 g/dL, largely comprising HbAT87Q.
- The T87Q substitution in the β-globin chain confers anti-polymerisation
  (anti-sickling) properties and allows differentiation of therapeutically
  produced HbAT87Q from native haemoglobin by high-performance liquid
  chromatography (HPLC).

## 12. Clinical Trials

### Key pivotal trials

#### HGB-207 (CLIMB THAL-111)
- **Design:** Phase 3, single-arm, open-label, multicentre study
- **Population:** Patients aged ≥12 years with transfusion-dependent
  β-thalassemia (TDT) and a non-β0/β0 genotype
- **Primary endpoint:** Transfusion independence (TI), defined as weighted
  average haemoglobin ≥9 g/dL without RBC transfusion for ≥12 consecutive
  months
- **Key results:** A substantial proportion of patients achieved transfusion
  independence; refer to full FDA prescribing information for exact response
  rates and confidence intervals.

#### HGB-212 (CLIMB THAL-112)
- **Design:** Phase 3, single-arm, open-label, multicentre study
- **Population:** Patients aged ≥12 years with TDT and a β0/β0 or
  β0/IVS-I-110 genotype (severe genotypes)
- **Primary endpoint:** Transfusion independence as defined above
- **Key results:** A proportion of patients with severe genotypes achieved
  transfusion independence; refer to full FDA prescribing information for
  exact rates and confidence intervals.

### General outcomes across trials
- Patients not achieving full transfusion independence demonstrated reductions
  in annualised RBC transfusion volume.
- HbAT87Q levels rose progressively over the first 12–24 months post-infusion.
- Long-term follow-up studies are ongoing to assess durability of response and
  safety (including insertional oncogenesis surveillance).
