# Editorial Product Information: Binimetinib

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically focused overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and must not be used as a substitute for the official, current Product Information in the jurisdiction where a clinician is practicing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Binimetinib (as an ATP-uncompetitive, reversible inhibitor of MEK1 and MEK2).
- **Chemical Class:** Antineoplastic agent; protein kinase inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** **MEKTOVI** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01EE03.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
  - Film-coated tablets: 15 mg (yellow/dark yellow, ovaloid, debossed with "A" and "15") [Australian].
  - Film-coated tablets: 45 mg (white/off-white, ovaloid, debossed with "45") [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pierre Fabre Australia Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Unresectable or Metastatic Melanoma:** Indicated in combination with encorafenib for adult patients with a BRAF V600E or V600K mutation, confirmed by a validated test [Australian].
- **Metastatic Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC):** Indicated in combination with encorafenib for adult patients with a BRAF V600E mutation, confirmed by a validated test [Australian].

*Note: Efficacy and safety have not been established in patients with wild-type BRAF melanoma or NSCLC [Australian].*

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dose:** 45 mg orally twice daily (total daily dose 90 mg), approximately 12 hours apart [Australian].
- **Administration:** May be taken with or without food. Tablets should be swallowed whole. 
- **Alternative Administration:** For patients with swallowing difficulties, the 15 mg tablets may be dispersed in 10 mL of water, orange juice, or apple juice and consumed immediately [Australian].
- **Dose Modification:** 
  - The recommended reduced dose is 30 mg twice daily.
  - If a patient cannot tolerate 30 mg twice daily, binimetinib should be discontinued [Australian].
  - **Dose Re-escalation:** May be considered if adverse reactions are managed, except if the reduction was due to left ventricular dysfunction (LVD) or any Grade 4 toxicity [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** If the next dose is due in less than 6 hours, skip the missed dose.
- **Vomiting:** Do not take an additional dose if vomiting occurs after administration; proceed with the next scheduled dose [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to binimetinib or any excipients (including lactose) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Left Ventricular Dysfunction (LVD):** Defined as symptomatic or asymptomatic decreases in ejection fraction. LVEF should be assessed before initiation, at 1 month, and every 3 months thereafter. Discontinue if LVEF is <50%, or if there is a ≥10% absolute decrease from baseline [Australian].
- **Ocular Toxicities:**
  - **Retinal Pigment Epithelial Detachment (RPED):** May cause symptoms like blurred vision or loss of vision. If symptomatic RPED (Grade 2/3) occurs, withhold treatment for up to 2 weeks.
  - **Retinal Vein Occlusion (RVO):** Binimetinib is not recommended in patients with a history of RVO. If RVO occurs during treatment, permanently discontinue [Australian].
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Liver laboratory abnormalities (AST/ALT) can occur. Monitor LFTs before initiation, monthly for the first 6 months, and then as clinically indicated [Australian].
- **Creatine Phosphokinase (CK) Elevation/Rhabdomyolysis:** Monitor CK and creatinine levels monthly for the first 6 months. Ensure adequate hydration [Australian].
- **Venous Thromboembolism (VTE):** Use with caution in patients at risk of VTE/PE. Manage with dose reduction or interruption [Australian].
- **Pneumonitis/Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD):** Withhold treatment if ILD is suspected. Permanently discontinue if ILD/pneumonitis is confirmed [Australian].
- **Haemorrhage:** Increased risk of major haemorrhagic events, particularly when used with anticoagulants or antiplatelet therapy [Australian].
- **New Primary Malignancies:** Cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma (cuSCC) and new primary melanomas have been observed. Perform dermatologic evaluations every 2 months during and for 6 months after treatment [Australian].
- **Tumour Lysis Syndrome (TLS):** May be fatal. Monitor patients with high tumour burden or renal insufficiency closely [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequencies reported in combination with encorafenib [Australian]:

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Fatigue, nausea, diarrhoea, vomiting, abdominal pain, arthralgia, myalgia, anaemia, headache, dizziness, peripheral neuropathy, hypertension, visual impairment, RPED, rash, constipation, hyperkeratosis, pyrexia, peripheral oedema, blood CK increased. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Left ventricular dysfunction, VTE, haemorrhage, uveitis, renal failure, photosensitivity, pruritus, dysgeusia, colitis, pancreatitis. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Rhabdomyolysis, facial paresis. |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Major haemorrhage, LVD, ILD, VTE, and rhabdomyolysis [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Metabolism:** Binimetinib is primarily metabolised via UGT1A1 and UGT2B7-mediated glucuronidation. CYP1A2 and CYP2C19 play minor roles (<20%).
- **UGT Interactions:** Use caution with UGT1A1/2B7 inducers (e.g., rifampicin) or inhibitors (e.g., indinavir, atazanavir, diclofenac) [Australian]. 
- **Transporters:** Binimetinib is a substrate for P-glycoprotein (P-gp) and Breast Cancer Resistance Protein (BCRP). Inhibitors of these transporters may increase binimetinib concentrations [Australian].
- **Encorafenib:** No clinically significant change in binimetinib exposure occurs when co-administered with encorafenib despite its UGT1A1 inhibitory potential [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Animal studies show embryo-foetal toxicity, including ventricular septal defects and abortion. Should only be used if the benefit to the mother outweighs the risk to the foetus. 
- **Contraception:** Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during treatment and for 1 month after the last dose. Note: Encorafenib may reduce the efficacy of hormonal contraceptives; barrier methods are advised [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Discontinue breastfeeding during treatment. It is unknown if binimetinib is excreted in human milk [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No human data. No reproductive organ effects seen in animal studies, but clinical impact is uncertain [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients <18 years have not been established. No data available [Australian].
- **Elderly (≥65 years):** No dose adjustment required. In clinical trials, some adverse events (diarrhoea, pruritus) were more frequent in patients ≥65 years [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
  - **Mild (Child-Pugh A):** No dose adjustment.
  - **Moderate/Severe (Child-Pugh B/C):** Not recommended. Exposure increases up to 3-fold for unbound drug [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild to severe impairment. Binimetinib undergoes minimal renal elimination [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Binimetinib is an ATP-uncompetitive, reversible inhibitor of MEK1 and MEK2. It prevents the phosphorylation of ERK, thereby inhibiting cellular proliferation in BRAF-mutant cell lines [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Rapid; median Tmax 1.5 hours. At least 50% absorbed. High-fat meals decrease Cmax by 17% but do not affect AUC.
  - **Distribution:** 97.2% protein bound. Blood-to-plasma ratio is 0.718.
  - **Metabolism:** Primarily via glucuronidation.
  - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life 8.66 hours. Clearance 28.2 L/h. Excretion: 62.3% faeces; 31.4% urine [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **COLUMBUS Trial (Melanoma):** A Phase III trial comparing binimetinib + encorafenib (Combo 450) vs. vemurafenib vs. encorafenib monotherapy. Combo 450 showed a median PFS of 14.9 months vs. 7.3 months for vemurafenib (HR 0.54, p<0.001) [Australian].
- **PHAROS Trial (NSCLC):** A Phase II open-label study for BRAF V600E mutant NSCLC. IRR-assessed ORR was 75% in treatment-naïve patients and 46% in previously treated patients [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging.
- **Handling:** Any unused medicine should be disposed of via local pharmacy programs [Australian].
