# Editorial Product Information: Bendamustine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of the clinical and regulatory profile of bendamustine, synthesised for healthcare professionals.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Bendamustine hydrochloride (as monohydrate)
- **Chemical Class:** Alkylating antitumour agent (mechlorethamine derivative)
- **Brand Names:** BENDAMUSTINE JUNO [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Powder for injection, for intravenous infusion: 25 mg and 100 mg vials [Australian]
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Juno Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Lymphocytic Leukaemia (CLL):** First-line treatment for Binet stage B or C disease. Note: Comparative efficacy against therapies other than chlorambucil is not established [Australian-approved].
- **Non-Hodgkin’s Lymphoma (NHL):** 
    - Previously untreated indolent CD20-positive, stage III-IV NHL, in combination with rituximab [Australian-approved].
    - Relapsed/Refractory indolent NHL [Australian-approved].
- **Mantle Cell Lymphoma (MCL):** Previously untreated CD20-positive, stage III-IV MCL in combination with rituximab for patients ineligible for autologous stem cell transplantation [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Chronic Lymphocytic Leukaemia:** 100 mg/m² body surface area (BSA) on days 1 and 2, every 4 weeks for up to 6 cycles [Australian].
- **Refractory Indolent NHL (Monotherapy):** 120 mg/m² BSA on days 1 and 2, every 3 weeks for 6 to 8 cycles [Australian].
- **Indolent NHL and MCL (Combination with Rituximab):** 90 mg/m² BSA on days 1 and 2, every 4 weeks for up to 6 cycles [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Administer via IV infusion over 30–60 minutes.
- Reconstitution: 25 mg vial with 10 mL Water for Injection; 100 mg vial with 40 mL Water for Injection (final concentration 2.5 mg/mL).
- Dilution: Dilute immediately with 0.9% NaCl to a final volume of approximately 500 mL.
- Treatment should not be initiated if leukocytes < 3 x 10⁹/L or platelets < 75 x 10⁹/L. 

### Dose Modifications
- **Haematological Toxicity:** Delay treatment if leukocyte or platelet counts fall below thresholds. Resume once leukocytes > 4 x 10⁹/L and platelets > 100 x 10⁹/L.
- **Non-haematological Toxicity:** 50% dose reduction for Grade 3 toxicity. Interrupt treatment for Grade 4 toxicity.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to bendamustine or mannitol [Australian].
- Severe hepatic impairment (serum bilirubin > 3.0 mg/dL) [Australian].
- Jaundice [Australian].
- Severe bone marrow suppression and severe blood count alterations (leukocytes < 3 x 10⁹/L or platelets < 75 x 10⁹/L) [Australian].
- Major surgery within 30 days of treatment initiation [Australian].
- Infections, particularly those involving leukocytopenia [Australian].
- Yellow fever vaccination [Australian].
- Breastfeeding [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Myelosuppression:** Bone marrow failure is common. Monitor leukocytes, platelets, haemoglobin, and neutrophils at least weekly. Prolonged lymphocytopenia and CD4+ T-cell depletion may occur, increasing susceptibility to infections.
- **Infections:** Serious and fatal infections (sepsis, pneumonia, PJP, VZV, CMV) have been reported. Progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML) has been reported, especially in combination with rituximab or obinutuzumab.
- **Hepatitis B Reactivation:** Carriers must be monitored for reactivation. Consult a liver expert for patients with prior HBV infection.
- **Skin Reactions:** Rash and severe reactions (SJS, TEN, DRESS) have occurred, sometimes fatal. Risk may increase with concomitant allopurinol. Discontinue if reactions are progressive.
- **Tumour Lysis Syndrome (TLS):** Onset typically within 48 hours. Ensure adequate hydration and monitor blood chemistry.
- **Cardiac Disorders:** Closely monitor potassium levels; supplement if K+ < 3.5 mEq/L. Perform ECGs. Fatal MI and cardiac failure have been reported.
- **Infusion Reactions/Anaphylaxis:** Symptoms are usually mild, but severe reactions occur. Consider premedication (antihistamines, corticosteroids) for subsequent cycles if reactions occur.
- **Extravasation:** Stop infusion immediately, aspirate, and cool the area.
- **Secondary Malignancies:** Increased risk of myelodysplastic syndrome and acute myeloid leukaemia.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Myelosuppression (most common Grade 3/4)
- Sepsis and opportunistic infections
- Stevens-Johnson Syndrome/Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis
- Myocardial infarction and cardiac failure
- Pulmonary fibrosis and pneumonitis

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP1A2 Inhibitors:** Potential to increase bendamustine levels (e.g., fluvoxamine, ciprofloxacin, aciclovir).
- **Myelosuppressive Agents:** Potentiation of bone marrow toxicity.
- **Immunosuppressants:** Concomitant use with cyclosporine or tacrolimus may cause excessive immunosuppression.
- **Live Vaccines:** Risk of fatal infection due to reduced antibody formation.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category D]. Bendamustine is embryo/feto-lethal and teratogenic. Do not use unless clearly necessary.
- **Contraception:** Women of childbearing potential and men must use effective contraception during and for 6 months after treatment [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. It is unknown if the drug enters breast milk.
- **Fertility:** Potential for impaired male fertility and irreversible infertility. Advice on sperm conservation is recommended prior to treatment.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established; no experience in children/adolescents.
- **Elderly Use:** No evidence that dose adjustments are required.
- **Renal Impairment:** No adjustment required for CrCl > 10 mL/min. Limited experience in severe impairment.
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - Mild (bilirubin < 1.2 mg/dL): No adjustment needed.
    - Moderate (bilirubin 1.2–3.0 mg/dL): 30% dose reduction recommended.
    - Severe (bilirubin > 3.0 mg/dL): Contraindicated.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bifunctional alkylating agent that cross-links DNA single and double strands, impairing DNA synthesis and repair. Effective against both dividing and quiescent cells.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Central volume of distribution is ~19.3 L. Protein binding is >95%.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hydrolysed to monohydroxy- and dihydroxy-bendamustine. Hepatic metabolism via CYP1A2 also occurs.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life (t½ß) is ~28.2 minutes. Total clearance is ~639 mL/min. Approximately 45% is recovered in urine and 25% in faeces.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study 02CLLIII (CLL):** Randomised trial comparing bendamustine (100 mg/m² IV) to chlorambucil (0.8 mg/kg oral). Bendamustine showed a significantly higher overall response rate (67.9% vs 30.6%) and longer progression-free survival (21.6 vs 8.3 months).
- **Study NHL1-2003 (Indolent NHL/MCL):** Bendamustine + rituximab (B-R) was compared to CHOP + rituximab (R-CHOP). B-R demonstrated significantly longer median PFS (69.5 vs 31.2 months) and a better safety profile (less alopecia and stomatitis).
- **Study SDX-105-03 (Refractory NHL):** Single-arm study in rituximab-refractory patients showing an overall response rate of 75% with a median duration of response of 40 weeks.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Vials:** Store below 25°C. Protect from light [Australian].
- **Reconstituted/Diluted Solution:** Use as soon as possible. If necessary, may be held for 3.5 hours at 25°C or 24 hours at 2°C–8°C [Australian].
