# Editorial Product Information: Bromfenac

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory information into a single, clinically focused reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Bromfenac sodium sesquihydrate (equivalent to bromfenac free acid).
- **Chemical Class:** Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID); phenylacetic acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Bromfenac Ophthalmic Solution [US].
- **ATC Code:** S01BC11.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Ophthalmic solution 0.07% (0.7 mg per 1 mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical ophthalmic.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Apotex Corp. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Postoperative Inflammation and Ocular Pain:** Indicated for the treatment of inflammation and the reduction of ocular pain following cataract surgery [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):**
    - Instill **one drop** into the affected eye(s) **once daily** [US].
- **Schedule:**
    - Initiation: 1 day prior to cataract surgery.
    - Day of surgery: Continue once-daily dosing.
    - Postoperative: Continue through the first 14 days following surgery [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - To prevent contamination, ensure the dropper tip does not touch the eye, eyelids, or any external surface.
    - If using other topical ophthalmic medications, administer them at least **5 minutes apart** [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None:** No contraindications are listed in the provided US documentation.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Sulfite Allergic Reactions:** Contains sodium sulfite. May cause allergic-type reactions, including anaphylaxis or life-threatening asthma, particularly in susceptible individuals or those with a history of asthma [US].
- **Slow or Delayed Healing:** Topical NSAIDs may slow or delay corneal healing. Concomitant use with topical corticosteroids significantly increases this risk [US].
- **Cross-Sensitivity:** Potential exists for cross-sensitivity to acetylsalicylic acid (aspirin), phenylacetic acid derivatives, and other NSAIDs. Exercise caution in patients with prior sensitivities to these agents [US].
- **Increased Bleeding Time:** Ocular NSAIDs may interfere with platelet aggregation and increase bleeding of ocular tissues (e.g., hyphemas) in conjunction with surgery. Use with caution in patients with known bleeding tendencies or those on anticoagulant therapy [US].
- **Keratitis and Corneal Reactions:**
    - Use may result in keratitis. Continued use in susceptible patients may lead to epithelial breakdown, corneal thinning, erosion, ulceration, or perforation, which may be sight-threatening [US].
    - High-risk patients include those with complicated ocular surgeries, corneal denervation, epithelial defects, diabetes mellitus, ocular surface disease (e.g., dry eye), rheumatoid arthritis, or repeat surgeries within a short period [US].
    - Discontinue immediately if corneal epithelial breakdown is evident.
- **Contact Lens Wear:** This product contains benzalkonium chloride, which may be absorbed by soft contact lenses. Remove lenses prior to instillation; they may be reinserted 10 minutes after administration [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions reported in clinical trials (frequency 3% to 8%) include:
- Anterior chamber inflammation
- Foreign body sensation
- Eye pain
- Photophobia
- Blurred vision

**Serious Adverse Effects:**
- Keratitis, corneal epithelial breakdown, corneal thinning, and corneal perforation (see Warnings and Precautions) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Topical Corticosteroids:** Concomitant use increases the risk of delayed corneal healing [US].
- **Other Ophthalmic Medications:** May be used with alpha-agonists, beta-blockers, carbonic anhydrase inhibitors, cycloplegics, and mydriatics, provided they are administered at least 5 minutes apart [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Systemic exposure is expected to be minimal; therefore, systemic drug-drug interactions are unlikely [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:**
    - **US Category:** Not specified as a letter grade (label follows current FDA Pregnancy and Lactation Labeling Rule).
    - **Risk Summary:** There are no adequate data on use in pregnant women. Systemic exposure is expected to be minimal after topical administration.
    - **Clinical Considerations:** Avoid use during late pregnancy (third trimester) due to the known risk of premature closure of the fetal ductus arteriosus by NSAIDs [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** No data available regarding the presence of bromfenac in human milk or effects on the breastfed infant. The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need and potential risk [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies (rats) showed no impairment of fertility at oral doses up to 0.9 mg/kg/day (males) and 0.3 mg/kg/day (females) [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in paediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness have been observed between patients aged 70 years and older and younger adults [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bromfenac is an NSAID with anti-inflammatory activity. It is thought to block prostaglandin synthesis by inhibiting cyclooxygenase (COX) 1 and 2 [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Systemic concentration is estimated to be below the limit of quantification (50 ng/mL) at steady-state following once-daily topical dosing [US].
    - **Distribution/Metabolism/Elimination:** Not specified for the topical route in the available source.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Prostaglandins are mediators of intraocular inflammation, causing vasodilation, increased vascular permeability, and disruption of the blood-aqueous humor barrier [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Design:** Two multi-center, randomized, double-masked, placebo-controlled studies.
- **Population:** Patients undergoing cataract surgery.
- **Endpoints:** The primary efficacy endpoint was the proportion of subjects with complete clearance of ocular inflammation (0 cells and no flare) by Day 15.
- **Outcomes:** Bromfenac 0.07% was superior to vehicle. In Study 1 and Study 2, approximately 45.5% of bromfenac-treated patients achieved clearance by Day 15 compared to 13–27% in the vehicle groups. Bromfenac also significantly increased the proportion of patients who were pain-free on Day 1 [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Keep the container tightly closed and avoid contamination of the tip.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription Only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
