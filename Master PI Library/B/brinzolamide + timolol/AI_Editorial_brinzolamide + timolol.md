# Editorial Product Information: brinzolamide + timolol

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **brinzolamide + timolol**
- Chemical class:
  - Brinzolamide: topical carbonic anhydrase inhibitor (sulfonamide derivative)
  - Timolol: non‑selective beta‑adrenergic receptor blocking agent
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Topical ophthalmic (eye drops)

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Reduction of elevated intraocular pressure (IOP) in patients with open‑angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension for whom monotherapy with either component (a topical carbonic anhydrase inhibitor or a topical beta‑blocker) provides insufficient IOP reduction.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General adult dosing
- One drop of **brinzolamide + timolol** in the conjunctival sac of the affected eye(s) twice daily.

### Dosing by indication
- Indication‑specific titration schemes or alternative dosing schedules: Not specified in available sources.

### Administration instructions
- Advise patients to shake the bottle well before use (suspension).
- After instillation, nasolacrimal occlusion or closing the eyelids for two minutes is recommended to reduce systemic absorption and potential systemic adverse effects and to increase local ocular activity.
- If more than one topical ophthalmic medicinal product is used, administer products at least 5 minutes apart.
- Remove soft contact lenses before instillation; wait at least 15 minutes after instillation before reinserting lenses (contains benzalkonium chloride which may discolour soft contact lenses).
- The dropper tip should not contact the eye or any surface to avoid contamination or injury.
- If substituting from another topical ocular antiglaucoma agent to **brinzolamide + timolol**, discontinue the prior agent and start **brinzolamide + timolol** the following day.
- Do not exceed one drop in the affected eye(s) twice daily.

### Missed dose
- If a dose is missed, continue with the next scheduled dose; do not double doses.

## 5. Contraindications
Include all contraindications reported:
- Hypersensitivity to **brinzolamide** (or other sulphonamides), **timolol**, or any excipient of the product.
- Reactive airway disease including bronchial asthma or a history of bronchial asthma.
- Severe chronic obstructive pulmonary disease.
- Hypersensitivity to other beta‑blockers.
- Sinus bradycardia.
- Second‑degree or third‑degree atrioventricular block.
- Overt cardiac failure.
- Cardiogenic shock.
- Sino‑atrial block.
- Sick sinus syndrome.
- Severe allergic rhinitis and bronchial hyperreactivity.
- Hyperchloraemic acidosis.
- Severe renal impairment (creatinine clearance < 30 mL/min).
- Not recommended for use in children below 18 years due to lack of data on safety and efficacy.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed / highlighted warnings
- FOR TOPICAL USE ONLY — not for injection or oral ingestion.
- Discontinue immediately at first sign of skin rash or any other sign of hypersensitivity.

### General warnings and precautions
- Systemic absorption: Both components can be systemically absorbed after topical ocular administration. Techniques to reduce systemic absorption (nasolacrimal occlusion, eyelid closure) are recommended.
- Sulphonamide hypersensitivity (brinzolamide): Hypersensitivity reactions seen with systemic sulphonamides, including Stevens‑Johnson syndrome (SJS) and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), can occur; advise patients of signs and stop therapy immediately if severe reactions occur.
- Acid‑base disturbances (brinzolamide): Carbonic anhydrase inhibition can cause metabolic/acid‑base disturbances; use with caution in patients at risk of renal impairment and in patients with conditions predisposing to metabolic acidosis.
- Concomitant oral carbonic anhydrase inhibitors: Concomitant administration of topical brinzolamide with an oral carbonic anhydrase inhibitor is not recommended due to potential additive systemic effects.
- Cardiovascular (timolol): Timolol may produce systemic beta‑blocking effects (bradycardia, hypotension, worsening heart failure); ensure cardiac failure is adequately controlled before initiation; monitor pulse rate and watch for signs of cardiac deterioration.
- Peripheral vascular disease: Use cautiously in patients with severe peripheral circulatory disturbances (e.g., severe Raynaud’s disease).
- Respiratory disease: Timolol may precipitate bronchospasm in patients with bronchial asthma or severe COPD; severe bronchospasm and fatal outcomes have been reported.
- Diabetes: Beta‑blockers may mask signs of hypoglycaemia and potentiate hypoglycaemic effects of antidiabetic agents; use with caution in patients with labile insulin‑dependent diabetes.
- Thyroid disease: Beta‑blockers may mask symptoms of hyperthyroidism.
- Myasthenia gravis / muscle weakness: Beta‑blockers may potentiate muscle weakness consistent with myasthenic symptoms.
- Anaphylaxis: Patients with history of atopy or severe anaphylactic reactions may be more reactive and may have an attenuated response to epinephrine while receiving beta‑blockers.
- Choroidal detachment: Reported with aqueous suppressant therapy (e.g., timolol) after filtration procedures.
- Surgical anaesthesia: Ophthalmic beta‑blockers may block systemic beta‑agonist effects; inform anaesthetist if patient is receiving timolol.
- Ocular considerations:
  - Not studied in narrow‑angle glaucoma; use not recommended in these patients.
  - Limited experience in pseudoexfoliative and pigmentary glaucoma — use caution and monitor IOP closely.
  - Potential effects on corneal endothelium have not been investigated in patients with compromised corneas (e.g., low endothelial cell count, corneal dystrophies, diabetes mellitus); carbonic anhydrase inhibitors can affect corneal hydration and may lead to corneal decompensation/edema.
  - Contains benzalkonium chloride: may cause ocular irritation and discolour soft contact lenses.
- Hepatic impairment: No studies performed; no dose adjustment specified in available sources.
- Renal impairment:
  - No clinical data for AZARGA in renal impairment; no dose adjustment required for mild to moderate impairment.
  - Contraindicated in severe renal impairment (creatinine clearance < 30 mL/min) and in patients with hyperchloraemic acidosis.
- Elderly: No dose modification recommended solely on the basis of age.
- Paediatric: Not recommended in children < 18 years due to lack of safety and efficacy data.
- Laboratory tests: Not specified in available sources.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor pulse and blood pressure periodically during therapy.
- Monitor for signs of respiratory compromise in patients with respiratory disease.
- Monitor renal function and acid‑base balance when clinically indicated (patients at risk of renal impairment or acid‑base disturbances).
- Monitor for hypersensitivity skin reactions and severe cutaneous adverse reactions (SJS/TEN).

## 7. Adverse Effects

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Additive cardiovascular and bradycardic effects:
  - Concomitant administration with oral calcium channel blockers, other beta‑adrenergic blocking agents, antiarrhythmics (including amiodarone), digitalis glycosides, or parasympathomimetics may increase risk of marked bradycardia and/or hypotension.
  - Co‑administration with another topical beta‑blocker or another topical carbonic anhydrase inhibitor is not recommended.
  - Beta‑blockade may potentiate hypertensive reactions to sudden clonidine withdrawal.
- Beta‑blocker specific cautions:
  - Beta‑adrenergic blockers can mask symptoms of hypoglycaemia and potentiate the hypoglycaemic effect of antidiabetic agents.
  - Beta‑blockers may reduce the effect of epinephrine used to treat anaphylaxis.
  - Concomitant use of ophthalmic beta‑blockers and systemic or ophthalmic epinephrine has been associated with occasional mydriasis.
- Respiratory interactions:
  - Timolol may provoke bronchospasm in susceptible individuals; co‑administration with other bronchoconstrictive agents requires caution.

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Brinzolamide:
  - Metabolised primarily by CYP3A4 and also by CYP2A6, CYP2B6, CYP2C8 and CYP2C9. CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, itraconazole, clotrimazole, ritonavir) are expected to inhibit brinzolamide metabolism; caution is advised if used concomitantly. However, renal elimination is the major route for brinzolamide and accumulation from CYP inhibition is considered unlikely.
  - Potential interactions with NSAIDs and salicylates should be considered (acid‑base/electrolyte effects).
- Timolol:
  - Potential for increased systemic beta‑blockade when given with CYP2D6 inhibitors (e.g., quinidine, cimetidine) has been reported.
  - Clinically significant pharmacokinetic interactions not specified beyond effect potentiation with CYP2D6 inhibitors.

### Management notes for clinically significant interactions
- Avoid co‑administration with other ocular beta‑blockers or topical carbonic anhydrase inhibitors.
- Monitor heart rate and blood pressure when combined with other drugs that can lower heart rate or blood pressure.
- In patients on oral carbonic anhydrase inhibitors, avoid co‑administration due to risk of additive systemic carbonic anhydrase inhibition.
- Use caution and monitor for increased systemic beta‑blockade when timolol is used with CYP2D6 inhibitors.
- Observe diabetic patients closely for hypoglycaemia and for masked symptoms when beta‑blockers are used.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Classification: Pregnancy Category C (Australian system reported).
- Brinzolamide:
  - No adequate human data following topical ocular use.
  - Animal studies with systemic administration showed developmental toxicity (rabbit: maternal toxicity and increase in fetal variations; rat: decreased fetal bodyweights at high doses attributed to maternal toxicity).
  - Potential risk to human fetus is unknown.
- Timolol:
  - Animal studies: not teratogenic in mice, rats, rabbits at certain doses; high systemic doses produced embryotoxicity in some animal studies.
  - Systemic beta‑blockers have been associated with intrauterine growth retardation in humans; neonatal signs of beta‑blockade (bradycardia, hypotension, respiratory distress, hypoglycaemia) have been observed when maternal beta‑blocker therapy continued until delivery.
  - Case reports: bradycardia and arrhythmia have been reported in a fetus of a woman treated with timolol eye drops.
- Recommendation: Use during pregnancy only if potential benefit justifies potential risk to the fetus. If treatment continues until delivery, monitor the neonate carefully for signs of beta‑blockade.

### Breastfeeding
- Brinzolamide:
  - It is not known whether brinzolamide is excreted in human milk following topical ophthalmic administration. Animal data show excretion into milk after oral dosing.
- Timolol:
  - Timolol is detectable in human milk following topical ocular administration.
- Recommendation: Because of potential for serious adverse reactions in breastfed infants from both brinzolamide and timolol, consider discontinuing breastfeeding or discontinuing the medicinal product, taking into account importance of therapy for the mother.

### Fertility
- Human data: Not available.
- Animal data: Oral brinzolamide (up to 18 mg/kg/day) and timolol (up to 100 mg/kg/day) in rats showed no adverse effects on male or female fertility.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Not recommended for use in children below 18 years due to lack of safety and efficacy data.

### Elderly use
- No dosage modification is required solely on the basis of age; use usual precautions and monitor comorbid conditions (cardiac, pulmonary).

### Renal impairment
- No studies conducted specifically with the combination product in renal impairment.
- No dosage adjustment specified for mild to moderate renal impairment.
- Contraindicated in severe renal impairment (creatinine clearance < 30 mL/min) and in patients with hyperchloraemic acidosis because brinzolamide and its metabolite are renally excreted.

### Hepatic impairment
- No specific studies conducted; no dosage adjustment specified in available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **Brinzolamide**: potent inhibitor of human carbonic anhydrase II (CA‑II) in the eye; decreases aqueous humour secretion by inhibiting CA‑II in the ciliary processes, reducing bicarbonate formation and subsequent sodium and fluid transport.
- **Timolol**: non‑selective beta‑adrenergic receptor blocker; lowers IOP primarily by decreasing aqueous humour formation; exact mechanism not fully established — studies suggest reduced aqueous production with possible minor increase in outflow facility.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: Systemic absorption occurs after topical ocular administration for both components.
- Distribution: Not specified in available sources.
- Metabolism:
  - Brinzolamide: metabolised primarily by CYP3A4 and also by CYP2A6, CYP2B6, CYP2C8 and CYP2C9; main metabolite eliminated renally.
  - Timolol: specific metabolic pathways not detailed in available sources.
- Elimination:
  - Brinzolamide and its principal metabolite are excreted predominantly via the kidney; accumulation risk in severe renal impairment.
  - Timolol: Not specified in available sources regarding elimination half‑life or renal/hepatic clearance specifics.
- Pharmacodynamics:
  - Brinzolamide: minimal cardiovascular effects reported; no QTc prolongation observed in available data.
  - Timolol: systemic beta‑blocking effects possible depending on systemic absorption.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Two clinical trials of 6 and 12 months duration involving patients treated with **brinzolamide + timolol** (reported collectively for 394 patients) identified transient blurred vision on instillation as the most frequently reported adverse reaction (approximately 3.6%).
- A 12‑month, double‑masked, randomized clinical trial enrolling 437 patients with open‑angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension is reported in the source documents (details of outcomes and comparative efficacy not specified in available excerpts).
- Additional efficacy and safety data: Not specified in available sources beyond the summarized adverse reaction incidence and trial durations above.
