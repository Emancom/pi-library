# Editorial Product Information: Benzylpenicillin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis incorporates information from the following jurisdictional Product Information (PI) documents:
    - **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**: Benzylpenicillin Kabi (Benzylpenicillin Sodium)
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a clinical synthesis intended for reference and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.
- **Note on Sources**: At the time of compilation, only the Australian regulatory source was provided for review. Information specific to other regions (US, European, etc.) is marked as "Not specified in available sources" where applicable.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Benzylpenicillin sodium (also known as Penicillin G).
- **Chemical Class**: Beta-lactam antibiotic; natural penicillin.
- **Brand Names**: Benzylpenicillin Kabi [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: J01CE01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s)**: 
    - Powder for injection: 600 mg (1,000,000 IU), 1200 mg (2,000,000 IU), and 3000 mg (5,000,000 IU) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration**: Intramuscular (IM) injection, Intravenous (IV) injection, or IV infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Fresenius Kabi Australia Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **General Infections**: Treatment of infections caused by sensitive organisms, particularly *Streptococcus pyogenes* and other Gram-positive pathogens [Australian].
- **Syphilis**: Indicated for all stages of syphilis [Australian].
- **Prophylaxis**: 
    - Prevention of bacterial endocarditis associated with dental or upper respiratory tract procedures [Australian].
    - Prevention of wound infections and sepsis in surgical procedures where Streptococci are likely pathogens [Australian].
- **Specific Infections**: Meningococcal and pneumococcal meningitis, subacute bacterial endocarditis, and clostridial infections (e.g., *C. perfringens*) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Children >10 years)
- **Minimum dose**: 300 mg every 6 hours [Australian].
- **Severe infections**: Dosages may range from 4 g to 24 g daily depending on the severity and resistance of the pathogen [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing
- **Children 3–10 years**: 150 mg to 300 mg every 6 hours [Australian].
- **Children <3 years**: 60 mg every 6 hours [Australian].
- **Premature babies and neonates**: 30 mg to 60 mg every 12 hours (reduced frequency due to lower renal clearance) [Australian].

### Specific Indication Dosing [Australian]
- **Meningococcal Meningitis (Children)**: Initial 600 mg dose followed by 300 mg IM every 4 to 6 hours.
- **Pneumococcal Meningitis**: Minimum 300 mg every 4 hours for 14 days, then every 6 hours for 7 days.
- **Subacute Bacterial Endocarditis**: Minimum 1.2 g daily in divided doses; up to 24 g daily may be required for 4–6 weeks.
- **Surgical Prophylaxis**: 600 mg IV immediately prior to surgery; for prolonged procedures, repeat 600 mg every 4 to 8 hours.
- **Clostridial Infections**: 1.2 g IV every 6 hours for 48 hours in addition to surgical intervention.

### Administration Instructions
- **Reconstitution**: Must be reconstituted with Water for Injections. 
    - For IM: 600 mg in 1.6 mL (300 mg/mL).
    - For IV: Recommended concentration is 60 mg/mL (e.g., 600 mg diluted to 10 mL).
- **IV Compatibility**: Compatible with Normal Saline 0.9% and Glucose 5%. Do not mix with Ringer’s solution or other sodium-containing solutions during initial reconstitution due to electrolyte content [Australian].
- **Stability**: Reconstituted solutions must be used immediately to reduce microbiological risk [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity**: Known history of hypersensitivity reactions to any beta-lactam antibiotics (penicillins, cephalosporins) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Anaphylaxis**: Serious and occasionally fatal hypersensitivity reactions have been reported. Immediate emergency treatment with adrenaline, oxygen, and airway management may be required.
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCAR)**: SJS, TEN, DRESS, and AGEP have been reported. Discontinue therapy immediately if SCAR is suspected [Australian].
- **Pseudomembranous Colitis**: *Clostridium difficile*-associated diarrhoea (CDAD) may occur during or weeks after treatment. Avoid drugs that delay peristalsis (e.g., opiates) [Australian].
- **Electrolyte Disturbance**: Each 1 g of benzylpenicillin sodium contains 3.0 mmol of sodium. Monitor electrolyte balance, especially in patients requiring high-dose therapy or those with renal/cardiac impairment [Australian].
- **Neurotoxicity**: Massive IV doses (e.g., >20 g) may lead to convulsions and coma, particularly in patients with renal failure [Australian].
- **Superinfection**: Prolonged use may result in the overgrowth of non-susceptible organisms, including fungi [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequencies are not strictly quantified in the source, but the following have been identified [Australian]:

- **Hypersensitivity (Most Common)**: 
    - **Common**: Rash, pruritus, urticaria.
    - **Serious**: Anaphylactic shock, bronchospasm, angioedema, Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN).
- **Gastrointestinal**: Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, and pseudomembranous colitis.
- **Central Nervous System**: Confusion, convulsions, and encephalopathy (associated with high doses or renal impairment).
- **Haematological**: Anaemia, agranulocytosis, neutropenia, eosinophilia, and coagulation disorders.
- **Hepatic**: Hepatitis and cholestatic jaundice.
- **Local Reactions**: Pain at the IM injection site; phlebitis at the IV injection site.
- **Jarisch-Herxheimer Reaction**: Malaise, fever, tachycardia, and headache; occurs in ~50% of patients treated for syphilis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Probenecid**: Inhibits renal tubular secretion, leading to increased and prolonged plasma levels of benzylpenicillin [Australian].
- **Bacteriostatic Antibiotics**: Tetracyclines, erythromycin, and chloramphenicol may antagonise the bactericidal effect of penicillin [Australian].
- **Aminoglycosides (e.g., Gentamicin)**: Physical incompatibility; do not mix in the same parenteral solution as inactivation occurs [Australian].
- **Oral Contraceptives**: May reduce the efficacy of hormonal contraceptives; patients should be advised accordingly [Australian].
- **Laboratory Tests**: May cause false-positive results for urinary glucose using copper reduction methods (Clinitest, Benedict’s). Enzymatic glucose oxidase tests should be used instead [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **Australian Category A**: Extensively used without evidence of teratogenicity or untoward effects on the human foetus.
- **Breastfeeding**: Excreted in breast milk. While generally considered compatible, alternative feeding or monitoring for neonatal sensitisation is recommended [Australian].
- **Fertility**: No data available in source documents [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Dosing is age-dependent. Neonates require reduced frequency due to immature renal function [Australian].
- **Elderly Use**: Renal clearance is often reduced; monitor blood levels if high dosages are administered to avoid neurotoxicity [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment**: Dosing must be adjusted based on creatinine clearance. For severe impairment, daily doses up to 6 g are generally well-tolerated, but larger doses require specific calculation to avoid CNS toxicity [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Bactericidal; inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis. Sensitive to inactivation by bacterial beta-lactamases [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Rapidly absorbed after IM injection (peak ~12 mg/L after 30 mins for a 600 mg dose) [Australian].
    - **Distribution**: Diffuses across the placenta; poor penetration into CSF unless meninges are inflamed [Australian].
    - **Metabolism**: ~30% inactivated in the liver to penicilloic acid [Australian].
    - **Elimination**: Primarily renal (70% within 6 hours). ~10% via glomerular filtration and ~90% via tubular secretion [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No specific pivotal trial data was described in the provided source documents. Benzylpenicillin has been in clinical use for over 50 years [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Dry Powder**: Store in a dry place below 25°C [Australian].
- **Reconstituted Solution**: Use immediately. Do not store for subsequent use; discard any unused portion [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule**: 
    - **Australian**: Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
    - **Other Regions**: Not specified in available sources.
