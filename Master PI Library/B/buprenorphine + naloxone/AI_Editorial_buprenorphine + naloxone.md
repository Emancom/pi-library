# Editorial Product Information: buprenorphine + naloxone

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **buprenorphine + naloxone**
- Chemical class: partial opioid agonist (buprenorphine) combined with opioid antagonist (naloxone)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: sublingual (place under the tongue until dissolved)

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General dosing (adult)
- Following induction, administer once daily as a single sublingual dose. Exact maintenance dose should be individualized and based on clinical response.

### Induction (patients dependent on short-acting opioids)
- Initiate only when objective signs of moderate opioid withdrawal are present (to reduce risk of precipitated withdrawal); do not initiate less than 6 hours after last opioid use.
- Day 1:
  - Recommended starting dose: one low initial tablet (equivalent to 1.4 mg buprenorphine / 0.36 mg naloxone in the source).
  - Up to a total of 5.7 mg/1.4 mg may be administered on Day 1 in divided doses.
  - Additional Day 1 doses should be divided into increments given at ~1.5–2 hour intervals; some patients with recent buprenorphine exposure may tolerate a single second dose of up to three times the initial low tablet strength.
- Day 2:
  - Single daily dose up to 11.4 mg/2.9 mg is recommended (titrate rapidly to an effective dose).

### Induction (patients dependent on methadone or long‑acting opioids)
- Patients taking methadone or other long-acting opioids are at increased risk of precipitated or prolonged withdrawal with combination buprenorphine/naloxone products.
- Recommendation: use buprenorphine monotherapy for induction in these patients; once induction and stabilization are achieved, consider transition to combined buprenorphine + naloxone maintenance as clinically appropriate.

### Maintenance
- Dose adjustments after Day 3 should generally be in increments or decrements of 2.9 mg/0.71 mg (or smaller).
- Typical maintenance range reported: 2.9 mg/0.71 mg to 17.2 mg/4.2 mg buprenorphine/naloxone per day.
- After induction to a recommended dose (e.g., 11.4 mg/2.9 mg per day in the source), titrate based on individual response.
- Doses above 17.2 mg/4.2 mg daily have not been investigated in randomized clinical trials; may be appropriate for some patients under clinical judgement.

### Switching between different buprenorphine/naloxone sublingual tablet formulations
- Bioavailability varies between different sublingual buprenorphine/naloxone products; corresponding tablet strengths between formulations may not be identical. Exercise caution when switching formulations and monitor for over- or under-dosing. Specific equivalence guidance is formulation-dependent (not reproduced here).

### Method of administration
- Administer whole by placing under the tongue until fully dissolved. Do not cut, chew, or swallow tablets.
- Patients should avoid eating or drinking until the tablet is completely dissolved.
- For multi‑tablet doses, place tablets in different locations under the tongue simultaneously.
- Swallowing the tablet before complete dissolution reduces bioavailability.
- After the tablet has dissolved, patients should be advised to sip water, swish gently around the teeth and gums, and swallow. Wait at least one hour after dosing before brushing teeth.
- Proper administration technique should be demonstrated.

### Clinical supervision and follow-up
- Initiate treatment with supervised administration; progress to unsupervised administration as clinical stability allows.
- Early in treatment, frequent follow-up (e.g., at least weekly during the first month) is recommended; once stable and compliant, visits may be less frequent (monthly may be reasonable for some).
- When considering unsupervised take-home supplies, assess patient stability, home security, and ability to manage medication; avoid multiple refills early or without appropriate follow-up.

### Discontinuation / Missed dose instructions
- When discontinuing, gradually taper to reduce risk of opioid withdrawal signs and symptoms.
- Specific missed-dose instructions are not specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed/major warnings
- **Risk of life‑threatening respiratory depression and death**: Buprenorphine (component) has been associated with life-threatening respiratory depression and fatalities, particularly with misuse (including parenteral administration), concomitant use of benzodiazepines or other CNS depressants (including alcohol), or in opioid‑naïve individuals.
- **Unintentional pediatric exposure**: **Buprenorphine + naloxone can cause severe and fatal respiratory depression in children.** Store safely out of sight and reach of children.
- **Neonatal opioid withdrawal syndrome (NOWS)**: Prolonged maternal use of opioids can result in NOWS, an expected and treatable condition that can be life‑threatening if not recognized and managed.

### General warnings and precautions
- Addiction, abuse, and misuse: Contains buprenorphine, a controlled opioid subject to abuse, misuse, and diversion. Monitor patients for addictive behaviors and diversion; avoid early multiple refills without follow-up.
- Concomitant CNS depressants: Concomitant use with benzodiazepines, other sedatives, hypnotics, tranquilizers, antipsychotics, other opioids, or alcohol may result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, or death. Use caution, monitor closely, and warn patients against self‑administration.
- Respiratory disease: Use with caution in patients with compromised respiratory function (e.g., COPD, decreased respiratory reserve, hypoxia, hypercapnia).
- Sleep-related breathing disorders: Opioids may cause or worsen central sleep apnea and sleep‑related hypoxemia; consider dose reduction if central sleep apnea occurs.
- Overdose risk in opioid‑naïve patients: Reports of deaths have occurred in opioid‑naïve individuals receiving buprenorphine at relatively low doses.
- Precipitated withdrawal: Use of buprenorphine/naloxone in patients currently dependent on full opioid agonists may precipitate opioid withdrawal, especially with parenteral misuse or if administered before agonist effects subside. Patients dependent on long‑acting opioids (e.g., methadone) are at higher risk.
- Hepatic events: Monitor liver function tests prior to initiation and during treatment; evaluate suspected hepatic injury. Buprenorphine/naloxone products are not recommended in severe hepatic impairment and may not be appropriate in moderate hepatic impairment.
- Adrenal insufficiency: Cases have been reported with opioids; if suspected, treat with physiologic corticosteroid replacement and consider weaning opioid.
- Risk of serotonin syndrome: Concomitant use with serotonergic drugs may lead to serotonin syndrome; discontinue if suspected.
- Dental adverse events: Reports of dental problems associated with buprenorphine sublingual administration have been described; advise appropriate oral hygiene and dental follow‑up.
- QTc prolongation: QTc prolongation has been reported; consider ECG monitoring in patients at risk for QT prolongation.
- Impairment of ability to drive or operate machinery: May impair cognitive or motor skills; caution patients about driving or operating machinery until they know how therapy affects them.
- Orthostatic hypotension: May cause orthostatic changes; monitor patients at risk.
- Elevations of intracranial/CSF pressure and intracholedochal pressure; effects in acute abdominal conditions: Use with caution in these settings.

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and periodic liver function tests.
- Assessment for signs of respiratory depression and sedation.
- Monitor for signs of adrenal insufficiency (e.g., fatigue, hypotension, weight loss, GI symptoms).
- Urine drug screening or other monitoring for adherence and illicit drug use as clinically indicated.
- Pregnancy status in women of childbearing potential; counsel regarding neonatal withdrawal risk.
- For patients receiving concomitant CYP3A4 inhibitors/inducers, monitor for over‑ or under‑dosing (see Drug Interactions).

## 8. Drug Interactions

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Benzodiazepines and other CNS depressants (including alcohol): Concomitant use increases risk of profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death. Use caution; co‑prescribing requires careful patient selection, dose adjustments, and monitoring. Warn patients against concomitant self‑administration.
- Serotonergic drugs: Concomitant use may cause serotonin syndrome; discontinue buprenorphine + naloxone if serotonin syndrome is suspected.

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- CYP3A4 inhibitors and inducers: Buprenorphine is metabolized in part via CYP3A4. Initiation or discontinuation of strong CYP3A4 inhibitors or inducers can alter buprenorphine exposure; monitor patients for signs of over- or under‑dosing and adjust dose if needed.
- Antiretrovirals:
  - Non‑nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors (NNRTIs): Patients on chronic buprenorphine therapy who have NNRTIs added to their regimen should have buprenorphine doses monitored.
  - Atazanavir (with or without ritonavir): Monitor and reduce buprenorphine dose if clinically warranted.

### Special interaction notes
- Naloxone in the combination product: Naloxone is minimally absorbed when given sublingually; however, when the product is misused parenterally, naloxone can precipitate opioid withdrawal. For this reason, induction in patients on long‑acting opioids may be performed with buprenorphine monotherapy.
- Opioid overdose reversal agents (naloxone, nalmefene): Higher than normal doses and repeated administration of an opioid overdose reversal agent may be necessary to reverse respiratory depression from buprenorphine + naloxone because of buprenorphine’s high mu‑receptor affinity and prolonged duration of action. Inform patients and caregivers about availability and use of opioid overdose reversal agents.

## 11. Pharmacology

### Mechanism of action
- Buprenorphine: partial agonist at the mu-opioid receptor (high affinity), with partial agonist/antagonist properties at other opioid receptor subtypes depending on concentration.
- Naloxone: opioid receptor antagonist included to deter parenteral misuse; minimally absorbed via the sublingual route but active if administered parenterally.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Route: sublingual absorption; patients must allow tablet to dissolve under the tongue for optimal bioavailability.
- Naloxone absorption: small when administered sublingually; significant if given parenterally (can precipitate withdrawal).
- Product bioavailability differences exist among different buprenorphine/naloxone sublingual tablet formulations; therefore, equivalent tablet strengths may vary between products.
- Other specific PK parameters (Cmax, Tmax, half‑life, clearance, effect of renal impairment, exact numeric values): Not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Buprenorphine’s high affinity and long duration at the mu receptor explain the potential for prolonged opioid effects and the need for repeated or higher doses of opioid overdose reversal agents in an overdose scenario.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Induction experience: Some clinical studies referenced indicate that overly gradual induction over several days resulted in higher dropout rates during induction; a more rapid titration to an adequate treatment dose was recommended in clinical practice.
