# Editorial Product Information: Brompheniramine Maleate, Pseudoephedrine Hydrochloride, and Dextromethorphan Hydrobromide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of the combination of brompheniramine maleate, pseudoephedrine hydrochloride, and dextromethorphan hydrobromide for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing within a specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** 
    - Brompheniramine maleate (Alkylamine derivative antihistamine)
    - Pseudoephedrine hydrochloride (Sympathomimetic amine)
    - Dextromethorphan hydrobromide (Centrally acting antitussive)
- **Brand Name(s):** Not specified in available sources (Generic manufacturer: Dr. Reddy's Laboratories Inc. [US]).
- **ATC Code:** R05DA20 (Dextromethorphan, combinations); R01BA52 (Pseudoephedrine, combinations).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Oral Syrup [US].
    - Each 5 mL (1 teaspoonful) contains:
        - Brompheniramine maleate: 2 mg
        - Pseudoephedrine hydrochloride: 30 mg
        - Dextromethorphan hydrobromide: 10 mg
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Dr. Reddy's Laboratories Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- For the relief of coughs and upper respiratory symptoms, including nasal congestion, associated with allergy or the common cold [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
Dosage should be administered every 4 hours. Do not exceed 6 doses within a 24-hour period [US].

| Patient Population | Dosage (per 4 hours) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Adults and Pediatrics (≥ 12 years) | 10 mL (2 teaspoonfuls) |
| Children (6 to < 12 years) | 5 mL (1 teaspoonful) |
| Children (2 to < 6 years) | 2.5 mL (½ teaspoonful) |
| Infants (6 months to < 2 years) | Dosage to be established by a physician |

### Administration Instructions
- A precise measuring device should be used for pediatric dosing.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to brompheniramine, pseudoephedrine, dextromethorphan, or any excipients [US].
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Use is contraindicated in patients currently receiving MAOIs or within 14 days of discontinuing such treatment [US].
- **Specific Populations:** Contraindicated in newborns, premature infants, and nursing mothers [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Severe hypertension or severe coronary artery disease [US].
- **Respiratory:** Antihistamines should not be used to treat lower respiratory tract conditions, including asthma [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- None listed in provided sources [US].

### General Warnings
- **Pediatric Safety:** In infants and small children, antihistamine overdosage may cause hallucinations, convulsions, and death. In young children, they may produce excitation rather than sedation [US].
- **CNS Depression:** Antihistamines may significantly diminish mental alertness. Patients should be cautioned against operating heavy machinery or driving [US].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Antihistamine Component:** Use with caution in patients with a history of bronchial asthma, narrow-angle glaucoma, gastrointestinal obstruction, or urinary bladder neck obstruction [US].
- **Sympathomimetic Component:** Use with caution in patients with diabetes, hypertension, heart disease, or thyroid disease [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- Not specified in available sources.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are listed by system organ class. Frequency data is not quantitatively defined in the source but "most frequent" effects are identified [US].

- **Very Common/Common:**
    - **CNS:** Sedation, dizziness, disturbed coordination.
    - **Respiratory:** Dryness of mouth, nose, and throat; thickening of bronchial secretions.
- **Other Reported Effects:**
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypotension, hypertension, cardiac arrhythmias, palpitation.
    - **CNS:** Tremor, irritability, insomnia, visual disturbances, weakness, nervousness, convulsions, headache, euphoria, dysphoria.
    - **Dermatologic:** Urticaria, drug rash, photosensitivity, pruritus.
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Epigastric discomfort, anorexia, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation.
    - **Genitourinary:** Urinary frequency, dysuria (difficult urination).
    - **Hematologic:** Hemolytic anemia, thrombocytopenia, agranulocytosis.
    - **Respiratory:** Tightness of chest, wheezing, shortness of breath.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **MAOIs:** Co-administration with dextromethorphan can lead to hyperpyrexia, hypotension, and death. MAOIs also intensify the anticholinergic effects of antihistamines and enhance the effects of pseudoephedrine [US].
- **CNS Depressants:** Alcohol and other CNS depressants (hypnotics, sedatives, tranquilizers) have additive effects when used with brompheniramine [US].
- **Antihypertensives:** The sympathomimetic effect of pseudoephedrine may reduce the efficacy of antihypertensive drugs [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category C.** Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted with the combination. 
    - Brompheniramine maleate alone showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm in rats/mice at 16 times the human dose. 
    - Use in pregnant women only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated in nursing mothers due to the higher risk of antihistamine intolerance in infants [US].
- **Fertility:** Not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in infants under 6 months have not been established [US]. See Section 3 for age-specific dosing.
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects $\ge$ 65 years to determine differential response. Caution is advised, starting at the low end of the dosing range, due to higher frequencies of hepatic, renal, or cardiac dysfunction. The elderly are more prone to dizziness, sedation, hypotension (from antihistamines), and adverse reactions to sympathomimetics [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dose selection should be cautious in the elderly with decreased renal function [US]. Specific dosing adjustments for eGFR stages are not specified.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Dose selection should be cautious in the elderly with decreased hepatic function [US]. Specific dosing adjustments are not specified.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
- **Brompheniramine Maleate:** An H1-receptor blocking agent (alkylamine class) that competes with histamine for receptor sites on effector cells. It also possesses anticholinergic and sedative properties [US].
- **Pseudoephedrine HCl:** Acts on sympathetic nerve endings and smooth muscle to produce vasoconstriction of dilated nasal arterioles via $\alpha$-sympathetic receptors [US].
- **Dextromethorphan HBr:** Acts centrally on the cough center in the medulla to elevate the threshold for coughing. It lacks analgesic or addictive properties [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Brompheniramine:** Well absorbed from the GI tract. Peak plasma concentration (after 4 mg dose) is reached in 5 hours. Metabolized primarily in the liver; eliminated mainly via urinary excretion [US].
- **Pseudoephedrine:** Onset of action within 30 minutes; peak activity at approximately 1 hour [US].
- **Dextromethorphan:** Onset of antitussive action occurs within 15 to 30 minutes; characterized by a long duration of action [US].
