# Editorial Product Information: Balsalazide Sodium

**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been compiled by integrating safety, efficacy, and administrative data from the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). Where regional differences exist—particularly regarding approved indications and specific warnings—the more comprehensive or conservative guidance has been included and labeled accordingly.

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** Balsalazide Sodium (available as Colazide [TGA] or generic equivalents).

**Active Ingredient:** Balsalazide sodium (or balsalazide disodium). 
*   **Capsules:** Each capsule contains 750 mg of balsalazide sodium, which is equivalent to 262.5 mg of the active moiety, mesalazine (5-ASA) [TGA].
*   **Tablets:** Each film-coated tablet contains 1.1 g of balsalazide disodium [FDA].

---

## 2. Approved Indications
Balsalazide is a locally acting aminosalicylate indicated for:

*   **Active Disease:** The treatment of mildly to moderately active ulcerative colitis. 
    *   *Note:* In the United States, effectiveness has only been established in male patients aged 18 years and older for a duration of up to 8 weeks [FDA]. In Australia, it is indicated for both genders for up to 12 weeks [TGA].
*   **Maintenance of Remission:** The maintenance of remission of ulcerative colitis, specifically in patients who cannot tolerate sulfasalazine [TGA].

---

## 3. Contraindications
Balsalazide is contraindicated in patients with:
*   Known or suspected hypersensitivity to balsalazide, its metabolites (including mesalazine), salicylates, or any excipients.
*   Severe hepatic impairment [TGA].
*   Moderate to severe renal impairment [TGA].
*   Active peptic ulceration [TGA].
*   A known pathological tendency toward bleeding [TGA].
*   The final weeks of pregnancy [TGA].

---

## 4. Warnings and Precautions

### Renal Effects
Renal toxicity has been reported with products that release mesalazine. Renal function (BUN/creatinine) and urinalysis should be evaluated prior to starting therapy and monitored periodically. Use with extreme caution in patients with a history of renal disease or those taking nephrotoxic agents (e.g., NSAIDs) [FDA, TGA].

### Mesalamine-Induced Acute Intolerance Syndrome
Balsalazide is converted to mesalazine, which may cause an acute intolerance syndrome. Symptoms include cramping, acute abdominal pain, bloody diarrhea, fever, headache, and rash. It can be difficult to distinguish from a colitis flare; if suspected, treatment must be discontinued immediately [FDA].

### Hepatic Effects
There have been reports of hepatic failure in patients with pre-existing liver disease. Use with caution in patients with mild to moderate hepatic impairment and monitor liver enzymes [FDA, TGA].

### Blood Dyscrasias
Rare reports of blood dyscrasias have occurred. Patients should be advised to report unexplained bleeding, bruising, sore throat, or fever. Complete blood counts should be performed if a dyscrasia is suspected [TGA].

### Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs)
Serious skin reactions, including Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), have been reported. Discontinue use at the first sign of skin rash or mucosal lesions [FDA].

### Other Precautions
*   **Photosensitivity:** Patients with pre-existing skin conditions (e.g., eczema) may experience severe photosensitivity; protective clothing and sunscreen are advised [FDA].
*   **Nephrolithiasis:** Mesalamine-containing stones have been reported. Ensure adequate fluid intake during treatment [FDA].
*   **Asthma:** Use with caution in patients with bronchial asthma [TGA].
*   **Sodium Content:** Each 1.1 g tablet contains approximately 126 mg of sodium. Consider the total daily sodium intake in patients on sodium-restricted diets [FDA].

---

## 5. Dosage and Administration
Balsalazide should be swallowed whole.

### 750 mg Capsules (Adults) [TGA]
*   **Active Disease:** 3 capsules (2.25 g) three times daily (total 6.75 g/day) until remission is achieved or for a maximum of 12 weeks.
*   **Maintenance:** 2 capsules (1.5 g) twice daily (total 3 g/day). Doses may be adjusted based on response; benefits have been observed at up to 8 capsules (6 g) daily.
*   **Administration:** Must be taken with food.

### 1.1 g Tablets (Adult Males) [FDA]
*   **Active Disease:** 3 tablets (3.3 g) twice daily (total 6.6 g/day) for up to 8 weeks.
*   **Administration:** May be taken with or without food.

---

## 6. Adverse Effects
Commonly reported adverse effects ($\ge$ 1%) include:
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Abdominal pain, diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, and flatulence.
*   **Nervous System:** Headache and dizziness.
*   **General:** Fatigue, fever, and flu-like symptoms.
*   **Respiratory:** Pharyngolaryngeal pain and nasopharyngitis [FDA].

**Post-marketing Experience:** 
Reports include hepatotoxicity (elevated LFTs, jaundice, cirrhosis), pancreatitis, interstitial nephritis, anemia, and alopecia. **Urine discoloration:** Urine may turn reddish-brown if it comes into contact with surfaces or water treated with hypochlorite bleach [FDA].

---

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Nephrotoxic Agents:** Concurrent use with NSAIDs or other nephrotoxic drugs increases the risk of renal reactions [FDA].
*   **Azathioprine/6-Mercaptopurine:** Concurrent use may increase the risk of blood disorders and bone marrow failure; monitor blood counts closely [FDA].
*   **Antibiotics:** Oral antibiotics may theoretically interfere with the bacterial release of mesalazine in the colon [TGA].
*   **Digoxin:** Balsalazide may potentially impair the absorption of digoxin; monitoring of digoxin levels is recommended [TGA].
*   **Methotrexate:** Potential for increased plasma levels of methotrexate due to competition for renal tubular secretion [TGA].

---

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

### Pregnancy
**Category C.** Balsalazide is contraindicated in the final weeks of pregnancy as it may cause premature closure of the ductus arteriosus and prolong labor [TGA]. While human data are limited, animal studies at high doses have not shown significant developmental toxicity [FDA]. Use during pregnancy only if the benefits clearly outweigh the risks.

### Breastfeeding
Caution should be exercised. Mesalazine and its metabolites are excreted in human milk in small amounts. There are case reports of diarrhea in breastfed infants. If used while breastfeeding, the infant should be monitored for diarrhea [FDA, TGA].

---

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
Balsalazide is a prodrug with negligible systemic absorption of the intact molecule (< 1%). 

*   **Metabolism:** In the colon, bacterial azo-reductases cleave balsalazide into mesalazine (5-ASA) and the carrier molecule 4-aminobenzoyl-$\beta$-alanine (4-ABA). Both are subsequently N-acetylated.
*   **Distribution:** Intact balsalazide is 99% bound to plasma proteins [TGA].
*   **Excretion:** Approximately 25% of the dose is recovered in the urine as metabolites; the majority is excreted in the feces [TGA].
*   **Food Effect:** Taking tablets with a high-fat meal reduces the peak concentration ($C_{max}$) of the active metabolite [FDA].

---

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Balsalazide delivers mesalazine (5-ASA) directly to the colon. While the exact mechanism is not fully defined, 5-ASA is thought to exert a local anti-inflammatory effect by inhibiting the cyclooxygenase and lipoxygenase pathways. This results in decreased production of pro-inflammatory arachidonic acid metabolites (prostanoids and leukotrienes) within the colonic mucosa.

---

## 11. Storage Conditions
Store in a cool, dry place below 25°C (77°F). Brief excursions between 15°C and 30°C are permitted [FDA, TGA]. Keep the container tightly closed and out of reach of children.
