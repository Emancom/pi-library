# Editorial Product Information: Benzathine Benzylpenicillin

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of benzathine benzylpenicillin, synthesised for use by healthcare professionals. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Benzathine benzylpenicillin tetrahydrate (the benzathine salt of benzylpenicillin).
- **Chemical Class:** Penicillin antibiotic; Natural penicillin.
- **Brand Name(s):** **BICILLIN L-A** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J01CE08.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Suspension for injection in pre-filled syringes [Australian]:
        - 600,000 Units / 1.17 mL
        - 1,200,000 Units / 2.3 mL
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Deep Intramuscular (IM) injection only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Intramuscular benzathine benzylpenicillin is indicated for treating infections caused by penicillin-sensitive microorganisms susceptible to the low, prolonged serum levels provided by this formulation.

- **Streptococcal Infections (Group A):** Mild-to-moderate upper respiratory tract infections (e.g., pharyngitis) without bacteraemia.
- **Venereal Infections:** Syphilis, yaws, bejel, and pinta.
- **Prophylaxis:** 
    - Prevention of recurrence for rheumatic fever and/or chorea.
    - Follow-up prophylaxis for rheumatic heart disease and acute glomerulonephritis.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Streptococcal Pharyngitis:** 1,200,000 Units as a single injection.
- **Syphilis (Primary, Secondary, Latent):** 2,400,000 Units as a single dose.
- **Syphilis (Late/Tertiary, including neurosyphilis):** 2,400,000 Units at 7-day intervals for three doses.
- **Yaws, Bejel, Pinta:** 1,200,000 Units as a single injection.
- **Rheumatic Fever Prophylaxis:** 1,200,000 Units once a month OR 600,000 Units every 2 weeks.

### Paediatric Dosing
- **Streptococcal Pharyngitis:**
    - Infants and children (< 27 kg): 300,000 to 600,000 Units (single dose).
    - Older children: 900,000 Units (single dose).
- **Congenital Syphilis (normal CSF):** 
    - Under 2 years: 50,000 Units/kg body weight.
    - 2–12 years: Adjust based on adult schedule.

### Administration Instructions
- **STRICTLY INTRAMUSCULAR:** Do not inject intravenously (IV), intra-arterially, or near a nerve. 
- **Site:** Deep IM injection into the upper, outer quadrant of the buttock. In infants/small children, the midlateral aspect of the thigh is preferred. 
- **Warning:** Repeated injections into the anterolateral thigh are not recommended due to risks of quadriceps femoris fibrosis and atrophy.
- **Technique:** Inject at a slow, steady rate to avoid needle blockage. Aspirate before injection to ensure a blood vessel has not been entered.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known history of hypersensitivity reaction to any of the penicillins [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Anaphylaxis:** Serious and occasionally fatal hypersensitivity reactions have been reported. Immediate emergency treatment with adrenaline is required for severe reactions.
- **Inadvertent Intravascular Injection:** Can cause cardiorespiratory arrest and death. Accidental intra-arterial injection may lead to **Nicolau syndrome** (livedoid dermatitis), resulting in severe neurovascular damage, transverse myelitis, gangrene, and permanent paralysis.
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCAR):** Including Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), DRESS, and AGEP. Discontinue immediately if suspected.
- **Antibiotic-Associated Colitis:** *C. difficile* associated-diarrhoea (CDAD) has been reported; severity may range from mild to life-threatening.
- **Hoigne’s Syndrome:** A syndrome of CNS symptoms (agitation, hallucinations, fear of impending death, seizures) may occur following inadvertent intravascular injection of penicillin procaine, or less commonly, benzathine/procaine combinations.
- **Monitoring:** Periodic assessment of renal, hepatic, and haematopoietic systems is recommended during prolonged therapy.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Frequencies are not explicitly specified in the source; the following have been reported during post-marketing/clinical use:

- **Immune System:** Anaphylaxis, serum sickness-like reactions (chills, fever, arthralgia), **Kounis syndrome** (allergic myocardial ischaemia).
- **Skin:** Urticaria, maculopapular eruptions, exfoliative dermatitis, SCAR (SJS/TEN).
- **Local:** Injection site pain, inflammation, lump, abscess, necrosis, and atrophy.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, blood in stool, pseudomembranous colitis.
- **Neurological:** Dizziness, somnolence, tremors, seizures, transverse myelitis.
- **Other:** Jarisch-Herxheimer reaction (common in syphilis treatment), haemolytic anaemia, leucopenia, and nephropathy.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Tetracyclines:** May antagonise the bactericidal effect of penicillin; concurrent use should be avoided.
- **Probenecid:** Decreases the renal tubular secretion of penicillin, resulting in increased and prolonged serum levels.
- **Laboratory Tests:** May cause false-positive results for glycosuria when using copper sulfate reagents (e.g., Benedict’s Solution); glucose oxidase methods are preferred.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category A]. This class of drugs has been taken by many pregnant women without a proven increase in malformations or harmful effects on the fetus. Use only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Soluble benzylpenicillin is excreted in human milk. Caution should be exercised, as the effect on the nursing infant is unknown.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric:** Safety and dosage recommendations are provided for infants and children (see Section 3). High risk of complications from accidental intravascular injection.
- **Elderly:** Benzathine benzylpenicillin is primarily excreted by the kidneys. Because elderly patients are more likely to have decreased renal function, renal monitoring is advised.
- **Renal Impairment:** Excretion is delayed; dosage adjustments may be required to avoid toxicity, including neuromuscular hyperirritability or seizures.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal. Inhibits the biosynthesis of cell-wall peptidoglycan during active multiplication, rendering the cell wall osmotically unstable.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Active against many strains of Staphylococci (not penicillinase-producing), Streptococci, and *Treponema pallidum*.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Absorbed very slowly from the IM site. 
    - **Distribution:** Approximately 60% protein-bound. Distributed widely; highest levels in kidneys, lower in liver, skin, and intestines.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily excreted via renal tubular secretion.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate). Do not freeze.
- **Excursions:** May be stored below 30°C for a single period of up to 2 months prior to expiry [Australian]. Once moved to room temperature, it must not be returned to refrigeration.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging.
