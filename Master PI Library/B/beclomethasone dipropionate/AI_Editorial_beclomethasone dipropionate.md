# Editorial Product Information: beclomethasone dipropionate

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **beclomethasone dipropionate**
- Chemical class: corticosteroid (intranasal glucocorticoid class)  
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: intranasal (nasal aerosol/spray)  
  - Intranasal use is the primary route described. In the clinical and postmarketing text, other inhalation formulations are referenced in relation to adverse reactions, but specific dosing/administration for non-intranasal formulations is Not specified in available sources.

- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources for any jurisdiction other than the above.  
- Registration/approval dates or last-update details for other jurisdictions: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of the nasal symptoms associated with seasonal and perennial allergic rhinitis in patients 4 years of age and older. [US-approved]  
- Indication status in other jurisdictions: Not specified in available sources

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (intranasal aerosol)
- Adults and adolescents (12 years and older): recommended total 320 mcg once daily administered as 2 actuations in each nostril of an 80 mcg actuation product (maximum total daily dose: 4 actuations per day).  
- Children (4 to 11 years of age): recommended total 80 mcg once daily administered as 1 actuation in each nostril of a 40 mcg actuation product (maximum total daily dose: 2 actuations per day).

### Dosing by indication
- Allergic rhinitis (seasonal and perennial): use dosing above for respective age groups. No other indications/dosing regimens are specified in available sources.

### Administration instructions
- Administer by the intranasal route only.  
- The product described does not require priming prior to first use (per available product instructions).  
- Users should follow the specific illustrated patient instructions for correct technique (patient leaflet).  
- Patients using intranasal corticosteroid therapy for months or longer should be examined periodically for local nasal adverse effects.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- No boxed warnings are specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Local nasal effects: reports of nasal discomfort, epistaxis, nasal erosions/ulceration, and impaired wound healing. Examine the nasal mucosa periodically in patients using the product for several months or longer. Discontinue if erosion or ulceration is observed. Avoid use in patients with recent nasal septal ulcers, recent nasal surgery, or recent nasal trauma until healing has occurred.
- Nasal septal perforation: has been reported following intranasal administration of beclomethasone dipropionate (postmarketing). One occurrence was reported in pediatric dose-ranging study; none reported in a particular 52-week trial at the indicated adult/adolescent dose.
- Local fungal infection (Candida albicans): localized infections of the nose and pharynx have been reported with intranasal beclomethasone in some formulations. If local fungal infection develops, consider topical antifungal therapy and discontinuation.
- Eye disorders: intranasal/inhaled corticosteroids may result in increased intraocular pressure, glaucoma, cataracts, or blurred vision. Monitor patients with changes in vision or with a history of increased intraocular pressure, glaucoma, or cataracts.
- Hypersensitivity reactions: anaphylaxis, angioedema, urticaria, and rash have been reported. Discontinue if such reactions occur.
- Immunosuppression and infections: corticosteroid use may increase susceptibility to infections and may worsen existing infections (including tuberculosis, fungal, bacterial, viral, or parasitic infections). Special caution with exposure to chickenpox or measles in susceptible individuals; prophylaxis (e.g., VZIG or pooled IG) may be indicated after exposure per standard recommendations. Use caution in patients with ocular herpes simplex.
- Hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal (HPA) axis effects: systemic corticosteroid effects such as hypercorticism and adrenal suppression can occur with higher-than-recommended dosages or in susceptible individuals at recommended dosages. If systemic corticosteroid effects occur, consider gradual withdrawal consistent with accepted procedures; monitor patients switched from prolonged systemic corticosteroids for adrenal insufficiency during stress.
- Effect on growth: corticosteroids may reduce growth velocity in pediatric patients. Routinely monitor growth in children receiving intranasal corticosteroids.

### Monitoring requirements
- Periodic nasal mucosa examinations for signs of erosion, ulceration, infection.  
- Monitor intraocular pressure and perform ophthalmologic assessment if patients have visual changes or a history of glaucoma/cataract.  
- Monitor growth routinely in pediatric patients.  
- Monitor for clinical signs of adrenal suppression in patients receiving high doses or those who are otherwise susceptible (e.g., recent systemic corticosteroid use).

### Specific clinical precautions
- Avoid use until healing after recent nasal surgery, septal ulcers, or nasal trauma due to impaired wound healing with corticosteroids.  
- Consider the potential for systemic effects in patients receiving multiple corticosteroid formulations or high doses.  
- Use with caution in patients with active or quiescent tuberculosis of the respiratory tract, or untreated local/systemic fungal, bacterial, viral or parasitic infections.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Pharmacokinetic interactions: No formal drug interaction studies have been performed with the intranasal product described in the available sources. Specific metabolic pathway interactions (e.g., CYP interactions) are Not specified in available sources.
- Pharmacodynamic interactions / clinically significant interactions:
  - Concomitant use of other immunosuppressive drugs or systemic corticosteroids may increase risk of infection and/or systemic corticosteroid effects. Exercise caution and monitor clinically.
  - Replacement of systemic corticosteroids with topical/intranasal corticosteroids requires monitoring for adrenal insufficiency; abrupt discontinuation of systemic corticosteroids may cause exacerbation of underlying disease.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Human data: Adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women are not available; available human data do not establish the presence or absence of drug-associated risk to the fetus.  
- Animal data (selected findings):
  - In mice and rabbits, subcutaneous administration produced adverse developmental effects (including cleft palate in mice, and external/skeletal malformations and increased fetal resorptions in rabbits) at doses equal to or greater than approximately 1.5 times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) (on a mg/m² basis in the reported studies).
  - In rats exposed by inhalation, fetal adrenal gland injury occurred at doses substantially higher than MRHD (e.g., ≥350 times MRHD); no external or skeletal malformations at inhalation doses up to 860 times MRHD in rats.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Indicated for symptomatic treatment of seasonal and perennial allergic rhinitis in patients aged 4 years and older. [US-approved]  
- Recommended pediatric dosing (4 to 11 years): 80 mcg once daily (1 actuation in each nostril of 40 mcg actuation product), maximum 2 actuations/day.  
- Clinical trial data: Pediatric trials (three placebo-controlled trials, 2–12 weeks) included a total of 1,360 children; common adverse events in the pediatric population included headache, pyrexia, upper respiratory tract infection, and nasopharyngitis. Based on dose-ranging, 80 mcg once daily was chosen for pediatric use.  
- Growth monitoring: Routinely monitor growth velocity in pediatric patients due to potential reduction in growth.

### Elderly use
- Clinical trial data did not include sufficient numbers of patients aged ≥65 years to determine whether they respond differently from younger patients. No specific dose adjustment recommended in available sources.

## 12. Clinical Trials
### Summary of key trials and safety findings described in available sources
- Adults and adolescents (short-term trials):
  - Four placebo-controlled trials, 2 to 6 weeks duration, evaluating doses from 80 to 320 mcg once daily in seasonal or perennial allergic rhinitis. Total N across these trials described: 1,394 patients; for the 320 mcg dose, 575 received active and 578 received placebo. Common adverse events (≥1% and greater than placebo) included nasal discomfort (5.2% active vs 4.8% placebo), epistaxis (1.9% vs 1.2%), and headache (2.3% vs 1.6%).
- Adults and adolescents (long-term safety trial):
  - One 52-week placebo-controlled safety trial in perennial allergic rhinitis: 415 patients treated with 320 mcg once daily and 111 with placebo. In the active group, epistaxis occurred in 45/415 (11%) compared with 2/111 (2%) in placebo; epistaxis was more severe and led to more withdrawals in the active group. Nasal erosions (4) and 1 nasal septum ulceration occurred in active-treated patients; no septal perforations in that trial.
- Paediatric trials:
  - Three placebo-controlled pediatric trials (2 to 12 weeks), doses 80 to 160 mcg once daily, total of 1,360 children. For the 80 mcg once-daily dose (chosen dose), common adverse events (≥2% and > placebo) included headache (3.4%), pyrexia (2.8%), upper respiratory tract infection (2.5%), and nasopharyngitis (2.2%). Epistaxis reported at ~4% in both active and placebo groups in pooled pediatric data.
- Efficacy endpoint details (e.g., primary efficacy outcomes, magnitude of symptom improvement) are Not specified in available sources.

### Trial designs and primary outcomes
- Trial designs: randomized, placebo-controlled trials of variable duration as noted above. Exact primary efficacy outcomes and numerical efficacy results are Not specified in available sources.
