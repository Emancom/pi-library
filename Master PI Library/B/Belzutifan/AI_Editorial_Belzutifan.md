# Editorial Product Information: Belzutifan

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data, regulatory warnings, and pharmacological profiles.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational use by healthcare professionals and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Prescribers should consult their local regulatory authority's approved documentation before initiating therapy.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Belzutifan; a hypoxia-inducible factor 2 alpha (HIF-2α) inhibitor.
- **Brand Name(s):** **WELIREG** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** L01XX74
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 40 mg film-coated tablets (blue, oval-shaped, debossed with "177" on one side) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Merck Sharp & Dohme (Australia) Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **von Hippel-Lindau (VHL) Disease-Associated Tumours:** Treatment of adult patients with VHL disease requiring therapy for associated renal cell carcinoma (RCC), central nervous system (CNS) haemangioblastomas, or pancreatic neuroendocrine tumours (pNET) that do not require immediate surgery [Australian].
- **Advanced Renal Cell Carcinoma (RCC):** Treatment of adult patients with advanced clear cell RCC following treatment with a programmed cell death 1/ligand 1 (PD-1/L1) inhibitor and a vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF)-targeted therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The standard dose is 120 mg (three 40 mg tablets) administered orally once daily.
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets should be swallowed whole with or without food. Do not chew, crush, or break the tablets.
- **Treatment Duration:** Continue until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occurs.
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, it may be taken as soon as possible on the same day. Resume the regular schedule the following day. Do not take extra tablets to compensate for a missed dose.
- **Vomiting:** If vomiting occurs after administration, do not retake the dose; take the next scheduled dose the following day.
- **Dose Modifications for Adverse Reactions:**
    - **Anaemia (Hgb <80 g/L or transfusion indicated):** Withhold until resolved to ≤ Grade 2. Resume at the same or a reduced dose (by 40 mg). For life-threatening anaemia (Grade 4), withhold until ≤ Grade 2 and resume at a reduced dose or permanently discontinue.
    - **Hypoxia (Asymptomatic Grade 3):** Consider withholding or continuing at a reduced dose (by 40 mg). 
    - **Hypoxia (Symptomatic Grade 3):** Withhold until resolved to ≤ Grade 2, then resume at a reduced dose. Discontinue if it recurs.
    - **Hypoxia (Life-threatening Grade 4):** Permanently discontinue.
    - **Other Grade 3 Reactions:** Withhold until resolved to ≤ Grade 2; consider resuming at a reduced dose. Permanently discontinue upon recurrence.
    - **Other Grade 4 Reactions:** Permanently discontinue.

## 4. Contraindications
- None specified in available sources [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Anaemia due to Decreased Erythropoietin:** Belzutifan causes a dose-dependent decrease in erythropoietin (EPO), leading to severe anaemia. In VHL trials, 90.2% of patients developed anaemia (9.8% Grade 3). In advanced RCC, 83% developed anaemia (32% Grade 3). Monitor haemoglobin levels prior to initiation and periodically during treatment. 
- **Hypoxia:** Severe hypoxia may occur, requiring supplemental oxygen, hospitalisation, or treatment discontinuation. Monitor oxygen saturation via pulse oximetry before and during therapy. Advise patients to report respiratory symptoms immediately.
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Animal studies indicate belzutifan causes fetal harm and loss at exposures lower than the human recommended dose. Verify pregnancy status before initiation.
- **UGT2B17 and CYP2C19 Poor Metabolisers:** Patients with reduced activity in both enzymes have significantly higher belzutifan exposure (up to 3.3-fold), increasing the risk and severity of adverse reactions (e.g., anaemia, hypoxia). Close monitoring is required for these populations.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** 
    - Blood: Anaemia (83–90%).
    - General: Fatigue (31–70%), peripheral oedema (13%).
    - Nervous System: Dizziness (12–44%), headache (44%).
    - Gastrointestinal: Nausea (18–36%), constipation (21%), abdominal pain (18%), diarrhoea (15%).
    - Respiratory: Dyspnoea (15–23%), hypoxia (15–29%).
    - Musculoskeletal: Myalgia (25%), arthralgia (21%), back pain (13%).
    - Investigations: Weight increased (16%), ALT increased (18%), AST increased (11%).
    - Other: Vision blurred (18%), hypertension (16%), insomnia (13%), anxiety (11%).
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaemia, hypoxia, intracranial haemorrhage, and urinary tract infection [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **Effect of Belzutifan on Other Drugs:** Belzutifan is a weak inducer of **CYP3A4**. Co-administration with CYP3A4 substrates (e.g., midazolam) reduces their plasma concentration.
    - **Hormonal Contraceptives:** Belzutifan reduces the efficacy of hormonal contraceptives, potentially leading to pregnancy. Use effective non-hormonal contraception.
    - **Transporters:** Belzutifan inhibits **MATE2K** (in vitro) and may inhibit **OCT1** and **MATE1**. It may increase exposure to drugs cleared by these transporters.
- **Effect of Other Drugs on Belzutifan:** 
    - Belzutifan is a substrate of **UGT2B17**, **CYP2C19**, and to a lesser extent, **CYP3A4**. 
    - **UGT2B17 or CYP2C19 Inhibitors:** Expected to increase belzutifan exposure. 
    - **CYP2C19 Inducers:** Expected to reduce belzutifan exposure.
    - **Dual Poor Metabolisers:** Co-administration with CYP3A4 inhibitors in patients who are poor metabolisers of both UGT2B17 and CYP2C19 is expected to further increase exposure.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Belzutifan is suspected to cause birth defects. Females of reproductive potential must use effective non-hormonal contraception during treatment and for 1 week after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on the presence of belzutifan in human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in breastfed infants, breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for 1 week after the last dose.
- **Fertility:** May impair fertility in both males and females. Animal studies showed non-reversible testicular atrophy and hypospermia. Males with female partners of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for 1 week after the final dose.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients under 18 years of age [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment is required. In the LITESPARK-005 study, 38% of patients were ≥65 years old; no overall differences in safety or efficacy were observed compared to younger patients.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild to moderate impairment (eGFR 30–89 mL/min). Not studied in severe renal impairment [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild hepatic impairment (total bilirubin ≤ULN with AST >ULN or total bilirubin >1 to 1.5x ULN). Not studied in moderate or severe hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Belzutifan is a potent and selective inhibitor of HIF-2α. In the absence of functional VHL protein, HIF-2α accumulates and translocates to the nucleus, driving genes for angiogenesis and tumour growth. Belzutifan blocks the interaction between HIF-2α and HIF-1β, reducing the expression of these target genes.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Reduces circulating erythropoietin (EPO) levels in a dose-dependent manner. Maximum suppression occurs within 2 weeks of dosing. No clinically relevant effects on the QTc interval were observed at the 120 mg dose.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Tmax is 1–2 hours. Steady-state reached in ~3 days. Food (high-fat/high-calorie) delays Tmax but has no meaningful effect on AUC.
    - **Distribution:** Vd is 120 L; plasma protein binding is 45%.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by UGT2B17 and CYP2C19; minor metabolism by CYP3A4.
    - **Elimination:** Half-life is ~14 hours. Clearance is 5.89 L/hr. Excreted in urine (49.6%) and faeces (51.7%), mostly as metabolites.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **VHL-Associated Tumours (LITESPARK-004):** A phase 2 open-label trial (n=61) showed an Objective Response Rate (ORR) of 59.0% in VHL-associated RCC. The median time to response was 47.6 weeks. DCR was 98.4%. High response rates were also seen in pNET (90%) and CNS haemangioblastomas (38%).
- **Advanced RCC (LITESPARK-005):** A phase 3 trial (n=746) comparing belzutifan to everolimus. Belzutifan demonstrated a statistically significant improvement in Progression-Free Survival (PFS) (HR 0.75; p=0.00077) and a higher ORR (22% vs 3.5%). Median OS was 21 months for belzutifan vs 18 months for everolimus (not statistically significant).
