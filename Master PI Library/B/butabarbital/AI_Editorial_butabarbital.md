# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of this writing, only the United States PI was provided for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It should be administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). It should be administered as a slow bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Loading Doses:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by equivalent doses over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours, and never less frequently than every 6 hours [US].
- **Monitoring:** Plasma carnitine concentrations should be obtained prior to starting parenteral therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Adjustments:** For ESRD patients, dose reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) levels [US].

### Compatibility
- Levocarnitine is compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These often occur within minutes of administration, particularly in ESRD patients. Treatment must be discontinued immediately upon the occurrence of a severe reaction [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients receiving levocarnitine, regardless of whether they have a pre-existing seizure disorder. In those with a known history, the frequency or severity of seizures may increase [US].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. High doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are frequently confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disorders.

### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
| Body System | Adverse Event (≥5% Frequency) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%), Nausea (8%), Abdominal pain (9%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%) |
| **Nervous System** | Dizziness (13%), Headache (22%), Paresthesia (5%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (23%), Cough increase (9%), Dyspnea (7%) |
| **Body as a Whole** | Chest pain (12%), Injection site reaction (33%), Infection (15%), Pain (30%) |

### Serious/Other Notable Effects
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), Hypervolemia (5%) [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis and body odor have been reported less frequently [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalised Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concurrently with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustment [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned/specified in updated labelling]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is not specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. The benefits of maternal supplementation must be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was observed in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg (see Section 3). Specific age-range safety profiles beyond metabolic/ESRD indications are not detailed [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific dose adjustments for the elderly are not provided in the source documentation, though general caution in dosing should apply [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. However, chronic high doses of **oral** levocarnitine should be avoided in patients with severely compromised renal function due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrates for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine helps clear accumulated toxic acyl-CoA compounds by forming acylcarnitine, which is then rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following a single IV dose (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours, and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, IV levocarnitine (20 mg/kg post-dialysis) increased mean trough plasma concentrations from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, and 40 mg/kg) demonstrated dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine levels. Clinical outcomes regarding signs and symptoms of carnitine deficiency in this population have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation lacks preservatives [US].
