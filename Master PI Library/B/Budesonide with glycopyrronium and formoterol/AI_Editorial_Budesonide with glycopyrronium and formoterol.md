# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided for the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI when prescribing or administering this medication in a specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (Generic/INN)
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US]
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US]
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg [US].
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - **Initial/Crisis Dosing:** A loading dose is often administered in patients with severe metabolic crisis, followed by equivalent doses every 24 hours. Dosing intervals should be every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered if clinically required [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations (targeted range: 40 µmol/L to 50 µmol/L) [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect parenteral products for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of IV administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue the drug immediately [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known seizure disorder, levocarnitine may increase the frequency or severity of seizures [US].
- **Monitoring:** 
    - **Metabolic Disorders:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (targeted free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
    - **ESRD:** Monitor trough plasma levels before adjusting doses [US].
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation):** Note that chronic high-dose administration of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (>5% in ESRD Trials):** 
    - **Body as a whole:** Abdominal pain, headache, infection, injection site reaction, fever, asthenia, chest pain, and "flu syndrome" [US].
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (up to 21%), hypotension, tachycardia [US].
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhea (up to 35%), vomiting (up to 21%), nausea, anorexia [US].
    - **Nervous:** Dizziness, paresthesia [US].
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis, cough, dyspnea, rhinitis [US].
- **Less Frequent/General:** Transient nausea and vomiting, body odor, and gastritis [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Clinical Management:** Monitor INR levels upon initiation of levocarnitine or after dosage adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not assigned a letter category in available sources, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits (up to 3.8x human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm [US].
    - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration. **Clinical Management:** Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is based on mg/kg (see Section 3).
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - Levocarnitine is indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD patients on dialysis. 
    - Levocarnitine is easily removed from plasma via dialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with specific organic acidopathies by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Excretion:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In clinical trials, ESRD patients undergoing hemodialysis for at least 6 months received 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly after dialysis. After 8 weeks, mean trough levocarnitine concentrations increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L. While it effectively increases carnitine concentrations, the effect on clinical outcomes in this specific population remains to be fully determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to expiry date on packaging.
- **Special Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only (Rx-only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
