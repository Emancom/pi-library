# Editorial Product Information: Bromocriptine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following official Product Information (PI) document:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: Parlodel (Bromocriptine mesilate) 2.5 mg tablets.
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements. 
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official Product Information approved in a specific jurisdiction for the purpose of prescribing or dispensing. At the time of writing, only the Australian PI was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Bromocriptine mesilate (a peptide ergot alkaloid).
- **Chemical Class**: Dopamine receptor agonist.
- **Brand Names**: **Parlodel** [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: N04BC01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**: 
    - **Oral Tablet**: 2.5 mg bromocriptine (as 2.9 mg mesilate) [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Sandoz Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inhibition of Physiological Lactation**: Prevention of the onset of lactation in the puerperium for clearly defined medical reasons (not for suppressing established lactation) [Australian].
- **Hyperprolactinaemia**: Treatment where surgery or radiotherapy is not indicated or has been unsuccessful. Includes the management of prolactin-secreting microadenomas or macroadenomas (prolactinomas) to reduce tumour size or facilitate removal [Australian].
- **Acromegaly**: Adjunctive therapy when surgery or radiotherapy is refused, unsuccessful, or pending [Australian].
- **Parkinson's Disease**: Treatment of idiopathic or post-encephalitic Parkinson's disease, either as monotherapy or in combination with other agents [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
**Bromocriptine** should always be taken with food to reduce the incidence of nausea.

### Dosing by Indication [Australian]
- **Inhibition of Lactation**: 2.5 mg twice daily with meals for 14 days. Start as soon as possible post-parturition (no sooner than 4 hours after delivery and once vital signs are stable).
- **Hyperprolactinaemia**: 
    - Initial: 1.25 mg (½ tablet) 2 to 3 times daily. 
    - Titration: Gradually increase to 2.5 mg 2 to 3 times daily as required. 
    - Prolactinomas: Max recommended dose is 15 mg daily in divided doses.
- **Acromegaly**: 
    - Initial: 1.25 mg at night. 
    - Titration: Increase over 1–2 weeks to 10 mg daily. 
    - Maintenance: Usually 10–30 mg daily; do not exceed 40 mg daily. Dose should be divided into four equal doses.
- **Parkinson's Disease**:
    - Initial: 1.25 mg once or twice daily for the first week.
    - Titration: Increase by no more than 1.25 mg every week based on tolerance. 
    - Maintenance: Typically 5–40 mg/day in divided doses (usually 6–8 hourly).

### Missed Dose
Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to bromocriptine, other ergot alkaloids, or any excipients [Australian].
- Uncontrolled hypertension [Australian].
- Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (e.g., eclampsia, pre-eclampsia, pregnancy-induced hypertension) [Australian].
- Postpartum hypertension or hypertension in the puerperium [Australian].
- Coronary artery disease and other severe cardiovascular conditions [Australian].
- Symptoms or history of serious psychiatric disorders [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Postpartum Serious Adverse Events**: Rare cases of seizures, stroke, myocardial infarction, and hypertension have been reported in postpartum women using bromocriptine for lactation inhibition. Therapy should not start until vital signs have stabilised and never before 4 hours post-delivery [Australian].
- **Fibrotic Conditions**: Long-term, high-dose treatment is associated with pleural/pericardial effusions, pulmonary fibrosis, and retroperitoneal fibrosis. Monitor for back pain, lower limb oedema, or impaired kidney function. Discontinue if fibrotic changes are suspected [Australian].
- **Cardiac Valve Fibrosis**: Very rare reports associated with long-term use [Australian].
- **Impulse Control Disorders**: Patients may experience pathological gambling, increased libido, hypersexuality, or compulsive spending/eating. Dose reduction or tapering should be considered if these occur [Australian].
- **Sudden Sleep Onset/Somnolence**: Particularly in Parkinson’s patients. Patients must be warned about driving or operating machinery [Australian].
- **Hypotension**: Postural hypotension is common, especially during initial dosing. Collapse has occurred following initial doses [Australian].
- **Visual Field Monitoring**: In macroprolactinoma patients, secondary loss of visual fields may occur despite normalised prolactin levels; monitoring is essential [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal Effects**: Gastric ulceration and fatal GI haemorrhage have been reported, particularly in acromegalic patients [Australian].
- **Excipients**: Contains lactose; not recommended for patients with galactose intolerance or severe lactase deficiency [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (>10%) | Nausea, headache, dizziness [Australian] |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Somnolence, nasal congestion, constipation, vomiting [Australian] |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Confusional state, psychomotor hyperactivity, hallucinations, dyskinesia, hypotension, orthostatic hypotension, dry mouth, allergic dermatitis, alopecia, muscle spasms, fatigue [Australian] |
| **Rare** (<0.1%) | Psychotic disorders, insomnia, paraesthesia, visual impairment, tinnitus, tachycardia, bradycardia, arrhythmia, pericardial effusion, pulmonary fibrosis, pleural effusion, dyspnoea, gastrointestinal ulcer, GI haemorrhage, retroperitoneal fibrosis, peripheral oedema [Australian] |
| **Very Rare** (<0.01%) | Cardiac valve fibrosis, sudden sleep onset, reversible pallor induced by cold (Raynaud's), Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome-like symptoms on abrupt withdrawal [Australian] |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors**: Macrolides (e.g., erythromycin), azole antimycotics, and HIV protease inhibitors may increase bromocriptine levels. Octreotide increases bioavailability by ~40% [Australian].
- **Dopamine Antagonists**: Antipsychotics (phenothiazines, butyrophenones) and metoclopramide/domperidone may reduce the efficacy of bromocriptine [Australian].
- **Sympathomimetics**: Co-administration (e.g., phenylpropanolamine) may lead to hypertension and severe headache [Australian].
- **Sumatriptan**: May potentiate vasospastic reactions [Australian].
- **Other Ergot Alkaloids**: May increase dopaminergic side effects [Australian].
- **Alcohol**: Reduces tolerability to bromocriptine [Australian].
- **Antihypertensives**: Possible additive hypotensive effects [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **Australian Category A**: Bromocriptine has been taken by a large number of pregnant women without an increase in malformations or spontaneous abortions. 
    - **Policy**: Discontinue when pregnancy is confirmed unless there is a medical reason to continue. Close supervision is required in patients with pituitary adenomas, as these may expand during pregnancy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding**: Contraindicated in mothers who wish to breastfeed as the drug prevents lactation [Australian].
- **Fertility**: Treatment commonly restores fertility in hyperprolactinaemic patients. Women not wishing to conceive should use contraception [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Not recommended for children [Australian].
- **Elderly Use**: Start at the lower end of the dosing range due to the higher frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment**: No specific studies performed [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Extensively metabolised by the liver; treat with care and consider dose adjustment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: A dopamine receptor agonist that stimulates central dopamine receptors (D2). It inhibits prolactin secretion from the anterior pituitary without affecting other pituitary hormones (except growth hormone, which it may suppress in acromegalic patients) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics** [Australian]:
    - **Absorption**: Rapidly absorbed; absorption half-life ~0.3 hours.
    - **Bioavailability**: Only ~7% reaches systemic circulation due to high first-pass metabolism.
    - **Distribution**: 96% plasma protein bound.
    - **Metabolism**: Extensively metabolised by the liver.
    - **Elimination**: Primarily via bile into the liver (94%); only 6% eliminated renally. Parent drug half-life is 2–8 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Hyperprolactinaemia/Acromegaly**: Evidence shows regression in tumour size for prolactinomas and reduction of growth hormone levels in ~50% of acromegalic patients [Australian].
- **Parkinson's Disease**: Studies show efficacy as monotherapy or in combination with levodopa, allowing for levodopa dose reduction [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage**: Store below 25°C. Protect from light [Australian].
- **Shelf Life**: Refer to packaging or regional register (e.g., ARTG in Australia) [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule**: 
    - **Australian**: Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
