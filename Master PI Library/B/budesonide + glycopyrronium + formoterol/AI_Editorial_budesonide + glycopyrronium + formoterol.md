# Editorial Product Information: budesonide + glycopyrronium + formoterol

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **budesonide + glycopyrronium + formoterol**
- Chemical class:
  - budesonide: inhaled corticosteroid (ICS)
  - glycopyrronium (as glycopyrronium bromide): long-acting muscarinic antagonist (LAMA; antimuscarinic)
  - formoterol fumarate dihydrate: long-acting β2-adrenergic agonist (LABA)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: inhalation (pressurised metered dose inhaler; route specified as inhalation)

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Maintenance treatment to prevent exacerbations and relieve symptoms in adults with moderate, severe, or very severe chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) who require combination therapy with an inhaled corticosteroid (ICS), a long-acting β2-agonist (LABA), and a long-acting muscarinic antagonist (LAMA).
- Not indicated for initiation of COPD therapy (i.e., not for patients who do not already require triple therapy).
- Not indicated for the treatment of asthma.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Recommended dose in adults (≥18 years): two actuations twice daily (two actuations in the morning and two actuations in the evening).
- Patients should be advised not to exceed two actuations twice daily.

### Strengths (delivered dose per actuation and per recommended dose)
- Delivered dose per actuation (as labelled in the available source):
  - budesonide: 160 µg per actuation (320 µg per 2-actuation dose)
  - glycopyrronium bromide: 9 µg per actuation (18 µg per 2-actuation dose); equivalent to glycopyrronium 7.2 µg per actuation (14.4 µg per 2-actuation dose)
  - formoterol fumarate dihydrate: 5 µg per actuation (10 µg per 2-actuation dose); equivalent to formoterol fumarate 4.8 µg per actuation (9.6 µg per 2-actuation dose)

### Dosing by indication
- COPD maintenance therapy as above. No alternative dosing schedules, titrations, or paediatric dosing provided for other indications in available sources.

### Administration instructions
- For inhalation use only.
- Patients should be taught correct inhaler technique and instructed to read the Instructions for Use provided with the product.
- Patients who have difficulty coordinating actuation and inhalation may use a spacer device to assist delivery.
- Not for acute relief: this product is not indicated for rescue therapy in acute bronchospasm or acute COPD exacerbations.

### Missed dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to budesonide, glycopyrronium (including glycopyrronium bromide), formoterol, or any excipient present in the product.
- No additional jurisdiction-specific contraindications are specified in the available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Treatment of COPD should follow current national guidelines and include a personalised action plan developed with the treating clinician.
- Not indicated for asthma; do not substitute for asthma therapy.
- Deterioration of disease: if treatment is ineffective at the highest recommended dose or there is sudden/progressive loss of control, urgent medical assessment is required. Consider need for additional therapy (e.g., oral corticosteroids, antibiotics if infection suspected).
- Transfer from systemic oral corticosteroids (OCS): exercise caution when switching patients from chronic OCS to this inhaled combination. Patients previously on high-dose or prolonged systemic corticosteroids may have impaired adrenal function and remain at risk of adrenal insufficiency; consider additional systemic corticosteroid cover during periods of stress or elective surgery.
- Paradoxical bronchospasm: may occur with inhaled medicines, potentially life-threatening. If it occurs, discontinue this product and institute alternative therapy.
- Not for acute use: not a rescue inhaler.

### Cardiovascular and related precautions
- Cardiovascular effects (including arrhythmias) may occur with muscarinic antagonists and sympathomimetics (i.e., glycopyrronium and formoterol). Use caution in patients with clinically significant cardiovascular disease (ischemic heart disease, recent myocardial infarction, cardiomyopathy, cardiac arrhythmias, severe heart failure) and in thyrotoxicosis.
- Exercise caution in patients with known or suspected QTc prolongation (congenital or drug-induced) and when co-administered with other QT-prolonging medicines.

### Systemic corticosteroid effects
- Systemic effects of ICS can occur, particularly at high doses and with prolonged use. Possible effects include Cushing's syndrome, Cushingoid features, adrenal suppression, decreased bone mineral density, cataract, and glaucoma. Consider bone health in patients on high doses or prolonged therapy with risk factors for osteoporosis.

### Ophthalmic effects
- Glaucoma, increased intraocular pressure and cataracts have been reported with long-term inhaled corticosteroid use and with anticholinergic use. Rare reports of central serous chorioretinopathy (CSCR) have been associated with corticosteroid exposure.
- Use with caution in patients with narrow-angle glaucoma; consider ophthalmology referral if ocular symptoms develop or for long-term users.

### Hypokalaemia
- β2-agonists may cause hypokalaemia, which may increase arrhythmia risk. The decrease is usually transient and not requiring supplementation. Hypokalaemia may be potentiated in severe COPD by hypoxia or concomitant medications (e.g., non–potassium-sparing diuretics).

### Hyperglycaemia
- High doses of inhaled β2-agonists can increase plasma glucose. Clinically relevant hyperglycaemia was not observed in clinical studies at the recommended dose.

### Anticholinergic (antimuscarinic) effects
- Due to anticholinergic activity, use with caution in patients with symptomatic prostatic hyperplasia, urinary retention, or narrow-angle glaucoma.

### Pneumonia
- Use of inhaled corticosteroids in COPD has been associated with an increased incidence of pneumonia, including pneumonia requiring hospitalisation. Some evidence suggests risk increases with higher steroid dose. Clinicians should remain vigilant because clinical features can overlap with COPD exacerbations.
- Risk factors for pneumonia: current smoking, older age, low BMI, and more severe COPD.

### Oropharyngeal candidiasis
- Local candidiasis can occur with inhaled corticosteroid therapy. Rinsing mouth with water and swallowing after inhalation reduces risk. Treat infections with appropriate local or systemic antifungals if they occur.

### Hepatic impairment
- Budesonide and formoterol are predominantly metabolised hepatically. Severe hepatic impairment may lead to increased systemic exposure of budesonide and formoterol. Use only if expected benefit outweighs risk; monitor patients closely.

### Renal impairment
- Glycopyrronium is primarily renally excreted. Patients with severe renal impairment (eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m2) should be treated only if expected benefit outweighs risk; monitor closely.

### Use in elderly
- No dosage adjustment is necessary for elderly patients; monitor as clinically indicated.

### Use in paediatric patients
- No relevant use in children and adolescents (<18 years) for the COPD indication.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor for signs of pneumonia and COPD exacerbation.
- Consider monitoring for systemic corticosteroid effects (adrenal suppression), bone mineral density, intraocular pressure, and visual changes with long-term use.
- Monitor serum potassium in patients at risk of hypokalaemia.
- Monitor blood glucose in patients at risk of hyperglycaemia.

### Study-specific pneumonia incidence
- KRONOS (24-week lung function study subset): pneumonia incidence with budesonide + glycopyrronium + formoterol was 1.9% in that study.
- ETHOS (52-week exacerbation study subset): pneumonia incidence with budesonide + glycopyrronium + formoterol was 4.2% in that study.

### Bone mineral density and ocular safety (subset)
- In a subset treated up to 52 weeks, budesonide + glycopyrronium + formoterol was non-inferior to glycopyrronium + formoterol for primary bone mineral density and ocular endpoints. Reported treatment differences (95% CI): bone mineral density -0.5% [-1.4%, 0.5%]; ocular endpoint 0.1% [0.0%, 0.2%].

### Serious adverse reactions
- Serious events reported include pneumonia (some requiring hospitalisation), cardiac arrhythmias, and severe hypersensitivity reactions; clinicians should be alert to these potential serious outcomes.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### General
- No formal in vivo drug interaction studies with the combination product were conducted in the available sources. Guidance is derived from known metabolic and elimination pathways of components and from limited interaction assessments.

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Budesonide: primarily metabolised by CYP3A4. Co-administration with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., itraconazole, ketoconazole, HIV protease inhibitors, cobicistat-containing products) is expected to increase systemic exposure and the risk of systemic corticosteroid effects. This is particularly relevant during long-term co-administration (limited concern for very short courses of strong inhibitors).
- Glycopyrronium: predominantly eliminated via renal excretion. In vitro, glycopyrronium is a substrate for renal transporters OCT2 and MATE1/2K. Co-administration of cimetidine (a probe OCT2/MATE inhibitor) with inhaled glycopyrronium produced a limited increase in total systemic exposure (AUC0–t increased by ~22%) and a decrease in renal clearance by ~23%.
- Formoterol: does not inhibit CYP450 enzymes at therapeutic concentrations.
- Budesonide and glycopyrronium do not inhibit or induce CYP450 enzymes at therapeutic concentrations.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions and clinically significant combinations
- Concomitant use with other long-acting β2-agonists (LABA) and/or long-acting muscarinic antagonists (LAMA) is not recommended; additive pharmacodynamic effects and increased adverse reactions may occur.
- Beta-adrenergic blockers (including ophthalmic preparations) may reduce or inhibit the bronchodilator effect of formoterol.
- Concomitant use with other beta-adrenergic agents can have additive effects; exercise caution.
- Medicines known to prolong the QTc interval (including monoamine oxidase inhibitors and tricyclic antidepressants) may potentiate cardiac effects; use with caution.
- Drugs that may produce hypokalaemia (e.g., non–potassium-sparing diuretics) may potentiate β2-agonist–induced hypokalaemia.
- Co-administration with other antimuscarinic or sympathomimetic agents has not been studied and is not recommended because of potential additive adverse effects.

### Management notes
- Avoid co-prescribing other LABA or LAMA products.
- Consider alternative therapies or enhanced monitoring when co-administering with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors, agents that affect renal excretion, QT-prolonging agents, or drugs that can produce hypokalaemia.
- Monitor serum potassium and ECG as clinically indicated in patients at risk.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Pregnancy data: No adequate data from controlled studies in pregnant women on the combination product.
- Animal data and component-specific data:
  - Budesonide: data from >2,500 exposed human pregnancies with inhaled budesonide do not indicate increased teratogenic risk. Animal studies: subcutaneous budesonide caused malformations and embryofetal loss in rats and rabbits at doses that in some studies were comparable on an mg/m2 basis, although inhalation studies in rats showed no malformations at inhaled doses up to 250 µg/kg/day.
  - Glycopyrronium: not teratogenic in rats or rabbits following subcutaneous administration at tested doses; fetal weight reductions at very high doses in animals.
  - Formoterol: no teratogenic effects in rats at tested doses; inconsistent fetal cardiovascular malformations in rabbits in one oral study at very high doses; decreased birth weight and increased peri/postnatal mortality observed in rats at higher doses in late gestation.
- As a β2-agonist, formoterol may inhibit labour because of uterine smooth muscle relaxation.
- Recommendation: Use during pregnancy only if expected benefits outweigh potential risks.
- Jurisdictional pregnancy category:
  - [Australian] Pregnancy category: B3 (as stated in the available source).

### Breastfeeding
- Budesonide: excreted in human breast milk after inhalation; a pharmacokinetic study did not detect budesonide in nursing infant blood samples. Estimated infant plasma concentration is <0.17% of maternal plasma concentration; no anticipated effects in breast-fed infants at therapeutic maternal doses.
- Glycopyrronium and formoterol: it is not known whether they are excreted in human milk. Animal data show significant lactational excretion of glycopyrronium in rats (milk concentrations up to 10× maternal blood) and small amounts of formoterol in rat milk.
- Recommendation: Breastfeeding mothers should use the combination only if the expected benefit to the mother outweighs any possible risk to the child.

### Fertility
- Budesonide: no effects on male/female fertility in rats at tested subcutaneous doses.
- Glycopyrronium bromide: no effects on fertility in rats at tested subcutaneous doses.
- Formoterol: reduced fertility in male rats at high oral doses (15 mg/kg/day) but not at lower doses; no effect on female fertility at tested doses.
- Clinical relevance: doses without effect in animals produced very large multiples of human systemic exposure.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- No relevant use in children and adolescents (<18 years) in the COPD indication. Not indicated in paediatric populations for COPD.

### Elderly
- No dosage adjustment required in elderly patients. Clinical monitoring as appropriate.

### Renal impairment
- Formal pharmacokinetic studies with the combination product in renal impairment were not conducted.
- Glycopyrronium is primarily renally excreted. Patients with severe renal impairment (eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m2) should be treated only if expected benefit outweighs risk; monitor closely.

### Hepatic impairment
- Formal pharmacokinetic studies with the combination product in hepatic impairment were not conducted.
- Budesonide and formoterol are predominantly metabolised by the liver; severe hepatic disease may result in accumulation. Use only if benefit outweighs risk and monitor patients carefully.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **budesonide**: inhaled corticosteroid — anti-inflammatory effects via glucocorticoid receptor agonism, reducing airway inflammation.
- **glycopyrronium**: long-acting muscarinic antagonist (anticholinergic) — blocks muscarinic receptors in airway smooth muscle to reduce bronchoconstriction.
- **formoterol**: long-acting β2-adrenoceptor agonist — induces bronchodilation through stimulation of β2-receptors in airway smooth muscle.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption/distribution: Not specified in available sources in quantitative detail (e.g., bioavailability, Tmax, half-life).
- Metabolism:
  - Budesonide: primarily metabolised by CYP3A4.
  - Formoterol: predominantly cleared by hepatic metabolism (specifics not provided).
  - Glycopyrronium: eliminated mainly by renal excretion.
- Drug transporter and interaction notes:
  - Glycopyrronium is a substrate for renal transporters OCT2 and MATE1/2K; co-administration of cimetidine increased systemic AUC by ~22% and decreased renal clearance by ~23% in an interaction assessment.
- Excretion:
  - Glycopyrronium: primarily renal.
  - Budesonide and formoterol: hepatic metabolism major route; specific excretion data not provided.
- CYP enzyme interactions:
  - Formoterol does not inhibit CYP450 enzymes at therapeutic concentrations.
  - Budesonide and glycopyrronium do not inhibit or induce CYP450 enzymes at therapeutically relevant concentrations.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Bronchodilation and anti-inflammatory control are achieved through the combined pharmacologic actions of ICS, LABA, and LAMA. Specific PD parameters (e.g., onset & duration of bronchodilation from trials) are not fully detailed in the available sources.

## 12. Clinical Trials
### Pivotal programme (summary as reported in available sources)
- Program included multiple Phase 3 studies evaluating lung function, exacerbation outcomes, and long-term safety.
- KRONOS: 24-week lung function study; included 639 patients with COPD in the pivotal lung function study.
  - Confirmed pneumonia incidence in KRONOS: 1.9% with the combination regimen.
- ETHOS: 52-week exacerbation study; included ~2,144 patients (reported in available sources for the combination arm).
  - Confirmed pneumonia incidence in ETHOS: 4.2% with the combination regimen.
- Long-term safety extension studies: PT010007 (subset of Japanese patients; 139 patients) and PT010008 (subset assessing bone mineral density and ocular safety; 194 patients); these were 28-week extensions following KRONOS.
- Pooled safety population (ETHOS and KRONOS follow-up studies) comprised N values reported in available sources; adverse event frequencies presented in Section 7 derive from this pooled dataset.
- Bone mineral density and ocular safety (subset in PT010008): combination therapy was non-inferior to glycopyrronium + formoterol for bone mineral density and ocular endpoints over up to 52 weeks; treatment differences reported (see Section 7).

### Primary outcomes and design notes
- KRONOS: lung function outcomes (details of specific endpoints such as FEV1 not provided in the available sources).
- ETHOS: exacerbation rate outcomes over 52 weeks.
- Safety and adverse event monitoring conducted across studies; pooled analyses used to characterise adverse event frequencies.
