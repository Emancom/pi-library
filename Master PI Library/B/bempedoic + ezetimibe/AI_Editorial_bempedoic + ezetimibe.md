# Editorial Product Information: bempedoic + ezetimibe

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **bempedoic + ezetimibe**
- Chemical class:
  - bempedoic acid: adenosine triphosphate citrate lyase (ACL) inhibitor
  - ezetimibe: dietary cholesterol absorption inhibitor
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 2020.
- Date of last revision/update of source PI provided: 01/2026.
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- As an adjunct to diet and exercise to reduce low‑density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL‑C) in adults with hypercholesterolemia, including heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HeFH).
- Reduction of major adverse cardiovascular events (cardiovascular death, myocardial infarction, stroke, or coronary revascularization) is an approved indication for the bempedoic acid component in adults at increased risk for these events who are unable to take recommended statin therapy (including those not taking a statin). [US‑approved]

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Recommended dose: one tablet once daily containing 180 mg bempedoic acid and 10 mg ezetimibe, taken orally.

### Administration instructions
- The product may be taken with or without food.
- Swallow whole.
- If a dose is missed, take the missed dose as soon as possible; do not double the next dose.
- Reassess lipid levels 8–12 weeks after initiation.

### Drug‑specific coadministration timing
- Coadministration with bile acid sequestrants (e.g., cholestyramine): administer the combination at least 2 hours before or at least 4 hours after the bile acid sequestrant.

### Titration schedules
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to ezetimibe or bempedoic acid or any excipient in the product.
- Serious hypersensitivity reactions including anaphylaxis, angioedema, rash, and urticaria have been reported; prior hypersensitivity is a contraindication.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- None specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Hyperuricemia: bempedoic acid may raise serum uric acid (likely via renal tubular OAT2 inhibition). New or worsening hyperuricemia and gout have been observed.
  - Assess serum uric acid when clinically indicated and advise patients to report symptoms of hyperuricemia/gout.
- Tendon rupture or tendon injury: bempedoic acid is associated with tendon rupture; cases reported within weeks to months of therapy initiation.
  - Discontinue immediately if tendon rupture occurs.
  - Avoid in patients with prior tendon disorders or previous tendon rupture.
  - Consider discontinuation if tendinitis (joint pain, swelling, inflammation) occurs.
  - Increased risk in patients >60 years, those taking systemic corticosteroids or fluoroquinolones, and in those with renal failure.
- Hepatic transaminase elevations: increases in AST/ALT reported; usually transient but monitor clinically.
- Hematologic effects: decreases in hemoglobin and leukocyte counts and increases in platelet counts observed; monitor as clinically indicated.
- Renal effects: small mean increases in serum creatinine and BUN were observed.
- Cholelithiasis: reported with bempedoic acid and ezetimibe coadministration, particularly when combined with fibrates; be alert for biliary symptoms.
- Hypersensitivity: serious allergic reactions reported with either component.

### Monitoring requirements
- Lipids: obtain lipid profile 8–12 weeks after starting therapy and as clinically indicated thereafter.
- Uric acid: assess when clinically indicated; monitor for gout symptoms.
- Liver tests: monitor hepatic transaminases as clinically indicated given reported elevations.
- Hematology: monitor hemoglobin, leukocyte and platelet counts if clinically indicated.
- Renal function: monitor serum creatinine/BUN as clinically indicated.
- When used with cyclosporine: monitor cyclosporine concentrations.
- When used concomitantly with fibrates: measure triglycerides and HDL‑C about 4 weeks after initiation of combined therapy and periodically thereafter.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Discontinue therapy for tendon rupture.
- In patients with suspected cholelithiasis on combination therapy with fenofibrate, perform gallbladder evaluation and consider alternative lipid‑lowering therapy.
- If marked increases in triglycerides or significant decreases in HDL‑C occur on concomitant fibrate therapy, discontinue one of the agents based on clinical judgment and monitor until levels return to baseline.

## 7. Adverse Effects
- All adverse reactions reported in the source product information are included below. Where source provided numerical incidence, frequencies are categorized using: very common (>10%), common (1–10%), uncommon (0.1–1%), rare (<0.1%), very rare (<0.01%). Postmarketing events with unspecified frequency are listed as "frequency not established."

### Summary of adverse reaction rates reported in clinical trials
- In placebo‑controlled primary hypercholesterolemia trials (bempedoic acid 180 mg daily; N=2,009), treatment duration ~52 weeks: discontinuation due to adverse reactions 11% (bempedoic acid) vs 8% (placebo).
- In a cardiovascular outcomes trial (median exposure 3.1 years; bempedoic acid N=7,001, placebo N=6,964), discontinuation due to adverse reactions 11% (bempedoic acid) vs 10% (placebo).
- In a 4‑arm factorial trial including the combination (12 weeks), 85 patients received bempedoic+ezetimibe; discontinuation due to adverse reactions on combination 8% vs 5% placebo.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Simvastatin:
  - Clinical effect: concomitant use increases simvastatin concentration and may increase risk of statin‑related myopathy.
  - Management: avoid concomitant use with simvastatin doses greater than 20 mg daily.
- Pravastatin:
  - Clinical effect: concomitant use increases pravastatin concentration and may increase risk of pravastatin‑related myopathy.
  - Management: avoid concomitant use with pravastatin doses greater than 40 mg daily.
- Cyclosporine:
  - Clinical effect: concomitant use increases ezetimibe and cyclosporine concentrations; increase in ezetimibe exposure may be greater in severe renal insufficiency.
  - Management: monitor cyclosporine concentrations; weigh risks/benefits of lipid effects versus increased exposures.
- Fibrates:
  - Clinical effects:
    - Both fenofibrate and ezetimibe may increase cholesterol excretion into bile and thereby increase risk of cholelithiasis.
    - Coadministration of bempedoic acid with fibrates has been associated with increased triglycerides and decreased HDL‑C in some patients.
  - Management:
    - Coadministration with fibrates other than fenofibrate is not recommended.
    - If cholelithiasis is suspected in a patient receiving the combination and fenofibrate, perform gallbladder evaluation and consider alternative lipid‑lowering therapy.
    - Monitor triglycerides and HDL‑C approximately 4 weeks after initiation of concomitant therapy and periodically thereafter; if clinically significant increases in triglycerides or decreases in HDL‑C occur, consider discontinuing one of the agents and monitor until levels return to baseline.
- Bile acid sequestrants (e.g., cholestyramine):
  - Clinical effect: coadministration decreases ezetimibe concentration and may reduce efficacy.
  - Management: administer combination product at least 2 hours before or at least 4 hours after the bile acid sequestrant.
- Other interactions:
  - No specific pharmacokinetic interaction studies with the fixed‑dose combination product were performed; listed interactions were identified from studies with the individual components.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **bempedoic acid**: inhibits ATP citrate lyase (ACL), a key enzyme in hepatic cholesterol biosynthesis, leading to reduced LDL‑C.
- **ezetimibe**: inhibits intestinal absorption of dietary and biliary cholesterol at the brush border of the small intestine.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Bempedoic acid reduces LDL‑C via ACL inhibition upstream of HMG‑CoA reductase; it can also increase serum uric acid through inhibition of renal tubular organic anion transporter 2 (OAT2), which may lead to hyperuricemia/gout in susceptible patients.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Specific numeric PK parameters (Cmax, t1/2, AUC) for the fixed‑dose combination are not specified in available sources.
- Noted PK interactions:
  - Bile acid sequestrants reduce ezetimibe exposure (administration timing recommended).
  - Coadministration with cyclosporine increases ezetimibe and cyclosporine exposures.
  - Coadministration with simvastatin or pravastatin increases statin concentrations.

## 12. Clinical Trials
### Primary hypercholesterolemia trials (bempedoic acid)
- Two placebo‑controlled trials in primary hypercholesterolemia included approximately 2,009 patients treated with bempedoic acid for up to 52 weeks (median duration 52 weeks).
- Typical baseline characteristics: mean age ~65 years, majority White, ~29% female in pooled populations; most patients received maximally tolerated statin therapy.
- Adverse reactions leading to discontinuation: 11% bempedoic acid vs 8% placebo.
- Trial exclusion: patients on simvastatin ≥40 mg/day were excluded.

### Fixed‑dose combination trial (bempedoic + ezetimibe)
- A randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled, 4‑arm factorial study of 12 weeks included 85 patients randomized to the fixed‑dose combination (180 mg bempedoic acid + 10 mg ezetimibe) once daily.
- Mean age ~62 years; 51% female; 78% White; 19% Black or African American.
- At baseline, ~61% had CVD and/or HeFH.
- Adverse reactions leading to discontinuation: 8% for the combination vs 5% placebo.
- Most common adverse reactions observed with the combination (incidence ≥3% and greater than placebo) included urinary tract infection, nasopharyngitis, and constipation.

### Cardiovascular outcomes trial (bempedoic acid)
- Large outcomes trial randomized ~13,965 patients (bempedoic acid N=7,001; placebo N=6,964) with a median exposure of 3.1 years.
- The bempedoic acid component is indicated to reduce the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events in adults at increased risk who are unable to take recommended statin therapy; specific outcome event rates and relative risk reductions are not specified in available sources.
