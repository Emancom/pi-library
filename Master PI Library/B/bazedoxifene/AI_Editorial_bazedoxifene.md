# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from official Product Information (PI) documents provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory guidelines before administration.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (a naturally occurring carrier molecule involved in fatty acid metabolism).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection containing 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic management of patients with inborn metabolic errors resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. This may be administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by intravenous infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** In cases of severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance Dose:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered if clinically required [US].

### Dosing in ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Initial Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg of dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustments:** Dosing should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine levels. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are adequate [US].

### Monitoring and Administration Instructions
- **Plasma Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to starting therapy. Regular weekly and monthly monitoring is advised, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels (target free carnitine range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Visual Inspection:** Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration before use [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None Known:** There are no known contraindications listed in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions manifest within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and manage appropriately [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in the frequency or severity of events has been observed [US].
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation Caution):** While not specific to the injection, the safety of high-dose *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function is a concern due to the accumulation of metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].
- **INR Monitoring:** Increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) has been reported when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. Monitor INR regularly after initiation or dose adjustment [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects
In clinical trials of ESRD patients on hemodialysis, the following events occurred at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) [US]:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (5–21%), headache (3–37%), infection (10–24%), injection site reaction (27–38%), pain (21–35%), fever (5–12%), flu syndrome (15–29%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%), chest pain (6–15%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), nausea (5–12%), vomiting (9–21%).
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%).

### Serious and Post-Marketing Adverse Effects
- **Anaphylaxis** and other severe hypersensitivity reactions [US].
- **Seizures** [US].
- **Body Odor:** Less frequent reports of transient nausea, vomiting, and body odor have been noted [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Concomitant use may lead to an increase in INR. Management: Frequent monitoring of INR is recommended for patients on anticoagulant therapy [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine is not bound to plasma proteins, reducing the likelihood of protein-binding displacement interactions [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified by letter, but noted as "use only if clearly needed"]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits using 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. There are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Clinical decisions should weigh the benefits of maternal therapy against potential risks to the infant from excess carnitine intake [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders (50 mg/kg) applies to paediatric patients. Specific age-related secondary deficiency in premature infants and newborns is defined by plasma concentrations below age-related norms [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific dose adjustments for the elderly are not detailed in the provided source; however, general caution regarding renal function is prudent given the drug's renal clearance [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** The drug is specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. It is easily removed by hemodialysis [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also facilitates the excretion of toxic organic acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (n=12), 20 mg/kg administered three times weekly after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis trough concentrations from ~20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks. Efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations was confirmed, though clinical outcomes related to symptoms in this specific population were not determined in these trials [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
