# Editorial Product Information: Brown Snake Antivenom

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a single, accessible reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and must not be used as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information when prescribing or administering this medication in a clinical setting.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Brown snake antivenom (equine).
- **Chemical Class:** Antivenom (purified equine globulins).
- **Brand Name:** Brown Snake Antivenom [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J06AA03.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Concentrated solution for injection. Each vial contains 1,000 units of antivenom, standardised to neutralise the average in vitro venom yield of the eastern brown snake.
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Seqirus Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Systemic Envenoming:** Indicated for the treatment of patients exhibiting manifestations of systemic envenoming following a bite by snakes belonging to the genus *Pseudonaja*. This includes the eastern brown snake (*P. textilis*), the dugite (*P. affinis*), and the gwardar/western brown snake (*P. nuchalis*) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** The initial dose is the contents of one vial (1,000 units). The dose is identical for both adults and children [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Must be administered via slow intravenous infusion. Intramuscular administration is not recommended.
    - Dilute the antivenom 1 in 10 with 0.9% w/v sodium chloride or Hartmann’s Solution. For patients at risk of fluid overload, a 1 in 5 dilution may be used under expert guidance [Australian].
    - Administer immediately after dilution; do not store diluted product [Australian].
- **Titration and Repeat Dosing:** Therapy aims to neutralise migrating venom. If the patient's condition deteriorates, additional vials may be required. Patients with severe envenoming (e.g., severe coagulopathy or myocardial depression) often require at least 3 vials; doses up to 13 vials have been recorded [Australian].
- **Clinical Management:** 
    - Do not remove first aid (pressure bandages/splints) until the antivenom is ready for infusion, as removal can trigger a sudden bolus of venom into the systemic circulation [Australian].
    - Resuscitation facilities and adrenaline must be immediately available during administration [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Absolute Contraindications:** There are no absolute contraindications to the use of this antivenom [Australian].
- **Clinical Threshold:** The product should not be administered unless there is clear evidence of systemic envenoming with the potential for serious toxic effects [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Anaphylaxis and Hypersensitivity:** As an equine-derived product, there is a significant risk of severe allergic reactions, including fatal anaphylaxis. Patients with known horse allergies or previous exposure to equine-derived products (e.g., equine Tetanus Antitoxin) are at higher risk. Discussion of risk-versus-benefit is essential prior to administration [Australian].
- **Premedication:** The use of prophylactic adrenaline and antihistamines is controversial and not universally recommended, though adrenaline must be prepared and ready for immediate use [Australian].
- **Infectious Disease Transmission:** Because this product is derived from animal plasma, the risk of transmitting infectious agents (including unknown pathogens) cannot be entirely excluded, though there are no recorded cases of viral transmission with this product [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Patients must be monitored for at least 6 hours post-administration. Suspected snake bite cases should be observed for 12 hours after the bite or after bandage removal before discharge [Australian].
- **Skin Testing:** Skin testing for allergy is unreliable and should not be performed [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions identified during post-approval use of equine antivenoms are categorised by frequency below:

| Frequency | System Organ Class | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Common** (≥1/100 to <1/10) | Immune System | Allergic reactions, anaphylactic shock, delayed serum sickness |
| | Nervous System | Headache |
| | Skin/Subcutaneous | Urticaria, rash |
| | General | Pyrexia, chills |
| **Uncommon** (≥1/1,000 to <1/100) | Gastrointestinal | Abdominal pain, vomiting, nausea, diarrhoea |
| | Musculoskeletal | Myalgia |
| | General | Local injection site reactions, chest pain |

- **Serum Sickness:** Delayed serum sickness is common (at least 5% in first-time recipients) and its incidence increases with larger volumes of antivenom. Symptoms include fever, rash, arthralgia, and lymphadenopathy, occurring days to weeks after administration [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Dynamic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Limited and inconclusive data are available. Use should involve a careful risk-benefit analysis, weighing the dangers of untreated envenoming against the potential risks of the antivenom [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** No information is available regarding use during lactation. A risk-benefit assessment is required [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** The dose for children is the same as for adults. Children may become critically ill more rapidly than adults and may require more frequent monitoring or higher doses [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No specific data available; however, patients should be monitored for renal impairment as a consequence of envenoming [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** The antivenom contains specific antibodies that neutralise the toxins present in the venom of *Pseudonaja* species [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Brown snake venom causes rapid-onset life-threatening coagulopathy, thrombocytopenia, and occasionally respiratory paralysis. The antivenom is designed to neutralise these effects [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** No specific data available regarding the absorption, distribution, metabolism, or excretion of the antivenom [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No clinical trial data are provided in the source documentation. Efficacy is based on the neutralisation of venom in vitro and clinical use [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze) [Australian].
- **Light:** Protect the vial from light [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
