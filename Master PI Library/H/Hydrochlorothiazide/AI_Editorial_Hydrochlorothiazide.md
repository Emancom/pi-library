# Editorial Product Information: Irbesartan and Hydrochlorothiazide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of the combination of irbesartan and hydrochlorothiazide for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Irbesartan (a nonpeptide angiotensin II receptor antagonist) and Hydrochlorothiazide (a thiazide diuretic).
- **Brand Name(s):** **ABISART HCTZ** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets [Australian]: 
        - 150 mg irbesartan / 12.5 mg hydrochlorothiazide (pink to red, oval-shaped, debossed ‘IH1’).
        - 300 mg irbesartan / 12.5 mg hydrochlorothiazide (yellow, oval-shaped, debossed ‘IH3’).
        - 300 mg irbesartan / 25 mg hydrochlorothiazide (pink to red, oval-shaped, debossed ‘IH2’).
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sun Pharma ANZ Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypertension:** Indicated for the treatment of hypertension. This fixed-dose combination (FDC) is not indicated for the initiation of therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** Once daily, with or without food.
- **Replacement Therapy:** The FDC may be substituted for the individual components if the patient is stable on the same dose levels.
- **Dose Titration:**
    - **150/12.5 mg:** For patients not adequately controlled on monotherapy with hydrochlorothiazide or 150 mg irbesartan.
    - **300/12.5 mg:** For patients not controlled on 300 mg irbesartan or the 150/12.5 mg FDC.
    - **300/25 mg:** For patients not controlled on the 300/12.5 mg FDC. 
- **Maximum Dose:** 300 mg irbesartan / 25 mg hydrochlorothiazide once daily.
- **Administration instructions:** Tablets should be swallowed whole. If needed, other antihypertensive agents may be co-administered.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to irbesartan, sulfonamide-derived drugs (e.g., thiazides), or any excipient (including lactose).
- **Anuria:** Contraindicated in patients with anuria.
- **Dual Blockade of RAAS:** 
    - Co-administration with aliskiren in patients with diabetes or moderate-to-severe renal impairment (CrCl < 60 mL/min).
    - Co-administration with ACE inhibitors in patients with diabetic nephropathy.
- **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated during pregnancy [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Non-melanoma Skin Cancer (NMSC):** Cumulative exposure to hydrochlorothiazide is associated with an increased risk of basal cell carcinoma and squamous cell carcinoma. Patients should monitor for new skin lesions and limit UV exposure.
- **Hypotension/Volume Depletion:** Symptomatic hypotension may occur in patients with sodium or volume depletion (e.g., vigorous diuretics, salt restriction, dialysis, or GI loss). These conditions should be corrected prior to initiation.
- **Intestinal Angioedema:** Reported with angiotensin II receptor antagonists. Symptoms include abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, and diarrhoea.
- **Renal Impairment:** Not recommended in severe renal impairment (CrCl ≤ 30 mL/min). Use with caution in patients with renal artery stenosis or severe congestive heart failure.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution in severe hepatic impairment or progressive liver disease; minor electrolyte shifts may precipitate hepatic coma.
- **Acute Respiratory Toxicity:** Severe cases of ARDS have been reported following hydrochlorothiazide intake. Discontinue immediately if ARDS is suspected.
- **Ocular Effects:** Hydrochlorothiazide may cause sulfonamide-idiosyncratic reactions resulting in choroidal effusion, acute myopia, or secondary acute angle-closure glaucoma.
- **Metabolic/Endocrine Effects:** Thiazides may impair glucose tolerance, increase cholesterol/triglyceride levels, and precipitate hyperuricaemia or gout. Irbesartan may induce hypoglycaemia in diabetic patients.
- **Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE):** Thiazides may exacerbate or activate SLE.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (>1% to <10%):** 
    - Fatigue, Dizziness (including orthostatic), Headache, Nausea/Vomiting.
    - Musculoskeletal pain, increase in blood creatinine phosphokinase (CPK).
- **Uncommon (>0.1% to <1%):** 
    - Bradycardia, tachycardia, flushing, hypotension, syncope.
    - Pruritus, skin discomfort.
    - Sexual dysfunction, libido changes, gout.
    - Diarrhoea, abdominal pain, dyspepsia, constipation, dry mouth.
    - Dyspnoea, wheezing.
- **Rare (<0.1%):**
    - Rhabdomyolysis [reported with ARBs].
- **Frequency Not Known:**
    - Non-melanoma skin cancer, ARDS, intestinal angioedema, jaundice, hepatitis, anaemia, thrombocytopenia.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Aliskiren/ACE Inhibitors:** Increased risk of hypotension, hyperkalaemia, and renal dysfunction (dual RAAS blockade).
- **Lithium:** Diuretics and ARBs reduce renal clearance of lithium, increasing the risk of toxicity.
- **NSAIDs (including COX-2 inhibitors):** May attenuate antihypertensive effects and increase the risk of renal impairment, especially in elderly or volume-depleted patients.
- **Potassium-Sparing Diuretics/Supplements:** Risk of hyperkalaemia; monitor serum potassium closely.
- **Antidiabetic Agents:** Thiazides may require dose adjustments of insulin or oral hypoglycaemics (e.g., repaglinide).
- **Colestyramine/Colestipol:** May delay or decrease hydrochlorothiazide absorption. Administer the FDC at least 1 hour before or 4 hours after these medications.
- **Alcohol/Barbiturates/Narcotics:** May potentiate orthostatic hypotension.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D [Australian]**. Drugs acting on the RAAS during the second and third trimesters can cause foetal injury and death (hypotension, neonatal skull hypoplasia, anuria, renal failure). Discontinue as soon as pregnancy is detected.
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. Hydrochlorothiazide is excreted in human milk and may inhibit lactation. Irbesartan is excreted in rat milk; human data is unavailable.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies with irbesartan alone showed no effect on fertility at high doses. Combination data is unavailable.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established.
- **Elderly Use:** No general dosage reduction is necessary for the 150/12.5 mg dose, though caution is advised with higher strengths in very elderly patients.
- **Renal Impairment:** No adjustment for CrCl > 30 mL/min. Not recommended for CrCl ≤ 30 mL/min.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for mild-to-moderate cirrhosis; use with caution in severe impairment.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Irbesartan:** Selective, high-affinity antagonist of the angiotensin II (AT1 subtype) receptor.
    - **Hydrochlorothiazide:** Thiazide diuretic that inhibits the renal tubular mechanism of electrolyte reabsorption, increasing sodium and chloride excretion.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The combination provides additive blood pressure reduction. Irbesartan tends to reverse the potassium loss associated with hydrochlorothiazide.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Oral bioavailability: 60-80% (irbesartan); 50-80% (HCTZ). Food does not affect bioavailability.
    - **Distribution:** Irbesartan is 90% protein-bound. HCTZ is 68% protein-bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Irbesartan is metabolised via CYP2C9 (glucuronide conjugation and oxidation). HCTZ is not metabolised.
    - **Elimination:** Irbesartan terminal half-life: 11-15 hours. HCTZ plasma half-life: 5-15 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- In placebo-controlled trials, the maximal blood pressure lowering effect was achieved within 6-8 weeks.
- Addition of 12.5 mg HCTZ to 300 mg irbesartan resulted in a further placebo-corrected diastolic reduction of 6.1 mmHg at trough.
- A mean 24-hour placebo-subtracted reduction of 15.8/10.0 mmHg was observed with the 150/12.5 mg dose via ambulatory monitoring [Australian].
