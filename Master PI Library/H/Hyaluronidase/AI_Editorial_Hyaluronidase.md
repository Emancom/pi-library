# Editorial Product Information: Hyaluronidase

- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a concise, structured format.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource. It is not a substitute for the official, legally binding Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Hyaluronidase (an enzyme purified from bovine sources).
- **Brand Names:** **Hyalase** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** B06AA03.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 
    - **Hyalase [Australian]:** Sterile, freeze-dried powder for injection, provided in 1 mL neutral glass ampoules containing 1,500 International Units (IU) of hyaluronidase.
- **Routes of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC), Intramuscular (IM). **Note:** Intravenous (IV) administration is contraindicated as the enzyme is rapidly inactivated.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sanofi-aventis Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Based on the available evidence [Australian-approved], hyaluronidase is indicated for:
- **Hypodermoclysis:** Facilitating the subcutaneous infusion of fluids.
- **Obstetric Anaesthesia:** Facilitating the spread of local anaesthetic agents.
- **Post-partum Haemorrhage Prevention:** Administered in combination with ergometrine to accelerate the onset of action.
- **Ophthalmology:** As an adjunct to local anaesthesia to improve diffusion and reduce tissue tension.
- **Fracture Reduction:** As an aid to local anaesthesia.
- **Radiography:** Facilitating the subcutaneous administration of radiopaque substances (e.g., in pyelography).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Instructions
- **Reconstitution:** Dissolve the 1,500 IU freeze-dried powder in approximately 1 mL of sterile pyrogen-free distilled water or directly into the solution with which it is to be combined.
- **Handling:** Solutions should be used immediately after preparation. Discard any remaining contents as the product is for single use only.
- **Stability:** Enzyme activity is lost within 12–24 hours; fresh solutions must be prepared for each administration.

### Indication-Specific Dosing [Australian]
- **Hypodermoclysis:** 1,500 IU is generally sufficient for the administration of 500–1,000 mL of most fluids. For whole blood, 3,000 IU may be required. The enzyme is typically injected through the infusion tubing at the start of the procedure.
- **Obstetric Anaesthesia:** A mixture of 1,500 IU hyaluronidase with adrenaline (0.5 mL of 1:1,000) and procaine hydrochloride (30 mL of 1% solution) is used for pudendal and perineal nerve blocks.
- **Post-partum Haemorrhage:** 1,500 IU dissolved in 1 mL of ergometrine solution (0.5 mg) is injected intramuscularly into the thigh at the crowning of the foetal head.
- **Ophthalmology:** 25 IU is added to each mL of anaesthetic solution to improve diffusion and akinesia.
- **Fracture Reduction:** 1,500 IU mixed with the local anaesthetic agent (e.g., 20–40 mL of 1% procaine) and injected into the fracture haematoma.
- **Radiography (Pyelography):** 0.5 mL of reconstituted hyaluronidase added to 5.0 mL of 35% diodone solution, made up to 15.0 mL with distilled water, and injected subcutaneously.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to hyaluronidase.
- **Route specific:** Do not use for intravenous injection (rapidly inactivated).
- **Site specific:** 
    - Do not inject into or around infected or acutely inflamed areas (risk of spreading infection).
    - Do not use at sites where malignancy is present.
    - Not to be used to reduce swelling from bites or stings.
- **Drug-drug interactions:** Do not use to enhance the absorption/dispersion of dopamine or alpha-agonist drugs.
- **Clinical scenario:** Not for use in anaesthetic procedures during unexplained premature labour.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Ocular Use:** Do not apply hyaluronidase directly to the cornea.
- **Isotonicity:** Solutions intended for subcutaneous administration should be maintained as isotonic with extracellular fluid.
- **Spread of Infection:** Due to its mechanism of increasing tissue permeability, there is a theoretical risk of spreading localised infection or malignancy.
- **Physical Incompatibilities:** Hyaluronidase has been reported to be physically incompatible with heparin, adrenaline, furosemide, benzodiazepines, and phenytoin.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **General Reactions:** Local irritation, infection, bleeding, and bruising (reported as rare).
- **Allergic/Immunological:** 
    - Severe allergic reactions, including anaphylaxis (rare).
    - Orbital/periorbital oedema and inflammation have been reported when used with local anaesthetics in ophthalmology.
- **Eye Disorders:** Conjunctival oedema (chemosis), inflammation, restricted eye movements, decreased visual acuity, blurred vision, and proptosis.
- **Administration Site:** Oedema has been associated with hypodermoclysis.
- **Drug-related:** Hyaluronidase may enhance the adverse events of co-administered medications.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Incompatibilities:** The following drugs should not be mixed with hyaluronidase due to physical incompatibility:
    - Heparin
    - Adrenaline
    - Furosemide
    - Benzodiazepines
    - Phenytoin
- **Pharmacodynamic Interaction:** The enzyme must not be used to enhance the dispersion of alpha-adrenergic agonists or dopamine.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian: Avoid use]. There are no adequate and well-controlled studies regarding use in pregnant women.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if the drug is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution when administering to nursing mothers.
- **Fertility:** No data available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - **Site Selection:** For children, the anterior abdominal wall is recommended for hypodermoclysis to maximise absorption and avoid napkin contamination [Australian].
    - **Radiography:** Specific volumes for pyelography are applicable to children aged a few weeks to five years, provided renal function is not severely impaired.
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available; follow general dosing guidelines.
- **Renal Impairment:** Exercise caution in pyelography if renal function is grossly impaired.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Hyaluronidase is a spreading agent. It exerts a temporary and reversible depolymerizing effect on hyaluronic acid, a polysaccharide in the intercellular matrix of connective tissue. This increases tissue permeability, facilitating the dispersal and absorption of injected substances and the dispersal of extravascular fluid.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The "spreading" effect allows for more effective local anaesthesia (greater area of effect, less tissue "ballooning") and faster systemic absorption of subcutaneous fluids.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** No data available in the primary source.
