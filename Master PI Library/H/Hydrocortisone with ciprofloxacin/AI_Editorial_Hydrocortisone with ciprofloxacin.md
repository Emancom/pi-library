# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **US (FDA)**.
- **Note:** While the user context mentioned Australian documentation for a different drug class, the source material provided for analysis is exclusively the US FDA-approved labeling for Levocarnitine Injection by Baxter Healthcare Corporation.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesizing clinical data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for jurisdictional legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from metabolic errors [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
  - Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg [US].
  - Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
  - Severe metabolic crisis: A loading dose followed by equivalent doses every 3 or 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours) over the first 24 hours [US].
  - Highest reported dose: 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:**
  - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
  - Administration: Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].

### Monitoring and Adjustments
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels before parenteral therapy [US].
- Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
- For ESRD, dose adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) concentrations [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, often within minutes of administration. These are most frequently reported in ESRD patients [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Parenteral Inspection:** Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events occurring at a frequency of ≥5% (regardless of causality) in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials:

| Body System | Adverse Event (≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, **injection site reaction (33%)**, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia, vascular disorder |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), anorexia, constipation, dyspepsia |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%), hypervolemia (5%) |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness (13%), paresthesia (5%), depression, insomnia |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), rhinitis (9%), dyspnea (7%) |

### Post-marketing Experience
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent: body odor and gastritis [US].
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].
- **Immune:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly. Frequent INR monitoring is recommended following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **US Category:** Not explicitly lettered, but the PI states the drug should be used "only if clearly needed" [US].
  - Animal data: Rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations. Benefits to the mother must be weighed against the risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is weight-based (mg/kg) and applicable to pediatric populations [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in provided sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
  - Safety/efficacy of oral levocarnitine not evaluated in renal insufficiency [US].
  - Chronic high-dose oral administration in severe renal impairment or ESRD may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites: trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria for oxidation and energy production [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound [US].
  - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
  - **Elimination:** Following IV administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine from 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies at 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat. Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservative [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources (refer to local jurisdiction).
