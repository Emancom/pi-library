# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises the provided regulatory information into a single clinical reference.
*   **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (generic name); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
*   **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
*   **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
*   **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Metabolic Errors:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
*   **Carnitine Deficiency in ESRD:** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease (ESRD) who are undergoing hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
*   **Metabolic Disorders:**
    *   The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    *   **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by equivalent doses every 3 or 4 hours (never less frequently than every 6 hours) via infusion or IV injection [US].
    *   **Daily Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically remain in the 50 mg/kg range, though higher doses may be required. The maximum recorded dose is 300 mg/kg [US].
*   **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    *   **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    *   **Titration:** Adjustments should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine levels. Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
*   Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].
*   **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These can occur within minutes of administration, primarily in ESRD patients. Treatment must be discontinued immediately upon a severe reaction [US].
*   **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine may cause seizures. In patients with pre-existing seizure disorders, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been reported. Seizures have also occurred in patients without a prior history [US].
*   **Metabolite Accumulation (Oral Formulation):** The safety of *oral* levocarnitine in renal impairment is not established. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally renally excreted [US].
*   **Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Monitor weekly and monthly, including blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic errors.
*   **Commonly Reported:** Transient nausea, vomiting, body odor, and gastritis [US].
*   **Clinical Trial Data (ESRD patients at ≥5% frequency):**
    *   **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%), chest pain (6–15%), arrhythmia (2–5%) [US].
    *   **Neurological:** Headache (3–37%), dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%) [US].
    *   **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (9–35%), abdominal pain (5–21%), vomiting (9–21%) [US].
    *   **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–33%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–19%) [US].
    *   **General:** Injection site reaction (27–38%), fever (5–12%), asthenia (8–12%) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
*   **Management:** Monitor INR levels at the start of levocarnitine therapy and following any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [US: Category Not Explicitly Lettered]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Not specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. Weigh the benefits of maternal supplementation against the potential risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
*   **Fertility:** No evidence of impairment was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Use is established for metabolic disorders and ESRD; see weight-based dosing in Section 3 [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in provided sources.
*   **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. Note the warning regarding the accumulation of metabolites if oral formulations are used in this population [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, thereby alleviating autointoxication from acylCoA compounds [US].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    *   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    *   **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
*   **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, carnitine deficiency is defined as plasma free carnitine <20 µmol/L. IV administration increases plasma carnitine concentrations [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ESRD Pharmacokinetic Study:** In 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg 3x weekly, mean pre-dialysis (trough) levels rose from a baseline of 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
*   **Dose-Ranging Study:** In ESRD patients, doses of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg resulted in mean trough levels at 24 weeks of 148, 240, and 456 µmol/L, respectively [US].
*   **Clinical Outcomes:** While IV levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the impact on specific clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, cardiomyopathy) has not been definitively determined in the provided clinical trial data [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
*   **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
*   **Handling:** Vials are single-dose; contains no preservative. Discard any unused portion [US].
