# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **US (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically focused overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the local approved PI for full prescribing details and legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (Generic name: L-carnitine).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not explicitly stated in source, but standard for this agent).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Clear, colorless sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism leading to secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Administer intravenously only.
- Parenteral products should be inspected for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
- Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL concentrations); stable for 24 hours at 25°C in PVC bags [US].

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg given as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Loading Dose:** Often used in severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 to 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours.
- **Maintenance:** Daily doses typically remain around 50 mg/kg but may be adjusted based on clinical response. Doses as high as 300 mg/kg have been used.

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Recommended Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
- **Titration:** Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels.

### Monitoring
- **Plasma Carnitine Levels:** Recommended prior to starting therapy and then weekly or monthly.
- **Target Levels:** Normal free carnitine concentrations should be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known based on current manufacturer documentation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and monitor for recurrence if re-administration is attempted [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Periodic blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations are essential to guide therapy [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5% in ESRD Trials)
The following events were reported in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials for ESRD patients, though causality was not always established:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (30%), headache (22%), infection (15%), fever (7%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), chest pain (12%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), abdominal pain (9%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%).
- **Nervous:** Dizziness (13%), paresthesia (5%).

### Other Reported Effects
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hypervolemia (5%).
- **Special Senses:** Taste perversion (3%), amblyopia (3%).
- **Other:** Body odor, gastritis, and transient nausea/vomiting have been noted [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concurrently with levocarnitine. Frequent INR monitoring is recommended after starting levocarnitine or adjusting the dose [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned/lettered in current PI]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of fetal harm or impaired fertility. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration. Clinicians must weigh the benefits to the mother against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Refer to the "Dosage and Administration" section; metabolic disorder dosing is based on mg/kg, which applies to paediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in the provided source.
- **Renal Impairment:** While used in ESRD, chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine (not necessarily injection) in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO). The injection bypasses the gut metabolism that primarily produces these metabolites [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This facilitates beta-oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, it alleviates metabolic abnormalities caused by carnitine loss during dialysis and reduced renal synthesis.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) and γ-butyrobetaine.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** Efficacy has been demonstrated in increasing plasma carnitine concentrations in patients undergoing dialysis. In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg after dialysis, mean trough levels rose from ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine levels in ESRD, the definitive effect on specific clinical outcomes in this population has not been fully determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription only).
- **Poison Schedule:** Not applicable in the US context (US: Rx-only).
