# Editorial Product Information: Haloperidol (as Decanoate)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. 
- **Note:** While the user instructions requested a harmonization of multiple international sources, only the Australian PI was provided for review. Consequently, this document presents the clinical position as established by the Australian regulatory authority.
- **Purpose:** This document serves as a harmonized editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a synthesis for clinical reference and does not substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidelines required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Haloperidol (as decanoate).
- **Chemical Class:** Butyrophenone derivative; long-acting antipsychotic.
- **Brand Name(s):** **HALDOL DECANOATE** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N05AD01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Slightly amber, viscous oily injection for intramuscular (IM) use. Available in 1 mL (50 mg haloperidol) and 3 mL (150 mg haloperidol) glass ampoules [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Deep intramuscular injection only. **Strictly contra-indicated for intravenous (IV) use.**
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Clinect Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Maintenance of Psychosis:** Indicated for the maintenance therapy of psychoses in adults, specifically for those requiring prolonged parenteral neuroleptic therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Pre-requisite:** Patients must be stabilized on oral haloperidol before converting to the decanoate formulation.
- **Transition from Oral:** A dose of 10 to 15 times the previous daily oral dose is recommended. For most patients, this is between 25 mg and 150 mg.
- **Initial Dose Cap:** The initial dose should not exceed 100 mg [Australian].
- **Maintenance:** The most effective range is typically 50 mg to 200 mg every 4 weeks.
- **Maximum Dose:** 300 mg every 4 weeks [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Site:** Deep IM injection into the gluteal region.
- **Equipment:** A 2-inch-long, 21-gauge needle is recommended.
- **Volume:** Do not exceed 3 mL per injection site. 
- **Rotation:** Alternate between the two gluteal muscles for subsequent injections.

### Supplementation
- Short-acting (non-decanoate) haloperidol may be used during transition or acute exacerbations. The combined dose must not exceed the equivalent of 20 mg/day oral haloperidol [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to haloperidol or excipients (Note: Contains sesame oil; potential cross-reactivity in patients with peanut allergy).
- Comatose states of any etiology.
- Central Nervous System (CNS) depression (alcohol or other depressant drugs).
- Significant depressive states.
- Previous spastic diseases.
- Parkinson’s syndrome (except in cases of levodopa-induced dyskinesia).
- Senile patients with pre-existing Parkinson-like symptoms.
- Dementia with Lewy bodies.
- Progressive supranuclear palsy [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Mortality Risk (Boxed Warning Equivalent):** Increased risk of death in elderly patients with dementia-related psychosis. Haloperidol is **not** indicated for dementia-related behavioral disturbances [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** Rare reports of QTc interval prolongation and ventricular arrhythmias (including Torsades de Pointes). Avoid use if QTc exceeds 500 ms. Baseline ECG and electrolyte monitoring (potassium, magnesium) are recommended.
- **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** Potentially fatal syndrome characterized by hyperthermia, muscle rigidity, and autonomic instability. Requires immediate discontinuation and intensive symptomatic treatment.
- **Tardive Dyskinesia (TD):** Risk increases with duration and total cumulative dose. May be irreversible. Discontinue treatment if signs appear.
- **Extrapyramidal Symptoms (EPS):** Frequently occurs; may require dose reduction or temporary use of anticholinergic anti-Parkinson medication.
- **Hepatobiliary:** Extensively metabolized in the liver; risk of cholestatic jaundice or hepatitis.
- **Seizures:** Haloperidol may lower the seizure threshold.
- **Venous Thromboembolism (VTE):** Cases have been reported; identify risk factors prior to treatment.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are characterized by their effect on the dopaminergic system and the oily depot delivery.

| Frequency | Adverse Event [Australian Trial Data] |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Extrapyramidal disorder (13.6%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Tremor (8.0%), Parkinsonism (7.3%), Muscle rigidity (6.1%), Somnolence (4.9%), Masked facies (4.1%), Dry mouth (3.4%), Akathisia (3.4%), Weight increased (2.9%), Sedation (2.7%), Constipation (2.0%), Sexual dysfunction (1.5%), Injection site reaction (1.2%) |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Akinesia, Dyskinesia, Dystonia, Cogwheel rigidity, Vision blurred, Tachycardia |

**Serious Adverse Effects (Post-marketing):** Agranulocytosis, pancytopenia, anaphylactic reaction, Torsade de pointes, ventricular fibrillation, acute hepatic failure, rhabdomyolysis, and neonatal drug withdrawal syndrome [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **QTc Prolonging Drugs:** Increased risk of arrhythmias (e.g., Class IA and III antiarrhythmics, certain antibiotics, and antidepressants).
    - **CNS Depressants:** Potentiates alcohol, opiates, and hypnotics.
    - **Adrenaline:** Haloperidol may antagonize adrenaline, leading to "epinephrine reversal" and profound hypotension.
    - **Lithium:** Rare reports of an encephalopathic syndrome (weakness, fever, confusion) when combined.
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **CYP3A4 and CYP2D6 Inhibitors:** (e.g., ketoconazole, fluoxetine, paroxetine) can increase haloperidol plasma levels.
    - **CYP3A4 Inducers:** (e.g., carbamazepine, rifampicin, St John’s Wort) can significantly decrease haloperidol levels, requiring dose adjustment [Australian].
    - **Tricyclic Antidepressants:** Haloperidol inhibits CYP2D6, potentially increasing TCA toxicity.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Neonates exposed during the third trimester are at risk of extrapyramidal and/or withdrawal symptoms (e.g., agitation, respiratory distress). Use only if benefits outweigh risks.
- **Breastfeeding:** Excreted in breast milk. Risk/benefit must be assessed; breastfeeding is generally discouraged or requires careful infant monitoring [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No human data; animal studies show impaired fertility and reproductive organ histopathological changes.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children <18 years have not been established; not recommended.
- **Elderly Use:** Higher plasma concentrations and lower clearance. Recommended starting dose: 12.5 mg to 25 mg. Maintenance dose should be approximately half the standard adult dose [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No specific dose adjustment recommended, but caution is advised. Not significantly removed by dialysis.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Halve the initial dose; increase more slowly and with longer intervals [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Potent central dopamine type 2 (D2) receptor antagonist.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Suppresses delusions and hallucinations by blocking dopaminergic signaling in the mesolimbic pathway. It has low alpha-1 antiadrenergic activity and no antihistaminergic or anticholinergic activity.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Slow release via esterases peaking at 3–9 days. Steady state reached in 2–4 months with monthly injections.
    - **Distribution:** 88–92% protein bound. Crosses the blood-brain barrier and placenta.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively hepatic via glucuronidation and CYP3A4/2D6.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 3 weeks [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Safety Profile:** Evaluation based on 410 subjects across 13 clinical trials for schizophrenia or schizoaffective disorder.
- **Therapeutic Concentrations:** Response typically seen at 1 to 10 ng/mL. Due to high inter-subject variability, dosing should be individualized based on clinical response [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Protection:** Protect from light [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on packaging or the ARTG summary.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **S4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
