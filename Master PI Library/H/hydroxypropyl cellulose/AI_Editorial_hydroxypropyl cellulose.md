# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis of available regulatory information and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of preparation, only the US (FDA) source documentation was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine [(R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt].
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with a primary or secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from metabolic errors [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
- **Administration:** Delivered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Maintenance Frequency:** Typically administered every 3 or 4 hours (q3h or q4h); frequency should never be less than every 6 hours (q6h).
- **Maximum Dose:** Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
- **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels. Adjustments (e.g., downward to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Monitoring and Administration Instructions
- **Plasma Monitoring:** Trough concentrations should be obtained prior to therapy. Recommended plasma free carnitine levels are between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Inspection:** Parenteral products must be visually inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported. These can occur within minutes of administration, particularly in ESRD patients. Treatment must be discontinued immediately upon a severe reaction [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine may increase the frequency or severity of seizures in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a prior history of epilepsy [US].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** High-dose oral levocarnitine (not the injection) is not evaluated for renal insufficiency and may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO) in ESRD patients [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology (ESRD or metabolic disease).

### Very Common (>10%) [US - Dialysis Population]
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), headache (22%), fever (12%), chest pain (12%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%).

### Common (1–10%) [US - Dialysis Population]
- **General:** Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, flu syndrome.
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, palpitations, vascular disorder.
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight changes.
- **Neurologic:** Dizziness, paresthesia, insomnia, anxiety.
- **Other:** Body odor (transient), nausea, gastritis, anemia, pruritus, rhinitis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. Frequent monitoring of INR is recommended upon initiation or dose adjustment [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine is not protein-bound, suggesting a low risk of displacement-based interactions [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US]: Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. There are no adequate/controlled studies in pregnant women. Use only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** [US]: Not specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations. Benefits to the mother should be weighed against potential risks to the infant; consideration may be given to discontinuing nursing or the drug.
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impairment in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg), which applies to paediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in provided sources; dosing should generally be cautious, reflecting the greater frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function.
- **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is specifically indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD patients on dialysis. However, safety and efficacy of high-dose *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal impairment [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This allows for beta-oxidation and energy production. It also facilitates the excretion of excess organic/fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, it alleviates metabolic abnormalities caused by the accumulation of toxic organic acids and corrects low plasma carnitine levels resulting from dialysis losses and reduced renal synthesis [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Distribution half-life is approximately 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) and γ-butyrobetaine [US].
    - **Elimination:** Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Studies (ESRD):** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV thrice weekly after dialysis increased mean trough levocarnitine concentrations from 21.3 µmol/L at baseline to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg showed dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Clinical Outcomes:** While IV levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations, the provided source notes that effects on specific clinical outcomes (signs/symptoms) in the ESRD population have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing.
- **Vial Integrity:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservative [US].
