# Editorial Product Information: Hydroxyurea

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of hydroxyurea for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a clinically applicable format.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Hydroxyurea (an antimetabolite) [US].
- **Brand Names:** Hydroxyurea Capsules, USP (Major Pharmaceuticals/Par Health) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Oral Capsules: 500 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Manufactured for Par Health USA; Distributed by Major Pharmaceuticals [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Resistant Chronic Myeloid Leukemia (CML):** [US-approved].
- **Locally Advanced Squamous Cell Carcinoma:** Specifically of the head and neck (excluding the lip), used in combination with concurrent chemoradiation [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing Principles:** 
    - Treatment must be individualised based on the specific tumour type, disease state, patient risk factors, clinical response, and current practice standards [US].
    - All dosages should be calculated based on the patient's actual or ideal weight, whichever is less [US].
- **Indication-Specific Dosing [US]:**
    - **Solid Tumours (Intermittent Therapy):** 80 mg/kg as a single dose every third day.
    - **Solid Tumours (Continuous Therapy):** 20 to 30 mg/kg as a single daily dose.
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Capsules must be swallowed whole. Do **not** open, break, or chew, as hydroxyurea is a cytotoxic agent [US].
    - If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as remembered unless it is almost time for the next dose.
- **Supportive Care:** Prophylactic administration of folic acid is recommended [US].
- **Monitoring:** Perform a complete blood count (CBC) at least once weekly during therapy. Severe anemia must be corrected prior to initiation [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Contraindicated in patients with a known history of hypersensitivity to hydroxyurea or any component of the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Myelosuppression:** [US Boxed Warning equivalent] Severe bone marrow suppression (leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, and anemia) occurs frequently. Do not initiate therapy if bone marrow function is markedly depressed. Recovery is typically rapid upon treatment interruption [US].
- **Malignancies:** Hydroxyurea is a known human carcinogen. Secondary leukemia and skin cancers have been reported with long-term use for myeloproliferative disorders. Patients should be advised to use sun protection [US].
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Can cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status before initiation [US].
- **Vasculitic Toxicities:** Cutaneous vasculitic ulcerations and gangrene have occurred, most commonly in patients with a history of or concurrent interferon therapy. Discontinue hydroxyurea if these occur [US].
- **Live Vaccinations:** Avoid live vaccines due to the risk of potentiation of virus replication and severe infection in immunocompromised patients [US].
- **Concomitant Antiretroviral Use:** Fatal and nonfatal pancreatitis, hepatotoxicity, and severe peripheral neuropathy have occurred in HIV-positive patients using hydroxyurea with didanosine and/or stavudine [US].
- **Pulmonary Toxicity:** Interstitial lung disease (including fatal cases) has been reported. Monitor for pyrexia, cough, and dyspnea; treat with corticosteroids and discontinue hydroxyurea if suspected [US].
- **Radiation Recall:** May exacerbate post-irradiation erythema [US].
- **Macrocytosis:** Self-limiting macrocytosis often occurs early in treatment and may mask the diagnosis of pernicious anemia [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Most common adverse reactions (≥30%) include hematological effects, gastrointestinal symptoms, and anorexia [US].

| System Organ Class | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Blood and Lymphatic** | Myelosuppression (leukopenia, anemia, thrombocytopenia), hemolytic anemia [US]. |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Stomatitis, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, gastric irritation, mucositis [US]. |
| **Metabolism/Nutrition** | Anorexia, tumor lysis syndrome [US]. |
| **Skin/Subcutaneous** | Maculopapular rash, skin ulceration, hyperpigmentation, alopecia, cutaneous lupus erythematosus, dermatomyositis-like changes, nail atrophy [US]. |
| **Nervous System** | Headache, dizziness, drowsiness, disorientation, hallucinations, convulsions, peripheral neuropathy [US]. |
| **Renal/Urinary** | Dysuria, elevations in serum uric acid, BUN, and creatinine [US]. |
| **Reproductive** | Azoospermia, oligospermia (sometimes reversible) [US]. |
| **General** | Fever (drug-induced pyrexia), chills, malaise, edema, asthenia [US]. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Antiretroviral Drugs:** Increased risk of pancreatitis, hepatotoxicity, and peripheral neuropathy when combined with didanosine or stavudine. This combination should be avoided [US].
- **Laboratory Test Interference:** 
    - **Assays:** May cause falsely elevated results in uric acid, urea, and lactic acid assays due to analytical interference with urease, uricase, and lactate dehydrogenase [US].
    - **Glucose Monitoring:** May falsely elevate sensor glucose results in certain continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) systems, potentially leading to hypoglycemia if used to calculate insulin doses [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not specified by letter, but documented as causing fetal harm [US].
    - **Risk:** Embryotoxic and teratogenic in animal studies at doses below the maximum recommended human daily dose [US].
    - **Contraception:** Females of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during and for at least 6 months after treatment. Males should use effective contraception during and for at least 1 year after treatment [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Hydroxyurea is excreted in human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions and carcinogenicity in the infant, breastfeeding should be discontinued during treatment [US].
- **Fertility:** Male fertility may be compromised; azoospermia and oligospermia have been observed. Male patients should be informed about sperm conservation prior to starting therapy [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in paediatric patients have not been established [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Elderly patients may be more sensitive to effects and are more likely to have renal impairment. Consider lower dose regimens and monitor renal function [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - Exposure is higher in patients with CrCl <60 mL/min or ESRD. 
    - **Dose Adjustment:** Reduce the initial dose by 50% (to 7.5 mg/kg daily) if CrCl <60 mL/min or in ESRD. 
    - **Dialysis:** On dialysis days, administer hydroxyurea following the hemodialysis session [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No specific data available; monitor hematologic parameters closely [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Precise mechanism is not fully described, but it is believed to inhibit DNA synthesis by acting as a ribonucleotide reductase inhibitor without interfering with RNA or protein synthesis. In head and neck cancer, it may sensitise cells to radiotherapy by holding them in the G1 phase and hindering DNA repair [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak plasma levels reached in 1 to 4 hours. AUC increases more than proportionally with dose [US].
    - **Distribution:** Distributes throughout total body water; concentrates in leukocytes and erythrocytes [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Up to 60% undergoes saturable hepatic metabolism and degradation by intestinal bacteria urease [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 40% is recovered in the urine [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F and 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Hydroxyurea is a cytotoxic drug. Caregivers should wear disposable gloves. Avoid exposure to crushed or opened capsules. Spills should be cleaned with detergent followed by water three times [US].
