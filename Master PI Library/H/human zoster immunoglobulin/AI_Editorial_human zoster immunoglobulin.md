# Editorial Product Information: human zoster immunoglobulin

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **human zoster immunoglobulin**
- Chemical class: Human plasma‑derived immunoglobulin preparation (predominantly IgG) with varicella‑zoster virus (VZV) antibodies
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intramuscular injection only

## 3. Approved Indications
Human zoster immunoglobulin is indicated for prophylaxis against varicella (chickenpox) in susceptible patients who meet all four of the following criteria:
- Presence of one of these underlying conditions:
  - Neoplastic disease (leukaemia or lymphoma)
  - Congenital or acquired immunodeficiency
  - Immunosuppressive therapy with corticosteroids or antimetabolites
- A qualifying exposure to a person with chickenpox or shingles, including:
  - Household contact
  - Playmate contact (greater than 1 hour play indoors)
  - Hospital contact (same 2–4 bedroom or adjacent beds in a large ward)
  - Newborn contact: newborn of a mother whose onset of chickenpox occurred <5 days before delivery or within 48 hours after delivery
  - Premature infant (≥28 weeks gestation) whose mother lacks a prior history of chickenpox
  - Premature infant (<28 weeks gestation or ≤1000 g) regardless of maternal history
- Negative or unknown prior history of chickenpox
- Ability to administer human zoster immunoglobulin within 96 hours after exposure (greatest expected effectiveness when given within this window; treatment after 96 hours is of uncertain value)

Additional notes on indications:
- Human zoster immunoglobulin, normal intramuscular immunoglobulin, or plasma are of no value in the treatment of established varicella or zoster infection and are not indicated for treatment of active disease.
- Not indicated for prophylactic use in immunodeficient children or adults with a prior history of varicella, except where the immunosuppressed status is associated with bone marrow transplantation.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (weight‑based)
- Dosage is based on body weight as follows:
  - 0–10 kg: 125 IU — 1 vial
  - 10.1–20 kg: 250 IU — 2 vials
  - 20.1–30 kg: 375 IU — 2 vials
  - 30.1–40 kg: 500 IU — 3 vials
  - >40 kg: 600 IU — 3 vials

(Individual vial potency as stated in available sources: at least 200 IU per vial. Confirm vial potency from product labelling prior to dosing.)

### Dosing by indication
- Prophylaxis after qualifying exposure only (see Section 3). No dosing schedules for treatment of established varicella/zoster provided.

### Administration instructions
- Route: Deep intramuscular injection only. **Must not be administered intravenously** because of the risk of severe anaphylactic reactions.
- Inspection: Do not use if solution is turbid or contains particulate matter/sediment.
- Preservative: Product is preservative‑free; use immediately after opening vial. Discard any unused portion.
- Preparation: Bring to room temperature before administration.
- Injection technique:
  - Use an appropriate needle for deep intramuscular injection.
  - Aspirate (draw back on the plunger) before injection to avoid intravascular administration.
  - If a large volume is required, administer divided doses at different sites.
    - For children up to 20 kg: divide doses when volume at one site would exceed 2 mL.
    - For persons >20 kg: divide doses when volume at one site would exceed 5 mL.
- Additives: Hyaluronidase and/or a suitable local anaesthetic may be added to the injection if desired (compatibility beyond those specifics not stated).
- Observation: Observe patients for at least 20 minutes after administration; observe for at least 1 hour when inadvertent intravenous injection is suspected.

### Titration, missed doses
- Titration schedules: Not specified in available sources.
- Missed dose instructions: Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- History of a true anaphylactic reaction to the active substance (human zoster immunoglobulin) or to any component of the product.
- Known immunoglobulin A (IgA) deficiency, unless the patient has been tested and shown not to have circulating anti‑IgA antibodies (patients with anti‑IgA antibodies may experience severe reactions to trace IgA in the product).
- Severe thrombocytopenia or any coagulation disorder that contraindicates intramuscular injections.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed/critical warnings
- **Do not administer intravenously** — intravenous administration can precipitate anaphylaxis.

### General warnings and precautions
- Hypersensitivity:
  - Contains trace amounts of IgA which may cause anaphylaxis in patients with anti‑IgA antibodies (particularly those with IgA deficiency).
  - Give with caution to patients with a history of prior systemic allergic reactions to human immunoglobulin preparations.
  - Severe anaphylactic reactions including sudden precipitous fall in blood pressure have been reported; these may occur even in patients who previously tolerated immunoglobulin products. If anaphylaxis occurs, stop administration immediately and initiate appropriate emergency treatment including shock therapy per local protocols.
- Route verification:
  - Ensure intramuscular route by aspiration prior to injection; inadvertent intravenous injection increases risk of severe systemic reactions and requires extended observation.
- Post‑administration observation:
  - Observe patients for at least 20 minutes following administration; observe for at least 1 hour if inadvertent IV administration is suspected.
- Pathogen safety (plasma‑derived product):
  - Product is derived from human plasma. Although donor screening and manufacturing viral inactivation/removal steps (e.g., pasteurisation, nanofiltration) reduce risk, transmission of infectious agents cannot be completely excluded. The product contains antibodies to certain viruses.
  - Record the product name and batch number each time the product is administered to maintain traceability.
- Vaccination interactions:
  - Passively acquired antibody can interfere with the immune response to live attenuated virus vaccines (e.g., measles, oral poliomyelitis). Administration of live vaccines should generally be deferred until approximately three months after passive immunisation. For measles, decreased vaccine efficacy may persist up to one year; consider checking post‑vaccination antibody status.
  - If a live attenuated vaccine was administered within 2 weeks prior to receiving human zoster immunoglobulin, the efficacy of the vaccine may be compromised. Consider revaccination approximately three months after immunoglobulin administration.
- Laboratory test interference:
  - Passive transfer of antibodies can cause transient rises in serological antibody levels and may yield false‑positive serology results.
  - Transfer of antibodies (e.g., anti‑A, anti‑B, anti‑D) may interfere with some red cell serology tests (e.g., direct Coombs’ test).
- Use in pregnancy, lactation, fertility:
  - Safety in pregnancy, lactation, and fertility has not been established in controlled clinical trials; use with caution (see Section 9).
- Use in special populations:
  - Paediatric and elderly use: Product use in these groups has not been established in appropriate controlled studies; available spontaneous reports do not indicate over‑representation in adverse events. Follow paediatric dosing guidance where provided (Section 4).

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor for signs of hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis during and after administration.
- Monitor injection sites for local reactions.
- Consider serological testing when required after recent passive immunoglobulin (e.g., after live vaccine administration).

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Do not mix human zoster immunoglobulin with other medicinal products, diluents, or solvents, except as specifically indicated under administration instructions (see Section 4).
- Interaction with live attenuated vaccines:
  - Passively acquired antibodies may interfere with the immune response to live attenuated vaccines (e.g., measles, oral poliomyelitis). Defer administration of live attenuated vaccines until approximately three months after passive immunisation. For measles, reduced vaccine efficacy may persist up to one year; consider post‑vaccination antibody testing.
  - If a live attenuated vaccine was given within two weeks before immunoglobulin, the vaccine’s efficacy may be compromised; consider re‑vaccination approximately three months after immunoglobulin administration.
- Pharmacokinetic interactions: Not specified in available sources.
- Other pharmacodynamic interactions: Not specified in available sources beyond interference with live vaccines.
- Jurisdictional differences: Not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- Pregnancy:
  - Safety in human pregnancy has not been established in controlled clinical trials. Use during pregnancy should be considered only if clearly needed and after assessing potential benefits versus risks.
  - Pregnancy categorization systems (e.g., Australian pregnancy categories, US pregnancy letter categories) for this product are not specified in the available sources.
- Breastfeeding:
  - Immunoglobulins are excreted in human breast milk; it is not known whether this applies to passively administered human zoster immunoglobulin. Use with caution in breast‑feeding mothers.
- Fertility:
  - No reproductive toxicity studies have been conducted with human zoster immunoglobulin. No reports of effects on fertility associated with CSL’s plasma‑derived therapeutic medicines were provided in the source.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **human zoster immunoglobulin** is a preparation of human plasma‑derived immunoglobulin (predominantly IgG) containing high levels of antibodies directed against varicella‑zoster virus (VZV). Passive transfer of these antibodies can prevent varicella in susceptible contacts following exposure.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Passive immunoglobulin confers immediate but temporary antibody‑mediated protection against VZV. Maximum effectiveness is expected when administered within 96 hours of exposure; efficacy when given after 96 hours is uncertain.

### Pharmacokinetics (absorption, distribution, metabolism, elimination)
