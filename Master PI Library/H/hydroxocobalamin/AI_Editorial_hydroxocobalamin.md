# Editorial Product Information: Hydroxocobalamin

**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been synthesized from the official regulatory filings of the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) labeling. Where clinical recommendations or safety warnings differ between jurisdictions, the more comprehensive or conservative guidance has been prioritized, with the source agency noted in brackets.

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** Hydroxocobalamin Injection  
**Active Ingredient:** Hydroxocobalamin (as acetate)  
**Strength:** 1 mg/mL  

Hydroxocobalamin is a naturally occurring, long-acting form of Vitamin B12. It is provided as a sterile, red-colored solution for injection.

## 2. Approved Indications
Hydroxocobalamin is indicated for the following:
*   **Treatment and Prophylaxis of Vitamin B12 Deficiency:** Including pernicious (Addisonian) anaemia and other macrocytic anaemias resulting from B12 deficiency [TGA].
*   **Prophylaxis in High-Risk Groups:** Patients with nutritional deficiencies, malabsorption syndromes, or those who have undergone a gastrectomy [TGA].
*   **Optic Neuropathies:** Treatment of tobacco amblyopia and Leber’s optic atrophy [TGA]. *(Note: See Section 3 regarding regional contraindications for Leber’s disease).*
*   **General Vitamin B12 Replacement:** Support of normal hematologic and neurologic function where deficiency is identified [FDA].

## 3. Contraindications
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known sensitivity to hydroxocobalamin, cobalt, or any excipients (including benzyl alcohol where present in the formulation) [TGA, FDA].
*   **Megaloblastic Anaemia of Pregnancy:** This product should not be utilized to treat megaloblastic anaemia occurring during pregnancy [TGA].
*   **Leber’s Disease:** Administration to patients with hereditary optic nerve atrophy (Leber’s disease) is contraindicated by some authorities as it may cause severe and rapid optic nerve atrophy [FDA]. 

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Route of Administration:** **Strictly for intramuscular (IM) use.** Do not administer intravenously [TGA, FDA].
*   **Hypersensitivity Testing:** A thorough sensitivity history must be taken. In patients with a suspected history of cobalamin sensitivity, an intradermal test dose is recommended prior to therapeutic administration [TGA].
*   **Intensive Therapy Risks:** In the treatment of severe megaloblastic anaemia, intensive therapy may lead to hypokalaemia and potential cardiac arrest. Serum potassium levels must be monitored closely during the initial treatment phase [TGA, FDA].
*   **Masking of Diagnosis:** Vitamin B12 may improve folate-deficient megaloblastic anaemia, which can obscure a true diagnosis and allow subacute degeneration of the spinal cord to progress. Diagnosis should be confirmed by laboratory testing before initiating treatment [TGA].
*   **Benzyl Alcohol Toxicity:** Formulations containing benzyl alcohol are associated with "gasping syndrome" in premature infants and neonates. Use with extreme caution in pediatric populations [FDA].
*   **Aluminium Toxicity:** Prolonged parenteral use in patients with renal impairment or in preterm infants may increase the risk of aluminium toxicity [TGA].
*   **Interference with Laboratory Tests:** Hydroxocobalamin’s deep red color may interfere with colorimetric clinical chemistry assays. It may cause artifactual increases in parameters such as bilirubin, creatinine, triglycerides, cholesterol, and glucose, and artifactual decreases in ALT and amylase [TGA].

## 5. Dosage and Administration
The injection must be administered intramuscularly. The following regimens are recommended [TGA]:

### Macrocytic Anaemia (Without Neurological Involvement)
*   **Initial:** 250 to 1,000 mcg on alternate days for 1–2 weeks, then 250 mcg weekly until blood counts normalize.
*   **Maintenance:** 1,000 mcg every 2 to 3 months.

### Macrocytic Anaemia (With Neurological Involvement)
*   **Initial:** 1,000 mcg on alternate days for 1–2 weeks.
*   **Maintenance:** 1,000 mcg every 2 months.

### Prophylaxis (Gastrectomy/Malabsorption)
*   1,000 mcg every 2 to 3 months.

### Tobacco Amblyopia and Leber's Optic Atrophy
*   **Initial:** 1,000 mcg daily for 2 weeks, followed by twice weekly for 4 weeks.
*   **Maintenance:** 1,000 mcg monthly.

## 6. Adverse Effects
*   **General:** Headache, dizziness, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, and fever/chills [TGA, FDA].
*   **Dermatological/Hypersensitivity:** Rash, pruritus, urticaria, flushing, angioedema, and acneiform eruptions. Anaphylaxis is rare but possible [TGA, FDA].
*   **Cardiovascular:** Transitory hypertension, palpitations, and peripheral vascular thrombosis. Pulmonary oedema and congestive heart failure may occur early in treatment due to increased blood volume [TGA].
*   **Injection Site:** Pain, erythema, and localized reactions [FDA].
*   **Body Fluids:** Patients should be advised that their skin, urine, and faeces may take on a pink or reddish discoloration [TGA].
*   **Laboratory:** Development of antibodies to the hydroxocobalamin-transcobalamin II complex [TGA].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Chloramphenicol:** May diminish the hematopoietic response to Vitamin B12 in deficient patients. Monitor haematological parameters closely if co-administered [TGA].
*   **Folic Acid:** High doses of folic acid may lower Vitamin B12 blood concentrations and mask deficiency symptoms [TGA].
*   **Oral Contraceptives:** May reduce serum concentrations of hydroxocobalamin; however, the clinical significance of this is uncertain [TGA].
*   **Antibiotics/Methotrexate:** May invalidate microbiological assays for Vitamin B12 [TGA].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** Vitamin B12 crosses the placenta. While no documented issues exist with normal daily intake, high-dose therapeutic use is only recommended if the benefit to the mother outweighs the potential risk to the fetus [TGA]. It is not recommended for the treatment of megaloblastic anaemia of pregnancy caused by folate deficiency [TGA].
*   **Lactation:** Hydroxocobalamin is excreted into breast milk. Benefit-risk assessment is required for breastfeeding mothers [TGA].

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
Hydroxocobalamin is absorbed rapidly following intramuscular injection. It provides higher and more sustained serum Vitamin B12 levels compared to cyanocobalamin because it disperses more slowly from the injection site and binds more strongly to plasma proteins. It is primarily stored in the liver and excreted via bile and urine, though at a slower rate than other cobalamins [TGA].

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Vitamin B12 is a vital coenzyme for metabolic processes including DNA synthesis, haemopoiesis, and the maintenance of myelin in the nervous system. Hydroxocobalamin participates in:
1.  The conversion of propionate to succinate (fat and carbohydrate metabolism).
2.  The transmethylation of homocysteine to methionine (protein and fat metabolism) [TGA].

Additionally, hydroxocobalamin acts as a cyanide antagonist by combining with cyanide to form non-toxic cyanocobalamin [TGA].

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   Store at controlled room temperature: 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Brief excursions between 15°C and 30°C are permitted [FDA].
*   Protect from light and freezing [TGA, FDA].
*   Keep the vial or ampoule in the outer carton until use [FDA].
*   Visual inspection for particulate matter or discoloration is required prior to administration [FDA].
