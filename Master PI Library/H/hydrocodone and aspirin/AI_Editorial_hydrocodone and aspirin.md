# Editorial Product Information: Hydrocodone Bitartrate and Aspirin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local regulatory requirements for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Hydrocodone bitartrate (opioid agonist) and Aspirin (nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug/salicylate).
- **Brand Name(s):** Generic (various) [US].
- **ATC Code:** N02BA51 (aspirin and opioids) [Inferred].
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Immediate-release tablets containing 5 mg hydrocodone bitartrate and 500 mg aspirin [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** LGM Pharma Solutions, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Management of Pain:** Indicated for the management of pain severe enough to require an opioid analgesic and for which alternative treatment options (e.g., non-opioid analgesics) are inadequate or not tolerated [US].
- **Limitations of Use:** Due to risks of addiction, abuse, and misuse, this combination should be reserved for patients for whom alternative treatments are insufficient. Long-term use should only continue if the pain remains severe and alternatives are inadequate [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):**
    - The usual dose is one (1) or two (2) tablets every four (4) to six (6) hours as needed for pain [US].
    - **Maximum Dose:** The total daily dose of aspirin must not exceed 4 grams [US].
- **Administration:** Administer with food or a full glass of water to minimize gastrointestinal (GI) distress [US].
- **Titration:** Dosage must be individualized based on pain severity, patient response, and prior analgesic experience. Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration possible [US].
- **Tapering/Discontinuation:** Do not abruptly discontinue in physically dependent patients. Use a patient-specific plan to taper the dose by 10% to 25% increments every 2 to 4 weeks to avoid withdrawal symptoms [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Significant respiratory depression [US].
- Acute or severe bronchial asthma in unmonitored settings or without resuscitative equipment [US].
- Known or suspected gastrointestinal obstruction, including paralytic ileus [US].
- Hypersensitivity to hydrocodone, aspirin, or other NSAIDs (e.g., angioedema, bronchospasm, rhinitis) [US].
- Patients with the syndrome of asthma, rhinitis, and nasal polyps [US].
- Hemophilia [US].
- **Pediatric use for viral infections:** Contraindicated in children or teenagers with viral illnesses (with or without fever) due to the risk of Reye’s syndrome [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings [US]:**
    - **Addiction, Abuse, and Misuse:** Risk of opioid addiction which can lead to overdose and death.
    - **Life-Threatening Respiratory Depression:** May occur even at recommended doses; monitoring is essential, particularly during initiation and titration.
    - **Accidental Ingestion:** Even one dose can be fatal, especially in children.
    - **Neonatal Opioid Withdrawal Syndrome (NOWS):** Prolonged use during pregnancy can result in life-threatening withdrawal in the neonate.
    - **Cytochrome P450 3A4 Interaction:** Concomitant use with CYP3A4 inhibitors (or discontinuation of inducers) can increase hydrocodone levels, leading to fatal respiratory depression.
    - **Risks from Concomitant Use with Benzodiazepines or Other CNS Depressants:** May result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death.
- **Adrenal Insufficiency:** Reported with opioid use, usually after >1 month of therapy [US].
- **Severe Hypotension:** Opioids may cause orthostatic hypotension and syncope, especially in patients with depleted blood volume or concurrent CNS depressant use [US].
- **Gastrointestinal Risks:** Aspirin increases the risk of GI bleeding, ulceration, and perforation. Risk is 10-fold higher in patients with a history of peptic ulcer disease or GI bleeding [US].
- **Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS):** Potential fatal multi-organ hypersensitivity reaction associated with NSAID use [US].
- **Coagulation Abnormalities:** Aspirin inhibits platelet function; use caution in patients with hepatic disease or vitamin K deficiency [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Frequencies are not specified in available sources (primarily postmarketing/literature data).*

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Addiction/abuse, life-threatening respiratory depression, NOWS, adrenal insufficiency, severe hypotension, GI bleeding/perforation, seizures, coagulation abnormalities, and Reye’s syndrome [US].
- **Systemic/General:** Fever, hypothermia, thirst.
- **Cardiovascular:** Dysrhythmias, hypotension, tachycardia.
- **Central Nervous System:** Agitation, cerebral edema, coma, confusion, dizziness, headache, lethargy, somnolence.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Dyspepsia, nausea, vomiting, transient elevation of hepatic enzymes, hepatitis, pancreatitis.
- **Hematologic:** Prolonged prothrombin time, thrombocytopenia, coagulopathy.
- **Metabolic/Fluid:** Hyperkalemia, metabolic acidosis, hypoglycemia (noted specifically in children), hyperglycemia.
- **Special Senses:** Tinnitus, hearing loss (tinnitus may be masked in patients with high-frequency loss).
- **Urogenital:** Interstitial nephritis, papillary necrosis, renal failure, proteinuria.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors (e.g., erythromycin, ketoconazole, ritonavir):** Increase hydrocodone exposure; may lead to respiratory depression [US].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., rifampin, carbamazepine):** Decrease hydrocodone efficacy or precipitate withdrawal [US].
- **CNS Depressants (including alcohol):** Additive effects on respiratory depression and sedation [US].
- **Serotonergic Drugs:** Increased risk of serotonin syndrome [US].
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Interaction may manifest as serotonin syndrome or opioid toxicity; avoid use within 14 days of MAOI therapy [US].
- **Anticoagulants/Antiplatelets (e.g., warfarin, heparin, clopidogrel):** Aspirin increases bleeding risk and may displace warfarin from protein binding sites [US].
- **NSAIDs:** Avoid concomitant use; increases GI toxicity and renal impairment risk [US].
- **Antihypertensives (ACE Inhibitors, Beta Blockers):** Aspirin may diminish hypotensive effects due to renal prostaglandin inhibition [US].
- **Methotrexate:** Aspirin may increase methotrexate toxicity by reducing renal clearance and displacing protein binding [US].
- **Uricosuric Agents (e.g., probenecid):** Aspirin inhibits their uricosuric effects [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:**
    - **Category:** Not explicitly letter-coded in updated labeling, but follows specific risk warnings [US].
    - **Fetal Toxicity:** Avoid NSAID use (including aspirin) at ~20 weeks gestation due to risk of fetal renal dysfunction/oligohydramnios; avoid at ~30 weeks gestation due to risk of premature closure of the fetal ductus arteriosus [US].
    - **NOWS:** Chronic use in pregnancy leads to neonatal withdrawal [US].
    - **Labor/Delivery:** Aspirin should be avoided one week prior to and during labor due to risk of excessive blood loss. Opioids may cause neonatal respiratory depression [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Both hydrocodone and salicylic acid are present in human milk. Monitor infants for excess sedation, respiratory depression, and adverse effects on platelet function. The risk of Reye's syndrome from salicylate in breast milk is unknown [US].
- **Fertility:** Long-term opioid use may cause reduced fertility in males and females. Aspirin may reversibly delay ovulation [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established. Contraindicated in children/teenagers with viral infections due to Reye’s syndrome [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased sensitivity to respiratory depression and NSAID-related GI/cardiovascular/renal events. Start at the low end of the dosing range and monitor renal function [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Hydrocodone levels may increase. Use low initial doses. **Avoid aspirin in severe hepatic impairment** [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Hydrocodone levels may increase. Use low initial doses. **Avoid aspirin in severe renal impairment (GFR <10 mL/min)** [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:**
    - **Hydrocodone:** Full opioid agonist with relative selectivity for the mu-opioid receptor, providing analgesia [US].
    - **Aspirin:** NSAID that inhibits prostaglandin synthesis by irreversibly inactivating cyclooxygenase (COX) via acetylation [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:**
    - **Hydrocodone:** Affects the CNS (respiratory centers, miosis), GI tract (reduced motility), and endocrine system (inhibits ACTH/cortisol/LH, stimulates prolactin/GH) [US].
    - **Aspirin:** Inhibits platelet aggregation (effect lasts the life of the platelet) and acts on the hypothalamus to reduce fever [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Hydrocodone:** Peak levels at 1.3 hours; half-life ~3.8 hours. Metabolized primarily via CYP3A4 (N-demethylation) and CYP2D6 (O-demethylation to hydromorphone). Excreted primarily via kidneys [US].
    - **Aspirin:** Rapidly absorbed and hydrolyzed to salicylic acid. Salicylate half-life is ~6 hours but increases with dose (up to 20+ hours in toxicity). Excretion is pH-dependent and follows zero-order kinetics at high doses [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F-86°F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from moisture. Dispense in tight, light-resistant containers with child-resistant closures [US].
- **Disposal:** Unused medication should be disposed of via drug take-back programs or by flushing down the toilet if no program is available (due to high risk of accidental fatal ingestion) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:**
    - **United States:** Schedule II Controlled Substance (CII). Rx-only.
