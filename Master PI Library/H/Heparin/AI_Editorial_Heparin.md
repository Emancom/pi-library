# Editorial Product Information: Heparin Sodium

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Practitioners should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved PI for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Heparin sodium (porcine-derived).
- **Chemical Class:** Anticoagulant; glycosaminoglycan (sulfated polysaccharide).
- **Brand Names:** Baxter Heparin Sodium in 0.9% Sodium Chloride [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources (Commonly B01AB01).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile, nonpyrogenic clear solution for intravenous (IV) infusion [Australian].
    - Available in 1000 mL (2000 IU heparin sodium) and 500 mL (1000 IU heparin sodium) bags, providing a concentration of 2 IU/mL [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous infusion only. Not for oral or intramuscular use [Australian].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Extracorporeal Circulation:** Anticoagulant use during procedures involving extracorporeal circuits [Australian].
- **Dialysis:** Anticoagulant maintenance during dialysis procedures [Australian].
- **Catheter Patency:** Utilised as an aid to maintain the patency of intravenous catheters [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** Dosage must be individualised based on the patient’s age, weight, clinical status, and laboratory response (coagulation tests) [Australian].
- **Catheter Patency:** An infusion rate of 3 mL/hour (6 IU/hour) has been found satisfactory for maintaining patency, though this may vary based on clinical conditions [Australian].
- **Extracorporeal Therapy:** 
    - **Priming:** The 2 units/mL formulation is used for priming the apparatus to prevent initial clotting [Australian].
    - **Systemic Heparinisation:** Therapeutic doses (e.g., 5,000 to 20,000 units) must be initiated in the patient and maintained according to specific equipment manufacturer directions to prevent blood path obstruction [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Use aseptic technique.
    - Inspect for particulate matter and discolouration.
    - Do not use in series connections or with vented administration sets in the open position due to air embolism risk [Australian].
    - Do not add supplementary medications to the infusion bag [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources (Infusion-based therapy).

## 4. Contraindications
- **Heparin-Induced Thrombocytopenia (HIT):** History of HIT (with or without thrombosis) within the previous 6 months, or any patient currently testing positive for HIT antibodies [Australian].
- **Severe Thrombocytopenia:** Pre-existing severe reduction in platelet count [Australian].
- **Monitoring Limitations:** Inability to perform appropriate blood coagulation tests at required intervals (applies to full therapeutic dosing) [Australian].
- **Active Bleeding:** Uncontrollable active bleeding states, except when due to disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC) [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to heparin, porcine derivatives, or any excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Neuraxial Haematomas:** Patients undergoing spinal/epidural anaesthesia or spinal puncture while anticoagulated are at risk of developing spinal or epidural haematomas, which may cause permanent paralysis. Risk is increased with indwelling catheters and concomitant NSAIDs or platelet inhibitors [Australian].
- **Heparin-Induced Thrombocytopenia (HIT/HITT):** A serious antibody-mediated reaction. "Rapid onset" HIT can occur within 24 hours in patients exposed to heparin in the prior 3 months. "Delayed onset" may occur weeks after stopping therapy. If the platelet count drops >50% from baseline or falls below 100,000/mm³, discontinue all heparin sources immediately [Australian].
- **Hemorrhage:** Can occur at any site. Use with extreme caution in subacute bacterial endocarditis, severe hypertension, major surgery (especially CNS or ophthalmic), and conditions with increased bleeding tendencies [Australian].
- **Hyperkalaemia:** Heparin may suppress aldosterone secretion. Monitor potassium levels in patients with diabetes, renal failure, metabolic acidosis, or those taking potassium-sparing drugs [Australian].
- **White Clot Syndrome:** New thrombus formation associated with thrombocytopenia due to platelet aggregation [Australian].
- **Heparin Resistance:** Often observed in patients with fever, infections, MI, cancer, or post-surgery. Coagulation tests (Anti-Factor Xa) should be monitored closely [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Vascular/Haemorrhagic:** Hemorrhage is the most common complication. Sites include gastrointestinal, adrenal (leading to acute insufficiency), retroperitoneal, and ovarian (corpus luteum) [Australian].
- **Haematologic:** Thrombocytopenia (incidence up to 30%), HIT, and HITT [Australian].
- **Metabolic:** Hyperkalaemia, hyperlipidaemia (and rebound hyperlipidaemia upon discontinuation) [Australian].
- **Skeletal:** Osteoporosis following long-term administration of high doses [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Chills, fever, urticaria (common); asthma, rhinitis, lacrimation, and anaphylactoid shock (rare). Itching/burning on the soles of the feet may occur [Australian].
- **Dermatologic:** Cutaneous necrosis, transient alopecia [Australian].
- **Reproductive:** Priapism, ovarian cysts [Australian].
- **Laboratory:** Significant elevations in AST (SGOT) and ALT (SGPT) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Platelet Inhibitors:** NSAIDs (e.g., aspirin, ibuprofen), clopidogrel, dextran, and glycoprotein IIb/IIIa antagonists increase bleeding risk. Dose reduction of either agent may be necessary [Australian].
- **Oral Anticoagulants:** Heparin prolongs prothrombin time (PT). Wait 5 hours after IV or 24 hours after SC heparin before drawing blood for a valid PT/INR [Australian].
- **Nitroglycerin (IV):** May decrease the anticoagulant effect of heparin; monitor PTT closely during co-administration [Australian].
- **Hyperkalaemia-inducing Agents:** ACE inhibitors and Angiotensin-II antagonists increase the risk of elevated potassium [Australian].
- **Antagonistic Agents:** Digitalis, tetracyclines, nicotine, and antihistamines may partially counteract heparin's effect [Australian].
- **Antithrombin III:** Enhances the anticoagulant effect of heparin [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category C. Heparin does not cross the placental barrier, but there are no adequate data in pregnant women. Use only if clearly needed [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Heparin is not excreted in human milk. Use with caution after weighing risks and benefits [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No animal studies on mutagenesis or impairment of fertility have been conducted [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly:** Higher incidence of bleeding reported in patients >60 years, particularly women. Lower doses may be indicated [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution; may result in sodium retention [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution in patients with liver disease and impaired haemostasis [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Heparin acts as an indirect anticoagulant by accelerating the activity of Antithrombin III (heparin cofactor). This complex inactivates Factor Xa and Thrombin (Factor IIa), preventing the conversion of prothrombin to thrombin and fibrinogen to fibrin [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Heparin inhibits clotting in vitro and in vivo. It does not possess fibrinolytic activity and will not lyse existing clots [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Not specified in available sources [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Catheter Patency:** Clinical trials have not demonstrated the superiority of heparin over normal saline in maintaining catheter patency [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Handling:** Single use only; discard any unused portion. Do not reconnect partially used bags [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Medicine) [Australian].
