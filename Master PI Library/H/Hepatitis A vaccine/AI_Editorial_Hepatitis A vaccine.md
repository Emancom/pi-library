# Editorial Product Information: Hepatitis A Vaccine (Inactivated)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following jurisdiction-specific Product Information (PI) source:
    - **United States** (Food and Drug Administration)
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **HAVRIX** for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Note**: This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference and does not replace the official, legally approved PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Hepatitis A virus (Strain HM175) antigen, inactivated with formalin and adsorbed onto aluminum hydroxide.
- **Brand Name**: **HAVRIX** [US].
- **ATC Code**: J07BC01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s)**:
    - Injectable suspension, supplied in single-dose vials or prefilled syringes [US].
    - **Pediatric/Adolescent**: 720 ELISA Units (EL.U.) per 0.5 mL.
    - **Adult**: 1440 EL.U. per 1.0 mL.
- **Route of Administration**: Intramuscular (IM) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: GlaxoSmithKline Biologicals [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Active Immunization**: Prevention of disease caused by the hepatitis A virus (HAV) in individuals aged 12 months and older [US].
- **Timing**: The primary immunization course should be completed at least 2 weeks prior to expected exposure to HAV [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Pediatric and Adolescent (12 months through 18 years)**:
    - Primary dose: 0.5 mL (720 EL.U.) at Month 0.
    - Booster dose: 0.5 mL (720 EL.U.) administered between 6 and 12 months after the first dose [US].
- **Adult (19 years and older)**:
    - Primary dose: 1.0 mL (1440 EL.U.) at Month 0.
    - Booster dose: 1.0 mL (1440 EL.U.) administered between 6 and 12 months after the first dose [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Preparation**: Shake well to obtain a homogeneous, turbid, white suspension. Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration before use.
- **Site**: Administer via intramuscular injection. In adults, the deltoid muscle is preferred; in small children, the anterolateral aspect of the thigh may be used.
- **Precautions**: **Do not administer in the gluteal region**, as this may result in a suboptimal immune response [US]. Do not administer intravenously, intradermally, or subcutaneously.
- **Concomitant administration**: May be given simultaneously with immune globulin, provided different syringes and separate injection sites are used [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity**: Severe allergic reaction (e.g., anaphylaxis) following a previous dose of any hepatitis A-containing vaccine [US].
- **Component Allergy**: Known hypersensitivity to any component of the vaccine, including the antibiotic **neomycin** (residual amounts may be present) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Syncope**: Fainting can occur following the administration of injectable vaccines. Procedures should be in place to prevent injury from falls [US].
- **Anaphylaxis**: Appropriate medical treatment (e.g., epinephrine) must be immediately available to manage potential allergic reactions [US].
- **Altered Immunocompetence**: Immunocompromised individuals, including those on high-dose corticosteroids or cytotoxic therapy, may have a diminished immune response [US].
- **Limitations of Vaccine Effectiveness**:
    - Vaccination may not protect 100% of individuals.
    - Because of the long incubation period of HAV (15 to 50 days), the vaccine may not prevent infection if the individual was exposed shortly before or after vaccination [US].
- **Latex Sensitivity**: The tip cap and plunger stopper of the prefilled syringes are not made with natural rubber latex [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience
Adverse reaction rates vary by age group. The most common reaction is injection site soreness.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection-site soreness (56% adults, 21% children), Headache (14% adults), Irritability (42% infants 11-25 months) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Fatigue, fever (>37.5°C), nausea, anorexia, induration, redness, swelling at injection site, malaise |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Pharyngitis, upper respiratory tract infections, abdominal pain, diarrhea, vomiting, pruritus, rash, urticaria, arthralgia, myalgia, lymphadenopathy, insomnia, dysgeusia, photophobia, vertigo |

### Postmarketing Experience (Frequency Not Established)
The following events have been reported voluntarily:
- **Immune System**: Anaphylaxis, serum sickness-like syndrome.
- **Nervous System**: Convulsions, dizziness, encephalopathy, Guillain-Barré syndrome, multiple sclerosis, myelitis, neuropathy, paresthesia, syncope.
- **Vascular**: Vasculitis.
- **Hepatobiliary**: Hepatitis, jaundice.
- **Hematologic**: Thrombocytopenia.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Immune Globulin**: May be administered concomitantly. While antibody titers may be lower than when the vaccine is given alone, the seroconversion rate is generally not affected [US].
- **Other Vaccines**: Clinical studies indicate **HAVRIX** can be administered simultaneously with MMR, Varicella, DTaP, Hib, and Pneumococcal 7-valent conjugate vaccines without compromising the immune response to any component [US].
- **Immunosuppressants**: May reduce the vaccine's efficacy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**:
    - [US]: There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. However, available human data from inadvertent exposures (n=175) do not suggest an increased risk of major birth defects or miscarriage [US].
- **Breastfeeding**:
    - No data are available regarding the presence of the vaccine in human milk or its effects on milk production or the breastfed infant. The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be considered [US].
- **Fertility**: No animal studies have been conducted to evaluate the effect on fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Pediatric Use**: Safety and effectiveness are established for children aged 12 months and older. Use in infants under 12 months is not established [US].
- **Geriatric Use**: Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65+ to determine if they respond differently than younger subjects [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Patients with chronic liver disease may have a lower antibody response compared to healthy individuals, though most still achieve seroconversion after the booster dose [US].
- **Renal Impairment**: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: **HAVRIX** induces the production of specific antibodies against the hepatitis A virus (anti-HAV). The presence of these antibodies confers protection against the disease [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics**: Seroconversion (defined as anti-HAV ≥20 mIU/mL) occurs in 80-98% of adults 15 days after the first dose, and in 96% by one month post-vaccination [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics**: As a vaccine, standard pharmacokinetic studies (absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion) are not applicable.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy (Thailand Study)**: In a double-blind, randomized study of 40,119 children (1–16 years), two doses of **HAVRIX** (360 EL.U.) provided 94% protective efficacy against clinical hepatitis A [US].
- **Immunogenicity**: In pediatric trials (11–25 months), 99-100% of participants achieved seroconversion one month after a two-dose series. In adults, a booster dose following the primary dose resulted in a 100% seropositive rate with high geometric mean titers (GMTs) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store refrigerated between 2°C and 8°C (36°F and 46°F).
- **Handling**: **Do not freeze.** Discard the vaccine if it has been frozen.
- **Light**: Protect from light.
- **Stability**: Not specified in available sources.
