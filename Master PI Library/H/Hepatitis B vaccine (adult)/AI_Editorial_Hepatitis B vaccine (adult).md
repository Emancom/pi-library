# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised clinical overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It serves as an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 
- **Note:** At the time of this editorial, only US-specific documentation was available for review. Clinical recommendations and regulatory classifications are presented accordingly.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not explicitly stated in source; provided for clinical context).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with metabolic defects resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion.
    - **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 or 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours).
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg or as clinically required. Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - **Timing:** Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Adjustment:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels <40–50 µmol/L. Dose adjustments (e.g., reduction to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough levels.

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8 mg/mL.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and monitor the patient closely if re-administration is considered [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring:** For patients with metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality):
- **Very Common (>10%):**
    - **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), flu syndrome (25%), headache (22%), chest pain (12%).
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%).
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%).
    - **Nervous:** Dizziness (13%).
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%).
- **Common (1–10%):** Abdominal pain, asthenia, fever, infection, arrhythmia, tachycardia, nausea, anemia, hypercalcemia, hypervolemia, and cough.

### Postmarketing and General Observations
- **GI Effects:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent reports of body odor and gastritis.
- **Metabolic:** Accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) may occur with chronic high-dose **oral** therapy in patients with severe renal impairment [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients on anticoagulant therapy.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not assigned under current labelling, but data provided]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. While supplementation has not been specifically studied in nursing mothers, studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations after exogenous administration. Weigh the benefits to the mother against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant.
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is calculated by weight (50 mg/kg). Safety and efficacy in paediatric ESRD patients have not been specifically detailed in the source trials.
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency; chronic high oral doses should be avoided in ESRD due to metabolite accumulation. Levocarnitine injection is specifically indicated for use post-dialysis in ESRD.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Levocarnitine is readily removed by hemodialysis.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis (trough) carnitine levels from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Clinical Outcomes:** While levocarnitine injection effectively increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the impact of supplementation on specific clinical outcomes (e.g., malaise, muscle weakness) in this population has not been definitively determined.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C.
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Single-dose vial; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservative.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not applicable for US jurisdiction; generally handled as a prescription-only medicine.
