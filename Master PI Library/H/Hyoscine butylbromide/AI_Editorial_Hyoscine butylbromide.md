# Editorial Product Information: Hyoscine Butylbromide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data into a concise, structured format.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory guidelines for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Hyoscine butylbromide (a quaternary ammonium derivative of hyoscine).
- **Chemical Class:** Anticholinergic/Antispasmodic.
- **Brand Names:** **Buscopan**, **Buscopan Forte** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A03BB01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Sugar-coated tablets: 10 mg [Australian].
    - Film-coated tablets: 20 mg [Australian].
    - Solution for injection (ampoules): 20 mg/1 mL [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral, Intramuscular (IM), or slow Intravenous (IV) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sanofi Consumer Healthcare / Sanofi-Aventis [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Gastrointestinal Spasm:** Relief of spasm of the gastrointestinal tract (all formulations) [Australian].
- **Biliary and Renal Spasm:** Relief of biliary and renal spasm (ampoules only) [Australian].
- **Radiological Diagnostic Aid:** Used as a diagnostic aid in radiology (ampoules only) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Oral Administration (Adults and Children > 6 years)
- **Standard Regimen:** 20 mg four times daily. 
- This may be administered as 2 x 10 mg tablets or 1 x 20 mg (Forte) tablet [Australian].
- **Pediatric use:** Not recommended for children under 6 years of age [Australian].

### Parenteral Administration (Adults)
- **Dose:** 1 or 2 ampoules (20–40 mg) via intramuscular or slow intravenous injection.
- **Maximum Daily Dose:** Should not exceed 100 mg [Australian].

### General Instructions
- Medications should not be taken on a continuous daily basis or for extended periods without investigating the cause of abdominal pain [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
**Hyoscine butylbromide** is contraindicated in patients with:
- Known hypersensitivity to hyoscine butylbromide or any excipients [Australian].
- Mechanical stenosis in the gastrointestinal tract [Australian].
- Achalasia and megacolon [Australian].
- Paralytic or obstructive ileus [Australian].
- Intestinal atony [Australian].
- Prostatic hypertrophy with urinary retention [Australian].
- Myasthenia gravis [Australian].
- Glaucoma [Australian].
- Pathological tachyarrhythmias [Australian].
- Porphyria (based on a single report of disease exacerbation) [Australian].
- **Parenteral Specific:** Contraindicated for IM injection in patients treated with anticoagulant drugs due to the risk of intramuscular haematoma; IV route may be used instead [Australian].
- **Excipient Specific:** 10 mg tablets are contraindicated in rare hereditary fructose intolerance (due to sucrose); 20 mg tablets are contraindicated in rare hereditary galactose intolerance, Lapp lactose deficiency, or glucose-galactose malabsorption [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Severe Abdominal Pain:** If unexplained abdominal pain persists, worsens, or occurs with fever, nausea, vomiting, bowel movement changes, tenderness, hypotension, fainting, or blood in the stool, medical advice must be sought immediately [Australian].
- **Narrow-Angle Glaucoma:** Anticholinergics may increase intraocular pressure. Patients should seek urgent ophthalmological advice if they develop a painful, red eye with loss of vision following administration [Australian].
- **Anaphylaxis:** Cases of anaphylaxis and shock have been observed following parenteral administration. Patients receiving injections should remain under observation [Australian].
- **Cardiac Conditions:** Use with caution in patients with cardiac failure, coronary heart disease, arrhythmia, hypertension, or those undergoing cardiac surgery. Tachycardia and hypotension may occur; monitoring is advised, and emergency equipment should be available [Australian].
- **Drowsiness and Vision:** May cause drowsiness and visual accommodation disturbances. Patients should not drive or operate machinery until vision has normalised and they are unaffected by drowsiness [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are primarily attributed to the drug's anticholinergic properties and are generally mild and self-limiting.

| Organ Class | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Immune System** | **Tablets:** Dyspnoea, skin reactions (urticaria, rash, erythema, pruritus), facial/periorbital swelling, anaphylactic shock/reactions. **Ampoules:** Anaphylactic shock (including fatal outcomes) [Australian]. |
| **Cardiac** | Tachycardia [Australian]. |
| **Vascular** | Dizziness, flushing, and decreased blood pressure (ampoules) [Australian]. |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Dry mouth [Australian]. |
| **Skin** | Dyshidrosis, abnormal sweating [Australian]. |
| **Renal/Urinary** | Impaired micturition, urinary retention [Australian]. |
| **Eye** | Mydriasis, accommodation disorders, increased intraocular pressure (ampoules) [Australian]. |
| **Nervous System** | Very rarely (post-marketing): coma, hallucinations, dystonia, confusion, agitation, and dizziness following parenteral use [Australian]. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Potentiation of Anticholinergic Effects:** The effects of the following drugs may be enhanced:
    - Amantadine, tri- and tetracyclic antidepressants, quinidine, antihistamines, antipsychotics, disopyramide, phenothiazines, and other anticholinergics (e.g., tiotropium, ipratropium) [Australian].
- **MAO Inhibitors:** May potentiate anticholinergic effects [Australian].
- **Beta-sympathomimetics:** Tachycardia induced by these agents may be potentiated [Australian].
- **Dopamine Antagonists:** Drugs such as metoclopramide may reciprocally antagonise the effects of hyoscine on GI tract motility [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category **B2**. There is limited data on use in pregnant women. As a precaution, use should be avoided during pregnancy [Australian].
- **Lactation:** Insufficient information exists regarding the excretion of hyoscine butylbromide or its metabolites in human milk. Avoid use during lactation as a precautionary measure [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for children over 6 years of age for oral tablets. Not recommended for children under 6 [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available in the provided source [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A quaternary ammonium compound that acts as an anticholinergic agent with high affinity for muscarinic receptors on smooth muscle. It also possesses a ganglion-blocking component. It reduces tone and peristalsis in smooth muscle of hollow organs [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Poorly absorbed from the GI tract. Oral bioavailability is approximately 0.13% [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Rapidly distributed. It has a high affinity for GI tract, liver, and kidney tissues. The distribution phase half-life (t-alpha) is approximately 3 minutes.
    - **Blood-Brain Barrier:** Due to its quaternary ammonium structure and low lipid solubility, it does not readily cross the blood-brain barrier [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Excretion rates are slow despite low measurable blood levels [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **10 mg Tablets:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **20 mg Tablets (Forte) and Ampoules:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

    - **Schedule 4 (S4 - Prescription Only Medicine):** 10 mg tablets (pack of 100) and 20 mg/1 mL ampoules [Australian].
