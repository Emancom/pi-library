# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis was developed using the official Product Information provided by the **US (FDA)** (Sponsor: Baxter Healthcare Corporation). 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically focused overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory guidance and pharmacological data.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (also known as L-carnitine); a naturally occurring carrier molecule essential for fatty acid metabolism.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative [(R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium].
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile injection, 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with metabolic defects resulting in secondary deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients undergoing chronic haemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion. 
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the next 24 hours. 
    - Administration frequency: Every 3 to 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours).
    - Maximum recorded dose: 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Haemodialysis:** 
    - Starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - Administration: Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - Dose adjustment: Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) can be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### 3.2 Administration and Monitoring
- Inspect vials for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal oedema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, particularly in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and provide appropriate medical care [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients both with and without pre-existing seizure disorders. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation):** While this PI focuses on the injection, it notes that chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events observed in clinical trials, particularly in ESRD patients, are often confounded by underlying pathology.

### 6.1 Frequency Data (ESRD Population)
| Frequency | Adverse Events |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site reaction (33–38%), Pharyngitis (15–27%), Headache (3–37%), Hypertension (18–21%), Diarrhoea (9–35%), Vomiting (9–21%), Nausea (5–12%), Pain (21–35%), Accidental injury (8–12%), Asthenia (8–12%), Chest pain (6–15%), Dyspnoea (3–11%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Abdominal pain, Fever, Allergic reaction, Arrhythmia, Hypotension, Tachycardia, Hypercalcaemia, Hyperkalaemia, Weight decrease, Dizziness, Insomnia, Pruritus, Rash |

### 6.2 Serious Adverse Effects
- **Anaphylaxis and Severe Hypersensitivity:** Potentially life-threatening [US].
- **Seizures:** Reported during post-marketing surveillance [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalised Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. 
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels closely upon initiation of levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin anticoagulation [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned, but follows former Category B profile]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine in milk following administration. Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the maternal benefits. Consider discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See Section 3 for weight-based dosing. Safety and efficacy are supported for use in children [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources; generally follow weight-based dosing.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for ESRD. Monitor for accumulation of metabolites if transition to oral therapy is considered in severe renal impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) and γ-butyrobetaine.
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** Clinical trials in ESRD patients undergoing haemodialysis (receiving 20 mg/kg 3 times weekly) demonstrated that levocarnitine significantly increases plasma carnitine concentrations. Pre-dialysis (trough) levels rose from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks of therapy. 
- **Note:** While efficacy in increasing carnitine levels is established, the impact on specific clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].
- **Stability (Admixture):** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s; stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags [US].
