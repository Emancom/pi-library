# Editorial Product Information: Amiloride Hydrochloride and Hydrochlorothiazide

**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been synthesized from the official regulatory filings of the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). It provides a comprehensive overview for healthcare professionals by combining the most conservative safety parameters and clinical guidance from both agencies. Where specific regional differences exist, the source agency is noted in brackets.

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredients
*   **Active Ingredients:** Amiloride hydrochloride and Hydrochlorothiazide.
*   **Formulation:** Each tablet typically contains 5 mg of amiloride hydrochloride (calculated on an anhydrous basis) and 50 mg of hydrochlorothiazide.

## 2. Approved Indications
This combination therapy is indicated for the management of:
*   **Hypertension:** Specifically in patients where potassium depletion is anticipated or where the maintenance of normal serum potassium levels is clinically essential (e.g., patients with significant cardiac arrhythmias or those taking digitalis).
*   **Edema of Cardiac Origin:** For the treatment of congestive heart failure.
*   **Hepatic Cirrhosis with Ascites:** [TGA]
*   **Note on Usage:** This fixed-dose combination is generally not recommended for the initial therapy of edema or hypertension, except in patients where the development of hypokalemia cannot be risked [FDA].

## 3. Contraindications
*   **Hyperkalaemia:** Serum potassium levels exceeding 5.5 mmol/L (5.5 mEq/L).
*   **Concurrent Antikaliuretic Therapy:** Use of other potassium-sparing agents (e.g., spironolactone, triamterene) or potassium supplements.
*   **Renal Impairment:** Anuria, acute or chronic renal failure, and diabetic nephropathy. The drug should be withheld if blood urea nitrogen (BUN) exceeds 10.7 mmol/L (30 mg/dL) or serum creatinine exceeds 0.13 mmol/L (1.5 mg/dL) [Unified].
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to amiloride, hydrochlorothiazide, or any sulfonamide-derived drugs.

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Hyperkalaemia
The most significant risk associated with this combination is hyperkalaemia, which can be fatal if uncorrected. The risk is approximately 1–2% in patients without renal impairment or diabetes but increases significantly in the elderly, severely ill patients, or those with underlying renal dysfunction. Serum potassium must be monitored closely, particularly during initiation and dosage adjustment.

### Non-Melanoma Skin Cancer (NMSC)
Epidemiological studies have shown an increased, dose-dependent risk of non-melanoma skin cancer (basal cell and squamous cell carcinoma) with cumulative exposure to hydrochlorothiazide. Patients should be advised to limit sun/UV exposure and regularly check for new or changing skin lesions [TGA/FDA].

### Respiratory Toxicity
Severe, acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) has been reported following hydrochlorothiazide use. Onset typically occurs minutes to hours after ingestion. If ARDS is suspected, the drug must be discontinued immediately [TGA].

### Metabolic and Ocular Effects
*   **Diabetes Mellitus:** Thiazides may impair glucose tolerance and increase insulin requirements. Latent diabetes may become manifest.
*   **Electrolyte Imbalance:** Monitor for hyponatremia, hypochloraemic alkalosis, and hypomagnesemia. 
*   **Ocular Disorders:** Hydrochlorothiazide can cause an idiosyncratic reaction resulting in choroidal effusion, acute myopia, or secondary angle-closure glaucoma [TGA].

## 5. Dosage and Administration
Tablets should be administered with food to improve gastrointestinal tolerance.
*   **General Dosage:** The usual starting dose is one tablet daily. This may be increased to a maximum of two tablets per day if necessary.
*   **Maintenance:** Once diuresis is achieved, the dose should be reduced to the lowest effective level. Maintenance may be performed on an intermittent basis.
*   **Maximum Dose:** Dosages exceeding four tablets per day are generally not recommended [TGA].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The combination is generally well tolerated, with most side effects related to diuresis or the underlying disease state.
*   **Common (>1%):** Headache, fatigue, nausea, diarrhea, and dizziness.
*   **Cardiovascular:** Arrhythmia, tachycardia, and orthostatic hypotension.
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Anorexia, gastrointestinal pain, and constipation.
*   **Integumentary:** Rash, pruritus, and diaphoresis.
*   **Laboratory:** Elevated serum potassium, increased BUN, and hyperuricemia.
*   **Rare/Severe:** Aplastic anemia, agranulocytosis, necrotizing angiitis, and interstitial nephritis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **ACE Inhibitors/ARBs:** Increased risk of hyperkalaemia. Use with extreme caution and frequent potassium monitoring.
*   **NSAIDs:** May reduce the diuretic and antihypertensive effects and increase the risk of renal failure and hyperkalaemia.
*   **Lithium:** Diuretics reduce the renal clearance of lithium, significantly increasing the risk of lithium toxicity. Concurrent use is generally not recommended.
*   **Antidiabetic Drugs:** Dosage adjustments of insulin or oral agents may be required.
*   **Cholestyramine/Colestipol:** These resins bind hydrochlorothiazide and impair its absorption; doses should be separated by several hours.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** Classified as Category C [TGA]. Diuretics are not recommended for routine use in healthy pregnant women. Thiazides cross the placental barrier and carry risks of fetal/neonatal jaundice and thrombocytopenia. Use only if the potential benefit justifies the risk to the fetus.
*   **Breastfeeding:** Thiazides are excreted in breast milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants, a decision should be made to either discontinue nursing or discontinue the drug.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Amiloride:** Onset of action occurs within 2 hours of oral administration. Peak electrolyte effects are reached between 6 and 10 hours, with a duration of approximately 24 hours. It is not metabolized by the liver and is excreted unchanged in the urine (50%) and feces (40%).
*   **Hydrochlorothiazide:** Diuresis begins within 2 hours, peaks at 4 hours, and lasts 6 to 12 hours. It is eliminated rapidly by the kidneys, with at least 61% of the dose excreted unchanged within 24 hours.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
This product combines two diuretic agents with different but complementary sites of action:
1.  **Amiloride Hydrochloride:** A potassium-conserving (antikaliuretic) agent that inhibits sodium reabsorption in the distal convoluted tubule and collecting duct. This reduces the electrical potential across the tubular membrane, thereby decreasing the secretion and subsequent excretion of potassium and hydrogen ions.
2.  **Hydrochlorothiazide:** A natriuretic diuretic that interferes with the renal tubular mechanism of electrolyte reabsorption, increasing the excretion of sodium and chloride in roughly equivalent amounts.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   Store below 30°C (86°F) [TGA]. 
*   The FDA specifically recommends storage between 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) in tight, light-resistant containers.
*   Keep all medications out of the reach of children.
