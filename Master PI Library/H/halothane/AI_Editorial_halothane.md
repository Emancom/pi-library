# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: US (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the United States (FDA) documentation was available for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule involved in mitochondrial fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Name:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Generic; Baxter Healthcare Corp).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not specified in source, provided for clinical reference).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Instructions
- Administer intravenously.
- Parenteral products should be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [US].

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; frequency should never be less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg or as clinically required.
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Monitoring
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations.
- Recommended weekly and monthly monitoring including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- None.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine (oral or IV) has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with existing seizure disorders, increased frequency or severity has been reported [US].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Renal Impairment (Oral formulation caveat):** While this PI describes the injection, note that chronic high-dose *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common and Serious Adverse Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting are common. Gastritis and abdominal pain have been observed.
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (see Section 5). Dizziness (10–18% in trials) [US].
- **Other:** Body odor [US].

### Clinical Trial Data (ESRD Patients)
In placebo-controlled trials of ESRD patients on hemodialysis, the following events occurred at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) [US]:
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), Hypotension (3–19%), Tachycardia (5–9%), Chest pain (6–15%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), Cough increase (10–18%), Dyspnea (3–11%).
- **Systemic:** Headache (3–37%), Infection (10–24%), Injection site reaction (27–38%), Pain (21–35%), Fever (5–12%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (9–35%), Vomiting (9–21%).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concurrently with levocarnitine. Monitor INR levels after initiation or dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not assigned under PLR]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies in nursing mothers have not been conducted. Levocarnitine is known to increase in dairy cow milk following administration. Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of maternal supplementation. Consider discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, S. cerevisiae, S. pombe) were negative. Long-term animal carcinogenicity studies have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See Section 3 (Dosage and Administration). Dosing is weight-based (mg/kg) for both metabolic disorders and ESRD [US].
- **Elderly:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** See Section 3 for ESRD dosing. Safety of oral levocarnitine is not established in renal failure due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. In patients with fatty acid metabolism defects or organic acidopathies, it promotes the excretion of excess organic acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Two-compartment model following IV bolus (20 mg/kg) [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Efficacy:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg post-dialysis three times weekly showed mean pre-dialysis (trough) levocarnitine levels increased from ~20 µmol/L at baseline to 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses in ESRD patients demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature (25°C) when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion. Does not contain preservatives [US].
