# Editorial Product Information: human c1 esterase inhibitor

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **human c1 esterase inhibitor**
- Chemical class: Serine protease inhibitor (member of the serine protease inhibitor family)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intravenous

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of acute attacks in patients with hereditary angioedema (HAE).  
- Use for other indications (including prophylaxis, capillary leak syndrome therapy) is not established or is not advised in available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General dosing (adult and paediatric)
- Recommended dose for treatment of acute HAE attacks: 20 IU per kg body weight (adult and paediatric patients).  
- A lower dose (10 IU/kg) was evaluated and did not show clinically significant benefit compared with placebo in the pivotal trial.

### Dosing by indication
- Acute HAE attacks (all locations: abdominal, facial, peripheral, laryngeal): recommended single intravenous dose of 20 IU/kg. No other indication-specific dosing regimens are specified in available sources.

### Administration instructions
- Treatment should be initiated under supervision of a physician experienced in C1 esterase inhibitor deficiency.
- Doses should be expressed in International Units written in full.
- Reconstitution and withdrawal must be performed under aseptic conditions.
- Use a silicone‑free syringe or the syringe supplied. Bring diluent to room temperature prior to use.
- A sterile filter transfer set is provided and intended to filter the contents of a single vial only; use a separate filter transfer set for each vial if multiple vials are required.
- Reconstitute by transferring diluent into the product vial using the provided transfer set, gently swirl until fully dissolved; do not shake. Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration; do not use if cloudy or containing particulates.
- Draw reconstituted concentrate into a sterile syringe via the transfer set then disconnect the transfer set prior to intravenous administration.
- The product must not be mixed with other medicinal products or other diluents.

### Recommended administration rates and post-reconstitution handling
- For lower-concentration presentation: recommended administration by slow intravenous injection at approximately 4 mL/minute. If not administered immediately, use within 24 hours if stored refrigerated (2–8°C); allow to reach room temperature before administration.  
- For higher-concentration presentation: recommended administration as a bolus intravenous injection. If not administered immediately, use within 8 hours if stored refrigerated (2–8°C); allow to reach room temperature before administration.
  - Note: These timeframes apply to reconstituted product as specified in available sources.

### Self-administration / home treatment
- Self-administration or home treatment may be considered if the treating physician deems it appropriate and adequate training has been provided.
- Ensure patient/carer training covers aseptic reconstitution, intravenous administration, recognition and initial management of hypersensitivity/allergic reactions, and appropriate actions for laryngeal or severe attacks (seek urgent medical care/ambulance).
- Patients/carers should be instructed not to initiate treatment if the attack has progressed to a stage where safe preparation/administration is unlikely.
- For abdominal attacks, patients should contact their physician after self-treatment to exclude other serious causes.

### Titration and missed doses
- Titration schedules: Not specified in available sources.
- Missed dose instructions: Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **human c1 esterase inhibitor** or any component of the product.  
- No additional region‑specific contraindications are specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed warnings
- None specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Hypersensitivity/anaphylactic reactions: Patients with known allergy tendency may receive prophylactic antihistamines and corticosteroids; if allergic or anaphylactic-type reactions occur, stop administration immediately and institute appropriate medical therapy (manage as per standard shock/anaphylaxis protocols).
- Laryngeal oedema: Patients with laryngeal attacks require particularly careful monitoring; given risk of airway obstruction, seeking immediate emergency care is advised in addition to treatment with **human c1 esterase inhibitor**.
- Capillary leak syndrome: Treatment of capillary leak syndrome with **human c1 esterase inhibitor** is not advised (see Adverse Effects and Serious Events).
- Sodium content: product can contain substantial sodium (up to 486 mg per 100 mL of solution); consider this for patients on controlled sodium diets.
- Home treatment limitations: Data on home treatment are limited; risks include administration-related errors and management of adverse reactions (especially hypersensitivity). Regular review of home treatment suitability is recommended.
- Pathogen transmission: As a plasma‑derived product, there is a potential risk of transmitting infectious agents despite donor screening and virus reduction steps in manufacturing (pasteurisation, chromatographic purification, nanofiltration). Consider vaccination where appropriate and counsel patients about residual risk.

### Monitoring requirements
- Observe patients during and after infusion for signs of hypersensitivity, anaphylaxis and infusion reactions.
- Monitor patients receiving high cumulative or high single doses (particularly >90 IU/kg) for signs of thrombotic events; thrombosis has been reported in the context of high-dose use during extracorporeal circulation.
- Record product name and batch number in patient records each time the product is administered to maintain traceability.
- Inspect reconstituted solution visually prior to administration.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Use caution and clinical judgment for use in newborns and young children undergoing cardiopulmonary bypass or extracorporeal circulation, as thrombosis has been reported in this setting with high doses.
- Vaccination status should be reviewed and updated as appropriate given plasma‑derived product use.

  - Controlled clinical safety data in human pregnancy are not available. Reported clinical experience (observational) indicates good tolerance with no negative impact on mother or child in the immediate observation period after birth, but data are limited.
  - Use during pregnancy should be considered only if clearly needed.
  - Animal reproductive toxicity studies have not been conducted with this product.
  - Pregnancy classification systems (e.g., national categories) are not specified in available sources.
- Breastfeeding:
  - Safety during breastfeeding has not been established in controlled clinical trials. Use during lactation should be considered only if clearly indicated.
- Fertility:

## 10. Use in Special Populations

### Paediatric use
- The recommended dose (20 IU/kg) applies to paediatric patients as well as adults.

### Elderly use

## 11. Pharmacology

### Mechanism of action
- **Human c1 esterase inhibitor** is a serine protease inhibitor that inhibits activated C1 components of the complement system (C1r and C1s), plasma kallikrein, and coagulation factors XIIa and XIa, thereby modulating complement, contact (kallikrein–kinin), fibrinolytic and coagulation cascade activity. Replacement of deficient or malfunctioning C1 esterase inhibitor alleviates symptoms of hereditary angioedema by inhibiting excessive bradykinin production and complement activation.

### Pharmacodynamics
- In vitro inhibition of classical complement activity: IC50 ≈ 1.05 IU/mL (human plasma) and ≈ 1.01 IU/mL (rat plasma) as reported in available sources.
- On intravenous administration, the product is immediately available in the plasma with plasma concentrations corresponding to the administered dose.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Bioequivalence between different preserved clinical presentations (500 IU and 1500 IU vial strengths) has been established in healthy volunteers in a dedicated trial; detailed PK parameters (e.g., half‑life, clearance, volume of distribution) are not specified in available sources.
- Absorption: Intravenous administration provides immediate plasma availability.
- Metabolism and elimination: Not specified in available sources.

## 12. Clinical Trials

### Pivotal phase III trial
- Design: Prospective, multinational, randomised, parallel‑group, placebo‑controlled, dose‑finding, three‑arm, double‑blind clinical study.
- Population: 124 adult and paediatric subjects (ages 6–72) with C1 esterase inhibitor deficiency experiencing an acute moderate to severe abdominal or facial HAE attack.
- Randomisation arms: 10 IU/kg, 20 IU/kg, and placebo; administered by slow IV infusion.
- Primary outcomes and results:
  - Median time to onset of symptom relief: 20 IU/kg group = 30 minutes vs placebo = 90 minutes (statistically significant, p = 0.0025).
  - Median time to complete resolution of HAE symptoms: 20 IU/kg group = 4.9 hours vs placebo = 7.8 hours (statistically significant, p = 0.0237).
  - The 20 IU/kg dose was significantly more efficacious than 10 IU/kg or placebo; 10 IU/kg did not show clinically significant difference vs placebo.

### Open‑label extension study
- Design: Prospective, open‑label, uncontrolled multicentre extension.
- Population: 57 subjects (age range noted in available sources 10–53 years in the safety analysis) with multiple subsequent HAE attacks treated.
- Exposure: 1085 HAE attacks treated with 20 IU/kg per attack; locations included abdominal, facial, peripheral, and laryngeal attacks.
- Key observations:
  - Laryngeal attacks (per‑attack analysis): median time to initial onset of symptom relief = 0.25 hours; median time to complete resolution = 8.4 hours.
  - Safety profile across attacks was consistent with prior clinical trial data.

### Other clinical data
- Phase I study in healthy volunteers comparing different concentrations/presentations reported comparable safety and tolerability between presentations.
- No controlled data support use for prophylaxis or for capillary leak syndrome; high‑dose experimental uses in extracorporeal circulation were associated with thrombotic events.
