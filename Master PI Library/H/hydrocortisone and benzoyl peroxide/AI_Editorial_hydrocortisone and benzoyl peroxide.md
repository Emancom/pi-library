# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, dosage recommendations, and safety profiles.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule involved in long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative [(R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt].
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Metabolic Disorders:** For the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It should be administered as a slow bolus (over 2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion. 
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose may be given, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours).
    - Subsequent daily doses typically remain at 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised based on clinical requirement [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - Administration should occur as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - Dosage adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels [US].

### Administration and Monitoring
- **Monitoring:** Pre-therapy plasma carnitine levels should be obtained. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- **Preparation:** Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate the risks/benefits of re-administration [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Toxicity of Oral Metabolites:** High doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are usually excreted in urine [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** In ESRD patients, trough carnitine levels must be monitored to guide dose adjustments [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported at a frequency of ≥5% in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials (regardless of causality) include:

| Body System | Adverse Event (≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, vascular disorder |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, anorexia, dyspepsia |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight changes |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, depression, insomnia, paresthesia |
| **Respiratory** | Cough increase, dyspnea, pharyngitis, rhinitis |
| **Special Senses** | Amblyopia, eye disorder, taste perversion |
| **Urogenital** | Kidney failure, urinary tract infection |

### Postmarketing Experience
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent: body odor and gastritis [US].
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (in patients with and without prior history) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dosage adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not specified; animal data provided]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine after exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine must be weighed against maternal benefits. Consider discontinuing nursing or treatment [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy are established for the treatment of carnitine deficiency associated with inborn errors of metabolism; see Section 3 for dosing [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Oral formulations should be used with caution in patients with renal failure due to metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO). For ESRD patients on dialysis, the intravenous route is preferred; see Section 3 for specific dialysis dosing [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria. This provides the substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, intravenous administration increases plasma carnitine concentrations and may alleviate metabolic abnormalities caused by toxic organic acid accumulation [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and [³H]-γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Excretion:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Efficacy:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly showed a significant increase in pre-dialysis (trough) carnitine levels from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses in ESRD patients demonstrated dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks.
- **Clinical Outcome Note:** While IV levocarnitine increases carnitine levels in ESRD patients, its effect on specific clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, arrhythmias) has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation is preservative-free [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only. NDC 36000-368-10 (10 x 5 mL vials) [US].
