# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: United States (FDA). 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically focused overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with a documented inborn error of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion. 
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. 
    - Frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours. 
    - Subsequent daily maintenance doses are typically 50 mg/kg or as clinically indicated; the maximum reported dose is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** 
    - The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - Administer as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - Dosage adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma carnitine levels [US].

### Administration and Monitoring
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Monitor weekly or monthly. Target free carnitine concentrations should ideally be between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Preparation:** Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use. The product contains no preservatives; discard any unused portion.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, often within minutes of administration. These are reported primarily in ESRD patients. If a reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and initiate appropriate medical management. 
- **Seizure Risk:** Levocarnitine (both oral and IV) has been associated with reports of seizures. In patients with pre-existing seizure disorders, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **Oral Formulation Limitation:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. High-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects observed in clinical trials of ESRD patients (occurring at ≥5% frequency, regardless of causality) include:

| Body System | Adverse Reactions |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain, abdominal pain, asthenia |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, arrhythmia |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, anorexia, dyspepsia |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, depression, paresthesia |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis |
| **Other** | Pruritus, back pain, accidental injury |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and other severe hypersensitivity reactions [US].
- **Post-marketing Reports:** Seizures; transient nausea, vomiting, and body odor have also been noted.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate that levocarnitine may increase the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not specified; however, reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (BSA) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm.] Use during pregnancy only if clearly required [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration. Weigh the benefits to the mother against potential risks to the infant; consider discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no evidence of impaired fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing guidelines for metabolic disorders are the same for children and adults (see Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Chronic high doses of the **oral** form should be avoided due to the accumulation of metabolites (TMA/TMAO) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria. This process provides substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, it alleviates metabolic abnormalities resulting from carnitine loss during dialysis and reduced renal synthesis.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis), trough levels increased from a baseline of 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks. 
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg confirmed dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks. While carnitine levels increased, the impact on specific clinical outcomes in this population remains to be fully determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].
