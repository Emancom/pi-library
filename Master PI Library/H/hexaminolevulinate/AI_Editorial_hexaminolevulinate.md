# Unified Product Information: Hexaminolevulinate Hydrochloride

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Names:** Hexvix (Australia), Cysview (United States)
**Active Ingredient:** Hexaminolevulinate hydrochloride. 

Hexaminolevulinate hydrochloride is a hydrochloride salt of an ester of aminolevulinic acid. Each vial contains 100 mg of hexaminolevulinate hydrochloride, which is equivalent to 85 mg of the active hexaminolevulinate moiety.

## 2. Approved Indications
Hexaminolevulinate is an optical imaging agent indicated for diagnostic use during cystoscopic procedures. It serves as an adjunct to standard white light cystoscopy to aid in the detection and management of non-muscle invasive bladder cancer.

*   **Detection:** It is indicated for the detection of bladder carcinoma, including carcinoma in situ (CIS), in patients with known or suspected lesions based on prior cystoscopy [FDA].
*   **Surveillance:** It is indicated for patients undergoing surveillance cystoscopy for bladder cancer [FDA].
*   **Limitations of Use:** This agent is not a replacement for random bladder biopsies or other standard diagnostic procedures [FDA].

## 3. Contraindications
Hexaminolevulinate is contraindicated in the following populations:
*   Patients with a known hypersensitivity to the active substance, hexaminolevulinate, or to any derivatives of aminolevulinic acid.
*   Patients with porphyria.
*   **Gross Haematuria:** The agent is contraindicated in patients presenting with gross haematuria [TGA]. *Note: The FDA removed this as a specific contraindication in 2026, but it remains a primary contraindication in Australian labeling.*

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Anaphylaxis and Hypersensitivity
Serious, though rare, anaphylactic and anaphylactoid reactions have been reported. Healthcare providers must have advanced life support facilities and trained personnel immediately available during administration.

### Risk of Failed Detection
Hexaminolevulinate may not detect all malignant lesions. It is imperative to perform a thorough examination under white light before switching to blue light. Necrotic or heavily coated lesions may not exhibit fluorescence.

### False Positive Fluorescence
Inflammation, recent surgical trauma, scar tissue, and recent intravesical therapies (such as BCG immunotherapy or chemotherapy) can lead to false-positive fluorescence. Specifically, false-positive rates may be higher between 6 weeks and 90 days following BCG or chemotherapy [FDA]. Widespread inflammation should be excluded via white light cystoscopy before the product is instilled [TGA].

### Provider Considerations
Healthcare providers with red-green color blindness may be unable to accurately interpret the red fluorescence against the blue background [FDA].

## 5. Dosage and Administration
### Preparation
All steps must be performed using aseptic technique. The 100 mg powder vial must be reconstituted with the provided 50 mL diluent. The resulting solution has a concentration of 2 mg/mL of hexaminolevulinate hydrochloride. The solution should be clear to slightly opalescent and colorless to pale yellow.

### Administration
1.  **Empty the Bladder:** The bladder should be completely drained via a catheter before instillation.
2.  **Instillation:** Instill the full 50 mL of reconstituted solution into the bladder.
3.  **Retention:** The patient should retain the fluid for a minimum of 1 hour. The maximum recommended retention time is 3 hours [FDA].

### Cystoscopic Procedure
*   **Timing:** The cystoscopic examination should ideally begin within 30 to 60 minutes after the bladder is evacuated. The total time between instillation and the end of the procedure should not exceed 3 hours.
*   **Light Source:** Use only authorized blue light imaging equipment (wavelength 360–450 nm [FDA] or 380–450 nm [TGA]).
*   **Visualization:** The bladder should be distended with clear irrigation fluid. Blood or floating debris can interfere with visualization. A full map of the bladder should be performed under both white and blue light.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The majority of adverse reactions reported in clinical trials were transient and mild to moderate in intensity, consistent with standard cystoscopic procedures.

*   **Common (>1/100 to <1/10):** Bladder spasm (reported in approximately 2.0% of patients), dysuria (1.6%), hematuria (1.5%), and bladder pain (1.4%).
*   **General/Procedural:** Post-procedural pain and pyrexia.
*   **Uncommon/Post-marketing:** Cystitis, urinary tract infection, sepsis, headache, and nausea.
*   **Serious:** Anaphylactic shock (reported in fewer than 1 in 100,000 exposures).

## 7. Drug Interactions
No specific clinical drug interaction studies have been conducted with hexaminolevulinate.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
Data regarding the use of hexaminolevulinate in pregnant women are limited or unavailable. While animal studies in rats and rabbits did not show direct embryo-fetal toxicity at high doses, these studies were conducted without the photostimulation required for the drug's activity. As a precaution, hexaminolevulinate should be avoided during pregnancy.

### Breastfeeding
It is unknown if hexaminolevulinate or its metabolites are excreted in human milk. A risk to the infant cannot be excluded. The TGA recommends that breastfeeding be discontinued during treatment with this agent.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Systemic bioavailability of hexaminolevulinate following intravesical instillation is low, estimated at approximately 7% (range 5-10%).
*   **Distribution:** Radiolabeled studies show high concentrations of the agent localized within the bladder wall.
*   **Elimination:** The agent follows a biphasic elimination pattern. The initial disposition half-life is approximately 39 minutes, followed by a terminal half-life of roughly 76 hours.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Hexaminolevulinate is a prodrug and an ester of the heme precursor, aminolevulinic acid (ALA). Once instilled into the bladder, it preferentially enters the intracellular space of rapidly proliferating or neoplastic mucosal cells. 

Within these cells, it is metabolically converted into photoactive porphyrins, primarily Protoporphyrin IX (PpIX). When excited by blue light (360–450 nm), PpIX fluoresces, emitting a bright red light. Because neoplastic cells accumulate porphyrins to a higher degree than normal urothelium, malignant lesions appear red against a dark blue background, enhancing the contrast for the surgeon.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Unopened Kit:** Store the kit at room temperature (below 30°C [TGA] or between 20°C to 25°C [FDA]).
*   **Reconstituted Solution:** After preparation, the solution should be used immediately. If not used immediately, it may be stored under refrigeration at 2°C to 8°C for no more than 2 hours. Any solution not used within this 2-hour window must be discarded.
*   **Incompatibilities:** This medicinal product must not be mixed with other drugs or solutions.
