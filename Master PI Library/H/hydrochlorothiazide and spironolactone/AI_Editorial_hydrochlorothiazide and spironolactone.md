# Editorial Product Information: Spironolactone and Hydrochlorothiazide

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of the combination product spironolactone and hydrochlorothiazide, intended for use by healthcare professionals. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis of available regulatory information and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Spironolactone (an aldosterone antagonist) and Hydrochlorothiazide (a thiazide diuretic).
- **Brand Names:** Generic formulation (distributed by Safecor Health LLC/Mylan Pharmaceuticals).
- **ATC Code:** C03EA01 (Spironolactone and diuretics).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Oral tablets containing 25 mg spironolactone and 25 mg hydrochlorothiazide [US]. 
- **Physical Description:** Ivory, round, unscored tablets debossed with "Mover 41" on one side [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Manufactured for Mylan Pharmaceuticals Inc. (US); Distributed by Safecor Health LLC (US); Made in Australia by Alphapharm Pty Ltd.

## 2. Approved Indications
The combination is indicated for the following conditions when individual titration suggests these doses are appropriate:
- **Edematous conditions:**
    - Congestive heart failure: For management of edema and sodium retention when there is an inadequate response to or intolerance of other measures.
    - Cirrhosis of the liver: For maintenance therapy in patients with edema and/or ascites.
    - Nephrotic syndrome: When treatment of the underlying disease and other diuretics fail to provide adequate response.
- **Essential Hypertension:**
    - As adjunctive therapy for patients in whom other measures are inadequate.
    - For the treatment of diuretic-induced hypokalemia in hypertensive patients when other measures are considered inappropriate.
- **Cardiovascular Risk Reduction:** Indicated to lower blood pressure as part of comprehensive cardiovascular risk management [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** Dosage should be individualised by titration of the components.
- **Edema (Adult):** The usual maintenance dose is 100 mg of each component daily (four tablets). Dosing may range from 25 mg to 200 mg of each component daily based on initial titration. Diuresis typically occurs promptly, but effects may persist for 2 to 3 days after discontinuation due to the spironolactone component.
- **Essential Hypertension (Adult):** Usually 50 mg to 100 mg of each component daily, administered in a single or divided dose.
- **Administration:** Tablets may be taken with food. Food has been shown to increase the bioavailability of unmetabolized spironolactone by nearly 100% [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Anuria.
- Acute renal insufficiency or significant impairment of renal excretory function.
- Hyperkalemia or hypercalcemia.
- Addison’s disease.
- Hypersensitivity to thiazide diuretics, spironolactone, or other sulfonamide-derived drugs.
- Acute or severe hepatic failure [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Tumorigenicity:** Spironolactone has been shown to be a tumorigen in chronic toxicity studies in rats. Unnecessary use should be avoided [US].
- **Hyperkalemia:** Potassium supplementation should not be used concurrently. Severe hyperkalemia may occur, particularly in patients with renal impairment or those taking ACE inhibitors, ARBs, or NSAIDs.
- **Electrolyte Imbalance:** Monitor for hyponatremia, hypochloremic alkalosis, and hypomagnesemia. Thiazides may decrease urinary calcium excretion, leading to intermittent elevations in serum calcium.
- **Hepatic Coma:** Use with caution in patients with impaired hepatic function; minor electrolyte shifts may precipitate hepatic encephalopathy.
- **Systemic Lupus Erythematosus:** Thiazides may exacerbate or activate SLE.
- **Acute Angle-Closure Glaucoma:** Hydrochlorothiazide can cause an idiosyncratic reaction resulting in acute angle-closure glaucoma and acute myopia, typically occurring within hours to weeks of initiation.
- **Non-melanoma Skin Cancer:** Hydrochlorothiazide is associated with an increased risk of squamous cell carcinoma. Patients should be advised to limit sun exposure and undergo regular skin screening [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are listed by component and body system:

### Hydrochlorothiazide
- **Cardiovascular:** Orthostatic hypotension (potentiated by alcohol/narcotics).
- **Digestive:** Pancreatitis, jaundice, cramping, gastric irritation.
- **Hematologic:** Aplastic anemia, agranulocytosis, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia.
- **Skin/Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylactic reactions, necrotizing angiitis, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), photosensitivity.
- **Special Senses:** Transient blurred vision, xanthopsia.

### Spironolactone
- **Digestive:** Gastric bleeding, ulceration, gastritis.
- **Endocrine/Reproductive:** Gynecomastia (usually reversible), inability to maintain erection, irregular menses or amenorrhea, postmenopausal bleeding.
- **Nervous System:** Mental confusion, ataxia, lethargy.
- **Liver:** Mixed cholestatic/hepatocellular toxicity (rarely fatal).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Potassium-Sparing Agents/Supplements:** Co-administration with ACE inhibitors, ARBs, or potassium supplements increases the risk of severe hyperkalemia.
- **Lithium:** Diuretics reduce the renal clearance of lithium, greatly increasing the risk of lithium toxicity. Generally, should not be given concurrently.
- **NSAIDs:** May reduce the diuretic and antihypertensive effects of the combination. Indomethacin plus spironolactone is associated with severe hyperkalemia.
- **Digoxin:** Spironolactone increases the half-life of digoxin. Thiazide-induced electrolyte imbalances (hypokalemia) also increase the risk of digitalis toxicity.
- **Norepinephrine:** Both components reduce vascular responsiveness to pressor amines; caution is required during anesthesia.
- **Abiraterone:** Spironolactone may increase PSA levels; concomitant use is not recommended.
- **Cholestyramine:** Hyperkalemic metabolic acidosis has been reported when used concurrently with spironolactone.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - [US]: Spironolactone is associated with antiandrogenic effects in animals, including feminization of male fetuses. Hydrochlorothiazide crosses the placental barrier. Use in pregnancy requires weighing anticipated benefits against potential hazards to the fetus, including neonatal jaundice and thrombocytopenia.
- **Breastfeeding:** Canrenone (a metabolite of spironolactone) and hydrochlorothiazide are excreted in breast milk. Use is generally not recommended; if deemed essential, an alternative method of infant feeding should be instituted.
- **Fertility:** In animal studies, spironolactone caused reduced fertility and increased stillbirths at high doses [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** More frequent monitoring of serum electrolytes is recommended due to the higher likelihood of renal or hepatic impairment.
- **Renal Impairment:** Thiazides may precipitate azotemia; cumulative effects of the drug may develop.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Extreme caution is required due to the risk of precipitating hepatic coma.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - Spironolactone: Competitive antagonist of aldosterone at the sodium-potassium exchange site in the distal renal tubule.
    - Hydrochlorothiazide: Inhibits sodium and water reabsorption in the cortical diluting segment of the distal renal tubule. 
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - Spironolactone is rapidly metabolized into sulfur-containing products (e.g., TMS and HTMS) and canrenone, which are pharmacologically active. It is >90% protein bound.
    - Hydrochlorothiazide is rapidly absorbed; onset occurs within 1 hour and persists for 6 to 12 hours. It is excreted unchanged in the urine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Not specified in available sources. The document notes that while antihypertensive drugs generally reduce cardiovascular mortality/morbidity, there are no controlled trials specifically demonstrating risk reduction with this combination product [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- Protect from light [US].
