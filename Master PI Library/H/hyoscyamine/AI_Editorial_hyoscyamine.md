# Editorial Product Information: Hyoscyamine Sulfate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) and labeling data from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data into a concise, professional reference.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or SmPC required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. It is noted that the specific product reviewed (Hyoscyamine Sulfate SL by Bryant Ranch Prepack) is listed as a drug not currently found by the FDA to be safe and effective, and its labeling has not been approved by the FDA [US].

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Hyoscyamine sulfate (a principal anticholinergic/antispasmodic component of belladonna alkaloids).
- **Chemical Class:** Belladonna alkaloid; tertiary amine antimuscarinic.
- **Brand Names:** Hyoscyamine Sulfate SL [US].
- **ATC Code:** A03BA03 (Hyoscyamine).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Sublingual (SL) tablets, 0.125 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Sublingual or oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Bryant Ranch Prepack (Repackager); Acella Pharmaceuticals, LLC (Manufacturer) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
Hyoscyamine sulfate is indicated for the following [US]:
- **Functional Intestinal Disorders:** Symptomatic relief of functional GI disorders, including mild dysenteries and diverticulitis.
- **Antispasmodic Actions:** Control of visceral spasm and hypermotility in cystitis, pylorospasm, and associated abdominal cramps.
- **Peptic Ulcer & IBS:** Adjunctive therapy in the treatment of peptic ulcer and irritable bowel syndrome (IBS).
- **Secretory Control:** Control of gastric secretion and excessive pharyngeal, tracheal, and bronchial secretions.
- **Colic:** Symptomatic relief of biliary and renal colic (when used alongside appropriate analgesics).
- **Rhinitis:** Drying agent for the symptomatic relief of acute rhinitis.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Adolescents 12 years and older)
- **Standard Dose:** 1 to 2 tablets (0.125 mg to 0.250 mg) administered sublingually or orally [US].
- **Frequency:** Three or four times daily, taken 30 minutes to one hour before meals and at bedtime.
- **Titration:** Dosage may be increased to every four hours as needed based on symptom severity.
- **Maximum Dose:** Do not exceed 12 tablets (1.5 mg) in a 24-hour period [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets may be taken with or without water [US].
- For optimal effect on gastric secretion, doses should be timed 30–60 minutes prior to meals.

## 4. Contraindications
- Glaucoma (particularly narrow-angle).
- Obstructive uropathy (e.g., bladder neck obstruction due to prostatic hypertrophy).
- Obstructive diseases of the gastrointestinal tract (e.g., achalasia, pyloroduodenal stenosis).
- Paralytic ileus and intestinal atony (especially in elderly or debilitated patients).
- Severe ulcerative colitis or toxic megacolon.
- Unstable cardiovascular status in acute hemorrhage.
- Myasthenia gravis.
- Myocardial ischemia.
- **Pediatrics:** Not recommended for use in children under 12 years of age [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Heat Prostration:** Use in high environmental temperatures can lead to heat stroke due to suppression of sweat gland activity [US].
- **Diarrhea:** May be an early sign of incomplete intestinal obstruction, particularly in patients with an ileostomy or colostomy; use of hyoscyamine in this setting is inappropriate and potentially harmful.
- **CNS Effects:** May cause drowsiness, dizziness, or blurred vision. Patients should exercise caution when operating motor vehicles or machinery [US].
- **Psychiatric/Geriatric Effects:** Geriatric patients may experience excitement, agitation, or confusion even at usual doses.
- **Secondary Oral Conditions:** Prolonged use may decrease salivary flow, increasing the risk of dental caries, periodontal disease, and oral candidiasis [US].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Hiatal Hernia:** Use with caution in patients with hiatal hernia associated with reflux esophagitis.
- **Autonomic Neuropathy:** Use with extreme caution.
- **Cardiovascular Disease:** Use with extreme caution in hyperthyroidism, coronary heart disease, congestive heart failure, and cardiac arrhythmias. Tachycardia should be investigated prior to administration as anticholinergics may increase heart rate [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Frequencies are not specifically defined in available sources, but the following effects are associated with the anticholinergic class [US]:
- **Gastrointestinal:** Dryness of mouth, loss of taste, nausea, vomiting, constipation, bloated feeling.
- **Genitourinary:** Urinary hesitancy and retention, impotence.
- **Ocular:** Blurred vision, mydriasis, cycloplegia, increased ocular tension.
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, palpitations.
- **Central Nervous System:** Headache, nervousness, drowsiness, weakness, dizziness, insomnia, ataxia, speech disturbance, mental confusion/excitement (especially in the elderly).
- **Dermatologic/Allergic:** Decreased sweating, urticaria, dermal manifestations, allergic reactions, or drug idiosyncrasies.
- **Other:** Suppression of lactation.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Absorption Alteration:** Decreased GI motility and delayed gastric emptying may reduce the absorption of various other oral medications [US].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
Anticholinergic effects may be intensified when used concurrently with:
- Other anticholinergics.
- Antacids (may interfere with absorption).
- Antidiarrheals (adsorbents).
- Haloperidol.
- Phenothiazines.
- Tricyclic antidepressants.
- Certain antihistamines.

### Specific Interaction Management [US]
- **Ketoconazole:** Anticholinergics may increase gastrointestinal pH, potentially decreasing the absorption of ketoconazole.
- **Metoclopramide:** Hyoscyamine may antagonize the effects of metoclopramide on GI motility.
- **Potassium Chloride:** Increased risk of GI mucosal lesions due to delayed gastric emptying.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** US Category C. Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. Hyoscyamine crosses the placenta. It should be administered to pregnant women only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Hyoscyamine is excreted in human milk. Use is not recommended for nursing mothers as it may also suppress lactation [US].
- **Fertility:** Data regarding the effect on human fertility are not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Pediatric Use:** Not recommended for children under 12 years [US]. Infants and young children are highly susceptible to toxic effects. Paradoxical hyperexcitability may occur.
- **Geriatric Use:** Increased risk of excitement, agitation, drowsiness, or confusion. Elderly patients are more susceptible to constipation, dry mouth, and urinary retention (especially males with prostatic hypertrophy). Caution is advised due to the risk of precipitating undiagnosed glaucoma or memory impairment [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Dosing adjustments by eGFR or Child-Pugh score are not specified in available sources; however, hyoscyamine is primarily excreted unchanged in the urine [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Hyoscyamine inhibits the muscarinic actions of acetylcholine on autonomic effector cells innervated by postganglionic cholinergic nerves. It acts as a competitive antagonist at muscarinic receptors, reducing GI motility and secretions [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Similar to atropine but with greater potency in both central and peripheral effects.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Totally and completely absorbed via sublingual and oral administration [US].
    - **Distribution:** Rapidly disappears from the blood and distributes throughout the body.
    - **Metabolism:** Not extensively detailed in available source.
    - **Elimination:** Majority is excreted in the urine unchanged within the first 12 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Trials:** Not specified in available source documents. The provided PI notes that the drug has not been found by the FDA to be safe and effective via standard approval pathways [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at controlled room temperature between 20°C and 25°C (68°F–77°F).
- **Packaging:** Dispense in tight, light-resistant containers with child-resistant closures [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only. Listed as an "unapproved drug other" in marketing categories [US].
