# Editorial Product Information: Hydroxyzine Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official PI when prescribing or dispensing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this publication, only the US-specific documentation was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Hydroxyzine hydrochloride (a piperazine derivative).
- **Brand Names:** Generic Atarax [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Oral Tablet:** 10 mg, 25 mg, and 50 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral (subsequent doses may be oral after initial intramuscular administration) [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Manufactured by Emcure Pharmaceuticals Ltd. for Avet Pharmaceuticals Inc.; repackaged by Preferred Pharmaceuticals Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Anxiety and Tension:** Symptomatic relief of anxiety and tension associated with psychoneurosis [US].
- **Organic Disease States:** Adjunctive therapy in organic disease conditions where anxiety is manifested [US].
- **Pruritus:** Management of pruritus associated with allergic conditions, including chronic urticaria, atopic and contact dermatoses, and histamine-mediated pruritus [US].
- **Sedation:** Used as a sedative for premedication and following general anaesthesia [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Anxiety/Tension:** 50 mg to 100 mg, four times daily (q.i.d.) [US].
- **Pruritus:** 25 mg, three (t.i.d.) or four (q.i.d.) times daily [US].
- **Sedation (Pre/Post-Operative):** 50 mg to 100 mg [US].

### Dosing by Indication (Paediatric)
- **Anxiety/Tension and Pruritus:**
    - Children < 6 years: 50 mg daily in divided doses [US].
    - Children > 6 years: 50 mg to 100 mg daily in divided doses [US].
- **Sedation (Pre/Post-Operative):** 0.6 mg/kg of body weight [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Dosage should be adjusted based on the patient's individual response to therapy [US].
- The effectiveness for long-term use (defined as > 4 months) for anxiety has not been systematically assessed; periodic reassessment is required [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to hydroxyzine hydrochloride, cetirizine hydrochloride, or levocetirizine hydrochloride [US].
- **QT Interval:** Patients with a known prolonged QT interval [US].
- **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated in early pregnancy [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **QT Prolongation/Torsade de Pointes (TdP):** Post-marketing cases of QT prolongation and TdP have occurred, typically in patients with existing risk factors (e.g., heart disease, electrolyte imbalance, or concomitant arrhythmogenic drugs). Use with caution in patients with congenital long QT syndrome, family history of long QT, or recent myocardial infarction [US].
- **CNS Depression:** Hydroxyzine may induce drowsiness. Patients should be cautioned against driving or operating dangerous machinery [US].
- **Acute Generalized Exanthematous Pustulosis (AGEP):** Rare but serious skin reaction characterized by fever and sterile pustules. Discontinue immediately at the first sign of skin rash or hypersensitivity [US].
- **CNS Depressant Potentiation:** The drug potentiates the action of other CNS depressants (narcotics, barbiturates, alcohol). Dosage of concomitant depressants should be reduced [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Side effects are generally mild and transitory [US].

| Category | Adverse Effect | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Anticholinergic** | Dry mouth | Not specified |
| **Nervous System** | Drowsiness (transitory), Headache | Not specified |
| **Psychiatric** | Hallucinations | Rare (post-marketing) |
| **Cardiac** | QT prolongation, Torsade de Pointes | Rare (post-marketing) |
| **Skin** | Pruritus, rash, urticaria, AGEP, fixed drug eruptions | Rare (post-marketing) |
| **General** | Allergic reactions | Rare (post-marketing) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Involuntary motor activity (tremor/convulsions) has been reported, usually with doses significantly higher than recommended [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CNS Depressants:** Potentiates narcotics, non-narcotic analgesics, and barbiturates. Concomitant dosage reduction is required [US].
- **Alcohol:** Effects of alcohol may be increased [US].
- **QT Prolonging Agents:** Caution is recommended with Class 1A (quinidine) and Class III (amiodarone, sotalol) antiarrhythmics, certain antipsychotics (ziprasidone, quetiapine), antidepressants (citalopram), and antibiotics (erythromycin, moxifloxacin) [US].
- **Epinephrine:** Hydroxyzine counteracts the pressor action of epinephrine; do not use epinephrine in hydroxyzine overdosage [US].
- **Non-Interactions:** Does not interfere with the action of digitalis or belladonna alkaloids [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated in early pregnancy. Fetal abnormalities have been demonstrated in animal studies (rat/mouse) at doses above the human therapeutic range [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is not known if hydroxyzine is excreted in human milk. Hydroxyzine should not be administered to nursing mothers [US].
- **Fertility:** Data not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for anxiety, pruritus, and sedation. Dosage is weight-based (0.6 mg/kg) for sedation or age-based (above/below 6 years) for other indications [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not determine if response differs from younger subjects. Dosing should be cautious, generally starting at the low end of the range due to higher frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Extent of renal excretion is undetermined; caution and dose selection care are advised in the elderly due to likely decreased renal function [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources; however, caution is advised in elderly populations where hepatic function may be reduced [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Not a cortical depressant. Action may be due to suppression of activity in subcortical regions of the CNS [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Demonstrates primary skeletal muscle relaxation, bronchodilator activity, antihistaminic, analgesic, and antiemetic effects. Does not increase gastric secretion [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed from the GI tract [US].
    - **Onset:** Clinical effects usually noted within 15 to 30 minutes of oral administration [US].
    - **Elimination:** Extent of renal excretion is not determined [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Systematic clinical studies have not assessed effectiveness for anxiety use beyond 4 months [US].
- Specific pivotal trial data (design/outcomes) are not detailed in the provided source document.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F) [US].
- **Container:** Dispense in a tight, child-resistant container [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
