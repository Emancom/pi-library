# Editorial Product Information: hydrocortisone + aciclovir

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **hydrocortisone + aciclovir**
- Chemical class:
  - Hydrocortisone: topical corticosteroid (glucocorticoid)
  - Aciclovir: antiviral nucleoside analogue active against herpes simplex viruses
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration: Topical (external use to lips/face)

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval: 28 October 2016 [Australian].
- Date of last PI revision/update: July 2024 [Australian].
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of recurrent herpes labialis (cold sores) when applied at the first signs or symptoms of a recurrence, to:
  - reduce progression of cold sore episodes to ulcerative lesions, and
  - shorten healing time of ulcerative lesions
  - Population: immunocompetent adults and adolescents aged 12 years and older [Australian].
- Use in indications other than recurrent herpes labialis (e.g., genital herpes, mucosal lesions) is not recommended/not indicated in the available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adults and adolescents ≥ 12 years)
- Apply topically five times daily for 5 days at approximately four‑hour intervals, omitting the night‑time application.
- Initiate treatment as early as possible, immediately after the first signs or symptoms of a recurrence.
- Apply a quantity sufficient to cover the affected area including the outer margin of lesions.
- Total duration: 5 days. If lesions are still present after 10 days, advise patient to consult a physician.

### Administration instructions
- For external use only; apply to cold sores on the lips and face.
- Wash hands before and after applying the cream.
- Avoid unnecessary rubbing of lesions and avoid touching lesions with towels to prevent aggravation or spread.
- Avoid contact with the eyes; do not apply to mucous membranes (e.g., inside the mouth or nose).
- Do not use with occlusive dressings (e.g., plasters or specialised cold sore patches/plasters).

### Titration / missed dose
- Titration schedules: Not applicable / Not specified in available sources.
- Missed dose: Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to aciclovir, valaciclovir, hydrocortisone, or any of the excipients.
- Skin lesions caused by viruses other than herpes simplex.
- Skin lesions due to fungal, bacterial or parasitic infections (use for these is contraindicated).

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- **For external use only:** Intended for lesions on the lips and face; not recommended for mucous membranes or genital herpes.
- Avoid contact with the eye. If accidental eye contact occurs, follow local first‑aid measures (not specified in available sources).
- Visual disturbance (e.g., blurred vision) has been reported with systemic and topical corticosteroid use. If patients develop blurred vision or other visual disturbances, consider referral to an ophthalmologist to evaluate for cataract, glaucoma or rare corticosteroid‑associated conditions such as central serous chorioretinopathy (CSCR).
- Do not use with occlusive dressings or cold sore patches/plasters.
- Not recommended for use by patients who are known to be immunocompromised; such patients should seek medical advice for management of their infection.
- Avoid long‑term continuous use; do not use for longer than 5 days.
- Contains excipients that may cause local irritation:
  - Cetostearyl alcohol — may cause local skin irritation/contact dermatitis.
  - Propylene glycol — may cause skin irritation.
- Use in hepatic impairment: Not specified in available sources.
- Use in renal impairment: Not specified in available sources.
- Use in the elderly: Not specified in available sources.
- Advise patients with particularly severe recurrent herpes labialis to seek medical advice.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor for signs of local skin irritation or hypersensitivity reactions.
- Monitor for visual symptoms; consider ophthalmology referral if visual disturbance develops.
- Monitor treatment response; if lesions persist beyond 10 days, advise medical review.

## 7. Adverse Effects

Frequency terms conform to conventional categories (very common >10%, common 1–10%, uncommon 0.1–1%, rare <0.1%, very rare <0.01%). Where the PI used category labels without numeric incidence, those labels are presented.

- Serious adverse effects highlighted: immediate hypersensitivity reactions including angioedema; visual disturbance (see Warnings).

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Pregnancy category: Category B3 [Australian].
- Guidance:
  - Systemic exposure to aciclovir and hydrocortisone following topical application is expected to be very low.
  - The product should be used in pregnancy only if the potential benefits justify the possible unknown risks.
  - Available human data: A post‑marketing aciclovir pregnancy registry has not shown an increase in birth defects among exposures to aciclovir formulations compared with the general population; no unique or consistent pattern of defects identified.
  - Animal data: Studies in animals have shown evidence of an increased occurrence of fetal damage for aciclovir in some non‑standard tests; topical corticosteroids have produced fetal abnormalities in animal studies. The clinical relevance of some animal findings to humans is uncertain.

### Breastfeeding
- Aciclovir and hydrocortisone pass into breast milk after systemic administration.
- The amount of drug a nursing infant would receive following maternal topical use is expected to be insignificant.

### Fertility
- Effects on human fertility following topical administration: No data available in humans to evaluate effect on fertility.

## 10. Use in Special Populations

### Elderly

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Aciclovir: antiviral guanine nucleoside analogue active against herpes simplex virus (HSV) types 1 and 2. It is phosphorylated within HSV‑infected cells (first phosphorylation by viral thymidine kinase) to the active triphosphate which inhibits viral DNA polymerase and prevents viral DNA synthesis.
- Hydrocortisone: a mild topical corticosteroid with anti‑inflammatory and immunomodulatory effects that reduces local inflammatory responses.

- The combination is intended to reduce viral replication and mitigate local inflammation, thereby reducing progression to ulcerative lesions and shortening lesion healing time. The exact mechanism of the added clinical benefit relative to antiviral alone is not fully characterised.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: Systemic absorption of both active ingredients after topical application is limited; systemic exposure to aciclovir and hydrocortisone from topical use is expected to be low.
- Glucocorticoids can penetrate the stratum corneum and reach deeper cell layers; systemic effects may occur with increased absorption (e.g., application to large inflamed areas, damaged skin, or when used under occlusion).
- No dedicated clinical pharmacokinetic studies with the combination product were reported in the available sources.
- Distribution, metabolism, elimination: Not specified in available sources for the topical combination.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Not specified beyond mechanism of action and clinical effects summarized in clinical trials.

### Preclinical safety data
- Genotoxicity: No data available.
- Carcinogenicity: No data available.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Key pivotal study:
  - Design: Double‑blind, randomised clinical trial (n=1,443) in subjects with recurrent herpes labialis.
  - Interventions: hydrocortisone + aciclovir cream vs aciclovir 5% in vehicle cream vs vehicle cream alone.
  - Treatment initiation: subjects instructed to start within 1 hour of noticing signs/symptoms.
  - Primary endpoint: prevention of progression to ulcerative lesions.
  - Results:
    - Progression to ulcerative lesions: 58% in the hydrocortisone + aciclovir group versus 65% in the aciclovir‑in‑vehicle group (p = 0.014), and 74% in vehicle alone group (p < 0.0001 versus combination).
    - The combination prevented an additional ~7% of recurrences progressing to ulcerative lesions compared with aciclovir in vehicle cream.
    - Timing of treatment mattered: lower proportion of ulcerative lesions when treatment was started earlier.
    - In subjects who developed ulcerative lesions, mean episode duration was not significantly different between hydrocortisone + aciclovir and aciclovir in vehicle; both active treatments reduced episode duration compared with vehicle alone (mean duration 5.7 days for combination vs 6.5 days for vehicle; p = 0.008).
  - Tolerability: treatments were well tolerated; proportion reporting treatment‑related adverse reactions was low and comparable among groups.

- Adolescent study:
  - Open‑label safety study in 254 adolescents aged 12–17 years using the same dosing regimen; safety profile similar to adults. Efficacy not assessed.

- Additional trial details and other trials: Not specified in available sources.
