# Editorial Product Information: Hydralazine Hydrochloride and Isosorbide Dinitrate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically focused overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, legally binding PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for jurisdictional compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Hydralazine hydrochloride (arteriolar vasodilator) and Isosorbide dinitrate (nitrate vasodilator).
- **Brand Name:** BiDil [US].
- **ATC Code:** C01DA58 (Isosorbide dinitrate, combinations).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Scored, film-coated tablets containing 37.5 mg hydralazine hydrochloride and 20 mg isosorbide dinitrate [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Azurity Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Heart Failure:** Indicated as an adjunct to standard therapy for the treatment of heart failure in self-identified black patients. The goal of therapy is to improve survival, extend the time to first heart failure-related hospitalization, and enhance patient-reported functional status [US-approved].
- **Limitations of Use:** Clinical experience is limited in patients with NYHA class IV heart failure [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** Initiation at one tablet three times daily.
- **Titration:** Dosage should be titrated upward based on patient tolerance. The maximum recommended dose is two tablets three times daily [US]. 
- **Dose Adjustments for Tolerability:** If intolerable adverse effects occur, the dose may be reduced to as little as one-half tablet three times daily. Clinicians should attempt to up-titrate once side effects resolve [US].
- **Titration Schedule:** While titration can be completed rapidly within 3 to 5 days, some patients may require a slower approach to reach the maximum tolerated dose [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to organic nitrates [US].
- Concomitant use of phosphodiesterase type 5 (PDE5) inhibitors (e.g., sildenafil, tadalafil, vardenafil, avanafil), which can result in severe hypotension, syncope, or myocardial ischemia [US].
- Concomitant use of soluble guanylate cyclase (sGC) stimulators (e.g., riociguat), due to the risk of severe hypotension [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Symptomatic Hypotension:** May occur even with low doses, particularly when standing. Risk is increased in patients who are volume- or salt-depleted. These states should be corrected prior to initiation [US].
- **Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE) Syndrome:** Hydralazine has been associated with drug-induced SLE. Symptoms typically resolve upon discontinuation of the medication [US].
- **Worsening Ischemic Heart Disease:** Hydralazine-induced tachycardia and hypotension may exacerbate myocardial ischemia and angina, especially in patients with hypertrophic cardiomyopathy [US].
- **Peripheral Neuritis:** Hydralazine has been linked to peripheral neuritis (paresthesia, numbness, tingling), possibly due to an antipyridoxine effect. Treatment with pyridoxine (Vitamin B6) may be necessary [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are common and often lead to discontinuation (21% in BiDil group vs. 12% in placebo) [US].

| Adverse Reaction | Frequency (BiDil) | Frequency (Placebo) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Headache** | 50% | 21% |
| **Dizziness** | 32% | 14% |
| **Asthenia** | 14% | 11% |
| **Nausea** | 10% | 6% |
| **Hypotension** | 8% | 4% |
| **Paresthesia** | 4% | 2% |
| **Tachycardia (Ventricular)**| 4% | 2% |

- **Serious/Postmarketing Effects:** Palpitations, syncope, dyspnea, erectile dysfunction, hepatitis, and blood dysprasias (agranulocytosis, purpura) have been reported [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **PDE5 Inhibitors & sGC Stimulators:** Contraindicated; risk of profound hypotension [US].
- **Beta-Blockers:** Hydralazine may increase the plasma concentration (Cmax and AUC) of beta-blockers such as propranolol and metoprolol [US].
- **Diuretics (Furosemide):** Co-administration with hydralazine may increase furosemide clearance [US-documented in Japanese patients].
- **ACE Inhibitors:** Hydralazine may increase the exposure of lisinopril, though enalapril concentrations appear unaffected [US].
- **General Vasodilators:** Effects may be additive with other vasodilating agents and alcohol [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not assigned under current PLLR rules; however, historically Category C]. There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Animal data indicate potential teratogenicity for hydralazine (mice/rabbits) and embryo-toxicity for isosorbide dinitrate (rabbits) at high multiples of the human dose. Heart failure itself increases the risk of preterm birth and maternal mortality [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if the drug is excreted in human milk. Benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the mother's clinical need and potential risks to the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies for isosorbide dinitrate showed no evidence of altered fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Isosorbide dinitrate and hydralazine may be eliminated more slowly. Initiate therapy at the low end of the dosing range to account for decreased hepatic/renal function and concomitant disease [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No specific studies with the combination product. No dose adjustment is generally required for the individual components. Dialysis does not effectively remove isosorbide dinitrate or its metabolites [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No specific studies with the combination. Isosorbide dinitrate concentrations increase in patients with cirrhosis [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** The exact mechanism in heart failure is not fully established. Isosorbide dinitrate releases nitric oxide, stimulating guanylyl cyclase to relax vascular smooth muscle (affecting both arteries and veins). Hydralazine is a selective arterial dilator and may mitigate the development of nitrate tolerance [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak levels reached in 1 hour. Bioavailability of hydralazine varies (10-26%) and is higher in "slow acetylators." Isosorbide dinitrate bioavailability is ~25% [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Hydralazine is metabolized via acetylation and ring oxidation. Isosorbide dinitrate undergoes extensive hepatic first-pass metabolism to active mononitrates [US].
    - **Elimination:** Hydralazine is primarily metabolized; isosorbide dinitrate is eliminated renally as conjugated metabolites [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **A-HeFT Trial:** A pivotal study of 1,050 self-identified black patients with NYHA class III/IV heart failure. The trial was terminated early due to a 43% reduction in all-cause mortality (p=0.012) and a 39% reduction in first hospitalization for heart failure (p<0.001) in the BiDil group compared to placebo [US].
- **V-HeFT I and II:** Earlier trials suggested a trend favoring the combination in black patients but found it inferior to enalapril in the overall (primarily white) population [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions permitted between 15-30°C (59-86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Keep bottles tightly closed. Protect from light. Dispense in a light-resistant, tight container [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
