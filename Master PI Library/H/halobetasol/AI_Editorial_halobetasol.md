# Editorial Product Information: Halobetasol Propionate

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of halobetasol propionate for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing in any particular region. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Halobetasol propionate (a synthetic corticosteroid).
- **Chemical Class:** Super-high potency (Group I) topical corticosteroid.
- **Brand Names:** Halobetasol Propionate Ointment [US].
- **ATC Code:** D07AC15.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Ointment, 0.05% (0.5 mg/g) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Encube Ethicals, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Corticosteroid-Responsive Dermatoses:** Indicated for the relief of inflammatory and pruritic manifestations of dermatoses that respond to corticosteroid therapy [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Adolescents ≥12 years):** Apply a thin layer to the affected skin once or twice daily. Rub in gently and completely [US].
- **Duration of Treatment:** Therapy should be limited to **two consecutive weeks**. Treatment should be discontinued once control is achieved. If no improvement is observed within 2 weeks, reassessment of the diagnosis is required [US].
- **Maximum Dosage:** The total weekly dose should not exceed **50 g per week** due to the risk of hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis suppression [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - For external dermatological use only; not for ophthalmic, oral, or intravaginal use.
    - Do **not** use with occlusive dressings unless directed by a physician [US].
    - Do **not** apply to the face, groin, or axillae [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to halobetasol propionate or any component of the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **HPA Axis Suppression:** Systemic absorption can produce reversible suppression of the HPA axis, potentially leading to glucocorticosteroid insufficiency. Manifestations of Cushing’s syndrome, hyperglycemia, and glucosuria may also occur. Risk factors include use over large surface areas, prolonged use, and use under occlusion [US].
- **Local Adverse Reactions:** If irritation develops, discontinue use and institute appropriate therapy. Allergic contact dermatitis is typically diagnosed by failure of the lesion to heal rather than acute exacerbation [US].
- **Concomitant Skin Infections:** If an infection is present or develops, an appropriate antifungal or antibacterial agent must be used. If the infection does not resolve promptly, discontinue halobetasol until the infection is controlled [US].
- **Site Restrictions:** Avoid use on the face, groin, or axillae due to the higher risk of skin atrophy in these areas [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Patients applying the drug to large areas or under occlusion should be periodically evaluated for HPA axis suppression via ACTH stimulation, A.M. plasma cortisol, and urinary free-cortisol tests [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally local and occur near the site of application.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Stinging or burning (1.6%) [US]. |
| **Infrequent/Rare (<1%)** | Pustulation, erythema, skin atrophy, leukoderma, acne, itching, secondary infection, telangiectasia, urticaria, dry skin, miliaria, paresthesia, and rash [US]. |

**Class Effects of Topical Corticosteroids:** 
Infrequently reported reactions (listed in decreasing order of occurrence) include: folliculitis, hypertrichosis, acneiform eruptions, hypopigmentation, perioral dermatitis, allergic contact dermatitis, secondary infection, striae, and miliaria [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category C:** Halobetasol propionate has been shown to be teratogenic (e.g., cleft palate, omphalocele) in rats and rabbits at systemic doses 3 to 33 times the human topical dose. There are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown whether topical administration results in sufficient systemic absorption to produce detectable quantities in human milk. Systemically administered corticosteroids are secreted into breast milk and may suppress growth. Exercise caution when administering to a nursing woman [US].
- **Fertility:** Studies in rats following oral administration (up to 50 mcg/kg/day) indicated no impairment of fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in children **under 12 years of age** have not been established. Use in this population is not recommended. Children are at a higher risk for HPA axis suppression, Cushing’s syndrome, and intracranial hypertension due to a higher skin surface area to body mass ratio [US].
- **Elderly Use:** In clinical trials (n=850), 21% of subjects were ≥61 years and 6% were ≥71 years. No overall differences in safety or efficacy were observed between elderly and younger patients, though greater sensitivity in some individuals cannot be ruled out [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Synthetic corticosteroid with anti-inflammatory, antipruritic, and vasoconstrictive properties. It is thought to induce phospholipase A$_2$ inhibitory proteins (lipocortins), which control the biosynthesis of prostaglandins and leukotrienes by inhibiting the release of arachidonic acid [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Percutaneous absorption is determined by the vehicle, epidermal integrity, and use of occlusion. 
    - **Distribution:** Once absorbed through the skin, topical corticosteroids follow pharmacokinetic pathways similar to systemically administered steroids.
    - **Metabolism/Elimination:** Metabolism occurs primarily in the liver, and excretion is via the kidneys and bile. In studies, less than 6% of the applied dose entered the circulation within 96 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **General Clinical Data:** Clinical studies involved 850 patients. While specific pivotal trial names were not provided in the source, the data established halobetasol propionate as a super-high potency agent for corticosteroid-responsive dermatoses [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Keep the tube tightly capped. Keep out of reach of children [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription Only (Rx-only). NDC 21922-108-04 (15 g), NDC 21922-108-14 (50 g) [US].
