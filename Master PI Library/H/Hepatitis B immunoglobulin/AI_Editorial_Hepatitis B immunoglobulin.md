# Editorial Product Information: Human Hepatitis B Immunoglobulin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data into a concise, structured format.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for full prescribing details and legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Human Hepatitis B Immunoglobulin.
- **Chemical Class:** Plasma-derived immunoglobulin (predominantly IgG).
- **Brand Names:** **Hepatitis B Immunoglobulin-VF** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J06BB04.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile, preservative-free solution for injection containing 160 mg/mL human plasma protein (≥98% IgG). The hepatitis B antibody titre is ≥100 IU/mL. Available in single vials of 100 IU and 400 IU [Australian].
- **Excipients:** Contains 22.5 mg/mL glycine and trace amounts of IgA (<0.5 mg/mL) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intramuscular (IM) injection only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** CSL Behring (Australia) Pty Ltd; distributed by Australian Red Cross Lifeblood [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Post-exposure prophylaxis:** Indicated for individuals following percutaneous or permucosal exposure to HBsAg-positive or suspected HBsAg-positive material (e.g., needle stick, oral ingestion, or sexual exposure). This applies to persons who:
    - Have not received prior vaccination.
    - Have an incomplete vaccination regimen.
    - Have inadequate antibody levels (<10 IU/L).
- **Neonatal prophylaxis:** Prophylaxis in infants born to HBsAg-positive mothers.
- **Note:** Prophylaxis is considered unnecessary if the individual already possesses adequate circulating hepatitis B antibody levels (≥10 IU/L) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Post-exposure Prophylaxis:** A single dose of 400 IU should be administered immediately. Treatment is most effective when given within 72 hours of exposure [Australian].

### Dosing by Indication/History
| Source Material | Vaccination History | Recommendation [Australian] |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Confirmed HBsAg+** | No/Incomplete vaccination | 400 IU IM immediately + initiate HBV vaccine series. |
| **Confirmed HBsAg+** | Completed vaccination | Test for HBs antibody. If <10 IU/L, give 400 IU IM + HBV vaccine booster. |
| **High Risk (Unconfirmed)** | No/Incomplete vaccination | Initiate HBV vaccine series. Test source; if HBsAg+, give 400 IU IM. |
| **High Risk (Unconfirmed)** | Completed vaccination | Test for HBs antibody. If <10 IU/L, test source; if HBsAg+, give 400 IU IM + booster. |

### Neonatal Dosing
- **Infants born to HBsAg+ mothers:** Administer 100 IU IM at birth. The first dose of the hepatitis B vaccine series should be administered at the same time in a different limb [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Route:** Deep intramuscular injection. **Do not administer intravenously.**
- **Preparation:** Bring to room temperature before use. Do not use if turbid or containing sediment. Use immediately once the vial is opened.
- **Technique:** Draw back on the plunger before injection to ensure the needle is not in a blood vessel.
- **Volume Management:** If large volumes are required, administer in divided doses at different sites. This is advised for doses >2 mL (children up to 20 kg) or >5 mL (persons >20 kg) [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known true anaphylactic reaction to human immunoglobulin or any components of the product.
- **IgA deficiency:** Contraindicated unless the patient has been tested and shown not to have circulating anti-IgA antibodies, due to the risk of severe hypersensitivity to trace IgA [Australian].
- **Bleeding Disorders:** Severe thrombocytopenia or other coagulation disorders where intramuscular injection is contraindicated.
- **HBsAg-positive status:** The product is not indicated for individuals already positive for the hepatitis B surface antigen [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Intravenous Administration:** **MUST NOT** be administered intravenously due to the risk of precipitous blood pressure drop and anaphylaxis.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Rare anaphylactic reactions can occur, even in patients who previously tolerated human immunoglobulin. Patients should be observed for at least 20 minutes (or 1 hour if IV administration was accidental) following injection [Australian].
- **Pathogen Safety:** As a product derived from human plasma, the risk of transmitting infectious agents (viruses, CJD agents) cannot be totally excluded. The manufacturing process includes pasteurisation (viral inactivation) and nanofiltration (virus removal), which are effective against HIV, HBV, HCV, HAV, and parvovirus B19 [Australian].
- **Laboratory Test Interference:** Passively transferred antibodies may lead to misleading positive results in serological testing. Antibodies to erythrocyte antigens (e.g., anti-A, anti-B, anti-D) may interfere with the Coombs’ test [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Batch numbers must be recorded for every administration to maintain a link between the patient and the product batch.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Local Reactions
- Common: Tenderness, erythema, stiffness, or pain at the injection site (may persist for several hours).
- Observed in trials: Bruising, induration, redness, and irritation [Australian].

### Systemic Reactions
- **Common:** Malaise, drowsiness, mild pyrexia.
- **Uncommon/Rare:** Chills, sweating, sensation of fever, urticaria, headache, dizziness, nausea.
- **Serious (Rare):** Generalised hypersensitivity reactions, anaphylaxis, and convulsions [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Live Attenuated Vaccines:** Passively acquired antibodies may interfere with the immune response to live vaccines (e.g., measles, mumps, rubella, polio).
    - Administration of live vaccines should be deferred for at least 3 months following immunoglobulin use.
    - In the case of measles, interference may last up to 1 year; antibody status should be checked.
    - If immunoglobulin is given within 2 weeks of a live vaccine, re-vaccination should be considered 3 months later [Australian].
- **Hepatitis B Vaccine:** May be administered concurrently, but must be given at a separate anatomical site/limb.
- **Incompatibilities:** Must not be mixed with other medicinal products or solvents [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category: Not specified in available source]. Safety in human pregnancy has not been established in controlled trials. Use with caution; should only be given to pregnant women if clearly needed [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been established in clinical trials. While immunoglobulins are excreted in breast milk, it is unknown if this applies to passively administered Hepatitis B Immunoglobulin-VF. Use with caution in nursing mothers [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No reproductive toxicity studies have been conducted [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in formal studies, though infants born to HBsAg-positive mothers are a target population for prophylaxis (100 IU dose). No over-representation of adverse events has been reported in this population [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in formal studies. No specific concerns identified in spontaneous reporting [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Provides passive immunity via specific neutralising antibodies (IgG) against the hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Reaches maximal serum concentration of IgG in approximately 8.0 ± 5.5 days after IM injection.
    - **Elimination:** The estimated half-life of IgG is approximately 27.2 ± 6.6 days.
    - **Duration:** IgG levels remain at protective levels for at least 6 weeks [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- A comparative trial of 58 healthy subjects investigated the effect of pasteurisation on the behaviour of intramuscular immunoglobulins. No significant clinical differences were observed between pasteurised (viral inactivated) and unpasteurised versions. Protective levels of IgG were maintained for the duration of the 6-week observation period [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze).
- Protect from light.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
