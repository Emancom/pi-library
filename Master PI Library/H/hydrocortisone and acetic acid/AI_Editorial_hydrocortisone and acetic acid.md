# Editorial Product Information: Hydrocortisone and Acetic Acid

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Hydrocortisone (synthetic corticosteroid) and Acetic Acid (glacial).
- **Brand Names:** Hydrocortisone and Acetic Acid Otic Solution, USP (Generic; distributed by Sun Pharmaceutical Industries/Taro Pharmaceuticals [US]).
- **ATC Code:** S02CA03 (Corticosteroids and anti-infectives in combination).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Non-aqueous otic solution containing Hydrocortisone 1% (10.4 mg/mL) and Acetic Acid 2% (20.8 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Auricular (Otic) use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sun Pharmaceutical Industries, Inc. (Labeler); Taro Pharmaceuticals Inc. (Manufacturer) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Superficial Otic Infections:** Indicated for the treatment of superficial infections of the external auditory canal when caused by organisms susceptible to the antimicrobial action of acetic acid and complicated by an inflammatory response [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
Before administration, the external ear canal should be carefully cleared of all cerumen and debris to allow the solution to contact the infected surfaces directly.

### Adult Dosage [US]
- **Initial Phase (Wick Method):** To ensure continuous contact, a cotton wick saturated with the solution should be inserted into the ear canal. The wick may be saturated before or after insertion.
- **Wick Maintenance:** The patient should keep the wick in place for at least 24 hours and keep it moist by applying 3 to 5 drops of the solution every 4 to 6 hours.
- **Maintenance Phase:** After the wick is removed, the patient should instill 5 drops into the affected ear 3 or 4 times daily. The duration of treatment should be determined by clinical response.

### Paediatric Dosage [US]
- **Dosage:** 3 to 4 drops may be sufficient for paediatric patients due to the smaller capacity of the ear canal.
- **Wick Method:** May be used as described for adults if appropriate for the child's age and canal size.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to hydrocortisone, acetic acid, or any excipients in the formulation (e.g., benzethonium chloride, propylene glycol) [US].
- **Viral Infections:** Presence of herpes simplex, vaccinia, or varicella [US].
- **Tympanic Membrane Perforation:** Perforation of the eardrum is considered a contraindication to the use of any medication in the external ear canal [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Sensitisation/Irritation:** Treatment should be discontinued immediately if the patient develops signs of sensitisation or local irritation [US].
- **Local Reaction:** Transient stinging or burning may occur, particularly during the first instillation into an acutely inflamed ear [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally limited to the site of application.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Occasional** | Stinging or burning upon instillation |
| **Very Rare** | Local irritation or sensitisation |

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
- **Acetic Acid:** Functions as an antibacterial and antifungal agent.
- **Hydrocortisone:** A synthetic corticosteroid providing anti-inflammatory, antiallergic, and antipruritic effects.

### Pharmacodynamics and Pharmacokinetics
- **Vehicle:** Propylene glycol is hydrophilic and provides low surface tension to facilitate spread within the canal.
- **Surfactant:** Benzethonium chloride is a surface-active agent that promotes contact between the solution and the ear tissues [US].
- **pH:** The solution is buffered to an acidic range of 2.0 to 4.0 [US].
- **Absorption:** Systemic absorption data is not provided in available sources.
