# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official PI for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the United States source documentation was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule involved in the transport of long-chain fatty acids.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injection (solution), 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2-to-3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US]. 
    - **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by equivalent doses every 3 or 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours. 
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are generally 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilized as clinically required [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2-to-3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Monitoring and Adjustment
- **Plasma Concentrations:** Monitoring should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels. The target plasma free carnitine concentration is between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Dose Titration (ESRD):** Initiation may be prompted by pre-dialysis plasma concentrations <40 µmol/L. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough levels [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine (oral or IV) has been associated with reports of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Precaution):** While this document concerns the injection, clinicians should note that chronic high-dose *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5% in ESRD clinical trials)
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (9%), headache (22%), injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), fever (7%), asthenia (9%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%).

### Other Reported Effects
- **General:** Transient nausea and vomiting, gastritis, and a "fishy" body odor have been observed.
- **Serious Effects:** Seizures, anaphylaxis, and laryngeal edema [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
    - **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin anticoagulation [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US]: Not specified in available sources (formerly Category B). Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies in lactating humans are unavailable. Studies in dairy cows showed increased carnitine concentrations in milk after exogenous administration. The benefits of maternal supplementation must be weighed against potential risks to the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is weight-based (mg/kg) as described in the Dosage and Administration section [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically in ESRD patients undergoing dialysis. Dose adjustments are based on pre-dialysis trough concentrations (see Section 3) [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein bound. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Dosing Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis showed that trough levocarnitine concentrations increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses over 12 and 24 weeks demonstrated a dose-dependent increase in trough carnitine levels. While carnitine levels were successfully increased, clinical outcomes regarding the signs and symptoms of deficiency in the ESRD population were not determined in these trials [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing.
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** [US]: Rx-only (Prescription only).
