# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises available regulatory information to provide clinical guidance on the drug’s use, safety profile, and pharmacology.
*   **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Prescribing Information approved by the regulatory authority in the specific jurisdiction where a clinician is practicing.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism.
*   **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (trimethylammonium-substituted butyrate).
*   **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
*   **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    *   Injection, solution: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) on Dialysis:** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 Metabolic Disorders
*   **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
*   **Loading Dose:** In cases of severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
*   **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; frequency should never be less than every 6 hours [US].
*   **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are recommended in the range of 50 mg/kg, though higher doses (up to 300 mg/kg) have been used if required by therapy [US].

### 3.2 ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
*   **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
*   **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
*   **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy for concentrations above the target range [US].

### 3.3 Monitoring
*   **Baseline:** Obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to initiating parenteral therapy [US].
*   **Ongoing:** Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended. Monitoring should include vital signs, blood chemistries, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### 5.1 Hypersensitivity Reactions
*   **Serious Reactions:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of IV administration [US].
*   **Management:** Discontinue treatment immediately and initiate appropriate medical care. Carefully weigh risks/benefits before considering re-administration [US].

### 5.2 Seizures
*   Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].

### 5.3 Renal Impairment (Oral Formulation Caution)
*   The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD or severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites: trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### 6.1 General Adverse Effects
*   **Common:** Transient nausea and vomiting.
*   **Less Frequent:** Body odor, gastritis.

### 6.2 Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events occurring at a frequency of ≥5% in controlled trials for ESRD patients (regardless of causality) include:
*   **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (5–21%), headache (3–37%), injection site reaction (27–38%), pain (21–35%), fever (5–12%), flu syndrome (15–29%), asthenia (8–12%).
*   **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), chest pain (6–15%), tachycardia (5–9%).
*   **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%).
*   **Metabolic/Nutritional:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hypervolemia (3–12%).
*   **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%).
*   **Nervous System:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%).

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
    *   **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   **Category:** Not formally letter-graded in source, but reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm [US].
    *   **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. In dairy cows, carnitine concentrations in milk increase following exogenous administration. Weigh the benefits of maternal treatment against the potential risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
*   **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** See Section 3 (Dosage and Administration) for weight-based dosing [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources; generally follow standard weight-based dosing and monitor renal function.
*   **Renal Impairment:** Dose adjustments in ESRD are based on trough concentrations (see Section 3.2). Avoid high-dose oral formulations in this population due to metabolite accumulation [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### 10.1 Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This provides the necessary substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters which are rapidly eliminated.

### 10.2 Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
*   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
*   **Elimination:** Following a single IV dose, approximately 76% is excreted in the urine within 24 hours [US]. 
*   **Half-life:** Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
*   **Clearance:** Total body clearance is approximately 4.00 L/h [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ESRD Pharmacokinetic Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, IV levocarnitine (20 mg/kg post-dialysis) increased mean trough concentrations from 21.3 µmol/L (baseline) to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
*   **Dose-Ranging Data:** A study of ~30 patients per group (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough levels at 12 and 24 weeks.
*   **Clinical Outcomes Note:** While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations in ESRD is demonstrated, the effect on specific signs/symptoms or clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
*   **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat. 
*   **Stability:** When mixed in Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL), the solution is stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags [US].
*   **Preservative Status:** Preservative-free; discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].
