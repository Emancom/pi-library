# Editorial Product Information: Hydrocodone Bitartrate and Ibuprofen

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) document provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical and regulatory data into a single, structured reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI or prescribing information required for clinical practice in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Hydrocodone Bitartrate (semisynthetic opioid agonist) and Ibuprofen (non-selective COX inhibitor; propionic acid derivative).
- **Brand Names:** Hydrocodone Bitartrate and Ibuprofen Tablets [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 7.5 mg hydrocodone bitartrate and 200 mg ibuprofen [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aurolife Pharma, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Pain Management:** Indicated for the short-term management of acute pain severe enough to require an opioid analgesic and for which alternative treatments are inadequate [US].
- **Limitations of Use:** 
    - Not indicated for the treatment of chronic conditions such as osteoarthritis or rheumatoid arthritis [US].
    - Use should be reserved for patients where non-opioid options are ineffective or not tolerated [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** 
    - The standard dose is one (1) tablet every 4 to 6 hours as needed for pain [US].
    - **Maximum Dose:** Dosage should not exceed five (5) tablets in a 24-hour period [US].
- **Duration of Therapy:** Intended for short-term use, generally less than 10 days [US].
- **Titration:** Dosage should be individualised based on pain severity, patient response, and prior analgesic experience. Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration [US].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, patients should take the next dose at the usual time; doses should not be doubled [US].
- **Discontinuation:** Do not abruptly discontinue in physically dependent patients. A gradual downward taper is required to avoid withdrawal symptoms [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Respiratory:** Significant respiratory depression; acute or severe bronchial asthma in unmonitored settings or without resuscitative equipment [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Known or suspected GI obstruction, including paralytic ileus [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to hydrocodone, ibuprofen, or any component. History of asthma, urticaria, or allergic-type reactions after taking aspirin or other NSAIDs [US].
- **Surgical:** Use in the setting of coronary artery bypass graft (CABG) surgery [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings [US]:**
    - **Addiction, Abuse, and Misuse:** Potential for opioid substance use disorder, which can lead to overdose and death.
    - **Life-Threatening Respiratory Depression:** Serious risk, especially during initiation or dose escalation.
    - **Accidental Ingestion:** Even one dose can be fatal, particularly in children.
    - **Neonatal Opioid Withdrawal Syndrome (NOWS):** Risk with prolonged use during pregnancy.
    - **CYP3A4 Interaction:** Concomitant use with CYP3A4 inhibitors (or stopping inducers) may increase hydrocodone levels to toxic levels.
    - **Risks with CNS Depressants:** Concomitant use with benzodiazepines or alcohol may result in profound sedation, coma, and death.
    - **Cardiovascular (CV) Events:** Increased risk of serious thrombotic events (MI, stroke).
    - **Gastrointestinal (GI) Events:** Increased risk of bleeding, ulceration, and perforation.
- **Opioid-Induced Hyperalgesia (OIH):** Opioids may paradoxically cause increased pain sensitivity [US].
- **Adrenal Insufficiency:** Reported with opioid use, usually after >1 month [US].
- **Hepatotoxicity:** NSAID use may cause elevations in liver enzymes; rare cases of severe injury/failure [US].
- **Renal Toxicity:** Long-term NSAID use can lead to renal papillary necrosis and other renal injuries [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Periodically monitor CBC and chemistry profiles (including renal/liver function) in patients on prolonged therapy [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse event rates generally increase with higher daily doses.

| Frequency | System Organ Class | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | CNS | Headache (27%), Somnolence (22%), Dizziness (14%) |
| | Digestive | Constipation (22%), Nausea (21%), Dyspepsia (12%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | General | Abdominal pain, Asthenia, Infection |
| | CNS | Anxiety, Insomnia, Nervousness |
| | Digestive | Diarrhea, Dry mouth, Flatulence, Vomiting |
| | Skin | Pruritus, Sweating |
| | Metabolic | Edema |
| **Uncommon/Rare (<1%)** | Cardiovascular | Arrhythmia, Hypotension, Tachycardia |
| | CNS | Agitation, Depression, Euphoria, Slurred speech, Tremor |
| | Respiratory | Asthma, Bronchitis, Dyspnea |
| | Special Senses | Tinnitus, Altered vision |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Myocardial infarction, stroke, GI perforation, anaphylaxis, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), and Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors/Inducers:** Inhibitors (e.g., erythromycin, ketoconazole) increase hydrocodone levels; inducers (e.g., rifampin) decrease levels [US].
- **Benzodiazepines/CNS Depressants:** Additive respiratory depression and sedation [US].
- **Serotonergic Drugs:** Risk of serotonin syndrome when used with SSRIs, SNRIs, TCAs, or MAOIs [US].
- **MAOIs:** Interaction may cause serotonin syndrome or opioid toxicity; do not use within 14 days of MAOI therapy [US].
- **Aspirin:** Ibuprofen may interfere with the antiplatelet effect of low-dose aspirin used for cardioprotection [US].
- **Antihypertensives (ACEIs, ARBs, Beta-blockers):** NSAIDs may diminish antihypertensive efficacy [US].
- **Diuretics:** Reduced natriuretic effect; increased risk of renal toxicity [US].
- **Lithium/Methotrexate:** NSAIDs may increase plasma levels of these drugs, increasing toxicity risk [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Fetal Toxicity:** NSAID use at ~20 weeks gestation or later may cause fetal renal dysfunction leading to oligohydramnios [US]. 
    - **Ductus Arteriosus:** Avoid NSAID use starting at 30 weeks gestation due to risk of premature closure of the fetal ductus arteriosus [US].
    - **NOWS:** Chronic opioid use during pregnancy can result in life-threatening neonatal withdrawal [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** 
    - Hydrocodone and its metabolite (hydromorphone) are present in human milk. Monitor infants for excess sedation and respiratory depression [US].
    - Ibuprofen is excreted in breast milk in very low amounts (0.06% to 0.6% of maternal dose) [US].
- **Fertility:** Chronic opioid use may cause reduced fertility. NSAIDs may cause a reversible delay in ovulation [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 16 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of gastrointestinal, cardiovascular, and renal adverse reactions. Constipation risk is higher. Initiate at the low end of the dosing range [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Higher hydrocodone plasma concentrations may occur. Use a low initial dose and monitor closely for respiratory depression [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Higher hydrocodone plasma concentrations may occur. Use a low initial dose and monitor for adverse events [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Hydrocodone:** Full opioid agonist with relative selectivity for the mu-opioid receptor, providing analgesia via the CNS [US].
    - **Ibuprofen:** Non-selective inhibitor of cyclooxygenase (COX-1 and COX-2), reducing prostaglandin synthesis in peripheral tissues [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak plasma levels achieved at 1.7 hours (hydrocodone) and 1.8 hours (ibuprofen) [US].
    - **Distribution:** Ibuprofen is 99% protein-bound. Hydrocodone protein binding is not definitively determined but expected to be 19-45% [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Hydrocodone is metabolised via CYP2D6 (to hydromorphone) and CYP3A4 (to norhydrocodone). Ibuprofen is metabolised to two primary inactive metabolites [US].
    - **Elimination:** Hydrocodone half-life is 4.5 hours; ibuprofen half-life is 2.2 hours. Both are primarily eliminated via the kidneys [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Post-Surgical Pain:** In single-dose studies of post-surgical pain (abdominal, gynecological, orthopedic) involving 940 patients, the combination of hydrocodone and ibuprofen produced greater efficacy than placebo or either component alone at the same dose [US].
- **Dose Response:** No clinical advantage was demonstrated for a two-tablet dose compared to a one-tablet dose in these studies [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container [US].
- **Security:** Store securely and out of reach of children to prevent accidental ingestion or diversion [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States [FDA]:** 
    - **Classification:** Rx-only.
    - **Schedule:** Controlled Substance Schedule II (C-II).
    - **REMS:** Subject to the Opioid Analgesic Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy (REMS).
