# Editorial Product Information: Hydrocodone Polistirex and Chlorpheniramine Polistirex

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial:
    - **United States (FDA)**: Product Information for Hydrocodone Polistirex and Chlorpheniramine Polistirex Extended-Release Oral Suspension (Revised 07/2024).
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals based on available regulatory data. 
- **Disclaimer**: This document is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics mandated by the regulatory authority in the jurisdiction where prescribing occurs. At the time of writing, only United States regulatory documentation was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients**: 
    - **Hydrocodone polistirex**: An opioid agonist and centrally-acting antitussive.
    - **Chlorpheniramine polistirex**: A H1-receptor antagonist (antihistamine) of the alkylamine class.
- **Brand Name(s)**: Not specified in available sources (Generic formulation by Cranbury Pharmaceuticals, LLC provided as reference).
- **ATC Code**: Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s)**: 
    - Extended-release oral suspension [US].
    - Each 5 mL contains hydrocodone polistirex equivalent to 10 mg of hydrocodone bitartrate and chlorpheniramine polistirex equivalent to 8 mg of chlorpheniramine maleate [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Cranbury Pharmaceuticals, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Cough and Upper Respiratory Symptoms**: Indicated for the temporary relief of cough and upper respiratory symptoms associated with allergy or the common cold [US-approved].
- **Approved Patient Population**: Adults 18 years of age and older [US-approved].

**Important Limitations of Use [US]:**
- Not indicated for paediatric patients under 18 years of age.
- Use should be reserved for adult patients in whom the benefits of cough suppression outweigh the risks (addiction, abuse, misuse) and after the etiology of the cough has been adequately assessed.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults $\ge$ 18 years)**: 5 mL every 12 hours as needed [US].
- **Maximum Dose**: Do not exceed 2 doses (10 mL) in any 24-hour period [US].
- **Administration Instructions**:
    - Administer via the oral route only.
    - May be taken with or without food.
    - **Shake well** before use.
    - Do not mix the suspension with other liquids or medications, as this may alter the extended-release properties.
    - Use an accurate milliliter measuring device; a household teaspoon is not an adequate measuring tool.
- **Monitoring/Maintenance**:
    - Prescribe for the shortest duration consistent with treatment goals.
    - Re-evaluate patients with unresponsive cough within 5 days to investigate underlying pathology.
    - Re-evaluate the patient's clinical status and risk of misuse prior to providing refills.
- **Discontinuation**: In physically dependent patients, taper the dose by 25% to 50% every 2 to 4 days to avoid withdrawal symptoms.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Paediatrics**: Children younger than 6 years of age [US]. (Note: Indications are limited to $\ge$ 18 years).
- **Respiratory Status**: Significant respiratory depression; acute or severe bronchial asthma in an unmonitored setting or in the absence of resuscitative equipment.
- **Gastrointestinal**: Known or suspected gastrointestinal obstruction, including paralytic ileus.
- **Hypersensitivity**: Known hypersensitivity to hydrocodone, chlorpheniramine, or any inactive ingredients (e.g., sodium metabisulfite).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [US]**: 
    - **Addiction, Abuse, and Misuse**: Risk of opioid addiction, which can lead to overdose and death.
    - **Life-Threatening Respiratory Depression**: Serious or fatal depression of respiration may occur. 
    - **Accidental Ingestion**: Even a single accidental dose, especially by children, can be fatal.
    - **CYP3A4 Interaction**: Concomitant use with CYP3A4 inhibitors/inducers can result in fatal hydrocodone overdose.
    - **CNS Depressants and Alcohol**: Concomitant use with benzodiazepines, other CNS depressants, or alcohol may cause profound sedation, coma, and death.
    - **Neonatal Opioid Withdrawal Syndrome**: Prolonged use during pregnancy can result in life-threatening withdrawal in the neonate.
- **Activities Requiring Alertness**: May produce marked drowsiness; patients should avoid driving or operating machinery.
- **Head Injury and Increased Intracranial Pressure (ICP)**: Avoid use in patients with head injury or brain tumors, as it may increase ICP and obscure the clinical course.
- **Hypotension**: May cause severe hypotension, including orthostatic hypotension and syncope.
- **Seizure Disorders**: May increase the frequency of seizures in patients with known seizure disorders.
- **Adrenal Insufficiency**: Reported with chronic opioid use (>1 month).

## 6. Adverse Effects
Frequencies are not strictly quantified in the provided source, but clinical presentations include:
- **Common**: Sedation (somnolence, mental clouding, lethargy), impaired mental/physical performance, dizziness, lightheadedness, headache, dry mouth, nausea, vomiting, and constipation.
- **Serious Adverse Effects**:
    - Respiratory depression and arrest.
    - Anaphylaxis.
    - Paralytic ileus.
    - Serotonin syndrome (when used with serotonergic drugs).
    - Severe hypotension.
- **Other Reported Effects**: 
    - *Cardiovascular*: Tachycardia, palpitations, syncope, prolonged QT interval.
    - *Gastrointestinal*: Biliary tract spasm, pancreatitis.
    - *Genitourinary*: Urinary retention, ureteral spasm.
    - *Hematologic*: Agranulocytosis, aplastic anemia, thrombocytopenia.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Alcohol**: Avoid concomitant use; increases hydrocodone plasma levels and risk of fatal overdose.
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors (e.g., Macrolides, Azole-antifungals, Protease inhibitors)**: May increase hydrocodone levels; avoid use.
- **CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., Rifampin, Carbamazepine, Phenytoin)**: May decrease hydrocodone efficacy or precipitate withdrawal.
- **Benzodiazepines and Other CNS Depressants**: Risk of fatal respiratory depression and sedation.
- **Serotonergic Drugs**: Risk of serotonin syndrome.
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs)**: Avoid use within 14 days of MAOI therapy; risk of opioid toxicity or serotonin syndrome.
- **Anticholinergic Drugs**: Increased risk of urinary retention and severe constipation/paralytic ileus.
- **Phenytoin**: Chlorpheniramine may inhibit phenytoin metabolism, leading to phenytoin toxicity.
- **Diuretics**: Opioids may reduce diuretic efficacy by inducing antidiuretic hormone release.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **US Category**: Not specified (Letter categories phased out), but the PI notes use is **not recommended** [US]. 
    - **Risks**: Prolonged use can cause Neonatal Opioid Withdrawal Syndrome. Animal data suggests teratogenic effects at high doses (70x MRHD).
- **Breastfeeding**: **Not recommended** [US]. Hydrocodone is present in human milk; risk of sedation and respiratory depression in the infant. Chlorpheniramine may also decrease milk production.
- **Fertility**: Chronic opioid use may cause reduced fertility in both males and females; it is unknown if these effects are reversible.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Not indicated for patients under 18 years. Contraindicated in children < 6 years due to risk of fatal respiratory depression [US].
- **Elderly Use ($\ge$ 65 years)**: Caution advised. Increased sensitivity to hydrocodone and higher risk of respiratory depression. Monitor for sedation and hypotension.
- **Renal Impairment**: Caution advised in severe impairment. Chlorpheniramine is cleared renally; risk of toxicity is increased.
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Caution advised in severe impairment. Chlorpheniramine is extensively metabolised by the liver.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**:
    - **Hydrocodone**: Opioid agonist with selectivity for the $\mu$-receptor; acts centrally on the cough center in the medulla.
    - **Chlorpheniramine**: H1-receptor antagonist that prevents histamine-induced vasodilation and edema of the respiratory mucosa; also possesses anticholinergic activity.
- **Pharmacokinetics [US]**:
    - **Absorption**: Peak plasma concentrations ($C_{max}$) after multiple dosing: Hydrocodone = 22.8 ng/mL at 3.4 hours ($T_{max}$); Chlorpheniramine = 58.4 ng/mL at 6.3 hours ($T_{max}$). Food has no significant effect on hydrocodone absorption.
    - **Distribution**: Chlorpheniramine $V_d \approx$ 3.2 L/kg; ~70% protein bound. Hydrocodone protein binding is not definitively determined but likely 19–45%.
    - **Metabolism**: Hydrocodone via CYP3A4 (N-demethylation) and CYP2D6 (O-demethylation to hydromorphone). Chlorpheniramine via CYP2D6.
    - **Elimination**: Both are primarily excreted via the kidneys. 
    - **Half-life ($t_{1/2}$)**: Hydrocodone $\approx$ 4 hours; Chlorpheniramine $\approx$ 21–24 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Data**: Not specified in available sources. The formulation was initially approved in the US in 1987.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling**: Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container with a child-resistant closure. Protect from light and direct sunlight.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States**:
    - **Poison Schedule**: Schedule II (CII) Controlled Substance.
    - **Legal Classification**: Prescription-only (Rx).
