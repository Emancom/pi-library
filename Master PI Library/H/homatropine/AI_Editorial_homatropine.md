# Editorial Product Information: Hydrocodone Bitartrate and Homatropine Methylbromide

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of hydrocodone bitartrate and homatropine methylbromide for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference. It is not a substitute for the official PI used for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Hydrocodone bitartrate (opioid agonist) and Homatropine methylbromide (muscarinic antagonist).
- **Brand Names:** Hydrocodone Bitartrate and Homatropine Methylbromide (Generic).
- **ATC Code:** R05DA20 (Hydrocodone and anticholinergics).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Tablets:** 5 mg hydrocodone bitartrate and 1.5 mg homatropine methylbromide [United States].
    - **Oral Solution:** 5 mg hydrocodone bitartrate and 1.5 mg homatropine methylbromide per 5 mL [United States].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** KVK-Tech, Inc. [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Symptomatic Relief of Cough:** Indicated for the management of cough in adult patients 18 years of age and older [United States].
- **Limitations of Use:** Due to the risks of addiction, abuse, and misuse, use should be reserved for patients where alternative treatments (non-opioid) are ineffective, not tolerated, or otherwise inadequate [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Adult Dosing (≥18 years):** One (1) tablet or 5 mL of oral solution every 4 to 6 hours as required [United States].
- **Maximum Dose:** Not to exceed six (6) tablets or 30 mL in any 24-hour period [United States].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Use an accurate milliliter measuring device for the oral solution; household teaspoons are not considered accurate and increase the risk of overdosage [United States].
    - Prescribe for the shortest duration consistent with treatment objectives.
- **Monitoring:** Reevaluate patients with an unresponsive cough within 5 days for possible underlying pathology [United States].
- **Discontinuation:** In physically dependent patients, do not abruptly discontinue. Taper the dose gradually (e.g., 25% to 50% every 2 to 4 days) to avoid withdrawal symptoms [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pediatric Use:** Contraindicated in all children younger than 6 years of age [United States].
- **Respiratory Status:** Significant respiratory depression; acute or severe bronchial asthma in unmonitored settings or without resuscitative equipment [United States].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Known or suspected GI obstruction, including paralytic ileus [United States].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to hydrocodone, homatropine, or any inactive ingredients [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [United States]:**
    - **Addiction, Abuse, and Misuse:** Risk of opioid addiction which can lead to overdose and death.
    - **Life-Threatening Respiratory Depression:** Serious or fatal depression of respiration may occur; monitor closely during initiation.
    - **Accidental Ingestion:** Even a single dose, especially if taken by a child, can be fatal.
    - **Cytochrome P450 3A4 Interaction:** Concomitant use with CYP3A4 inhibitors or stopping inducers can lead to fatal hydrocodone overdose.
    - **CNS Depressants:** Concomitant use with benzodiazepines, alcohol, or other CNS depressants may cause profound sedation, coma, and death.
    - **Neonatal Opioid Withdrawal Syndrome:** Prolonged use during pregnancy can lead to life-threatening withdrawal in the newborn.
- **Head Injury and Increased Intracranial Pressure (ICP):** Avoid use; hydrocodone may increase ICP and obscure the clinical course of head injuries [United States].
- **Seizure Disorders:** May increase seizure frequency; monitor during therapy [United States].
- **Severe Hypotension:** Use with caution in patients with compromised blood pressure or circulatory shock [United States].
- **Adrenal Insufficiency:** Observed with chronic opioid use (>1 month); if diagnosed, treat with corticosteroids and wean the patient off the opioid [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most common adverse reactions include:
- **Central Nervous System:** Sedation (somnolence, mental clouding, lethargy), lightheadedness, dizziness, headache, impaired physical/mental performance.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Dry mouth, nausea, vomiting, and constipation.

**Serious/Other Adverse Effects [United States]:**
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis.
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, orthostatic hypotension, syncope, prolonged QT interval.
- **Endocrine:** Serotonin syndrome (with serotonergic drugs), adrenal insufficiency, hypogonadism, infertility (chronic use).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Paralytic ileus, biliary tract spasm (sphincter of Oddi), opioid-induced esophageal dysfunction (OIED).
- **Laboratory:** Transient elevations in serum amylase or lipase (interfering with diagnosis of pancreatitis).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors (e.g., Macrolides, Azole Antifungals):** May increase hydrocodone plasma levels, increasing the risk of respiratory depression [United States].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., Rifampin, Phenytoin):** May decrease efficacy or precipitate withdrawal upon discontinuation [United States].
- **Benzodiazepines and CNS Depressants (including Alcohol):** Additive effect increasing risk of fatal respiratory depression and profound sedation [United States].
- **Serotonergic Drugs:** Risk of serotonin syndrome.
- **MAOIs:** Avoid use in patients taking MAOIs or within 14 days of stopping them due to potential for serotonin syndrome or opioid toxicity [United States].
- **Anticholinergics:** Concurrent use with homatropine may increase risk of urinary retention and severe constipation [United States].
- **Diuretics:** Opioids may reduce diuretic efficacy [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Not recommended [United States]. 
    - **US Category:** Not specified (Lettered categories discontinued), but animal data show teratogenicity (cranioschisis) in hamsters at 45 times the MRHD.
    - **Neonatal Opioid Withdrawal Syndrome:** Risk with prolonged maternal use.
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. Hydrocodone is excreted in human milk; risk of infant sedation and fatal respiratory depression [United States]. Homatropine may decrease milk production.
- **Fertility:** Chronic opioid use may cause reduced fertility in both males and females; reversibility is unknown [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not indicated for patients <18 years. Contraindicated in children <6 years [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of respiratory depression. Use caution, starting at the low end of the dosing range, and monitor renal/hepatic function [United States].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution in severe impairment; monitor for respiratory depression and sedation [United States].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution in severe impairment; monitor closely for toxicity [United States].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:**
    - **Hydrocodone:** Semisynthetic opioid agonist that acts centrally on the medullary cough center to suppress cough. It also has analgesic and respiratory depressant properties [United States].
    - **Homatropine Methylbromide:** A muscarinic antagonist added in sub-therapeutic doses to discourage deliberate overdosage/abuse of the opioid component [United States].
- **Pharmacodynamics:**
    - Causes miosis, reduced GI motility, and peripheral vasodilation [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Mean peak concentration (10 mg dose) is 23.6 ng/mL at ~1.3 hours. Food has no significant effect [United States].
    - **Distribution:** Protein binding ~19%–45%.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP3A4 (N-demethylation) and CYP2D6 (O-demethylation to hydromorphone) [United States].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal; mean plasma half-life is approximately 4 hours [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Not specified in available sources. The product was initially approved in the U.S. in 1943; modern pivotal trial data are not detailed in the source document.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Packaging:** Dispense in tight, light-resistant containers with a child-resistant closure [United States].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Schedule II Controlled Substance (C-II); Rx-only.
