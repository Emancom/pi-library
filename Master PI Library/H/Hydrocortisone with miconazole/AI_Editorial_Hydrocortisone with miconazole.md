# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (US FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data and regulatory instructions.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. The information provided is based solely on the source material provided.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN). 
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Generic), marketed by Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US]; Manufactured by Baxter Pharmaceuticals India Private Ltd [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Metabolic Disorders)
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours.
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg.

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels below 40–50 µmol/L. Adjustments (e.g., downward to 5 mg/kg) may be made by the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter and discoloration before use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL).
- Discard any unused portion (preservative-free).

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Contraindications:** None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- None reported in available sources.

### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some occurred within minutes of IV administration. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs.
- **Neurologic Effects (Seizures):** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed.

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Oral Formulation in Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. High oral doses in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) normally excreted in urine.
- **Monitoring:** Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended. Monitor blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma free carnitine concentrations (target range: 35 to 60 µmol/L).

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common and Frequent Adverse Effects
In clinical trials of ESRD patients (doses 10–40 mg/kg), the following events occurred at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality):
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (5–21%), headache (3–37%), infection (10–24%), injection site reaction (27–38%), pain (21–35%), fever (5–12%), flu syndrome (15–29%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), chest pain (6–15%), tachycardia (5–9%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%).
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%).
- **Respiratory:** Cough increase (10–18%), pharyngitis (15–27%), dyspnea (3–11%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hypervolemia (3–12%).

### Serious and Other Adverse Effects
- **Neurologic:** Seizures.
- **Allergic:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis, body odor (less frequent).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed. 
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category C equivalent]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (BSA) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. No adequate human studies exist. Use only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine milk concentrations following exogenous administration. Risks to the child must be weighed against maternal benefit.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies indicate no impairment of fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg). Clinical data for ESRD is primarily in adults, though dosing is specified by weight (mg/kg).
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for ESRD. Cautions regarding the use of oral formulations due to metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO) apply to patients with severely compromised renal function [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Mean distribution half-life: 0.585 hours.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Terminal elimination half-life: 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels).
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Increases plasma carnitine concentrations. Normal plasma free carnitine levels are defined as 40–50 µmol/L; deficiency is defined as <20 µmol/L.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis showed mean pre-dialysis trough concentrations increased from 21.3 µmol/L (baseline) to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Evaluated 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses. Trough concentrations at 24 weeks were 148, 240, and 456 µmol/L, respectively. 
- **Note:** While effective at increasing carnitine levels, the effect on specific clinical outcomes in ESRD patients has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Shelf Life:** Not specified in available sources (Revision date 2026-02-27 noted).
