# Editorial Product Information: Hemin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis for clinical reference and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Hemin (an enzyme inhibitor derived from processed human red blood cells; previously known as hematin).
- **Brand Name:** **Panhematin** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile, lyophilized powder for reconstitution. Each single-dose vial contains the equivalent of 350 mg hemin, 240 mg sodium carbonate, and 335 mg sorbitol [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Recordati Rare Diseases Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Intermittent Porphyria (AIP):** Indicated for the amelioration of recurrent attacks of AIP temporally related to the menstrual cycle in susceptible women [US]. 
- **Therapeutic Context:** Usage is indicated after initial carbohydrate therapy (e.g., 400 g glucose/day for 1–2 days) is known or suspected to be inadequate [US].
- **Limitations of Use:**
    - Not effective for repairing neuronal damage resulting from the progression of porphyria attacks [US].
    - Therapy is intended to prevent attacks from reaching the critical stage of neuronal degeneration [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** 1 to 4 mg/kg/day of hemin for 3 to 14 days, based on clinical signs. The standard dose in clinical practice is 3 to 4 mg/kg/day [US].
- **Severe Cases:** The dose may be repeated no earlier than every 12 hours. Do not exceed a total of 6 mg/kg in any 24-hour period [US].
- **Preparation:** 
    - Reconstitute with 48 mL of Sterile Water for Injection, USP. 
    - Shake well for 2 to 3 minutes to ensure dissolution.
    - Resulting concentration is approximately 7 mg/mL [US].
    - Because the solution contains no preservatives and undergoes rapid chemical decomposition, it must be reconstituted immediately before use. Discard any unused portion [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Use a sterile 0.45 micron or smaller filter to remove undissolved particulates [US].
    - Infuse over at least 30 minutes via a separate line [US].
    - Following infusion, flush the vein with 100 mL of 0.9% NaCl [US].
- **Clinical Monitoring:** Effectiveness is demonstrated by a decrease in urinary concentrations of δ-aminolevulinic acid (ALA), porphobilinogen (PBG), uroporphyrin, or coproporphyrin [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **Panhematin** or any of its components [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Phlebitis:** Risk of phlebitis is present. Administration should occur via a large arm vein or a central venous catheter [US].
- **Iron Overload:** Increased levels of iron and serum ferritin have been reported. Monitor iron and ferritin levels in patients receiving multiple treatments. Consider iron chelation therapy if levels are elevated [US].
- **Anticoagulant Effects:** Hemin exhibits a transient, mild anticoagulant effect. Concurrent anticoagulant therapy should be avoided [US].
- **Renal Effects:** Reversible renal shutdown has occurred with excessive dosing (e.g., 12.2 mg/kg in a single infusion). Recommended dosage guidelines must be strictly followed [US].
- **Transmissible Infectious Agents:** As a product derived from human blood, it carries a potential risk of transmitting infectious agents (e.g., viruses, vCJD). While donors are screened and the manufacturing process includes viral inactivation, the risk cannot be totally eliminated [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common Adverse Reactions (>1%):** 
    - Nervous System: Headache (9.2%)
    - Vascular: Phlebitis/Injection site phlebitis (3.6%)
    - General: Pyrexia (4.6%), Catheter-related complications (3.6%)
    - Skin: Rash (1.5%)
    - Infections: Cellulitis (1.5%)
- **Post-marketing Experience (Frequency not established):**
    - **Blood/Lymphatic:** Thrombocytopenia, coagulopathy (prolonged PT/PTT), hemolysis [US].
    - **Immune System:** Hypersensitivity reactions, including infusion-related anaphylactoid reactions and circulatory collapse [US].
    - **Vascular:** Injection site venous thrombosis (including large veins like the venae cavae) [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Iron overload, increased serum ferritin [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP Inducers:** Avoid drugs that induce δ-aminolevulinic acid synthetase 1 (ALAS1) through feedback mechanisms. These include:
    - Estrogens (e.g., oral contraceptives)
    - Barbiturates
    - Steroid metabolites [US].
- **Anticoagulants:** Avoid concurrent use due to the transient hypocoagulable state induced by hemin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not specified by letter [US]. 
    - Clinical data are insufficient to establish drug-associated risks. AIP attacks in pregnancy can be fatal; hemin should be used only if clearly needed [US].
    - **Special Caution:** Avoid use in severe pre-eclampsia due to the theoretical risk of potentiating coagulation disorders [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if hemin is excreted in human milk. Consider the developmental benefits of breastfeeding alongside the mother's clinical need and potential risks to the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal reproduction and fertility studies have not been conducted [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 16 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Data are insufficient to determine if subjects aged 65+ respond differently. Dosing should be cautious, typically starting at the low end of the range to account for decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Specific dosing adjustments for impairment are not provided, though caution is advised in the elderly regarding these functions [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Hemin acts as an enzyme inhibitor to limit the hepatic and/or marrow synthesis of porphyrin. This is likely achieved by inhibiting ALAS1, the rate-limiting enzyme in the porphyrin/heme biosynthetic pathway [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Symptomatic improvement is observed in acute porphyria episodes, though the drug is not curative [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - Following IV administration, an increase in fecal urobilinogen is observed, suggesting an enterohepatic pathway as a route of elimination [US].
    - Bilirubin metabolites are excreted in the urine [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Compassionate Use Study:** In a study of 130 patients, hemin was used for acute attacks and prophylaxis. Physician-assessed clinical response was achieved in 73% of acute attacks (81 of 111 patients). In prophylaxis, 68% of patients (21 of 31) treated for >1 month did not require subsequent treatment for acute attacks [US].
- **Open-Label Studies:** In five studies involving 99 patients (primarily AIP), treatment with 3–4 mg/kg/day resulted in a clinical response rate of 85.5%. Chemical response (reduction in urinary ALA/PBG) was observed in nearly all patients [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Lyophilized Powder:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Reconstituted solution contains no preservatives and should be used immediately.
- **Safety Note:** The vial stopper contains natural rubber latex [US].
