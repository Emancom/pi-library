# Editorial Product Information: Hydroxychloroquine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory information into a clinically relevant format.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this writing, only the Australian PI was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Hydroxychloroquine sulfate (Chemical class: 4-aminoquinoline).
- **Brand Names:** APO-Hydroxychloroquine [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** P01BA02.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 200 mg film-coated tablets [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA):** Treatment of active RA.
- **Lupus Erythematosus:** Management of mild systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) and discoid lupus erythematosus (DLE).
- **Malaria:** Suppression and treatment of acute attacks of malaria caused by *P. vivax*, *P. malariae*, and susceptible strains of *P. falciparum* (not effective against gametocytes or exo-erythrocytic forms).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Tablets should be taken at meal times to improve tolerability.
- **Retinopathy Prevention:** Maintenance doses should not exceed 6 mg/kg (calculated by ideal/lean body weight) per day [Australian].

### Adult Dosing
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis:** 
    - *Initial:* 400–600 mg daily. 
    - *Maintenance:* 200–400 mg daily once an optimum response is achieved (usually 4–12 weeks).
    - *Discontinuation:* If no objective improvement occurs within six months, the drug should be ceased.
- **Lupus Erythematosus:** 
    - *Initial:* 400–800 mg daily for several weeks.
    - *Maintenance:* 200–400 mg daily based on patient response.
- **Malaria Suppression:** 400 mg (310 mg base) taken on the same day each week, starting two weeks prior to exposure and continuing for eight weeks after leaving the endemic area.
- **Acute Malaria Attack:** Total dose of 2 g (1.55 g base) over three days (800 mg initially, followed by 400 mg at 6–8 hours, then 400 mg daily for two days).

### Paediatric Dosing (Malaria only)
- **Suppression:** 5 mg/kg (base) weekly, not to exceed the adult dose.
- **Acute Attack:** Total dose of 25 mg/kg (base) over three days:
    - *Dose 1:* 10 mg/kg (max 620 mg base).
    - *Dose 2:* 5 mg/kg (max 310 mg base) 6 hours after dose 1.
    - *Dose 3 & 4:* 5 mg/kg at 18 and 24 hours after the previous dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- Pre-existing maculopathy of the eye [Australian].
- Known hypersensitivity to 4-aminoquinoline compounds [Australian].
- Use for long-term therapy in children [Australian].
- Use in children under 6 years of age (200 mg tablets are not suitable for lower body weights) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Ophthalmological Toxicity:** Irreversible retinal damage is a major risk, particularly with high daily doses or long-term use (>8 years). Baseline eye exams (fundoscopy, visual fields, colour vision) are required, with follow-ups every 6 months, or more frequently (3–4 monthly) in high-risk patients (elderly, renal impairment, or doses >6 mg/kg lean weight) [Australian].
- **Cardiac Toxicity:** Chronic use may lead to life-threatening cardiomyopathy and conduction disorders (e.g., bundle branch block, AV block). Cardiac monitoring is advised; therapy must be stopped if cardiotoxicity is suspected.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Serious liver injury, including fulminant hepatic failure, has been reported. Monitor LFTs in symptomatic patients or those with pre-existing liver disease.
- **QT Prolongation:** Hydroxychloroquine may prolong the QTc interval. Avoid in patients with congenital or acquired QT prolongation or those taking other pro-arrhythmic medications.
- **Hypoglycaemia:** Can cause severe, life-threatening hypoglycaemia even in patients without diabetes.
- **Psychiatric Effects:** Suicidal behaviour and psychosis may occur, often within the first month of treatment.
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Monitor for SJS, TEN, DRESS, and AGEP. Discontinue immediately if suspected.

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Abdominal pain, nausea |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Diarrhoea, vomiting, anorexia, affect lability, headache, blurring of vision, skin rashes, pruritus |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Dizziness, vertigo, tinnitus, nerve deafness, corneal/retinal changes (pigmentation/visual field defects), abnormal LFTs |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Bone marrow depression, anaemia, leukopenia, convulsions, cardiomyopathy, psoriasis exacerbation |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Agranulocytosis, psychosis, suicidal behaviour, DILI, SCARs, exfoliative dermatitis |
| **Not Known** | Hypoglycaemia, extrapyramidal disorders, hearing loss, renal phospholipidosis |

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **QT-Prolonging Drugs:** Increased risk of ventricular arrhythmias with Class IA/III antiarrhythmics, tricyclic antidepressants, and antipsychotics.
- **Macrolide Antibiotics:** Specifically azithromycin; co-administration increases cardiovascular events and mortality [Australian].
- **Antidiabetics:** May enhance hypoglycaemic effects; insulin or oral agent doses may require reduction.
- **Antimalarials:** Mefloquine and others may lower the convulsion threshold.

### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **P-glycoprotein (P-gp) Substrates:** Hydroxychloroquine inhibits P-gp. Monitor levels of digoxin and dabigatran closely.
- **CYP Enzyme Interactions:**
    - **Inhibition:** Hydroxychloroquine is a moderate inhibitor of **CYP3A4** (increases simvastatin/ciclosporin exposure) and a mild inhibitor of **CYP2D6** (increases metoprolol/flecainide exposure).
    - **Metabolism:** Hydroxychloroquine is metabolised by CYP2C8, 3A4, and 2D6. Strong inhibitors (e.g., gemfibrozil, ritonavir) may increase exposure, while inducers (e.g., rifampicin, St John's Wort) may decrease efficacy.
- **Antacids/Kaolin:** May reduce absorption; space administration by at least 2 hours.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Hydroxychloroquine crosses the placenta. Therapeutic doses have been linked to CNS damage, ototoxicity, and retinal changes in the fetus. A small increase in major malformations has been noted at daily doses $\ge$ 400 mg. Avoid in pregnancy unless the benefit to the mother outweighs the risk to the fetus (e.g., in high-risk malaria areas).
- **Breastfeeding:** Excreted in breast milk (estimated <2% of maternal dose). Infants are extremely sensitive to 4-aminoquinolines. Use with caution; the amount in milk is insufficient to protect the infant from malaria [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal data suggests potential reductions in male fertility (testosterone/sperm parameters); human data is not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Contraindicated for long-term use in children. Tablets are not suitable for children <6 years of age or those weighing <35 kg [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Higher risk of retinal toxicity; requires more frequent ophthalmological monitoring.
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution. Exposure may increase by 17–30% in severe impairment. Dosage reduction may be necessary [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution. Exposure may increase by 41–57% in moderate-to-severe disease. Dosage reduction may be necessary [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** While the precise mechanism in autoimmune disease is unknown, it acts as an antimalarial by interfering with the erythrocytic forms of susceptible parasites. It also possesses anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory properties.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - *Absorption:* Bioavailability is ~79%. Peak plasma levels occur in 3–4 hours.
    - *Distribution:* Extremely large volume of distribution (5500 L from blood); accumulates extensively in tissues (eyes, liver, lungs) and blood cells.
    - *Metabolism:* Primarily hepatic via CYP2C8, 3A4, and 2D6 to N-desethylhydroxychloroquine.
    - *Elimination:* Terminal half-life is very long (30–50 days). Approximately 20–25% is excreted unchanged in the urine.
