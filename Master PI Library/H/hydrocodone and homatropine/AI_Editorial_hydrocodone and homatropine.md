# Editorial Product Information: Hydrocodone Bitartrate and Homatropine Methylbromide

## Editorial Note
- The jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributing to this editorial include: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the active ingredients hydrocodone bitartrate and homatropine methylbromide for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Hydrocodone bitartrate (opioid agonist) and homatropine methylbromide (muscarinic antagonist).
- **Brand Names:** Available as various generics; specific manufacturer included in source: KVK-Tech, Inc [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Oral Solution:** 5 mg hydrocodone bitartrate and 1.5 mg homatropine methylbromide per 5 mL [US].
    - **Tablets:** 5 mg hydrocodone bitartrate and 1.5 mg homatropine methylbromide per tablet [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** KVK-Tech, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Symptomatic Relief of Cough:** Indicated for the management of cough in adult patients 18 years of age and older [US-approved].
- **Limitations of Use:** Due to risks of addiction, abuse, and misuse, use should be reserved for patients for whom alternative treatments are ineffective, not tolerated, or inadequate [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Adult Dosing (18 years and older):** One (1) tablet or 5 mL of oral solution every 4 to 6 hours as needed [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** Do not exceed six (6) tablets or 30 mL in any 24-hour period [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Oral solution must be measured with an accurate milliliter measuring device; household teaspoons are not acceptable [US].
    - Prescribe for the shortest duration consistent with treatment goals [US].
- **Monitoring:** Re-evaluate patients with unresponsive cough within 5 days for underlying pathology. Re-evaluate patients prior to any prescription refill [US].
- **Discontinuation:** In physically dependent patients, do not abruptly discontinue. Taper the dose gradually (e.g., 25% to 50% every 2 to 4 days) while monitoring for withdrawal symptoms [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pediatric Patients:** Contraindicated in all children younger than 6 years of age [US].
- **Respiratory Status:** Significant respiratory depression [US].
- **Asthma:** Acute or severe bronchial asthma in unmonitored settings or without resuscitative equipment [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Known or suspected gastrointestinal obstruction, including paralytic ileus [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to hydrocodone, homatropine, or any inactive ingredients [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings [US]:**
    - **Addiction, Abuse, and Misuse:** Potential for opioid addiction, which can lead to overdose and death.
    - **Life-Threatening Respiratory Depression:** Serious or fatal depression may occur; monitor closely, especially during initiation.
    - **Accidental Ingestion:** Even a single dose, especially by children, can be fatal.
    - **Medication Errors:** Dosing errors with oral solution can result in accidental overdose.
    - **CYP3A4 Interactions:** Concomitant use with CYP3A4 inhibitors or discontinuation of inducers can increase hydrocodone levels to fatal levels.
    - **CNS Depressants:** Concomitant use with benzodiazepines, alcohol, or other CNS depressants may cause profound sedation, coma, and death.
    - **Neonatal Opioid Withdrawal Syndrome (NOWS):** Prolonged use during pregnancy can result in life-threatening withdrawal in the neonate.
- **General Warnings:**
    - **Activities Requiring Mental Alertness:** May impair physical or mental abilities; avoid driving or operating machinery [US].
    - **Head Injury and Increased Intracranial Pressure (IICP):** Avoid use; may increase IICP and obscure the clinical course of head injuries [US].
    - **Seizures:** May increase seizure frequency in patients with seizure disorders [US].
    - **Adrenal Insufficiency:** Reported with opioid use, typically after >1 month. If diagnosed, treat with corticosteroids and wean from the opioid [US].
    - **Hypotension:** May cause severe hypotension, including orthostatic hypotension and syncope [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Commonly Reported:** Sedation (somnolence, mental clouding, lethargy), impaired mental/physical performance, dizziness, lightheadedness, headache, dry mouth, nausea, vomiting, and constipation [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** 
    - **Respiratory:** Life-threatening respiratory depression, apnea [US].
    - **Cardiovascular:** Prolonged QT interval, tachycardia, hypotension [US].
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Paralytic ileus, biliary tract spasm (sphincter of Oddi spasm), pancreatitis [US].
    - **Immune:** Anaphylaxis [US].
    - **Endocrine:** Hypoglycemia, androgen deficiency (chronic use), serotonin syndrome [US].
- **Frequencies:** Specific frequency percentages (e.g., >10%) were not specified in available sources.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors/Inducers:** Inhibitors (e.g., macrolides, azoles) increase hydrocodone levels; inducers (e.g., rifampin, phenytoin) decrease levels. Avoid concomitant use [US].
- **Benzodiazepines and CNS Depressants:** Additive effect increases risk of respiratory depression and death. Avoid use unless alternatives are inadequate [US].
- **Alcohol:** Potentiates opioid effects and may result in fatal plasma levels. Avoid all alcohol consumption [US].
- **Serotonergic Drugs:** Increased risk of serotonin syndrome; monitor during initiation and discontinue if suspected [US].
- **MAOIs:** Avoid use in patients taking MAOIs or within 14 days of stopping them; may increase opioid or antidepressant effects [US].
- **Anticholinergic Drugs:** Increased risk of urinary retention and severe constipation/paralytic ileus [US].
- **Diuretics:** Hydrocodone may reduce diuretic efficacy by inducing antidiuretic hormone release [US].
- **Muscle Relaxants:** Hydrocodone may enhance neuromuscular blocking action [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - Not recommended for use in pregnant women [US]. 
    - **Neonatal Opioid Withdrawal Syndrome (NOWS):** Risk with prolonged use [US].
    - **Category:** Not explicitly assigned in the provided current PLR format, but documented as causing potential fetal harm and teratogenic effects (cranioschisis) in animal studies at 45x the MRHD [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. Hydrocodone is excreted in breast milk; risk of sedation and fatal respiratory depression in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Chronic opioid use may cause reduced fertility in males and females; reversibility is unknown [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established in patients <18 years. Use is not recommended for cough in this age group as risks outweigh benefits [US]. (Contraindicated in <6 years).
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of respiratory depression; monitor closely. Use with caution due to higher frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Pharmacokinetics not characterized. Use with caution in severe impairment; monitor for sedation and respiratory depression [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Pharmacokinetics not characterized. Use with caution in severe impairment; monitor closely [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:**
    - **Hydrocodone:** Opioid agonist with relative selectivity for mu-receptors; acts centrally on the cough center [US].
    - **Homatropine:** Anticholinergic (muscarinic antagonist) that inhibits muscarinic acetylcholine receptors; added in sub-therapeutic doses to discourage deliberate overdosage [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics [US]:**
    - **Absorption:** Mean peak concentration (10 mg dose) is 23.6 ng/mL at 1.3 hours. Food has no significant effect.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily N-demethylation (CYP3A4) to norhydrocodone and O-demethylation (CYP2D6) to hydromorphone.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal; mean plasma half-life is approximately 4 hours.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at controlled room temperature, 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Dispense in tight, light-resistant containers with child-resistant closures [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:**
    - **United States:** Schedule II (CII) controlled substance [US].
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
