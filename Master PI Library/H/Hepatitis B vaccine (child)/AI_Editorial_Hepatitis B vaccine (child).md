# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**. 
- Information from other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian, Japanese) was not provided for this specific drug and is therefore noted as "Not specified in available sources" where applicable.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI used for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule for fatty acid transport).
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Metabolic Disorders)
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** In cases of severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Adjustments:** Dose adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations [US].

### Administration and Monitoring
- **Monitoring:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of plasma carnitine concentrations (target range: 35 to 60 µmol/L), blood chemistries, and vital signs is recommended [US].
- **Preparation:** Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known:** There are no known contraindications listed in available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Discontinue immediately if a reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral formulation precaution):** While this PI covers the injection, it is noted that safety/efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine in renal impairment has not been evaluated; high-dose oral use in ESRD may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are reported regardless of causality and are common in the dialysis population.

| Frequency | Adverse Event [US] |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (30–35%), headache (22–37%), hypertension (18–21%), diarrhea (9–35%), pharyngitis (15–27%), vomiting (9–21%), cough increase (10–18%), dizziness (10–18%), chest pain (6–15%). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Abdominal pain, fever, nausea, accidental injury, asthenia, infection, tachycardia, palpitations, anemia, hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, depression, dyspnea, pruritus. |
| **Serious/Rare** | Anaphylaxis, seizures, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm. |

- **Body Odor:** Transient nausea, vomiting, and body odor have been observed with carnitine supplementation [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not explicitly lettered in current PI; however, animal data exists]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against maternal benefit [US].
- **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, yeast) were negative. Long-term animal carcinogenicity studies have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use in children is supported by the metabolic disorder indications; dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. Monitor trough levels to avoid accumulation.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also facilitates the excretion of excess organic/fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound; distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Increases plasma carnitine concentrations; in ESRD, it addresses the increased ratio of acylcarnitine to carnitine resulting from dialytic loss and reduced dietary intake [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean trough carnitine levels from 21.3 µmol/L at baseline to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) showed dose-proportional increases in plasma concentrations [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Discard unused portions of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservative [US].
