# Editorial Product Information: human immunoglobulin g

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **human immunoglobulin g**
- Chemical class: Immunoglobulin G (pooled human plasma-derived IgG)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intravenous only

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 2025
- Date of last revision/update in provided source: Revised 9/2025
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Primary indication:
  - Treatment of adults with Primary Humoral Immunodeficiency (PI), including but not limited to congenital agammaglobulinemia, common variable immunodeficiency (CVID), X‑linked agammaglobulinemia, Wiskott‑Aldrich syndrome, and severe combined immunodeficiencies.
- Measles prophylaxis:
  - Post‑exposure prophylaxis: administer 400 mg/kg as soon as possible after exposure.
  - Pre‑exposure prophylaxis: for patients who routinely receive less than 400 mg/kg every 3–4 weeks and who are at risk of measles exposure (e.g., travel to endemic area), administer at least 400 mg/kg prior to expected exposure.
- Other indications or region‑specific approvals: Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Typical replacement dose for PI: 300–800 mg/kg every 3–4 weeks.
- Adjust dose and frequency to achieve target serum IgG trough levels and clinical response.

### Infusion rates (recommended)
- First infusion:
  - Start at 1 mg/kg/min (0.01 mL/kg/min) for 30 minutes; if tolerated, increase every 30 minutes to a maximum of 8 mg/kg/min (0.08 mL/kg/min).
- From the second infusion onward:
  - Start at 2 mg/kg/min (0.02 mL/kg/min) for 15 minutes; if tolerated, increase every 15 minutes to a maximum of 8 mg/kg/min (0.08 mL/kg/min).
- For patients judged to be at increased risk for thrombosis or renal dysfunction, administer at the minimum dose and the minimum infusion rate practicable.

### Dosage by indication
- Primary immunodeficiency: 300–800 mg/kg every 3–4 weeks (see above infusion rates).
- Measles prophylaxis:
  - Post‑exposure: 400 mg/kg (4 mL/kg).
  - Pre‑exposure: at least 400 mg/kg (4 mL/kg) if routine dosing is <400 mg/kg.

### Preparation and handling (clinical use)
- Inspect the solution visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration when feasible; do not use if cloudy, turbid, or containing particulate matter.
- Do not shake the product. Do not dilute.
- Use aseptic technique when pooling multiple containers for a single administration.
- Use separate infusion line; at end of infusion flush the infusion tubing with saline solution or Dextrose 5% in water (D5W) to ensure the entire dose is delivered.
- Do not mix this product with other intravenous medications or other IGIV products (including normal saline) in the same container.
- This product is intended for single‑use; discard any opened or partially used product in accordance with local requirements.

### Administration instructions and precautions
- Hydrate adequately before initiation of infusion.
- Monitor vital signs throughout the infusion.
- If adverse reactions occur, slow or stop the infusion. If reactions resolve promptly, the infusion may be resumed at a lower rate as tolerated.
- For patients who have not previously received an IVIG product, who are switching from another IVIG product, or who have had a long interval since the last infusion, prolong the post‑infusion observation period.
- If a dose is missed, administer the missed dose as soon as possible and then resume the regular schedule.

## 5. Contraindications
- History of anaphylactic or severe systemic reactions to human immune globulins.
- IgA deficiency with documented anti‑IgA antibodies and a history of hypersensitivity reactions.
- Any other contraindications: Not specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warning
- **[US] WARNING:** Thrombosis, renal dysfunction, and acute renal failure can occur with intravenous immunoglobulin products, including human immunoglobulin g. Use the minimum dose and infusion rate practicable in patients at risk; ensure adequate hydration and monitor for signs of thrombosis and renal dysfunction.

### General warnings and precautions
- Hypersensitivity: Severe hypersensitivity reactions including anaphylaxis may occur. Epinephrine and other appropriate therapy should be available. Patients with known anti‑IgA antibodies are at increased risk.
- Thrombosis: Thrombotic events have been reported. Risk factors include advanced age, prolonged immobilization, hypercoagulable states, prior thrombosis, estrogen therapy, indwelling central vascular catheters, hyperviscosity, and cardiovascular risk factors. Thrombosis can occur in the absence of known risk factors.
  - Consider baseline assessment of blood viscosity in patients at risk for hyperviscosity (e.g., cryoglobulins, chylomicronemia, very high triglycerides, monoclonal gammopathies).
  - For patients at risk, use the minimum dose and infusion rate practicable and ensure adequate hydration.
- Renal injury: Acute renal dysfunction, acute renal failure, osmotic nephrosis, and tubular injury have been reported.
  - Patients at increased risk include those with pre‑existing renal insufficiency, diabetes mellitus, volume depletion, sepsis, paraproteinemia, advanced age (>65 years), overweight, or concomitant nephrotoxic drugs.
  - Assess renal function before initial infusion and periodically thereafter. If renal function deteriorates, consider discontinuation.
  - Products containing sucrose have higher reported risk; this product does not contain sucrose.
- Hyperproteinemia / Increased serum viscosity / Hyponatremia: May occur; distinguish true hyponatremia from pseudohyponatremia due to hyperproteinemia to avoid inappropriate fluid management that could worsen viscosity and thrombotic risk.
- Aseptic meningitis syndrome (AMS): May occur, typically within hours to 2 days after infusion. Symptoms include severe headache, nuchal rigidity, fever, photophobia, nausea/vomiting, and nuchal signs. CSF often shows pleocytosis and elevated protein with negative cultures. Discontinuation of IVIG has led to resolution.
- Hemolysis: Hemolysis and positive direct antiglobulin test (Coombs) can occur due to passively transferred blood group antibodies. Risk factors include high doses (≥2 g/kg), non‑O blood group, and underlying inflammation. Monitor hemoglobin/hematocrit in higher‑risk patients before and within ~36 hours and again 7–10 days after infusion.
- Transfusion‑related acute lung injury (TRALI): May occur within 1–6 hours after infusion. If suspected, stop infusion immediately, evaluate for anti‑neutrophil and anti‑HLA antibodies in patient and product, and provide respiratory support as needed.
- Transmission of infectious agents: This product is derived from human plasma and therefore carries a risk of transmitting infectious agents despite donor screening and validated viral inactivation/removal steps.
- Interference with laboratory tests: Passive transfer of antibodies can lead to misleading positive serologic tests (including viral serologies) and can interfere with red cell antibody testing (direct or indirect antiglobulin/Coombs tests).

### Monitoring requirements
- Renal function: measure BUN and serum creatinine prior to initial infusion and at appropriate intervals thereafter in patients at increased risk.
- Hematology: monitor hemoglobin/hematocrit in patients at risk for hemolysis; perform Coombs testing if clinically indicated.
- Thrombosis: monitor for signs and symptoms of venous or arterial thrombosis; consider blood viscosity measurement in at‑risk patients.
- Pulmonary: monitor for respiratory distress suggestive of TRALI.
- Neurologic: assess promptly for signs of AMS and evaluate with CSF studies to exclude infectious meningitis if indicated.
- If TRALI is suspected, perform appropriate antibody testing in patient and product where available.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Passive transfer of antibodies:
  - May interfere with the efficacy of live viral vaccines (e.g., measles, mumps, rubella, varicella); antibody administration can reduce vaccine response and timing considerations may be required.
  - May produce misleading positive results in serological testing (including viral serologies and tests for red cell antibodies).
- Loop diuretics:
  - Effect referenced in contents but specific interaction details and management recommendations: Not specified in available sources.
- Other pharmacokinetic or pharmacodynamic interactions:
  - Specific interactions beyond those above are not detailed in the available sources.
- Management notes:
  - When patients receive passive antibodies, consider timing of live vaccines and interpret serologic tests with caution.
  - If laboratory testing is required that could be affected by passive antibodies, consider the recent administration of human immunoglobulin g in test interpretation.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Key clinical study summarized in the available source:
  - Design: Single clinical study in patients with Primary Immunodeficiency (PI).
  - Population: 47 adult patients with PI.
  - Dosing: 266–826 mg/kg every 3 or 4 weeks.
  - Exposure: 643 infusions (136 in every‑3‑week schedule; 507 in every‑4‑week schedule).
  - Premedication: 4 of 47 patients (9%) received premedication during the study.
  - Efficacy outcomes and primary endpoints: Not specified in available sources.
