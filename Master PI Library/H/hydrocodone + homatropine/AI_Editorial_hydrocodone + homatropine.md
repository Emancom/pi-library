# Editorial Product Information: hydrocodone + homatropine

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **hydrocodone + homatropine**
- Chemical class:
  - Hydrocodone: opioid (μ-opioid receptor agonist)
  - Homatropine (methylbromide salt): antimuscarinic (anticholinergic) agent
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 1943 [US]
- Date of last revision/update of available source PI: Revised 4/2026 [US]
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources
- Other jurisdictional registration dates or PI revision dates: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Symptomatic relief of cough in adult patients 18 years of age and older. [US]
- Limitations:
  - Not indicated for pediatric patients under 18 years of age. [US]
  - Contraindicated in pediatric patients younger than 6 years of age. [US]

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Adults (≥18 years): one tablet or 5 mL of oral solution every 4–6 hours as needed; do not exceed six tablets or 30 mL in 24 hours. [US]

### Administration instructions
- Administer by the oral route only.
- Use an accurate milliliter measuring device for oral solution dosing; household teaspoons are not acceptable and can lead to dosing errors.
- Pharmacists should provide an appropriate measuring device and instruction when a device is not supplied.
- Rinse the measuring device with water after use.
- Do not increase dose or dosing frequency above recommended limits.

### Monitoring, titration and discontinuation
- Prescribe for the shortest duration compatible with treatment goals; reevaluate prior to refilling.
- Reassess patients whose cough does not respond within 5 days (or sooner) to investigate alternative or underlying pathology.
- Do not rapidly reduce or abruptly stop therapy in a physically dependent patient.
- Recommended taper for patients physically dependent: reduce total daily dose by 25–50% every 2–4 days while monitoring for withdrawal; if withdrawal occurs, increase to prior dose and taper more slowly (by decreasing the amount or increasing the interval between decreases).

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
List includes all contraindications reported in available source material:
- All pediatric patients younger than 6 years of age. [US]
- Significant respiratory depression. [US]
- Acute or severe bronchial asthma in an unmonitored setting or in the absence of resuscitative equipment. [US]
- Known or suspected gastrointestinal obstruction, including paralytic ileus. [US]
- Hypersensitivity to hydrocodone, homatropine, or any inactive ingredient in the product. [US]

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed/major warnings
- **[US] WARNING:** Addiction, abuse, and misuse; life‑threatening respiratory depression; accidental ingestion (especially by children); medication errors leading to overdose; interactions with cytochrome P450 3A4 modulators; concomitant use with benzodiazepines or other CNS depressants; interaction with alcohol; neonatal opioid withdrawal syndrome. Reserve use for adults when alternatives are ineffective, not tolerated, or inadequate.

### General warnings and precautions
- Addiction, abuse and misuse: assess individual risk before prescribing; monitor regularly for signs of addiction, abuse, or misuse; prescribe smallest effective quantity and limit duration to that required for treatment goals.
- Respiratory depression: may be serious, life‑threatening, or fatal; greatest risk during initiation and in patients with compromised respiratory function, elderly, cachectic, debilitated patients, or when combined with other CNS depressants.
- Accidental ingestion: even a single dose can be fatal in a child.
- Medication errors: ensure accurate prescribing, dispensing, and administration; dosing errors can cause overdose and death.
- Cytochrome P450 3A4 interactions: concomitant use with CYP3A4 inhibitors may increase hydrocodone plasma levels and risk of fatal respiratory depression; discontinuation of CYP3A4 inducers may also increase hydrocodone exposure. Avoid use with CYP3A4 inhibitors or inducers when possible.
- Concomitant CNS depressants: concomitant use with benzodiazepines or other CNS depressants (including alcohol) may cause profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death; avoid concurrent use.
- Alcohol: patients must be instructed not to drink alcoholic beverages or use products containing alcohol during therapy; co‑ingestion can raise hydrocodone plasma concentrations to potentially fatal levels.
- Gastrointestinal risks: anticholinergic component and opioid effects may precipitate paralytic ileus; avoid concomitant use with other anticholinergic agents that could increase this risk.
- Use in head injury/increased intracranial pressure: may increase intracranial pressure, obscure neurologic assessment, or exacerbate sedation—avoid use in these settings.
- Seizure risk: use with caution in patients with seizure disorders; monitor for seizure exacerbation.
- Severe hypotension: may occur; monitor during initiation and avoid in circulatory shock.
- Neonatal opioid withdrawal syndrome: prolonged opioid use during pregnancy can result in neonatal withdrawal; avoid use in pregnant women when possible and ensure neonatal expertise is available at delivery for mothers who have used opioids chronically.
- Adrenal insufficiency: reported with opioid use; if diagnosed, treat with physiologic corticosteroid replacement and taper opioids.
- Activities requiring mental alertness: may impair ability to drive or operate machinery; advise patients accordingly.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor closely for respiratory depression during initiation (especially first 24–72 hours) and dose increases.
- Reevaluate need for therapy prior to refill and assess for effectiveness and adverse reactions, including signs of misuse or addiction.
- Monitor patients with seizure disorders, head injuries, severe hepatic or renal impairment, elderly or debilitated patients, and those on interacting medications.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- CYP3A4 inhibitors: concomitant use may increase hydrocodone plasma concentrations, increasing or prolonging adverse effects including potentially fatal respiratory depression; avoid concomitant use. [US]
- CYP3A4 inducers: discontinuation of a concomitant CYP3A4 inducer may increase hydrocodone concentrations and risk; avoid use when possible. [US]
- CYP2D6: referenced in PI contents (section headings) but specific interaction details and management are Not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Benzodiazepines and other CNS depressants: coadministration may cause profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death; avoid concomitant use. [US]
- Alcohol: co‑ingestion can increase hydrocodone plasma levels and be fatal; patients must not consume alcohol or products containing alcohol while taking the drug. [US]
- Serotonergic drugs: concomitant use may lead to serotonin syndrome; discontinue serotonergic drugs if serotonin syndrome is suspected.
- Monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs): MAOI use can potentiate hydrocodone effects; avoid coadministration and do not initiate within 14 days of MAOI discontinuation.
- Muscle relaxants: avoid concomitant use (co‑sedation and respiratory depression risk).
- Diuretics: hydrocodone may reduce diuretic efficacy; monitor for decreased effect.
- Anticholinergic drugs: concurrent use may increase risk of paralytic ileus and other anticholinergic adverse effects.
  
### Clinically significant interactions and management
- Avoid combination with CYP3A4 inhibitors or inducers where possible; if unavoidable, monitor closely for signs of respiratory depression and consider dose adjustment or alternative therapy. [US]
- Avoid concurrent use with benzodiazepines or other CNS depressants; if coadministration is necessary, use lowest effective doses, limit duration, and monitor for respiratory depression and sedation. [US]
- Instruct patients to avoid alcohol and products containing alcohol to prevent fatal increases in hydrocodone exposure. [US]
- Discontinue serotonergic drugs if serotonin syndrome develops; monitor for symptoms (e.g., agitation, hallucinations, tachycardia, labile blood pressure, hyperthermia, hyperreflexia, incoordination).
- Avoid MAOIs and do not start hydrocodone within 14 days of stopping MAOI therapy.

- Jurisdiction-specific interaction guidance: The strong CYP3A4 interaction warning and avoidance recommendation is stated in the available US source. Other jurisdictions: Not specified in available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Hydrocodone: acts as an opioid agonist at μ-opioid receptors producing analgesia and centrally mediated cough suppression; can cause dose‑related respiratory depression by depressing the brainstem respiratory center.
- Homatropine (methylbromide): antimuscarinic agent that contributes anticholinergic effects (e.g., reduction of secretions, dry mouth) and is included to discourage deliberate overuse.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Specific ADME parameters (absorption, bioavailability, distribution volumes, protein binding, metabolic pathways, half‑life, routes of elimination) are Not specified in available sources.
- The product warning highlights clinically relevant metabolism via cytochrome P450 3A4 (interactions with inhibitors and inducers that alter hydrocodone plasma concentrations). Details on CYP2D6 referenced in PI headings but specifics not provided in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Hydrocodone: depresses respiratory drive in a dose‑dependent manner; causes sedation and impairment of mental and physical performance.
- Homatropine: produces anticholinergic effects (e.g., dry mouth, reduced gastrointestinal motility), which can exacerbate constipation and, when combined with other agents, risk paralytic ileus.
