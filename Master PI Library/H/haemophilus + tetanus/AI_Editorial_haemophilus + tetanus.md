# Editorial Product Information: haemophilus + tetanus

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **haemophilus + tetanus**
- Chemical class: Haemophilus influenzae type b (PRP) capsular polysaccharide covalently conjugated to inactivated tetanus toxoid (protein conjugate vaccine)
- Potency per recommended dose: each 0.5 mL dose formulated to contain 10 mcg purified PRP polysaccharide conjugated to 24 mcg inactivated tetanus toxoid (contains sucrose as stabilizer and other residual manufacturing components)  
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intramuscular injection (anterolateral thigh in infants; deltoid in older infants/children)

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 1993 [US]
- Date of last revision/update reported in the available source: May 2026 [US]
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources
- Registration/approval dates for jurisdictions other than the United States: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Prevention of invasive disease caused by Haemophilus influenzae type b in infants and children 2 months through 5 years of age. [US-approved]
- Indications in jurisdictions other than the United States: Not specified in available sources

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing
- Recommended primary series: three 0.5 mL intramuscular doses administered at 2, 4, and 6 months of age.
- Recommended booster: a single 0.5 mL intramuscular dose at 15 through 18 months of age.

### Dosing by indication
- Indication is prevention of invasive H. influenzae type b disease in infants/children aged 2 months through 5 years; dosing schedule above applies to the recommended primary/booster regimen for this indication.

### Administration instructions
- Administer intramuscularly as a single 0.5 mL dose into the anterolateral thigh (infants) or deltoid (older infants/children).
- Inspect vaccine visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration when container and solution permit; do not administer if particulate matter or discoloration is present.
- Do not administer intravenously, intradermally, or subcutaneously.
- Do not mix this vaccine in the same syringe with other parenteral products.

### Reconstitution (vaccine supplied as lyophilized powder with diluent)
- Reconstitute lyophilized vaccine using the supplied saline diluent: withdraw the indicated volume of diluent, inject into the vial of lyophilized vaccine, agitate to achieve complete reconstitution, and withdraw a 0.5 mL dose for intramuscular injection.
- After reconstitution, discard any unused portion.

### Concomitant administration
- May be administered concomitantly (at separate injection sites) with routine pediatric vaccines given according to immunization schedules; no impairment of antibody responses was demonstrated when given at the same visit and at separate anatomic sites with DTaP, MMR, hepatitis B, and inactivated poliovirus vaccines in clinical trials.

### Titration / dose adjustment
- Not applicable.

### Missed dose instructions
- Specific guidance for missed doses is not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Severe allergic reaction (e.g., anaphylaxis) after a previous dose of any Haemophilus influenzae type b or tetanus toxoid–containing vaccine, or after a prior exposure to any component of the vaccine (including known allergy to components such as casein derivatives) — vaccination is contraindicated in such patients.
- If there are other jurisdiction-specific contraindications, they are not specified in available sources.
- Note: vial stoppers are not made with natural rubber latex.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- **Management of acute allergic reactions:** Appropriate medical treatment and supervision must be readily available for immediate management of anaphylactic reactions; epinephrine and other emergency agents should be available.
- **Guillain‑Barré syndrome (GBS):** If GBS occurred within 6 weeks of receipt of a prior tetanus toxoid–containing vaccine, carefully weigh potential benefits and risks before administering this vaccine (tetanus toxoid is the conjugate protein).
- **Altered immunocompetence:** Immunosuppressed persons, including those receiving immunosuppressive therapy (e.g., irradiation, antimetabolites, alkylating agents, cytotoxic drugs, corticosteroids at greater than physiologic doses), may have reduced immune responses; effectiveness may be diminished.
- **Limitations of vaccine effectiveness:** Vaccination may not protect 100% of individuals.
- **Tetanus immunization:** Immunization with this conjugate vaccine does not replace routine tetanus immunization schedules; separate tetanus vaccination recommendations should be followed as indicated.
- **Interference with diagnostic tests:** Haemophilus b capsular polysaccharide antigen can be detected in the urine of some vaccine recipients; urine antigen detection tests for H. influenzae type b may yield positive results and may lack diagnostic value for 1–2 weeks after vaccination.
- **Syncope:** Fainting has been reported following vaccination; observe patients for syncope and implement procedures to avoid injury from fainting.
- **Component sensitivity:** The manufacturing process uses milk‑derived raw materials (casein derivatives); consider potential hypersensitivity in persons with severe milk-protein allergies. Residual formaldehyde is reduced to low levels during manufacture.
- **Monitoring requirements:** Monitor for signs of anaphylaxis immediately after administration and observe for syncope. No specific laboratory monitoring requirements are specified in available sources.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Concomitant vaccination: When administered at separate injection sites, concomitant administration with diphtheria–tetanus–acellular pertussis (DTaP), measles–mumps–rubella (MMR), hepatitis B, inactivated poliovirus vaccine (IPV), pneumococcal conjugate vaccine, and other routine infant vaccines did not demonstrate impairment of antibody responses to the individual antigens in clinical trials.
- Immunosuppressive therapy: Immunosuppressive treatments (e.g., irradiation, antimetabolites, alkylating agents, cytotoxic drugs, corticosteroids at greater than physiologic doses) may reduce the immune response to the vaccine; consider timing vaccination with respect to immunosuppression and consult immunization guidelines for immunocompromised hosts.
- Interference with diagnostic tests: Vaccine-derived Haemophilus b capsular polysaccharide may be detected in urine antigen assays for 1–2 weeks after vaccination; urine antigen testing may therefore be unreliable for diagnosis of H. influenzae type b infection during this period.
- Other pharmacokinetic or pharmacodynamic interactions: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Approved for infants and children aged 2 months through 5 years. [US-approved]
- Safety and effectiveness have not been established in infants younger than 6 weeks of age or in children and adolescents 6 years of age and older.
- Immunogenicity in special pediatric populations:
  - Children 12–15 months: single-dose immunization produced geometric mean anti-PRP concentrations around 5.12 mcg/mL with approximately 90% achieving ≥1.0 mcg/mL.
  - Children 17–24 months: single-dose immunization produced geometric mean anti-PRP concentrations around 4.40 mcg/mL with approximately 82% achieving ≥1.0 mcg/mL.
  - Alaskan Native American population: lower serologic responses observed in one study (≈75% achieving ≥1.0 mcg/mL after a three‑dose schedule beginning at 6 weeks in that population).
  - Sickle cell disease: immunogenic responses comparable to children without sickle cell disease; after two doses given two months apart, 89% achieved ≥1.0 mcg/mL in one study.

- Not applicable / not approved for individuals 6 years of age and older.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- The vaccine induces a T‑cell dependent immune response to the Haemophilus influenzae type b capsular polysaccharide (polyribosyl-ribitol-phosphate, PRP) through conjugation to tetanus toxoid, resulting predominantly in IgG anti-PRP antibodies and immunologic memory. Bactericidal activity against H. influenzae type b correlates with the anti-PRP antibody response.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Anti-PRP IgG is the principal protective isotype induced. A post‑vaccination anti-PRP antibody concentration of >1.0 mcg/mL has been associated with long-term protection after unconjugated PRP vaccine in older children; this threshold continues to be used as an indicator of long-term protection though the precise relevance after conjugate vaccination may differ because of immunologic memory.
- In clinical studies, a high proportion of infants reached anti-PRP concentrations ≥1.0 mcg/mL after the primary series and nearly all after booster dosing in the age groups studied.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Standard pharmacokinetic parameters (absorption, distribution, metabolism, elimination) are not applicable or not specified for this vaccine in the available sources.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Clinical development included multiple trials assessing immunogenicity and safety in infants and young children; more than 7,000 infants and young children (≤2 years of age) received at least one dose in U.S. clinical trials.
- Key trial findings:
  - Immunogenicity after primary series: In pooled and individual studies, the three‑dose primary series (2, 4, 6 months) produced geometric mean anti-PRP concentrations and seroprotection rates (percent ≥1.0 mcg/mL) that varied by study; examples include ≥90% achieving ≥1.0 mcg/mL after the primary series in some studies.
  - Booster response: A booster dose at 15–18 months produced marked increases in anti-PRP antibody concentrations; >98% of infants achieved anti-PRP ≥1.0 mcg/mL after booster in clinical studies summarized in the available sources.
  - Study P3T06: A U.S. trial in which 1,454 children received the vaccine at 2, 4, and 6 months concomitantly with other routine infant vaccines reported solicited systemic reactions (e.g., fussiness, decreased activity, inconsolable crying) commonly and a low incidence of serious adverse events; one seizure with apnea occurring on the day of vaccination was judged possibly related by investigators.
  - Single-dose immunogenicity in previously unvaccinated 12–24 month olds: Trials reported geometric mean concentrations of anti-PRP of approximately 5.12 mcg/mL (12–15 months) and 4.40 mcg/mL (17–24 months) with 90% and 82% achieving ≥1.0 mcg/mL respectively.
  - Comparative trials: Studies compared anti-PRP responses to other Hib conjugate vaccines in infants and toddlers; results varied by vaccine and population, with some populations (e.g., certain Native American groups) showing lower serologic responses.

- Trial designs: randomized comparative immunogenicity trials, single‑dose immunogenicity studies in toddlers, and large safety/immunogenicity studies in infants with concomitant administration of routine vaccines. Primary outcomes included anti-PRP antibody concentrations and rates achieving ≥1.0 mcg/mL; safety outcomes included solicited local and systemic reactions, unsolicited adverse events, and serious adverse events.
