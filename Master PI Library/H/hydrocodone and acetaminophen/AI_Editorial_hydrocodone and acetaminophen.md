# Editorial Product Information: Hydrocodone Bitartrate and Acetaminophen

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA). 
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of hydrocodone bitartrate and acetaminophen, synthesised for healthcare professionals. 
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labeling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredients:** Hydrocodone bitartrate (opioid agonist) and Acetaminophen (non-opiate, non-salicylate analgesic and antipyretic).
*   **Brand Names:** Various generic and proprietary formulations available by manufacturer [US].
*   **ATC Code:** N02BE51 (Paracetamol, combinations excl. psycholeptics) - *Note: Not explicitly stated in source, provided for clinical context.*
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths [US]:**
    *   5 mg hydrocodone bitartrate / 325 mg acetaminophen
    *   7.5 mg hydrocodone bitartrate / 325 mg acetaminophen
    *   10 mg hydrocodone bitartrate / 325 mg acetaminophen
*   **Route of Administration:** Oral.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** St. Mary's Medical Park Pharmacy / Cranbury Pharmaceuticals, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Severe Pain Management:** Indicated for the management of pain severe enough to require an opioid analgesic and for which alternative treatments (e.g., non-opioid analgesics) are inadequate or not tolerated [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
*   **Initiation:** Use the lowest effective dosage for the shortest duration consistent with individual patient goals.
*   **5 mg/325 mg Strength:** 1 to 2 tablets every 4 to 6 hours as needed for pain. Maximum: 8 tablets per day [US].
*   **7.5 mg/325 mg and 10 mg/325 mg Strengths:** 1 tablet every 4 to 6 hours as needed for pain. Maximum: 6 tablets per day [US].

### Administration and Titration
*   **Titration:** Dosage should be individualised based on pain severity, patient response, and prior analgesic experience.
*   **Maintenance:** Continually reevaluate the need for continued opioid therapy and the balance between analgesia and adverse effects.
*   **Discontinuation:** In physically dependent patients, do not abruptly stop therapy. Gradually taper the dose (e.g., 10% to 25% reduction every 2 to 4 weeks) to avoid withdrawal symptoms [US].

### Missed Dose
*   If a dose is missed, patients should take the next dose at the usual time. Do not double doses [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   Significant respiratory depression [US].
*   Acute or severe bronchial asthma in an unmonitored setting or without resuscitative equipment [US].
*   Known or suspected gastrointestinal obstruction, including paralytic ileus [US].
*   Hypersensitivity (e.g., anaphylaxis) to hydrocodone or acetaminophen [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
*   **Addiction, Abuse, and Misuse:** Risk of opioid addiction which can lead to overdose and death.
*   **Life-Threatening Respiratory Depression:** Serious or fatal depression may occur, especially at initiation or following dose increases.
*   **Accidental Ingestion:** Even one dose, especially by children, can be fatal.
*   **Risks from Concomitant Use with Benzodiazepines or Other CNS Depressants:** May result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death.
*   **Neonatal Opioid Withdrawal Syndrome (NOWS):** Prolonged use during pregnancy can lead to life-threatening withdrawal in the neonate.
*   **Cytochrome P450 3A4 Interaction:** Concomitant use with CYP3A4 inhibitors may increase hydrocodone concentrations; discontinuation of CYP3A4 inducers may have a similar effect.
*   **Hepatotoxicity:** Acetaminophen is associated with acute liver failure, often exceeding 4,000 mg/day or involving multiple products.
*   **Opioid Analgesic REMS:** Healthcare providers are encouraged to complete REMS-compliant education programs.

### General Precautions
*   **Opioid-Induced Hyperalgesia (OIH):** Paradoxical increase in pain despite stable or increased opioid dosing [US].
*   **Adrenal Insufficiency:** Reported with opioid use, typically after one month of therapy [US].
*   **Severe Hypotension:** Risk of orthostatic hypotension and syncope, especially in patients with compromised blood volume or concurrent CNS depressant use [US].
*   **Serious Skin Reactions:** Acetaminophen may cause rare but fatal reactions like Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS) and Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN) [US].
*   **Seizure Disorders:** Hydrocodone may increase seizure frequency in patients with pre-existing conditions [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*   **Most Common:** Lightheadedness, dizziness, sedation, nausea, and vomiting [US].
*   **Central Nervous System:** Drowsiness, mental clouding, lethargy, anxiety, psychological dependence, mood changes.
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Constipation, biliary tract spasm (Sphincter of Oddi).
*   **Genitourinary:** Ureteral spasm, urinary retention.
*   **Special Senses:** Hearing impairment or permanent loss (predominantly with chronic overdose).
*   **Hematological:** Thrombocytopenia, agranulocytosis.
*   **Metabolic:** Hypoglycemia [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **CYP3A4 Inhibitors (e.g., Erythromycin, Ketoconazole):** Increase hydrocodone levels; monitor for respiratory depression [US].
*   **CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., Rifampin, Carbamazepine):** Decrease hydrocodone levels; may cause withdrawal or reduced efficacy [US].
*   **Benzodiazepines/CNS Depressants:** Additive risk of respiratory depression and death [US].
*   **Serotonergic Drugs (e.g., SSRIs, SNRIs, Triptans):** Risk of Serotonin Syndrome [US].
*   **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Avoid use within 14 days of MAOI therapy due to risk of serotonin syndrome or opioid toxicity [US].
*   **Mixed Agonist/Antagonist Opioids (e.g., Buprenorphine, Pentazocine):** May reduce analgesic effect or precipitate withdrawal [US].
*   **Anticholinergics:** Increased risk of urinary retention and severe constipation/paralytic ileus [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** Prolonged use may cause Neonatal Opioid Withdrawal Syndrome (NOWS). There are no adequate, well-controlled studies in humans. Use only if benefits justify the risk to the fetus [US].
*   **Labor/Delivery:** Opioids cross the placenta; monitor neonates for respiratory depression. May prolong labor by reducing uterine contractions [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Hydrocodone and acetaminophen are present in human milk. Monitor breastfed infants for excess sedation and respiratory depression [US].
*   **Fertility:** Chronic opioid use may lead to reduced fertility in males and females; reversibility is unknown [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US].
*   **Geriatric Use (≥65 years):** Increased sensitivity to adverse effects; start at the low end of the dosing range and monitor renal function [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Higher plasma concentrations may occur; use a low initial dose and monitor closely [US].
*   **Renal Impairment:** Increased risk of adverse reactions; use a low initial dose and monitor renal function [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Hydrocodone is a full mu-opioid receptor agonist. Acetaminophen’s mechanism is not fully established but involves central analgesic and antipyretic actions [US].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   *Hydrocodone:* Mean peak concentration (10 mg dose) is 23.6 ± 5.2 ng/mL at 1.3 hours. Half-life is 3.8 hours. Metabolised via CYP3A4 (N-demethylation) and CYP2D6 (O-demethylation to hydromorphone). Primarily eliminated renally [US].
    *   *Acetaminophen:* Rapidly absorbed. Half-life is 1.25 to 3 hours. Primarily metabolised in the liver via conjugation and the P450 (CYP2E1) pathway to a reactive intermediate. 85% excreted in urine within 24 hours [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F). Excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
*   **Packaging:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container with a child-resistant closure [US].
*   **Security:** Store securely in a location not accessible by others to prevent accidental ingestion or theft [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **United States:** Classified as a Schedule II (CII) controlled substance. Rx-only.
