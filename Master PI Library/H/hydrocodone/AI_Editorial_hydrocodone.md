# Editorial Product Information: Hydrocodone Bitartrate and Acetaminophen

## Editorial Note
- This editorial was developed using official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single coherent reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for educational and reference purposes for healthcare professionals. it is not a substitute for the official PI or local clinical guidelines. Prescribers must consult the current, approved PI within their specific jurisdiction before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Hydrocodone Bitartrate (opioid analgesic) and Acetaminophen (non-opiate, non-salicylate analgesic and antipyretic).
- **Brand Names:** Available under various generic and brand-name formulations; the source document specifies Hydrocodone Bitartrate and Acetaminophen Tablets, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** N02AJ13 (Hydrocodone and non-opioid analgesics).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - 5 mg Hydrocodone Bitartrate / 325 mg Acetaminophen [US].
    - 7.5 mg Hydrocodone Bitartrate / 325 mg Acetaminophen [US].
    - 10 mg Hydrocodone Bitartrate / 325 mg Acetaminophen [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Cranbury Pharmaceuticals, LLC; Repacked by St. Mary's Medical Park Pharmacy [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Pain Management:** Indicated for the management of pain severe enough to require an opioid analgesic and for which alternative treatments (e.g., non-opioid analgesics) are inadequate or not tolerated [US].
- **Limitations of Use:** Should not be used for an extended period unless the pain remains severe and alternative options continue to be inadequate [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Dosing must be individualised, using the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration necessary. 
- **5 mg/325 mg strength:** 1 to 2 tablets every 4 to 6 hours as needed for pain. Maximum: 8 tablets per day [US].
- **7.5 mg/325 mg and 10 mg/325 mg strengths:** 1 tablet every 4 to 6 hours as needed for pain. Maximum: 6 tablets per day [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets should be taken at the lowest effective dose.
- Total daily acetaminophen intake from all sources must not exceed 4,000 mg [US].

### Titration and Maintenance
- Titrate based on individual response. Reserve higher doses for patients in whom lower doses are insufficient and benefits outweigh the risks of opioid-related adverse effects [US].
- Continually re-evaluate pain control and signs of addiction or misuse.

### Discontinuation and Tapering
- **Do not abruptly discontinue** in physically dependent patients. 
- Use a gradual, patient-specific taper (e.g., 10% to 25% dose reduction every 2 to 4 weeks) to avoid withdrawal symptoms [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Significant respiratory depression [US].
- Acute or severe bronchial asthma in an unmonitored setting or without resuscitative equipment [US].
- Known or suspected gastrointestinal obstruction, including paralytic ileus [US].
- Hypersensitivity (e.g., anaphylaxis) to hydrocodone bitartrate or acetaminophen [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
- **Addiction, Abuse, and Misuse:** Potential for opioid addiction, which can lead to overdose and death.
- **Life-Threatening Respiratory Depression:** Serious, fatal respiratory depression may occur, especially at initiation or dose increases.
- **Accidental Ingestion:** Even one dose, especially by children, can be fatal.
- **Risks from Concomitant Use with Benzodiazepines or Other CNS Depressants:** May result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death.
- **Neonatal Opioid Withdrawal Syndrome (NOWS):** Prolonged use during pregnancy can result in life-threatening withdrawal in the neonate.
- **Cytochrome P450 3A4 Interaction:** Concomitant use with CYP3A4 inhibitors (or discontinuation of inducers) can increase hydrocodone concentrations and lead to fatal respiratory depression.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Acetaminophen is associated with acute liver failure, potentially leading to transplant or death.

### General Precautions
- **Opioid-Induced Hyperalgesia (OIH):** Paradoxical increase in pain or sensitivity despite dose increases [US].
- **Adrenal Insufficiency:** Reported with opioid use, typically after >1 month. 
- **Severe Hypotension:** Risk of orthostatic hypotension and syncope, especially in patients with reduced blood volume or on concurrent CNS depressants.
- **Increased Intracranial Pressure:** Use with caution in patients with brain tumours or head injury.
- **Biliary Tract Disease:** May cause spasm of the sphincter of Oddi and increase serum amylase.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are reported voluntarily; frequencies are not always definitively established.
- **Very Common (>10%):** Light-headedness, dizziness, sedation, nausea, and vomiting [US].
- **Other Reported Effects:**
    - **CNS:** Drowsiness, mental clouding, lethargy, anxiety, fear, dysphoria, psychological dependence, mood changes.
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Constipation.
    - **Genitourinary:** Ureteral spasm, urinary retention.
    - **Special Senses:** Hearing impairment or permanent loss (predominantly in chronic overdose).
    - **Dermatological:** Skin rash, pruritus.
    - **Serious Reactions:** Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), and acute generalized exanthematous pustulosis (AGEP) due to acetaminophen [US].
    - **Hematological:** Thrombocytopenia, agranulocytosis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors/Inducers:** Inhibitors (e.g., erythromycin, ketoconazole, ritonavir) increase hydrocodone levels; Inducers (e.g., rifampin, carbamazepine) decrease levels [US].
- **CNS Depressants:** Benzodiazepines, alcohol, gabapentinoids, and other sedatives increase the risk of fatal respiratory depression.
- **Serotonergic Drugs:** Risk of Serotonin Syndrome when used with SSRIs, SNRIs, TCAs, or MAOIs.
- **MAOIs:** Not recommended within 14 days of stopping MAOI therapy due to risk of serotonin syndrome or opioid toxicity [US].
- **Mixed Agonist/Antagonist Analgesics:** (e.g., buprenorphine, pentazocine) may reduce analgesic effect and precipitate withdrawal [US].
- **Anticholinergics:** Increased risk of urinary retention and severe constipation.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Not recommended for use during or immediately prior to labour. Prolonged use results in **Neonatal Opioid Withdrawal Syndrome (NOWS)**. US Pregnancy Category is not specified as a single letter in the current labeling, but risk of fetal harm is noted [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Hydrocodone and acetaminophen are present in human milk. Monitor infants for excess sedation and respiratory depression. Withdrawal can occur in the infant if the mother stops the medication [US].
- **Fertility:** Long-term opioid use may cause reduced fertility in both males and females; reversibility is unknown [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased sensitivity; start at the low end of the dosing range. Monitor renal function as hydrocodone is substantially excreted by the kidneys [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Patients may have higher plasma concentrations. Use a low initial dose and monitor closely [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Risk of increased plasma hydrocodone and acetaminophen-induced toxicity. Use a low initial dose and monitor for signs of sedation/respiratory depression [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Hydrocodone:** Full opioid agonist with relative selectivity for the mu (μ) receptor. Analgesia is the primary therapeutic action.
    - **Acetaminophen:** Non-opiate, non-salicylate analgesic/antipyretic. Precise mechanism is unknown but involves central actions [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics (Hydrocodone):**
    - **Tmax:** 1.3 ± 0.3 hours.
    - **Half-life:** 3.8 ± 0.3 hours.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily N-demethylation (CYP3A4) to norhydrocodone and O-demethylation (CYP2D6) to hydromorphone [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics (Acetaminophen):**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed from the GI tract.
    - **Half-life:** 1.25 to 3 hours (increased by liver damage).
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily liver metabolism (conjugation with glucuronide and sulfate); a minor pathway involves CYP2E1 to form a reactive intermediate [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F). Excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Special Handling:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container with a child-resistant closure. 

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** Schedule II controlled substance [US].
