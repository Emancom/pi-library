# Editorial Product Information: human cytomegalovirus immunoglobulin

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **human cytomegalovirus immunoglobulin**
- Chemical class: Human plasma‑derived immunoglobulin (primarily IgG; contains trace IgA)  
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intravenous (IV) only

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval: 04 November 1991 (as stated in available sources)
- Date(s) of last revision/update of source PI: 22 June 2021 (as stated in available sources)
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Prevention of cytomegalovirus (CMV) infection following bone marrow transplantation and renal transplantation, specifically when a seronegative recipient receives a graft from a CMV‑positive donor.
- Adjunctive therapy in patients with established CMV infection (example cited: CMV pneumonitis).  
(If any jurisdictional differences exist in other sources, they are not specified in the available source.)

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General dosing principles
- Dosage is empirical; product activity is expressed in CMV EIA units. Each vial contains a stated CMV immunoglobulin activity (units per vial) and at least 20,000 units/mL of CMV immunoglobulin activity (actual vial volume is on the label).
- Dosing for patients ≤15 kg: dose by units/kg (see indications below).
- For patients >15 kg where calculated dose requires a fraction of a vial, administer the higher whole number of vials.
- Consider the volume and acid load when administering to neonates.
- Product must be administered intravenously only.

### Dosing by indication
- Prophylaxis (transplant setting): 25,000 units/kg given on days −4 and −2, on the day of transplantation (intra‑operatively), and then weekly for a further two months.
- Therapy (established CMV infection): initial dose 50,000 units/kg, repeat after 4–5 days, then every 10–14 days until clinical improvement.

### Practical vial/weight dosing (as provided)
- Body weight 0–≤15 kg: dose based on units/kg (use individual weight‑based calculation)
- >15–30 kg: 1 vial (prophylaxis); 1 vial (therapy)
- 31–60 kg: 1 vial (prophylaxis); 2 vials (therapy)
- 61–90 kg: 2 vials (prophylaxis); 3 vials (therapy)
- 91–120 kg: 2 vials (prophylaxis); 4 vials (therapy)

### Administration instructions
- Allow vial to reach room temperature prior to infusion.
- Remove dust cover; disinfect exposed rubber stopper (e.g., povidone‑iodine or 70% ethanol) and allow to dry.
- Product contains no antimicrobial preservative; use immediately after opening and discard any unused portion. Use in one patient on one occasion only.
- Do not freeze. Do not use if turbid or if sediment is present.
- Infuse intravenously only. Commence infusion at a low rate and increase gradually as tolerated:
  - Usual commencement: 1 mL per minute.
  - After ~15 minutes, the rate may be gradually increased to a maximum of 3–4 mL per minute over the next ~15 minutes, provided vital signs remain satisfactory.
  - Infusion rate and increments should be followed carefully, particularly until the treating physician is familiar with the patient’s tolerance.
- CMV immunoglobulin may be administered undiluted or diluted with up to four times its volume of 0.9% saline or 5% glucose if necessary.
- Administer separately from other intravenous medications/fluids (do not co‑infuse). No other compatibilities have been evaluated.
- If infusion‑related reactions occur, temporarily stop infusion until improvement (5–10 minutes) then consider resuming at a slower rate.
- In case of suspected anaphylaxis, stop infusion immediately and institute appropriate emergency measures.

### Titration schedules
- Not specified beyond the infusion rate escalation and repeat dosing schedules above.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known history of a true anaphylactic reaction to **human cytomegalovirus immunoglobulin** or to any component of the product.
- Patients with IgA deficiency, unless tested and shown not to have circulating anti‑IgA antibodies, because trace IgA in the product may induce severe reactions.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- **Route restriction:** Intravenous administration only. Other routes have not been evaluated.
- **Hypersensitivity / anaphylaxis:** Product contains trace IgA and can cause anaphylactic reactions, including precipitous fall in blood pressure. Such reactions may occur even in patients who previously tolerated immunoglobulin. Infusion rate may influence risk—follow recommended infusion increments. Monitor vital signs during infusion and for at least 20 minutes after administration. Emergency resuscitation medicines and equipment (e.g., epinephrine/adrenaline, oxygen, antihistamines, corticosteroids) should be immediately available.
- **Acute renal dysfunction / acute renal failure:** Reports have occurred with IVIg products. Patients at increased risk include those with pre‑existing renal insufficiency, diabetes mellitus, age >65 years, volume depletion, sepsis, monoclonal gammopathy, and those receiving concomitant nephrotoxic medicines. Many reports have been associated with sucrose‑stabilised products; this product contains maltose rather than sucrose. Precautions:
  - Ensure adequate hydration prior to infusion.
  - Do not exceed recommended dose.
  - Monitor renal function in patients at increased risk; discontinue IVIg if renal function deteriorates.
  - In patients at increased risk of renal failure, administer at the minimum infusion rate and dose possible based on clinical judgement.
- **Hemolytic anaemia:** IVIg preparations can contain blood‑group antibodies and may induce positive direct antiglobulin test and, rarely, hemolysis. Risk factors: high IVIg doses, non‑O blood groups, and underlying inflammatory state. Monitor for clinical and laboratory evidence of hemolysis; discontinue therapy if hemolysis develops.
- **Aseptic meningitis syndrome (AMS):** Reported infrequently after IVIg, typically within hours to two days. Symptoms include severe headache, nuchal rigidity, drowsiness, fever, photophobia, painful eye movements, nausea, vomiting. CSF frequently shows pleocytosis (predominantly granulocytic) and elevated protein. Perform neurological assessment and CSF studies to exclude other causes. AMS has often resolved on discontinuation without sequelae.
- **Thromboembolic events:** Clinical association reported with IVIg use (assumed related to increased blood viscosity). Caution in patients with risk factors for thrombosis: advanced age, estrogen use, indwelling vascular catheters, history of thrombosis, hypercoagulable states, cardiovascular disease (including atherosclerosis or impaired cardiac output), prolonged immobilisation, severe hypovolaemia, hyperviscosity states (cryoglobulinaemia, chylomicronemia, hypertriglyceridaemia), monoclonal gammopathies. Consider baseline blood viscosity measurement in high‑risk individuals. Use minimum infusion rate and dose consistent with clinical needs; monitor for thromboembolic complications.
- **Pathogen safety / transmission risk:** This product is plasma‑derived. Manufacturing includes donor screening, viral marker testing, low pH incubation (viral inactivation) and nanofiltration (virus removal). These processes reduce risk for enveloped and certain non‑enveloped viruses (HIV, HBV, HCV, HAV, parvovirus B19) but do not eliminate theoretical risk of transmission of known or unknown pathogens (including CJD agents). Vaccination should be considered where appropriate for recipients of plasma‑derived products. Record product name and batch number each time the product is administered to maintain traceability.
- **Acid load:** Product pH is acidic (pH specified in source). In patients with limited acid–base compensatory reserve (e.g., neonates), consider effect of acid load.
- **Effects on laboratory tests:** Passive transfer of antibodies may cause misleading positive serology and interfere with serological tests for red cell alloantibodies (e.g., direct antiglobulin/Coombs’ test). Maltose in the product may interfere with some blood glucose assays (notably certain glucose dehydrogenase‑based tests) and produce falsely elevated glucose readings—this may lead to inappropriate insulin administration and hypoglycaemia if unrecognized. Review compatibility of glucose meters/test strips with maltose prior to use.
- **Use in pregnancy:** Safety in pregnancy not established in controlled trials. Use only if clearly needed.
- **Use in lactation:** Immunoglobulins are excreted into breast milk and may transfer protective antibodies; no harmful effects expected based on clinical experience.
- **Use in elderly and paediatric populations:** Use has not been established in appropriate studies; these populations are not over‑represented in spontaneous adverse event reports for similar IVIg products. Specific caution noted for neonates because of volume and acid load.
- **Vaccination interactions:** Passively acquired antibodies can interfere with live attenuated vaccines. Defer live vaccine administration approximately three months after passive immunisation; for measles the decrease in efficacy may persist up to one year—check antibody status if measles vaccination is given. Conversely, do not administer immunoglobulin for at least two weeks after a vaccine has been given.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor vital signs (including blood pressure, pulse, temperature, respiratory status) regularly during infusion and for at least 20 minutes after administration.
- Monitor renal function in patients at increased risk of renal impairment.
- Monitor for signs/symptoms of hemolysis and perform appropriate laboratory testing if suspected.
- Monitor for neurological symptoms suggestive of aseptic meningitis; perform CSF studies if indicated.
- Monitor for signs of thromboembolic events; in high‑risk patients consider baseline blood viscosity measurement.
- Record product name and batch number in patient records for traceability.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Live attenuated virus vaccines: Passively acquired antibodies can interfere with the immune response to live attenuated vaccines. Recommendation:
  - Defer administration of live vaccines (e.g., poliomyelitis, measles) for approximately three months after passive immunisation. For measles, decreased vaccine efficacy may persist up to one year; check antibody status if measles vaccine is given within this period.
  - Do not administer immunoglobulins for at least two weeks after live vaccination.
- Glucose monitoring: Maltose in the preparation may interfere with certain blood glucose assays (notably some glucose dehydrogenase‑based methods), producing falsely elevated glucose readings. Review manufacturer guidance for glucose meters/test strips to ensure no maltose interference. If a glucose result will guide treatment, use a validated method that is not affected by maltose.
- Pharmacologic/compatibility interactions: No other medicine interactions or compatibilities have been evaluated in the available source. CMV immunoglobulin should be administered separately from other intravenous fluids or medications.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- Fertility: No reproductive toxicity studies conducted; no reports of reproductive effects associated with similar plasma‑derived immunoglobulin medicines in the available source.
- Pregnancy: Safety for use in human pregnancy has not been established in controlled clinical trials. Use only if clearly needed; clinical experience with other immunoglobulin preparations has not suggested adverse fetal effects.
- Breastfeeding: Immunoglobulins are excreted in breast milk and may contribute to passive transfer of protective antibodies to the neonate. It is not known if passively administered product behaves identically, but no harmful effects on the neonate are expected based on clinical experience.

## 10. Use in Special Populations

### Paediatric use
- Use in the paediatric population has not been established in appropriate studies. To date, paediatric patients are not over‑represented in spontaneous adverse event reports for related IVIg products. For children ≤15 kg, dosing should be calculated on a units/kg basis. Consider volume and acid load in neonates.

### Elderly use
- Use in elderly populations has not been established in appropriate studies. The elderly are not over‑represented in spontaneous adverse event reports for related IVIg products. Exercise caution in elderly patients due to increased risk of renal dysfunction, volume overload, and thromboembolic events.

### Renal impairment
- Patients with pre‑existing renal insufficiency are at increased risk of renal dysfunction and acute renal failure following IVIg. Monitor renal function and consider dose/rate minimisation; discontinue if renal function deteriorates. No specific dose adjustments by eGFR or CKD stage are provided in the available source.

### Hepatic impairment
- Not specified in available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology

### Mechanism of action
- The product is derived from selected human plasma donations with high titres of CMV‑specific antibodies. The active protein is not chemically or enzymatically modified. Protective effect is mediated by passively transferred CMV‑specific immunoglobulin (primarily IgG).

### Pharmacokinetics
- Serum half‑life: approximately three weeks in general; may be considerably shortened in immunosuppressed patients.
- Absorption: Not applicable for IV administration.
- Distribution, metabolism, elimination: Not specified in available sources beyond half‑life information.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Not further specified in available sources beyond the mechanism of passive immunity via transferred CMV antibodies.
