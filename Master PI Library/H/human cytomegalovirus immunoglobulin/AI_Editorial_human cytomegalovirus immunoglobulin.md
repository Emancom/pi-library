# Editorial Product Information: human cytomegalovirus immunoglobulin

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **human cytomegalovirus immunoglobulin**
- Chemical class: Human plasma‑derived immunoglobulin preparation (predominantly IgG; IgG subclass distribution approximates that of normal human plasma)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intravenous only

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval: 04 November 1991 [as stated in source]
- Date of last revision/update of available PI: 22 June 2021 [as stated in source]
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Prevention of cytomegalovirus (CMV) infection following bone marrow transplantation when the recipient is CMV seronegative and receives a graft from a CMV‑positive donor. [Australian]
- Prevention of CMV infection following renal transplantation when the recipient is CMV seronegative and receives a graft from a CMV‑positive donor. [Australian]
- Adjunctive therapy in established CMV infection (for example, CMV pneumonitis). [Australian]

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General dosing principles
- Dosing is empirical because there is no international standard for CMV immunoglobulin activity units.
- Product concentration and vial content as stated in the available PI:
  - Solution contains human plasma protein 50–70 mg/mL (≥98% immunoglobulin, mainly IgG).
  - CMV immunoglobulin activity is stated as 1.5 million units per vial.
  - The product also states a concentration of at least 20,000 units/mL; the actual vial volume is indicated on the label. (Both statements are included in the available source.)
- The preparation contains maltose (292 mmol/L) and has a pH of approximately 4.25.
- The product is preservative‑free. Use for one patient on one occasion only. Do not freeze. Discard any unused solution.

### Prophylaxis (recommended regimen)
- 25,000 units/kg on days −4 and −2 prior to transplantation, on the day of transplantation (intra‑operatively), then weekly for a further two months.

### Therapy (treatment of established CMV disease)
- Initial dose: 50,000 units/kg, repeat after 4–5 days, then administer every 10–14 days until clinical improvement.

### Vial / weight guidance (practical dosing table from PI)
- Patients ≤15 kg: dose by body weight (units/kg). [Use units/kg rather than fixed vial counts.]
- If patient >15 kg and calculated quantity includes a fractional vial, administer the higher whole number of vials.
- Representative vial counts provided in the source (for pragmatic use):
  - >15–30 kg: 1 vial (prophylaxis or therapy dosing per table)
  - 31–60 kg: 1 vial (prophylaxis), 2 vials (therapy)
  - 61–90 kg: 2 vials (prophylaxis), 3 vials (therapy)
  - 91–120 kg: 2 vials (prophylaxis), 4 vials (therapy)
  - (For 0–≤15 kg: dose based on units/kg rather than whole vials.)
- If only a portion of a vial is used, residual must be discarded; single‑use vial intended for one patient and one occasion.

### Administration technique and infusion rate
- Intravenous administration only. Other routes not evaluated.
- Allow product to reach room temperature before infusion.
- It is customary to begin at a low rate and gradually increase during the first 30 minutes if vital signs are stable:
  - Commence at approximately 1 mL per minute.
  - After ~15 minutes, the rate may be gradually increased to a maximum of about 3–4 mL per minute over the next ~15 minutes (i.e., full escalation over 30 minutes), provided tolerance is acceptable.
- May be infused undiluted. If dilution is necessary, up to four volumes of 0.9% saline or 5% glucose may be used.
- Administer separately from other intravenous fluids or medications.
- Apply aseptic technique to vial stopper prior to withdrawal.
- Monitor vital signs and observe for infusion reactions throughout infusion and for at least 20 minutes afterwards (longer as clinically indicated).

### Missed dose
- Not specified in available sources.

### Titration schedules
- No specific dose titration schedules other than the infusion rate escalation described above.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known history of a true anaphylactic reaction to **human cytomegalovirus immunoglobulin** or to any component of the product. [Australian]
- Known IgA deficiency with anti‑IgA antibodies, unless tested and shown not to have circulating anti‑IgA antibodies, because trace IgA in the preparation may provoke severe reactions. [Australian]

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed/critical warnings
- Not specified as a formal boxed warning in the available source; the following critical safety issues are emphasised in the PI and should be treated as high‑priority precautions:
  - Severe hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis, including abrupt hypotension and shock — monitor and be prepared to stop infusion and treat emergently.
  - Renal dysfunction and acute renal failure have been reported with IV immunoglobulin products; use caution in patients with risk factors for renal impairment.
  - Thromboembolic events have been reported in association with IV immunoglobulin administration.

### General warnings and precautions
- Hypersensitivity and anaphylaxis:
  - Trace IgA may cause anaphylaxis in IgA‑deficient patients with anti‑IgA antibodies.
  - Anaphylactic reactions can occur even in patients who previously tolerated treatment; reactions may relate to infusion rate.
  - If anaphylaxis occurs, stop infusion immediately; monitor vital signs; treat according to standard shock/anaphylaxis protocols (epinephrine/adrenaline, oxygen, antihistamines, corticosteroids available).
- Renal dysfunction / acute renal failure:
  - Reported with IVIg products; patients at increased risk include those with pre‑existing renal insufficiency, diabetes mellitus, age >65 years, volume depletion, sepsis, monoclonal gammopathy, and concurrent nephrotoxic medications.
  - Many reports associated with sucrose stabiliser products; this product contains maltose rather than sucrose, but renal monitoring and precautions remain indicated.
  - Ensure adequate hydration, do not exceed recommended dose, monitor renal function, consider discontinuation if renal function deteriorates. In at‑risk patients use the lowest effective dose and the minimum infusion rate compatible with clinical judgment.
- Thromboembolic events:
  - IVIg therapy associated with thromboembolic events (venous and arterial), possibly related to increased blood viscosity.
  - Risk factors include advanced age, estrogen use, indwelling vascular catheters, history of thrombosis, hypercoagulable states, cardiovascular disease (including atherosclerosis/impaired cardiac output), prolonged immobilisation, severe hypovolaemia, hyperviscosity conditions, and monoclonal gammopathies.
  - Use minimum infusion rate and dose in at‑risk patients, monitor for signs/symptoms of thromboembolism; consider baseline blood viscosity measurement where clinically appropriate.
- Haemolytic anaemia:
  - IVIg can contain blood group antibodies, which may coat RBCs (positive direct antiglobulin test) and rarely cause haemolysis or clinically significant haemolytic anaemia.
  - Risk factors: high doses, non‑O blood group, underlying inflammatory state.
  - Monitor for haemolysis and obtain appropriate laboratory testing if clinically suspected; consider discontinuation if haemolysis occurs.
- Aseptic meningitis syndrome (AMS):
  - Reported infrequently after IVIg; usually begins within several hours to two days following infusion.
  - Symptoms: severe headache, nuchal rigidity, drowsiness, fever, photophobia, painful eye movements, nausea, vomiting. CSF commonly shows pleocytosis (predominantly granulocytic) and elevated protein.
  - AMS may be more frequent with high‑dose IVIg (e.g., 2 g/kg). Discontinuation typically leads to resolution within several days without sequelae.
- Pathogen safety:
  - Manufactured from human plasma; despite donor screening and viral reduction steps (e.g., low pH incubation, nanofiltration, Cohn fractionation steps described in PI), there remains a theoretical risk of transmission of infectious agents.
  - Specific viral inactivation/removal measures described as effective against enveloped viruses (HIV, HBV, HCV) and reduction/removal steps for non‑enveloped viruses (HAV, parvovirus B19) are included in the manufacturing process per the PI.
  - Record product name and batch number at each administration to maintain traceability.
  - Vaccination considerations: vaccination should be considered as appropriate in patients receiving plasma‑derived products (see Interactions).
- Acid load and neonates:
  - The product has an acidic pH; consider acid load and volume/acid burden in neonates and patients with compromised acid‑base compensatory mechanisms.
- Infusion reactions and monitoring:
  - Reactions are more likely during the first hour and are often related to infusion rate; monitor vital signs during infusion and for at least 20 minutes afterwards.
- Effects on laboratory tests:
  - Passive transfer of antibodies may produce transient positive serologies and interfere with serological testing, including tests for red cell alloantibodies; passive anti‑A/anti‑B/anti‑D may affect Coombs testing.
  - Maltose may interfere with some blood glucose assays (notably some glucose dehydrogenase methods) and produce falsely elevated glucose readings; review the glucose meter/test strip manufacturer information before use in patients receiving this product.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor vital signs during infusion and observe for infusion reactions.
- Monitor renal function in patients at increased risk of renal impairment.
- Monitor for clinical and laboratory evidence of haemolysis where indicated.
- Monitor for signs/symptoms of thromboembolism and AMS.
- Verify and record product batch number and patient details for traceability.

## 8. Drug Interactions

### Vaccine interactions
- Passively acquired antibody from immunoglobulin preparations can interfere with the immune response to live attenuated virus vaccines.
  - Defer administration of live attenuated virus vaccines (e.g., poliomyelitis, measles) until approximately three months after passive immunisation.
  - For measles vaccine, decreased efficacy may persist for up to one year after administration of immunoglobulin; patients receiving measles vaccine should have antibody status checked.
  - Conversely, immunoglobulins should not be administered for at least two weeks after a vaccine has been given.

### Laboratory/assay interactions
- Maltose in the product can interfere with some blood glucose measurement methods (notably certain glucose dehydrogenase assays), producing falsely elevated glucose readings. Review glucose meter/test strip manufacturer guidance before use in patients receiving this product.
- Passive transfer of antibodies may cause false‑positive serological tests (including tests for red cell alloantibodies) and may interfere with direct antiglobulin testing.

### Other medicines
- Concomitant use of nephrotoxic medications increases risk of renal dysfunction when receiving IV immunoglobulin preparations; monitor renal function and use caution.
- No specific pharmacokinetic drug–drug interactions (such as CYP interactions) are described in the available source.
- No other medicine compatibilities/interactions have been systematically evaluated; the product should be administered separately from other IV fluids or medications.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- Pregnancy:
  - Safety in human pregnancy has not been established in controlled clinical trials; administer during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
  - Clinical experience with other immunoglobulin preparations suggests no adverse fetal effects, but controlled data for this specific product are not available.
  - Pregnancy category systems (e.g., US letter categories, Australian categories) are not provided in the available source.
- Breastfeeding:
  - Immunoglobulins are excreted in breast milk and may transfer protective antibodies to the neonate; it is not known whether this applies specifically to passively administered **human cytomegalovirus immunoglobulin**.
  - Clinical experience with immunoglobulins suggests no harmful effects on the neonate are expected.
- Fertility:
  - No reproductive toxicity studies have been conducted with this product. No reports of effects on fertility associated with CSL’s plasma‑derived therapeutic medicines are reported in the PI.

## 10. Use in Special Populations

### Paediatric use
- Use in the paediatric population has not been established in appropriate studies according to the available source.
- Dosing guidance: patients ≤15 kg should be dosed by units/kg (see Dosage section). Consider volume/acid load in neonates; monitor accordingly.
- The PI states that paediatric patients are not over‑represented in spontaneous adverse event reports for the manufacturer’s IVIg medicines to date.

### Elderly use
- Use in the elderly has not been established in appropriate studies; the elderly are not over‑represented in spontaneous adverse event reports to date.
- Age >65 years is a listed risk factor for renal dysfunction associated with IVIg products; exercise caution, monitor renal function, and use conservative dosing/infusion rates in elderly patients.

### Renal impairment
- Patients with pre‑existing renal insufficiency are at increased risk of renal dysfunction and acute renal failure; monitor renal function and consider discontinuation if renal function deteriorates.
- No specific dosing adjustments by eGFR or CKD stage are provided in the available source.

### Hepatic impairment
- Not specified in available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology

### Mechanism of action
- Product is prepared from selected human plasma donations with high levels of CMV‑specific antibodies; the therapeutic effect is mediated by passive transfer of CMV‑specific immunoglobulin (predominantly IgG) to provide immediate humoral immunity against CMV.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Serum half‑life: approximately three weeks in normal individuals; may be substantially shortened in immunosuppressed patients.
- Absorption: administered intravenously; systemic exposure is immediate.
- Distribution, metabolism, elimination: specific pharmacokinetic parameters beyond half‑life are not specified in the available source.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Passive antibody transfer results in transient increases in corresponding antibody titres in the recipient; serological tests can be affected.

### Preclinical safety data
- Genotoxicity: No genotoxicity studies have been conducted for this product; no reports of genotoxicity associated with the manufacturer’s plasma‑derived therapeutic medicines.
- Carcinogenicity: No carcinogenicity studies have been conducted; no reports associated with use of the manufacturer’s plasma‑derived therapeutic medicines.
