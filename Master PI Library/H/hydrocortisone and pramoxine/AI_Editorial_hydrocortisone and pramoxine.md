# Editorial Product Information: Hydrocortisone Acetate and Pramoxine Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (Food and Drug Administration)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of the clinical and regulatory profile of this combination product for use by healthcare professionals.
- **IMPORTANT REGULATORY NOTE:** The specific product reviewed (Pramosone) is classified in the United States as an unapproved drug. It has not been found by the FDA to be safe and effective for its labeled indications, and the labeling has not been formally approved by the agency [US]. 
- This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Hydrocortisone acetate (corticosteroid) and Pramoxine hydrochloride (topical anesthetic).
- **Brand Name(s):** Pramosone [US].
- **ATC Code:** D07AA52 (Hydrocortisone, combinations) [Inferred; not specified in source].
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Topical Ointment: Hydrocortisone acetate 1% w/w or 2.5% w/w with Pramoxine hydrochloride 1% w/w [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sebela Pharmaceuticals Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- Relief of inflammatory and pruritic manifestations associated with corticosteroid-responsive dermatoses [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** Apply a thin film to the affected skin areas three to four times daily, depending on the severity of the condition [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - For external use only.
    - Avoid contact with eyes [US].
    - Treated areas should not be bandaged or covered with occlusive dressings unless specifically directed by a physician [US].
- **Occlusive Dressings:** May be utilised for the management of psoriasis or other recalcitrant conditions. If a local infection develops under occlusion, the dressing must be discontinued and appropriate antimicrobial therapy initiated [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known history of hypersensitivity to hydrocortisone acetate, pramoxine hydrochloride, or any excipient in the preparation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **HPA Axis Suppression:** Systemic absorption of topical corticosteroids can produce reversible hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis suppression, Cushing’s syndrome, hyperglycemia, and glucosuria. Risk increases with the use of high-potency steroids, application over large surface areas, prolonged use, and the use of occlusive dressings [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Patients receiving large doses or using occlusive dressings should be periodically evaluated for HPA axis suppression using the ACTH stimulation test or urinary free cortisol test. If suppression is detected, the drug should be withdrawn, the frequency of application reduced, or a less potent steroid substituted [US].
- **Local Irritation:** If irritation occurs, the product should be discontinued and appropriate alternative therapy instituted [US].
- **Infections:** In the presence of dermatological infections, an appropriate antifungal or antibacterial agent should be used. If the infection does not respond promptly, the corticosteroid should be discontinued until the infection is controlled [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following local adverse reactions are reported infrequently with topical corticosteroids. Frequency may increase with the use of occlusive dressings. Reactions are listed in approximate decreasing order of occurrence [US]:
- Burning, Itching, Irritation, and Dryness.
- Folliculitis and Hypertrichosis.
- Acneiform eruptions.
- Hypopigmentation.
- Perioral dermatitis and Allergic contact dermatitis.
- Skin maceration and Skin atrophy.
- Secondary infection.
- Striae and Miliaria.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- Not specified in available sources [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **United States Category C:** Corticosteroids are generally teratogenic in laboratory animals at low systemic doses and some following dermal application. There are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus. Avoid extensive use, large amounts, or prolonged treatment in pregnant patients [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if topical administration results in sufficient systemic absorption to produce detectable amounts in breast milk. While systemically administered corticosteroids are secreted into breast milk in quantities unlikely to harm the infant, caution should be exercised when prescribing to nursing women [US].
- **Fertility:** Long-term animal studies have not been performed to evaluate the effect of topical corticosteroids on fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - Paediatric patients are at a higher risk of HPA axis suppression and Cushing’s syndrome compared to adults due to a larger skin surface area to body weight ratio [US].
    - Reported manifestations in children include linear growth retardation, delayed weight gain, and low plasma cortisol levels. 
    - Intracranial hypertension (bulging fontanels, headaches, bilateral papilledema) has also been reported [US].
    - Use should be limited to the minimum amount necessary for therapeutic efficacy.
    - Caregivers should avoid using tight-fitting diapers or plastic pants on children being treated in the diaper area, as these act as occlusive dressings [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Hydrocortisone acetate:** A topical corticosteroid with anti-inflammatory, anti-pruritic, and vasoconstrictive properties. The exact mechanism of anti-inflammatory activity remains unclear [US].
    - **Pramoxine hydrochloride:** A topical anesthetic that provides temporary relief from pain and itching by stabilizing the neuronal membrane of local nerve endings [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Percutaneous absorption is determined by the vehicle, epidermal integrity, and use of occlusion. Inflammation and skin disease increase absorption [US].
    - **Distribution:** Once absorbed, corticosteroids are bound to plasma proteins in varying degrees [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised in the liver [US].
    - **Elimination:** Excreted via the kidneys and, to some extent, in the bile [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions are permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F–86°F). 
- Keep the container tightly closed [US].
