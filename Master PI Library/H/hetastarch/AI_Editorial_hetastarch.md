# Editorial Product Information: Hetastarch (6% in Lactated Electrolyte Injection)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial document has been synthesized from official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **United States (FDA)**. 
- **Note on Jurisdiction:** At the time of this synthesis, only the US (FDA) documentation was provided for review. Consequently, all regulatory classifications, indications, and boxed warnings reflect the US clinical and regulatory landscape.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonized editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. It is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference; it is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Hetastarch (Hydroxyethyl Starch; HES)
- **Chemical Class:** Artificial colloid (derived from waxy starch amylopectin)
- **Brand Name:** **HEXTEND** [US]
- **ATC Code:** B05AA07
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injection, solution; 6% (6 g per 100 mL) in a lactated electrolyte vehicle. 
- **Electrolyte Composition (mEq/L):** Sodium 143, Chloride 124, Lactate 28, Calcium 5, Potassium 3, Magnesium 0.9.
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Hospira, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Treatment of Hypovolemia:** Indicated for volume expansion when adequate alternative treatments (e.g., crystalloids) are unavailable or unsuitable [US].
- **Clinical Limitation:** Not intended as a substitute for blood or plasma [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:** The typical dose ranges from **500 mL to 1000 mL**. 
- **Maximum Dose:** Doses exceeding 1500 mL per day (approx. 20 mL/kg) are generally not required. Larger volumes may be utilized in cases of severe blood loss, typically in conjunction with blood products [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Administer via IV infusion only.
    - Rate of infusion is dependent on the patient’s clinical status, age, weight, and the extent of fluid loss.
    - If using pressure infusion, all air must be expelled from the bag prior to administration to prevent air embolism.
    - Do not connect plastic containers in series.
    - Equipment should be replaced every 24 hours.
- **Incompatibilities:** Due to the calcium content (5 mEq/L), do not administer simultaneously with blood through the same administration set to avoid the risk of coagulation.

## 4. Contraindications
- **General Contraindication:** Hydroxyethyl starch (HES) products must not be used unless adequate alternative treatments are unavailable [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to hetastarch or corn (hetastarch is derived from corn starch) [US].
- **Specific Populations:** Contraindicated in critically ill patients, including those with sepsis, due to increased risk of mortality and renal injury [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [US]:** 
    - **Mortality and Kidney Injury:** Increased risk of death and acute kidney injury (AKI) requiring renal replacement therapy (RRT) in critically ill patients, including those with sepsis and blunt trauma.
    - **Coagulopathy:** Increased risk of bleeding, particularly in surgical patients.
- **Renal Function:** Avoid use in patients with pre-existing renal dysfunction. Discontinue at the first sign of renal injury. Monitor renal function for at least 90 days post-administration, as RRT may be required up to 90 days later.
- **Coagulation Monitoring:** Large volumes may dilute coagulation factors and platelets. Monitor the coagulation status of surgical patients; discontinue at the first sign of coagulopathy. Use with caution in patients receiving anticoagulants.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Rare, life-threatening anaphylactic/anaphylactoid reactions can occur. Discontinue immediately if a reaction is suspected.
- **Electrolyte and Fluid Balance:** 
    - Use with caution in patients with hyperkalemia, renal failure, congestive heart failure, or clinical states of edema with sodium retention.
    - Contains lactate; use with caution in patients with metabolic or respiratory alkalosis or hepatic insufficiency.
- **Laboratory Interference:** May cause temporary elevation of serum amylase (3–5 days) which does not signify pancreatitis. Elevated specific gravity of urine may occur, potentially obscuring a diagnosis of renal failure.

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Frequencies are not specifically categorized by percentage in the source; the following are reported based on clinical experience and post-marketing data.*

- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylactic/anaphylactoid reactions, angioedema, bronchospasm, urticaria, and rash.
- **Cardiovascular:** Circulatory overload, congestive heart failure, pulmonary edema, cardiac arrest, ventricular fibrillation, hypotension, bradycardia, and tachycardia.
- **Hematologic:** Intracranial bleeding, anemia (due to hemodilution), Factor VIII deficiency, acquired von Willebrand’s-like syndrome, and coagulopathy.
- **Dermatologic:** Hydroxyethyl starch-associated pruritus (may be delayed and persist for weeks).
- **Gastrointestinal/General:** Vomiting, submaxillary/parotid gland enlargement, fever, chills, and muscle pains.
- **Metabolic:** Metabolic acidosis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Corticosteroids/Corticotropin:** Caution is advised when administering sodium-containing parenteral fluids to patients receiving these agents due to risk of sodium retention [US].
- **Anticoagulants:** Increased risk of bleeding complications when used concurrently with drugs that influence the coagulation system.
- **Digitalis:** Use potassium-containing solutions with caution in digitalized patients due to the risk of cardiac arrhythmias.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly lettered in current labeling, but the PI notes that hetastarch has shown embryocidal effects in animal models (rabbits and rats) when administered at 0.5 to 2.3 times the maximum human dose.
    - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if hetastarch is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution when administering to nursing women [US].
- **Fertility:** Long-term animal studies have not been performed to evaluate the impact on fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established. A small study in pediatric cardiac surgery patients (ages 1–15.5 years) suggested increased prothrombin time when doses exceeded 20 mL/kg [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials (n=119) included 30% of patients aged 65 and older. While no overall differences in response were noted, elderly patients are more likely to have decreased renal function, necessitating careful dose selection and monitoring [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Significant risk of toxicity and AKI. Hetastarch is substantially excreted by the kidneys and is **not** eliminated by hemodialysis.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution; impaired utilization of lactate ions may occur in severe hepatic insufficiency.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A plasma volume expander that supports oncotic pressure. Its large molecular weight permits retention within the intravascular space until the polymer is replaced by blood proteins.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Intravenous infusion results in volume expansion that typically decreases over 24 to 36 hours.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Plasma volume expansion occurs immediately.
    - **Metabolism:** Smaller molecules are rapidly excreted; larger molecules are slowly hydrolyzed by serum amylase.
    - **Elimination:** Molecules below 50,000 Daltons are rapidly eliminated via renal excretion. Approximately 33% of a 500 mL dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Biliary excretion accounts for <1% over 14 days.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Hypovolemia in Elective Surgery:** Randomized, double-blind studies (n=60) comparing HEXTEND to 6% hetastarch in saline showed no significant differences in adverse events or the requirement for blood products.
- **Critical Care Studies (referenced in US PI):**
    - **VISEP Trial:** Reported increased mortality and RRT use in patients with severe sepsis treated with HES.
    - **CHEST Trial:** Reported no difference in mortality but significantly increased use of RRT in a heterogeneous ICU population.
    - **Vascular Surgery:** Retrospective analysis associated HES use with increased 30-day mortality and RRT compared to crystalloids.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at room temperature, 25°C (77°F). Brief exposure up to 40°C (104°F) is permissible.
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Inspect for particulate matter and discoloration before use. The solution should be clear and pale yellow to amber. Do not use if turbid or if a crystalline precipitate is present.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as **Rx-only**. Includes a Boxed Warning regarding mortality and kidney injury in specific populations.
