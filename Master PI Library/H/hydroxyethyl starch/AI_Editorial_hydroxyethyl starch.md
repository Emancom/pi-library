# Editorial Product Information: hydroxyethyl starch

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **hydroxyethyl starch**
- Chemical class: Hydroxyethylated amylopectin derivative (artificial colloid; hetastarch)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intravenous only

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of hypovolemia when plasma volume expansion is desired and adequate alternative treatment is unavailable.
- Not a substitute for blood or plasma.
- Use in other situations (e.g., routine use outside treatment of hypovolemia) has not been adequately evaluated in available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Specific standard dosing regimens, infusion rates and maximum cumulative dose instructions: Not specified in available sources.
- Clinical trial usage examples (for context only, not formal dosing recommendations):
  - Mean infused volumes in elective surgery studies: approximately 1.4–1.6 L.
  - A single ~500 mL dose (6% solution) resulted in urinary elimination of approximately 33% of the dose within 24 hours in one report.
  - References in animal studies and PI text use a “maximum recommended therapeutic human dose” of 1500 mL in toxicology contexts; this should not be interpreted as an approved maximum dosing regimen absent explicit dosing instructions in the source PI.

### Dosing by indication
- Specific indication-based dosing regimens (e.g., sepsis, trauma, surgery): Not specified in available sources.

### Administration instructions
- Administer by intravenous infusion only.
- Do not administer solutions containing calcium simultaneously with blood through the same administration set because of the likelihood of coagulation.
- If administration is by pressure infusion, withdraw or expel all air from the bag through the medication port prior to infusion.
- Do not use in leukapheresis.
- Monitor fluid balance and hemodynamic response; titrate infusion to clinical effect.
- Discontinue at first sign of renal injury or coagulopathy.

### Titration schedules
- Not specified in available sources.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not applicable / Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Do not use hydroxyethyl starch products unless adequate alternative treatment is unavailable. (Absolute statement as presented in available sources.)
- Do not use in leukapheresis.
- Other absolute contraindications: Not specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warning
- **[US] WARNING: INCREASED MORTALITY; KIDNEY INJURY (AKI); COAGULOPATHY. Do not use hydroxyethyl starch products unless adequate alternative treatment is unavailable.**  
  - Use is associated with increased risk of mortality, acute kidney injury (including requirement for renal replacement therapy), and coagulopathy in critically ill patients (including sepsis), surgical patients, and blunt trauma patients.

### General warnings and precautions
- Hypersensitivity and anaphylaxis: Life‑threatening anaphylactic/anaphylactoid reactions have been rarely reported; death has been reported in association with such reactions. Hypersensitivity reactions can occur after discontinuation.
- Renal injury: Avoid use in patients with pre-existing renal dysfunction when possible. Discontinue at first sign of renal injury. Continue to monitor renal function for at least 90 days after administration because renal replacement therapy (RRT) has been reported up to 90 days following exposure.
- Coagulation and bleeding:
  - Solutions containing hydroxyethyl starch may transiently alter coagulation due to hemodilution and direct inhibitory action on Factor VIII; may be associated with an acquired von Willebrand‑like syndrome.
  - Increased bleeding has been reported in surgical patients, including those undergoing cardiopulmonary bypass.
  - Monitor coagulation status during and after administration; discontinue on first sign of clinically relevant coagulopathy.
- Circulatory overload/pulmonary edema: Monitor for signs of volume overload; exercise caution in patients at risk for pulmonary edema or congestive heart failure.
- Electrolyte/acid‑base disturbances:
  - Contains sodium, chloride, lactate, calcium, potassium, magnesium and dextrose; use caution in patients prone to sodium or potassium retention (e.g., severe renal insufficiency, congestive heart failure).
  - Lactate-containing solutions should be used with care in severe hepatic insufficiency or when lactate metabolism is impaired.
  - Potassium-containing solutions should be used with caution in hyperkalemia or conditions with potassium retention.
- Liver function: Monitor liver function; elevations in indirect bilirubin have been observed transiently in studies.
- Pancreatitis testing: Serum amylase may be transiently elevated for 3–5 days after administration; cannot rely on serum amylase to assess pancreatitis during this period. Lipase not shown to be increased.
- Allergies to corn: Use with caution in patients with corn allergy (cross-reactivity possible).
- Concomitant anticoagulants: Exercise caution when hydroxyethyl starch is used in patients anticoagulated with other agents that impair coagulation.
- Hemodialysis/extracorporeal removal: Hydroxyethyl starch is not eliminated by hemodialysis; utility of other extracorporeal elimination techniques has not been evaluated.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor:
  - Renal function before and after administration and periodically for at least 90 days when clinically indicated.
  - Coagulation parameters (e.g., PT, aPTT, fibrinogen, platelet count, clinical bleeding).
  - Fluid balance, signs of volume overload.
  - Electrolytes and acid–base status.
  - Liver function tests when clinically indicated.
  - Signs or symptoms of hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Not a substitute for blood or plasma; consider blood products if excessive hemodilution occurs.
- Caution in pediatric patients; safety and efficacy not established broadly — see Section 10.
- Use with extreme caution, if at all, in patients with severe hepatic insufficiency (lactate metabolism) or in those with conditions leading to impaired utilization of lactate.

  - In randomized surgical studies comparing hydroxyethyl starch formulations, no significant differences in number of adverse events were found between some study arms; however, increased bleeding has been reported in specific surgical settings (e.g., cardiopulmonary bypass).
  - Hydroxyethyl starch‑associated pruritus has been reported and may be associated with deposits in peripheral nerves.
  - The serious safety signals of increased mortality and AKI (including need for RRT) have been reported in critically ill and sepsis populations and observed in blunt trauma; these are highlighted as major safety concerns.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Pharmacodynamic interactions:
  - Concomitant anticoagulants: additive negative effects on coagulation; increased bleeding risk. Monitor coagulation closely; use caution.
  - Blood products and calcium-containing fluids: **Do not administer calcium‑containing hydroxyethyl starch solutions simultaneously with blood through the same administration set** due to the likelihood of coagulation.
- Pharmacokinetic interactions:
  - Hydroxyethyl starch is not eliminated by hemodialysis; removal by extracorporeal techniques other than hemodialysis has not been evaluated.
- Other interaction considerations:
  - Exercise caution when administering sodium‑containing fluids to patients receiving corticosteroids or corticotropin (risk of sodium/volume retention).
  - Lactate‑containing solutions may not be effective as a bicarbonate source in patients with impaired lactate metabolism (e.g., severe hepatic insufficiency).
- Jurisdiction‑specific interactions: Not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Animal data: Embryocidal effects were reported in rabbits and rats at certain doses in animal studies cited in the source PI; teratogenicity was not demonstrated in some animal models at other dose levels.
- Human data: No adequate and well‑controlled studies in pregnant women.
- Recommendation: Use during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus.
- Pregnancy categorisation systems (e.g., national pregnancy categories): Not specified in available sources.

### Breastfeeding
- It is not known whether hydroxyethyl starch is excreted in human milk. Because many drugs are excreted in human milk, exercise caution when administering to a nursing woman.

### Fertility
- Long‑term carcinogenicity and formal fertility studies: Not specified in available sources for effects on fertility; the source PI states that long‑term carcinogenicity studies have not been performed.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Safety and effectiveness in pediatric patients have not been established in general.
- One small double‑blind study in 47 pediatric patients (ages 1 year to 15.5 years) undergoing repair of congenital heart disease with moderate hypothermia compared hydroxyethyl starch to albumin in the first 24 hours post‑operatively; no differences in coagulation parameters or replacement fluids were seen with doses ≤20 mL/kg. In children receiving >20 mL/kg of hydroxyethyl starch, an increase in prothrombin time was reported (p = 0.006).
- Neonatal safety: Not established; no neonates included in the referenced pediatric study.

### Elderly use
- Clinical trial exposure included elderly patients (approximately 30% ≥65 years); no definitive differences identified between elderly and younger patients, but elderly patients may have decreased renal function increasing risk of toxic reactions. Use caution and consider monitoring renal function and dose selection carefully.

### Renal impairment
- Hydroxyethyl starch is principally eliminated by renal excretion; exercise caution in patients with impaired renal clearance.
- Avoid use in patients with pre‑existing renal dysfunction when possible.
- Discontinue at first sign of renal injury; continue renal monitoring for at least 90 days after exposure.
- Dosing adjustments by eGFR/stage: Not specified in available sources.

### Hepatic impairment
- Lactate metabolism may be impaired in severe hepatic insufficiency; lactate‑containing solutions should be used with great care in such patients.
- Specific dosing adjustments by severity of hepatic impairment: Not specified in available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Hydroxyethyl starch is an artificial colloid plasma volume expander that increases oncotic pressure, promoting retention of intravascular fluid until replaced by endogenous proteins. It exerts a direct inhibitory action on Factor VIII and can affect coagulation.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: Administered intravenously; immediate intravascular distribution.
- Distribution/Duration: Plasma volume expansion induced by hydroxyethyl starch decreases over the subsequent 24–36 hours following infusion.
- Metabolism: Hydroxyethyl groups are not cleaved by the body; the polymer is hydrolyzed to yield products with a range of molecular weights. The hydroxyethyl ether groups remain attached to glucose units when excreted.
- Elimination:
  - Renal excretion is the principal elimination route. Molecules below ~50,000 Da are eliminated rapidly via the kidneys.
  - Example: A single ~500 mL dose (6% formulation) resulted in urinary elimination of approximately 33% of dose within 24 hours in one study; intravascular concentration generally <10% of total dose by two weeks.
  - Biliary excretion: less than 1% of dose over 14 days in one small study.
  - Not eliminated by hemodialysis; other extracorporeal elimination techniques: not evaluated.
- Pharmacokinetic parameters (clearance, half‑life, volume of distribution): Not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Oncotic effect leading to plasma volume expansion approximating other hetastarch 6% formulations; pharmacodynamic impact dependent on baseline intravascular status.
- Direct inhibitory impact on coagulation, particularly Factor VIII activity; may cause dilutional declines in total protein, albumin, fibrinogen and prolongation of clotting times.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Elective surgery, randomized double‑blind studies:
  - Study comparing hydroxyethyl starch in lactated electrolyte solution versus 6% hetastarch in 0.9% sodium chloride for treatment of hypovolemia in elective surgery: 60 patients received hydroxyethyl starch (mean 1596 mL) and 59 patients received control (mean 1428 mL); no serious related adverse events were reported in that comparison. Approximately 59% of patients in both groups required packed red blood cells or other blood‑derived products intraoperatively or postoperatively.
- Comparative studies vs albumin:
  - Randomized, controlled comparative studies of 6% hetastarch in 0.9% sodium chloride (n = 92) and albumin (n = 85) in surgical patients reported no patients in either group with a bleeding complication and no significant difference in blood loss between groups in those studies cited.
- Severe sepsis trial (using an HES product not licensed in the United States):
  - A randomized controlled trial (N = 804) reported increased mortality with HES (relative risk 1.17; 95% CI 1.01–1.36; p = 0.03) and increased requirement for renal replacement therapy (relative risk 1.35; 95% CI 1.01–1.80; p = 0.04) in the HES treatment arm.
- Blunt trauma:
  - Clinical trials have shown increased mortality and acute kidney injury in blunt trauma subjects receiving HES formulations.
- Pediatric trial:
  - Small double‑blind study in infants/children/adolescents (n = 47, ages 1–15.5 years) undergoing repair of congenital heart disease with moderate hypothermia: 38 children required colloid replacement; in those receiving ≤20 mL/kg no differences in coagulation parameters were detected versus albumin; children receiving >20 mL/kg of hetastarch showed increased prothrombin time (p = 0.006).
- Other clinical observations:
  - Increased bleeding has been reported in patients undergoing open heart surgery in association with cardiopulmonary bypass following use of 6% hetastarch formulations.
  - Intracranial bleeding, including fatal cases, has been reported in association with repeated use over days (e.g., in subarachnoid hemorrhage context).

- Additional trial details, endpoints, and full study reports: Not specified in available sources beyond the summaries above.
