# Editorial Product Information: Histrelin Acetate

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the regulatory authorities of: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of histrelin acetate (specifically the 12-month subcutaneous implant) for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or prescribing information mandated in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Histrelin acetate (synthetic nonapeptide analog of GnRH).
- **Chemical Class:** Gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) agonist.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Supprelin LA** [US].
- **ATC Code:** H01CA03.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile, non-biodegradable, diffusion-controlled hydrogel polymer reservoir. Each implant contains 50 mg histrelin acetate, delivering approximately 65 mcg/day over 12 months [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Subcutaneous implantation.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Endo USA, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Central Precocious Puberty (CPP):** Indicated for the treatment of children with CPP (neurogenic or idiopathic). Eligibility is generally defined by the early onset of secondary sexual characteristics (before age 8 in females and age 9 in males) and significantly advanced bone age [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Paediatric):** One 50 mg subcutaneous implant every 12 months [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - The implant must be inserted subcutaneously in the inner aspect of the upper arm using aseptic technique and the provided insertion tool.
    - Site of insertion: Halfway between the shoulder and elbow, in the crease between the biceps and triceps muscles [US].
    - Removal and replacement: The implant should be removed after 12 months of therapy. A new implant may be inserted at the time of removal. The device is designed to provide a few additional weeks of release to allow for scheduling flexibility [US].
- **Monitoring for Discontinuation:** Discontinuation should be considered at the appropriate time for the onset of puberty (approximately age 11 for females and age 12 for males) at the physician's discretion [US].
- **Post-insertion Care:** Keep the arm dry for 24 hours and avoid strenuous exertion for 7 days [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known history of hypersensitivity to GnRH or GnRH analogs [US].
- **Pregnancy:** Histrelin may cause foetal harm or loss when administered to a pregnant patient [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Initial Agonistic Action:** During the first week of treatment, a transient increase in sex steroids (estradiol/testosterone) occurs. This may cause a temporary worsening of puberty symptoms or the onset of new symptoms. Suppression is typically achieved within 4 weeks [US].
- **Implant Breakage:** Breakage has occurred during removal. Localization may require ultrasound, CT, or MRI (the implant is not radiopaque). All pieces must be retrieved to ensure cessation of drug release [US].
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Reports include Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), DRESS syndrome, and AGEP. Therapy should be interrupted for suspected SCARs and permanently discontinued if confirmed [US].
- **Psychiatric Events:** GnRH agonists are associated with emotional lability (e.g., crying, irritability, anger, aggression). Monitor for new or worsening psychiatric symptoms, including depression and suicidal ideation [US].
- **Convulsions:** Observed in patients with or without a history of seizures, epilepsy, or CNS anomalies, as well as those on concomitant medications that lower the seizure threshold (e.g., bupropion, SSRIs) [US].
- **Pseudotumor Cerebri (Idiopathic Intracranial Hypertension):** Reported in paediatric patients. Monitor for headache, papilledema, blurred/double vision, and dizziness [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** 
    - Assess LH, FSH, and sex steroids at 1 month post-implantation and every 6 months thereafter.
    - Monitor height (for height velocity) and bone age every 6–12 months [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** 
    - Implant site reactions (51.1%), including pain, bruising, soreness, erythema, and swelling [US].
- **Common (1–10%):** 
    - Keloid scar, scar, suture-related complications (6.4%).
    - Application site pain, post-procedural pain (4.3%) [US].
- **Frequency Not Defined (Post-marketing/Clinical Trial):** 
    - Wound infection, breast tenderness, dysmenorrhea, epistaxis, gynecomastia, headache, migraine, weight increase, influenza-like illness, and metrorrhagia [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** SCARs, seizures, pituitary adenoma (rare), and pseudotumor cerebri [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No formal drug-drug interaction studies have been performed [US].
- **Drug-Laboratory Interactions:** 
    - Histrelin suppresses the pituitary-gonadal system. Diagnostic tests of pituitary gonadotropic and gonadal functions may be affected during and immediately after therapy [US].
    - Studies noted an ~11% decrease in IGF-1 levels and potential increases in DHEA concentrations [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not explicitly lettered under current labeling, but contraindicated]. Expected hormonal changes increase the risk of pregnancy loss. Animal studies showed increased foetal mortality and abortion at clinically relevant exposures [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on the presence of histrelin in human milk or its effects on the breastfed infant. Systemic activity is not expected via ingestion due to the peptide nature of the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats and monkeys showed full reversibility of fertility suppression after 6 months of treatment [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established in children under 2 years of age; use in this population is not recommended [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not applicable for the indicated condition (CPP) [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Histrelin is a potent GnRH agonist. Continuous administration causes reversible down-regulation of pituitary GnRH receptors and desensitization of gonadotropes, ultimately reducing ovarian and testicular steroidogenesis [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** LH response to GnRH is suppressed to prepubertal levels within 1 month. This slows linear growth velocity and improves the likelihood of attaining predicted adult height [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Continuous release over 12 months.
    - **Distribution/Metabolism/Elimination:** Not detailed in provided source.
    - **Steady State:** Median maximum serum concentration is approximately 0.43 ng/mL [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Data:** Two single-arm, open-label studies (N=47) evaluated efficacy.
- **Outcomes:** 
    - Primary measure: LH suppression (peak LH <4 mIU/mL following GnRH analog stimulation).
    - Results: Suppression was induced in 100% of treatment-naïve subjects by Month 1 and maintained through Month 12. 
    - Secondary outcomes: Stabilization of bone age advancement and clinical progression of puberty were observed [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Refrigerate at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) [US].
- **Handling:** 
    - The implant must be protected from light and kept in its sealed vial/pouch until insertion. 
    - **Excursion:** Permitted to remain at room temperature (up to 25°C/77°F) for up to 7 days before use. 
    - Do not freeze [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** 
    - [US]: Prescription Only (Rx Only).
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** 
    - [US]: Not a controlled substance.
