# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the official Product Information for levocarnitine injection. It is intended for use by healthcare professionals as a clinically accurate, consolidated reference.
*   **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
*   **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
*   **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
*   **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
*   **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Metabolic Disorders:** For the management of acute and chronic secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn errors of metabolism [US].
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
*   **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    *   Recommended dose: 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion. 
    *   In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is typically followed by equivalent doses every 3 or 4 hours (minimum frequency every 6 hours).
    *   Subsequent daily doses generally range from 50 mg/kg upwards as clinically required; doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
*   **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    *   Starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    *   Administration: Slow bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    *   Dose adjustment: Adjustments (e.g., reduction to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
*   Parenteral products must be visually inspected for particulate matter or discolouration prior to use.
*   **Compatibility:** Compatible with Sodium Chloride 0.9% and Lactated Ringer’s solution. Concentrations may range from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL. Mixed solutions are stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags [US].
*   Discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

### Monitoring
*   Baseline plasma carnitine levels should be obtained.
*   Ongoing monitoring should include weekly and monthly plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine levels between 35 and 60 µmol/L), blood chemistries, and vital signs [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported, primarily in dialysis patients. Onset can occur within minutes of injection. In the event of a severe reaction, discontinue treatment immediately and assess the risks/benefits of re-administration [US].
*   **Seizures:** Levocarnitine may increase seizure frequency or severity in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a prior history of seizure activity [US].
*   **Metabolite Accumulation:** Safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been established in patients with renal impairment. Long-term high-dose oral administration in renal failure may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally renally excreted [US].

*   Anaphylaxis and severe respiratory distress (bronchospasm) [US].
*   Seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels upon initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine in patients on anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [US: Not formally categorised by letter, but source indicates animal studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm.] Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Not specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations following administration. The benefits to the mother must be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant; discontinuation of nursing or the drug should be considered [US].
*   **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, yeast) were negative. Long-term animal carcinogenicity studies have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg) as in adults for metabolic disorders [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Renal Impairment:** IV administration is indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD. However, caution is advised regarding oral levocarnitine due to the accumulation of TMA and TMAO metabolites [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Distribution half-life is approximately 0.585 hours [US].
*   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
*   **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
*   **Bioavailability:** Absolute bioavailability of oral formulations is low (approx. 15-16%) [US].

### Pharmacodynamics
Levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with specific metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ESRD Study:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly, mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine concentrations increased from a baseline of 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
*   **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C [US].
*   **Conditions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **United States:** Rx-only. Scheduled as a Human Prescription Drug [US].
