# Editorial Product Information: Hydroxycarbamide

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the Product Information (PI) for hydroxycarbamide, designed for use by healthcare professionals as a clinically accurate reference. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official PI. Clinicians should consult the current official PI in their specific jurisdiction before prescribing or administering this medication. At the time of this report, only the Australian (TGA) source was provided for evaluation.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Hydroxycarbamide (also known as hydroxyurea).
- **Chemical Class:** Antineoplastic agent (antimetabolite).
- **Brand Names:** **Hydrea** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01XX05.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Capsules:** 500 mg (Opaque green cap and opaque pink body, imprinted with "CHP 500") [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Link Medical Products Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Myelocytic Leukaemia (CML):** Indicated for the pretreatment phase and for palliative care [Australian].
- **Carcinoma of the Ovary:** Indicated for recurrent, metastatic, or inoperable cases [Australian].
- **Head and Neck Carcinoma:** For use in concomitant therapy with irradiation for squamous cell (epidermoid) carcinomas, excluding the lip [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing Principles:** Dosage must be calculated based on the patient's actual or ideal weight, whichever is less [Australian].
- **Solid Tumours:**
    - **Intermittent Therapy:** 80 mg/kg administered as a single oral dose every third day [Australian].
    - **Continuous Therapy:** 20 to 30 mg/kg administered as a single daily oral dose [Australian].
    - **Concomitant Irradiation (Head and Neck):** 80 mg/kg every third day, beginning at least seven days prior to starting irradiation [Australian].
- **Resistant Chronic Myelocytic Leukaemia:**
    - **Continuous Therapy:** 20 to 30 mg/kg as a single daily dose [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** Therapy should be continued indefinitely if tumour regression or growth arrest is observed. An adequate trial period is typically six weeks [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Patients should consult their physician or pharmacist regarding missed doses [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Marked bone marrow depression: Leucopenia (< 2500 WBC/mm³), thrombocytopenia (< 100,000/mm³), or severe anaemia [Australian].
- Known hypersensitivity to hydroxycarbamide or any excipients in the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available Australian source; however, significant warnings regarding myelosuppression and secondary malignancies are prominent.
- **Myelosuppression:** Treatment should not be initiated if bone marrow function is depressed. Leucopenia is the most common and earliest manifestation; thrombocytopenia and anaemia occur less frequently. Recovery is usually rapid upon drug interruption [Australian].
- **Secondary Malignancies:** Long-term use for myeloproliferative disorders has been linked to secondary leukaemia and skin cancer. Patients should be advised to practice sun protection and undergo regular skin screenings [Australian].
- **Pancreatitis and Hepatotoxicity:** Fatal events have occurred in HIV-infected patients when hydroxycarbamide was combined with antiretroviral agents, particularly didanosine and stavudine. This combination should be avoided [Australian].
- **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD):** Reports of pulmonary fibrosis, pneumonitis, and alveolitis exist, which may be fatal. Discontinue immediately and treat with corticosteroids if respiratory symptoms develop [Australian].
- **Cutaneous Vasculitic Toxicities:** Ulceration and gangrene have occurred, particularly in patients with a history of interferon therapy. Discontinue if vasculitic ulcers develop [Australian].
- **Glucose Monitoring Interference:** Hydroxycarbamide may falsely elevate sensor results in certain continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) systems, potentially leading to hypoglycemia if used for insulin dosing [Australian].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** CBC (haemoglobin, total leucocytes, and platelets) must be performed at least once weekly. Kidney and liver function should be assessed prior to and during treatment [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Bone marrow depression (leucopenia, anaemia, thrombocytopenia). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Gastrointestinal: Stomatitis, anorexia, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation. Dermatological: Maculopapular rash, hyperpigmentation, skin atrophy. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Skin cancer, dysuria, alopecia, neurological disturbances (hallucinations, convulsions). |
| **Frequency Not Specified** | Haemolytic anaemia, drug-induced fever (>39°C), pulmonary oedema, interstitial lung disease, tumour lysis syndrome, systemic lupus erythematosus [Australian]. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Cutaneous vasculitic ulcers, gangrene, fatal pancreatitis (in HIV combination therapy), and secondary leukaemia [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Myelosuppressive Agents/Radiation:** Concurrent use increases the risk of bone marrow depression [Australian].
- **Uricosuric Medications:** Hydroxycarbamide may elevate serum uric acid; dose adjustments for uricosurics may be necessary [Australian].
- **Live Vaccines:** Increased risk of fatal systemic vaccine disease. Live vaccines are not recommended for immunosuppressed patients [Australian].
- **Antiretroviral Agents (HIV):** Increased risk of hepatotoxicity, pancreatitis, and severe peripheral neuropathy (notably with didanosine/stavudine) [Australian].
- **Laboratory Interference:** Analytical interference occurs with enzymes (urease, uricase, LDH) used to determine urea, uric acid, and lactic acid [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category D:** Hydroxycarbamide is a known teratogen. It should only be used if the potential benefit outweighs the risk to the foetus. 
    - **Contraception:** Female patients should use effective contraception during and for 6 months after therapy. Male patients should use safe contraception during and for at least 1 year after therapy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** The drug is excreted in human milk. A decision must be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the drug's importance to the mother [Australian].
- **Fertility:** May cause azoospermia or oligospermia in men (sometimes reversible). Sperm conservation should be discussed prior to treatment [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in children [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Older patients may be more sensitive and may require a lower dose regimen [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Renal excretion is a primary elimination pathway. Use with caution and consider dose reduction in patients with marked renal dysfunction [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Specific guidance is unavailable; however, close monitoring of haematologic parameters is recommended [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** While not fully elucidated, hydroxycarbamide is believed to cause immediate inhibition of DNA synthesis without affecting RNA or protein synthesis. In radiotherapy, it is thought to sensitise cells by holding them in the G1 phase and inhibiting the repair of cells damaged by radiation [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Readily absorbed from the GI tract.
    - **Distribution:** Reaches peak serum levels within 2 hours; serum levels are negligible by 24 hours.
    - **Excretion:** Approximately 80% is recovered in the urine within 12 hours of oral or IV administration [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Not specified in available sources. The Australian PI notes significant tumour responses in CML and ovarian carcinoma based on general clinical data but does not list specific pivotal trial names [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Handling:** As a cytotoxic agent, hydroxycarbamide must be handled with extreme care. Impervious gloves should be worn. Contents of capsules should not be inhaled or allowed to contact skin/mucous membranes. Pregnant women should not handle the product [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
