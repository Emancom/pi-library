# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: US (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical and regulatory data to facilitate quick reference.
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt)
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US]
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn errors of metabolism [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Initial Management:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Maintenance Dosing:** Dosing should be administered every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Dose Range:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised based on clinical requirement [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels. Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate the risks/benefits of re-challenge [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine may increase seizure frequency or severity in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a prior history of epilepsy [US].
- **Monitoring:** In metabolic disorders, monitor plasma carnitine levels (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L), blood chemistry, and vital signs weekly/monthly. In ESRD, monitor trough plasma levels (normal range: 40–50 µmol/L) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (1–10%):** Transient nausea and vomiting, stomach atony, and gastritis.
- **Other Reported Effects:** Body odor (rare).
- **Dialysis-Specific Adverse Events (≥5% regardless of causality):** 
    - **General:** Abdominal pain, headache, fever, infection, injection site reaction, pain.
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension, hypotension, chest pain, tachycardia.
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea, dyspepsia, vomiting.
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis.
    - **Nervous System:** Dizziness, paresthesia [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalised Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Clinical Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not explicitly assigned in modern format]. Reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. Weigh the benefits of maternal treatment against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg) and applicable to paediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO). Intravenous levocarnitine is specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients undergoing dialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for beta-oxidation and energy production. It also facilitates the excretion of toxic acyl-CoA esters in patients with metabolic defects [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Carnitine deficiency is defined biochemically as plasma levels <20 µmol/L. Administration increases plasma carnitine and facilitates acyl-carnitine excretion [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and gamma-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% excreted in urine (0–24h). Mean distribution half-life: 0.585 hours; terminal elimination half-life: 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Bioequivalence:** A study in 15 healthy volunteers confirmed bioequivalence between oral tablets and oral solution (Cmax ~80 µmol/L).
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg post-dialysis showed trough levels increased from ~20 µmol/L to between 54 and 180 µmol/L after one week of therapy. Long-term studies (12–24 weeks) confirmed dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine concentrations [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** Not specified in available US sources.
