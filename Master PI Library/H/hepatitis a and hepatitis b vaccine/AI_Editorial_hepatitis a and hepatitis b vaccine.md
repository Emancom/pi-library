# Editorial Product Information: Hepatitis A and Hepatitis B (Recombinant) Vaccine (Twinrix)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the **United States (FDA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- This document is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the United States PI was available for review; therefore, all information is attributed to this jurisdiction unless otherwise noted.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Inactivated hepatitis A virus (strain HM175) and noninfectious hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg).
- **Chemical Class:** Combined viral vaccine (inactivated virus and recombinant protein).
- **Brand Name:** **Twinrix** [US].
- **ATC Code:** J07BC20 (Hepatitis A, inactivated, whole virus and hepatitis B, surface antigen).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injectable suspension. Each 1 mL dose contains 720 ELISA Units of inactivated hepatitis A virus and 20 mcg of recombinant HBsAg protein.
- **Route of Administration:** Intramuscular (IM).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** GlaxoSmithKline Biologicals SA [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Active Immunisation:** Indicated for active immunisation against disease caused by the hepatitis A virus and infection by all known subtypes of the hepatitis B virus [US].
- **Approved Population:** For use in individuals aged 18 years or older [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults $\ge$ 18 years)
- **Standard Schedule:** A 3-dose series (1 mL each) administered at 0, 1, and 6 months [US].
- **Accelerated Schedule:** A 4-dose series (1 mL each) administered on Days 0, 7, and 21 to 30, followed by a booster dose at Month 12 [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Preparation:** Shake vigorously for at least 15 seconds to obtain a uniform, hazy white suspension. Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].
- **Site:** Administer intramuscularly. Do **NOT** administer in the gluteal region, as this may result in a suboptimal immune response [US].
- **Concomitant Administration:** May be administered with immune globulin, but different syringes and separate injection sites must be used [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Severe allergic reaction (e.g., anaphylaxis) following a previous dose of any hepatitis A-containing or hepatitis B-containing vaccine [US].
- **Excipients:** Known hypersensitivity to any component of **Twinrix**, including yeast and neomycin (residual from the manufacturing process) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Syncope:** Fainting can occur with injectable vaccines. Clinicians should have procedures in place to avoid injury from falling [US].
- **Anaphylaxis:** Appropriate medical treatment must be immediately available to manage potential allergic reactions [US].
- **Acute Illness:** Vaccination should be postponed in patients with moderate or severe acute febrile illness unless the risk of immediate infection outweighs the risk of delaying the vaccine [US].
- **Altered Immunocompetence:** Immunocompromised individuals (including those on immunosuppressive therapy) may exhibit a reduced immune response [US].
- **Multiple Sclerosis:** Clinical data indicates no association between hepatitis B vaccination and the development or relapse of multiple sclerosis [US].
- **Limitations of Effectiveness:** The vaccine may not protect all individuals. It will not prevent infection in those with an unrecognised hepatitis A or B infection at the time of vaccination due to the long incubation periods of these viruses [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Breakdown [US]
| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site soreness (35–41%), headache (13–22%), fatigue (11–14%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Injection site redness (8–11%), swelling (4–6%), diarrhea (3–6%), nausea (2–7%), fever (2–4%), upper respiratory tract infection, injection site induration |
| **Uncommon (<1%)** | Anorexia, agitation, insomnia, dizziness, migraine, paresthesia, somnolence, syncope, vertigo, flushing, abdominal pain, vomiting, erythema, petechiae, rash, sweating, urticaria, arthralgia, back pain, myalgia, injection site pruritus, irritability, weakness |

### Postmarketing Experience [US]
Because these events are reported voluntarily from a population of uncertain size, frequency cannot be reliably estimated:
- **Neurological:** Bell’s palsy, convulsions, encephalitis, Guillain-Barré syndrome, myelitis, optic neuritis, paralysis, transverse myelitis.
- **Immune System:** Anaphylaxis, serum sickness-like syndrome (days to weeks post-vaccination).
- **Blood/Lymphatic:** Thrombocytopenia, thrombocytopenic purpura.
- **Cardiovascular:** Palpitations, tachycardia, vasculitis.
- **Hepatobiliary:** Jaundice, hepatitis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Laboratory Test Interference:** Hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) from the vaccine may be transiently detected in blood samples for up to 28 days post-vaccination. Serum HBsAg detection during this window may not have diagnostic value [US].
- **Immunosuppressants:** Therapies such as alkylating agents, antimetabolites, cytotoxic drugs, irradiation, or high-dose corticosteroids may diminish the immune response to the vaccine [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Pregnancy Category not assigned]. Human data from pregnancy registries (2001–2015) do not suggest an increased risk of major birth defects or miscarriage when administered within 28 days of conception or during pregnancy. Rates of adverse outcomes were consistent with estimated background rates [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There is no information regarding the presence of the vaccine in human milk or its effects on the breastfed child. The benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need for the vaccine [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in female rats showed no adverse effects on fertility. Male fertility has not been evaluated in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established in patients younger than 18 years [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65 and older to determine if they respond differently than younger adults [US].
- **Hepatic/Renal Impairment:** Specific dose adjustments for hepatic or renal impairment are not specified in available sources [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Confers active immunity by inducing specific antibodies (anti-HAV and anti-HBs).
- **Pharmacodynamics:** 
    - Anti-HBs concentrations $\ge$ 10 mIU/mL are recognised as conferring protection against HBV [US].
    - Anti-HAV titers equal to or greater than the assay cut-off are considered seroconverted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Not applicable to vaccines.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Standard Schedule (0, 1, 6 months):** In 11 clinical trials involving 1,551 healthy adults, seroconversion for HAV was 99.9% and seroprotection for HBV was 98.5% one month after the third dose [US].
- **Accelerated Schedule:** In a study of 496 adults, the accelerated schedule (Days 0, 7, 21–30, and Month 12) was non-inferior to monovalent vaccines administered separately [US].
- **Durability:** Antibodies to both HAV and HBV have been shown to persist for at least 4 years after the first dose of the 3-dose series [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store refrigerated between 2°C and 8°C (36°F and 46°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Do **NOT** freeze. Discard if the product has been frozen. Protect from light [US].
- **Packaging:** Prefilled syringes (TIP-LOK) are not made with natural rubber latex [US].
