# Editorial Product Information: Triamterene and Hydrochlorothiazide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a concise, structured format.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidance required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Triamterene (potassium-conserving diuretic) and Hydrochlorothiazide (thiazide/natriuretic agent).
- **Brand Names:** Available as generic formulations; distributed by Northwind Health Company, LLC [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Oral Tablet: 37.5 mg triamterene / 25 mg hydrochlorothiazide [US].
    - Oral Tablet: 75 mg triamterene / 50 mg hydrochlorothiazide [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Rubicon Research Ltd. (Manufacturer); Advagen Pharma Ltd. (Distributor) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypertension:** For the treatment of hypertension in patients who develop hypokalemia on hydrochlorothiazide alone, or in patients where the development of hypokalemia cannot be risked (e.g., those on digitalis or with a history of cardiac arrhythmias) [US].
- **Edema:** For the treatment of edema in patients who develop hypokalemia on hydrochlorothiazide alone, or in patients where hypokalemia must be avoided [US].
- **Important Limitation:** This fixed-dose combination is not indicated for initial therapy of edema or hypertension except in individuals where the risk of hypokalemia is clinically significant [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):**
    - **37.5 mg/25 mg Strength:** 1 or 2 tablets daily, administered as a single dose [US].
    - **75 mg/50 mg Strength:** 1 tablet daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - May be used alone or in combination with other antihypertensive agents (e.g., beta-blockers). Dosage adjustments of concomitant agents may be necessary [US].
    - Absorption of both components is not influenced by food intake [US].
- **Indication-Specific Notes:**
    - If blood pressure is not controlled with the 37.5 mg/25 mg strength, the dose may be increased to two tablets daily (as a single dose) or one 75 mg/50 mg tablet daily [US].
- **Titration and Transfer:**
    - Patients receiving 25 mg HCTZ who become hypokalemic may be transferred to the 37.5 mg/25 mg combination [US].
    - Patients receiving 50 mg HCTZ who become hypokalemic may be transferred to the 75 mg/50 mg combination [US].
- **Monitoring:** Frequent monitoring of serum potassium is required during initiation and dosage titration [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hyperkalemia:** Elevated serum potassium levels (≥ 5.5 mEq/L) [US].
- **Potassium Supplements/K-Sparing Agents:** Concomitant use of other potassium-conserving agents (e.g., spironolactone, amiloride) or potassium supplementation (medication, salt substitutes, or potassium-enriched diets) [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Anuria, acute or chronic renal insufficiency, or significant renal impairment [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to triamterene, hydrochlorothiazide, or other sulfonamide-derived drugs [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Hyperkalemia:** Abnormal elevation of serum potassium (≥ 5.5 mEq/L) can occur, which may be fatal. Risk is highest in patients with renal impairment or diabetes, and in the elderly or severely ill. Serum potassium must be monitored frequently, especially at initiation or following dose changes [US].
- **Acute Myopia and Secondary Angle-Closure Glaucoma:** Hydrochlorothiazide can cause an idiosyncratic reaction resulting in acute transient myopia and acute angle-closure glaucoma. Symptoms occur within hours to weeks of initiation. Discontinue immediately to prevent permanent vision loss [US].
- **Electrolyte Imbalance:** Risk of hyponatremia, hypochloridemic alkalosis, hypokalemia, and hypomagnesemia. Frequent serum electrolyte determination is required, particularly if the patient is vomiting or receiving parenteral fluids [US].
- **Metabolic/Respiratory Acidosis:** Avoid use in severely ill patients where acidosis may occur, as acidosis can cause rapid elevations in serum potassium [US].
- **Non-melanoma Skin Cancer:** Hydrochlorothiazide is associated with an increased risk of squamous cell carcinoma and basal cell carcinoma. Patients should protect skin from sun exposure and undergo regular screening [US].
- **Systemic Lupus Erythematosus:** Thiazides may exacerbate or activate SLE [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Jaundice (intrahepatic cholestatic), pancreatitis, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, anorexia, gastric irritation [US].
- **Central Nervous System:** Dizziness, headache, fatigue, insomnia, drowsiness, paresthesias [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Orthostatic hypotension (potentiated by alcohol/narcotics), tachycardia, chest pain [US].
- **Renal:** Acute renal failure, interstitial nephritis, renal stones (triamterene-associated), urine discoloration [US].
- **Hematologic:** Leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, agranulocytosis, aplastic anemia, hemolytic anemia, megaloblastosis [US].
- **Dermatologic/Hypersensitivity:** Photosensitivity, rash, urticaria, purpura, necrotizing angiitis, anaphylaxis [US].
- **Metabolic:** Hyperuricemia (may precipitate gout), hyperglycemia, glycosuria [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **ACE Inhibitors:** Greatly increased risk of hyperkalemia; use with extreme caution and frequent potassium monitoring [US].
- **Lithium:** Diuretics reduce renal clearance of lithium, increasing the risk of toxicity. Generally, should not be given together [US].
- **Non-Steroidal Anti-inflammatory Agents (NSAIDs):** Concurrent use may lead to acute renal failure. Caution is advised [US].
- **Antihypertensive Drugs:** Thiazides may potentiate the action of other antihypertensives; dosage reduction of those agents may be necessary [US].
- **Skeletal Muscle Relaxants (e.g., Tubocurarine):** Thiazides may increase responsiveness to these agents [US].
- **Norepinephrine:** Thiazides may decrease arterial responsiveness to norepinephrine, though not enough to preclude its use [US].
- **Quinidine:** Triamterene and quinidine have similar fluorescence spectra; triamterene may interfere with the fluorescent measurement of quinidine levels [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly letter-coded in text, but specified for use only if the potential benefit justifies the risk to the fetus [US].
    - **Clinical Considerations:** Thiazides and triamterene cross the placental barrier. Risks include fetal/neonatal jaundice, thrombocytopenia, and pancreatitis. Diuretics do not prevent toxemia of pregnancy and are not indicated for dependent edema caused by venous restriction [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Thiazides are excreted in human milk; triamterene is excreted in animal milk and likely in human milk. If the drug is essential, breastfeeding should be discontinued [US].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in animal studies for hydrochlorothiazide. Studies for the combination or triamterene alone have not been conducted [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clearance of hydrochlorothiazide and triamterene metabolites is reduced in the elderly. Use with caution and monitor renal function/electrolytes closely [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in significant renal impairment or anuria. Cumulative effects may occur in patients with impaired renal function [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution in patients with progressive liver disease; minor electrolyte changes may precipitate hepatic coma [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:**
    - **Hydrochlorothiazide:** Blocks renal tubular absorption of sodium and chloride ions, primarily in the distal tubule [US].
    - **Triamterene:** Inhibits sodium reabsorption in exchange for potassium and hydrogen in the distal tubule. It is not a mineralocorticoid antagonist (active even in Addison's disease) [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Both components are well absorbed. Hydrochlorothiazide peak plasma levels: ~2 hours. Triamterene peak plasma levels: ~1 hour [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Triamterene is primarily metabolised to the active sulfate conjugate of hydroxytriamterene [US].
    - **Elimination:** Hydrochlorothiazide is excreted rapidly and unchanged in the urine [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Dose-Response Evaluations:** Steady-state evaluations in hypertensive patients demonstrated that 75 mg of triamterene provided optimal potassium conservation when added to 50 mg of HCTZ. Similarly, 37.5 mg of triamterene was optimal when added to 25 mg of HCTZ. Doses above these levels did not yield additional potassium-sparing benefits [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from light and moisture. Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container with a child-resistant closure [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only (Prescription Only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources (N/A for US jurisdiction).
