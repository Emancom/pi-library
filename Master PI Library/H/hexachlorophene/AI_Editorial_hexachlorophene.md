# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- Information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian, Japanese) was not available in the provided source documents and is therefore omitted or noted as "Not specified in available sources."
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. It is a clinical synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule involved in mitochondrial fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Class:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt; a naturally occurring mammalian energy metabolism component.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not explicitly stated in source but consistent with class).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic management of patients with inherited metabolic defects that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance haemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection over 2 to 3 minutes or via infusion. 
- **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose may be given, followed by an equivalent dose split over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours (and no less frequently than every 6 hours) [US].
- **Monitoring:** Plasma carnitine concentrations should be obtained prior to therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels (target range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine levels (normal range 40 to 50 µmol/L). Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of treatment if levels are adequate [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known:** There are no listed contraindications in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal oedema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in dialysis patients. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If severe reactions occur, discontinue therapy immediately [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In those with known epilepsy, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **General Precautions:** The safety of oral levocarnitine in patients with renal impairment or ESRD on dialysis has not been established; chronic high doses may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].
- **Monitoring:** Periodic monitoring of carnitine levels and metabolic parameters is essential.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported in $\ge$ 5% of patients in placebo-controlled trials, regardless of causality, include:

| Body System | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, arrhythmia, atrial fibrillation |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting, anorexia, constipation, dyspepsia |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcaemia, hyperkalaemia, hypervolaemia, peripheral oedema, weight changes |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia, depression |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough, dyspnoea, rhinitis |

### Other Effects
- **Common:** Transient nausea and vomiting.
- **Uncommon/Rare:** Body odour and gastritis [US].
- **Serious:** Anaphylaxis and seizures (post-marketing).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalised Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended that INR levels be monitored upon initiation of levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned a letter, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration. Clinicians must weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal reproductive studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is included in the general metabolic disorder recommendations (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the available source [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Oral levocarnitine is not recommended for chronic use in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO); however, the injection is specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients post-dialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is essential for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, carnitine deficiency may contribute to malaise, muscle weakness, and cardiac arrhythmias. Levocarnitine administration increases plasma concentrations [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. 
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (primarily in faeces) [US].
    - **Excretion:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Efficacy:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg after dialysis showed that trough concentrations reached steady state (54 to 180 µmol/L) within one week.
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses confirmed a dose-dependent increase in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine levels in ESRD patients, its effect on specific clinical outcomes in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature [US].
- **Single-use:** Discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction

- **Other Regions:** Not specified in available sources.
