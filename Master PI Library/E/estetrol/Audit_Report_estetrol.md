# Originality Audit Report: estetrol

## Audit Summary
- **Originality Score:** 72/100
- **Verbatim Copied Passages Found:** 4
- **Overall Assessment:** Concern

## Close Paraphrases
1.  **Missed Dose Instructions:** The logic and phrasing for the "1 tablet missed," "2 or more missed (Weeks 1 or 2)," and "Week 3" instructions (Section 5) are extremely close to the wording in **Table 2** of both the TGA and FDA documents. While some words like "immediately" are used instead of "as soon as possible," the structural sequence remains a direct mirror of the regulatory tables.
2.  **Pregnancy Outcomes:** The sentence "Epidemiological studies have not shown an increased risk of birth defects in children born to women who used COCs shortly before or during early pregnancy" (Section 8) is a very close paraphrase of TGA Section 4.6 and FDA Section 8.1, which both state: "Epidemiologic studies and meta-analyses have not found an increased risk of... birth defects following exposure to COCs before conception or during early pregnancy."
3.  **Metabolic Enzyme Inducers:** The description of CYP3A4 inducers (Section 7) uses nearly identical phrasing to the "Clinical Effect" column of Table 4 in the TGA document regarding "contraceptive failure or breakthrough bleeding."

## Originality Strengths
- **Harmonization Framework:** The editorial successfully creates a comparative structure, specifically where it contrasts the TGA and FDA guidelines for BMI (Section 2) and Storage Conditions (Section 11).
- **Mechanism of Action:** The synthesis of Estetrol’s origin (fetal liver) and its selective binding properties (Section 10) is well-rewritten and avoids the list-heavy technical jargon found in the source documents.
- **Section Headers:** The writer has reorganized the clinical data into a more readable "Editorial" flow rather than the standard 17-section regulatory format.

## Differences from Source PIs
- **Unified Storage Guidance:** The editorial identifies a discrepancy (TGA permits 30°C, FDA prefers 25°C) and provides a conservative "recommendation" for stability, which is an additive editorial judgment not present in either source.
- **BMI Contextualization:** The editorial explicitly attributes the BMI effectiveness limitation to the FDA, whereas the TGA document discusses it more broadly in the Clinical Trials section without the same "Limitation of Use" header.
- **Omissions:** The editorial significantly condenses the "Post-marketing Observation" and "Preclinical Safety Data" sections, focusing on clinical utility rather than exhaustive laboratory results.

## Recommendation
**CONCERN**

The editorial is a high-quality synthesis, but it relies too heavily on verbatim or near-verbatim phrasing in high-stakes safety sections (Contraindications, Cardiovascular Warnings, and Missed Dose instructions). While accuracy is paramount in medical writing, a "strict originality audit" requires more significant linguistic transformation.

**Required Revisions:**
1.  **Section 4 (Smoking):** Rewrite the cardiovascular warning to avoid the "Cigarette smoking increases the risk of serious..." standard regulatory opening.
2.  **Section 5 (Missed Doses):** Further distance the language from the source tables. For example, instead of "Discard other missed tablets," use "Any additional missed tablets should be left in the pack."
3.  **Section 9 (Pharmacokinetics):** Convert the list of binding percentages into a narrative sentence to avoid the direct copy-paste of the albumin/SHBG/CBG data string.
