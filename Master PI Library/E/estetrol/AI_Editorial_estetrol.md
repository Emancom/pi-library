# Unified Product Information: Estetrol and Drospirenone

**Editorial Note:** This document represents a unified synthesis of Product Information for Nextstellis (estetrol and drospirenone), incorporating clinical data and regulatory guidance from the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). Where recommendations or warnings differ between the two agencies, the more comprehensive or conservative guidance has been included and attributed accordingly.

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** Nextstellis
**Active Ingredients:** Estetrol (as monohydrate) and Drospirenone

Each active film-coated tablet contains 14.2 mg of estetrol (equivalent to 15 mg of estetrol monohydrate) and 3 mg of drospirenone. The regimen also includes inert (inactive) tablets containing no active substances.

## 2. Approved Indications
Nextstellis is a combined oral contraceptive (COC) indicated for the prevention of pregnancy in individuals of reproductive potential.

**Limitations of Use:**
Clinical data suggest that the contraceptive efficacy of Nextstellis may be reduced in patients with a Body Mass Index (BMI) $\ge$ 30 kg/m². Effectiveness appears to decrease further as BMI increases [FDA].

## 3. Contraindications
Nextstellis is contraindicated in patients with the following conditions:
*   **Vascular Disease:** Current or history of venous thromboembolism (VTE), including deep vein thrombosis (DVT) or pulmonary embolism (PE); known hereditary or acquired predisposition to VTE (e.g., Factor V Leiden, Protein C/S deficiency).
*   **Arterial Disease:** Current or history of arterial thromboembolism (ATE), including myocardial infarction, stroke, or prodromal conditions (e.g., TIA, angina pectoris); history of migraine with focal neurological symptoms (aura).
*   **Cardiovascular Risk:** Women over 35 years of age who smoke [FDA].
*   **Hormonally Sensitive Malignancy:** Known or suspected breast cancer or other estrogen-sensitive malignancies.
*   **Hepatic Conditions:** Acute hepatitis, severe (decompensated) cirrhosis, or the presence of hepatic tumors (benign or malignant).
*   **Renal and Adrenal Status:** Renal impairment/failure or adrenal insufficiency.
*   **Other:** Undiagnosed abnormal uterine bleeding; known or suspected pregnancy; hypersensitivity to any component of the formulation.
*   **Drug Interactions:** Concomitant use of Hepatitis C drug combinations containing ombitasvir/paritaprevir/ritonavir, with or without dasabuvir [FDA].

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Cardiovascular Events and Smoking
Cigarette smoking increases the risk of serious cardiovascular events from COC use. This risk is significantly higher in women over 35 years of age and increases with the intensity of smoking (number of cigarettes per day). Nextstellis is strictly contraindicated in smokers over the age of 35.

### Thromboembolic Disorders
The use of COCs increases the risk of VTE and ATE. If a thrombotic event occurs, Nextstellis must be discontinued immediately. Treatment should not be initiated sooner than 4 weeks postpartum due to the elevated risk of thromboembolism following delivery.

### Hyperkalemia
Drospirenone acts as an aldosterone antagonist with potassium-sparing properties. Nextstellis is contraindicated in patients with conditions predisposing them to hyperkalemia (e.g., renal or hepatic impairment). In patients taking long-term medications that may increase serum potassium (e.g., ACE inhibitors, ARBs, potassium-sparing diuretics), serum potassium levels should be monitored during the first treatment cycle.

### Hypertension
Blood pressure should be monitored periodically. If a significant or persistent rise in blood pressure occurs while taking Nextstellis, the medication should be discontinued.

### Liver Disease
Elevated liver enzymes may necessitate the temporary or permanent discontinuation of Nextstellis. Estrogens are associated with an increased risk of hepatic adenomas, the rupture of which can lead to life-threatening internal bleeding.

### Glucose and Lipid Metabolism
Patients with pre-diabetes or diabetes should be monitored closely, as COCs may decrease glucose tolerance. For patients with uncontrolled dyslipidemia or hypertriglyceridemia, alternative contraception should be considered due to the increased risk of pancreatitis.

### Depression and Mood Disorders
Hormonal contraceptives are linked to an increased risk of depression. Patients with a history of depressive disorders should be observed closely; the drug should be stopped if serious depression recurs.

## 5. Dosage and Administration
Nextstellis is administered in a 28-day cycle consisting of 24 pink active tablets followed by 4 white inactive tablets.

*   **Routine Dosing:** Take one tablet daily at approximately the same time for 28 consecutive days.
*   **Initiation:** Start on Day 1 of the natural menstrual cycle. If started after Day 1, a non-hormonal backup method (e.g., condoms) must be used for the first 7 days of tablet-taking.
*   **Missed Doses:**
    *   **1 tablet missed:** Take the missed tablet as soon as possible (even if it means taking two tablets in one day) and continue the pack as normal.
    *   **2 or more tablets missed (Weeks 1 or 2):** Take the most recent missed tablet immediately and the current day's tablet. Discard other missed tablets. Use backup contraception for 7 days.
    *   **2 or more tablets missed (Week 3):** Take the most recent missed tablet. Finish the active pink tablets in the pack and skip the 4 inactive white tablets, starting a new pack the following day. Use backup contraception for 7 days.
*   **Gastrointestinal Disturbances:** If vomiting or severe diarrhea occurs within 3–4 hours of taking an active tablet, it should be treated as a missed dose.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most common adverse reactions (occurring in $\ge$ 2% of participants) reported in clinical trials include:
*   Irregular uterine bleeding (metrorrhagia, vaginal hemorrhage, or spotting)
*   Headache or migraine
*   Mood disturbances (including depression and mood swings)
*   Breast symptoms (tenderness, pain, or enlargement)
*   Dysmenorrhea
*   Acne
*   Weight increased
*   Decreased libido

**Post-marketing Observation:** Rare but serious events include VTE/ATE, gallbladder disease, and chloasma (especially in those with a history of chloasma gravidarum).

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Metabolic Enzyme Inducers:** Drugs that induce CYP3A4 (e.g., rifampicin, carbamazepine, St. John’s wort) can decrease the plasma concentrations of drospirenone and estetrol, potentially leading to contraceptive failure or breakthrough bleeding. Backup contraception is required during use and for 28 days after stopping the inducer.
*   **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Strong inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole) may increase drospirenone exposure, increasing the risk of hyperkalemia.
*   **Effect on Other Drugs:** COCs may decrease the plasma levels of lamotrigine (antiepileptic), potentially requiring dose adjustments. Estrogens may also increase levels of thyroid-binding globulin, necessitating an increase in thyroid hormone replacement doses in some patients.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** Nextstellis is not indicated during pregnancy. If pregnancy occurs, the medication must be discontinued immediately. Epidemiological studies have not shown an increased risk of birth defects in children born to women who used COCs shortly before or during early pregnancy.
*   **Breastfeeding:** COCs can reduce the quantity and quality of breast milk. Small amounts of contraceptive steroids and/or their metabolites (approximately 0.02% of the drospirenone dose) are excreted in human milk. Use of Nextstellis is generally not recommended until the infant is completely weaned.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Estetrol (E4):**
    *   **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed with a median $T_{max}$ of 0.5–2 hours.
    *   **Metabolism:** Primarily undergoes Phase 2 metabolism to form glucuronide and sulfate conjugates via UGT2B7 and SULT1E1.
    *   **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 24–27 hours. Excreted mainly in urine.
*   **Drospirenone (DRSP):**
    *   **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ occurs at approximately 1–3 hours.
    *   **Distribution:** 95%–97% bound to serum albumin; does not bind to SHBG or CBG.
    *   **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolized by CYP3A4.
    *   **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 34 hours.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Nextstellis prevents pregnancy primarily through the suppression of ovulation.
*   **Estetrol:** A synthetic analogue of a native human estrogen produced by the fetal liver. It binds selectively to nuclear estrogen receptors (ER$\alpha$ and ER$\beta$) and acts as an estrogen agonist. Unlike ethinylestradiol, it has a minimal impact on hepatic protein production (such as SHBG and coagulation factors).
*   **Drospirenone:** A synthetic progestogen with pharmacological properties similar to natural progesterone, exhibiting anti-gonadotropic, anti-androgenic, and mild anti-mineralocorticoid activity.

## 11. Storage Conditions
Store the medication at room temperature between 20°C and 25°C [FDA]. Keep the blister pack in the outer carton to protect from light and moisture. In Australia, excursions are permitted up to 30°C [TGA]. To ensure maximum stability, the 20°C to 25°C range is recommended [FDA]. Keep out of reach of children.
