# Editorial Product Information: eplontersen

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): eplontersen  
- Chemical class: Transthyretin-directed antisense oligonucleotide conjugated to a triantennary N‑acetyl galactosamine (GalNAc) ligand (ASO‑GalNAc conjugate)  
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources  
- Route(s) of administration: Subcutaneous injection

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 2023 (as stated in available sources)  
- Date of last revision/update in available source PI: April 2026  
- Withdrawal/suspension/restriction history: Not specified in available sources  
- Other jurisdictions’ approval dates or revision dates: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of the polyneuropathy of hereditary transthyretin‑mediated (hATTR) amyloidosis in adults.  
- Indication statements for other jurisdictions: Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Recommended dose: 45 mg administered subcutaneously once every 4 weeks (monthly).

### Dosing by indication
- Indication-specific dosing: single recommended regimen above for treatment of polyneuropathy of hATTR amyloidosis in adults.

### Administration instructions
- Administer the entire contents of one 45 mg dose by subcutaneous injection. Preferred injection sites are the abdomen or the anterior thigh. The posterior aspect of the upper arm may be used if a healthcare provider or trained caregiver performs the injection.  
- Avoid injecting into areas of skin that are red, warm, tender, bruised, scaly, hard, or within approximately 2 inches (5 cm) of the navel. Rotate injection sites with each dose.  
- Autoinjector: intended for patient self‑administration or caregiver administration after appropriate training.  
- Prefilled syringe: intended for administration by a healthcare provider only. Do not remove the needle cover until ready to inject. Avoid touching needle guard activation clips to prevent premature activation of the needle safety guard. Do not use if dropped, damaged, appears tampered with, or expired. Allow the prefilled syringe to reach room temperature for approximately 30 minutes prior to injection; do not use other warming methods. Visually inspect—the solution should be colorless to yellow; do not use if cloudiness, particulate matter, or discoloration is observed.  
- Prefilled syringe injection technique (healthcare provider): remove syringe from tray, hold syringe body and pull needle cover straight off (do not recap). If small air bubbles are present, do not expel them before administration. Gently pinch the skin and insert the needle subcutaneously at an approximately 45° angle. After complete injection, the syringe’s needle safety device will engage and cover the needle automatically.  
- Do not inject partial doses. Deliver the entire contents of a single device for the 45 mg dose.

### Missed dose
- If a dose is missed, administer eplontersen as soon as possible. Thereafter, resume dosing at monthly intervals calculated from the date of the most recently administered dose.

### Titration schedules
- No titration schedule specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- None specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- None specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- **Reduced serum vitamin A and recommended supplementation:** Treatment with eplontersen causes marked reductions in serum transthyretin (TTR) and is associated with substantial decreases in circulating vitamin A. Patients should receive supplementation at the recommended daily allowance of vitamin A while receiving eplontersen. Do not administer doses of vitamin A above the recommended daily allowance solely to normalize serum vitamin A levels during treatment, because serum vitamin A does not represent total body vitamin A stores. Refer patients to an ophthalmologist if ocular symptoms suggestive of vitamin A deficiency (for example, night blindness or dry eyes) occur.  
- **Cardiac conduction events:** Serious atrioventricular (AV) heart block events, including complete AV block, were observed among treated patients (see Adverse Effects). Monitor clinically as appropriate for symptoms/signs; specific routine ECG monitoring instructions are not specified in available sources.  
- **Immunogenicity:** Treatment‑emergent anti‑drug antibodies (ADAs) were observed; clinical consequences are uncertain (see Pharmacology—Immunogenicity and Adverse Effects).  
- **Injection site reactions:** Injection site reactions (erythema, pain, pruritus) have been reported; follow appropriate injection technique and patient education.

### Monitoring requirements
- Specific routine laboratory or cardiac monitoring protocols are not specified in available sources beyond vitamin A supplementation and ophthalmology referral for ocular symptoms.  
- Clinicians should be aware of frequent biochemical reductions in serum vitamin A and the potential for clinically relevant ocular manifestations; the PI recommends vitamin A supplementation at the recommended daily allowance and referral to ophthalmology if ocular symptoms occur.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Do not attempt to correct serum vitamin A concentrations during eplontersen therapy by administering supra‑physiologic vitamin A doses; avoid attempting to “normalize” serum vitamin A with doses above the recommended daily allowance.

## 7. Adverse Effects
- Most commonly reported adverse reactions (≥9% in Study 1): decreased vitamin A and vomiting.

- Additional adverse reactions and observations reported in clinical development and Study 1 are detailed below. Frequencies are derived from Study 1 unless otherwise noted. Where both biochemical incidence and adverse‑event reporting are available for the same finding (e.g., vitamin A decrease), both are presented.

### Summary of adverse events from Study 1 (N=144 treated; durations up to median ~15 months)
- Biochemical finding: 95% of patients treated developed low serum vitamin A during the study (all had normal vitamin A at baseline).  
- Adverse reaction reporting: decreased vitamin A was reported as an adverse reaction in 15% of patients.  
- Other adverse reactions (all from Study 1): vomiting 9%; proteinuria 8%; injection site reactions 7% (e.g., erythema, pain, pruritus); blurred vision 6%; cataract 6%.  
- Serious adverse reactions: three serious AV heart block events (2%); one case of complete AV block reported.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Clinical drug‑drug interaction (DDI) studies: No clinical DDI studies have been performed.  
- In vitro findings and DDI expectations: Eplontersen is not a substrate or inhibitor of common drug transporters in vitro, does not interact with highly plasma protein‑bound drugs, and is not an inhibitor or inducer of cytochrome P450 enzymes. Oligonucleotide therapeutics, including eplontersen, are not typically metabolized by CYP enzymes. Therefore, eplontersen is not expected to cause, nor be subject to, clinically relevant DDIs mediated by transporters, plasma protein displacement, or CYP enzymes.  
- Clinically significant pharmacodynamic or other interactions: Not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Human data: No available human data to inform drug‑associated risk of adverse developmental outcomes.  
- Effect of maternal vitamin A supplementation and pharmacologic reduction of maternal TTR on embryo/fetal development is unknown. Vitamin A is essential for embryofetal development but excessive vitamin A is associated with teratogenicity; counsel accordingly.  
- Animal data: In mice, subcutaneous administration of eplontersen (doses up to 75 mg/kg) or a mouse‑specific surrogate prior to mating and during organogenesis produced no adverse effects on embryofetal development in the studies reported.  
- Pregnancy category/system: Not specified in available sources.

### Breastfeeding
- Human milk data: No information available regarding presence of eplontersen in human milk, effects on breastfed infant, or effects on milk production. Consider the developmental benefits of breastfeeding along with the mother’s clinical need and potential adverse effects.  
- Recommendation: Not specified beyond the above in available sources.

### Fertility
- Animal fertility data: No adverse effects on fertility were observed in mice at doses tested (up to 75 mg/kg) in the reported studies.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Safety and efficacy in pediatric patients have not been established.

### Elderly use
- No dose adjustment is required in patients aged ≥65 years. In Study 1, 31% were aged 65–74 and 5.6% were ≥75; no overall differences in safety or efficacy were observed, though increased sensitivity in some older individuals cannot be ruled out.

### Renal impairment
- No dose adjustment necessary for mild to moderate renal impairment (estimated glomerular filtration rate [eGFR] ≥30 to <90 mL/min/1.73 m²).  
- Eplontersen has not been studied in patients with severe renal impairment or end‑stage renal disease.

### Hepatic impairment
- No dose adjustment necessary for mild hepatic impairment (defined in available sources as total bilirubin ≤1 × ULN and AST >1 × ULN, or total bilirubin >1.0 to 1.5 × ULN and any AST).  
- Eplontersen has not been studied in patients with moderate or severe hepatic impairment.

### Prior liver transplant
- Not studied in patients with prior liver transplant (not specified beyond that statement).

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Eplontersen is an antisense oligonucleotide conjugated to GalNAc that binds transthyretin (TTR) mRNA in hepatocytes, promoting degradation of mutant and wild‑type TTR mRNA and resulting in reduced hepatic TTR protein synthesis and lower circulating TTR concentrations and tissue TTR deposits.

### Pharmacodynamics
- In Study 1, monthly administration at recommended dosing produced rapid reductions in serum TTR, with mean reductions of approximately 81% from baseline at Week 35. Eplontersen also reduced mean steady‑state serum vitamin A by approximately 71% by Week 37 in treated patients.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: Following subcutaneous administration, time to maximum plasma concentration (Tmax) is approximately 2 hours (population estimate).  
- Distribution: Expected distribution primarily to liver and renal cortex. In vitro plasma protein binding >98%. Population estimates: apparent central volume ~12 L; apparent peripheral volume ~11,100 L.  
- Metabolism: Metabolized by endo‑ and exonucleases within the liver to shorter oligonucleotide fragments.  
- Elimination: Terminal elimination half‑life approximately 3 weeks. Mean fraction of unchanged drug eliminated in urine within 24 hours was <1% of administered dose. No accumulation of Cmax or AUC observed with once‑monthly dosing.  
- Dose proportionality: Cmax and AUC increased slightly greater than dose‑proportionally over single subcutaneous doses ranging from 45 to 120 mg. Population steady‑state estimates following 45 mg monthly: Cmax ≈ 283 ± 152 ng/mL; AUCτ ≈ 2190 ± 689 ng·h/mL (population estimates).  
- Specific populations: Population PK/PD analyses showed no clinically meaningful differences by age, body weight, sex, race, Val30Met status, mild/moderate renal impairment, or mild hepatic impairment. Not studied in severe renal impairment, end‑stage renal disease, moderate/severe hepatic impairment, or prior liver transplant.

### Immunogenicity
- In Study 1 (up to ~85 weeks), 37% (53/144) of patients developed treatment‑emergent anti‑drug antibodies. ADAs did not affect plasma Cmax or AUC but were associated with increased trough concentrations in some patients. Available data did not demonstrate an effect of ADAs on pharmacodynamics, safety, or efficacy, but data are limited.

## 12. Clinical Trials
### Key pivotal trial (Study 1; NCT04136184)
- Design: Randomized, open‑label, multicenter clinical trial in adults with polyneuropathy caused by hATTR amyloidosis. Patients were randomized 6:1 to eplontersen 45 mg every 4 weeks (N=144) or inotersen 284 mg weekly (N=24). The efficacy analysis compared the eplontersen arm with an external placebo group (N=60) from a prior randomized trial in a comparable population. The majority of eplontersen patients completed at least 35 weeks of assigned treatment.  
- Baseline population (eplontersen arm): median age 52 years; 69% male; genotype: 59% Val30Met, others various; disease stages: 80% Stage 1, 20% Stage 2. Mean duration of treatment ~15 months.  
- Primary/efficacy endpoints: Change from baseline to Week 35 in modified Neuropathy Impairment Score+7 (mNIS+7) and change from baseline to Week 35 in Norfolk Quality of Life‑Diabetic Neuropathy (Norfolk QoL‑DN) total score, comparing eplontersen treatment to the external placebo control.  
- Results (comparison to external placebo control at Week 35):  
  - mNIS+7: treatment difference LS mean −9.0 (95% CI −13.5, −4.5); p < 0.001 (improvement favoring eplontersen).  
  - Norfolk QoL‑DN total score: treatment difference LS mean −11.8 (95% CI −16.8, −6.8); p < 0.001 (improvement favoring eplontersen).  
- Interpretation: Eplontersen produced statistically significant and clinically meaningful improvements in objective neuropathy measures and patient‑reported neuropathy quality of life compared with the external placebo control at Week 35 in the trial design described.

### Other clinical observations
- Safety signals observed in the trial population included frequent biochemical reductions in vitamin A, gastrointestinal adverse reactions (vomiting), proteinuria, injection site reactions, ocular findings (blurred vision, cataract), and rare serious cardiac conduction events (AV block). Immunogenicity (ADAs) was observed in a subset of patients.
