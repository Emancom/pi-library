It is intended for use by healthcare professionals as a comprehensive reference for the clinical use of elacestrant.

# Editorial Product Information — Elacestrant (ORSERDU)

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** ORSERDU  
**Active Ingredient:** Elacestrant (available as elacestrant dihydrochloride)  
**Formulation:** Film-coated tablets (86 mg and 345 mg elacestrant).

## 2. Approved Indications
ORSERDU is indicated as a monotherapy for the treatment of postmenopausal women and adult men with estrogen receptor (ER)-positive, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative, advanced or metastatic breast cancer. 

Eligible patients must demonstrate an activating *ESR1* mutation—as determined by an approved companion diagnostic test—and have experienced disease progression following at least one line of endocrine therapy, which must have included a CDK 4/6 inhibitor.

## 3. Contraindications
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to elacestrant dihydrochloride or to any of the excipients contained within the tablet [TGA].
*   **Other:** The FDA indicates no specific contraindications for this product [FDA].

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Dyslipidemia
Patients treated with elacestrant may experience significant increases in serum lipids. In clinical trials, hypercholesterolemia and hypertriglyceridemia occurred in approximately 30% and 27% of patients, respectively. Lipid profiles should be established prior to treatment initiation and monitored periodically during therapy.

### Embryo-Fetal Toxicity
Based on its mechanism of action and animal data, elacestrant can cause fetal harm. Pregnant women should be apprised of the risks to the fetus. Females of reproductive potential and male patients with female partners of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for one week following the final dose.

### Thromboembolic Events
Thromboembolic events are frequently observed in patients with advanced malignancies. Such events have been reported in clinical studies of elacestrant and should be considered when prescribing to patients at elevated risk [TGA].

### Hepatic Impairment
Elacestrant is primarily metabolized by the liver. Use is not recommended in patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C). Caution and dose adjustments are required for patients with moderate impairment (Child-Pugh B).

## 5. Dosage and Administration
### Recommended Dosing
The standard dosage is **345 mg taken orally once daily**. Tablets should be swallowed whole (not crushed, chewed, or split) at approximately the same time each day.

### Administration with Food
ORSERDU should be administered with food (specifically a light meal [TGA]) to improve bioavailability and reduce the incidence of gastrointestinal side effects such as nausea and vomiting.

### Missed Doses and Vomiting
*   **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed by more than 6 hours, it should be skipped, and the regular schedule resumed the following day.
*   **Vomiting:** If vomiting occurs after administration, an additional dose should not be taken that day.

### Dose Modifications for Toxicity
If adverse reactions occur, the following reduction schedule is recommended:
1.  **First reduction:** 258 mg once daily (three 86 mg tablets).
2.  **Second reduction:** 172 mg once daily (two 86 mg tablets) [FDA].
    *   *Note: TGA guidelines suggest discontinuation if a patient cannot tolerate 258 mg daily [TGA].*

### Hepatic Impairment Adjustments
*   **Mild (Child-Pugh A):** No adjustment required.
*   **Moderate (Child-Pugh B):** Reduce dose to 258 mg once daily.
*   **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Avoid use.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The safety profile of elacestrant is characterized primarily by gastrointestinal and musculoskeletal symptoms, as well as laboratory abnormalities.

**Very Common Adverse Reactions (≥10%):**
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea (most frequent), vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, abdominal pain, dyspepsia.
*   **Musculoskeletal:** Back pain, arthralgia, musculoskeletal pain.
*   **General:** Fatigue, asthenia, headache, hot flush.
*   **Metabolic:** Decreased appetite, dyslipidemia (increased cholesterol and triglycerides).

**Common Laboratory Abnormalities:**
*   Increased AST and ALT (transaminases).
*   Increased creatinine.
*   Decreased hemoglobin (anemia).
*   Decreased sodium and potassium.

Serious adverse reactions occurred in approximately 12% of patients, including musculoskeletal pain and nausea. Fatal outcomes were rare (1.7%) and included cardiac arrest and septic shock [FDA].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Effect of Other Drugs on Elacestrant
*   **Strong/Moderate CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Co-administration significantly increases elacestrant exposure, elevating the risk of toxicity. Concomitant use should be avoided. If a strong inhibitor is mandatory, reduce the ORSERDU dose to 86 mg; if a moderate inhibitor is used, reduce to 172 mg [TGA].
*   **Strong/Moderate CYP3A4 Inducers:** These agents significantly decrease elacestrant plasma concentrations, potentially diminishing efficacy. Concomitant use should be avoided.

### Effect of Elacestrant on Other Drugs
*   **P-gp and BCRP Substrates:** Elacestrant is an inhibitor of P-glycoprotein (P-gp) and Breast Cancer Resistance Protein (BCRP). Monitor for increased toxicities of co-administered substrates (e.g., digoxin, rosuvastatin) and reduce their dosage according to their respective PI.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
**Category D [TGA].** There are no human data regarding elacestrant use during pregnancy. Animal studies have shown embryo-fetal lethality and structural malformations. Treatment is not recommended during pregnancy or in women of childbearing potential not using contraception.

### Breastfeeding
It is unknown if elacestrant or its metabolites are excreted in human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in the breastfed infant, patients should be advised not to breastfeed during treatment and for one week after the final dose.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Oral bioavailability is approximately 10%. Peak plasma concentration ($T_{max}$) is reached within 1–4 hours. 
*   **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (>99%), independent of concentration. It penetrates the blood-brain barrier.
*   **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized via the CYP3A4 pathway, with minor contributions from CYP2A6 and CYP2C9.
*   **Elimination:** The elimination half-life is approximately 30 to 50 hours. Excretion is primarily fecal (82%), with minimal renal elimination (7.5%).

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Elacestrant is a non-steroidal selective estrogen receptor modulator/degrader (SERM/SERD). It binds to estrogen receptor-alpha ($ER\alpha$) with high affinity, acting as an antagonist that inhibits $17\beta$-estradiol-mediated cell proliferation. Furthermore, elacestrant promotes the degradation of the ER protein via the proteasomal pathway. It has demonstrated potent antitumor activity in models resistant to fulvestrant and CDK 4/6 inhibitors, specifically those harboring *ESR1* mutations.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **TGA Recommendation:** Store below 30°C [TGA].
*   **FDA Recommendation:** Store at 20°C to 25°C; excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C [FDA].
*   Keep the medication in its original container to protect from moisture and keep out of reach of children.
