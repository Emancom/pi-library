# Originality Audit Report: elacestrant

## Audit Summary
- **Originality Score:** 92/100
- **Verbatim Copied Passages Found:** 0
- **Overall Assessment:** Pass

## Verbatim Copied Passages
No verbatim copying detected. While the editorial uses specific medical terminology and numerical data found in the sources (e.g., "345 mg taken orally once daily," "30% and 27%"), the sentence structures and contextual framing are original to the editorial.

## Close Paraphrases
The following sections are close paraphrases necessitated by the technical nature of the content:

1.  **Contraindications (Hypersensitivity):** 
    - **Editorial:** "Known hypersensitivity to elacestrant dihydrochloride or to any of the excipients contained within the tablet [TGA]."
    - **Source (Doc 1 - TGA, p. 3):** "Hypersensitivity to elacestrant dihydrochloride or to any of the excipients listed in Section 6.1..."
    - **Note:** This is a standard regulatory statement; the editorial slightly rewords it for flow.

2.  **Mechanism of Action:**
    - **Editorial:** "...inhibits $17\beta$-estradiol-mediated cell proliferation. Furthermore, elacestrant promotes the degradation of the ER protein via the proteasomal pathway."
    - **Source (Doc 2 - FDA, p. 10):** "...inhibited 17β-estradiol mediated cell proliferation at concentrations inducing degradation of ERα protein mediated through proteasomal pathway."
    - **Note:** The phrasing of the biological mechanism is very similar, though the editorial splits the sentence and uses "furthermore" to improve readability.

3.  **Serious Adverse Reactions:**
    - **Editorial:** "Serious adverse reactions occurred in approximately 12% of patients... Fatal outcomes were rare (1.7%) and included cardiac arrest and septic shock [FDA]."
    - **Source (Doc 2 - FDA, p. 5):** "Serious adverse reactions occurred in 12% of patients... Fatal adverse reactions occurred in 1.7% of patients... including cardiac arrest, septic shock..."
    - **Note:** This is a concise summary of the clinical trial statistics provided in the FDA document.

## Originality Strengths
- **Harmonization:** The editorial performs an excellent job of citing which specific regulatory body (TGA vs. FDA) provides a piece of information, particularly where they differ (e.g., Section 11 Storage Conditions and Section 5 Dose Modifications).
- **Synthesis:** The "Adverse Effects" section (Section 6) successfully merges the "Summary of safety profile" from the TGA document with the specific trial results and percentages from the FDA document into a unified, easy-to-read list.
- **Structural Clarity:** The document is reorganized into a logical clinical reference format that does not strictly follow the numbering or layout of either source PI.
- **Narrative Voice:** The "Editorial Note" and the introductory sentences for each section are entirely original writing.

## Differences from Source PIs
- **Dose Modification Guidance:** The editorial highlights a key regulatory difference: the TGA recommends discontinuation if a patient cannot tolerate 258 mg, while the FDA provides a protocol for a second reduction to 172 mg.
- **Categorization:** The editorial uses TGA's "Pregnancy Category D" which is absent from the FDA labeling (as the US moved away from letter categories).
- **Thromboembolic Events:** The editorial includes a warning for thromboembolic events citing TGA, a section that is less emphasized in the "Highlights" of the FDA document.
- **Simplified Instructions:** The editorial simplifies complex PK data (Section 9) into a high-level summary suitable for a clinical overview, omitting many of the specific geometric mean values while retaining the clinically relevant ranges (e.g., half-life of 30-50 hours).

## Recommendation
The editorial **PASSES** the originality review. It is a high-quality synthesis that provides original value by comparing and contrasting two different regulatory jurisdictions. No revisions for originality are required.
