# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource only. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the United States (FDA) documentation was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism which result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - Dosing frequency should be every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    - Subsequent daily doses typically remain in the range of 50 mg/kg, though higher doses (up to 300 mg/kg) have been administered [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - Administration should occur as a slow bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - Therapy initiation may be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations (normal range: 40–50 µmol/L).
    - Dose adjustments (e.g., downward to 5 mg/kg) should be guided by trough levels and may be implemented as early as the third or fourth week of treatment [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].

### Monitoring
- For metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring of plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L), blood chemistries, and vital signs is recommended [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known:** There are no listed contraindications for levocarnitine injection in the provided source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in patients with ESRD on dialysis. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate risks versus benefits before re-administration [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been reported [US].
- **Oral Formulation Safety in Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. High doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- **INR Monitoring:** Increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) has been observed when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. Monitor INR levels following treatment initiation or dose adjustments [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disorders.

### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
The following events occurred at a frequency of ≥5% in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials [US]:

| Body System | Adverse Event (Incidence ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), headache (22%), infection (15%), abdominal pain (9%), fever (7%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), chest pain (12%), palpitations (5%), tachycardia (6%) |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), anorexia (5%), dyspepsia (5%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%) |
| **Neurological** | Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), paresthesia (5%), depression (5%) |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%), hypervolemia (5%) |
| **Musculoskeletal**| Leg cramps (4%) |
| **Skin** | Pruritus (5%), rash (3%) |

### Serious and Notable Adverse Effects
- **Seizures:** Reported in postmarketing experience.
- **Serious Allergic Reactions:** Anaphylaxis and laryngeal edema.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; body odor has been observed with less frequency [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Concomitant use may result in an increased INR. Healthcare professionals should monitor the INR after starting levocarnitine or changing the dose [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources. Levocarnitine is not protein-bound, suggesting a low potential for protein-binding displacement interactions [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly lettered in the provided text, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. 
    - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased carnitine concentrations in milk after administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders apply to paediatric patients; however, specific age-range safety profiles were not detailed beyond general metabolic crisis management [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in the provided source.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. Note that high-dose *oral* therapy is cautioned against in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This provides substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production in all tissues except the brain. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Levocarnitine clears toxic acyl-CoA compounds by forming acylcarnitine, which is rapidly excreted [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Study:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg IV three times per week showed that trough levocarnitine concentrations increased from a baseline of ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks of therapy [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses confirmed dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the effect on specific clinical outcomes in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
