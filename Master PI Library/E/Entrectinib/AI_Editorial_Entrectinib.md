# Editorial Product Information: Entrectinib

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical and regulatory data into a concise reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to the local approved labelling for definitive legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Entrectinib (a tyrosine kinase inhibitor).
- **Chemical Class:** Tropomyosin receptor tyrosine kinase (TRK), ROS1, and anaplastic lymphoma kinase (ALK) inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** Rozlytrek [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01EX14.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
  - Hard capsules: 100 mg (yellow body/cap, "ENT 100") and 200 mg (orange body/cap, "ENT 200").
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Roche Products Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **ROS1-positive Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC):** Treatment of adult patients with advanced NSCLC whose tumours are ROS1-positive [Australian-approved].
- *Editorial Note:* Prior indications regarding NTRK-positive solid tumours were noted in revision history but are not present in the current primary therapeutic indications of the source document provided.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dose:** 600 mg once daily.
- **Administration Instructions:** 
  - Capsules should be swallowed whole; do not open or dissolve.
  - May be taken with or without food.
  - ROS1-positive status must be confirmed via a validated assay prior to treatment.
- **Duration of Treatment:** Continue until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.
- **Dose Modifications (Adverse Reactions):** 
  - Reductions should occur in increments: First reduction to 400 mg daily; Second reduction to 200 mg daily. 
  - Permanently discontinue if 200 mg is not tolerated.
  - Specific protocols exist for withholding/reducing dose based on the severity of congestive heart failure, CNS effects, hepatotoxicity, hyperuricaemia, QTc prolongation, vision disorders, and haematologic toxicities (Anaemia/Neutropenia).
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, it may be taken unless the next scheduled dose is due within 12 hours. If vomiting occurs immediately after a dose, that dose may be repeated.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to entrectinib or any excipients (including lactose) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Congestive Heart Failure (CHF):** Reported across clinical trials. Assess LVEF prior to initiation in patients with symptoms or risk factors. Monitor for clinical signs (shortness of breath, oedema).
- **Central Nervous System (CNS) Effects:** Including cognitive impairment, mood disorders, dizziness, and sleep disturbances. Caution patients against driving or operating machinery if CNS symptoms occur.
- **Skeletal Fractures:** Risk is increased; prompt evaluation of signs like pain or deformity is required.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Monitor liver function tests (ALT/AST) every 2 weeks for the first month, then monthly.
- **Hyperuricaemia:** Assess serum urate before and during treatment. Initiate urate-lowering therapy as clinically indicated.
- **QTc Interval Prolongation:** Monitor patients at high risk (congenital long QT, bradyarrhythmias, heart failure). Assess QT and electrolytes at baseline and periodically.
- **Vision Disorders:** Reported in 21% of patients. Withhold or reduce dose if new visual changes interfere with daily living.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Strong inhibitors (e.g., itraconazole) significantly increase entrectinib exposure (6-fold AUC increase). Avoid concomitant use; if unavoidable, reduce entrectinib to 100 mg daily. For moderate inhibitors, reduce to 200 mg daily.
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Strong inducers (e.g., rifampicin) reduce entrectinib exposure by 77%. Avoid concomitant use.
- **Gastric pH-increasing agents:** PPIs (e.g., lansoprazole) decrease entrectinib AUC by 25%.
- **Effect of Entrectinib on other drugs:** 
  - **CYP3A substrates:** Entrectinib is a weak inhibitor (increases midazolam AUC by 50%).
  - **P-gp substrates:** May increase concentrations (increases digoxin Cmax by 28%).
- **Grapefruit products:** Avoid during treatment.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category D]. Entrectinib may cause embryo-fetal harm based on mechanism and animal data (malformations observed). Verify pregnancy status in females of reproductive potential before initiation.
- **Contraception:** Females should use highly effective methods during treatment and for 5 weeks post-dose. Males with female partners of reproductive potential should use contraception during treatment and for 3 months post-dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety not established. Advise against breastfeeding during treatment and for 7 days after the final dose.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in ROS1-positive NSCLC patients <18 years not established. (Note: Fractures were significantly more frequent in paediatric populations in expanded safety data).
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety/efficacy observed between patients ≥65 and younger patients, though data are limited for patients ≥75.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild-to-moderate impairment. Not studied in severe impairment, though renal excretion is negligible.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild (Child-Pugh A), moderate (Child-Pugh B), or severe (Child-Pugh C) impairment. Monitor patients with severe impairment closely.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits TRKA, TRKB, TRKC, ROS1, and ALK kinases. These kinases, when fused to other proteins, hyperactivate downstream pathways leading to cell proliferation. Entrectinib crosses the blood-brain barrier (brain-to-plasma ratios 0.4–2.2 in animals).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Tmax 4–6 hours. No significant food effect.
  - **Distribution:** >99% plasma protein bound.
  - **Metabolism:** Predominantly via CYP3A4 (~76%). Active metabolite M5 is the only major circulating metabolite (40% of parent exposure).
  - **Elimination:** Excreted mainly in faeces (83%). Half-life: ~20 hours (parent), ~40 hours (M5).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ROS1-positive NSCLC:** Efficacy based on a pooled subgroup from ALKA, STARTRK-1, and STARTRK-2 (n=92). 
- **Primary Outcomes:** Overall Response Rate (ORR) was 74% (95% CI: 64, 83). Complete response (CR) was 15%. 
- **CNS Response:** In patients with measurable CNS metastases at baseline (n=10), 7 responded to treatment.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 30°C.
- Keep the bottle tightly closed and in the original container to protect from moisture.
