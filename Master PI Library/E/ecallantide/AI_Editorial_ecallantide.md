# Editorial Product Information: Ecallantide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial product information has been developed using the official Product Information (PI) source from the **United States (FDA)**.
- **Note:** At the time of this publication, only the United States PI was available for synthesis. Information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian, Japanese) is not specified in the available source document.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ecallantide (a 60-amino-acid recombinant protein human plasma kallikrein inhibitor)
- **Brand Name:** KALBITOR [US]
- **ATC Code:** B06AC03
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Solution for injection; 10 mg/mL in a single-dose glass vial [US]
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous injection
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Takeda Pharmaceuticals U.S.A., Inc. [US]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hereditary Angioedema (HAE):** Treatment of acute attacks of HAE in patients 12 years of age and older [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric $\ge$ 12 years)
- **Recommended Dose:** 30 mg (3 mL) administered subcutaneously.
- **Repeat Dosing:** If the HAE attack persists, an additional 30 mg dose may be administered within a 24-hour period [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **HCP Administration Only:** Due to the risk of anaphylaxis, ecallantide must only be administered by a healthcare professional with appropriate medical support to manage anaphylaxis and HAE.
- **Preparation:** Vials should be refrigerated and protected from light. The solution should be clear and colorless.
- **Procedure:** 
    - Withdraw 1 mL (10 mg) from each of three vials using a large-bore needle.
    - Change to a 27-gauge needle for injection.
    - Administer as three separate 1 mL subcutaneous injections into the abdomen, thigh, or upper arm.
    - Injection sites should be separated by at least 2 inches (5 cm) and kept away from the anatomical site of the HAE attack.
    - Site rotation is not required for the three injections within a single dose [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Do not administer to patients with a known clinical hypersensitivity to ecallantide [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning: Anaphylaxis [US]
- **Risk Summary:** Anaphylaxis has been reported in 4% of patients treated with ecallantide.
- **Management:** Administration must occur in a clinical setting equipped to manage both anaphylaxis and HAE.
- **Clinical Mimicry:** Healthcare professionals should be aware that symptoms of hypersensitivity may overlap with acute HAE symptoms; close monitoring is required.

### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** In addition to anaphylaxis, potentially serious hypersensitivity reactions may occur. Observed symptoms include chest discomfort, flushing, pharyngeal edema, pruritus, rhinorrhea, wheezing, and hypotension. Most reactions occur within the first hour of dosing [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions observed in $\ge$3% of patients and at a higher rate than placebo in controlled trials:

| Adverse Reaction | Ecallantide (N=100) | Placebo (N=81) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Headache | 8% | 7% |
| Nausea | 5% | 1% |
| Diarrhea | 4% | 4% |
| Pyrexia | 4% | 0% |
| Injection site reactions* | 3% | 1% |
| Nasopharyngitis | 3% | 0% |

*\*Includes local pruritus, erythema, pain, irritation, urticaria, and bruising.*

**Serious Adverse Effects:**
- **Anaphylaxis:** Reported in 3–4% of patients [US].
- **Immunogenicity:** 20.2% of patients developed anti-ecallantide antibodies. Anti-ecallantide IgE antibodies were detected in 4.7% of tested patients, which may increase the risk of hypersensitivity [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Dynamic Interactions:** No formal drug-drug interaction studies have been performed. No *in vitro* metabolism studies have been conducted [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned (Current US labeling uses a Risk Summary). 
    - **Data:** Pharmacovigilance data has not identified a drug-associated risk of major birth defects or miscarriage. However, animal studies (rats) using intravenous doses 1.6 times the MRHD showed increased early fetal deaths in the presence of maternal toxicity. No structural abnormalities were observed in rats or rabbits [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on the presence of ecallantide in human milk or its effects on the breastfed infant. The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be considered alongside the mother's clinical need and potential risks [US].
- **Fertility:** In animal studies, ecallantide had no effects on fertility or reproductive performance in rats at doses up to 2.7 times the MRHD [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established in patients 12 to 17 years of age. Efficacy is extrapolated from adult data with support from population PK analyses. Safety and effectiveness in children under 12 have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65 and over to determine if they respond differently. Dosing should be cautious, typically starting at the low end of the range [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No pharmacokinetic data are available for these populations. Ecallantide is a small protein, and renal elimination has been demonstrated in urine [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Ecallantide is a potent, selective, and reversible inhibitor of plasma kallikrein. It binds to plasma kallikrein and blocks the conversion of high molecular weight (HMW) kininogen to bradykinin, a vasodilator responsible for the inflammation and pain associated with HAE [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Prolongation of activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT) has been observed at IV doses $\ge$ 20 mg/m². No significant QT prolongation has been observed at the 30 mg SC dose [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Mean $C_{max}$ of 586 ng/mL reached in 2–3 hours post-SC dose.
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is approximately 26.4 L.
    - **Elimination:** Mean elimination half-life is 2.0 $\pm$ 0.5 hours. Plasma clearance is 153 mL/min [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** EDEMA3 and EDEMA4 (randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled).
- **Design:** 143 unique patients with HAE received 30 mg SC or placebo for acute attacks.
- **Outcomes:** In both trials, ecallantide-treated patients showed significantly greater improvement in the Mean Symptom Complex Severity (MSCS) score and Treatment Outcome Score (TOS) at 4 hours compared to placebo. Fewer ecallantide patients required medical intervention for unresolved symptoms within 24 hours (14–33%) compared to placebo (36–50%) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Refrigeration:** Must be stored at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F).
- **Room Temperature:** Vials removed from refrigeration may be stored below 30°C (86°F) and must be used within 14 days or returned to refrigeration.
- **Handling:** Protect from light. Do not freeze [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription only (Rx Only).
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available source.
