# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **United States (FDA)** for Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation).
- **Note on Jurisdictional Scope:** While this document follows the harmonisation framework, the primary source material provided was limited to the **United States (FDA)** regulatory filing. Comparable documents from other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian, Japanese) were not provided for this specific synthesis. Where information is absent across all sources, it is marked as "Not specified in available sources."
- This document serves as a harmonised clinical overview for healthcare professionals and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. 
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [United States].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) sterile solution in single-dose vials [United States].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Metabolic):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [United States].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Initial/Loading Dose:** 50 mg/kg given as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Maintenance Dose:** In patients with severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Administer every 3 or 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours).
- **Subsequent Daily Dosing:** Typically 50 mg/kg/day, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg/day may be required.
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Monitor weekly/monthly; target plasma free carnitine concentrations between 35 and 60 µmol/L [United States].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow (2 to 3 minute) bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
- **Adjustments:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels <40 to 50 µmol/L. Downward dose adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations [United States].

### General Instructions
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Visual Inspection:** Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration. Discard unused portions as the product contains no preservatives.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** Not explicitly listed as a contraindication in the source, but warnings are provided regarding serious reactions.
- **General:** None known [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [United States].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, occasionally within minutes of intravenous administration. These have been reported primarily in ESRD patients. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [United States].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [United States].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Levocarnitine Caution):** While the injection is indicated for ESRD, high doses of **oral** levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (>10%):** 
    - **General:** Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (21–35%), headache (3–37%), chest pain (6–15%), flu syndrome (15–29%).
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%), abdominal pain (5–21%).
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%).
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%).
    - **Nervous System:** Dizziness (10–18%).
- **Common (1–10%):** Fever, asthenia, back pain, infection, arrhythmia, tachycardia, anemia, hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, peripheral edema, weight changes, leg cramps, paresthesia, pruritus, and rhinitis [United States].
- **Rare/Uncommon (<1%):** Not specified in frequency tables, but gastritis and body odor are noted as "less frequent" [United States].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm, and seizures [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. 
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels frequently after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Classification:** [Category not lettered, but text-based summary provided]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm.
    - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased milk concentrations of levocarnitine following administration.
    - **Management:** Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [United States].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg); see Section 3. Secondary deficiency in newborns and premature infants is defined by plasma concentrations below age-related normals [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed; clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65+ to determine if they respond differently than younger subjects [United States].
- **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. However, chronic high-dose **oral** therapy should be avoided in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [United States].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [United States].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Administration to ESRD patients results in increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Study (9-week):** 12 patients on hemodialysis received 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly. Mean pre-dialysis (trough) levels rose from ~21 µmol/L (baseline) to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Evaluated 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses. At 24 weeks, mean trough concentrations were 148, 240, and 456 µmol/L, respectively. 
- **Clinical Outcome Note:** While IV levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the effect on specific signs, symptoms, or clinical outcomes in this population has not been determined [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C.
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [United States].
