# Editorial Product Information: empagliflozin + linagliptin

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **empagliflozin + linagliptin**
- Chemical class:
  - Empagliflozin: sodium‑glucose co‑transporter‑2 (SGLT2) inhibitor
  - Linagliptin: dipeptidyl peptidase‑4 (DPP‑4) inhibitor
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources.
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources.
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycaemic control in adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus when combined treatment with both empagliflozin and linagliptin is appropriate.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General adult dosing
- Recommended starting dose: empagliflozin 10 mg + linagliptin 5 mg once daily.
- Patients requiring additional glycaemic control and tolerating the starting dose may be increased to empagliflozin 25 mg + linagliptin 5 mg once daily.
- For patients already receiving empagliflozin as monotherapy at a given empagliflozin dose, the combination product should provide the empagliflozin dose equivalent to that already being taken.

### Administration instructions
- Can be taken with or without food and at any time of day.

### Titration / switching
- Dose escalation from 10 mg/5 mg to 25 mg/5 mg applies only to the empagliflozin component when additional glycaemic control is required and is tolerated.
- When switching from empagliflozin monotherapy, select the combination strength that matches the empagliflozin dose already used by the patient.

### Concomitant therapy considerations
- When used with insulin or a sulfonylurea, consider reducing the dose of insulin or sulfonylurea to reduce the risk of hypoglycaemia.

### Renal impairment and dosing
- Glycaemic efficacy is reduced in patients with markedly reduced renal function.
- No dose adjustment is required for patients with estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) ≥ 30 mL/min/1.73 m2.
- Contraindicated in patients with eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m2 (see Section 5).
- Therapeutic experience is limited in patients with eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73 m2.

### Elderly
- No routine dose adjustment based on age alone.
- Initiation in patients ≥ 75 years is not recommended and therapy in this population should be prescribed with caution.

### Paediatric population
- Safety and effectiveness in patients < 18 years have not been established; not recommended.

### Missed dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to empagliflozin or linagliptin or to any excipient.
- Severe renal impairment (eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m2), end‑stage renal disease, or patients on dialysis.
- Other contraindications specifically listed in source documents: Not specified beyond the above items.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed / highlight warnings
- No boxed warning statement specified in available sources. Critical safety concerns are described below.

### Key safety issues and clinical precautions
- **Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)**: Cases of DKA, including fatal cases, have been reported with SGLT2 inhibitors including empagliflozin. DKA may occur with only moderately elevated or even normal blood glucose. Assess for ketoacidosis in patients presenting with signs or symptoms of metabolic acidosis. If suspected, discontinue **empagliflozin + linagliptin**, evaluate and institute prompt treatment (insulin, fluids, potassium and carbohydrate as appropriate). Ketoacidosis and glucosuria may persist beyond expected time after stopping empagliflozin; consider monitoring for ketones even after treatment interruption. Restarting an SGLT2 inhibitor in a patient who experienced DKA while on an SGLT2 inhibitor is not recommended unless a clear and resolved precipitating cause is identified.
  - Predisposing factors: low carbohydrate diet, dehydration, acute illness, surgery, prior DKA, insulin deficiency or insulin pump failure, history of pancreatitis or pancreatic surgery, malnutrition/reduced caloric intake, infection, alcohol abuse, and situations causing increased insulin requirements.

- General diabetes type considerations: Should not be used in patients with type 1 diabetes (use in type 1 diabetes is not appropriate).

- Surgery: Temporarily discontinue prior to major surgery or procedures associated with prolonged fasting. Patients scheduled for non‑urgent surgery who have not ceased empagliflozin should be assessed and consideration given to postponing procedures. Therapy may be restarted once the patient's condition stabilises and oral intake is normal.

- Hypoglycaemia: When combined with insulin or insulin secretagogues (e.g., sulfonylureas), risk of hypoglycaemia is increased; consider dose reduction of the insulin or secretagogue.

- Pancreatitis: Acute pancreatitis has been observed in patients taking linagliptin. If pancreatitis is suspected, discontinue therapy and investigate promptly.

- Volume depletion and hypotension: Empagliflozin may cause osmotic diuresis and modest blood pressure reductions. Exercise caution in patients at risk (e.g., concomitant antihypertensive therapy, known cardiovascular disease, elderly ≥ 75 years). In conditions causing fluid loss (e.g., gastrointestinal illness), monitor volume status and electrolytes and consider temporary interruption until corrected.

- Urinary tract infections and serious urosepsis/pyelonephritis: SGLT2 inhibitor therapy increases risk of urinary tract infections; serious cases including urosepsis and pyelonephritis requiring hospitalisation have been reported. Evaluate and treat promptly; consider discontinuation for recurrent infections.

- Genital infections and necrotising fasciitis (Fournier’s gangrene): Cases of severe genital infections including necrotising fasciitis of the perineum have been reported with SGLT2 inhibitors. Patients presenting with genital/perineal pain, erythema, swelling, fever or malaise should be evaluated; if suspected, discontinue therapy and institute urgent treatment (including broad‑spectrum antibiotics and surgical debridement when necessary).

- Lower limb amputations: An increased risk of lower limb amputation was observed with another SGLT2 inhibitor in a long‑term study. It is unknown whether this is a class effect. In pooled and large trials with empagliflozin, overall frequencies of lower limb amputation were similar between empagliflozin and placebo; in one large trial, amputation rates were 1.8% (empagliflozin 10 mg), 2.0% (empagliflozin 25 mg), and 1.8% (placebo). Regular foot examination and advice on routine foot care for all diabetic patients is important.

- Bullous pemphigoid: Cases have been observed with linagliptin; discontinue therapy if suspected.

- Arthralgia: Severe joint pain has been reported with DPP‑4 inhibitors; consider discontinuation if new or worsening joint symptoms occur.

- Combination with GLP‑1 receptor agonists: Linagliptin has not been studied in combination with GLP‑1 analogues; safety and efficacy of such combinations are not established.

- Renal function effects and acute kidney injury (AKI): Empagliflozin may cause an initial fall in eGFR and increases in serum creatinine. AKI, in some cases requiring hospitalisation and dialysis, has been reported. Patients with hypovolaemia may be more susceptible. Use with caution and monitor renal function.

### Monitoring requirements
- Assess renal function prior to initiation and periodically thereafter (at least yearly). More intensive monitoring is recommended for patients with eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73 m2, particularly if < 45 mL/min/1.73 m2, and following initiation.
- Discontinue therapy when eGFR is persistently < 30 mL/min/1.73 m2 or CrCl < 30 mL/min.
- Monitor for signs and symptoms of ketoacidosis and consider ketone monitoring in at‑risk situations.
- Monitor volume status, blood pressure, electrolytes and haematocrit as clinically indicated.
- Monitor serum lithium more frequently when co‑administered with lithium (see Section 8).

### Laboratory test effects
- Urine glucose will be positive while taking the product due to urinary glucose excretion.
- 1,5‑anhydroglucitol (1,5‑AG) assays are unreliable in patients on SGLT2 inhibitors; do not use 1,5‑AG to monitor glycaemic control.
- Increased haematocrit has been observed; investigate marked elevations for underlying haematological disease.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### General
- No pharmacokinetic interactions between empagliflozin and linagliptin were observed in clinical studies of the fixed‑dose combination.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Insulin and sulfonylureas: increase risk of hypoglycaemia; consider lowering the dose of insulin or sulfonylurea.
- Diuretics (thiazide and loop diuretics): additive diuretic effect may increase risk of dehydration and hypotension; monitor volume status.

### Pharmacokinetic interactions and specific medicines
- Empagliflozin metabolism: primarily via UDP‑glucuronosyltransferase (UGT) pathways; clinically relevant effects from UGT inhibitors are not expected per available data.
- Lithium: empagliflozin may increase renal lithium excretion and reduce blood lithium levels; monitor serum lithium more frequently after initiation and dose changes of empagliflozin and coordinate with the lithium prescriber.
- Rifampicin (strong P‑glycoprotein and CYP3A4 inducer): Co‑administration decreased linagliptin steady‑state AUC and Cmax (~39.6% and 43.8% respectively) and reduced trough DPP‑4 inhibition by about 30%; full efficacy of linagliptin may not be achieved with strong P‑gp inducers.
- Ritonavir (P‑gp and CYP3A4 inhibitor): Multiple doses of ritonavir increased linagliptin AUC ≈ 2‑fold and Cmax ≈ 3‑fold after a single linagliptin dose; simulations indicate no clinically relevant accumulation at steady state; dose adjustment not required with ritonavir or other P‑gp/CYP3A4 inhibitors per available data.
- No clinically meaningful interactions were observed between empagliflozin or linagliptin and many commonly used medicinal products in available studies; specific other interactions not detailed in provided sources.

### Management notes
- Monitor for hypoglycaemia when combined with insulin or insulin secretagogues and reduce doses as needed.
- Monitor renal function and volume status when given with diuretics or in conditions predisposed to volume depletion.
- Monitor serum lithium concentrations closely when co‑administered.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Pregnancy category: **Category D** [Australian].
- Limited human data available; use during pregnancy is not recommended unless clearly needed.
- Animal data:
  - Co‑administration at very high doses produced decreased fetal weight and increased minor skeletal abnormalities in rats at maternally toxic doses.
  - Empagliflozin alone: non‑teratogenic in rats and rabbits at certain high multiples of human exposures; maternally toxic doses caused fetal skeletal malformations and increased embryofetal loss at very high exposures. Developmental renal toxicity observed with other SGLT2 inhibitors in animals in periods corresponding to second/third trimesters of human pregnancy; similar changes were seen with empagliflozin at high exposures and were reversible after recovery.
  - Linagliptin: crosses the placenta in rats and rabbits; not teratogenic in animal studies at very high multiples of human exposure but increases in post‑implantation loss and some fetal variation occurred at higher doses associated with maternotoxicity.

### Breastfeeding
- No human data on excretion into human milk.
- Empagliflozin and linagliptin and/or metabolites are excreted into animal milk; effects in nursing infants are unknown.
- Recommendation: discontinue breastfeeding during treatment with **empagliflozin + linagliptin**.

### Fertility
- No dedicated human fertility studies available.
- Animal fertility studies did not indicate adverse effects attributable to empagliflozin or linagliptin at exposures many times the human clinical exposures.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Safety and efficacy in patients < 18 years have not been established; not recommended.

### Elderly
- No routine dose adjustment required for age alone.
- Initiation in patients ≥ 75 years is not recommended; use caution in this population due to increased risk of volume depletion.

### Renal impairment
- Contraindicated in patients with eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m2, end‑stage renal disease and patients on dialysis.
- No dose adjustment required for patients with eGFR ≥ 30 mL/min/1.73 m2, but glycaemic efficacy is reduced as renal function declines.
- Therapeutic experience limited in patients with eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73 m2.
- Monitor renal function prior to initiation and periodically thereafter (at least yearly; more frequent monitoring if eGFR < 60, particularly if < 45).

### Hepatic impairment
- No dose adjustment recommended for patients with hepatic impairment per available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Empagliflozin: SGLT2 inhibition in the proximal renal tubule reduces renal glucose reabsorption, increasing urinary glucose excretion and producing osmotic diuresis.
- Linagliptin: DPP‑4 inhibition increases endogenous incretin levels (GLP‑1, GIP), enhancing glucose‑dependent insulin secretion and reducing glucagon secretion.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: Not specified in available sources (numerical parameters not provided).
- Distribution: Not specified in available sources.
- Metabolism:
  - Empagliflozin: primarily metabolised via UGT pathways.
  - Linagliptin: substrate for P‑glycoprotein; CYP3A4 involvement noted in interaction studies with modulators.
- Elimination:
  - Specific clearance rates and half‑life values are not specified in available sources (half‑life referenced qualitatively in relation to persistence of glucosuria/DKA after discontinuation; numerical value not provided).
- Pharmacodynamic effects:
  - Produces urinary glucose excretion (urine glucose positive on testing).
  - Modest reductions in blood pressure due to osmotic diuresis.
  - Initial declines in eGFR and increases in serum creatinine have been observed; increased haematocrit has been reported.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Clinical safety program for the fixed‑dose combination included 2,173 patients with type 2 diabetes, of whom 1,005 received the combination product; treatment durations were up to 24 or 52 weeks. The most frequently reported adverse reaction in these clinical trials was urinary tract infection.
- EMPA‑REG OUTCOME trial (empagliflozin monotherapy evaluation): 7,020 patients enrolled; in that trial lower limb amputation rates were reported as 1.8% (empagliflozin 10 mg), 2.0% (empagliflozin 25 mg) and 1.8% (placebo) in the reported subgroup of cases.
- Pooled safety analysis across studies involving empagliflozin included 12,620 patients with type 2 diabetes and found similar frequencies of lower limb amputations between empagliflozin and placebo groups.
- Additional detailed efficacy and endpoint data (e.g., HbA1c reductions, cardiovascular outcomes specific to the combination product, and full pivotal trial tables) are not specified in available sources.
