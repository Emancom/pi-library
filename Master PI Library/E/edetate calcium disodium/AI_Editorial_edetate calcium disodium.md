# Editorial Product Information: Edetate Calcium Disodium

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of edetate calcium disodium for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises the clinical and regulatory information available from the provided source.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Edetate calcium disodium (also known as Calcium EDTA or Calcium Disodium Ethylenediaminetetraacetate).
- **Chemical Class:** Chelating agent.
- **Brand Names:** Edetate Calcium Disodium Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** V03AB03.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
  - Sterile injectable solution: 200 mg/mL in 5 mL single-dose vials (1,000 mg/vial) [US].
- **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion or Intramuscular (IM) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Rising Pharma Holdings, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- Reduction of blood lead levels and depletion of depot stores in lead poisoning (both acute and chronic) [US].
- Treatment of lead encephalopathy [US].
- **Note:** Approved for use in both pediatric and adult populations [US]. Chelation therapy must be used in conjunction with measures to eliminate further lead exposure [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric)
- **Standard Dose:** 1,000 mg/m²/day [US].
- **Course of Therapy:** Administered daily for five days. Following a course, therapy should be interrupted for 2 to 4 days to permit lead redistribution and prevent the depletion of essential metals (e.g., zinc) [US].
- **Repeat Courses:** Typically, two courses are employed, though further treatment depends on the severity of lead toxicity and patient tolerance [US].

### Dosing in Lead Nephropathy
- For adults with pre-existing renal impairment (lead nephropathy), the following suggested schedules apply based on serum creatinine (SCr) [US]:
  - **SCr 2 to 3 mg/dL:** 500 mg/m² every 24 hours for 5 days.
  - **SCr 3 to 4 mg/dL:** 500 mg/m² every 48 hours for 3 doses.
  - **SCr > 4 mg/dL:** 500 mg/m² once weekly.
  - These regimens may be repeated at one-month intervals [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Intravenous (IV):** Dilute the total daily dose (1,000 mg/m²) in 250 to 500 mL of 5% dextrose or 0.9% sodium chloride. Infuse over 8 to 12 hours. Rapid infusion must be avoided in patients with encephalopathy [US].
- **Intramuscular (IM):** The total daily dose (1,000 mg/m²) should be divided into equal doses spaced 8 to 12 hours apart. Lidocaine or procaine (final concentration 0.5%) should be added to the injection to reduce pain at the site [US]. 
- **Combination Therapy:** In patients with blood lead levels > 70 mcg/dL or those with clinical symptoms of poisoning, edetate calcium disodium should be used in conjunction with BAL (dimercaprol) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Anuria [US].
- Active renal disease [US].
- Hepatitis [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning [US]
- **Fatal Toxicity:** Edetate calcium disodium is capable of producing fatal toxic effects.
- **Encephalopathy Risks:** Patients with lead encephalopathy and cerebral edema may suffer a lethal increase in intracranial pressure during IV infusion; the IM route is preferred for these patients.
- **Infusion Rate:** If IV administration is necessary, rapid infusion must be avoided.
- **Dose Adherence:** The recommended daily dose must never be exceeded.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Nephrotoxicity:** Dose-dependent renal damage may occur, including hydropic degeneration of the proximal tubules. Adequate diuresis should be established before beginning therapy [US].
- **Monitoring:** Urine flow must be monitored; therapy must be discontinued if anuria or severe oliguria develops. Patients should be monitored for cardiac rhythm irregularities during IV therapy [US].
- **Lead Redistribution:** Edetate calcium disodium may initially increase brain lead levels due to redistribution before excretion occurs [US].

### Laboratory Tests
- Prior to and during therapy, monitor: urinalysis, renal function, hepatic function, and serum electrolytes [US].
- **Renal Signs:** Discontinue therapy at the first sign of renal toxicity (e.g., large renal epithelial cells, proteinuria, or microscopic hematuria) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are listed by system; frequencies are not specified in available sources unless noted [US].

- **Renal/Urinary:** Acute necrosis of proximal tubules (potentially fatal nephrosis), proteinuria, microscopic hematuria, glycosuria, and presence of renal epithelial cells in sediment.
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypotension, cardiac rhythm irregularities.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, anorexia, excessive thirst, and cheilosis.
- **Hepatic:** Common mild increases in SGOT (AST) and SGPT (ALT), usually returning to normal within 48 hours post-therapy.
- **Nervous System:** Tremors, headache, numbness, tingling.
- **Body as a Whole:** Pain at the IM injection site, fever, chills, malaise, fatigue, myalgia, and arthralgia.
- **Immunogenic:** Histamine-like reactions (sneezing, lacrimation, nasal congestion) and rash.
- **Hematopoietic/Metabolic:** Transient bone marrow depression, anemia, zinc deficiency, and hypercalcemia.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Steroids:** Animal studies suggest steroids may enhance the renal toxicity of edetate calcium disodium [US].
- **Zinc Insulin:** Edetate calcium disodium interferes with the action of zinc insulin preparations by chelating the zinc component [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified by letter, but label states "use only if clearly needed"]. Animal studies in rats showed no harm at 13x the human dose, but malformations occurred at 25–40x the human dose (preventable by zinc supplementation) [US]. There are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if the drug is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution when administering to nursing women [US].
- **Fertility:** Long-term animal studies to evaluate effects on fertility have not been conducted [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Used in patients of all ages. IM administration is preferred by some for young pediatric patients. Mortality rates for lead encephalopathy have historically been high in this population [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed; however, since the drug is renally excreted, caution is required regarding renal function [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in active renal disease or anuria. Reduced doses are required for patients with pre-existing mild renal disease [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with hepatitis [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Chelates divalent and trivalent metals (notably lead, zinc, cadmium, and manganese) to form stable, soluble complexes excreted in the urine. It does not mobilise copper and does not effectively chelate mercury [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Therapy results in a significant increase in urinary lead excretion and a decrease in blood lead levels [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
  - **Absorption:** Poorly absorbed from the GI tract [US].
  - **Distribution:** Distributed primarily in extracellular fluid; does not penetrate cells. Only ~5% reaches the spinal fluid [US].
  - **Metabolism:** Almost no metabolism occurs [US].
  - **Elimination:** Excreted primarily by the kidney via glomerular filtration. Half-life is 20 to 60 minutes. Roughly 50% is excreted within one hour and >95% within 24 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
Specific pivotal trial data summaries were not provided in the source PI; however, the label references historical clinical literature regarding lead mobilization and treatment efficacy in pediatric and adult populations [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F).
- **Excursions:** Permitted between 15° to 30°C (59° to 86°F) [US].
- **Special Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].
