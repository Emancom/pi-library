# Editorial Product Information: Ertapenem

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from the following official Product Information (PI) source: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory and clinical data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local guidelines and the most current official PI for their region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ertapenem sodium (a 1-β methyl-carbapenem).
- **Brand Names:** Ertapenem-AFT [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J01DH03 (Noted as J01D HXX in [Australian] source).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Powder for Injection:** 1 g ertapenem (as sodium) per vial [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion or Intramuscular (IM) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AFT Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Ertapenem is indicated for the treatment of patients (aged 3 months or older) with moderate to severe infections caused by susceptible strains of microorganisms, particularly when proven or suspected to be resistant to other antibiotics, or when other antibiotics are not tolerated [Australian].

**Approved indications include:**
- **Complicated Intra-abdominal Infections:** Initial empiric therapy.
- **Acute Pelvic Infections:** Including postpartum endomyometritis, septic abortion, and post-surgical gynaecological infections.
- **Diabetic Foot Infections:** Requiring parenteral therapy, caused by susceptible pathogens (resistant to other antibiotics or where others are not tolerated).
- **Other Moderate to Severe Infections:** Except meningitis [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Adults and Adolescents (≥13 years):** 1 gram (g) once daily [Australian].
- **Paediatric Patients (3 months to 12 years):** 15 mg/kg twice daily, not to exceed a total dose of 1 g/day [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Intravenous Infusion:** Reconstitute 1 g vial with 10 mL of Water for Injections, 0.9% Sodium Chloride, or Bacteriostatic Water for Injections. Transfer to 50 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride. Infuse over 30 minutes. Do not use diluents containing dextrose [Australian].
- **Intramuscular Injection:** Reconstitute 1 g vial with 3.2 mL of 1.0% lidocaine (lignocaine) HCl (without adrenaline). Administer by deep IM injection into a large muscle mass. Must be used within 1 hour of reconstitution and **must not** be administered intravenously [Australian].

### Duration of Therapy
- Usually 3 to 14 days, depending on infection type and severity. Transition to an appropriate oral antimicrobial may occur once clinical improvement is observed [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **General:** Known hypersensitivity to ertapenem sodium or any other component of the product; known hypersensitivity to other carbapenems; history of anaphylactic reactions to beta-lactams [Australian].
- **IM-Specific:** Due to the use of lidocaine HCl as a diluent, IM administration is contraindicated in patients with known hypersensitivity to amide-type local anaesthetics, severe shock, or heart block [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity:** Serious and occasionally fatal anaphylactic reactions have been reported with beta-lactams. In case of an allergic reaction, the drug must be discontinued immediately.
- **Central Nervous System (CNS):** Seizures have occurred (0.5% of adult patients), most commonly in those with pre-existing CNS disorders (e.g., brain lesions, history of seizures) or compromised renal function. Dosage should be re-evaluated if focal tremors, myoclonus, or seizures occur.
- **Clostridium difficile-associated diarrhoea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all antibacterial agents; severity ranges from mild diarrhoea to fatal colitis.
- **Superinfection:** Prolonged use may result in the overgrowth of non-susceptible organisms.
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCAR):** Including Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), and DRESS have been reported. Discontinue therapy if SCAR is suspected.
- **Meningitis:** Not recommended for the treatment of meningitis due to insufficient CSF penetration [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Most adverse experiences reported in clinical trials were mild to moderate in severity.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Diarrhoea (4.3%), Infused vein complication (3.9%), Nausea (2.9%), Headache (2.1%), Phlebitis/thrombophlebitis (1.3%), Vomiting (1.0%), Rash (1.3% in children), Infusion site pain (5.5% in children) |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Candidiasis, hypotension, bradycardia, constipation, C. difficile-associated diarrhoea, dizziness, insomnia, somnolence, dyspnoea, pruritus, vaginal pruritus |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Seizure (0.2% in adults) |

**Post-Marketing Experience:** Anaphylaxis, altered mental status (agitation, delirium, disorientation), hallucinations, dyskinesia, encephalopathy (especially in renal impairment), and muscular weakness [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Probenecid:** Competes for active tubular secretion, increasing ertapenem half-life by 19% and AUC by 25%. Co-administration to extend half-life is not recommended [Australian].
- **Valproic Acid/Divalproex Sodium:** Carbapenems may reduce serum concentrations of valproic acid below the therapeutic range, increasing the risk of breakthrough seizures. This combination is generally not recommended; if necessary, supplemental anti-convulsant therapy should be considered [Australian].
- **Cytochrome P450:** Ertapenem does not inhibit metabolism mediated by the six major CYP isoforms (1A2, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 2E1, 3A4); drug interactions via this mechanism are unlikely [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** Ertapenem is excreted in human milk. Because of the potential for adverse effects on the infant, a decision should be made whether to discontinue nursing or discontinue the drug, taking into account the importance of the drug to the mother [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in rat studies at high doses [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for use from 3 months of age. No data are available for infants under 3 months [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients ≥65 years are comparable to younger patients; no dose adjustment is necessary [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - CrCl >30 mL/min/1.73 m²: No adjustment.
    - CrCl ≤30 mL/min/1.73 m² (including hemodialysis): 500 mg daily [Australian].
    - **Haemodialysis:** If the 500 mg daily dose is given within 6 hours prior to dialysis, a supplementary dose of 150 mg is recommended post-session [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is recommended due to limited hepatic metabolism [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal; inhibits cell wall synthesis by binding to penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs). It has a high affinity for PBPs 2 and 3 in *E. coli*.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Stable against hydrolysis by many beta-lactamases, including extended-spectrum beta-lactamases (ESBLs), but susceptible to metallo-beta-lactamases.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Bioavailability of IM injection is ~92%. Peak plasma concentration (Cmax) reached in ~2 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (~95% in healthy adults). Volume of distribution is ~8 L in adults.
    - **Metabolism:** The major metabolite is a ring-opened derivative formed by hydrolysis of the beta-lactam ring.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (80% recovered in urine, 38% as unchanged drug). Mean plasma half-life is ~4 hours in adults and ~2.5 hours in children [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Intra-abdominal Infections:** In a study of 665 adults, ertapenem (1 g IV daily) demonstrated a clinical success rate of 89.6% at 1–2 weeks post-therapy, compared to 82.7% for piperacillin/tazobactam.
- **Acute Pelvic Infections:** In a study of 412 patients, clinical success rates were 93.9% for ertapenem and 91.5% for piperacillin/tazobactam.
- **Diabetic Foot Infections:** In a study of 586 adults, clinical success rates at 10 days post-therapy were 87.4% for ertapenem and 82.7% for piperacillin/tazobactam [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials (Before Reconstitution):** Store below 25 °C [Australian].
- **Reconstituted/Diluted Solutions:**
    - **IV:** Use within 6 hours at room temperature or 24 hours if refrigerated (2 to 8 °C). Do not freeze.
    - **IM:** Use within 1 hour of reconstitution [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Prescription Only Medicine (S4) [Australian].
