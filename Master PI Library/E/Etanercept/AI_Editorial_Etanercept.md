# Editorial Product Information: Etanercept

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically accurate editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing within a particular region. While only the Australian source was provided for this synthesis, clinical practices may vary across other regions.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Etanercept, a dimeric soluble form of the p75 tumour necrosis factor (TNF) receptor produced by recombinant DNA technology in a Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) mammalian expression system.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Brenzys** (a biosimilar to Enbrel) [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L04AB01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Solution for injection in a single-use pre-filled syringe: 50 mg/1.0 mL [Australian].
    - Solution for injection in a single-use pre-filled Auto-injector: 50 mg/1.0 mL [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Samsung Bioepis AU Pty Ltd; Distributed by Organon Pharma Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Brenzys** is indicated for the treatment of the following conditions in adults (≥ 18 years):
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA):** Active disease in adults with an inadequate response to one or more disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs). It can be used alone or with methotrexate. It is also indicated for severe, active RA to slow structural damage progression in patients at high risk of erosive disease.
- **Psoriatic Arthritis (PsA):** Active and progressive disease in adults with an inadequate response to previous DMARD therapy. 
- **Ankylosing Spondylitis (AS):** Active disease in adults.
- **Non-radiographic Axial Spondyloarthritis (nr-AxSpA):** Active disease in adults with objective signs of inflammation (elevated CRP and/or MRI changes) who have had an inadequate response to NSAIDs.
- **Plaque Psoriasis:** Moderate to severe chronic plaque psoriasis in adults who are candidates for systemic therapy or phototherapy.

*Note: This specific brand is not indicated for paediatric use in Australia due to its 50 mg fixed-dose presentation.*

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults ≥ 18 years)
- **RA, PsA, AS, and nr-AxSpA:** The recommended dose is **50 mg once weekly** via subcutaneous injection [Australian].
- **Plaque Psoriasis:** The standard dose is **50 mg once weekly**. A higher initial response may be achieved by using 50 mg twice weekly (administered 3–4 days apart) for up to 12 weeks, followed by a reduction to 50 mg once weekly. Treatment should be discontinued if no significant PASI response is seen after 12 weeks [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Site Selection:** Thigh, abdomen, or upper arm. Rotate sites; new injections should be at least 3 cm from a previous site.
- **Preparation:** Allow the pre-filled syringe or Auto-injector to reach room temperature (approximately 30 minutes) before injecting. Do not remove the needle cover during this time.
- **Technique:** The solution should be clear and colourless-to-pale yellow. The first injection must be performed under the supervision of a qualified healthcare professional.

### Missed Dose
- Patients should administer a missed dose as soon as remembered, unless the next scheduled dose is the following day. In that case, the missed dose should be skipped. Do not double the dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **etanercept** or any excipients in the formulation.
- Patients with, or at risk of, sepsis.
- Initiation of treatment in patients with serious, active infections (including chronic or localised infections).
- **Concurrent treatment with Interleukin-1 (IL-1) antagonists (e.g., anakinra)** [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Infections:** TNF blockers are associated with an increased risk of serious and potentially fatal infections, including sepsis, tuberculosis (TB), and opportunistic infections (fungal, viral, bacterial). Patients must be evaluated for latent and active TB before initiation.
- **Hepatitis B Reactivation:** Reactivation has occurred in chronic carriers of HBV. Evaluate at-risk patients before starting therapy.
- **Malignancies:** Cases of lymphoma and other malignancies (including non-melanoma skin cancer and melanoma) have been reported. A higher rate of lymphoma has been observed compared to the general population, particularly in RA patients with highly active disease.
- **Neurological Events:** Rare cases of CNS demyelinating disorders (e.g., multiple sclerosis, optic neuritis, transverse myelitis) and peripheral demyelinating polyneuropathies (e.g., Guillain-Barré syndrome) have occurred.
- **Hematological Reactions:** Rare cases of pancytopenia and very rare aplastic anaemia (some fatal) have been reported. Discontinue therapy if blood dyscrasias are confirmed.
- **Congestive Heart Failure (CHF):** Use with caution in patients with pre-existing CHF; monitor closely for worsening symptoms.
- **Vaccinations:** Live vaccines should not be given concurrently. Paediatric immunisations should be brought up to date before starting etanercept (if applicable to other brands).
- **Diabetes:** Hypoglycaemia has been reported following etanercept initiation in patients taking anti-diabetic medications, requiring dose reductions in some cases.

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site reactions (bleeding, bruising, erythema, itching, pain, swelling), infections (URTI, bronchitis, cystitis), headache. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Serious infections (pneumonia, cellulitis, sepsis), allergic reactions, pruritus, rash, pyrexia, elevated liver enzymes, autoantibody formation. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Thrombocytopenia, anaemia, leukopenia, neutropenia, non-melanoma skin cancers, angioedema, urticaria, psoriasis (new onset or exacerbation), uveitis, scleritis, IBD, worsening CHF, interstitial lung disease. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Pancytopenia, melanoma, lymphoma, leukaemia, serious allergic/anaphylactic reactions, CNS demyelinating events, seizures, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, cutaneous vasculitis, new-onset CHF, autoimmune hepatitis. |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Aplastic anaemia, toxic epidermal necrolysis. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Anakinra (IL-1 Antagonist):** Concurrent use is contraindicated due to an increased risk of serious infections and neutropenia without added clinical benefit.
- **Abatacept:** Combination therapy is not recommended; clinical trials showed increased serious adverse events without increased benefit.
- **Sulfasalazine:** A significant decrease in white blood cell counts has been observed when used in combination with etanercept.
- **Methotrexate:** No significant pharmacokinetic interaction; etanercept may be safely used in combination.
- **Digoxin and Warfarin:** Etanercept does not significantly affect the exposure of these drugs, though minor reductions in etanercept exposure were noted in some subjects (clinical significance uncertain).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Etanercept crosses the placenta. It should only be used during pregnancy if the potential benefits to the mother outweigh the risks to the fetus. Women of childbearing potential should use contraception during and for three weeks after therapy.
- **Breastfeeding:** Etanercept has been detected in human milk at low levels. Use during breastfeeding requires a risk-benefit assessment. Because etanercept is likely degraded in the infant's GI tract, systemic exposure is expected to be low.
- **Live Vaccines in Infants:** Administration of live vaccines to infants exposed to etanercept *in utero* or via breast milk is generally not recommended for 16 weeks after the mother's last dose.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** **Brenzys** is not indicated for patients < 18 years of age in Australia because it is only available in 50 mg strengths. Other etanercept brands may be available for JIA and paediatric psoriasis [Australian].
- **Elderly (≥ 65 years):** No dosage adjustment is required. Elderly patients may be at higher risk for infections; use with caution.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required based on available pharmacokinetic data.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Etanercept is a competitive inhibitor that binds specifically to TNF (both TNFα and TNFβ/lymphotoxin α) and blocks its interaction with cell surface TNF receptors. This modulates the inflammatory and immune responses driven by TNF.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Slowly absorbed after SC injection; $T_{max}$ is 24–96 hours. Absolute bioavailability is approximately 76%.
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution at steady state is approximately 10.4 L.
    - **Metabolism:** No formal studies; however, it is a fusion protein and expected to be degraded into amino acids.
    - **Elimination:** Cleared slowly (0.066 L/hr in RA patients); half-life is approximately 80 hours (range 7 to 300 hours).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis (Study SB4-G31-RA):** A Phase III study in 596 patients demonstrated the equivalence of **Brenzys** to the reference product (EU-sourced Enbrel). The ACR20 response at Week 24 was 78.1% for Brenzys versus 80.5% for the reference product in the per-protocol set. The 95% confidence interval for the difference fell within the pre-defined equivalence margin of [-15%, 15%].
- **Psoriasis:** Studies demonstrated that etanercept (50 mg twice weekly) significantly improved PASI scores compared to placebo, with benefits maintained through extension periods.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Refrigeration:** Store at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze.
- **Room Temperature:** May be stored at temperatures up to 25°C for a single period of up to **4 weeks**. Discard if not used within 4 weeks of removal from the refrigerator.
- **Protection:** Keep in the outer carton to protect from light.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
