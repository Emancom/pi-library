# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory and clinical data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource only. It is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing in any particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule involved in mitochondrial fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for both acute and chronic management of patients with inborn metabolic errors that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Pediatrics)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is **50 mg/kg** administered as a slow 2- to 3-minute bolus or by infusion. 
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by equivalent doses over 24 hours. 
    - Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours (q3h or q4h) and never less frequent than every 6 hours (q6h). 
    - Subsequent maintenance doses range from 50 mg/kg/day or as clinically indicated. The maximum reported dose is 300 mg/kg/day [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:**
    - The recommended starting dose is **10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg** (dry body weight).
    - Administered as a slow 2- to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - Dose adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration before use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known:** There are no listed contraindications for levocarnitine injection in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and monitor if re-administration is attempted [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** 
    - Weekly and monthly monitoring of plasma carnitine levels is recommended for metabolic disorders.
    - Target plasma free carnitine concentrations should be between 35 and 60 µmol/L.
    - Monitor blood chemistries, vital signs, and overall clinical status [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** The safety of oral levocarnitine in renal impairment has not been established. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted renally [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions observed in clinical trials (specifically in hemodialysis patients) with a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) include:

| Body System | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, allergic reaction, asthenia, back pain, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, arrhythmia, palpitations |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, anorexia, dyspepsia |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia, hyperkalemia, weight changes, peripheral edema |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, depression, paresthesia |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis |

- **Serious/Notable Reactions:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, and seizures (reported post-marketing).
- **Other:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent reports of body odor and gastritis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. 
- **Management:** It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine therapy or after any dose adjustment in patients receiving warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned/unspecified]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits using doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. However, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response, use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is established for pediatric populations (see Section 3). Secondary carnitine deficiency in newborns and premature infants is defined by plasma levels below age-related norms [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific clinical studies in the elderly are not detailed; however, general caution regarding dose selection is standard in geriatric medicine [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Intravenous:** Used specifically for carnitine deficiency in ESRD patients on dialysis.
    - **Oral:** Safety and efficacy of oral formulations have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn metabolic errors, levocarnitine facilitates the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted. This process helps alleviate "autointoxication" caused by acyl-CoA accumulation [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (found in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (found in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours, and the mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, IV levocarnitine (20 mg/kg post-dialysis) significantly increased plasma carnitine concentrations. Trough levels rose from a baseline of 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses confirmed a dose-dependent increase in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservative [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription only (Rx-only) [US].
