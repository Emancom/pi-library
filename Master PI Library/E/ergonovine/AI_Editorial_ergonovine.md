# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial Product Information was synthesized from the official Product Information (PI) document provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview designed for use by healthcare professionals to provide a clear, concise, and clinically accurate summary of the drug's profile.
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing or dispensing in any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine [(R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt].
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule for fatty acid transport (amino acid derivative).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Paediatrics)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose:** Often administered in patients with severe metabolic crisis, followed by equivalent doses over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
    - **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Maximum Dose:** The highest administered dose reported is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - **Dose Adjustment:** Should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations (normal range 40–50 µmol/L). Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [US].
- Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

### Monitoring
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations prior to therapy [US].
- Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occurred within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate appropriate medical management [US].
- **Seizures:** Reports of seizures have occurred in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Oral Formulation in Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported at a frequency of ≥5% in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials (regardless of causality) include:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), flu syndrome (25%), headache (22%), chest pain (12%), fever (7%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), palpitations (5%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), abdominal pain (9%), anorexia (5%).
- **Metabolic/Nutritional:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%), peripheral edema (5%).
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), paresthesia (5%), depression (5%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), rhinitis (9%), dyspnea (7%).

### Postmarketing Experience
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent reports of body odor and gastritis [US].
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. Monitor INR levels following the initiation of treatment or after dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned a letter, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess intake must be weighed against maternal benefits. Consider discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See **Section 3: Dosage and Administration** for metabolic disorder dosing [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** See **Section 3** for ESRD dosing and **Section 5** for precautions regarding oral metabolites in renal failure [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and [3H]-γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine over 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous carnitine). Mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Bioavailability:** Absolute bioavailability of oral formulations is approximately 15–16% [US].

### Pharmacodynamics
- Carnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted. This mechanism helps alleviate metabolic acidosis in patients with specific organic acidopathies [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Dosing Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (n=12), 20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis trough concentrations from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. 
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** ESRD patients receiving 10, 20, or 40 mg/kg three times weekly showed dose-proportional increases in trough concentrations after 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the impact on clinical outcomes in this population has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].
