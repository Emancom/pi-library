# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: United States (FDA). 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals by integrating clinical data from available regulatory sources.
- This document is an editorial synthesis and is not intended to replace the official PI used for prescribing in specific jurisdictions. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved labeling for definitive prescribing information.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP); chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule).
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available source.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection solution: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion. 
    - **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Dosing should be every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** 
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - **Timing:** Administer as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Adjustments:** Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives.

### Monitoring
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations before starting parenteral therapy.
- Conduct weekly and monthly monitoring, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine level: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in patients with ESRD on dialysis. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, treatment must be discontinued immediately and appropriate medical management initiated [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Levocarnitine in Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Serious Adverse Effects
- Anaphylaxis and hypersensitivity reactions.
- Neurological: Seizures.

### Common Adverse Effects
The following events occurred at a frequency of ≥5% in clinical trials of ESRD patients (regardless of causality) [US]:

| Body System | Adverse Event | Frequency (%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Injection site reaction, Pain, Flu syndrome, Headache, Infection, Abdominal pain, Fever | 7% – 33% |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, Hypotension, Tachycardia, Chest pain | 6% – 20% |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, Vomiting, Nausea, Anorexia | 5% – 16% |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, Paresthesia, Depression | 5% – 13% |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, Cough, Rhinitis, Dyspnea | 7% – 23% |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, Hyperkalemia, Hypervolemia | 5% – 9% |

### Other Reported Effects
- Transient nausea and vomiting.
- Less frequent: Body odor and gastritis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating treatment or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Pregnancy Category not assigned under current labeling, but document states use "only if clearly needed"]. Animal reproduction studies (rats/rabbits) at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is not known if levocarnitine is excreted in human milk; however, studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after administration. For nursing mothers, the benefits of supplementation must be weighed against the potential risks of excess carnitine intake for the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, yeast) were negative. Long-term animal studies for carcinogenic potential have not been conducted [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders apply to pediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available source.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used to treat carnitine deficiency in ESRD patients on dialysis. High-dose **oral** therapy should be avoided in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available source.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, it alleviates metabolic abnormalities resulting from the accumulation of toxic organic acids and corrects low plasma carnitine levels.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein or albumin bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine from 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While carnitine concentrations increased, clinical outcomes regarding signs and symptoms of deficiency in this population have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) [US].
