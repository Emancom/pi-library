## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Edrophonium chloride (Anticholinesterase agent).
- **Brand Name(s):** Enlon [US].
- **ATC Code:** N07AA03 (Not explicitly stated in source, but standard classification).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection: 10 mg/mL [US].
- **Inactive Ingredients:** Phenol 0.45% (preservative), sodium sulfite 0.2% (antioxidant), sodium citrate, citric acid [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV), Intramuscular (IM).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Myasthenia Gravis (MG) Diagnosis:** Differential diagnosis of myasthenia gravis [US].
- **Treatment Evaluation:** Adjunct in evaluating the requirements for treatment in MG [US].
- **Crisis Evaluation:** Evaluation of emergency treatment during myasthenic crises [US].
- **Curare Antagonism:** Reversal of neuromuscular blockade produced by curare, tubocurarine, gallamine triethiodide, or dimethyl-tubocurarine [US].
- **Respiratory Depression:** Adjunctive treatment for respiratory depression resulting from curare overdosage [US].

*Note: Edrophonium is not recommended for maintenance therapy in MG due to its brief duration of action.*

## 3. Dosage and Administration

### General Administration
Edrophonium should be administered by a clinician prepared to manage respiratory or cardiac arrest. A syringe containing **1 mg of atropine sulfate** must be immediately available to counteract potential severe cholinergic reactions [US].

### MG Differential Diagnosis (Adults)
- **Intravenous:** 
    1. Prepare 10 mg (1 mL) in a tuberculin syringe.
    2. Inject 2 mg (0.2 mL) IV within 15–30 seconds.
    3. If no reaction occurs after 45 seconds, inject the remaining 8 mg (0.8 mL).
    4. If a cholinergic reaction occurs after the initial 2 mg, discontinue the test and administer 0.4 mg to 0.5 mg of atropine IV.
- **Intramuscular:** In patients with inaccessible veins, a 10 mg (1 mL) dose is used. If hyperreactivity occurs, retest after 30 minutes with 2 mg IM to rule out false-negatives [US].

### MG Treatment Requirement Evaluation
- **Dose:** 1 mg to 2 mg (0.1 mL to 0.2 mL) IV, administered 1 hour after the last oral dose of the patient's maintenance medication [US].

### MG Crisis Evaluation
- **Condition:** Testing must only begin once adequate respiratory exchange is secured.
- **Initial Dose:** 1 mg (0.1 mL) IV.
- **Secondary Dose:** If no further impairment occurs after 1 minute, an additional 1 mg (0.1 mL) may be injected.
- **Interpretation:** If no respiratory improvement occurs after 2 mg, discontinue anticholinesterase therapy and secure controlled ventilation [US].

### Curare Antagonist
- **Dose:** 10 mg (1 mL) IV given slowly over 30–45 seconds.
- **Titration:** May repeat as necessary.
- **Maximum Total Dose:** 40 mg for any single patient [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to anticholinesterase agents [US].
- Mechanical intestinal obstruction [US].
- Mechanical urinary tract obstruction [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Emergency Preparedness:** Immediate availability of **atropine sulfate (1 mg)** is mandatory to treat severe cholinergic reactions [US].
- **Cardiorespiratory Caution:** Use with extreme caution in patients with bronchial asthma or cardiac dysrhythmias. Isolated reports of cardiac and respiratory arrest have occurred, attributed to vagotonic effects [US].
- **Sulfite Sensitivity:** This product contains sodium sulfite, which may cause anaphylaxis or life-threatening asthmatic episodes in susceptible individuals (more common in asthmatics) [US].
- **Anticholinesterase Insensitivity:** Patients may develop periods of insensitivity to the drug; careful monitoring and respiratory support may be required. Dosing should be withheld or reduced until sensitivity returns [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are primarily extensions of the drug's cholinergic pharmacological action.

| System | Potential Reactions |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Eye** | Increased lacrimation, miosis (pupillary constriction), spasm of accommodation, diplopia, conjunctival hyperemia. |
| **Respiratory** | Increased tracheobronchial secretions, laryngospasm, bronchiolar constriction, paralysis of respiratory muscles. |
| **Cardiac** | Arrhythmias (particularly bradycardia), hypotension (due to decreased cardiac output), cardiac arrest. |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Increased salivary/gastric/intestinal secretions, nausea, vomiting, increased peristalsis, diarrhea, abdominal cramps. |
| **CNS** | Convulsions, dysarthria, dysphonia, dysphagia. |
| **Skeletal Muscle** | Muscle weakness, fasciculations. |
| **Miscellaneous** | Diaphoresis, urinary frequency, urinary incontinence. |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Cardiac standstill, respiratory paralysis, and anaphylactic reactions to sulfites [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Anticholinesterase Agents:** Use caution in patients already taking maintenance anticholinesterase drugs for MG. Edrophonium may exacerbate a cholinergic crisis, which can clinically mimic myasthenic weakness [US].
- **Neuromuscular Blockers:** Edrophonium is **ineffective** against decamethonium bromide and succinylcholine chloride [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Safety in human pregnancy has not been established. Use only when potential benefits outweigh possible hazards to the mother and fetus [US]. No formal pregnancy category was assigned in the source text.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety during lactation has not been established [US].
- **Fertility:** Not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:**
    - **Infants:** Recommended dose is 0.5 mg (0.05 mL) IV [US].
    - **Children (< 75 lbs):** IV dose is 1 mg (0.1 mL). If no response in 45 seconds, titrate in 1 mg increments every 30–45 seconds to a max of 5 mg. IM dose is 2 mg [US].
    - **Children (> 75 lbs):** IV dose is 2 mg (0.2 mL). If no response in 45 seconds, titrate in 1 mg increments every 30–45 seconds to a max of 10 mg. IM dose is 5 mg [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Edrophonium is a rapid-acting acetylcholinesterase inhibitor. It inhibits the breakdown of acetylcholine at cholinergic transmission sites, prolonging its effect at the neuromuscular junction [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** 
    - **Onset of Action:** 30 to 60 seconds after IV injection [US].
    - **Duration of Action:** Average of 10 minutes [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Not specified in available sources (beyond onset/duration).
