# Editorial Product Information: Estropipate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the **United States (FDA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a clinical reference format.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Estropipate (formerly piperazine estrone sulfate).
- **Chemical Class:** Estrogen; a natural estrogenic substance prepared from purified crystalline estrone, solubilised as the sulfate and stabilised with piperazine.
- **Brand Name(s):** Ogen [US], Ortho-Est [US].
- **ATC Code:** G03CA07.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Oral Tablets: 0.75 mg, 1.5 mg, 3 mg, and 6 mg [US].
    - *Note: 0.75 mg estropipate is calculated as 0.625 mg sodium estrone sulfate.*
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Watson Laboratories / Physicians Total Care, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Vasomotor Symptoms:** Treatment of moderate to severe symptoms associated with menopause [US-approved].
- **Vulvar and Vaginal Atrophy:** Treatment of moderate to severe symptoms associated with menopause [US-approved].
- **Hypoestrogenism:** Treatment of hypoestrogenism due to hypogonadism, castration, or primary ovarian failure [US-approved].
- **Postmenopausal Osteoporosis:** Prevention of osteoporosis in women at significant risk for whom non-estrogen medications are inappropriate [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing Principles
Therapy should be initiated at the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration consistent with treatment goals. Patients should be re-evaluated every 3 to 6 months to determine the ongoing necessity of treatment.

### Indication-Specific Dosing [US]
- **Vasomotor Symptoms and Vulvar/Vaginal Atrophy:** 
    - Usual range: 0.75 mg to 6 mg daily. 
    - Administration should be cyclic (e.g., 3 weeks on, 1 week off).
- **Female Hypogonadism:**
    - Usual range: 1.5 mg to 9 mg daily for the first three weeks of a theoretical cycle, followed by a rest period of 8 to 10 days.
- **Female Castration or Primary Ovarian Failure:**
    - Usual range: 1.5 mg to 9 mg daily for the first three weeks of a cycle, followed by a 10-day rest period. Adjust for maintenance based on patient response.
- **Prevention of Osteoporosis:**
    - Standard dose: 0.75 mg daily for 25 days of a 31-day cycle.

### Administration Instructions
- For women with an intact uterus, a progestin should be added to reduce the risk of endometrial cancer.
- If a patient has not menstruated within the last two months, cyclic administration may start arbitrarily; if menstruating, start on Day 5 of bleeding.

## 4. Contraindications
- Undiagnosed abnormal genital bleeding [US].
- Known, suspected, or history of cancer of the breast [US].
- Known or suspected estrogen-dependent neoplasia [US].
- Active deep vein thrombosis (DVT), pulmonary embolism (PE), or a history of these conditions [US].
- Active or recent (within the past year) arterial thromboembolic disease, including stroke or myocardial infarction (MI) [US].
- Liver dysfunction or disease [US].
- Known hypersensitivity to estropipate or any tablet ingredients [US].
- Known or suspected pregnancy [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
- **Endometrial Cancer:** Unopposed estrogen use in women with a uterus increases the risk of endometrial cancer.
- **Cardiovascular Risks:** Estrogens (with or without progestins) should not be used for the prevention of cardiovascular disease. Increased risks of MI, stroke, DVT, and PE have been reported.
- **Dementia:** An increased risk of developing probable dementia in postmenopausal women ≥65 years has been observed.
- **Breast Cancer:** Increased risk of invasive breast cancer has been reported with estrogen/progestin combinations.

### General Warnings
- **Venous Thromboembolism:** Discontinue therapy at least 4 to 6 weeks before surgery associated with an increased risk of thromboembolism or during periods of prolonged immobilisation.
- **Gallbladder Disease:** A 2-to-4-fold increase in the risk of gallbladder disease requiring surgery has been reported.
- **Visual Abnormalities:** Retinal vascular thrombosis has been reported; discontinue if there is sudden partial or complete loss of vision or sudden onset of proptosis/diplopia.
- **Hypercalcemia:** Estrogens may lead to severe hypercalcemia in patients with breast cancer and bone metastases.

### Precautions and Monitoring
- **Blood Pressure:** Substantial increases in blood pressure have occurred in idiosyncratic reactions; monitor regularly.
- **Lipids:** In patients with pre-existing hypertriglyceridemia, therapy may lead to pancreatitis.
- **Fluid Retention:** Caution in patients with cardiac or renal dysfunction.
- **Thyroid Function:** Estrogens increase thyroid-binding globulin (TBG); patients on thyroid replacement may require increased doses.

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Note: Frequencies were not specifically categorised by percentage in the source documents.*

- **Genitourinary:** Breakthrough bleeding, spotting, dysmenorrhea, increase in size of uterine leiomyomata, vaginitis, ovarian cancer, endometrial hyperplasia.
- **Breasts:** Tenderness, enlargement, pain, nipple discharge, galactorrhea, fibrocystic changes, breast cancer.
- **Cardiovascular:** Deep/superficial venous thrombosis, pulmonary embolism, thrombophlebitis, MI, stroke, hypertension.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, abdominal cramps, bloating, cholestatic jaundice, gallbladder disease, pancreatitis.
- **Skin:** Chloasma/melasma (may persist), erythema multiforme, alopecia, hirsutism, pruritus, rash.
- **CNS:** Headache, migraine, dizziness, mental depression, chorea, nervousness, dementia.
- **Miscellaneous:** Weight changes, reduced carbohydrate tolerance, edema, arthralgias, leg cramps, changes in libido.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Drugs such as St. John’s Wort, phenobarbital, carbamazepine, and rifampin may decrease plasma estrogen concentrations, potentially reducing therapeutic effects or changing the uterine bleeding profile [US].
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Erythromycin, clarithromycin, and ketoconazole may increase plasma estrogen concentrations [US].

### Laboratory Test Interactions
Estrogens may cause:
- Accelerated prothrombin time and increased platelet count [US].
- Increased thyroid-binding globulin (TBG) leading to increased circulating total thyroid hormone (T4/T3) [US].
- Increased corticosteroid-binding globulin (CBG) and sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG) [US].
- Impaired glucose tolerance and reduced response to the metyrapone test [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not formally lettered but explicitly contraindicated]. Estropipate should not be used during pregnancy. There is no indication for its use, and exposure during early pregnancy appears to carry little to no increased risk of birth defects based on inadvertent use of oral contraceptives.
- **Breastfeeding:** Estrogens are excreted into human milk and can decrease the quantity and quality of milk. Caution should be exercised [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in paediatric patients have not been established. [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Data from the WHIMS study indicated a higher risk of probable dementia in women >65 years. Of the cases of dementia observed, 90% occurred in women >70 years [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in active liver dysfunction or disease, as estrogens may be poorly metabolised in these patients [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified, but caution is advised due to potential fluid retention [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Estrogens bind to nuclear receptors in estrogen-responsive tissues. They modulate pituitary secretion of gonadotropins (LH and FSH) through a negative feedback mechanism, reducing the elevated levels seen in postmenopausal women.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Well absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract and through the skin.
    - **Distribution:** Widely distributed; bound to sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG) and albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolised primarily in the liver. Estradiol is converted reversibly to estrone; both can be converted to estriol (the major urinary metabolite). Estrogens undergo enterohepatic recirculation.
    - **Excretion:** Estradiol, estrone, and estriol are excreted in the urine as glucuronide and sulfate conjugates.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Women’s Health Initiative (WHI):** This large-scale study assessed the risks/benefits of conjugated estrogens (CE) alone or with medroxyprogesterone acetate (MPA). Key findings included increased risks of MI, stroke, breast cancer, and VTE in the combination arm, leading to early termination of that substudy.
- **WHIMS Study:** A substudy of WHI that reported a 2.05 relative risk for probable dementia in women ≥65 years taking CE/MPA.
- **Osteoporosis Studies:** A double-blind, placebo-controlled two-year study showed that 0.75 mg estropipate daily for 25 days of a 31-day cycle prevents vertebral bone mass loss in postmenopausal women.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- Keep out of reach of children.
