# Editorial Product Information: Epirubicin

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **epirubicin** for use by healthcare professionals.
- This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the Australian source document was provided for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Epirubicin hydrochloride.
- **Chemical Class:** Anthracycline cytotoxic antibiotic.
- **Brand Names:** Epirubicin Accord [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01DB03.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Concentrated injection (2 mg/mL). Available in 10 mg/5 mL, 20 mg/10 mL, 50 mg/25 mL, and 200 mg/100 mL vials [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) or Intravesical. Must not be administered via intramuscular, subcutaneous, or oral routes.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Accord Healthcare Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Epirubicin** is indicated for the treatment of various neoplastic diseases, including:
- **Breast Cancer:** As a component of adjuvant therapy in patients with axillary node-positive disease and for locally advanced or metastatic disease.
- **Gastric Cancer.**
- **Ovarian Cancer.**
- **Small Cell Lung Cancer.**
- **Non-Hodgkin’s Lymphoma.**
- **Advanced/Metastatic Soft Tissue Sarcoma.**
- **Superficial Bladder Cancer:** Treatment of Carcinoma in situ (Tis) and Ta tumours.
- **Prophylaxis of Bladder Cancer Recurrence:** Following transurethral resection of stage T1 or Ta (grade 2 and 3) papillary cancers.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
The recommended lifetime cumulative dose limit for **epirubicin** is **900 mg/m²**.

### Intravenous Administration
- **Standard Dose:** 75 to 90 mg/m² as a single injection every 21 days.
- **High-Dose Regimens:** 
  - Single agent: Up to 135 mg/m² every 3–4 weeks.
  - Combination therapy: 120 mg/m² every 3–4 weeks.
  - Adjuvant breast cancer (node-positive): 100 to 120 mg/m² every 3–4 weeks.
- **Dose Modifications:** Lower starting doses (75 to 90 mg/m²) should be considered for heavily pre-treated patients, those with bone marrow depression, or neoplastic marrow infiltration.

### Intravesical Administration
- **Papillary Transitional Cell Carcinoma:** 50 mg weekly for eight weeks. Reduce to 30 mg if local toxicity (chemical cystitis) occurs. For carcinoma in situ, the dose may be increased to 80 mg based on tolerance.
- **Prophylaxis of Recurrence:** 50 mg weekly for four weeks, followed by 50 mg monthly for 11 months.
- **Instructions:** Instil via catheter and retain in the bladder for one hour. Patients should avoid fluids for 12 hours prior to instillation to prevent dilution.

### Administration Instructions
- **IV Bolus:** Administer over 3–20 minutes into the tubing of a freely running IV infusion (0.9% Sodium Chloride or 5% Glucose). Direct push is not recommended due to extravasation risk.
- **Extravasation:** If a burning or stinging sensation occurs, terminate the infusion immediately and restart in a different vein.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to **epirubicin**, other anthracyclines, or anthracenediones.
- **Intravenous Route Contraindications:**
  - Persistent myelosuppression or severe stomatitis from previous therapy.
  - Generalised infections.
  - Marked hepatic impairment.
  - History of or current cardiac impairment (severe arrhythmias, cardiomyopathy, previous MI).
  - Unstable angina pectoris.
  - Maximum cumulative doses of other anthracyclines (e.g., doxorubicin, daunorubicin) or mitomycin C/mitoxantrone already reached.
  - Pregnancy and lactation.
- **Intravesical Route Contraindications:**
  - Invasive tumours (penetrated the muscular layer).
  - Urinary tract infections or bladder inflammation.
  - Catheterisation difficulties.
  - Haematuria.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** Not specified in available sources, though heavy emphasis is placed on cardiac and haematologic monitoring.
- **Cardiac Function:** Risk of early (ECG changes, arrhythmias) and late (congestive heart failure, cardiomyopathy) cardiotoxicity. Risk increases sharply above a cumulative dose of 900 mg/m². Baseline and periodic LVEF assessment (MUGA or ECHO) is mandatory.
- **Haematologic Toxicity:** Dose-dependent, reversible leukopenia and neutropenia are the most common dose-limiting toxicities. Nadir typically occurs between days 10 and 14.
- **Secondary Leukaemia:** Reported with or without a pre-leukaemic phase, often with a 1–3 year latency.
- **Tumour Lysis Syndrome:** Hyperuricaemia may occur; monitor uric acid, potassium, calcium, and phosphate.
- **Extravasation:** Can cause severe tissue necrosis and cellulitis.
- **Urine Discoloration:** Patients should be advised that **epirubicin** may impart a red colour to urine for 1–2 days after administration.

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Myelosuppression, leukopenia, neutropenia, anaemia, thrombocytopenia, alopecia (60–90%), nausea, vomiting, mucositis (stomatitis), red urine. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Secondary infection, febrile neutropenia, diarrhoea, transient ECG changes (T-wave flattening, ST depression). |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Sepsis, septicaemia, skin and nail hyperpigmentation, photosensitivity, rash, urticaria, dehydration. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Anaphylaxis, cardiomyopathy, congestive heart failure, acute leukaemia. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** CHF, severe myelosuppression (sepsis, septic shock), secondary leukaemia, and thromboembolism (including pulmonary embolism).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Cardiotoxic Agents:** Concomitant use with trastuzumab, cyclophosphamide, 5-fluorouracil, or taxanes requires strict cardiac monitoring. **Epirubicin** should ideally be avoided for up to 7 months after stopping trastuzumab due to its long half-life.
- **Cimetidine:** Increases **epirubicin** AUC by 50%; discontinue during treatment.
- **Paclitaxel:** When given before **epirubicin**, it increases plasma concentrations of unchanged **epirubicin**.
- **Propranolol:** May result in additive cardiotoxic effects.
- **Vaccines:** Avoid live or live-attenuated vaccines due to the risk of serious or fatal infection in immunocompromised patients.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **[Australian: Category D]**. **Epirubicin** is embryotoxic and fetotoxic. Effective contraception is required for males and females during and for a period after treatment (6.5 months for females; 3.5 months for males [Australian]).
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Breastfeeding should be avoided during treatment and for at least 7 days after the final dose.
- **Fertility:** May cause amenorrhea, premature menopause, oligospermia, or permanent azoospermia. Fertility preservation should be discussed prior to treatment.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No data available in source documents.
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available, but general caution is advised regarding organ function.
- **Renal Impairment:** Consider lower starting doses in severe impairment (serum creatinine > 5 mg/dL).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Dose reduction is mandatory based on serum bilirubin:
  - Bilirubin 20–50 µmol/L: 1/2 normal dose.
  - Bilirubin > 50 µmol/L: 1/4 normal dose.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Forms a complex with DNA by intercalation between base pairs, inhibiting nucleic acid synthesis (DNA and RNA) and mitosis. 
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Distribution:** Extensive tissue distribution; does not cross the blood-brain barrier.
  - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised by the liver to the 13-OH derivative (epirubicinol).
  - **Excretion:** Primarily via the hepatobiliary system. Plasma clearance is high (0.9 L/min). Approximately 11% is excreted in urine within 48 hours.
  - **Half-life:** Mean terminal half-life is approximately 40 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Early Breast Cancer:** Phase III studies demonstrated that intensive regimens (e.g., CEF-120: epirubicin 60 mg/m² on days 1 and 8) significantly improved 5-year disease-free survival (62% vs 53%) and overall survival (77% vs 70%) compared to standard CMF regimens [Australian].
- **Advanced Breast Cancer:** Higher dose intensities (>90 mg/m²) generally correlate with higher response rates.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze).
- **Protection:** Keep in the outer carton to protect from light.
- **Stability:** Use as soon as practicable after preparation; if storage is necessary, hold at 2°C–8°C for no more than 24 hours.
