# Editorial Product Information: Eribulin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction:
    - **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of eribulin for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Clinicians should consult the local PI approved in their region before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Eribulin mesilate, a first-in-class synthetic analogue of the marine sponge natural product halichondrin B.
- **Chemical Class:** Microtubule dynamics inhibitor (halichondrin B-based).
- **Brand Names:** **ERIBULIN DR. REDDY’S** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 
    - Solution for injection: 1 mg eribulin mesilate in 2 mL (equivalent to 0.88 mg eribulin free base per 2 mL) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Dr. Reddy’s Laboratories (Australia) Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metastatic Breast Cancer:** Treatment of patients with locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer who have progressed following at least one chemotherapeutic regimen for advanced disease. Prior therapy must have included an anthracycline and a taxane (adjuvant or metastatic setting) unless contraindicated [Australian].
- **Liposarcoma:** Treatment of patients with unresectable liposarcoma who have received prior chemotherapy for advanced or metastatic disease [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Recommended Dose:** 1.4 mg/m² administered intravenously over 2 to 5 minutes on Days 1 and 8 of every 21-day cycle [Australian].
- **Antiemetic Prophylaxis:** Consider antiemetic regimens, including corticosteroids, as patients may experience nausea or vomiting [Australian].

### Dose Delays and Modifications
- **Delays:** Administration on Day 1 or Day 8 should be delayed for a maximum of 1 week for:
    - Absolute Neutrophil Count (ANC) < 1 x 10⁹/L
    - Platelets < 75 x 10⁹/L
    - Grade 3 or 4 non-haematological toxicities [Australian].
- **Omissions:** If toxicities do not resolve to $\le$ Grade 2 by Day 15, the dose should be omitted.
- **Reductions:** If toxicities resolve to $\le$ Grade 2 by Day 15, the dose should be reduced as follows:

| Adverse Reaction after Previous Dose | Recommended Dose |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Haematological:** ANC < 0.5 x 10⁹/L (> 7 days); febrile neutropenia; Platelets < 25 x 10⁹/L; or Platelets < 50 x 10⁹/L with hemorrhage/transfusion requirements. | 1.1 mg/m² |
| **Non-haematological:** Any Grade 3 or 4 in the previous cycle. | 1.1 mg/m² |
| **Reoccurrence:** Any of the above specified reactions. | 0.7 mg/m² |
| **Persistent Reoccurrence:** Reoccurrence despite reduction to 0.7 mg/m². | Consider discontinuation |

*Note: Once reduced, the dose should not be re-escalated [Australian].*

### Administration Instructions
- **Preparation:** May be used undiluted or diluted in up to 100 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride.
- **Incompatibilities:** Must **not** be mixed with other products or diluted in 5% glucose infusion [Australian].
- **Flushing:** The IV line should be flushed after administration to ensure the complete dose is delivered [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to eribulin mesilate or any excipients (including ethanol) [Australian].
- Breastfeeding [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Myelosuppression:** Dose-dependent and primarily manifested as neutropenia. Monitoring of complete blood counts (CBC) is required prior to each dose. Initiation requires ANC $\ge$ 1.5 x 10⁹/L and platelets > 100 x 10⁹/L [Australian].
- **Peripheral Neuropathy:** Patients should be monitored for signs of motor and sensory neuropathy. Severe neurotoxicity requires dose delay or reduction [Australian].
- **QT Prolongation:** Observed on Day 8 of treatment. Monitoring is recommended in patients with heart failure, bradyarrhythmias, or those taking drugs known to prolong the QT interval. Electrolyte imbalances (hypokalaemia/hypomagnesaemia) must be corrected prior to therapy [Australian].
- **Hepatic Transport Proteins:** Co-administration with inhibitors of hepatic transport proteins (e.g., OATPs) is not recommended as it may increase eribulin exposure [Australian].
- **Excipients:** Contains 5% v/v ethanol (alcohol), approximately 0.1 mL per vial [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Summary [Australian]
- **Very Common (>10%):** Neutropenia (82%), Anaemia (58%), Fatigue/Asthenia (54%), Alopecia (45%), Peripheral neuropathy (35%), Nausea (35%), Pyrexia (21%), Weight decreased (21%), Anorexia/Decreased appetite (20%), Arthralgia/Myalgia (22%), Constipation (25%), Diarrhoea (18%), Vomiting (18%), Cough (14%), Dyspnoea (16%), Headache (19%).
- **Common (1–10%):** Urinary tract infection, Febrile neutropenia, Thrombocytopenia, Hypokalaemia, Hypomagnesaemia, Dysgeusia, Dizziness, Lacrimation increased, Tachycardia, Oropharyngeal pain, Stomatitis, Abdominal pain, Increased ALT/AST, Rash, Pruritus, Muscle spasms, Dysuria.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Sepsis/Septic shock, Deep vein thrombosis, Pulmonary embolism, Renal failure, Interstitial lung disease.

### Serious and Post-Marketing Adverse Effects
- **Fatalities:** Rare fatal cases of febrile neutropenia, neutropenic sepsis, and septic shock have been reported.
- **Spontaneous Reports:** Disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC), pancreatitis, Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Profile:** Eribulin is a substrate of P-glycoprotein (Pgp) but not a substrate of CYP3A4. Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole) or inducers (e.g., rifampicin) do not cause clinically meaningful changes in exposure [Australian].
- **Effect on Other Drugs:** In vitro, eribulin is a CYP3A4 inhibitor. Caution and monitoring are advised when co-administering eribulin with narrow-therapeutic-index drugs eliminated via CYP3A4 [Australian].
- **Transport Proteins:** Co-administration with substances inhibiting hepatic transport proteins may increase exposure and is not recommended [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category D:** Eribulin is embryotoxic, foetotoxic, and teratogenic in animal studies. It must not be used during pregnancy [Australian].
    - **Contraception:** Women of childbearing potential and male patients with female partners must use effective contraception during and up to 3 months after treatment [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. A risk to newborns/infants cannot be excluded [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal data suggest male fertility may be compromised (testicular toxicity). Male patients should seek advice on sperm conservation prior to treatment [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in children (0–18 years) for breast cancer or soft tissue sarcoma [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Safety profile is generally similar to younger patients, though an increasing trend in asthenia/fatigue has been noted with age. No specific dose adjustments are recommended [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - Mild (GFR 60–89 mL/min): No adjustment needed.
    - Moderate to Severe (GFR < 60 mL/min): Recommended dose is 0.7 mg/m² [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment (due to metastases):**
    - Mild (Child-Pugh A): 1.1 mg/m²
    - Moderate (Child-Pugh B): 0.7 mg/m²
    - Severe (Child-Pugh C): Not studied; marked dose reduction expected to be necessary [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Eribulin inhibits the growth phase of microtubules without affecting the shortening phase, sequestering tubulin into non-productive aggregates. This leads to G2/M cell-cycle block and apoptotic cell death. It also remodels tumour vasculature and reverses the epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Rapid distribution phase; terminal half-life of ~40 hours. Weakly protein bound (49–67%).
    - **Metabolism:** Minimal metabolism; unchanged eribulin is the major circulating species.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily biliary excretion (70% in faeces) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **EMBRACE (Study 305):** A Phase 3 study in metastatic breast cancer showed a significant improvement in Overall Survival (OS) with eribulin (13.1 months) compared to treatment of physician's choice (10.6 months); HR 0.809, p=0.041 [Australian].
- **Study 301:** A Phase 3 study comparing eribulin to capecitabine in earlier-line metastatic breast cancer showed similar OS (15.9 vs 14.5 months) and PFS (4.1 vs 4.2 months) [Australian].
- **Study 309:** A Phase 3 study in liposarcoma showed a significant OS benefit for eribulin (15.6 months) versus dacarbazine (8.4 months) in the liposarcoma subgroup; HR 0.511, p=0.0006 [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Shelf Storage:** Store below 25°C. Do not refrigerate or freeze [Australian].
- **In-use Stability (Syringe):** Undiluted solution in a syringe is stable for 4 hours at 25°C (ambient light) or 24 hours at 2°C–8°C.
- **In-use Stability (Diluted):** Diluted solution (0.02 mg/mL to 0.2 mg/mL in 0.9% NaCl) is stable for 24 hours at 2°C–8°C [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 – Prescription Only Medicine [Australian].
