# Editorial Product Information: Etoposide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for educational and professional reference. It does not replace the official PI or local clinical guidelines required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Etoposide (a semi-synthetic derivative of podophyllotoxin).
- **Brand Name(s):** Etoposide Ebewe [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01CB01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Concentrated injection: 20 mg/mL [Australian].
    - Available in 50 mg/2.5 mL, 100 mg/5 mL, 200 mg/10 mL, 400 mg/20 mL, and 1000 mg/50 mL vials [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only. 
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sandoz Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Small cell carcinoma of the lung** [Australian].
- **Acute monocytic and myelomonocytic leukaemia** [Australian].
- **Hodgkin's disease** [Australian].
- **Non-Hodgkin's lymphoma** [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Standard Regimen:** 50 to 60 mg/m²/day administered intravenously for five consecutive days.
- **Cycle Frequency:** Treatment should be followed by a treatment-free interval of two to four weeks.
- **Maximum Dose:** Total dose should not exceed 400 mg/m² per treatment course.

### Administration Instructions
- **Dilution Required:** Must be diluted prior to use with either 0.9% sodium chloride or 5% glucose to a final concentration of **0.2 to 0.4 mg/mL**. Concentrations above 0.4 mg/mL may result in crystal formation.
- **Infusion Rate:** Administer by slow IV infusion over **30 to 60 minutes**. Rapid administration is associated with hypotension.
- **Strict Prohibition:** Must NOT be given by rapid IV push or via intrapleural, intraperitoneal, or intra-cavity injection [Australian].
- **Equipment Note:** Use of hard plastic devices containing acrylic or ABS may lead to cracking and leaking when used with undiluted etoposide.
- **Site Monitoring:** Ensure proper catheter positioning; extravasation may cause significant local irritation and tissue necrosis.

### Handling and Storage
- Admixtures should be used as soon as possible. If storage is necessary, diluted solutions may be held at 2 to 8°C for no more than 24 hours.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Severe Hepatic or Renal Dysfunction** [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known history of hypersensitivity to etoposide or any excipients (e.g., polysorbate 80, benzyl alcohol).
- **Severe Bone Marrow Failure:** WBC < 2,000 cells/mm³ or platelet count < 75,000 cells/mm³ (not due to malignancy) [Australian].
- **Vaccinations:** Concomitant use with yellow fever vaccine or other live vaccines in immunocompromised patients [Australian].
- **Lactation:** Breastfeeding is contraindicated during etoposide therapy.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Myelosuppression:** Fatal cases have been reported. Dose-limiting bone marrow suppression is the primary toxicity. Nadirs typically occur at 7–14 days (granulocytes) and 9–16 days (platelets). Therapy should not start if platelets are < 50,000/mm³ or ANC < 500/mm³ [Australian].
- **Secondary Leukaemia:** Risk of acute leukaemia (with or without myelodysplastic syndrome) exists, often with an 11q23 chromosome abnormality. Median time to development is approximately 32 months.
- **Anaphylaxis:** Manifestations include chills, fever, tachycardia, bronchospasm, and dyspnoea. Administration must be terminated immediately upon suspicion.
- **Tumour Lysis Syndrome:** Reported in patients with bulky, treatment-sensitive tumours; monitor renal function and uric acid levels.
- **Hypotension:** Associated with rapid IV infusion; usually resolves with infusion cessation and fluid support.
- **Mutagenic/Teratogenic Potential:** Effective contraception is required for both males and females during and for at least 6 months after treatment.
- **Low Serum Albumin:** Patients with hypoalbuminaemia may be at increased risk for etoposide-associated toxicities.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Category: Very Common (>10%)
- **Haematological:** Leukopenia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, anaemia.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, anorexia, abdominal pain, constipation, mucositis.
- **Skin:** Reversible alopecia (up to 66%), pigmentation.
- **General:** Asthenia, malaise, chills, fever.
- **Hepatobiliary:** Elevated liver enzymes (ALT, AST, alkaline phosphatase, bilirubin), hepatotoxicity.

### Frequency Category: Common (1–10%)
- **Cardiovascular:** Myocardial infarction, arrhythmia, hypertension, or transient hypotension.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Stomatitis, diarrhoea, esophagitis, taste alteration.
- **Neurological:** Dizziness.
- **Skin:** Pruritus, rash, urticaria.
- **Other:** Infection, anaphylactic-like reactions (0.7–2%).

### Frequency Category: Uncommon to Rare (<1%)
- **Uncommon:** Haemorrhage, peripheral neuropathy (0.7%), extravasation-related soft tissue inflammation.
- **Rare:** Seizures, optic neuritis, transient cortical blindness, interstitial pneumonitis/pulmonary fibrosis, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN).
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Fatal myelosuppression, secondary leukaemia, cardiac arrest, and fatal anaphylaxis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Ciclosporin (High Dose):** Concentrations > 2,000 ng/mL can increase etoposide exposure (AUC) by 80% and decrease clearance by 38%.
- **Cisplatin:** Concurrent therapy is associated with reduced total body clearance of etoposide.
- **Antiepileptics (e.g., Phenytoin):** May increase etoposide clearance and reduce efficacy; may also lead to decreased seizure control.
- **Warfarin:** May result in an elevated International Normalised Ratio (INR); monitor closely.
- **Plasma Protein Binding Displacement:** Phenylbutazone, sodium salicylate, and aspirin may displace etoposide from protein binding sites.
- **Myelosuppressive Agents:** Additive or synergistic effects are expected with other bone marrow-suppressing drugs.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category D:** Etoposide can cause fetal harm and is known to be teratogenic and embryotoxic in animal models. It should only be used if the clinical benefit outweighs the risk to the fetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** Etoposide is excreted in human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in infants, breastfeeding must be discontinued [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Etoposide causes irreversible testicular atrophy and spermatogenesis defects in animal studies. Preservation of sperm should be considered prior to therapy.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been systematically established. Use of products containing polysorbate 80 in premature infants has been linked to a life-threatening syndrome involving renal/hepatic failure and pulmonary deterioration.
- **Elderly Use:** Generally dosed as per adults, though caution is required due to increased likelihood of decreased renal function. Elderly patients may experience higher rates of leukopenia and anorexia.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosage adjustment is required based on creatinine clearance (CrCl):
    - **CrCl > 50 mL/min:** 100% of dose.
    - **CrCl 15–50 mL/min:** 75% of dose.
    - **CrCl < 15 mL/min:** Data unavailable; consider further dose reduction [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe dysfunction (bilirubin > 85 micromol/L). Use with caution in mild to moderate impairment.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Etoposide is a cell-cycle phase-specific agent, primarily acting in the S and G2 phases. It induces single-stranded DNA breaks and inhibits DNA synthesis. Unlike other podophyllotoxins, it does not interfere with microtubule assembly.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (approx. 97%). Minimally distributes into CSF. Crosses the placenta.
    - **Metabolism:** Approximately 66% metabolized; involves the CYP450 3A4 pathway.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 30% excreted unchanged in urine. Beta-phase half-life is approximately 11.5 hours.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Concentrate:** Store below 25°C. Protect from light.
- **Stability:** Diluted solutions (1:50 in 0.9% NaCl) are chemically stable for four hours. Any evidence of crystal formation necessitates disposal.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
