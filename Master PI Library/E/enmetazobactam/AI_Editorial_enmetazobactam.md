# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis was developed using official Product Information (PI) documents from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a concise reference format.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Clinicians should refer to their local regulatory authority's current approved labelling for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) on Hemodialysis:** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - Administration: Slow intravenous bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or infusion.
    - Acute Crisis: A loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    - Maximum Dose: The highest administered dose reported in sources is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - Administration: Slow intravenous bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - Dose Adjustments: Should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Monitoring
- Plasma free carnitine concentrations should be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L.
- Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations is recommended [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks/benefits before attempting re-administration [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a known seizure history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been reported [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulation Caution):** The safety of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Commonly Reported (General):** Transient nausea, vomiting, body odor, and gastritis.
- **ESRD Population (Double-blind trials, ≥5% incidence regardless of causality):**
    - **General:** Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (21–35%), flu syndrome (15–29%), headache (3–37%), chest pain (6–15%), asthenia (8–12%), fever (5–12%), abdominal pain (5–21%).
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%), palpitations (3–8%).
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%), anorexia (3–6%).
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%), rhinitis (6–11%).
    - **Neurological/Nervous:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%), insomnia (3–6%), depression (5–6%).
    - **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hypervolemia (3–12%).
    - **Other:** Anemia (3–12%), pruritus (3–8%).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not specified by letter system in available sources.
    - **Animal Data:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm.
    - **Human Data:** There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after exogenous administration. Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, S. cerevisiae, S. pombe) were negative. Long-term animal studies for carcinogenic potential have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders apply to paediatric patients (see Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Indicated for use in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. Use caution with oral formulations in this population due to metabolite accumulation (see Section 5). Levocarnitine is readily removed by dialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (found in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (found in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following intravenous bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis), trough levocarnitine concentrations increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. While the increase in concentrations is proven, the effects on clinical outcomes or signs/symptoms of carnitine deficiency in ESRD have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** When diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL), the solution is stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
