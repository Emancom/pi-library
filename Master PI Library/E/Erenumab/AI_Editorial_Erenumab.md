# Editorial Product Information: Erenumab

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of erenumab for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory specifics.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved documentation.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Erenumab (INN); a human immunoglobulin G2 (IgG2) monoclonal antibody.
- **Chemical Class:** Calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) receptor antagonist.
- **Brand Name:** **Aimovig** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N02CD01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Solution for injection in a single-use prefilled syringe or autoinjector.
    - Available as 70 mg/mL and 140 mg/mL [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Novartis Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Migraine Prophylaxis:** Indicated for the preventive treatment of migraine in adult patients [Australian]. 
    - *Note:* Efficacy has not been established in patients with fewer than 4 migraine days per month [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** The standard recommended dose is 70 mg administered subcutaneously once every 4 weeks.
- **Dose Escalation:** Some patients may derive additional clinical benefit from a dose of 140 mg every 4 weeks [Australian].
- **Initiation:** Treatment should be initiated by a specialist or neurologist experienced in migraine management [Australian].
- **Response Evaluation:** Clinical benefit should be assessed by the prescriber after 8 to 12 weeks of therapy. Continued need for treatment should be re-evaluated every 3 to 6 months [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Inject into the abdomen, thigh, or upper arm.
    - Rotate injection sites; avoid areas where the skin is bruised, red, tender, or indurated.
    - Before use, allow the device to reach room temperature (approx. 30 minutes) and protect from direct sunlight.
- **Missed Dose:** Administer as soon as possible. Subsequent doses should be scheduled monthly from the date of the last actual dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known clinically significant hypersensitivity to erenumab or any of the excipients (sucrose, glacial acetic acid, polysorbate 80, sodium hydroxide).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, angioedema, and rash, have occurred (some within minutes, others more than one week post-dose). If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue erenumab permanently and initiate appropriate therapy.
- **Constipation with Serious Complications:** Post-marketing reports include cases of constipation requiring hospitalisation and surgical intervention. The risk is higher in patients with a history of constipation or those taking concurrent medications that decrease gastrointestinal motility. Monitor for severe constipation.
- **Hypertension:** New-onset or worsening of pre-existing hypertension may occur, often after the first dose. Blood pressure should be monitored; discontinuation should be considered if an alternative etiology for blood pressure elevation is not found.
- **Latex Sensitivity:** The needle cover of the prefilled syringe and autoinjector contains dry natural rubber (a latex derivative), which may trigger allergic reactions in latex-sensitive individuals [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Most adverse drug reactions (ADRs) reported in clinical trials were mild to moderate in severity.

| System Organ Class | Adverse Reaction | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **General/Administration Site** | Injection site reactions (pain, erythema, pruritus) | Common (1–10%) |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Constipation | Common (1–10%) |
| **Musculoskeletal** | Muscle spasms | Common (1–10%) |
| **Skin/Subcutaneous** | Pruritus (generalized, rash, or pruritic rash) | Common (1–10%) |

**Serious Adverse Effects:**
- Serious hypersensitivity reactions.
- Severe constipation with complications (e.g., intestinal obstruction).
- Clinically significant hypertension.
- Post-marketing reports also include alopecia and oral sores (stomatitis, mucosal blistering).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Cytochrome P450 Enzymes:** As a monoclonal antibody, erenumab is not metabolised by CYP450 enzymes. Interactions with substrates, inducers, or inhibitors of these enzymes are unlikely.
- **Specific Interaction Studies:**
    - **Oral Contraceptives:** No pharmacokinetic interaction was observed with ethinylestradiol and norgestimate.
    - **Sumatriptan:** Concomitant use does not affect the pharmacokinetics of sumatriptan or resting blood pressure.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category B1 [Australian]:** Animal studies (monkeys) showed no embryo-fetal toxicity at exposures significantly higher than clinical doses. However, erenumab crosses the placental barrier. Use during pregnancy only if the potential benefit outweighs the potential risk.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if erenumab is excreted in human milk. A decision should be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the importance of the treatment to the mother.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in monkeys showed no adverse effects on reproductive organs or surrogate markers of fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients under 18 years of age.
- **Elderly Use:** Pharmacokinetics are not influenced by age. Clinical trials included limited numbers of patients $\ge$ 65 years; no specific dose adjustment is currently recommended.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for patients with mild to moderate renal impairment. Erenumab has not been studied in severe renal impairment (eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 $m^2$).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not studied; however, as a monoclonal antibody, hepatic clearance is not a major elimination pathway, and no dose adjustment is expected to be necessary.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Erenumab is a potent, specific antagonist that binds to the CGRP receptor. By competing with CGRP and inhibiting its function at the receptor, it modulates nociceptive signalling and vasodilation associated with migraine pathophysiology.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is ~82%. Peak serum concentrations occur around 6 days post-dose.
    - **Distribution:** Limited tissue distribution (steady-state volume of distribution ~7600 mL).
    - **Metabolism & Elimination:** Non-linear kinetics (at low concentrations via saturable target binding; at higher concentrations via non-specific proteolytic pathways).
    - **Half-life:** Approximately 28 days.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Chronic Migraine (Study 20120295):** 667 patients. Erenumab (70mg and 140mg) significantly reduced monthly migraine days (MMD) by approximately 2.5 days compared to placebo over 12 weeks.
- **Episodic Migraine (Study 20120296):** 955 patients. Both doses showed significant reduction in MMD over 6 months; 140mg showed a -1.85 day difference vs. placebo.
- **Long-term Safety:** Open-label studies (up to 5 years) demonstrate a consistent safety profile with sustained efficacy and no evidence of rebound or withdrawal effects upon discontinuation.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C.
- **Handling:** Keep in the original carton to protect from light. Do not freeze or shake.
- **Ex-refrigerator Stability:** If removed from the refrigerator, the product may be kept at room temperature (up to 30°C) for a maximum of 7 days. If not used within 7 days, it must be discarded.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Medicine)** [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Subject to additional monitoring in certain regions (e.g., Black Triangle Scheme in Australia) to facilitate rapid identification of new safety data.
