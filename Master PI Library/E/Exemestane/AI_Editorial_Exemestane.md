# Editorial Product Information: Exemestane

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (TGA).
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of the Product Information (PI) for **exemestane**. It is designed to provide a coherent, clinically accurate overview for healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is intended for informational purposes and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local regulatory documentation before initiating treatment.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Exemestane (an irreversible, steroidal aromatase inactivator; structurally related to androstenedione).
- **Brand Names:** APO-Exemestane [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 25 mg sugar-coated, circular, biconvex tablets [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Early Breast Cancer:** Sequential adjuvant treatment for postmenopausal women with estrogen receptor (ER) positive early breast cancer who have completed 2–3 years of adjuvant tamoxifen therapy [Australian].
- **Advanced Breast Cancer:** Treatment of ER-positive advanced breast cancer in women with natural or induced postmenopausal status whose disease has progressed following antiestrogen therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dose:** The standard dose is 25 mg (one tablet) once daily [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets should preferably be taken after a meal to enhance absorption [Australian].
- **Duration of Treatment:** 
    - **Early Breast Cancer:** Treatment should continue until the completion of five years of combined adjuvant hormonal therapy (tamoxifen followed by exemestane) or until tumour relapse [Australian].
    - **Advanced Breast Cancer:** Treatment should continue until tumour progression is evident [Australian].
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy:** Use is strictly contraindicated due to the risk of foetal harm [Australian].
- **Lactation:** Contraindicated in women who are breastfeeding [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to the active ingredient or any excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Menopausal Status:** **Exemestane** is only for use in postmenopausal women. Status should be confirmed via luteinising hormone (LH), follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), and oestradiol levels if there is any clinical doubt [Australian].
- **Bone Health:** As a potent oestrogen-lowering agent, **exemestane** is associated with reductions in bone mineral density (BMD) and an increased risk of fractures. Women with osteoporosis or at high risk should undergo BMD assessment at baseline and regular intervals. Prophylaxis or treatment for osteoporosis should be initiated where appropriate [Australian].
- **Vitamin D Deficiency:** Routine assessment of 25-hydroxy vitamin D levels is recommended prior to starting aromatase inhibitor therapy due to a high prevalence of deficiency in patients with breast cancer; supplementation should be provided if levels are low [Australian].
- **Tendon Disorders:** Use of third-generation aromatase inhibitors is linked to tendonitis, tenosynovitis, and potential tendon rupture. Patients should be monitored for signs and symptoms of tendon dysfunction [Australian].
- **Hepatic/Renal Impairment:** Caution is advised in patients with moderate to severe hepatic impairment or severe renal insufficiency due to increased systemic exposure (see Section 9) [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally mild to moderate and often related to oestrogen deprivation.

### Frequency Categories
- **Very Common (≥10%):** Pain, fatigue, hot flushes, nausea, abdominal pain, increased hepatic enzymes (ALT, GGT), increased bilirubin, increased alkaline phosphatase, headache, dizziness, insomnia, depression, hyperhidrosis, and joint/musculoskeletal pain (including arthralgia, myalgia, and stiffness) [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Peripheral oedema, asthenia, vomiting, constipation, diarrhoea, dyspepsia, anorexia, carpal tunnel syndrome, alopecia, rash, osteoporosis, and fractures [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Thrombocytopenia [Australian].
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Somnolence, hepatitis, cholestatic hepatitis, urticaria, pruritus, and acute generalised exanthematous pustulosis [Australian].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Cardiovascular:** In clinical trials, non-significant increases in myocardial infarction (0.6%) and cardiac failure (1.1%) were observed compared to tamoxifen [Australian].
- **Musculoskeletal:** Tendon rupture [Australian].
- **Haematological:** Decreases in white blood cells (specifically lymphocytes) and leukopenia have been observed [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Estrogens:** Should not be co-administered as they negate the pharmacological action of **exemestane** [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Potent inducers of CYP3A4 may decrease **exemestane** plasma levels. While ketoconazole (a CYP3A4 inhibitor) showed no significant effect, caution is warranted with inducers [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Substrates:** Use caution when co-administering with drugs metabolised via CYP3A4 that have a narrow therapeutic window [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category C.** 
    - Animal studies indicate embryotoxicity, fetotoxicity, and abortifacient effects. **Exemestane** disrupts oestrogen-dependent metabolism. If pregnancy occurs during treatment, the patient must be informed of the potential for foetal loss or harm [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. It is unknown if the drug is excreted in human milk, but it has been detected in the milk of lactating rats [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal data show reduced fertility in female rats (decreased litter size) at doses eight times the human equivalent [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for use in children [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustments are required; systemic exposure does not correlate significantly with age [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** In severe renal insufficiency (CLcr ≤ 30 mL/min), systemic exposure is doubled. No specific dose adjustment is required due to high tolerability, but use with caution [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** In moderate to severe hepatic impairment, systemic exposure is 2–3 times higher. No specific dose adjustment is required, but use with caution [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Exemestane** is a "suicidal" (irreversible) steroidal aromatase inactivator. It binds to the active site of the aromatase enzyme, preventing the conversion of androgens into oestrogens in peripheral tissues [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Reduces whole-body aromatisation by 98% and lowers serum oestrogen concentrations by 80–90% at the 25 mg dose. It lacks significant progestogenic or oestrogenic activity [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; peak plasma levels (18 ng/mL) reached within 2 hours. Bioavailability is limited by first-pass metabolism. Food increases plasma levels by 30–40% [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** 90% protein bound; negligible distribution into red blood cells [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised via CYP3A4 (oxidation) and aldoketoreductases (reduction). Metabolites are either inactive or less active than the parent drug [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 24 hours. Excreted in nearly equal proportions in urine and faeces; <1% excreted unchanged in urine [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Early Breast Cancer (Study 031/IES):** A multicentre, double-blind trial of 4,724 postmenopausal women. Sequential treatment with **exemestane** after 2–3 years of tamoxifen significantly improved disease-free survival (DFS) compared to 5 years of tamoxifen. At 119-month follow-up, **exemestane** reduced the risk of recurrence by 14% (HR 0.86) and showed a significant 14% reduction in the risk of death (adjusted HR 0.86) [Australian].
- **Advanced Breast Cancer:** A Phase III study of 763 patients showed that **exemestane** 25 mg daily provided significantly longer time to progression (20.3 vs 16.6 weeks) and longer survival compared to megestrol acetate [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store in a cool, dry place below 30°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Not specified in available sources (refer to packaging) [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
