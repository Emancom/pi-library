# Editorial Product Information: elbasvir + grazoprevir

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **elbasvir + grazoprevir**
- Chemical class: Combination of an HCV NS5A inhibitor (elbasvir) and an HCV NS3/4A protease inhibitor (grazoprevir)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 2016 [US].
- Date of last revision/update of source PI: March 2026 [US].
- Withdrawals, suspensions, restrictions: Not specified in available sources for jurisdictions other than the statements above.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of chronic hepatitis C virus (HCV) genotype 1 or 4 infection in adults and pediatric patients 12 years of age and older or weighing at least 30 kg. [US-approved]
- Use with ribavirin is indicated for certain patient subpopulations as described under Dosage and Administration. [US]
- HCV/HIV-1 coinfected patients: follow the same dosing recommendations as for HCV-monoinfected patients. [US]

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult and pediatric ≥12 years or ≥30 kg)
- Recommended dose: one tablet containing 50 mg elbasvir + 100 mg grazoprevir taken orally once daily with or without food.

### Dosing by genotype and prior treatment (recommended regimens and durations) [US]
- Genotype 1a, treatment-naïve or peginterferon/ribavirin (PegIFN/RBV)-experienced:
  - Without baseline NS5A resistance-associated polymorphisms at positions 28, 30, 31, or 93: elbasvir + grazoprevir for 12 weeks.
  - With baseline NS5A resistance-associated polymorphisms at positions 28, 30, 31, or 93: elbasvir + grazoprevir plus ribavirin for 16 weeks.
- Genotype 1b, treatment-naïve or PegIFN/RBV-experienced:
  - Elbasvir + grazoprevir for 12 weeks.
- PegIFN/RBV/protease inhibitor-experienced (genotype 1a or 1b):
  - Elbasvir + grazoprevir plus ribavirin for 12 weeks.
  - Note: the optimal ZEPATIER-based regimen and duration for PegIFN/RBV/PI‑experienced genotype 1a patients with baseline NS5A polymorphisms at positions 28, 30, 31, or 93 has not been established. [US]
- Genotype 4:
  - Treatment-naïve: elbasvir + grazoprevir for 12 weeks.
  - PegIFN/RBV-experienced: elbasvir + grazoprevir plus ribavirin for 16 weeks.

### Ribavirin
- When ribavirin is required, administer ribavirin dosing and modifications per the ribavirin product labeling (weight-based dosing for CrCl > 50 mL/min; refer to ribavirin prescribing information for CrCl ≤ 50 mL/min and pediatric dosing). [US]

### Special testing and administration instructions
- Test all patients for hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection prior to initiation by measuring HBsAg and anti-HBc; manage and monitor as clinically indicated. [US]
- For genotype 1a infections, testing for baseline NS5A resistance-associated polymorphisms at positions 28, 30, 31, or 93 is recommended prior to initiating therapy to guide regimen selection. [US]
- Hepatic laboratory testing: perform prior to therapy, at treatment week 8, and as clinically indicated. For patients receiving 16 weeks of therapy, perform additional hepatic laboratory testing at week 12. More frequent testing may be warranted in patients with compensated cirrhosis (Child-Pugh A) or evidence of advanced liver disease. [US]

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
(Include all contraindications stated in available sources.)
- Moderate or severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh B or C). [US]
- Any history of prior hepatic decompensation. [US]
- Concomitant use with inhibitors of organic anion transporting polypeptides 1B1/3 (OATP1B1/3) that are known or expected to significantly increase grazoprevir plasma concentrations. [US]
- Concomitant use with strong inducers of cytochrome P450 3A (CYP3A). [US]
- Concomitant use with efavirenz. [US]
- When administered with ribavirin, the contraindications applicable to ribavirin also apply to the combination regimen (refer to ribavirin labeling). [US]
- Specific drugs listed as contraindicated with elbasvir + grazoprevir (not exhaustive of all possible CYP3A inducers or OATP1B1/3 inhibitors): 
  - Anticonvulsants: phenytoin, carbamazepine.
  - Antimycobacterial: rifampin.
  - Herbal products: St. John's wort (Hypericum perforatum).
  - HIV medications (inducers/reducers/inhibitors noted): efavirenz (reduces exposure), atazanavir, darunavir, lopinavir, saquinavir, tipranavir (these protease inhibitors may increase grazoprevir exposure and risk of ALT elevations).
  - Immunosuppressant: cyclosporine (may increase grazoprevir concentrations and risk of ALT elevations).
  - This list is not exhaustive; other strong CYP3A inducers and OATP1B1/3 inhibitors may also be contraindicated. [US]

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed/boxed-style warning
- **Risk of Hepatitis B Virus Reactivation:** HBV reactivation has been reported in patients coinfected with HCV and HBV who were undergoing or had completed treatment with HCV direct-acting antivirals and were not receiving HBV antiviral therapy; some cases have resulted in fulminant hepatitis, hepatic failure, and death. Test all patients for current or prior HBV infection before initiating therapy and monitor coinfected patients during and after treatment; initiate appropriate management for HBV infection as clinically indicated. [US]

### General warnings and precautions
- ALT elevations: Late ALT elevations (typically at or after week 8) have been observed. In clinical trials, 1% of subjects experienced ALT elevations from normal to >5× upper limit of normal (ULN). Subpopulations with higher rates of late ALT elevations included female sex, Asian race, and patients ≥65 years. Consider discontinuing therapy if ALT remains >10× ULN or if ALT elevation is accompanied by signs or symptoms of liver inflammation or increasing bilirubin, alkaline phosphatase, or INR. [US]
- Hepatic decompensation/failure: Postmarketing cases of hepatic decompensation/failure, including fatal outcomes, have been reported in patients treated with NS3/4A protease inhibitor–containing regimens, including elbasvir + grazoprevir. Reported events occurred in patients with baseline cirrhosis (with and without moderate or severe liver impairment) and in some patients without cirrhosis. Monitor patients for clinical and laboratory evidence of hepatic decompensation; discontinue if decompensation occurs. [US]
- Risks associated with ribavirin combination therapy: When used with ribavirin, the full spectrum of ribavirin warnings and precautions (including pregnancy avoidance) apply. Refer to ribavirin prescribing information for details. [US]
- Drug interactions: Concomitant use with certain drugs may result in clinically significant interactions leading to increased exposure of elbasvir/grazoprevir or decreased exposure causing loss of therapeutic effect and potential resistance. See Drug Interactions section. [US]
- Clearance of HCV infection may alter hepatic function and thereby affect the disposition and safe use of concomitant medications (e.g., drugs requiring monitoring for INR or blood glucose); monitor and adjust doses of concomitant medications as needed. [US]

### Monitoring requirements
- Test all patients for HBsAg and anti-HBc prior to initiating therapy. Monitor for HBV reactivation in patients with evidence of current or prior HBV infection during treatment and post-treatment follow-up. [US]
- Perform hepatic laboratory testing before therapy, at week 8, and as clinically indicated; if course is 16 weeks, perform additional testing at week 12. More frequent monitoring in patients with compensated cirrhosis or other evidence of advanced liver disease. [US]
- Monitor for symptoms of liver injury (e.g., fatigue, weakness, anorexia, nausea, vomiting, jaundice, pale stools) and advise patients to report such symptoms promptly. [US]

### Specific clinical precautions
- NS5A resistance-associated polymorphism testing is recommended in genotype 1a-infected patients to determine appropriate regimen and duration. Lower SVR12 rates were observed in genotype 1a-infected patients with baseline NS5A resistance-associated polymorphisms at amino acid positions 28, 30, 31, or 93. [US]

## 7. Adverse Effects
- All adverse reactions below are taken from the available product information. Where the PI reports events specifically when elbasvir + grazoprevir is used with ribavirin, this is noted.

### Summary of common adverse reactions (placebo-controlled trials)
- In subjects receiving elbasvir + grazoprevir for 12 weeks, the most commonly reported adverse reactions (≥5% in placebo-controlled trials) were: fatigue, headache, and nausea. [US]
- In subjects receiving elbasvir + grazoprevir plus ribavirin for 16 weeks, the most commonly reported moderate or severe adverse reactions (≥5%) included anemia and headache. [US]

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Mechanisms of interaction
- Elbasvir + grazoprevir exposure is affected by:
  - CYP3A metabolism: strong CYP3A inducers can significantly decrease elbasvir and grazoprevir concentrations; strong CYP3A inhibitors can increase concentrations. [US]
  - OATP1B1/3 transport: inhibitors of OATP1B1/3 can significantly increase grazoprevir plasma concentrations. [US]
- Efavirenz (a CYP3A inducer) markedly reduced grazoprevir exposure (≥80% reduction reported). [US]

### Clinically significant interactions and management
- Concomitant use that may lead to loss of virologic response due to decreased elbasvir/grazoprevir exposure (strong CYP3A inducers): not recommended/contraindicated. Examples include: phenytoin, carbamazepine, rifampin, St. John's wort. [US]
- Concomitant use that increases grazoprevir plasma concentrations and may increase risk of ALT elevations (OATP1B1/3 inhibitors and certain HIV protease inhibitors): contraindicated. Examples include: atazanavir, darunavir, lopinavir, saquinavir, tipranavir, cyclosporine. [US]
- Efavirenz: contraindicated due to marked reduction in grazoprevir exposure. [US]
- Moderate CYP3A inducers: co-administration not recommended as they may decrease elbasvir/grazoprevir plasma concentrations. [US]
- Certain strong CYP3A inhibitors: co-administration not recommended as they may increase plasma concentrations of elbasvir/grazoprevir. [US]
- Ribavirin: when used in combination, refer to ribavirin labeling for drug interaction considerations. [US]

### Monitoring and management notes
- Consult full prescribing information for a comprehensive list of established and potentially significant drug interactions and for specific management steps. [US]
- Because hepatic function may change after HCV clearance, monitor concomitant drugs that require hepatic metabolism monitoring (e.g., warfarin/INR, drugs affecting blood glucose), and adjust dosages as clinically indicated. [US]

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Indicated in pediatric patients 12 years of age and older or weighing at least 30 kg. The recommended dosage is one tablet once daily with or without food (same as adult dosing). Details of pediatric clinical trial outcomes and specific dosing by weight/age beyond the age/weight threshold are not provided in the available sources. [US]

### Geriatric use
- Specific dose adjustments for elderly patients are not provided. Late ALT elevations were more frequently reported in patients aged ≥65 years (2% [3/177]); monitor hepatic function closely in older patients. [US]

### Renal impairment
- No dosage adjustment is recommended for any degree of renal impairment, including patients on hemodialysis. Administer elbasvir + grazoprevir with or without ribavirin according to recommended regimens; for ribavirin dosing in renal impairment, refer to ribavirin prescribing information. [US]

### Hepatic impairment
- Mild hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh A): no dosage adjustment recommended. [US]
- Moderate or severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh B or C): contraindicated due to significantly increased grazoprevir plasma concentrations and increased risk of ALT elevations and hepatic decompensation/failure. [US]
- Any history of prior hepatic decompensation: contraindicated. [US]

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **Elbasvir**: HCV NS5A inhibitor.
- **Grazoprevir**: HCV NS3/4A protease inhibitor.
- The combination acts on two different viral targets to inhibit HCV replication. [US]

### Pharmacokinetics
- Specific numeric PK parameters (absorption rates, Cmax, AUC, half-life, bioavailability) are not provided in the available sources.
- Clinically relevant PK interactions:
  - OATP1B1/3 inhibition significantly increases grazoprevir plasma concentration. [US]
  - Strong CYP3A inducers significantly decrease elbasvir and grazoprevir concentrations; strong CYP3A inhibitors may increase concentrations. [US]
  - Efavirenz reduces grazoprevir exposure by ≥80%. [US]
- Renal impairment including hemodialysis: no dose adjustment necessary. [US]
- Hepatic impairment: moderate/severe hepatic impairment increases grazoprevir exposure significantly; contraindicated in Child-Pugh B and C. [US]

### Pharmacodynamics
- Baseline NS5A resistance-associated polymorphisms (positions 28, 30, 31, or 93) in genotype 1a infections are associated with lower rates of sustained virologic response (SVR12) with 12‑week elbasvir + grazoprevir regimens; testing is recommended to guide therapy. [US]

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Safety and efficacy were evaluated in a program that included randomized, placebo-controlled and uncontrolled Phase 2 and 3 trials in approximately 1,700 adult subjects with compensated HCV infection (with and without cirrhosis) and in pediatric subjects. [US]
- Key trials referenced in available sources:
  - C-EDGE TN: Phase 3, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in treatment‑naïve adults (n=421) comparing elbasvir + grazoprevir for 12 weeks vs placebo; adverse reaction data reported. [US]
  - C-EDGE COINFECTION: trial in HCV/HIV-1 coinfected subjects (details not provided in available sources). [US]
  - C-SURFER: trial in subjects with genotype 1 HCV and severe renal impairment including subjects on hemodialysis (details not provided in available sources). [US]
  - Pediatric trial(s) in genotype 1 or 4 chronic hepatitis C infection are referenced; specific outcomes and dosing beyond the stated pediatric indication are not provided in the available sources. [US]
- Efficacy notes:
  - SVR12 rates were lower in genotype 1a-infected patients with one or more baseline NS5A resistance-associated polymorphisms at amino acid positions 28, 30, 31, or 93 when treated for 12 weeks (hence the recommendation to test genotype 1a patients for NS5A polymorphisms). [US]
- Detailed trial results (numerical SVR rates, confidence intervals, subgroup analyses) are not specified in the available excerpts; consult full prescribing information for complete trial data. [US]
