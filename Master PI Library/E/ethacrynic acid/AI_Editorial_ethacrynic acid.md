# Editorial Product Information: Ethacrynic Acid

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of ethacrynic acid, synthesised for healthcare professionals. 
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Ethacrynic acid (unsaturated ketone derivative of an aryloxyacetic acid).
*   **Brand Name(s):** Not specified in provided source; marketed as generic **Ethacrynic Acid Tablets, USP** [US].
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    *   Oral Tablet: 25 mg [US].
    *   Injectable: Ethacrynate sodium (intravenous) [US].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral, Intravenous.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Upsher-Smith Laboratories, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Edema Management:** Treatment of edema where a potent diuretic is required, including that associated with:
    *   Congestive heart failure [US].
    *   Cirrhosis of the liver [US].
    *   Renal disease, including nephrotic syndrome [US].
*   **Short-term Management of Ascites:** Due to malignancy, idiopathic edema, or lymphedema [US].
*   **Pediatric Edema:** Short-term management in hospitalized pediatric patients (excluding infants) with congenital heart disease or nephrotic syndrome [US].
*   **Acute Pulmonary Edema:** Specifically for intravenous ethacrynate sodium when rapid diuresis is required [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing (Oral)
*   **Initiation:** The smallest dose required to achieve gradual weight loss (0.5–1.0 kg/day) is recommended.
*   **Standard Regimen:** 
    *   **Day 1:** 50 mg once daily after a meal [US].
    *   **Day 2:** 50 mg twice daily (if necessary) [US].
    *   **Day 3:** 100 mg in the morning and 50–100 mg after the evening meal, depending on response [US].
*   **Maintenance:** Usually 50 mg to 200 mg daily on a continuous or intermittent schedule.
*   **Maximum Dose:** Up to 200 mg twice daily may be required in severe, refractory edema [US].
*   **Titration:** Adjustments should be made in 25–50 mg increments [US].

### Pediatric Dosing (Oral)
*   **Initial Dose:** 25 mg daily [US].
*   **Titration:** Increments of 25 mg to reach effective maintenance [US].
*   **Contraindication:** Therapy is contraindicated in infants [US].

### Administration Instructions
*   Oral doses should be taken after meals to minimize gastrointestinal effects [US].
*   Intermittent dosing (e.g., alternate days or rest periods) is recommended to allow for electrolyte correction [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   **Anuria** [US].
*   **Infants:** Safety and efficacy in infants have not been established [US].
*   **Severe, Progressive Renal Disease:** Discontinue if increasing azotemia, oliguria, or electrolyte imbalances occur [US].
*   **Severe Diarrhea:** If severe watery diarrhea occurs, the drug must be permanently discontinued [US].
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to ethacrynic acid or any component of the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Profound Diuresis:** **Ethacrynic acid is a potent diuretic. Excessive doses can lead to profound water and electrolyte depletion.** Careful medical supervision and individualized dosing are mandatory [US].
*   **Hepatic Coma:** In patients with advanced hepatic cirrhosis, electrolyte imbalances may precipitate hepatic encephalopathy and death. Initiation in cirrhotic patients with ascites is best performed in a hospital setting [US].
*   **Ototoxicity:** Deafness, tinnitus, and vertigo (with a sense of fullness in the ears) have occurred, most frequently in patients with renal impairment or those receiving high-dose intravenous therapy. Hearing loss is usually reversible (1–24 hours) but may be permanent [US].
*   **Hypotension and Thromboembolism:** Vigorous diuresis may cause acute hypotension. In elderly cardiac patients, rapid plasma volume contraction may lead to fatal cerebral thrombosis or pulmonary emboli [US].
*   **Monitoring Requirements:** Frequent determinations of serum electrolytes, CO2, and BUN are required early in therapy and periodically during active diuresis [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Anorexia, abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, dysphagia, and diarrhea (frequent with high doses). GI bleeding and rare acute pancreatitis have been reported [US].
*   **Metabolic:** Reversible hyperuricemia, acute gout, hyperglycemia, and symptomatic hypoglycemia with convulsions (reported in uremic patients). Rarely, jaundice or abnormal liver function tests [US].
*   **Hematologic:** Rarely, agranulocytosis, severe neutropenia (in critically ill patients), thrombocytopenia, and Henoch-Schönlein purpura [US].
*   **Central Nervous System:** Headache, fatigue, apprehension, and confusion [US].
*   **Special Senses:** Blurred vision, deafness, and tinnitus [US].
*   **Miscellaneous:** Skin rash, fever, chills, and hematuria [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Lithium:** Diuretics reduce the renal clearance of lithium, posing a high risk of lithium toxicity. Concurrent use is generally not recommended [US].
*   **Ototoxic Drugs:** Ethacrynic acid may potentiate the ototoxicity of aminoglycosides and certain cephalosporin antibiotics; concurrent use should be avoided [US].
*   **Warfarin:** Ethacrynic acid may displace warfarin from plasma proteins; anticoagulant dose reductions may be necessary [US].
*   **NSAIDs:** Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory agents may reduce the diuretic and natriuretic effects of loop diuretics [US].
*   **Corticosteroids:** Increased risk of gastric hemorrhage and potassium depletion [US].
*   **Antihypertensives:** May cause orthostatic hypotension; dosage adjustments of antihypertensives may be required [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   **US Category:** Not explicitly staged by letter, but the PI states ethacrynic acid should be used during pregnancy **only if clearly needed** [US]. 
    *   **Data:** Animal studies at 50x human doses showed no external fetal abnormalities. Reduction in fetal body weights occurred in rats at 100 mg/kg/day [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if the drug is excreted in human milk. A decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the importance of the medication to the mother [US].
*   **Fertility:** No effect on fertility was observed in rat or mouse studies at 10x the human dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Indicated for children (excluding infants) with congenital heart disease or nephrotic syndrome. IV use is not established in pediatric patients [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of hemoconcentration and thromboembolic episodes. Monitor renal function closely as the drug is substantially excreted by the kidneys [US].
*   **Renal Impairment:** Effective in patients with renal insufficiency, but carries a higher risk of ototoxicity. Discontinue if azotemia or oliguria increases during treatment of progressive renal disease [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with extreme caution in advanced cirrhosis to avoid precipitating hepatic coma [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Loop diuretic that inhibits the reabsorption of sodium and chloride in the ascending limb of the loop of Henle and the proximal and distal tubules [US].
*   **Pharmacodynamics:** Does not inhibit carbonic anhydrase. Effective regardless of clinical acidosis or alkalosis. Increases excretion of sodium, chloride, potassium, and hydrogen ions [US].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Onset (Oral):** Within 30 minutes [US].
    *   **Peak Effect (Oral):** Approximately 2 hours [US].
    *   **Duration (Oral):** 6 to 8 hours [US].
    *   **Onset (IV):** Within 5 minutes [US].
    *   **Distribution:** Does not cross the blood-brain barrier [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Pivotal Data:** Specific pivotal trial names/designs are not detailed in the provided PI. Use in pediatric patients is supported by empiric evidence rather than well-controlled trials [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store in a tightly closed container at 25°C (77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C (59°F–86°F) [US].
*   **Handling:** Tablets should be dispensed in well-closed containers [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription only).
