# Editorial Product Information: Eluxadoline

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
*   **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of official product information for healthcare professionals. It provides a concise, clinically focused overview of the pharmacological profile and regulatory requirements for eluxadoline.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Eluxadoline (a mu-opioid receptor agonist, delta-opioid receptor antagonist, and kappa-opioid receptor agonist).
*   **Brand Name(s):** **Viberzi** [US].
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    *   Film-coated tablets: 75 mg (capsule-shaped, pale-yellow to light tan, debossed with "FX75").
    *   Film-coated tablets: 100 mg (capsule-shaped, pink-orange to peach, debossed with "FX100").
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AbbVie, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Irritable Bowel Syndrome with Diarrhea (IBS-D):** Indicated for the treatment of adults with IBS-D [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
*   **Standard Adult Dosing:** 100 mg taken orally twice daily with food [US].
*   **Reduced Dosing (75 mg twice daily with food):** Recommended for patients who:
    *   Are unable to tolerate the 100 mg dose.
    *   Are receiving concomitant OATP1B1 inhibitors.
    *   Have mild (Child-Pugh Class A) or moderate (Child-Pugh Class B) hepatic impairment.
    *   Have moderate or severe renal impairment (eGFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m²), including end-stage renal disease (ESRD) not yet on dialysis (eGFR <15 mL/min/1.73 m²) [US].
*   **Administration Instructions:** Tablets should be taken with food. 
*   **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, the patient should take the next dose at the regular time; two doses should not be taken at once to make up for a missed dose [US].
*   **Discontinuation:** Therapy must be discontinued if severe constipation develops [US].

## 4. Contraindications
**VIBERZI** is contraindicated in patients with:
*   **Absence of a gallbladder:** Significantly increases the risk of serious pancreatitis and Sphincter of Oddi spasm [US].
*   **Biliary duct obstruction** or known/suspected **Sphincter of Oddi disease/dysfunction** [US].
*   **Alcoholism, alcohol abuse, or addiction:** Defined as consuming more than three alcoholic beverages per day (increased risk of acute pancreatitis) [US].
*   **History of pancreatitis** or structural diseases of the pancreas (including known or suspected pancreatic duct obstruction) [US].
*   **Severe hepatic impairment** (Child-Pugh Class C) [US].
*   **Severe or chronic constipation** or sequelae from constipation; known or suspected mechanical gastrointestinal obstruction [US].
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity reaction to eluxadoline [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Pancreatitis:** Serious and sometimes fatal cases have been reported, primarily in patients without a gallbladder. Most cases occurred within the first week of treatment, often after just one or two doses. Evaluate alcohol intake and monitor for abdominal pain radiating to the back or shoulder [US].
*   **Sphincter of Oddi Spasm:** May result in pancreatitis or hepatic enzyme elevation with acute biliary-type pain. Risk is highest in those without a gallbladder. Discontinue immediately if symptoms occur and do not restart [US].
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Anaphylaxis and angioedema (swelling of face/throat, dyspnea) have been reported [US].
*   **Constipation:** Can lead to hospitalization, intestinal obstruction, perforation, and fecal impaction. Avoid concurrent use with other drugs that cause constipation (e.g., alosetron, opioids) [US].

### Serious Adverse Effects
*   **Pancreatitis:** 0.2% to 0.3% incidence in clinical trials [US].
*   **Sphincter of Oddi Spasm:** 0.2% to 0.8% incidence; 80% occurred within the first week of treatment [US].
*   **Postmarketing Reports:** Angioedema, dyspnea, and intestinal perforation [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **OATP1B1 Inhibitors (e.g., Cyclosporine, Gemfibrozil, certain Antiretrovirals):** Increase eluxadoline exposure (cyclosporine increases AUC 4.4-fold). **Dose reduction of eluxadoline to 75 mg BID is required** [US].
*   **OATP1B1 and BCRP Substrates (e.g., Rosuvastatin):** Eluxadoline may increase exposure to these drugs. Use the lowest effective dose of rosuvastatin [US].
*   **Constipating Agents (e.g., Alosetron, Anticholinergics, Opioids):** Increased risk of severe constipation. Avoid chronic use; loperamide should only be used occasionally for acute management and stopped immediately if constipation occurs [US].
*   **CYP Enzymes:** No clinically meaningful interactions expected with CYP3A4 substrates (e.g., midazolam) or oral contraceptives [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [US: Not assigned a specific letter category]. Animal studies in rats and rabbits at 51 and 115 times human exposure showed no teratogenic effects. There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women; use only if the potential benefit justifies the risk [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Eluxadoline is present in rat milk. No data are available regarding its presence in human milk or its effects on the breastfed infant. Consider the developmental/health benefits of breastfeeding alongside the mother's clinical need [US].
*   **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility or reproductive performance were observed in rats at 10 times human exposure [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [US].
*   **Geriatric Use:** Patients ≥65 years reported higher rates of adverse reactions (66% vs 59%) and serious adverse reactions (9% vs 4%) compared to younger patients. Use with caution [US].
*   **Renal Impairment:** Reduce dose to 75 mg BID for moderate/severe impairment (eGFR <60) and ESRD not on dialysis. No adjustment needed for mild impairment [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    *   **Mild (Class A) to Moderate (Class B):** Reduce dose to 75 mg BID.
    *   **Severe (Class C):** Contraindicated (exposure increases 16-fold) [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Eluxadoline interacts with opioid receptors in the gut. It is a mu-opioid receptor agonist, a delta-opioid receptor antagonist, and a kappa-opioid receptor agonist [US].
*   **Pharmacodynamics:** Does not prolong the QT interval at 10 times the maximum recommended dose [US].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption:** Peak plasma levels (Tmax) occur at 1.5 hours (fed) and 2 hours (fasting). A high-fat meal decreases Cmax by 50% and AUC by 60% [US].
    *   **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is 81% [US].
    *   **Metabolism:** Minimal involvement of CYP450 or UGT pathways [US].
    *   **Elimination:** Primarily fecal (82.2%); renal excretion is <1%. Mean elimination half-life is 3.7 to 6 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Trial Design:** Two randomized, multi-center, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials (Study 1 and 2) in adults with IBS-D (Rome III criteria) [US].
*   **Primary Outcome:** Composite responder rate (improvement in worst abdominal pain score by ≥30% and reduction in Bristol Stool Scale to <5 on at least 50% of days over 12 weeks) [US].
*   **Results:** In both studies, eluxadoline (75 mg and 100 mg BID) demonstrated significantly higher responder rates (range 24%–30%) compared to placebo (16%–17%) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
*   **Excursions:** Permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
