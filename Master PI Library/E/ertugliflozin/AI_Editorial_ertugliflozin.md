# Editorial Product Information: Ertugliflozin and Sitagliptin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document provided for the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a single clinical reference.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Ertugliflozin (as ertugliflozin L-pyroglutamic acid) and Sitagliptin (as sitagliptin phosphate monohydrate).
- **Chemical Class:** Ertugliflozin is a sodium-glucose co-transporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitor; Sitagliptin is a dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP-4) inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** **STEGLUJAN** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available source.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets [US]:
        - 5 mg ertugliflozin / 100 mg sitagliptin (Beige, almond-shaped, debossed with "554").
        - 15 mg ertugliflozin / 100 mg sitagliptin (Brown, almond-shaped, debossed with "555").
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Merck Sharp & Dohme LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM):** Indicated as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycemic control in adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus [US].
- **Limitations of Use [US]:**
    - Not recommended for patients with type 1 diabetes mellitus (increased risk of ketoacidosis).
    - Not studied in patients with a history of pancreatitis; it is unknown if these patients are at increased risk for pancreatitis during treatment.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The recommended starting dose is 5 mg ertugliflozin / 100 mg sitagliptin once daily. 
- **Titration:** For patients requiring additional glycemic control who tolerate the starting dose, the dosage may be increased to 15 mg ertugliflozin / 100 mg sitagliptin once daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** Take once daily in the morning, with or without food [US].
- **Pre-initiation Requirements:**
    - Assess renal function (eGFR) prior to initiation and periodically thereafter [US].
    - Assess and correct volume status in patients at risk of volume depletion (e.g., elderly, renal impairment, low systolic blood pressure, or those on diuretics) [US].
- **Temporary Interruption for Surgery:** Withhold treatment for at least 4 days prior to major surgery or procedures associated with prolonged fasting to reduce the risk of ketoacidosis. Resume only when the patient is clinically stable and oral intake has resumed [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Instruct patients to take the dose as soon as remembered. If it is nearly time for the next dose, the missed dose should be skipped; doses should not be doubled [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Renal Impairment:** Severe renal impairment (eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m²), end-stage renal disease (ESRD), or patients on dialysis [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** History of serious hypersensitivity reactions (e.g., anaphylaxis or angioedema) to ertugliflozin, sitagliptin, or any component of the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA):** Risk is significantly increased in T1DM. DKA may occur in T2DM even with near-normal blood glucose levels (<250 mg/dL). Discontinue immediately if ketoacidosis is suspected [US].
- **Pancreatitis:** Postmarketing reports include fatal and non-fatal hemorrhagic or necrotizing pancreatitis associated with sitagliptin. Discontinue promptly if pancreatitis is suspected [US].
- **Lower Limb Amputation:** Increased risk observed in clinical trials with ertugliflozin. Monitor for infections, new pain, tenderness, or ulcers in the lower limbs [US].
- **Acute Renal Failure:** Sitagliptin has been associated with worsening renal function, sometimes requiring dialysis. Monitor renal function throughout therapy [US].
- **Volume Depletion:** SGLT2 inhibitors cause osmotic diuresis, potentially leading to symptomatic hypotension or acute kidney injury. Assess and monitor volume status [US].
- **Genitourinary Infections:** Increased risk of urinary tract infections (UTIs) and genital mycotic infections. Serious cases of **Fournier’s Gangrene** (necrotizing fasciitis of the perineum) have been reported, requiring urgent surgical intervention [US].
- **Heart Failure:** Observed with other DPP-4 inhibitors. Consider risks/benefits in patients with a history of heart failure or renal impairment [US].
- **Hypoglycemia:** Risk increases when used in combination with insulin or insulin secretagogues. A lower dose of the concomitant agent may be required [US].
- **Severe and Disabling Arthralgia:** Reported with DPP-4 inhibitors; symptoms may resolve upon discontinuation [US].
- **Bullous Pemphigoid:** Postmarketing reports with DPP-4 inhibitors may require hospitalization. Discontinue if suspected [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):**
    - Female genital mycotic infections (at 15 mg ertugliflozin dose: 12.2%) [US].
- **Common (1–10%):**
    - **Ertugliflozin-related:** Female genital mycotic infections (at 5 mg dose), male genital mycotic infections, UTIs, headache, vaginal pruritus, increased urination, thirst, weight decrease [US].
    - **Sitagliptin-related:** Upper respiratory tract infection, nasopharyngitis, headache [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** 
    - Ketoacidosis, acute pancreatitis, acute renal failure, Fournier’s gangrene, and lower limb amputations [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Insulin and Secretagogues:** Increased risk of hypoglycemia; requires dose monitoring of the insulin/secretagogue [US].
- **Lithium:** SGLT2 inhibitors may increase renal lithium excretion, potentially decreasing serum lithium levels. Monitor serum lithium closely [US].
- **Laboratory Interference:**
    - **Urine Glucose Test:** SGLT2 inhibitors increase urinary glucose excretion; use alternative methods for glycemic monitoring [US].
    - **1,5-anhydroglucitol (1,5-AG) Assay:** Measurements are unreliable; use alternative methods for glycemic monitoring [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Not recommended during the second and third trimesters. Animal data for ertugliflozin showed adverse renal developmental effects (pelvic and tubule dilatations) during periods corresponding to human renal development [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. There is a potential risk to the developing human kidney based on animal data (ertugliflozin and sitagliptin are excreted in rat milk) [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no effects on fertility at exposures significantly higher than human clinical doses [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment recommended based on age. However, elderly patients are more prone to renal impairment and volume depletion; more frequent renal monitoring is advised [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - eGFR ≥ 45: No dose adjustment [US].
    - eGFR 30 to < 45: Initiation is not recommended. Continued use is not recommended [US].
    - eGFR < 30: Contraindicated [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild or moderate impairment. Not studied and not recommended in patients with severe hepatic impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Ertugliflozin:** Inhibits SGLT2 in the proximal renal tubules, reducing glucose reabsorption and increasing urinary glucose excretion [US].
    - **Sitagliptin:** Inhibits the DPP-4 enzyme, slowing the inactivation of incretin hormones (GLP-1 and GIP), thereby increasing insulin release and decreasing glucagon levels in a glucose-dependent manner [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics [US]:**
    - **Absorption:** Ertugliflozin bioavailability ~100%; Sitagliptin ~87%. 
    - **Metabolism:** Ertugliflozin is primarily metabolized via UGT1A9 and UGT2B7 (O-glucuronidation). Sitagliptin is primarily excreted unchanged (79%) with minor CYP3A4 metabolism.
    - **Elimination:** Ertugliflozin half-life ~16.6 hours; Sitagliptin half-life ~12.4 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Glycemic Control:** Studied in three phase 3 trials (n=1,985). Combination therapy (ertugliflozin + sitagliptin) provided statistically significant reductions in HbA1c compared to placebo or individual components [US].
- **Cardiovascular Outcomes (VERTIS CV):** A study of 8,246 patients with T2DM and established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease showed that ertugliflozin was non-inferior to placebo for the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F and 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from moisture and store in a dry place [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only [US].
- **Other Jurisdictions:** Not specified in available sources.
