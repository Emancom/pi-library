# Editorial Product Information: estradiol + norethisterone

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **estradiol + norethisterone**
- Chemical class:
  - Estradiol: natural estrogen (oestrogen)
  - Norethisterone (norethisterone acetate in the transdermal product): synthetic progestogen
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Transdermal (patch)
- DO NOT include brand or trade names, dosage form physical descriptions, or schedule listings

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Short-term treatment of symptoms of estrogen deficiency in menopausal women who have an intact uterus.
- Prevention of postmenopausal bone mineral density loss in women at increased risk of future osteoporotic fractures who are intolerant of, or contraindicated for, non-estrogen medicinal products approved for prevention of bone mineral density loss. When used solely for this indication, therapy should be reserved for women at high fracture risk who are intolerant/contraindicated to other approved non-estrogen therapies.
- Combination estrogen-progestogen therapy should not be used in women who have had a hysterectomy because progestogen is not required and may increase breast cancer risk.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Two transdermal release rates are described:
  - Estradiol 50 μg/day with norethisterone acetate 140 μg/day
  - Estradiol 50 μg/day with norethisterone acetate 250 μg/day
- Typical regimen described:
  - Initiate therapy with the higher release-rate combination (50/250 μg/day) applied every 3 or 4 days during a 28-day cycle.
  - Dose may be reduced to the lower progestogen-release option (50/140 μg/day) if progestogen-related adverse effects occur. The lower-release option may also be used as the starting dose when considered appropriate.

### Dosing by indication
- Symptomatic menopausal estrogen deficiency: as above.
- Prevention of postmenopausal bone mineral density loss in high-risk women intolerant/contraindicated to other agents: as above; use lowest effective dose and consider shortest duration consistent with treatment goals.

### Administration instructions
- Apply to clean, dry, non-irritated, non-oily skin (avoid use with moisturisers, creams, oils at the application site).
- Site of application: smooth, hair-free area on buttocks or abdomen; avoid the waistline and never apply to or near the breasts.
- Replace patch every 3 to 4 days. Rotate application sites; allow at least one week before reusing the same site.
- Application technique: remove sachet liner as directed and press patch firmly to skin for at least 10 seconds, smoothing edges. Avoid prolonged direct sun exposure of the applied patch.
- If a patch becomes dislodged (e.g., after strenuous activity or friction), the same patch may be reapplied to another area; if necessary a new patch may be applied. Continue original treatment schedule.
- Removal: remove slowly to minimise skin irritation. If adhesive residue remains, allow area to dry ~15 minutes then remove residue with an oil-based cream or lotion. Fold used patches adhesive-to-adhesive and discard safely.

### Titration schedules
- Reduce to the lower progestogen-release option (50/140) if progestogen-related side effects occur. No other titration schedules are specified in available sources.

### Missed dose instructions
- If a patch falls off, reapply the same patch if intact or apply a new patch and continue the original application schedule. No additional missed-dose guidance is specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
(establish presence of any of the following — therapy should not be used)
- Known or suspected pregnancy
- Breastfeeding
- Known, past or suspected cancer of the breast
- Known or suspected estrogen-dependent neoplasia, including cancer of the endometrium
- Known or suspected pituitary or hypothalamic tumours
- Undiagnosed abnormal vaginal bleeding
- Severe hepatic impairment
- Endometriosis
- Connective tissue disease or otosclerosis
- Active venous thromboembolism (e.g., deep venous thrombosis, pulmonary embolism)
- Known thrombophilic or thromboembolic disorders (e.g., thrombophlebitis)
- Arterial thromboembolic disease (e.g., coronary heart disease, stroke) or a documented history of these conditions
- Porphyria
- Hypersensitivity to estrogens, progestogens, or any component of the product

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed/Prominent warnings
- **[Australian] Estrogens and progestogens should not be used for the prevention of cardiovascular disease or dementia.** Large randomised trials have demonstrated increased risks of myocardial infarction, stroke, invasive breast cancer, pulmonary embolism, deep vein thrombosis and probable dementia in specific populations treated with combined estrogen-progestogen regimens.

### General warnings and precautions
- Always assess benefits versus risks before initiating and periodically during treatment; use the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration consistent with treatment goals.
- Estrogen/progestogen therapy is associated with increased risks of:
  - Coronary heart disease events (including myocardial infarction)
  - Stroke
  - Venous thromboembolism (VTE), including deep venous thrombosis and pulmonary embolism
  - Invasive breast cancer
  - Probable dementia in women aged ≥65 years (see Clinical Trials)
- If any of the above events occur or are suspected during therapy, discontinue treatment immediately.
- Risk factors for arterial vascular disease (e.g., hypertension, diabetes mellitus, smoking, hypercholesterolaemia, obesity) and for VTE (personal/family history of VTE, severe obesity [BMI > 30 kg/m2], systemic lupus erythematosus) should be identified and managed appropriately.
- If feasible, discontinue estrogens at least 4–6 weeks before surgery associated with increased thromboembolism risk or during prolonged immobilisation.
- Clinical surveillance (including endometrial investigation when indicated) is important. Persistent or recurrent abnormal vaginal bleeding requires evaluation to exclude malignancy.
- Addition of a progestogen is recommended for women with an intact uterus to reduce incidence of endometrial hyperplasia associated with estrogen alone; however, combination therapy may carry additional risks including a possible increased breast cancer risk and impairment of glucose tolerance.
- Monitor blood pressure at regular intervals.
- In patients with pre-existing hypertriglyceridaemia, estrogens may increase plasma triglycerides and precipitate pancreatitis; monitor triglycerides closely.
- Use with caution in patients with impaired liver function or history of cholestatic jaundice; transdermal administration reduces first-pass metabolism but estrogens may be poorly metabolised in hepatic impairment.
- Estrogen therapy increases thyroid-binding globulin (TBG) levels; patients receiving thyroid hormone replacement may require higher replacement doses and monitoring of thyroid function.
- Estrogens/progestogens may cause fluid retention; use with caution in patients with cardiac or renal dysfunction.
- Use with caution in patients with severe hypocalcaemia.
- Epidemiological data suggest a small increased risk of ovarian cancer with HRT use.
- Cases of anaphylactic/anaphylactoid reactions have been reported; these may require emergency management.
- Estrogens may induce or exacerbate angioedema, particularly in women with hereditary angioedema.
- Estrogens may exacerbate endometriosis and other conditions such as asthma, diabetes mellitus, epilepsy, migraine or severe headache, porphyria, systemic lupus erythematosus, and certain hepatic disorders.

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and periodic assessment for cardiovascular risk factors.
- Baseline and periodic breast examination; arrange mammography according to age, risk factors and previous mammogram results.
- Monitor for signs/symptoms of VTE; instruct patients to report sudden unilateral leg pain/swelling, chest pain, or sudden dyspnoea.
- Monitor blood pressure periodically.
- In patients on thyroid hormone replacement, monitor thyroid function and adjust dose as necessary.
- Monitor lipid profile (particularly triglycerides) in patients with hyperlipidaemia.
- Monitor serum calcium in patients with known breast cancer and bone metastases or if symptoms suggest hypercalcaemia.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Not recommended for prevention of cardiovascular disease or dementia.
- Avoid application to breasts or irritated skin.
- Use with caution in women with history of migraine with aura, as hormonal therapy may increase cerebrovascular risk.
- Caution in women with a personal or strong family history suggestive of thrombophilia; investigate recurrent spontaneous abortions for thrombophilic predisposition prior to therapy.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Pharmacokinetic interactions:
  - Estrogen therapy increases thyroid‑binding globulin (TBG); patients dependent on thyroid replacement therapy may require increased thyroid hormone doses and monitoring of thyroid function.
  - Transdermal administration reduces first‑pass hepatic metabolism compared with oral estrogens; however, estrogens may still be poorly metabolised in hepatic impairment. Specific metabolic enzyme interactions (e.g., CYP inducers/inhibitors) are not specified in available sources.
- Pharmacodynamic interactions:
  - Estrogen/progestogen therapy may impair glucose tolerance; monitor diabetic patients and adjust antidiabetic therapy as needed.
  - Estrogens may increase triglyceride levels in patients with pre-existing hypertriglyceridaemia, increasing risk of pancreatitis.
- Clinically significant interactions with management notes:
  - Thyroid hormone: monitor thyroid function and adjust replacement dose when initiating or discontinuing estrogen therapy.
  - Diabetes mellitus: monitor glycaemic control.
  - Hypertriglyceridaemia: monitor lipid profile; exercise caution in patients with known hypertriglyceridaemia.
- Jurisdiction‑specific interactions: Not specified in available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Estradiol provides estrogen replacement to alleviate symptoms of estrogen deficiency and to help preserve bone mineral density in postmenopausal women.
- Norethisterone (progestogen) is included in combination therapy to reduce the risk of endometrial hyperplasia and endometrial cancer that can be induced by unopposed estrogen in women with an intact uterus.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Transdermal delivery provides continuous systemic absorption and avoids first‑pass hepatic metabolism associated with oral estrogens. Specific absorption rates for the two release strengths are described in product data (estradiol 50 μg/day with two norethisterone acetate release rates); estradiol hemihydrate equivalence is noted in the source texts (1 mg estradiol hemihydrate Ph. Eur. = 0.968 mg estradiol).
- Detailed parameters for distribution, metabolism (enzymes, active metabolites), elimination and half‑life are not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Estrogen replacement reduces menopausal vasomotor symptoms and can prevent bone mineral density loss in selected postmenopausal women.
- Addition of a progestogen (norethisterone) reduces estrogen-induced endometrial hyperplasia incidence compared with estrogen alone.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Women’s Health Initiative (WHI) — estrogen plus progestogen substudy:
  - Population: Postmenopausal women aged 50–79 years.
  - Findings (mean follow-up approximately 5–6 years): Increased risks with combined conjugated estrogens + medroxyprogesterone acetate of myocardial infarction (37 vs 30 per 10,000 women‑years), stroke (29 vs 21 per 10,000 women‑years), invasive breast cancer (41 vs 33 per 10,000 women‑years), and venous thromboembolism (34 vs 16 per 10,000 women‑years) compared with placebo. Increase in some risks was observed within the first year and persisted.
- Women’s Health Initiative Memory Study (WHIMS):
  - Population: Women aged 65–79 years.
  - Findings: Increased risk of probable dementia in women receiving estrogen with or without progestogen compared with placebo (pooled RR 1.76; estrogen+progestogen RR 2.05 in the substudy).
- Heart and Estrogen/progestogen Replacement Study (HERS) and HERS II:
  - Population: Postmenopausal women with documented coronary heart disease.
  - Findings: Estrogen plus progestogen did not reduce overall rate of CHD events; more CHD events occurred in the treatment group in year 1 but not subsequently; overall rates comparable over extended follow-up.
- Million Women Study (observational, Europe):
  - Findings: Reported increased risk of mortality due to breast cancer among current users of estrogens alone or estrogens plus progestogens compared to never users (observational data).
- Additional trial-specific details (dosing regimens, complete inclusion/exclusion criteria, full safety event listings and subgroup analyses) are not provided in available sources.
