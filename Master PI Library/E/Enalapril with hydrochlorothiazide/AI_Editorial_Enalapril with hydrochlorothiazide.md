# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises available regulatory information into a clinically relevant reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis based on the provided United States product information. It does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within specific jurisdictions. At the time of preparation, only the US (FDA) documentation was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative [(R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt].
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US]; Manufactured by Baxter Pharmaceuticals India Private Ltd [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
Levocarnitine must be administered intravenously.

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are generally 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus (2–3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Monitoring
- **Baseline:** Obtain plasma carnitine levels prior to starting parenteral therapy [US].
- **Frequency:** Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations [US].
- **Target Range:** Normal plasma free carnitine concentrations are generally between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** No specific contraindications are listed in the available source; however, serious hypersensitivity reactions have been reported during use (see Section 5) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks/benefits before re-administration [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with reports of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation Note):** While this PI concerns the injection, clinicians should note that chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disorders.

### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
In placebo-controlled trials, the following events occurred at a frequency $\ge$ 5%:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (9%), headache (22%), injection site reaction (33%), chest pain (12%), fever (7%), infection (15%), pain (30%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), nausea (8%), vomiting (15%).
- **Neurologic:** Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), paresthesia (5%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%).

### Other Reported Reactions
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hypervolemia (5%).
- **Special Senses:** Taste perversion (3%), amblyopia (3%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent reports of body odor and gastritis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended to monitor INR levels at the initiation of levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not explicitly assigned, but follows narrative risk summary]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. However, no adequate, well-controlled studies exist in pregnant women. Use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Animal data (dairy cows) indicate carnitine levels in milk increase following administration. The benefits of maternal treatment should be weighed against the risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is calculated by weight (50 mg/kg); see Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the available source.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. Safety and efficacy of the *oral* form have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine compounds, which are rapidly eliminated [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg administered three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis trough levels from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. 
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** A study of doses (10, 20, and 40 mg/kg) showed a dose-proportional increase in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks. While carnitine levels increased, the impact on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].
