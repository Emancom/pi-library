# Editorial Product Information: Empagliflozin and Linagliptin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Empagliflozin and Linagliptin.
- **Chemical Class:** 
    - Empagliflozin: Sodium-glucose co-transporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitor.
    - Linagliptin: Dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP-4) inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** **GLYXAMBI** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A10BD19.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets (arc triangular, bevel-edged).
    - 10 mg empagliflozin / 5 mg linagliptin (pale yellow).
    - 25 mg empagliflozin / 5 mg linagliptin (pale pink).
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Boehringer Ingelheim Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM):** Indicated as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycaemic control in adults when treatment with both empagliflozin and linagliptin is clinically appropriate [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg empagliflozin / 5 mg linagliptin once daily.
- **Titration:** In patients who tolerate the starting dose but require additional glycaemic control, the dose may be increased to 25 mg empagliflozin / 5 mg linagliptin once daily.
- **Administration:** Tablets may be taken with or without food at any time of the day. 
- **Combination Therapy:** When used with insulin or a sulfonylurea, a dose reduction of the insulin or sulfonylurea may be necessary to mitigate the risk of hypoglycaemia.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to empagliflozin, linagliptin, or any excipients.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - Severe renal impairment (eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m²).
    - End-stage renal disease (ESRD).
    - Patients requiring dialysis.
- **Note:** The efficacy of the empagliflozin component is dependent on renal function [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
- **Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA):** SGLT2 inhibitors are associated with an increased risk of DKA, which can be life-threatening or fatal. Cases may present with atypical (euglycaemic) blood glucose levels (<13.8 mmol/L). Treatment should be discontinued immediately if DKA is suspected. Risk factors include low carbohydrate diets, acute illness, surgery, and insulin deficiency.
- **Surgery:** Treatment should be ceased prior to major surgery or procedures requiring prolonged fasting. Therapy may restart once the patient is stable and oral intake is normal.
- **Pancreatitis:** Acute pancreatitis has been reported with linagliptin. If suspected, discontinue the drug immediately.
- **Necrotising Fasciitis of the Perineum (Fournier’s Gangrene):** A rare but serious and life-threatening infection reported in both genders. Prompt evaluation for pain, tenderness, erythema, or swelling in the genital/perineal area is required.
- **Volume Depletion:** SGLT2 inhibitors cause osmotic diuresis. Caution is required in patients at risk of hypotension (e.g., elderly, patients on antihypertensives, or those with cardiovascular disease).
- **Urinary Tract Infections (UTIs):** Increased risk of serious UTIs, including urosepsis and pyelonephritis.
- **Lower Limb Amputation:** While not definitively linked to empagliflozin in pooled analyses, an increase in amputations (primarily toes) has been noted as a class effect for SGLT2 inhibitors. Regular foot care and monitoring are essential.
- **Bullous Pemphigoid:** Reported with DPP-4 inhibitors. Discontinue if suspected.
- **Arthralgia:** Severe and disabling joint pain has been reported with DPP-4 inhibitors. 

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by frequency based on clinical trial data:

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | None specified as "very common" for the combination. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Urinary tract infection (UTI), vaginal moniliasis, vulvovaginitis, balanitis, nasopharyngitis, cough, constipation, arthralgia, increased urination, increased serum lipids. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Hypersensitivity, urticaria, pancreatitis, volume depletion, pruritus, dysuria, ketoacidosis (dose-dependent frequency). |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Mouth ulceration (linagliptin-derived). |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Tubulointerstitial nephritis. |
| **Not Known** | Urosepsis, pyelonephritis, necrotising fasciitis of the perineum (Fournier’s gangrene), angioedema, bullous pemphigoid. |

- **Laboratory Changes:** Empagliflozin may cause increased serum creatinine, decreased eGFR, and increased haematocrit [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Insulin and Sulfonylureas:** Increased risk of hypoglycaemia; dose adjustments of these agents may be required.
- **Diuretics:** Potential for increased risk of dehydration and hypotension when combined with thiazide or loop diuretics.
- **Lithium:** Empagliflozin may increase renal excretion of lithium, potentially decreasing blood lithium levels. Monitoring of lithium concentrations is recommended [Australian].
- **Rifampicin:** Co-administration with this strong P-gp/CYP3A4 inducer reduced linagliptin exposure by approximately 40%. Full efficacy of the linagliptin component may not be achieved.
- **Ritonavir:** Co-administration with this P-gp/CYP3A4 inhibitor increased linagliptin exposure twofold; however, this is not considered clinically relevant and requires no dose adjustment [Australian].
- **Laboratory Interference:** SGLT2 inhibitors interfere with 1,5-anhydroglucitol (1,5-AG) assays; this method should not be used for glycaemic monitoring.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category D [Australian]:** There is limited data in pregnant women. Animal studies showed adverse effects on renal development in the second and third trimesters. Use should be avoided unless clearly necessary.
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. Nonclinical data show both empagliflozin and linagliptin are excreted in animal milk. A risk to newborns/infants cannot be excluded.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies do not indicate direct or indirect harmful effects on fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in children under 18 years of age have not been established. Use is not recommended.
- **Elderly Use:** Patients $\ge$75 years are at higher risk for volume depletion and UTIs. Initiation is not recommended in patients aged 75 years and older [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - eGFR $\ge$30 mL/min/1.73 m²: No dose adjustment required.
    - eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m²: Contraindicated.
    - Intensive monitoring of renal function is recommended if eGFR is <60 mL/min/1.73 m².
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is recommended for patients with hepatic impairment.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Empagliflozin:** Inhibits SGLT2 in the proximal renal tubules, reducing glucose reabsorption and increasing urinary glucose excretion. This mechanism is independent of insulin.
    - **Linagliptin:** Reversibly binds to and inhibits DPP-4, preventing the degradation of incretin hormones (GLP-1 and GIP). This increases insulin secretion and decreases glucagon secretion in a glucose-dependent manner.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed. Empagliflozin median $T_{max}$ 1.5 h; Linagliptin median $T_{max}$ 1.5 h. Food does not have a clinically relevant effect on the pharmacokinetics of either component.
    - **Distribution:** Empagliflozin protein binding is 86.2%. Linagliptin protein binding is concentration-dependent (99% at 1 nmol/L to 75-89% at $\ge$30 nmol/L).
    - **Metabolism:** Empagliflozin is primarily metabolised via glucuronidation (UGT2B7, 1A3, 1A8, 1A9). Linagliptin undergoes minimal metabolism.
    - **Elimination:** Empagliflozin terminal $T_{1/2}$ is 12.4 h (54.4% urine, 41.2% faeces). Linagliptin terminal $T_{1/2}$ is >100 h due to tight DPP-4 binding (80% faeces, 5% urine).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Factorial Design Studies:** In patients inadequately controlled on metformin, the combination of empagliflozin and linagliptin provided statistically significant improvements in $HbA_{1c}$ and fasting plasma glucose (FPG) compared to the individual components.
- **Cardiovascular Safety (EMPA-REG OUTCOME):** Empagliflozin significantly reduced the risk of CV death, non-fatal MI, or non-fatal stroke (MACE-3) in adults with T2DM and established CV disease.
- **Cardiovascular Safety (CARMELINA/CAROLINA):** Linagliptin demonstrated non-inferiority for cardiovascular safety compared to placebo and glimepiride.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Disposal:** Unused medicine should be disposed of via local pharmacy programs.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
