# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (Food and Drug Administration)**. 
- At the time of publication, PI documents from other major jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian, Japanese) were not provided for comparison. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a synthesis of available regulatory data and is not intended to replace the official PI used for prescribing in specific jurisdictions.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule for fatty acid transport; amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic management of patients with genetic metabolic defects that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing chronic hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Administer as an intravenous injection only.
- Parenteral products should be visually inspected for discoloration or particulate matter prior to administration [US].
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in patients presenting with severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 to 4 hours; frequency should never be less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though higher doses may be required based on clinical response. The maximum recorded dose is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
- **Administration Timing:** Administer as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Adjustments:** Dosing should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma carnitine concentrations (target 40 to 50 µmol/L). Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of treatment [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occurred within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate appropriate medical care. Re-challenge should only be considered after weighing risks and benefits, with intensive monitoring [US].
- **Neurologic Reactions (Seizures):** Seizures have been reported in patients receiving levocarnitine, regardless of whether they had a pre-existing seizure disorder. In patients with known seizure activity, an increase in frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring:** In ESRD patients, chronic high-dose oral administration (not necessarily IV) may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) normally excreted in urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse event profiles in ESRD patients are often confounded by the underlying pathology of renal failure.

### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
- **Very Common (>10%):** Injection site reaction (33–38%), flu syndrome (25–29%), headache (22–37%), pharyngitis (15–27%), nausea/vomiting (8–21%), hypertension (18–21%), chest pain (6–15%), diarrhea (9–35%), dizziness (10–18%), and dyspnea (3–11%) [US].
- **Common (1–10%):** Abdominal pain, fever, accidental injury, asthenia, back pain, palpitations, tachycardia, anemia, hypercalcemia, and peripheral edema [US].
- **General Reactions:** Transient nausea and vomiting. Less frequent reactions include body odor and gastritis [US].

### Postmarketing Experience
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].
- **Immune System:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients on anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned/specified as per newer labeling, but reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm]. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased milk concentrations following exogenous administration. In nursing mothers, the risk of excess carnitine intake by the infant must be weighed against the benefit of supplementation for the mother. Consideration may be given to discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (mg/kg) and applicable to pediatric populations [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the available source.
- **Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. IV levocarnitine is specifically indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis (see Section 3) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This process provides substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production in all tissues except the brain [US]. It also facilitates the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), the mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours, and the mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, IV administration increases plasma carnitine concentrations. Pre-dialysis (trough) levels increase significantly over 8 to 24 weeks of therapy [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg post-dialysis three times per week showed mean trough levels increasing from 21.3 µmol/L at baseline to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the specific impact on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, cardiomyopathy) has not been definitively determined in this population [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].
