# Editorial Product Information: Elotuzumab

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (TGA).
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **elotuzumab** for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory instructions into a single reference.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Elotuzumab (humanised recombinant IgG1 monoclonal antibody).
- **Chemical Class:** Immunostimulatory monoclonal antibody targeting SLAMF7.
- **Brand Name:** **Empliciti** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Lyophilized powder for concentrate for solution for intravenous infusion; available in 300 mg and 400 mg single-use vials [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Bristol-Myers Squibb [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Multiple Myeloma:** Indicated for use in combination with lenalidomide and dexamethasone for the treatment of patients with multiple myeloma who have received at least one prior therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
The recommended dose is 10 mg/kg administered intravenously according to a 28-day cycle.

### Dosing Schedule
- **Cycles 1 and 2:** 10 mg/kg administered weekly (Days 1, 8, 15, and 22).
- **Cycle 3 and beyond:** 10 mg/kg administered every two weeks (Days 1 and 15).
- **Duration:** Continue treatment until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.

### Premedication
Patients must receive the following regimen 45–90 minutes prior to every **elotuzumab** infusion:
- **Dexamethasone:** 8 mg IV. (Note: On infusion days, the total dexamethasone dose is 40 mg; 28 mg is taken orally 3–24 hours before infusion, and 8 mg is given IV).
- **H1 Blocker:** Diphenhydramine (25–50 mg orally) or equivalent.
- **H2 Blocker:** Ranitidine (50 mg IV or 150 mg orally) or equivalent.
- **Analgesic:** Paracetamol (650–1000 mg orally).

### Administration and Infusion Rates
Infusion must be initiated at 0.5 mL/min. If well-tolerated, the rate may be increased stepwise:
- **Cycle 1, Dose 1:** 0.5 mL/min (0-30 min) → 1 mL/min (30-60 min) → 2 mL/min (≥60 min).
- **Cycle 1, Dose 2:** 3 mL/min (0-30 min) → 4 mL/min (≥30 min).
- **Subsequent Doses:** 5 mL/min (maximum rate).

### Reconstitution and Dilution
- Reconstitute vials to a concentration of 25 mg/mL using Sterile Water for Injection (13 mL for 300 mg vial; 17 mL for 400 mg vial).
- Dilute the required volume into 230 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride or 5% Glucose. The infusion volume should not exceed 5 mL/kg of patient weight.
- Use a 0.2–1.2 µm low-protein-binding filter.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **elotuzumab** or any of its excipients (sodium citrate dihydrate, citric acid monohydrate, sucrose, polysorbate 80) [Australian].
- Contraindications applicable to lenalidomide and dexamethasone also apply to the combination regimen.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Infusion Reactions (IRs):** IRs (mostly Grade 1 or 2) are common, typically occurring during the first dose. Premedication is mandatory. If a Grade ≥2 reaction occurs, interrupt the infusion. Upon resolution (≤Grade 1), restart at 0.5 mL/min and escalate slowly. Permanent discontinuation is required for very severe reactions.
- **Infections:** Increased incidence of infections (including pneumonia) has been observed. Monitor for signs of infection and treat promptly. Prophylaxis for viral or *Pneumocystis jiroveci* pneumonia should be considered case-by-case.
- **Second Primary Malignancies (SPMs):** Higher rates of invasive SPMs (6.9% vs 4.1% in control) have been reported, primarily driven by non-melanoma skin cancers. Monitor patients regularly for new malignancies.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Elevations in liver enzymes (ALT/AST >3x ULN) may occur. Monitor liver function periodically. If Grade 3 or higher elevation occurs, stop **elotuzumab**; resumption may be considered after returning to baseline.
- **Interference with Laboratory Testing:** **Elotuzumab** (an IgG kappa monoclonal antibody) can be detected on serum protein electrophoresis (SPEP) and immunofixation (IFE) assays. This may interfere with the determination of "Complete Response" in patients with endogenous IgG kappa myeloma protein.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following frequencies are based on the Eloquent-2 trial (elotuzumab/lenalidomide/dexamethasone vs. lenalidomide/dexamethasone).

| Adverse Event | All Grades Frequency (E-Ld) | Grade 3/4 Frequency (E-Ld) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Fatigue** | 61.6% | 12.6% |
| **Diarrhoea** | 46.9% | 5.0% |
| **Pyrexia** | 37.4% | 2.5% |
| **Constipation** | 35.5% | 1.3% |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Pneumonia is the most serious common adverse reaction.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No formal studies conducted. As a monoclonal antibody, **elotuzumab** is not metabolised by CYP450 enzymes; therefore, interference with these pathways is not expected.
- **Laboratory Interference:** Can interfere with SPEP and IFE assays used for monitoring myeloma response [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. There are no data on use in pregnant women. IgG1 antibodies are known to cross the placenta. It should not be used unless the benefit clearly outweighs the risk.
- **Contraception:** Because **elotuzumab** is used with lenalidomide, patients must adhere to the pregnancy prevention/contraception programme required for lenalidomide.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if **elotuzumab** is excreted in human milk. A decision must be made whether to discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the benefit to the mother and infant.
- **Fertility:** Effects on male and female fertility are unknown.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in children [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment is required for patients >65 years. Safety and efficacy were generally similar between older and younger patients.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required for patients with mild, moderate, or severe renal impairment, including those with end-stage renal disease requiring dialysis.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment is required for mild hepatic impairment. **Elotuzumab** has not been studied in patients with moderate or severe hepatic impairment.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Elotuzumab** targets SLAMF7, a glycoprotein highly expressed on multiple myeloma cells and Natural Killer (NK) cells. It acts via a dual mechanism:
    1. Directly activating NK cells through the SLAMF7 pathway.
    2. Targeting SLAMF7 on myeloma cells, tagging them for NK cell-mediated killing via Antibody-Dependent Cellular Cytotoxicity (ADCC).
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Exhibits non-linear (target-mediated) clearance. Clearance decreases with increasing dose. Steady-state is reached by approximately 3 months. Volume of distribution approximates serum volume.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Eloquent-2 (Study CA204-004):** A Phase 3 trial in patients with relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma (1–3 prior therapies).
    - **Outcome:** **Elotuzumab** + Lenalidomide/Dexamethasone (E-Ld) significantly improved Progression-Free Survival (PFS) compared to Ld alone (Median PFS 18.5 months vs 14.3 months; HR 0.68). 
    - **Overall Response Rate (ORR):** 78.5% (E-Ld) vs 65.5% (Ld).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (refrigerate). Do not freeze or shake. Keep in the original carton to protect from light.
- **Reconstituted/Diluted Solution:** Stability has been demonstrated for 24 hours under refrigeration (2°C–8°C). A maximum of 8 hours of that 24-hour window can be at room temperature (20°C–25°C).

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
