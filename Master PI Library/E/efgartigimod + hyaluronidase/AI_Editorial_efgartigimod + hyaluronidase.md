# Editorial Product Information: efgartigimod + hyaluronidase

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **efgartigimod + hyaluronidase**
- Chemical class:
  - efgartigimod: neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn) blocker (biologic)
  - hyaluronidase (recombinant human): endoglycosidase that facilitates subcutaneous dispersion/absorption
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration: subcutaneous only.

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 2023.
- Last revision(s) of source PI: revisions noted in available source include 10/2025, 2/2026, and 5/2026 (dates reported in the provided PI text).
- Withdrawals, suspensions, restrictions: Not specified in available sources for any jurisdiction other than the revisions above.
- If registration dates or additional jurisdictional approval histories are required: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Indicated for treatment of adult patients with:
  - generalized myasthenia gravis (gMG)
  - chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy (CIDP)
- Jurisdictional variations of indication approval: Not specified in available sources (the available PI text reports the above indications without region-specific restriction).

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Administration is by subcutaneous injection only. Do not administer intravenously. Do not dilute.
- Two presentation/administration methods described:
  - Prefilled syringe: intended for patient and/or caregiver administration after proper instruction.
  - Single-dose vial: intended for administration by a healthcare professional using a winged infusion set.

### Dosing by indication
- Generalized myasthenia gravis (gMG)
  - Administer in cycles of once-weekly injections for 4 weeks.
  - Prefilled syringe: 1,000 mg efgartigimod + 10,000 units hyaluronidase administered subcutaneously over approximately 20–30 seconds.
  - Vial: 1,008 mg efgartigimod + 11,200 units hyaluronidase administered subcutaneously over approximately 30–90 seconds.
  - Subsequent treatment cycles: administer based on clinical evaluation.
- Chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy (CIDP)
  - Administer as once-weekly injections.
  - Prefilled syringe: 1,000 mg efgartigimod + 10,000 units hyaluronidase administered over ~20–30 seconds.
  - Vial: 1,008 mg efgartigimod + 11,200 units hyaluronidase administered over ~30–90 seconds.
  - Dosing schedule: continue once-weekly dosing per clinical response; not all patients respond. Consider stopping if neurological worsening occurs after initiation.

### Administration instructions
- Prefilled syringe:
  - Remove from refrigeration ≥30 minutes before injection to allow to reach room temperature; do not use external heat sources.
  - Visually inspect solution: yellowish, clear to opalescent, no particulate matter; do not use if visible particles present.
  - Use a safety needle 25G, 5/8 inch, thin-wall type (needle not included).
  - Injection site: abdomen, at least 2 inches away from the navel; rotate sites; avoid irritated, infected, bruised, tender, hardened skin, moles, or scars.
  - Technique: pinch skin, inject at 45°–90° angle over ~20–30 seconds.
  - Discard any unused medication remaining in the syringe.
  - Monitor patient for signs of hypersensitivity for at least 30 minutes after administration.
- Single-dose vial (healthcare professional only):
  - Remove from refrigeration ≥15 minutes before injection; do not use external heat sources.
  - Visually inspect solution: yellowish, clear to opalescent; do not use if opaque or foreign particles present.
  - Use aseptic technique; vial contains overfill—withdraw entire content with polypropylene syringe and an 18G transfer needle.
  - Use a winged infusion set (PVC tubing), 25G, 12-inch tubing, maximum priming volume 0.4 mL; prime tubing to 5.6 mL so solution is present at tubing tip prior to placement.
  - Injection site: abdomen, 2–3 inches from navel; rotate sites; avoid irritated or compromised skin.
  - Technique: pinch skin, inject at ~45° over ~30–90 seconds.
  - Administer immediately after preparation; discard any unused portions and all disposables.
  - Monitor patient for signs of hypersensitivity for at least 30 minutes after administration.

### Missed dose
- If a scheduled dose is missed, administer up to 3 days after the scheduled time point. After that, resume the original dosing schedule until the treatment cycle is completed.

### Titration
- Not applicable / Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications

- Additional jurisdiction-specific contraindications: Not specified in available sources (no other region-specific contraindications provided).

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- No boxed warnings specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Infections:
  - Treatment may increase risk of infection. Delay administration in patients with an active infection until resolution.
  - Monitor patients for signs and symptoms of infection during treatment.
  - Because the product causes transient reduction in IgG levels, vaccination with live vaccines is not recommended during treatment. Evaluate the need for age-appropriate vaccines according to immunization guidelines prior to initiation of a new treatment cycle.
- Hypersensitivity reactions:
  - Hypersensitivity reactions have occurred, including rash, angioedema, dyspnea, urticaria, and anaphylaxis. Anaphylaxis and hypotension leading to syncope have been reported in postmarketing experience with related intravenous formulations.
  - Monitor for clinical signs and symptoms of hypersensitivity for at least 30 minutes after administration; institute appropriate measures if a reaction occurs.
  - Contraindicated in patients with a history of serious hypersensitivity to the active ingredients or excipients.
- Infusion/injection-related reactions:
  - Infusion-related reactions (reported with intravenous formulations) have included hypertension, chills/shivering, and chest/abdominal/back pain. For severe reactions, initiate appropriate therapy and reconsider re-administration; for mild-to-moderate reactions consider rechallenge with slower administration, increased observation, and pre-medication if appropriate.
- Hematologic changes:
  - Cases of decreased white blood cell, lymphocyte, and neutrophil counts were observed; monitor laboratory parameters as clinically indicated.
- Neurological worsening:
  - Postmarketing reports describe worsening of CIDP symptoms/signs when transitioning from intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) to this product; monitor closely when switching therapies and consider clinical risk/benefit.

### Monitoring requirements
- Clinical monitoring for:
  - Signs and symptoms of infection during treatment.
  - Hypersensitivity reactions for at least 30 minutes post administration.
  - Neurologic status, particularly in CIDP patients and during transitions from IVIG.
- Laboratory monitoring:
  - Consider monitoring complete blood counts (noting observed reductions in WBC, lymphocytes, neutrophils) as clinically indicated.
- Immunization status:
  - Evaluate vaccination status prior to initiating a new treatment cycle; avoid live vaccines during treatment.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Use appropriate aseptic technique for vial preparation and administration.
- Ensure patients/caregivers trained in subcutaneous injection technique if self-administration is intended.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic / pharmacokinetic interactions
- Concomitant use with medications that bind to the human neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn):
  - This product may reduce the effectiveness of medications whose mechanism depends on FcRn binding. When long-term concomitant use of such medications is essential, consider discontinuing efgartigimod + hyaluronidase and using alternative therapies; closely monitor for reduced effectiveness.
  - Specific interacting agents and quantitative interaction data: Not specified in available sources.
- Immunizations:
  - Because the product reduces IgG levels transiently, the immune response to vaccines and the safety of live vaccines during treatment are unknown; live vaccines are not recommended during treatment. Evaluate and, if appropriate, administer age-appropriate vaccines before initiating a new treatment cycle.
- Transition from IVIG:
  - Postmarketing reports indicate possible worsening of CIDP when transitioning from IVIG to this product; monitor closely during therapeutic transitions.

### Clinically significant interactions — management notes
- Monitor clinical response of concomitant FcRn-dependent agents; consider alternative therapies if clinically necessary.
- Defer live vaccination during treatment; assess immunization prior to new treatment cycle.
- When switching from IVIG (or other immunomodulatory therapies) to this product, monitor neurologic status carefully; consider timing and bridging strategies as clinically indicated.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **efgartigimod**: a biologic that blocks the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn), leading to increased catabolism of IgG and a reduction in circulating IgG levels.
- **hyaluronidase (recombinant human)**: an endoglycosidase that transiently degrades hyaluronan in the extracellular matrix to increase dispersion and absorption of subcutaneously co-administered drugs.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Detailed absorption, distribution, metabolism, and elimination parameters: Not specified in available sources.
- Time course and magnitude of IgG reduction: described qualitatively as a transient reduction in IgG levels; quantitative PK/PD parameters not provided in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Primary pharmacodynamic effect: reduction in circulating IgG levels through FcRn blockade (transient).

## 12. Clinical Trials
### Key trial summaries (as described in available sources)
- Study 1 (gMG, intravenous efgartigimod alfa-fcab trial referenced)
  - Design: double-blind, placebo-controlled study of intravenous efgartigimod alfa-fcab.
  - Safety population: 364 patients received at least one dose of intravenous efgartigimod alfa-fcab; 84 patients received 10 mg/kg in Study 1.
  - Demographics (Study 1 subset): mean age 46 years (range 19–78); ~75% female; 82% White, 11% Asian.
  - Safety findings: most common adverse reactions (≥10%) included respiratory tract infection (33%), headache (32%), urinary tract infection (10%); hematologic decreases in white blood cells, lymphocytes, and neutrophils observed.
  - Dosing intervals and cycles in Study 1: minimum time to subsequent cycle per protocol was 28 days; average of 2 cycles administered; mean/median time to second cycle ~54/50 days.
  - Efficacy and primary outcome data: Not specified in available sources.
- Study 2 (gMG, anti-AChR antibody-negative subgroup)
  - Design: placebo-controlled study in anti-AChR antibody-negative patients.
  - Safety finding: nausea reported in 7% of treated patients vs 5% placebo.
  - Other efficacy/safety outcomes: Not specified in available sources.
- Study 3 (gMG, active-controlled comparing subcutaneous product to IV efgartigimod)
  - Design: active-controlled study in gMG. Randomized patients received one cycle of once-weekly administrations for 4 weeks (4 doses total) of subcutaneous efgartigimod + hyaluronidase versus intravenous efgartigimod alfa-fcab.
  - Randomized N=110 (55 subcutaneous; 55 intravenous).
  - Open-label extension: included 128 patients who switched from IV to the subcutaneous combination.
  - Safety findings: injection site reactions occurred in 38% of subcutaneous-treated patients (rash, erythema, pruritus, bruising, pain, urticaria); injection site reactions were mild–moderate and did not lead to discontinuation; headache was also a common adverse event.
  - Efficacy outcomes, primary endpoints and results: Not specified in available sources.
- Study 4 (CIDP)
  - Design: placebo-controlled study (stage B described) in patients with CIDP.
  - Randomized N=221 (111 subcutaneous efgartigimod + hyaluronidase; 110 placebo).
  - Mean duration of treatment in stage B: 25 weeks.
  - Safety findings: injection site reactions in 15% of treated patients vs 6% placebo (most commonly bruising and erythema); injection site reactions were mild–moderate and most occurred during first 3 months.
  - Efficacy outcomes and primary endpoint results: Not specified in available sources.

### Additional clinical observations
- Most injection site reactions occurred early in treatment and decreased with subsequent cycles.
- Postmarketing reports include hypersensitivity (including anaphylaxis), infusion/injection-related reactions, and reports of CIDP worsening when transitioning from IVIG.
