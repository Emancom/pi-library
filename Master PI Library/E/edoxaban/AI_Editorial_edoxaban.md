# Editorial Product Information: Edoxaban

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of edoxaban product information for healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, regulatory requirements, and prescribing guidance into a single coherent reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labelling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Edoxaban (as edoxaban tosylate monohydrate), a selective Factor Xa inhibitor.
- **Brand Names:** **SAVAYSA** [US].
- **ATC Code:** B01AF03.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Film-coated tablets:** 15 mg (orange), 30 mg (pink), and 60 mg (yellow) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Daiichi Sankyo, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Reduction in the Risk of Stroke and Systemic Embolism (SE):** Indicated for patients with nonvalvular atrial fibrillation (NVAF) [US].
    - *Limitation of Use:* Edoxaban should not be used in NVAF patients with a creatinine clearance (CrCL) > 95 mL/min due to an increased risk of ischemic stroke compared to warfarin [US].
- **Treatment of Venous Thromboembolism (VTE):** Indicated for the treatment of deep vein thrombosis (DVT) and pulmonary embolism (PE) following 5 to 10 days of initial therapy with a parenteral anticoagulant [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration

### General Dosing (Adult)
CrCL should be assessed using the Cockcroft-Gault equation before initiating therapy.

### Nonvalvular Atrial Fibrillation (NVAF)
- **CrCL > 95 mL/min:** Do not use [US].
- **CrCL > 50 to ≤ 95 mL/min:** 60 mg once daily [US].
- **CrCL 15 to 50 mL/min:** 30 mg once daily [US].

### Treatment of DVT and PE
- **Standard Dose:** 60 mg once daily following 5–10 days of parenteral anticoagulation [US].
- **Reduced Dose (30 mg once daily):** Indicated for patients with any of the following [US]:
    - CrCL 15 to 50 mL/min.
    - Body weight ≤ 60 kg.
    - Concomitant use of specific P-gp inhibitors (e.g., verapamil, quinidine, short-term azithromycin, clarithromycin, erythromycin, oral itraconazole, or oral ketoconazole).

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets may be taken with or without food [US].
- **Swallowing Difficulties:** Tablets may be crushed and mixed with 2 to 3 ounces of water or applesauce for immediate oral administration or delivery via a gastric tube [US].

### Missed Dose
- The dose should be taken as soon as possible on the same day.
- Do not double the dose the next day to make up for a missed dose [US].

### Transitioning to/from Edoxaban
- **From Warfarin to Edoxaban:** Stop warfarin; start edoxaban when INR ≤ 2.5 [US].
- **From Non-VKA Oral Anticoagulants to Edoxaban:** Stop current agent; start edoxaban at the next scheduled dose [US].
- **From LMWH to Edoxaban:** Stop LMWH; start edoxaban at the next scheduled dose [US].
- **From Unfractionated Heparin to Edoxaban:** Stop infusion; start edoxaban 4 hours later [US].
- **From Edoxaban to Warfarin (Oral Option):** Reduce edoxaban dose by 50% (e.g., 60 mg to 30 mg) and begin warfarin concomitantly. Discontinue edoxaban once stable INR ≥ 2.0 is achieved [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Active pathological bleeding [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed Warnings [US]
1.  **Reduced Efficacy in NVAF with CrCL > 95 mL/min:** Edoxaban should not be used in this population. Increased rates of ischemic stroke were observed compared to warfarin in clinical trials.
2.  **Premature Discontinuation Risk:** Discontinuing any oral anticoagulant without an adequate alternative increases the risk of ischemic events.
3.  **Spinal/Epidural Hematoma:** Neuraxial anesthesia or spinal puncture in anticoagulated patients may result in hematomas causing long-term or permanent paralysis. Risk is increased by indwelling catheters, concomitant hemostatic drugs (NSAIDs, platelet inhibitors), or traumatic punctures.

### General Precautions
- **Bleeding Risk:** Edoxaban increases the risk of serious and potentially fatal bleeding. There is no specific reversal agent available [US].
- **Mechanical Heart Valves/Mitral Stenosis:** Use is not recommended [US].
- **Antiphospholipid Syndrome (APS):** Not recommended, particularly in triple-positive patients, due to increased risk of recurrent thrombotic events [US].
- **Surgery/Procedures:** Discontinue at least 24 hours prior to invasive or surgical procedures [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects

### Frequency Data
| Adverse Reaction | NVAF (SAVAYSA 60mg) | DVT/PE (SAVAYSA 60mg) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bleeding (All)** | Common (≥ 5%) | Common (≥ 1%) |
| **Anemia** | Common (≥ 5%) | Common (≥ 1%) |
| **Rash** | Not specified | Common (≥ 1%) |
| **Abnormal LFTs** | Not specified | Common (≥ 1%) |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Major Bleeding:** Most common site is the gastrointestinal tract (1.8% per year in NVAF) [US].
- **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD):** Rare cases reported; often confounded by amiodarone use or pneumonia [US].
- **Postmarketing Reports:** Thrombocytopenia, angioedema, hypersensitivity, and anticoagulant-related nephropathy [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions

### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **P-gp Inducers:** Avoid concomitant use with **rifampin** [US].
- **P-gp Inhibitors:** 
    - *NVAF:* No dose reduction recommended based on trial data [US]. 
    - *DVT/PE:* Reduce dose to 30 mg once daily [US].
- **Cytochrome P450:** Edoxaban has minimal metabolism via CYP3A4 and does not inhibit or induce major CYP enzymes [US].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Anticoagulants/Antiplatelets/NSAIDs:** Concomitant use increases bleeding risk. Long-term use with other anticoagulants is not recommended [US].
- **SSRIs/SNRIs:** May increase bleeding risk due to effects on platelet aggregation [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned under current labelling; however, data in pregnant women are insufficient to determine drug-associated risks. Animal studies (rats/rabbits) showed no malformations at doses up to 49 times the human exposure, though increased post-implantation loss occurred at maternally toxic doses [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Edoxaban is present in rat milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions (hemorrhage) in nursing infants, breastfeeding is not recommended [US].
- **Fertility:** No effects on fertility or early embryonic development were observed in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established. In a VTE study of 145 patients (birth to <18 years), effectiveness was not demonstrated [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy were observed between elderly (≥65 years) and younger patients, though higher bleeding rates may occur in those >75 years [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **CrCL 15–50 mL/min:** Reduce dose to 30 mg once daily [US].
    - **CrCL < 15 mL/min:** Not recommended [US].
    - **Hemodialysis:** Does not significantly clear edoxaban (<7% reduction in exposure) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild (Child-Pugh A):** No dose adjustment required [US].
    - **Moderate/Severe (Child-Pugh B and C):** Not recommended [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Edoxaban is a selective, reversible inhibitor of Factor Xa (FXa). It inhibits free FXa and prothrombinase activity, thereby reducing thrombin generation and thrombus formation. It does not require antithrombin III for activity [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Bioavailability is approximately 62%. Peak plasma concentration ($C_{max}$) reached in 1–2 hours [US].
    - **Distribution:** Protein binding is ~55%. $Vd_{ss}$ is 107 L [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily unchanged in plasma. Minimal metabolism via hydrolysis (CES1), conjugation, and CYP3A4 [US].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is 10 to 14 hours. Total clearance is 22 L/h, with renal clearance accounting for approximately 50% (11 L/h) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ENGAGE AF-TIMI 48:** A non-inferiority study in 21,105 NVAF patients. Edoxaban 60 mg was non-inferior to warfarin for the primary endpoint of stroke or systemic embolic events (SEE), with significantly lower rates of major bleeding and intracranial hemorrhage [US].
- **Hokusai VTE:** A study in 8,292 patients with DVT/PE. Edoxaban was non-inferior to warfarin for recurrent VTE following initial heparin therapy, with lower rates of clinically relevant bleeding [US].
- **Hokusai VTE Cancer:** A study in 1,050 patients with cancer-associated VTE. Edoxaban was non-inferior to dalteparin for recurrent VTE/major bleeding, though major bleeding rates were higher with edoxaban (primarily GI) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Keep out of reach of children.
