## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of major depressive disorder (major depression) [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.1 Therapeutic indications].
- Treatment of social anxiety disorder (social phobia) [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.1 Therapeutic indications].

- Treatment of obsessive‑compulsive disorder (OCD) [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.1 Therapeutic indications].

## 4. Dosage and Administration
- General dosing (adult):
  - Typical starting and usual maintenance dose is 10 mg once daily; dose may be increased to a maximum of 20 mg once daily depending on response [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2 Dose and method of administration].
  - Single daily oral dose; may be taken with or without food [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2 Dose and method of administration].
- Dosing by indication:
  - Major depression: start 10 mg once daily; may increase to 20 mg once daily based on response. A therapeutic response can take 2–4 weeks or longer; treatment of a single depressive episode should continue for a minimum of 6 months after remission to consolidate response [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2 Dose and method of administration].
  - Social anxiety disorder: start 10 mg once daily; may increase to 20 mg once daily based on response. Long‑term treatment is recommended to consolidate response and prevent relapse [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2 Dose and method of administration].
  - Generalised anxiety disorder: start 10 mg once daily; may increase to 20 mg once daily based on response. Long‑term treatment is recommended to consolidate response and prevent relapse [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2 Dose and method of administration].
  - Obsessive‑compulsive disorder: recommended starting dose 10 mg once daily; may increase to 20 mg once daily based on response. Long‑term treatment has been studied up to 40 weeks, and relapse‑prevention data include continuation after an open‑label phase [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2 Dose and method of administration].
- Special titration / initial dosing:
  - Hepatic impairment: initial dose of 5 mg once daily for the first two weeks is recommended; may then be increased to 10 mg once daily depending on response [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2 Dose and method of administration].
  - Known CYP2C19 poor metabolisers: initial dose of 5 mg once daily for the first two weeks is recommended; may then be increased to 10 mg once daily depending on response [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2 Dose and method of administration].
  - Elderly (≥65 years): recommended maximum maintenance dose is 10 mg once daily because of increased exposure (longer half‑life and decreased clearance) in this group [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2 Dose and method of administration; Section 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use].
- Renal impairment:
  - No dose adjustment required for mild or moderate renal impairment; no information available for severe renal impairment (creatinine clearance < 30 mL/min) and use should be cautious in that group [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2 Dose and method of administration; Section 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use].
- Paediatric use:
  - Safety and efficacy have not been established; **Escitalopram should not be used in children and adolescents under 18 years** [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2 Dose and method of administration; Section 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use].
- Missed dose:
  - Specific missed‑dose instructions are not provided in the available source; general caution noted that inadvertent missed doses have been associated with discontinuation‑type symptoms in some reports [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use].

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to citalopram, escitalopram or any excipients [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.3 Contraindications].
- Concomitant use with monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs) — including reversible MAOI (RIMA) moclobemide — or within 14 days of discontinuing a MAOI; at least one day after stopping a reversible MAOI (RIMA) such as moclobemide; and at least 14 days should elapse after stopping escitalopram before starting a MAOI or RIMA, due to risk of serotonin syndrome [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.3 Contraindications].
- Concomitant use with pimozide is contraindicated [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.3 Contraindications].
- Other contraindications beyond the above are not specified in the provided source document [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.3 Contraindications].

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- Boxed / highlighted warnings:
  - Clinical worsening and suicide risk: emergence or worsening of suicidal ideation and behaviour can occur, particularly at treatment initiation and during dose changes; close monitoring is required, especially in patients at increased risk (history of suicide attempts, severe ideation) [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use].
- General warnings and precautions (selected; full text in source):
  - Monitor for emergence of agitation, anxiety, panic attacks, irritability, hostility, impulsivity, akathisia, hypomania and mania; these symptoms have been reported in patients treated with antidepressants and may precede suicidality [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use].
  - Akathisia/psychomotor restlessness has been associated with SSRI use and is most likely within first few weeks; increasing dose may worsen symptoms [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use].
  - Bleeding risk: SSRIs (including escitalopram) have been associated with bleeding abnormalities (skin and mucous membranes). Use caution with concomitant anticoagulants, antiplatelet agents (including NSAIDs, aspirin), ticlopidine, dipyridamole, or in patients with bleeding diatheses; consider gastroprotection in high‑risk patients [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use; Section 4.5 Interactions with other medicines].
  - Serotonin syndrome: risk when combined with other serotonergic agents (e.g. triptans, opioids including tramadol, tryptophan). If serotonin syndrome is suspected, discontinue escitalopram and initiate supportive care [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use; Section 4.5 Interactions with other medicines].
  - Hyponatraemia / SIADH: rare reports; elderly and patients with cirrhosis or on concomitant hyponatraemia‑causing drugs are at greater risk [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use].
  - Seizures: discontinue escitalopram if seizures occur; avoid in unstable epilepsy and monitor patients with controlled epilepsy for increased seizure frequency [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use].
  - Mania/hypomania: antidepressants can precipitate manic episodes in patients with bipolar disorder; screen patients for risk prior to initiating treatment [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use].
  - Sexual dysfunction: SSRIs/SNRIs may cause sexual adverse effects; rare reports of persistent sexual dysfunction after discontinuation have been reported [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use].
  - Angle‑closure glaucoma: mydriasis possible; use caution in patients at risk for angle‑closure glaucoma [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use].
  - Electroconvulsive therapy (ECT): limited published experience with concurrent SSRIs and ECT; exercise caution [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use].
  - Discontinuation symptoms: abrupt cessation can cause dizziness, sensory disturbances (including paraesthesia and electric shock sensations), sleep disturbances, agitation/anxiety, nausea/vomiting, tremor, confusion, sweating, headache, diarrhoea, palpitations, emotional instability, irritability and visual disturbances; taper gradually over several weeks where possible to minimise symptoms [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2 Dose and method of administration; Section 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use].
  - Cardiac disease: escitalopram causes a small decrease in heart rate; caution when initiating in patients with pre‑existing bradycardia; limited experience in patients with recent myocardial infarction or unstable heart disease [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use].
  - Use in hepatic impairment: clearance reduced and plasma concentrations increased in hepatic impairment; dose reduction recommended (see dosing section) [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use; Section 4.2 Dose and method of administration].
  - Use in renal impairment: escitalopram is extensively metabolised; unchanged drug excreted in urine to a minor extent. No information for severe renal impairment (CrCl < 30 mL/min); use cautiously [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use; Section 4.2 Dose and method of administration].
- Monitoring requirements:
  - Close clinical monitoring for suicidality and clinical worsening during initiation and dose changes; monitor for signs of serotonin syndrome and bleeding; monitor seizure frequency in epileptic patients [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use; Section 4.5 Interactions with other medicines].
- Laboratory tests:
  - Effects on laboratory tests are referred to in the source but specific routine laboratory monitoring parameters are not detailed in the available excerpt [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use; Section 4.8 Adverse effects (Undesirable effects) referenced].

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Pharmacodynamic interactions:
  - Concomitant use with MAOIs (irreversible and reversible RIMAs) and linezolid (a reversible non‑selective MAOI) may precipitate serotonin syndrome; co‑administration is contraindicated or requires strict timing and close monitoring per guidelines described (see contraindications) [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.3 Contraindications; Section 4.5 Interactions with other medicines].
  - Concomitant serotonergic agents (e.g. triptans including sumatriptan, opioids including tramadol, tryptophan, St John’s Wort) may increase risk of serotonin syndrome — avoid or use with caution and monitor [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5 Interactions with other medicines; Section 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use].
  - Combined use with lithium or tryptophan has reported enhanced effects; use with caution [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5 Interactions with other medicines].
  - Concomitant use with agents affecting haemostasis (NSAIDs, aspirin, anticoagulants including warfarin, ticlopidine, dipyridamole) may potentiate bleeding risk; exercise caution and monitor [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5 Interactions with other medicines].
- Pharmacokinetic interactions:
  - Escitalopram biotransformation involves CYP2C19, CYP3A4 and CYP2D6 pathways; it is a very weak inhibitor of several CYPs and a weak inhibitor of CYP2D6 [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5 Interactions with other medicines].
  - CYP2C19 inhibitors (e.g. omeprazole) increase escitalopram plasma concentrations (~50% increase) and increase terminal half‑life (~31%); cimetidine increased plasma concentrations by ~70% — caution at upper dose range and consider dose reduction if clinically warranted [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5 Interactions with other medicines].
  - Escitalopram is a CYP2D6 inhibitor in vivo: co‑administration may increase plasma concentrations of drugs mainly metabolised by CYP2D6 with a narrow therapeutic index (e.g. flecainide, propafenone, some antiarrhythmics; some antidepressants and antipsychotics). Desipramine levels increased approximately two‑fold when co‑administered with escitalopram; metoprolol levels doubled but without clinically significant blood pressure/heart rate effects in the cited study [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5 Interactions with other medicines].
  - Ritonavir and ketoconazole co‑administration did not change escitalopram pharmacokinetics in cited studies; racemic citalopram data are referenced for some interactions [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5 Interactions with other medicines].
- Clinically significant interactions and management:
  - MAOIs, linezolid, pimozide: contraindicated or require strict timing/avoidance due to severe risk (serotonin syndrome; QT interaction with pimozide) [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.3 Contraindications; Section 4.5 Interactions with other medicines].
  - If co‑administering with CYP2C19 inhibitors, use caution at higher escitalopram doses and consider dose reduction based on clinical judgement [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5 Interactions with other medicines; Section 4.2 Dose and method of administration].
  - For co‑administration with drugs highly dependent on CYP2D6 clearance with narrow therapeutic indices, monitor for increased effects/toxicity and consider dose adjustment of the concomitant drug [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5 Interactions with other medicines].
  - Avoid or monitor combinations that increase bleeding risk (concomitant anticoagulant or antiplatelet therapy) [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5 Interactions with other medicines].
  - Detailed interaction management steps beyond these summaries are not specified in the available excerpt; refer to the full PI for complete interaction tables and clinical actions [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5 Interactions with other medicines].

## 10. Use in Special Populations
- Paediatric use:
  - Not approved; safety and efficacy not established in children and adolescents < 18 years; escitalopram should not be used in this group [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2 Dose and method of administration; Section 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use].
- Elderly:
  - Pharmacokinetic studies showed increased AUC and longer half‑life in subjects ≥ 65 years; recommended maximum maintenance dose in the elderly is 10 mg once daily [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2 Dose and method of administration; Section 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use].
- Renal impairment:
  - No dosage adjustment required for mild or moderate renal impairment; no information available for severe renal impairment (CrCl < 30 mL/min); use with caution in severe renal impairment [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2 Dose and method of administration; Section 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use].
- Hepatic impairment:
  - Clearance decreased and plasma concentrations increased in hepatic impairment; dose reduction is recommended — initial 5 mg once daily for first two weeks, with possible increase to 10 mg once daily depending on response [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2 Dose and method of administration; Section 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use].
- CYP2C19 poor metabolisers:
  - Recommended to start at 5 mg once daily for first two weeks and then consider increase to 10 mg once daily based on response [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2 Dose and method of administration].

## 11. Pharmacology
- Mechanism of action:
  - Escitalopram is a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI) — described as part of the SSRI class in the safety sections [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use].
- Pharmacokinetics (selected details available in the source):
  - Metabolism: biotransformation involves CYP2C19, CYP3A4 and CYP2D6 pathways [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5 Interactions with other medicines].
  - Effect of inhibitors: co‑administration with omeprazole (CYP2C19 inhibitor) increased escitalopram plasma concentrations by ~50% and increased terminal half‑life by ~31%; cimetidine increased plasma concentrations by ~70% in cited studies [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5 Interactions with other medicines].
  - Escitalopram is a weak inhibitor of CYP2D6 in vivo and a very weak inhibitor of CYP1A2, 2C9, 2C19, 2E1 and 3A4 [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5 Interactions with other medicines].
  - Distribution / elimination: escitalopram is extensively metabolised; excretion of unchanged drug in urine is a minor elimination route [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use].
  - Population differences: elderly subjects show increased AUC and half‑life compared to younger subjects [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use].
- Pharmacodynamics:
  - Not specified in detail in the provided excerpt beyond class effect as an SSRI; detailed pharmacodynamic parameters are Not specified in available sources [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 5 Pharmacology referenced but not provided in excerpt].

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Key trial information included in the provided PI excerpt:
  - OCD long‑term study: patients responding to a 16‑week open‑label phase were randomised into a 24‑week placebo‑controlled relapse‑prevention phase receiving 10 or 20 mg daily; long‑term treatment studied for up to 40 weeks in total [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2 Dose and method of administration].
  - Pooled analyses referenced: pooled analyses of 24 short‑term (4–16 week), placebo‑controlled trials of several antidepressants in children/adolescents showing increased suicidality signal; this is a class‑level pooled safety analysis referenced in the warnings (not escitalopram‑only pivotal trials) [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use].
