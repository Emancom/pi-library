# Editorial Product Information: Erdafitinib

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of erdafitinib for healthcare professionals, synthesized from available regulatory sources to ensure clinical clarity and structural consistency.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidance required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Erdafitinib, a small-molecule kinase inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** **BALVERSA** [US].
- **ATC Code:** L01EN01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 3 mg (yellow), 4 mg (orange), and 5 mg (brown) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Janssen Products, LP [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Urothelial Carcinoma:** Indicated for the treatment of adult patients with locally advanced or metastatic urothelial carcinoma (mUC) that has susceptible FGFR3 genetic alterations and has progressed on or after at least one line of prior systemic therapy [US].
- **Limitations of Use:** Not recommended for patients who are eligible for, but have not yet received, prior PD-1 or PD-L1 inhibitor therapy [US].
- **Patient Selection:** Treatment candidacy must be confirmed by an approved companion diagnostic for FGFR3 genetic alterations [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The recommended starting dose is **8 mg orally once daily**.
- **Dose Titration:** Increase to **9 mg once daily** at 14 to 21 days if the following criteria are met:
    - Serum phosphate level is < 9.0 mg/dL.
    - No ocular disorders are present.
    - No Grade 2 or higher adverse reactions have occurred [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Swallow tablets whole; do not crush or chew.
    - May be taken with or without food.
    - If vomiting occurs after administration, do not take an additional dose; resume the next day as scheduled.
- **Missed Dose:** Can be taken as soon as possible on the same day. Resume the regular schedule the following day. Do not double the dose to make up for a missed one [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None specified in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Ocular Disorders:** **BALVERSA** can cause central serous retinopathy or retinal pigment epithelial detachment (CSR/RPED), which may lead to visual field defects. 
    - *Monitoring:* Monthly ophthalmological exams for the first 4 months, then every 3 months.
    - *Management:* Withhold treatment if CSR/RPED occurs; permanently discontinue if it does not resolve within 4 weeks or if Grade 4 in severity [US].
- **Hyperphosphatemia and Soft Tissue Mineralization:** Increases in serum phosphate are a primary pharmacodynamic effect. This may lead to soft tissue mineralization, cutaneous calcinosis, and vascular calcification.
    - *Monitoring:* Monitor serum phosphate monthly.
    - *Management:* Restrict dietary phosphate (600–800 mg daily). Use phosphate binders, dose interruptions, or reductions based on severity [US].
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** May cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status before initiation. Advise females of reproductive potential and males with female partners to use effective contraception during and for one month after the final dose [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Very Common (>20%)
- **Laboratory Abnormalities:** Increased phosphate (76%), increased creatinine (52%), increased alkaline phosphatase, increased ALT/AST, decreased hemoglobin, decreased sodium, increased calcium, increased potassium.
- **Clinical Reactions:** Nail disorders (70%), stomatitis (56%), diarrhea (63%), fatigue (29%), dry mouth (39%), dry skin, decreased appetite, dysgeusia, constipation, dry eye, alopecia, and palmar-plantar erythrodysesthesia syndrome [US].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Serious adverse reactions occurred in 41% of patients; the most common (>2%) were urinary tract infection, hematuria, hyponatremia, and acute kidney injury [US].
- Fatal reactions (4.4%) included sudden death, pneumonia, and renal failure [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Moderate CYP2C9 or Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Increase erdafitinib concentration and toxicity risk. Consider alternatives or monitor closely [US].
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inducers:** Avoid concomitant use; these significantly decrease erdafitinib exposure [US].
- **Moderate CYP3A4 Inducers:** If a moderate inducer is required, increase erdafitinib dose to 9 mg [US].
- **P-glycoprotein (P-gp) Substrates:** Erdafitinib may increase concentrations of P-gp substrates. Separate administration by at least 6 hours for substrates with a narrow therapeutic index [US].
- **Phosphate-Altering Agents:** Avoid concomitant use with agents that alter serum phosphate during the initial dose-assessment period (Days 14–21) [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** No lettered category provided, but the drug is known to be teratogenic based on animal data (skeletal malformations and embryo-fetal death at exposures <0.1% of human levels). Avoid use [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on its presence in human milk. Advise women not to breastfeed during treatment and for one month after the final dose [US].
- **Fertility:** Based on animal studies, erdafitinib may impair female fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established. Skeletal adverse reactions (e.g., epiphysiolysis, fractures) have been reported in pediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in efficacy were observed compared to younger patients, but those ≥65 years had higher rates of treatment discontinuation due to adverse reactions [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for mild-to-moderate renal impairment. Data are limited for severe renal impairment [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild-to-moderate (Child-Pugh A or B) impairment. Data are limited for severe impairment [US].
- **Pharmacogenomics:** Erdafitinib exposure is predicted to be 50% higher in **CYP2C9*3/*3** genotype individuals (Poor Metabolizers). Monitor these patients closely for increased toxicity [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Erdafitinib is a kinase inhibitor that binds to and inhibits the enzymatic activity of FGFR1, FGFR2, FGFR3, and FGFR4. It inhibits signaling and decreases cell viability in cells expressing FGFR genetic alterations [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Median $T_{max}$ of 2.5 hours. No meaningful food effect.
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (99.7%), primarily to alpha-1-acid glycoprotein.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized by CYP2C9 (39%) and CYP3A4 (20%).
    - **Elimination:** Mean half-life of 59 hours. Excreted primarily in feces (69%) and urine (19%) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study BLC3001 (Cohort 1):** A randomized, open-label trial comparing erdafitinib to chemotherapy (docetaxel or vinflunine) in patients with mUC and FGFR3 alterations. Erdafitinib demonstrated superior Median Overall Survival (12.1 months vs. 7.8 months; HR 0.64) and Progression-Free Survival (5.6 months vs. 2.7 months) [US].
- **Study BLC2001:** A multi-center, single-arm study in 87 patients which initially established an Objective Response Rate (ORR) of 32.2% [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Dispense in original bottle.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription drug) [US].
