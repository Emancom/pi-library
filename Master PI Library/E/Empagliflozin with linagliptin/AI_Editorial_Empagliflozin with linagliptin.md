# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial synthesis: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview created for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises available regulatory data into a single, clinically focused reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) when prescribing or administering this medication in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP), also known as L-carnitine.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule for fatty acid transport).
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It should be administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion. In patients with severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) over the first 24 hours. Subsequent daily doses typically remain at 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). It should be administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Monitoring and Titration
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- **ESRD:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations <40–50 µmol/L. Downward dose adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough levels [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral drug products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Metabolite Accumulation:** While this PI concerns the injectable form, clinicians should note that chronic high doses of **oral** levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].
- **Monitoring:** In ESRD patients, the effects of supplemental carnitine on clinical outcomes (rather than just plasma levels) have not been fully determined [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are often confounded by the underlying pathology of metabolic disorders or ESRD.

### Other Reported Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; gastritis; body odor [US].
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. Recommendation: Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine is not bound to plasma proteins, reducing the likelihood of protein-binding displacement interactions [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category C]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. However, there are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after administration. Risk/benefit must be weighed, and consideration given to discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders follows the weight-based guidelines (50 mg/kg) used in adults [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical documents do not specify unique dose adjustments for the elderly, though general caution regarding renal function is prudent [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **ESRD:** Use is indicated for patients on dialysis to treat carnitine deficiency.
    - **Metabolic Disorders with Renal Impairment:** Use of **oral** levocarnitine is cautioned due to metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO); however, the injectable form bypasses initial GI metabolism and is readily removed by dialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Information not specified in available sources [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Following IV administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (n=12), 20 mg/kg post-dialysis increased mean plasma levocarnitine from 20 µmol/L (pre-dialysis) to troughs of 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks. 
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].
