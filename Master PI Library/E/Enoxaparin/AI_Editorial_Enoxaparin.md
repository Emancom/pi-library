# Editorial Product Information: Enoxaparin Sodium

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for educational and quick-reference purposes. It does not replace the official PI or local clinical guidelines required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the Australian PI was provided for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Enoxaparin sodium.
- **Chemical Class:** Low molecular weight heparin (LMWH).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Clexane**, **Clexane Forte** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** B01A B05.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Clexane:** Prefilled syringes in 20 mg/0.2 mL, 40 mg/0.4 mL, 60 mg/0.6 mL, 80 mg/0.8 mL, and 100 mg/1 mL [Australian].
    - **Clexane Forte:** Prefilled syringes in 120 mg/0.8 mL and 150 mg/1 mL [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) injection; Intravenous (IV) bolus injection (specific indications only); Injection into the arterial line of a dialysis circuit [Australian].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sanofi-Aventis Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
[All indications listed are Australian-approved]:
- Prevention of thromboembolic disorders of venous origin in patients undergoing orthopaedic or general surgery.
- Prophylaxis of venous thromboembolism (VTE) in medical patients bedridden due to acute illness.
- Prevention of thrombus formation in extra-corporeal circulation during haemodialysis.
- Treatment of established deep vein thrombosis (DVT).
- Treatment of unstable angina and non-Q-wave myocardial infarction (NSTEMI), administered concurrently with aspirin.
- Treatment of acute ST-segment Elevation Myocardial Infarction (STEMI), including patients managed medically or with subsequent Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **VTE Prophylaxis in Surgery:** 
    - **Moderate Risk:** 20 mg SC once daily. Initial dose ~2 hours preoperatively.
    - **High Risk (e.g., Orthopaedic):** 40 mg SC once daily. Initial dose ~12 hours preoperatively.
- **VTE Prophylaxis in Medical Patients:** 40 mg SC once daily for 6–14 days.
- **Treatment of DVT:** 1.5 mg/kg SC once daily OR 1 mg/kg SC twice daily. 
- **Unstable Angina/NSTEMI:** 1 mg/kg SC every 12 hours (with oral aspirin).
- **Acute STEMI:**
    - **Patients <75 years:** Single 30 mg IV bolus plus 1 mg/kg SC dose, followed by 1 mg/kg SC every 12 hours (Max 100 mg for the first two SC doses).
    - **Patients ≥75 years:** No IV bolus. 0.75 mg/kg SC every 12 hours (Max 75 mg for the first two SC doses).
- **Haemodialysis:** 1 mg/kg into the arterial line at the start of the session (usually sufficient for a 4-hour session).

### Administration Instructions
- **Subcutaneous:** Administer by deep SC injection, preferably while the patient is reclining. Alternate between left and right anterolateral abdominal wall. Introduce the needle vertically (90°) into a skin fold. **Do not** expel the air bubble from the syringe before injection. **Do not** rub the site after administration.
- **Intravenous (STEMI only):** Administer through an IV line; flush with saline or dextrose before and after the bolus.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to enoxaparin sodium, heparin, or other low molecular weight heparins [Australian].
- Acute bacterial endocarditis [Australian].
- Conditions with a high risk of uncontrolled haemorrhage (e.g., major bleeding disorders, haemorrhagic stroke, active GI ulceration) [Australian].
- History of immune-mediated heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT) within the last 100 days or in the presence of circulating antibodies [Australian].
- Thrombocytopenia associated with a positive in vitro test for anti-platelet antibodies in the presence of enoxaparin [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning/Serious Risk (Neuraxial Haematoma):** There is a risk of spinal/epidural haematoma when enoxaparin is used concurrently with spinal/epidural anaesthesia or spinal puncture, potentially resulting in long-term or permanent paralysis. Risk is increased by indwelling catheters, concomitant NSAIDs/platelet inhibitors, or traumatic/repeated punctures [Australian].
- **Interchangeability:** LMWH products are not clinically interchangeable as they differ in manufacturing, molecular weight, and anti-Xa activity [Australian].
- **Heparin-Induced Thrombocytopenia (HIT):** Use with extreme caution in patients with a history of HIT (>100 days ago) without circulating antibodies. Monitor platelet counts regularly [Australian].
- **Haemorrhage:** Use with extreme caution in conditions with increased bleeding risk (e.g., impaired haemostasis, severe hypertension, recent ischaemic stroke).
- **Prosthetic Heart Valves:** Use for thromboprophylaxis in patients with mechanical prosthetic heart valves is not recommended, especially in pregnant women, due to reports of valve thrombosis [Australian].
- **Hyperkalaemia:** Heparins can suppress adrenal aldosterone secretion; monitor potassium in patients at risk (e.g., diabetes, renal failure).

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Haemorrhage, Thrombocytosis (platelets >400 x 10⁹/L), Reversible increase in liver enzymes (transaminases) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Anaemia, Thrombocytopenia, Allergic reaction, Urticaria, Pruritus, Erythema, Injection site haematoma/pain/edema |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Intracranial haemorrhage, Retroperitoneal haemorrhage, Bullous dermatitis, Local irritation/skin necrosis at injection site |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Hyperkalaemia, Anaphylactic/anaphylactoid reactions, Spinal/neuraxial haematoma |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Major haemorrhage (potentially fatal), HIT with thrombosis (can lead to organ infarction or limb ischaemia), and osteoporosis following long-term therapy (>3 months) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Agents Affecting Haemostasis:** It is recommended to discontinue agents that increase bleeding risk unless strictly indicated, including:
    - Anticoagulants (e.g., warfarin).
    - Thrombolytics.
    - NSAIDs (including ketorolac).
    - Salicylates/Aspirin.
    - Antiplatelet agents (e.g., clopidogrel, ticlopidine, glycoprotein IIb/IIIa antagonists).
    - Systemic glucocorticoids [Australian].
- **Hyperkalaemic Agents:** Careful monitoring is required when co-administering with drugs that increase serum potassium [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Enoxaparin does not cross the placental barrier during the second trimester; data for the first and third trimesters are absent. Use only if a clear need is established by a physician.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if enoxaparin is excreted in human milk. As a precaution, breastfeeding is not recommended while using enoxaparin [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No effect on fertility or reproductive performance was observed in animal studies (rats) [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment is needed for prophylaxis unless renal function is impaired. In patients ≥75 years treated for STEMI, the IV bolus is eliminated and the SC dose is reduced (see Section 3).
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Severe (CrCl <30 mL/min):** Dosage adjustment is required.
        - Prophylaxis: 20 mg SC once daily.
        - Treatment: 1 mg/kg SC once daily.
        - STEMI (<75y): 30 mg IV bolus + 1 mg/kg SC, then 1 mg/kg SC once daily.
    - **Mild/Moderate:** No dose adjustment required, but clinical observation for bleeding is advised [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution due to lack of clinical studies [Australian].
- **Low Body Weight:** Increased exposure observed in women <45 kg and men <57 kg; monitor for bleeding [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Enoxaparin is a LMWH with high anti-Xa activity and low anti-IIa (anti-thrombin) activity (ratio >4). It acts primarily through binding to antithrombin III [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Bioavailability:** ~100% (SC route).
    - **Distribution:** Maximum anti-Xa activity occurs 3–5 hours post-SC injection.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic (desulphation and depolymerisation).
    - **Elimination:** Characterised by a half-life of ~4.4 hours (40 mg dose). Elimination is prolonged in patients with severe renal impairment [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Hip Replacement:** Trials demonstrated that extended prophylaxis (3 weeks post-discharge) significantly reduced DVT incidence compared to placebo (12% vs 28%) [Australian].
- **ESSENCE Trial:** In unstable angina/NSTEMI, enoxaparin (1 mg/kg BID) significantly reduced the composite endpoint of death, MI, or recurrent angina compared to unfractionated heparin (UFH).
- **ExTRACT-TIMI 25:** In STEMI patients, enoxaparin significantly reduced the rate of death or myocardial re-infarction at 30 days compared to UFH (9.9% vs 12.0%) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Conditions:** Do not freeze [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** 
    - 20 mg to 100 mg syringes: 3 years.
    - 120 mg and 150 mg syringes (Clexane Forte): 2 years.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (S4)** - Prescription Only Medicine [Australian].
