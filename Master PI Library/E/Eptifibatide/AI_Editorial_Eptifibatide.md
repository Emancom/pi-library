# Editorial Product Information: Eptifibatide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing in a particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Eptifibatide (as acetate).
- **Chemical Class:** Platelet aggregation inhibitor; a synthetic cyclic heptapeptide and RGD (arginine-glycine-aspartate)-mimetic.
- **Brand Name(s):** Eptifibatide Medsurge [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Bolus Injection:** 20 mg/10 mL (2 mg/mL) single-dose vial [Australian].
    - **Intravenous Infusion:** 75 mg/100 mL (0.75 mg/mL) single-dose vial [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Medsurge Pharma Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI):** Treatment of patients undergoing nonurgent PCI with intracoronary stenting [Australian].
- **Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS):** Management of patients with unstable angina (UA) or non-Q-wave myocardial infarction (NQMI) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing (≥18 years)
Dosing is dependent on renal function, determined via the Cockcroft-Gault equation using actual body weight.

**1. Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI):**
- **CrCl ≥50 mL/min:** Initial IV bolus of 180 µg/kg immediately before the procedure, followed by a second 180 µg/kg bolus 10 minutes later. Simultaneously, a continuous infusion of 2.0 µg/kg/min is initiated.
- **CrCl <50 mL/min:** Dual bolus of 180 µg/kg (separated by 10 minutes) as above, but with a reduced continuous infusion of 1.0 µg/kg/min.
- **Duration:** Continue infusion until hospital discharge or for 18–24 hours post-procedure (minimum 12 hours recommended).

**2. Unstable Angina / Non-Q-Wave MI (UA/NQMI):**
- **CrCl ≥50 mL/min:** IV bolus of 180 µg/kg administered as soon as possible after diagnosis, followed by a continuous infusion of 2.0 µg/kg/min.
- **CrCl <50 mL/min:** IV bolus of 180 µg/kg followed by a continuous infusion of 1.0 µg/kg/min.
- **Duration:** Continue for up to 72 hours, until discharge, or until coronary artery bypass graft (CABG) surgery, whichever occurs first. If PCI is performed during therapy, the infusion may be continued for 20–24 hours post-PCI (maximum total duration 96 hours).

### Administration Instructions
- Eptifibatide should be used in conjunction with heparin and aspirin unless contraindicated.
- **Heparin Dosing (UA/NQMI):** For patients ≥70 kg, a 5,000 U bolus followed by 1,000 U/hour infusion. For patients <70 kg, a 60 U/kg bolus followed by 12 U/kg/hour. Target aPTT: 50–70 seconds.
- **Heparin Dosing (PCI):** Initial bolus of 60 U/kg if no heparin was given within 6 hours. Target ACT: 200–300 seconds.

## 4. Contraindications
Eptifibatide is contraindicated in patients with [Australian]:
- Active abnormal bleeding (gastrointestinal, genitourinary, etc.) within the previous 30 days.
- History of stroke within the last 30 days or any history of haemorrhagic stroke.
- Known intracranial disease (e.g., neoplasm, aneurysm, arteriovenous malformation).
- Major surgery or severe trauma within the past 6 weeks.
- History of bleeding diathesis.
- Thrombocytopenia (platelet count <100 x 10⁹/L).
- Prothrombin time >1.2 times control or INR ≥2.0.
- Severe hypertension (SBP >200 mmHg or DBP >110 mmHg on therapy).
- Dependency on renal dialysis.
- Clinically significant hepatic impairment.
- Concurrent or planned use of thrombolytics or another parenteral GP IIb-IIIa inhibitor.
- Hypersensitivity to eptifibatide or any excipients.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Bleeding Risk:** The most common complication. Risk is increased in women, the elderly, and those with low body weight. Most bleeding occurs at the arterial access site (femoral).
- **Thrombocytopenia:** Rare instances of immune-mediated thrombocytopenia have been reported, even in patients previously unexposed to GP IIb-IIIa inhibitors. If platelet count drops <100 x 10⁹/L, discontinue eptifibatide and heparin immediately.
- **Arterial Procedures:** Careful haemostasis is required. Sheaths should only be removed once coagulation returns to normal (e.g., aPTT <45 seconds).
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Baseline PT, aPTT, serum creatinine, platelet count, haemoglobin, and haematocrit are required. Follow-up Hb, Hct, and platelets should be checked within 6 hours of starting therapy and daily thereafter.
- **Surgery:** If emergency cardiac surgery is required, stop the infusion immediately. For semi-elective surgery, stop at an appropriate time to allow platelet function to recover.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events are primarily related to bleeding and cardiovascular complications.

| Bleeding Event (PURSUIT Trial) | Eptifibatide (%) | Placebo (%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Major Bleeding** | 10.8% | 9.3% |
| **Minor Bleeding** | 13.1% | 7.6% |
| **Transfusion Requirement** | 12.8% | 10.4% |

- **Very Common (>10%):** Bleeding (major and minor).
- **Common (1–10%):** Hypotension, atrial fibrillation, congestive heart failure, cardiac arrest, nausea, headache, injection site reactions.
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Acute profound thrombocytopenia, pulmonary haemorrhage, anaphylaxis.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Intracranial haemorrhage (uncommon, ~0.1–0.2%), fatal bleeding (very rare).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Increased bleeding risk when combined with other agents affecting haemostasis (clopidogrel, ticlopidine, thrombolytics, oral anticoagulants, NSAIDs, dipyridamole).
- **Thrombolytics:** Use with streptokinase significantly increases major bleeding risk (15% vs 0% in placebo). 
- **Incompatibilities:** Eptifibatide is **incompatible** with furosemide in the same IV line. 
- **Compatibilities:** Compatible with 0.9% NaCl and 5% Dextrose/0.9% NaCl. Can be co-administered via the same line with atropine, dobutamine, heparin, lidocaine, midazolam, and morphine.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. No clinical studies in pregnant women. Animal studies in monkeys showed haemorrhage at human-equivalent doses. Use only if maternal benefit outweighs fetal risk.
- **Breastfeeding:** Excretion in human milk is unknown. Interruption of breastfeeding during the treatment period is recommended [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No effect on fertility observed in male or female rats at doses up to 72 mg/kg/day IV.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children <18 years have not been established; use is not recommended.
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of bleeding; monitor closely.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Mild (CrCl ≥50 mL/min):** No dose adjustment required.
    - **Moderate to Severe (CrCl <50 mL/min):** Reduce the infusion rate to 1.0 µg/kg/min.
    - **Dialysis:** Contraindicated in dialysis-dependent patients.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Experience is limited. Use with caution in patients with hepatic impairment that may affect coagulation. Contraindicated if impairment is clinically significant.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Eptifibatide reversibly inhibits platelet aggregation by preventing the binding of fibrinogen and von Willebrand factor to the glycoprotein (GP) IIb-IIIa receptor.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Immediate onset of action after bolus. At steady state (2.0 µg/kg/min infusion), >90% inhibition of ex vivo platelet aggregation is achieved. Platelet function returns toward baseline ~4–6 hours after stopping infusion.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is ~185–260 mL/kg. Plasma protein binding is ~25%.
    - **Metabolism:** No major metabolites detected in plasma; deamination occurs and is excreted in urine.
    - **Elimination:** Half-life is ~2.5 hours. Renal clearance accounts for ~50% of total body clearance.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **PURSUIT Trial (UA/NQMI):** A study of 10,948 patients showed that eptifibatide (180/2.0 regimen) significantly reduced the 30-day incidence of death or new MI compared to placebo (14.2% vs 15.7%; p=0.034). Benefit was primarily seen in the reduction of MI.
- **ESPRIT Trial (PCI):** A study of 2,064 patients undergoing nonurgent PCI. Eptifibatide (dual bolus + infusion) significantly reduced the primary endpoint (death, MI, urgent TVR, or rescue therapy) at 48 hours (6.6% vs 10.5%; p=0.0015) and 30 days.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze).
- **Light Protection:** Store in the original container to protect from light. Light protection is not required during administration.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian]. Single-use only; discard unused residue.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
