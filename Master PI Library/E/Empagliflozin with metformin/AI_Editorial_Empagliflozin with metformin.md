# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial:
    - **US (FDA)**: Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation)
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises available data into a single clinical reference.
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Levocarnitine (also known as L-carnitine).
- **Chemical Class**: A naturally occurring carrier molecule; chemically designated as (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium.
- **Brand Names**: Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code**: A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength**: 
    - Sterile injection solution: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration**: Intravenous only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency**: Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD)**: Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Metabolic Disorders**:
    - **Recommended Dose**: 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose**: Often initiated in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
    - **Frequency**: Doses should be given every 3 or 4 hours, and never less frequently than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Maximum Dose**: Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis**:
    - **Starting Dose**: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
    - **Administration**: Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - **Dose Adjustments**: Adjustments (e.g., reduction to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations [US].
- **Monitoring**: It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Regular monitoring (weekly/monthly) should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Preparation**: Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration. Discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions**: Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks/benefits before re-administration [US].
- **Seizures**: Levocarnitine may increase the frequency or severity of seizures in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a prior history of epilepsy [US].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions**: While not directly applicable to the injection, clinicians should note that chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5% in ESRD clinical trials)
Based on placebo-controlled trials in chronic hemodialysis patients [US]:
- **General**: Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), headache (22%), abdominal pain (9%), fever (7%).
- **Cardiovascular**: Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), chest pain (12%).
- **Digestive**: Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), anorexia (5%).
- **Respiratory**: Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%).
- **Metabolic**: Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%).
- **Neurologic**: Dizziness (13%), paresthesia (5%).

### Serious and Other Adverse Effects
- **Hypersensitivity**: Anaphylaxis and laryngeal edema [US].
- **Neurologic**: Seizures [US].
- **Gastrointestinal**: Transient nausea and vomiting; gastritis; body odor [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin**: Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
    - **Management**: Monitor INR levels closely following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**:
    - **US Classification**: Not formally assigned a letter category in this document, but animal reproductive studies (rats/rabbits) at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm [US].
    - **Recommendation**: Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk. The risks of excess carnitine intake by the infant must be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility**: No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Dosing for infants and children is included in the metabolic disorder recommendations (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use**: Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment**: Specifically indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD patients undergoing dialysis. Dosing adjustments should be based on pre-dialysis trough levels [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Levocarnitine is essential for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Distribution**: Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism**: Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination**: Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies**: Clinical trials demonstrated that 20 mg/kg doses administered three times weekly post-dialysis significantly increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Trough levels typically stabilised or increased over 8 to 24 weeks of therapy. While biochemical correction was achieved, the impact on specific clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions**: Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing [US].
- **Stability**: When diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL), the solution is stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification**: Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule**: Not specified in available sources.
