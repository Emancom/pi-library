# Editorial Product Information: Ezetimibe

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory guidance for the most current legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ezetimibe; a lipid-modifying compound belonging to the class of selective intestinal cholesterol absorption inhibitors.
- **Brand Names:** AKM Ezetimibe [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 10 mg oral tablets.
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pharmacor Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Ezetimibe is indicated for the following:

- **Primary Hypercholesterolaemia:** As adjunctive therapy to diet, either as monotherapy or co-administered with an HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor (statin), in patients with primary (heterozygous familial and non-familial) hypercholesterolaemia [Australian].
- **Homozygous Familial Hypercholesterolaemia (HoFH):** Administered in combination with a statin (and other adjunctive treatments such as LDL apheresis) [Australian].
- **Homozygous Sitosterolaemia (Phytosterolaemia):** For the reduction of elevated sitosterol and campesterol levels [Australian].
- **Prevention of Cardiovascular Disease:** In combination with the maximum tolerated dose of a statin in patients with coronary heart disease (CHD) and a history of acute coronary syndrome (ACS) who require additional LDL-C lowering [Australian].
- **Paediatric Patients (10-17 years):** Adjunctive therapy to diet in Heterozygous Familial Hypercholesterolaemia (HeFH) or HoFH when co-administered with simvastatin (up to 40 mg) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** 10 mg once daily, administered with or without food at any time of the day [Australian].
- **Combination Therapy:** When used with a statin, ezetimibe 10 mg may be taken at the same time as the statin [Australian].
- **Bile Acid Sequestrants:** Ezetimibe should be administered ≥2 hours before or ≥4 hours after a bile acid sequestrant to avoid absorption interference [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment (Chronic Kidney Disease):**
    - **Monotherapy:** No dose adjustment required [Australian].
    - **Combo with Simvastatin:** In patients with eGFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m², the dose is ezetimibe 10 mg plus simvastatin 20 mg once daily in the evening [Australian].
- **Dietary Considerations:** Patients must maintain an appropriate lipid-lowering diet throughout treatment [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to ezetimibe or any excipients [Australian].
- **When co-administered with a statin:**
    - Active liver disease or unexplained persistent elevations in serum transaminases [Australian].
    - Pregnancy and lactation [Australian].
- **When co-administered with fenofibrate:**
    - Pre-existing gallbladder disease [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Liver Enzymes:** There is a causal association between ezetimibe monotherapy and drug-induced liver injury (DILI). Liver function tests (LFTs) should be performed at the initiation of therapy and as clinically indicated. When used with a statin, follow the LFT monitoring requirements of the specific statin [Australian].
- **Skeletal Muscle:** Myopathy and rhabdomyolysis have been reported. Patients should promptly report unexplained muscle pain, tenderness, or weakness. Therapy (and any concomitant statin) should be discontinued if myopathy (CPK >10x ULN) is diagnosed or suspected [Australian].
- **Fibrates:** Co-administration with fibrates other than fenofibrate is not recommended due to a lack of safety/efficacy data [Australian].
- **Cyclosporin:** Use with caution; monitor cyclosporin concentrations due to increased ezetimibe exposure [Australian].
- **Anticoagulants:** Monitor International Normalised Ratio (INR) when adding ezetimibe to warfarin or other coumarin anticoagulants [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Ezetimibe is generally well tolerated. Adverse reactions are typically mild and transient.

### Frequency of Adverse Effects
| System Organ Class | Common (1–10%) | Uncommon (0.1–1%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Abdominal pain, diarrhoea, flatulence | Dyspepsia, GERD, dry mouth, gastritis |
| **Musculoskeletal** | Myalgia (especially in combo with statins) | Muscle spasms, neck pain, back pain, weakness |
| **General** | Fatigue | Asthenia, peripheral edema, chest pain |
| **Investigations** | ALT/AST increased (in combo) | GGT increased, abnormal LFTs |
| **Nervous System** | Headache | Dizziness, paraesthesia |

- **Serious/Post-marketing effects:** Anaphylaxis, angioedema, rash, urticaria, Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), pancreatitis, thrombocytopenia, and hepatitis [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Cyclosporin:** Results in a significant increase in ezetimibe exposure (mean AUC increased 3.4-fold to 12-fold in renal transplant patients). Monitor accordingly [Australian].
- **Bile Acid Sequestrants:** Colestyramine reduces the mean AUC of total ezetimibe by approximately 55%. Separate dosing is required [Australian].
- **Fibrates:** Fenofibrate and gemfibrozil increase total ezetimibe concentrations (1.5-fold and 1.7-fold respectively), though not considered clinically significant [Australian].
- **Statins:** No clinically significant pharmacokinetic interactions observed with atorvastatin, simvastatin, pravastatin, lovastatin, or fluvastatin [Australian].
- **Anticoagulants:** Potential for increased INR when combined with warfarin [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3 [Australian]**. Use only if potential benefit outweighs risk. Ezetimibe in combination with statins is strictly contraindicated in pregnancy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Excreted in rat milk; unknown if excreted in human milk. Use is not recommended unless potential benefit justifies the risk [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No effect on fertility in animal studies at high doses [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:**
    - **10–17 years:** Approved for HeFH and HoFH (co-administered with simvastatin). No dosage adjustment required. Long-term safety (>1 year) not established [Australian].
    - **<10 years:** Not recommended. Limited data in ages 6-10; no data in ages <6 [Australian].
- **Elderly:** No dosage adjustment required. Plasma concentrations are ~2-fold higher in the elderly, but safety and LDL-C reduction are comparable to younger patients [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment necessary for ezetimibe monotherapy [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild (Child-Pugh 5-6):** No adjustment required [Australian].
    - **Moderate/Severe (Child-Pugh >7):** Not recommended due to significantly increased ezetimibe exposure (4-fold) [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selectively inhibits the Niemann-Pick C1-Like 1 (NPC1L1) sterol transporter at the brush border of the small intestine. This inhibits the absorption of biliary and dietary cholesterol without affecting the absorption of triglycerides, fatty acids, or fat-soluble vitamins [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed and extensively conjugated to ezetimibe-glucuronide (the active metabolite). Cmax for glucuronide: 1-2 hours; ezetimibe: 4-12 hours [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (99.7%) [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily in the small intestine and liver via glucuronidation; minimal oxidative metabolism [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Undergoes significant enterohepatic recycling. Eliminated mostly in faeces (78%) and urine (11%). Half-life is ~22 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Monotherapy:** In 12-week studies (n=1719), ezetimibe 10 mg significantly reduced LDL-C by ~18% compared to placebo [Australian].
- **IMPROVE-IT (n=18,144):** Demonstrated that adding ezetimibe to simvastatin in ACS patients provided a 6.4% relative risk reduction in the primary composite endpoint of CV death, major coronary events, and non-fatal stroke over 7 years [Australian].
- **SHARP (n=9,438):** In patients with chronic kidney disease, the combination of ezetimibe 10 mg and simvastatin 20 mg reduced the risk of major vascular events by 16% compared to placebo over a median of 4.9 years [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C in a dry place [Australian].
- Shelf life varies by pack size; refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].
