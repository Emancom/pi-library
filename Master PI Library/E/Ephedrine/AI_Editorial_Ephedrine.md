# Editorial Product Information: Ephedrine Sulfate

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of ephedrine sulfate for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a single, structured reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for educational and professional reference. It does not replace the official PI or local regulatory guidelines required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the Australian PI was available for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ephedrine sulfate
- **Chemical Class:** Sympathomimetic amine (Alpha and Beta-adrenergic agonist)
- **Brand Name(s):** DBL Ephedrine Sulfate Injection [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** R03CA02 (Systemic adrenergics); AB01 (Adrenergic and dopaminergic agents) [Not explicitly stated in source, included for clinical context]
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 30 mg ephedrine sulfate and 3 mg sodium chloride per mL [Australian]
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intramuscular (IM), Subcutaneous (SC), or slow Intravenous (IV) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Shock:** Treatment of shock states that remain unresponsive to fluid replacement [Australian].
- **Anaesthesia-Related Hypotension:** Management of hypotension secondary to spinal anaesthesia [Australian].
- **Bronchospasm:** Treatment of bronchial asthma and reversible bronchospasm; however, use for this indication is largely superseded by more selective beta-2 adrenergic agonists [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Pressor Effect (IM/SC):** Usual dose is 25 mg to 50 mg (range 10–50 mg).
- **Pressor Effect (IV):** 10 mg to 25 mg, administered slowly. May be repeated every 5 to 10 minutes until the desired response is achieved. 
- **Maximum Dose:** Parenteral adult dose should not exceed 150 mg in 24 hours [Australian].

### Dosing by Indication
- **Bronchospasm (Adult):** 12.5 mg to 25 mg via IM, SC, or IV route. Subsequent dosing is determined by clinical response [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing
- **Standard Dose:** 3 mg/kg/day or 100 mg/m²/day, administered via IV or SC routes in 4 to 6 divided doses [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **IV Administration:** Must be given slowly. 
- **Extravasation Risk:** Caution is required to avoid extravasation, as this may lead to tissue necrosis and sloughing [Australian].
- **Blood Pressure Targets:** Maintain systolic BP at 80–100 mmHg in previously normotensive patients. In previously hypertensive patients, maintain systolic BP at 30–40 mmHg below their usual baseline [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachyarrhythmias, ventricular fibrillation, and asymmetric septal hypertrophy (idiopathic hypertrophic subaortic stenosis) [Australian].
- **Endocrine:** Pheochromocytoma (risk of severe hypertension) [Australian].
- **Ocular:** Closed-angle glaucoma [Australian].
- **Psychiatric:** Psychoneurosis [Australian].
- **Drug Interactions:** Concurrent use of Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs) or use within 14 days of discontinuing MAOI therapy [Australian].
- **Anaesthesia:** Use during general anaesthesia with cyclopropane, halothane, or other halogenated hydrocarbons [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to ephedrine [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypovolaemia:** Ephedrine is not a substitute for fluid, blood, plasma, or electrolyte replacement. Volume depletion should be corrected prior to therapy. It must not be used as sole therapy in hypovolaemic patients [Australian].
- **Tissue Stores:** Prolonged use may deplete noradrenaline stores at sympathetic nerve endings, potentially reducing the drug's efficacy (tachyphylaxis) [Australian].
- **Organ Perfusion:** Prolonged administration of pressor agents has been linked to oedema, haemorrhage, and necrosis of the intestine, liver, kidneys, and myocardium [Australian].
- **Physiological Balance:** Hypoxia, hypercapnia, and acidosis may decrease ephedrine's effectiveness or increase the risk of adverse effects; these should be corrected before or during administration [Australian].
- **Specific Comorbidities:** Use with extreme caution in patients with hypertension, hyperthyroidism, cardiovascular disease (angina, arrhythmia, coronary insufficiency), or diabetes (risk of drug-induced hyperglycaemia) [Australian].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Continuous monitoring of blood pressure, ECG, cardiac output, central venous pressure (CVP), and pulmonary artery pressure is required. Urinary output must also be monitored [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Note: Frequencies were not specified in the available Australian source.*

- **Cardiovascular:** Palpitations, tachycardia, bradycardia, hypertension, hypotension, extrasystole, precordial pain, and arrhythmias (including ventricular fibrillation).
- **Nervous System:** Nervousness, anxiety, restlessness, insomnia, irritability, trembling, dizziness, vertigo, confusion, delirium, and euphoria. Long-term high-dose use may result in psychosis (paranoia, hallucinations, depression).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, mild epigastric distress.
- **Genito-urinary:** Difficult or painful urination, acute urinary retention (especially in prostatic hypertrophy).
- **Respiratory:** Dyspnoea, shortness of breath.
- **General/Other:** Pallor, fever, headache, sweating, dryness of the mouth/nose/throat.
- **Addiction:** Physical addiction is reported after excessive long-term use (more common with oral administration) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **MAOIs:** Potentiation of cardiac and pressor effects (Contraindicated) [Australian].
- **Alpha-Blockers:** May reduce the vasopressor effect of ephedrine [Australian].
- **Beta-Blockers:** May inhibit cardiac and bronchodilator effects [Australian].
- **General Anaesthetics (Halogenated):** Increased risk of arrhythmias [Australian].
- **Ergot Alkaloids/Oxytocin:** Potentiation of pressor effects; risk of peripheral vascular ischaemia and gangrene [Australian].
- **Antihypertensives:** May decrease the effects of guanethidine; concurrent use with methyldopa or reserpine may reduce the pressor response [Australian].
- **Tricyclic Antidepressants (TCAs):** Potentiation of cardiovascular and pressor effects [Australian].
- **Urinary Alkalinisers (e.g., Acetazolamide, Sodium Bicarbonate):** Increase the half-life and decrease the elimination of ephedrine, potentially leading to toxicity [Australian].
- **Cardiac Glycosides:** Increased risk of arrhythmias [Australian].
- **Theophylline:** Increased incidence of CNS and GI adverse effects [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category A:** Considered safe based on extensive use in pregnant women without proven increases in malformations or harmful effects.
    - **Clinical Notes:** May accelerate foetal heart rate when used for spinal anaesthesia-induced hypotension during delivery. Should not be used if maternal blood pressure exceeds 130/80 mmHg [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Distributed into breast milk. Not recommended for use during lactation due to the risk of adverse effects in the infant [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Data not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing established (see Section 3). Specific safety data not otherwise available in source [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Caution advised in geriatric males, particularly those with prostatic hypertrophy, due to the high risk of acute urinary retention [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A sympathomimetic that stimulates both alpha and beta-adrenergic receptors and induces the release of noradrenaline from storage sites [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Increases cardiac output, increases systolic and diastolic blood pressure (via vasoconstriction and cardiac stimulation), and relaxes bronchial smooth muscle. It also decreases intestinal motility and affects bladder tone (relaxes detrusor, contracts sphincter) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed following IM or SC administration.
    - **Distribution:** Onset of action is 10–20 minutes (IM). Duration of response is approximately 1 hour after IV (10–25 mg) or IM/SC (25–50 mg) administration. Crosses the placenta [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Small amounts metabolised in the liver.
    - **Elimination:** Majority excreted unchanged in the urine. Plasma half-life is 3 to 6 hours. Elimination is increased (half-life decreased) as urinary pH decreases [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Light:** Protect from light [Australian].
- **Incompatibilities:** Physically incompatible with phenobarbitone sodium, pentobarbitone sodium, quinalbarbitone sodium, thiopentone sodium, and hydrocortisone sodium succinate [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
