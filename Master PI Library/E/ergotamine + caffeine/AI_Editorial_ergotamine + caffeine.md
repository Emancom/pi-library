## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment to abort or prevent vascular headache, for example:
  - Migraine
  - Migraine variants
  - “Histaminic cephalalgia” (as listed in the product information)
- Use should be limited to migraine/headache indications; not effective as a general analgesic.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Initial dose at onset of attack: 2 tablets.
- If needed, 1 additional tablet may be taken every ½ hour.
- Maximum per single attack: 6 tablets.
- Maximum per 7‑day period: 10 tablets.
- Do not use for chronic daily administration.

### Timing and special measures
- For optimal effect, begin dosing at the first sign of a migraine attack.
- In selected patients, short‑term administration at bedtime may be considered as a preventive measure, with careful attention to maximum dose limits.

### Administration instructions
- Take at onset of migraine; follow the specified incremental dosing (2 tablets initially, then 1 tablet every ½ hour as needed up to limits given).
- If vomiting prevents oral intake, rectal administration of ergotamine (in formulations intended for rectal use) has been described as an alternative; dosing/administration details for rectal use are not specified for this product.

## 5. Contraindications
List of contraindications (all items taken from available product information):
- Concomitant use with potent CYP3A4 inhibitors (examples provided: ritonavir, nelfinavir, indinavir, erythromycin, clarithromycin, troleandomycin, ketoconazole, itraconazole). Coadministration has been associated with acute ergot toxicity (vasospasm, ischemia, and in some cases amputation or death).
- Women who are pregnant or may become pregnant (ergotamine may cause fetal harm; oxytocic effects).
- Peripheral vascular disease.
- Coronary heart disease.
- Hypertension.
- Impaired hepatic function.
- Impaired renal function.
- Sepsis.
- Hypersensitivity to any component of the product.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warning
- **Serious and/or life‑threatening peripheral ischemia and other vasospastic events have been associated with coadministration of ergotamine with potent CYP3A4 inhibitors. Concomitant use with potent CYP3A4 inhibitors is contraindicated. Elevated ergotamine exposure from CYP3A4 inhibition may produce vasospasm leading to cerebral ischemia and/or extremity ischemia; cases have resulted in amputation and death.**

### General warnings and precautions
- Vasospasm / ergotism: Ergotamine can cause intense arterial vasoconstriction leading to peripheral ischemia. Chronic intoxication may produce intermittent claudication, muscle pain, numbness, coldness, pallor of digits, and ultimately gangrene if untreated. Although most severe cases are associated with overdosage or long‑term indiscriminate use, vasospastic events have been reported with short‑term or recommended dosing.
- Fibrotic complications: Reports exist of retroperitoneal and pleuropulmonary fibrosis and rare reports of valvular fibrotic thickening following long‑term continuous ergotamine use. Ergotamine should not be used for chronic daily administration.
- Pregnancy and labour: Contraindicated in pregnancy because of oxytocic effects and potential fetal harm. Contraindicated in labour and delivery due to maximal oxytocic effect in late pregnancy.
- Skin and dye hypersensitivity: The product contains FD&C Yellow No. 5 (tartrazine), which can cause allergic‑type reactions (including bronchial asthma) in susceptible individuals, particularly those with aspirin sensitivity.
- Drug abuse and dependence: Reports of psychological dependence and drug abuse exist; counsel patients to adhere to recommended dosing limits.
- Intercurrent vasoconstrictor use: Do not administer with other vasoconstrictors. Use with sympathomimetic pressor agents may cause excessive blood pressure elevation.
- Cardiovascular caution: Use caution in patients at risk for coronary or cerebrovascular disease; monitor for chest pain, arrhythmia, and ECG changes.
- Hepatic/renal impairment: Product information lists impaired hepatic or renal function as contraindications; avoid use in these patients.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor for signs and symptoms of peripheral ischemia (e.g., numbness, tingling, coldness, pallor, diminished or absent peripheral pulses), chest pain or precordial distress, changes in heart rate, hypertension/hypotension, and neurologic changes (e.g., visual field changes, vertigo).
- During prolonged or repeated therapy, monitor for signs of fibrotic complications (respiratory symptoms, abdominal/back pain suggestive of retroperitoneal fibrosis, or new cardiac murmurs/valvular disease).

### Specific clinical precautions
- Avoid coadministration with potent CYP3A4 inhibitors; consider potential for serious toxicity with less potent CYP3A4 inhibitors and agents known to affect CYP3A4 activity (examples listed under Interactions).
- Caution in patients who smoke or use nicotine products — nicotine may enhance vasoconstrictive response.
- Propranolol has been reported to potentiate ergotamine vasoconstrictive effects; exercise caution with concomitant beta‑blockers.
- Ergotamine is reported to inhibit CYP3A4‑catalyzed reactions — consider bidirectional interactions.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Potent CYP3A4 inhibitors markedly increase ergotamine exposure and have been associated with acute ergot toxicity (vasospasm, ischemia, gangrene, cerebral ischemia, and death). Concomitant use with potent CYP3A4 inhibitors is contraindicated.
  - Examples of potent inhibitors listed: ritonavir, nelfinavir, indinavir, erythromycin, clarithromycin, troleandomycin, ketoconazole, itraconazole.
- Macrolide antibiotics and protease inhibitors have been specifically reported to raise ergotamine blood levels when coadministered orally, presumably via CYP3A4 inhibition.
- Ergotamine itself has been reported to inhibit CYP3A4‑catalyzed reactions.
- No pharmacokinetic interactions involving other CYP isoenzymes are specified.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Concomitant use with other vasoconstrictors is not recommended; risk of additive vasospasm.
- Sympathomimetic (pressor) agents: may cause extreme increases in blood pressure when used with ergotamine.
- Beta‑blockers (propranolol): reported to potentiate ergotamine’s vasoconstrictive action by antagonizing epinephrine‑mediated vasodilation.
- Nicotine: may provoke vasoconstriction and predispose to greater ischemic response with ergot therapy.

### Management notes and cautions
- Coadministration with potent CYP3A4 inhibitors: contraindicated.
- Use with less potent CYP3A4 inhibitors: there is potential risk for serious toxicity; prescribers should consider the CYP3A4 inhibitory effect of concomitant agents before prescribing ergotamine. Examples of less potent CYP3A4 inhibitors listed include saquinavir, nefazodone, fluconazole, fluoxetine, grapefruit juice, fluvoxamine, zileuton, metronidazole, and clotrimazole.
- Do not administer with other vasoconstrictors or sympathomimetic pressor agents.
- Monitor closely if combining with agents that may influence vascular tone or CYP3A4 activity.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Product information states that ergotamine + caffeine is contraindicated in pregnancy due to oxytocic effects and potential for fetal harm.
- [US] Pregnancy category: X.
- Rationale included in source: caffeine crosses the placenta and has been teratogenic in animal studies; ergotamine crosses the placenta in small amounts and prolonged uterine vasoconstriction/myometrial effects may contribute to fetal growth retardation observed in animals.

### Breastfeeding
- Ergotamine is excreted in human milk and may produce adverse effects in nursing infants (reported symptoms include vomiting, diarrhea, weak pulse, and unstable blood pressure).
- Recommendation in the product information: weigh importance of therapy to the mother against potential for serious adverse reactions in the nursing infant; consider discontinuing nursing or the drug.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Ergotamine: described in the product information as exerting direct actions on smooth muscle of peripheral and cranial blood vessels (vasoconstrictive) and producing central depression of vasomotor centers; it also has properties of serotonin antagonism. The product information contrasts ergotamine’s adrenergic blocking and vasoconstrictive properties relative to hydrogenated ergotamine.
- Caffeine: described as a cranial vasoconstrictor added to enhance the vasoconstrictive effect without increasing ergotamine dose.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Metabolism: ergotamine is metabolized by cytochrome P450 3A (CYP3A4); inhibitors of CYP3A4 increase systemic exposure to ergotamine.
- Ergotamine has been reported to inhibit CYP3A4‑catalyzed reactions.
- Absorption, distribution (including plasma protein binding), elimination half‑life, bioavailability, and excretion parameters: Not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Vasoconstriction of cranial and peripheral vessels; central vasomotor depression contributing to therapeutic effect; caffeine potentiates cranial vasoconstriction.
