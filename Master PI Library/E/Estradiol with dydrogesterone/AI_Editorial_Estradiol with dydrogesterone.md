# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from the official Product Information (PI) source provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. It is designed to consolidate clinical data into a concise, structured format.
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. 
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [United States].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection solution: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [United States].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [United States].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow (2 to 3 minute) bolus injection or by infusion. 
    - **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours (q3h or q4h) and never less frequently than every 6 hours (q6h).
    - **Maximum Dose:** Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [United States].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** 
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [United States].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- **Compatibility:** Stable and compatible for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [United States].

### Monitoring
- Establish baseline plasma carnitine levels prior to therapy.
- Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known:** There are no listed contraindications for levocarnitine injection in the available source [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs [United States].
- **Seizures:** Reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [United States].
- **Oral Formulation Caution:** The safety of oral levocarnitine in patients with renal impairment or ESRD on dialysis has not been established. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these populations may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites: trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are frequently confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic errors.

### Very Common (>10%)
- **Body as a Whole:** Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (30–35%), headache (up to 37% [United States]), fever (up to 12%), asthenia (up to 12%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (up to 19%), chest pain (up to 15%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%).
- **Nervous:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (up to 12%).

### Common (1–10%)
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hyperkalemia (6%), peripheral edema (3–6%).
- **Special Senses:** Taste perversion (2–9%), amblyopia (up to 6%).
- **Postmarketing Reports:** Seizures, body odor, and gastritis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when co-administered with levocarnitine.
- **Management:** It is recommended to monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned a letter grade in the source, but animal reproductive studies (rats/rabbits) at 3.8 times the human dose revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm.
    - **Clinical Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate exogenous levocarnitine increases milk concentrations. The risks to the infant of excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg for metabolic disorders; 10–20 mg/kg for ESRD). Refer to Section 3 for specific instructions.
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the available source; however, dose selection should generally be cautious, reflecting the greater frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Intravenous:** Specifically indicated for ESRD patients on hemodialysis.
    - **Oral:** Caution advised due to potential accumulation of TMA and TMAO metabolites [United States].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours.
    - **Dialysis:** Levocarnitine is easily removed from plasma by hemodialysis [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Pharmacokinetic Study:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x/week post-dialysis), mean trough levocarnitine concentrations increased from 21.3 µmol/L (baseline) to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Evaluated 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses. Trough concentrations at 24 weeks were 148, 240, and 456 µmol/L, respectively. Note: While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations was demonstrated, effects on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population were not determined in these trials [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C.
- **Handling:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing.
- **Stability:** The product contains no preservative; discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial [United States].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as **Rx-only**.
