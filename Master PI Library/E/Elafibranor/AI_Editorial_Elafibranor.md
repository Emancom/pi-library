# Editorial Product Information: Elafibranor

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **elafibranor** for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a clinically focused reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Elafibranor
- **Chemical Class:** Peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor (PPAR) agonist
- **Brand Name(s):** **IQIRVO** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** A05AX06
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 80 mg film-coated tablets [Australian]
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Ipsen Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Primary Biliary Cholangitis (PBC):** Indicated for the treatment of PBC in adults as:
    - Combination therapy with ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA) in patients with an inadequate response to UDCA.
    - Monotherapy in patients who are unable to tolerate UDCA.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** 80 mg administered once daily.
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets should be taken orally and may be administered with or without food.
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** If a dose is missed, the patient should skip the missed dose and resume the normal schedule at the next designated time. Patients must not double the dose to compensate for a missed one.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to **elafibranor** or any of the excipients (including microcrystalline cellulose, povidone, croscarmellose sodium, magnesium stearate, and colloidal anhydrous silica).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Liver-Related Events:** Increases in transaminases and bilirubin have been observed (3.7% in treated patients vs 5.7% in placebo). 
    - *Monitoring:* Baseline liver function tests (LFTs) are required before initiation. Continued monitoring should follow routine clinical management. 
    - *Management:* If liver dysfunction or significant biochemical increases occur, investigate the cause promptly; treatment interruption should be considered.
- **Myalgia, Myopathy, and Rhabdomyolysis:** Increases in blood creatine phosphokinase (CPK) were reported in 3.7% of treated patients. One case of rhabdomyolysis occurred in a patient with cirrhosis concurrently taking an HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor (statin).
    - *Monitoring:* CPK should be evaluated prior to initiation. Periodic CPK measurements are recommended, particularly for patients on concomitant statin therapy.
    - *Patient Advice:* Patients should report any unexplained muscle pain, tenderness, or weakness.
- **Embryo-Foetal Toxicity:** Based on animal data, **elafibranor** may cause foetal harm.
    - *Requirement:* Pregnancy status must be verified in females of reproductive potential before starting treatment.
    - *Contraception:* Effective contraception is required during treatment and for 3 weeks following the final dose.
- **Additional Monitoring:** This product is subject to additional monitoring to facilitate quick identification of new safety information [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The safety profile is derived primarily from the Phase 3 ELATIVE study.

| Frequency | System Organ Class | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (>10%) | Gastrointestinal | Abdominal pain, Diarrhoea, Nausea, Vomiting |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Nervous System | Headache |
| | Gastrointestinal | Constipation |
| | Hepatobiliary | Cholelithiasis |
| | Musculoskeletal | Myalgia |
| | Investigations | Blood CPK increased |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Skin | Rash pruritic |

- **Serious/Notable Effects:** The most frequent cause for treatment discontinuation was increased blood CPK (3.7%). Other common events reported in ≥5% of patients (higher than placebo) include weight gain, arthralgia, muscle injury, and gastroesophageal reflux disease.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Profile:** Elafibranor is a substrate for OATP1B3 and BCRP. Its active metabolite, GFT1007, is a substrate for MRP2 and MRP3.
- **Transporter Interactions:** Elafibranor inhibits OATP1B3 and BCRP in vitro.
- **Clinical Study Results:**
    - **Warfarin:** No significant impact on exposure (AUC/Cmax) or INR.
    - **Statins (Simvastatin/Atorvastatin):** No significant increase in exposure observed. However, caution is advised due to the risk of myopathy (see Section 5).
    - **Sitagliptin & Indomethacin:** No clinically relevant pharmacokinetic interactions observed.
- **Metabolism:** CYP and UGT enzymes do not play a major role in the metabolism of elafibranor; therefore, significant interactions with CYP/UGT modulators are not expected.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Elafibranor is not recommended during pregnancy or in women of childbearing potential not using effective contraception. Animal studies showed fetal loss, malformations, and stillbirths at clinically relevant exposures.
- **Breastfeeding:** Use is not recommended. It is unknown if the drug or its metabolites are excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding should be avoided during treatment and for at least 3 weeks after the last dose.
- **Fertility:** No human data available. Animal studies showed no effects on fertility indices at doses significantly exceeding the human recommended dose.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients under 18 years of age.
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment is required for patients over 65 years of age.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for patients with renal impairment.
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild to Moderate (Child-Pugh A/B):** No dose adjustment required.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Not recommended. Elafibranor and its metabolite show a 3-fold increase in the unbound fraction in severe impairment.
    - **Decompensated Cirrhosis:** Use is not recommended in patients with or developing decompensation (e.g., ascites, variceal bleeding, encephalopathy).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Elafibranor and its active metabolite (GFT1007) are dual PPAR-alpha/delta agonists (with some PPAR-gamma activity in vitro). They regulate bile acid homeostasis by decreasing bile acid synthesis, increasing detoxification, and modulating output, thereby reducing bile toxicity.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak plasma levels occur within 1.25 hours. Steady state is achieved by Day 14. Food intake delays Tmax but does not significantly impact overall AUC.
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (99.7%), primarily to albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolised by 15-ketoprostaglandin 13-Δ reductase (PTGR1) to GFT1007.
    - **Elimination:** Mean half-life is approximately 68.2 hours. Elimination is primarily faecal (77.1%).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ELATIVE Study:** A Phase 3, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (N=161).
- **Primary Endpoint:** Cholestasis response at Week 52 (ALP <1.67 x ULN, TB ≤ ULN, and ALP decrease ≥15%). 
    - **Results:** 51% of the elafibranor group achieved the response vs. 4% in the placebo group (p < 0.0001).
- **Secondary Endpoints:** 15% achieved ALP normalization vs. 0% in placebo. While pruritus scores improved, the change did not reach statistical significance in the Worst Itch NRS.
