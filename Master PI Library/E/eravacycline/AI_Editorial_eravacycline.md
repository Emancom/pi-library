# Editorial Product Information: Eravacycline

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from official product labeling provided by the **United States (FDA)**. Information from other regions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian, Japanese) was not available for comparison in the source documentation provided.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonized editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals to facilitate clinical decision-making. 
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) authorized for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Always consult the local regulatory labeling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Eravacycline (as eravacycline dihydrochloride).
- **Chemical Class:** Tetracycline-class antibacterial; specifically a synthetic fluorocycline.
- **Brand Name:** **Xerava** [US].
- **ATC Code:** J01AA13.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Lyophilized powder for injection in single-dose vials:
    - 50 mg eravacycline (equivalent to 63.5 mg eravacycline dihydrochloride) [US].
    - 100 mg eravacycline (equivalent to 127 mg eravacycline dihydrochloride) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Tetraphase Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Complicated Intra-abdominal Infections (cIAI):** Approved for the treatment of cIAI in patients aged 18 years and older caused by susceptible microorganisms, including:
    - *Aerobic Gram-negative:* **Escherichia coli**, **Klebsiella pneumoniae**, **Citrobacter freundii**, **Enterobacter cloacae**, **Klebsiella oxytoca**.
    - *Aerobic Gram-positive:* **Enterococcus faecalis**, **Enterococcus faecium**, **Staphylococcus aureus**, **Streptococcus anginosus** group.
    - *Anaerobic:* **Clostridium perfringens**, **Bacteroides** species, and **Parabacteroides distasonis**.
- **Limitations of Use:** Eravacycline is **not** indicated for the treatment of complicated urinary tract infections (cUTI).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosage:** 1 mg/kg via intravenous infusion over approximately 60 minutes every 12 hours.
- **Duration of Therapy:** 4 to 14 days, guided by clinical response and the severity/location of infection.
- **Hepatic Impairment Adjustment:**
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** 1 mg/kg every 12 hours on Day 1, followed by 1 mg/kg every 24 hours from Day 2 onwards.
    - **Mild to Moderate (Child-Pugh A/B):** No dosage adjustment required.
- **Concomitant Strong CYP3A Inducer Adjustment:** Increase dose to 1.5 mg/kg every 12 hours.
- **Preparation Instructions:**
    - Reconstitute 50 mg or 100 mg vials with 5 mL Sterile Water for Injection or 0.9% Sodium Chloride.
    - Further dilute in 0.9% Sodium Chloride to a target concentration of 0.3 mg/mL (range 0.2 to 0.6 mg/mL).
    - Do not shake the bag. Swirl gently to avoid foaming.
- **Administration Notes:** Administer through a dedicated line or Y-site. If used sequentially with other drugs, flush the line with 0.9% Sodium Chloride before and after infusion.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to eravacycline, other tetracycline-class antibacterial drugs, or any of the product excipients [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Life-threatening anaphylactic reactions have been reported. Eravacycline is structurally similar to other tetracyclines; avoid use in patients with known class hypersensitivity.
- **Tooth Discoloration and Enamel Hypoplasia:** Use during tooth development (second/third trimester of pregnancy and children up to age 8) may cause permanent yellow-gray-brown discoloration and enamel defects.
- **Inhibition of Bone Growth:** Reversible inhibition of bone growth has been observed with use during the second and third trimesters of pregnancy and in children under age 8.
- ***Clostridioides difficile*-Associated Diarrhea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all systemic antibacterials. Severity may range from mild diarrhea to fatal colitis.
- **Tetracycline-Class Adverse Reactions:** As a member of the class, eravacycline may share risks of photosensitivity, pseudotumor cerebri, and anti-anabolic actions (leading to increased BUN, azotemia, and acidosis).
- **Microbial Overgrowth:** Prolonged use may result in the overgrowth of non-susceptible organisms, including fungi.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Strong CYP3A Inducers (e.g., Rifampin):** Co-administration decreases eravacycline exposure (AUC) by approximately 35%. Increase eravacycline dose to 1.5 mg/kg every 12 hours.
- **Anticoagulants:** Tetracyclines may depress plasma prothrombin activity. Monitor and adjust anticoagulant (e.g., warfarin) dosage as necessary.
- **Strong CYP3A Inhibitors:** Itraconazole increases eravacycline AUC by 32%; however, no dosage adjustment is currently recommended [US].
- **Transporters:** Eravacycline is a substrate for P-gp, OATP1B1, and OATP1B3.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Risk:** Use during the second and third trimesters is discouraged due to risks of permanent tooth discoloration and bone growth inhibition in the fetus.
    - **Animal Data:** Fetal weight loss and skeletal ossification delays were seen in rats and rabbits at exposures ~3 times the clinical AUC.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is not known if eravacycline is excreted in human milk. Because of the potential for serious adverse reactions in the infant, breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for **4 days** after the final dose.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed impaired spermiation and sperm maturation in male rats at high doses; these effects were reversible. No adverse effects on female fertility were observed.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established in patients under 18. Avoid use in children <8 years old due to tooth/bone risks.
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy observed in patients ≥65 years. No age-related pharmacokinetic adjustments required.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is necessary, including for patients with end-stage renal disease (ESRD). Hemodialysis does not significantly remove eravacycline.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for mild-to-moderate (Child-Pugh A and B). Adjustment is required for **Severe (Child-Pugh C)** hepatic impairment (see Section 3).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Fluorocycline antibacterial that disrupts protein synthesis by binding to the 30S ribosomal subunit. It is generally bacteriostatic against Gram-positives and bactericidal against specific strains of *E. coli* and *K. pneumoniae*.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Protein binding 79%–90%. Vd at steady state is ~321 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily mediated by CYP3A4 and FMO-mediated oxidation.
    - **Elimination:** Mean half-life is 20 hours. Excretion is roughly 34% in urine and 47% in feces (as unchanged drug and metabolites).
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The AUC/MIC ratio is the best predictor of antibacterial activity.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **cIAI:** In two Phase 3 trials (Trial 1 vs. ertapenem; Trial 2 vs. meropenem), eravacycline demonstrated clinical cure rates of 86.8% and 90.8%, respectively, meeting non-inferiority criteria against both carbapenem comparators.
- **cUTI:** Two Phase 3 trials (Trial 4 and 5) failed to demonstrate efficacy for the combined endpoints of clinical cure and microbiological success in complicated urinary tract infections.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F). Keep in the original carton to protect from light.
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Stable in the vial for only 1 hour at room temperature.
- **Diluted Infusion Solution:** Must be used within 12 hours if stored at room temperature (≤25°C) or within 8 days if refrigerated (2°C to 8°C). Do not freeze.
