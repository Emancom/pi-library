# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from official Product Information (PI) source documents provided for the following jurisdiction: **United States** (Food and Drug Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing. Practitioners should consult the approved labeling in their specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine [(R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt].
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [United States].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Single-dose vials containing 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [United States].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [United States].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing hemodialysis [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dose:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
- **Administration:** Delivered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Severe Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and never less frequently than every 6 hours (by infusion or IV injection).
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg.
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to starting therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring is advised, including blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels (target free carnitine range: 35–60 µmol/L).

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Titration:** Adjustments may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels below the normal range (40–50 µmol/L).
- **Maintenance:** Downward dose adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be implemented as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, particularly in ESRD patients. Some reactions manifest within minutes of administration. In the event of a severe reaction, discontinue treatment immediately and assess risks before considering re-administration [United States].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In patients with known epilepsy, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures may occur [United States].
- **Oral vs. Intravenous Formulation Note:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) which are normally renally excreted [United States].
- **Monitoring:** ESRD patients should have trough levocarnitine levels monitored regularly.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### General
- **Commonly Observed:** Transient nausea and vomiting. 
- **Less Frequent:** Gastritis and body odor [United States].

### ESRD Population (Clinical Trial Data)
Adverse events reported at a frequency of ≥5% in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials for ESRD patients include:
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), arrhythmia (2%), atrial fibrillation (2%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), abdominal pain (9%).
- **Body as a Whole:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), headache (22%), infection (15%), fever (7%), asthenia (9%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%), hypervolemia (5%).
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), paresthesia (5%), depression (5%).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Clinical Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [United States: Not categorized by letter in provided text]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. However, adequate and well-controlled human studies are lacking. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies in nursing mothers have not been conducted. Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations following levocarnitine administration. Weigh the benefits of maternal supplementation against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [United States].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal reproductive studies.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg body weight; see Section 3 for specific instructions [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for ESRD patients on hemodialysis. Use caution and monitor for metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO) if oral forms are used in this population [United States].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, it alleviates metabolic abnormalities related to carnitine deficiency (malaise, muscle weakness, arrhythmias).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Excretion:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours.
    - **Half-life:** Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg three times weekly), pre-dialysis (trough) levels increased from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L by week 8.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Evaluated 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses. Trough concentrations at 24 weeks were 148, 240, and 456 µmol/L, respectively. 
- **Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the impact on specific clinical outcomes in this population remains to be fully determined [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C.
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL concentration) for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature [United States].
- **Preservative Status:** Does not contain preservatives; discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial.
