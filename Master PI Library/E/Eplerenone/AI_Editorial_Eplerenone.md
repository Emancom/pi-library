# Editorial Product Information: Eplerenone

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised clinical overview of eplerenone, synthesised from available regulatory Product Information to support healthcare professionals in clinical decision-making. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference. It does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Eplerenone; a relatively selective mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist.
- **Brand Names:** APO-Eplerenone [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 25 mg and 50 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Eplerenone is indicated to reduce the risk of:
- **Post-Myocardial Infarction Heart Failure:** Cardiovascular death in patients with evidence of heart failure and left ventricular impairment, initiated 3–14 days following an acute myocardial infarction (MI) [Australian].
- **Chronic Heart Failure (NYHA Class II):** Cardiovascular mortality and morbidity in adult patients with New York Heart Association (NYHA) Class II chronic heart failure and left ventricular systolic dysfunction (LVEF ≤ 30%, or LVEF ≤ 35% with a QRS duration > 130 msec) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Administration:** May be taken with or without food.
- **Post-MI Heart Failure:** Initiation at 25 mg once daily, titrated to a target dose of 50 mg once daily within 4 weeks, as tolerated.
- **NYHA Class II Heart Failure:** For patients with eGFR ≥ 50 mL/min/1.73 m², initiation at 25 mg once daily, titrated to 50 mg once daily within 4 weeks [Australian].

### Serum Potassium-Based Adjustments
Serum potassium must be measured before initiation, during the first week, and at one month after starting or adjusting the dose.
- **K+ < 5.0 mmol/L:** Increase dose (e.g., from 25 mg every other day to 25 mg daily; or 25 mg daily to 50 mg daily).
- **K+ 5.0–5.4 mmol/L:** Maintain current dose.
- **K+ 5.5–5.9 mmol/L:** Decrease dose (e.g., from 50 mg daily to 25 mg daily; or 25 mg daily to 25 mg every other day).
- **K+ ≥ 6.0 mmol/L:** Withhold therapy.
- **Restarting:** Therapy may be resumed at 25 mg every other day once potassium falls below 5.0 mmol/L [Australian].

### Missed Dose
Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to eplerenone or any tablet excipients.
- Clinically significant hyperkalaemia (serum potassium > 5.0 mmol/L at initiation).
- Severe renal insufficiency (eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m²).
- Severe hepatic insufficiency (Child-Pugh Class C).
- Concomitant use of potassium-sparing diuretics (e.g., spironolactone, amiloride).
- Concomitant use of strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, itraconazole, ritonavir, clarithromycin) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hyperkalaemia:** The primary risk associated with eplerenone. It can cause serious, potentially fatal arrhythmias. Risk is higher in patients with renal impairment, diabetes (particularly with proteinuria), and the elderly.
- **Monitoring:** Periodic monitoring of serum potassium and renal function (eGFR) is mandatory.
- **Combination Therapy:** The triple combination of eplerenone, an ACE inhibitor, and an angiotensin receptor blocker (ARB) is not recommended due to the increased risk of hyperkalaemia [Australian].
- **Excipients:** Contains lactose monohydrate; caution in patients with rare hereditary galactose intolerance or glucose-galactose malabsorption [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequencies (based on heart failure trials)
- **Common (1–10%):** Hyperkalaemia, dizziness, syncope, hypotension, cough, diarrhoea, nausea, constipation, pruritus, muscle spasms, musculoskeletal pain, and renal impairment.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Eosinophilia, hypothyroidism, dehydration, hypercholesterolaemia, hypertriglyceridaemia, hyponatraemia, insomnia, headache, hypoesthesia, atrial fibrillation, left ventricular failure, tachycardia, orthostatic hypotension, arterial thrombosis of the limb, pharyngitis, pyelonephritis, flatulence, vomiting, cholecystitis, gynaecomastia (males), abnormal vaginal bleeding (females), asthenia, malaise, and increased blood urea/creatinine [Australian].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Hyperkalaemia-induced arrhythmias.
- Angioedema and rash (reported post-marketing) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Eplerenone is a CYP3A4 substrate. 
    - **Strong inhibitors:** Contraindicated (e.g., ketoconazole 5.4-fold AUC increase).
    - **Mild-to-moderate inhibitors:** Dose should not exceed 25 mg daily (e.g., erythromycin, verapamil, fluconazole, amiodarone).
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** St John’s Wort, rifampicin, and phenytoin decrease eplerenone exposure; concomitant use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Lithium:** Interaction studies are lacking, but toxicity has been reported with other diuretics. Avoid co-administration; if necessary, monitor lithium levels frequently.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Potassium-Raising Agents:** ACE inhibitors, ARBs, trimethoprim, cyclosporin, and tacrolimus increase hyperkalaemia risk.
- **NSAIDs:** May reduce antihypertensive effects and lead to severe hyperkalaemia in renally impaired patients.
- **Antihypertensives:** Alpha-1-blockers, tricyclic antidepressants, and neuroleptics may increase the risk of hypotension/postural hypotension [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B3.** No adequate data in pregnant women. Animal studies showed no teratogenicity but noted decreased foetal weights at high doses (40x human exposure). Use only if benefit outweighs risk.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if eplerenone is excreted in human milk. Because rat studies show presence in milk and decreased pup weight gain, a decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed slightly decreased fertility in male rats at very high doses (24x clinical AUC). Reversible prostate atrophy was noted in dogs [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established; use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No initial dose adjustment required. However, due to age-related declines in renal function, the risk of hyperkalaemia is increased. Older patients may also be more sensitive to blood pressure-lowering effects [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - eGFR ≥ 50: Standard dosing.
    - eGFR 30–49: Start at 25 mg every other day; maximum dose 25 mg daily.
    - eGFR < 30: Contraindicated [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - Mild-to-moderate (Child-Pugh A/B): No initial dose adjustment required; monitor potassium frequently.
    - Severe (Child-Pugh C): Contraindicated [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selective mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist. It prevents the binding of aldosterone, thereby inhibiting the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) involved in blood pressure regulation and cardiovascular disease pathophysiology.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Produces sustained increases in plasma renin and serum aldosterone due to loss of negative feedback. It reduces vascular inflammation and remodelling independent of blood pressure lowering.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak plasma levels reached in ~1.5 hours. Bioavailability is 69%.
    - **Distribution:** ~50% protein bound (primarily to alpha-1-acid glycoproteins).
    - **Metabolism:** Predominantly CYP3A4 mediated; no active metabolites.
    - **Elimination:** Half-life of 3–5 hours. Excreted via urine (67%) and faeces (32%) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **EPHESUS Trial:** In 6,632 patients post-MI with LVEF ≤ 40%, eplerenone significantly reduced the risk of all-cause mortality (15% reduction; HR 0.85) and the composite of cardiovascular death or cardiovascular hospitalisation when added to standard therapy.
- **EMPHASIS-HF Trial:** In 2,737 patients with NYHA Class II heart failure and LVEF ≤ 35%, eplerenone significantly reduced the primary endpoint of cardiovascular death or heart failure hospitalisation (37% reduction; HR 0.63) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to expiry date on packaging [Australian].
