# Editorial Product Information: etonogestrel + ethinyl

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **etonogestrel + ethinyl**
- Chemical class: Combination estrogen–progestin hormonal contraceptive (etonogestrel = progestin; ethinyl = ethinyl estradiol, an estrogen)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Vaginal (vaginal ring delivery)

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 2001
- Last revision/update of available PI: Revised: 7/2026 [US]
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources
- Registration/approval dates for other jurisdictions: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Contraception: Use by females of reproductive age to prevent pregnancy.
  - [US-approved] indicated as an estrogen/progestin combination hormonal contraceptive for prevention of pregnancy.
- Other indications: Not specified in available sources

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- One vaginal ring is inserted and left in place for three consecutive weeks, followed by a one‑week ring‑free interval. After the one‑week ring‑free interval, a new ring is inserted.

### Product release specification
- Each ring contains 11.7 mg etonogestrel and 2.7 mg ethinyl estradiol and releases on average approximately 0.120 mg/day etonogestrel and 0.015 mg/day ethinyl estradiol during a three‑week use period.

### Administration instructions
- Insert the ring into the vagina; exact position within the vagina is not critical to efficacy.
- The ring may be inserted by the user using manual compression; an applicator may be available as an optional alternative [US].
- Remove by hooking a finger under the rim or grasping the rim and pulling it out.
- After removal, place the used ring in its sachet/foil pouch and discard in household waste out of reach of children and pets (do not flush). [Note: specific disposal guidance by jurisdiction or manufacturer not reproduced here.]

### How to start
- No hormonal contraceptive use in the preceding cycle: Insert on Day 1 of menstrual bleeding. If started on Days 2–5, advise the patient to use an additional barrier method (e.g., male condom with spermicide) for the first 7 days of use.
- Switching from another combined hormonal contraceptive (CHC): May switch on any day, but at the latest on the day after the usual hormone‑free interval, provided consistent and correct prior use or reasonable certainty of not being pregnant.
- Switching from a progestin‑only method (POP, implant, injection, IUS): 
  - From POP: start the day after the last POP.
  - From implant or IUS: start on the day of removal.
  - From injectable: start on the day the next injection would be due.
  - In these switches, advise use of an additional barrier method for 7 days.
- After abortion/miscarriage:
  - Within first 5 days following a complete first‑trimester abortion/miscarriage: may start without additional contraception.
  - If not started within 5 days after a first‑trimester event, follow the “No hormonal contraceptive use” instructions and use non‑hormonal contraception until then.
  - Start no earlier than 4 weeks after a second‑trimester abortion/miscarriage because of increased thromboembolism risk.
- Postpartum:
  - Start no earlier than 4 weeks postpartum in women who are not breastfeeding (due to elevated postpartum thromboembolism risk). If started earlier, counsel regarding thromboembolism risk.
  - Advise breastfeeding women not to use this product and to use alternate contraception until weaned.
  - If initiated postpartum, use an additional barrier method for the first 7 days after insertion if the woman has not yet had a period; consider the possibility of ovulation and conception prior to initiation.

### Deviations from recommended regimen / missed‑use guidance
- General: Counsel users to check ring presence (e.g., before/after intercourse). Do not deviate from the 3‑weeks‑in / 1‑week‑out schedule to maintain contraceptive efficacy.
- Inadvertent removal or expulsion:
  - If the ring is out of the vagina for less than 3 continuous hours: rinse with cool to lukewarm (not hot) water and reinsert as soon as possible (efficacy not reduced).
  - If the ring is lost: insert a new ring and continue regimen without schedule change.
  - If the ring was out of the vagina for more than 3 continuous hours:
    - During Weeks 1–2: reinsert as soon as remembered; use a barrier method (e.g., male condom with spermicide) until the ring has been used continuously for 7 days.
    - During Week 3: discard the ring and either
      1) insert a new ring immediately (this starts the next 3‑week use period; withdrawal bleeding from the previous cycle may not occur), or
      2) insert a new ring no later than 7 days after removal/expulsion (choose this only if the ring was used continuously for at least 7 days prior to removal). In both options, use a barrier method until the new ring has been used continuously for 7 days.
  - If out‑of‑vagina time is unknown: consider pregnancy and perform a pregnancy test prior to inserting a new ring.
- Prolonged ring‑free interval:
  - If the ring‑free interval is longer than 1 week, consider pregnancy and require back‑up contraception until the ring has been used continuously for 7 days.
- Prolonged continuous ring use:
  - If the ring is left in place for up to one extra week (i.e., up to 4 weeks total), contraceptive protection is maintained; remove and observe a one‑week ring‑free interval before inserting a new ring.
  - If left in place longer than 4 weeks, remove the ring, rule out pregnancy, and if pregnancy is excluded, restart and use back‑up contraception until the ring has been used continuously for 7 days.
- Ring breakage:
  - Reported cases of ring disconnecting at the weld joint; if discovered, discard and replace with a new ring. Vaginal discomfort, expulsion, or vaginal injury have been reported in association with breakage.

### Use with other vaginal products
- The ring may interfere with proper placement of certain female barrier methods (diaphragm, cervical cap, female condom); these methods are not recommended as back‑up methods for ring users.
- Pharmacokinetic data indicate tampons do not affect systemic hormone absorption.

## 5. Contraindications
Etonogestrel + ethinyl is contraindicated in females with any of the following conditions:
- Cigarette smoking in women older than 35 years (do not use) — **bold** critical warning in boxed warnings section below.
- Current or history of venous thromboembolism (deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism)
- Known thrombogenic valvular or rhythm disease of the heart (e.g., subacute bacterial endocarditis with valvular disease, atrial fibrillation)
- Known thrombophilic disorders (inherited or acquired hypercoagulopathies)
- Cerebrovascular disease (history of stroke, transient ischemic attack)
- Coronary artery disease or history of myocardial infarction
- Uncontrolled hypertension
- Diabetes mellitus with vascular disease
- Migraine headaches with focal neurological symptoms or aura
- Women over age 35 with any migraine headaches
- Current diagnosis or history of breast cancer (which may be hormonally sensitive)
- Known or suspected pregnancy
- Liver tumors (benign or malignant) or active liver disease
- Undiagnosed abnormal uterine bleeding
- Hypersensitivity (including anaphylaxis, angioedema) to any component of the product
- Co‑administration with hepatitis C treatment combinations containing ombitasvir/paritaprevir/ritonavir, with or without dasabuvir (contraindicated due to risk of clinically significant ALT elevations)
- Any other condition listed as a contraindication in local jurisdictional labeling: Not specified in available sources

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warning
- **CIGARETTE SMOKING AND SERIOUS CARDIOVASCULAR EVENTS** — **Cigarette smoking increases the risk of serious cardiovascular events from combination hormonal contraceptive use; risk increases with age and number of cigarettes smoked. For this reason, CHCs, including etonogestrel + ethinyl vaginal ring, should not be used by women who are over 35 years of age and smoke.** [US]

### General warnings and precautions
- Thromboembolic disorders and other vascular problems:
  - Stop use if an arterial thrombotic or venous thromboembolic event occurs.
  - Stop at least 4 weeks before and through 2 weeks after major surgery or other surgeries known to increase thromboembolic risk, when feasible.
  - Start no earlier than 4 weeks after delivery in non‑breastfeeding women.
  - Evaluate immediately for retinal vein thrombosis if unexplained loss of vision, proptosis, diplopia, papilledema, or retinal vascular lesions occur.
  - Known VTE risk factors (smoking, obesity, family history of VTE, age, etc.) increase risk; consider these when prescribing.
  - Epidemiologic data indicate VTE risk with etonogestrel + ethinyl is similar to that observed with combined oral contraceptives in studied populations; see Clinical Trials (Section 12) for study data.
- Toxic Shock Syndrome (TSS):
  - Consider TSS when users present with compatible signs/symptoms; evaluate and treat appropriately.
- Liver disease:
  - Discontinue if jaundice develops.
  - Contraindicated in known liver tumors or active liver disease.
- Risk of liver enzyme elevations with concomitant hepatitis C combination therapy containing ombitasvir/paritaprevir/ritonavir ± dasabuvir: concurrent use is contraindicated due to ALT elevations.
- High blood pressure:
  - Monitor blood pressure in women with controlled hypertension; discontinue if blood pressure rises significantly.
- Hypersensitivity reactions:
  - Monitor for and discontinue for severe hypersensitivity including angioedema or anaphylaxis.
- Vaginal use considerations:
  - Monitor for device‑related events (expulsion, discomfort, foreign body sensation), vaginal infection, vaginal discomfort.
  - Rare reports of inadvertent urinary bladder insertion have been described; evaluate if unusual symptoms occur.
- Gallbladder disease:
  - Use with caution in women with a history of gallbladder disease.
- Carbohydrate and lipid metabolic effects:
  - Monitor women with diabetes mellitus (including prediabetic conditions). Consider alternate contraception for uncontrolled dyslipidemia.
- Headache:
  - Evaluate significant changes in headache pattern; discontinue if indicated.
- Bleeding irregularities and amenorrhea:
  - Evaluate persistent irregular bleeding or amenorrhea.
- Depression:
  - Monitor for new or worsening mood changes, including depression; discontinue if clinically indicated.
- Malignant neoplasms:
  - Use with caution in women with a current or past history of hormonally sensitive neoplasms (e.g., breast cancer); contraindicated in current or recent diagnosis.
- Effect on binding globulins, hereditary angioedema, chloasma, other: listed as considerations in labeling; follow local guidance.

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and periodic assessment as clinically indicated:
  - Blood pressure monitoring
  - Assess for signs/symptoms of thromboembolism
  - Monitor liver function if clinically indicated (e.g., in presence of hepatic disease or concomitant hepatotoxic drugs)
  - Monitor glucose and lipid profile in diabetic or dyslipidemic patients
  - Monitor mood and mental health
  - Evaluate abnormal uterine bleeding per standard gynecologic practice

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Enzyme inducers: Drugs or herbal products that induce certain hepatic enzymes (notably CYP3A4) may decrease systemic hormone levels and reduce contraceptive effectiveness and/or increase breakthrough bleeding. Counsel patients to use a non‑hormonal backup or alternative contraception when enzyme inducers are co‑administered.
  - Examples not exhaustively listed in available sources; specific interacting inducers and management strategies: Not specified in available sources.
- Hepatitis C combination therapy:
  - Co‑administration with regimens containing ombitasvir/paritaprevir/ritonavir, with or without dasabuvir, is contraindicated because of risk of clinically significant ALT elevations.
- Effects of the ring on the pharmacokinetics of other drugs and interference with laboratory tests: Not specified in available sources (sections referenced in PI but details not provided).

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Additive vascular risk with other agents or conditions that predispose to thrombosis: use caution and assess risk factors when combining therapies or in patients with comorbidities that increase thrombotic risk.
- Interactions affecting lactation: estrogens may reduce milk production; avoid use in breastfeeding women if possible.

### Clinically significant interactions — management notes
- When enzyme inducers are needed, advise use of back‑up or alternative contraception during induction and for a specified period after discontinuation per clinical judgment (exact durations not specified in available sources).
- Do not co‑administer with the specified hepatitis C regimens (ombitasvir/paritaprevir/ritonavir ± dasabuvir).
- Monitor liver enzymes if clinically indicated; discontinue for clinically significant hepatic impairment or jaundice.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Contraindicated in pregnancy; there is no indication for use of combination hormonal contraceptives during pregnancy.
- If pregnancy is suspected (e.g., missed periods after nonadherence), discontinue and evaluate.
- Pregnancy category systems by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources for systems other than contraindication statements.

### Breastfeeding
- Not recommended for breastfeeding women; may reduce milk production.
- Recommended to avoid use while breastfeeding and to use other contraception until the child is weaned.
- Initiation postpartum: start no earlier than 4 weeks postpartum in non‑breastfeeding women; breastfeeding women should defer use until weaning.

## 12. Clinical Trials
### Key epidemiologic and safety studies related to thromboembolic risk
- Transatlantic Active Surveillance on Cardiovascular Safety (TASC):
  - Design: Large prospective, observational study including initiators (new users), switchers, and restarters; follow‑up 24–48 months in populations representative of routine clinical users.
  - Findings: VTE incidence among etonogestrel + ethinyl vaginal ring users was 8.3 per 10,000 woman‑years (WY); among users of combined oral contraceptives (COCs) overall 9.2 per 10,000 WY. Adjusted hazard ratio vs all COCs: 0.8 (95% CI 0.5–1.5). Hazard ratio vs COCs excluding desogestrel/gestodene‑containing OCs: 0.8 (95% CI 0.4–1.7).
- Retrospective cohort study (FDA‑funded; Kaiser Permanente and Medicaid databases):
  - Design: Retrospective cohort of first‑time CHC users during study period across 4 US health plans.
  - Findings: VTE incidence for new users of etonogestrel + ethinyl ring: 11.4 events per 10,000 WY; for new users of a levonorgestrel (LNG)‑containing COC: 9.2 per 10,000 WY; for users of other COCs available during the study: 8.2 per 10,000 WY. Adjusted hazard ratios: versus all COCs HR 1.1 (95% CI 0.6–2.2); versus LNG/0.03 mg ethinyl estradiol HR 1.0 (95% CI 0.5–2.0). (See PI for detailed methodology and adjustments.)
- General statement: Epidemiologic evidence indicates an increased absolute VTE risk associated with CHC use; comparative incidence in the cited studies showed similar or modestly different rates versus COCs, with confidence intervals crossing unity in adjusted analyses.

## 16. Patient Counselling Information
- Advise patients on correct use: insert one ring for 3 weeks, remove for 1 week, then insert a new ring on the same day of the week as prior insertion.
- Counsel regarding the increased risk of serious cardiovascular events with cigarette smoking, especially in women >35 years — **do not use in women >35 who smoke**.
- Counsel on signs and symptoms of thrombosis (leg pain/swelling, chest pain, sudden shortness of breath, visual changes), and advise to seek immediate care if these occur.
- Advise on what to do if the ring is expelled or removed (see Section 4 Dosage and Administration).
- Counsel on potential adverse effects: vaginal symptoms, headaches, mood changes (including depression), nausea, breast tenderness, changes in bleeding patterns, decreased milk production if breastfeeding.
- Advise breastfeeding women to use alternative contraception until weaning.
- Inform patients that certain hepatitis C combination therapies (ombitasvir/paritaprevir/ritonavir ± dasabuvir) should not be used concurrently due to risk of ALT elevations.
- Advise use of back‑up contraception when taking concomitant enzyme‑inducing drugs/herbal products and during periods of potential reduced efficacy per clinical guidance.
