# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (Food and Drug Administration)** via Baxter Healthcare Corporation. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport; (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metabolic Disorders:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
- **Titration/Frequency:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often given, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours. 
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest administered dose reported is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### General Instructions
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine levels before starting parenteral therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is advised [US].
- **Preparation:** Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration before use.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and monitor for recurrence if re-administration is attempted [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine in patients with renal insufficiency have not been evaluated. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].
- **Monitoring:** Monitor International Normalized Ratio (INR) in patients receiving concomitant warfarin [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in INR when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified by letter, but animal data provided]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. However, adequate human studies are lacking. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after exogenous administration. Weigh the benefits to the mother against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No mutagenic effects were found in standard tests. Long-term animal carcinogenicity studies have not been conducted [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Refer to the "Metabolic Disorders" dosing section; specific paediatric age-range approvals were not detailed beyond weight-based dosing [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. High-dose oral levocarnitine is cautioned against in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Carnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following IV administration (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (20 mg/kg post-dialysis), mean pre-dialysis carnitine levels increased from 21.3 µmol/L at baseline to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While the increase in concentrations is proven, the definitive effect on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been fully determined [US].
- **Bioavailability:** Absolute bioavailability of oral formulations (tablets and solution) is approximately 15% [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservative.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) [US].
