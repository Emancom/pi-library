# Editorial Product Information: Epoetin alfa

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of epoetin alfa (recombinant human erythropoietin) for healthcare professionals, synthesising data from available regulatory Product Information (PI) documents.

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Renal Failure:** Treatment of symptomatic or transfusion-requiring anaemia associated with chronic renal failure. [Australian]
- **Chemotherapy-Induced Anaemia:** Treatment of anaemia in patients with non-myeloid malignancies where anaemia results from concomitantly administered chemotherapy and blood transfusion is not considered appropriate. [Australian]
- **Elective Surgery:** Treatment of adult patients with mild-to-moderate anaemia (Hb > 100 to ≤ 130 g/L) scheduled for elective surgery with an expected moderate blood loss (2–4 units) to reduce allogeneic blood transfusion and facilitate erythropoietic recovery. [Australian]
- **Autologous Blood Donation:** To augment autologous blood collection and limit the decline in haemoglobin in anaemic adult patients scheduled for major elective surgery who cannot pre-deposit their complete peri-operative blood needs. [Australian]

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing Principles
Doses must be individualised to maintain haemoglobin (Hb) at an appropriate level for the patient, generally not exceeding 120 g/L. Haematological parameters must be monitored regularly.

### Indication-Specific Dosing [Australian]
- **Chronic Renal Failure:**
    - **Correction Phase:** Initial dose is 50 IU/kg three times weekly (IV/SC). If Hb increase is < 10 g/L after one month, the dose may be increased by 25 IU/kg increments at monthly intervals.
    - **Maintenance Phase:** Generally 75 to 300 IU/kg per week.
- **Cancer Patients:**
    - Treatment starts when Hb falls below 100–110 g/L. 
    - Initial dose: 150 IU/kg SC three times weekly.
    - If response is inadequate after 4 weeks (Hb increase < 10 g/L), increase to 300 IU/kg SC three times weekly. Discontinue if response remains inadequate after a further 4 weeks.
- **Elective Surgery:** 
    - 600 IU/kg SC weekly for three weeks prior to surgery and on the day of surgery.
    - Alternative (short lead time): 300 IU/kg SC daily for 10 days before surgery, on the day of, and 4 days after.
- **Autologous Pre-donation:** 300–600 IU/kg IV twice weekly for three weeks.

### Administration Instructions
- **IV Administration:** Over 1–2 minutes. For dialysis patients, administer after the procedure.
- **SC Administration:** Maximum volume of 1 mL per injection site. 
- **Handling:** Do not shake; do not dilute or mix with other liquids. 

## 4. Contraindications
- Uncontrolled hypertension. [Australian]
- Known hypersensitivity to the active substance, excipients, or mammalian cell-derived products. [Australian]
- Patients scheduled for elective surgery who have severe coronary, peripheral arterial, carotid, or cerebral vascular disease (including recent MI or CVA). [Australian]
- Surgery patients unable to receive adequate antithrombotic prophylaxis. [Australian]
- Development of Pure Red Cell Aplasia (PRCA) following treatment with any erythropoietin. [Australian]

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Increased Mortality/Cancer Risk:** Use of ESAs in cancer patients is associated with increased mortality and potential tumour stimulation. Hb levels should not exceed 120 g/L. [Australian Boxed-style Warning]
- **Cardiovascular and Thrombotic Events:** Increased incidence of TVEs, including MI, CVA, DVT, and pulmonary emboli. Risks are higher with Hb > 120 g/L. [Australian]
- **Pure Red Cell Aplasia (PRCA):** Antibody-mediated PRCA has been reported, primarily with SC administration in chronic renal failure. If PRCA is diagnosed, therapy must be stopped immediately and the patient should not switch to other ESAs. [Australian]
- **Hypertension:** Blood pressure must be controlled prior to and during therapy. Hypertensive crisis with encephalopathy and seizures can occur. [Australian]
- **Severe Cutaneous Reactions:** Discontinue immediately if Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS) or Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN) is suspected. [Australian]
- **Latex Sensitivity:** The needle cover contains dry natural rubber, which may cause allergic reactions. [Australian]

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Reactions (Clinical Trials)
| System/Organ Class | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting |
| **General** | Pyrexia, chills, influenza-like illness, injection site reaction |
| **Nervous System** | Headache, convulsions |
| **Vascular** | Hypertension, thrombotic vascular events |
| **Musculoskeletal** | Arthralgia, bone pain, myalgia |

### Frequency Tiers [Australian Postmarketing]
- **Very Common (>10%):** Hypertension, nausea, diarrhoea, vomiting, pyrexia, headache.
- **Common (1–10%):** Cough, rash, arthralgia, bone pain, myalgia, chills, TVEs (e.g., DVT, embolism).
- **Very Rare (<0.01%):** Antibody-mediated PRCA, thrombocythaemia.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Cyclosporin:** Binds to red blood cells; monitoring of cyclosporin levels and dose adjustment may be required as haematocrit rises. [Australian]
- **Haematinics:** The effect of epoetin alfa may be potentiated by iron supplementation in deficiency states. [Australian]
- **Trastuzumab:** Subcutaneous epoetin alfa (40,000 IU/mL) does not affect the pharmacokinetics of trastuzumab (6 mg/kg). [Australian]

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category B3 [Australian]:** Administer only if clearly needed and the potential benefit justifies the risk to the foetus. Animal studies show decreased fertility and foetal weight at high IV doses.
- **Breastfeeding:** Use only if clearly needed. It is unknown if epoetin alfa is excreted in human milk. [Australian]

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Available data supports a dose of 25 IU/kg three times weekly for anaemia correction. Long-term effects on growth and quality of life are unproven. [Australian]
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution due to decreased metabolism, which may increase erythropoiesis. [Australian]
- **Elderly:** No specific data available in provided sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** See Section 3 for specific dosing in chronic renal failure.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Recombinant human erythropoietin (EPO) stimulates RBC production by binding to erythropoietin receptors on erythroid progenitor cells, activating signal transduction and stimulating proliferation. [Australian]
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **IV Half-life:** 4.0–6.1 hours in healthy volunteers; 6.5–9.3 hours in renal failure patients.
    - **SC Bioavailability:** ~20-30% of IV; peak levels reached 12–18 hours post-dose.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 10% excreted via kidneys. [Australian]

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Chemotherapy Anaemia:** Trials (N=131) showed significant haematocrit increase (p<0.008) and reduction in blood transfusion needs (22% vs 43% in placebo) over 12 weeks. [Australian]
- **Metastatic Breast Cancer:** A study of 2098 women showed a median progression-free survival of 7.4 months; epoetin alfa did not meet non-inferiority criteria regarding tumour progression compared to standard of care. [Australian]
- **Elective Surgery:** Trials (N=316) showed that 300 IU/kg epoetin alfa reduced allogeneic transfusion risk to 16% (vs 45% in placebo). [Australian]

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate). [Australian]
- **Handling:** Do not freeze or shake. Protect from light.
- **Temporary Storage:** When in use, the product may be stored at room temperature (below 25°C) for a maximum single period of seven days. [Australian]
