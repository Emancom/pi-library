# Editorial Product Information: Etodolac (Extended-Release)

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: US (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of etodolac extended-release tablets for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesizes clinical and regulatory information to facilitate safe and effective prescribing.
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this writing, only the United States (FDA) documentation was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Etodolac (a member of the pyranocarboxylic acid group of nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs).
- **Chemical Class**: Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID).
- **Brand Names**: Not specified in available sources; identified by the generic name **Etodolac Extended-Release Tablets** [US].
- **ATC Code**: Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**: 
    - Extended-release tablets: 400 mg, 500 mg, and 600 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: TaSun Pharmaceutical Industries, Inc.; Distributed by Taro Pharmaceuticals U.S.A., Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Osteoarthritis**: Management of signs and symptoms of osteoarthritis [US].
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis**: Management of signs and symptoms of rheumatoid arthritis [US].
- **Juvenile Rheumatoid Arthritis**: Relief of signs and symptoms of juvenile rheumatoid arthritis in patients aged 6 to 16 years [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Osteoarthritis and Rheumatoid Arthritis**: The recommended starting dose is 400 mg to 1000 mg taken orally once daily [US].
- **Dose Optimization**: Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration consistent with individual patient goals. Dosage should be adjusted based on clinical response [US].

### Dosing by Indication (Pediatric)
- **Juvenile Rheumatoid Arthritis (Ages 6–16)**: Dosing is weight-based, administered once daily [US]:
    - 20 to 30 kg: 400 mg (1 x 400 mg tablet)
    - 31 to 45 kg: 600 mg (1 x 600 mg tablet)
    - 46 to 60 kg: 800 mg (2 x 400 mg tablets)
    - > 60 kg: 1000 mg (2 x 500 mg tablets)

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets must be swallowed whole; they should not be crushed or chewed.
- Efficacy for chronic conditions is typically observed within one to two weeks [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity**: Known hypersensitivity to **etodolac** or any components of the formulation [US].
- **Aspirin Sensitivity**: History of asthma, urticaria, or allergic-type reactions after taking aspirin or other NSAIDs. Severe, rarely fatal, anaphylactic-like reactions have been reported in such patients [US].
- **Surgical Setting**: Contraindicated for the treatment of peri-operative pain in the setting of coronary artery bypass graft (CABG) surgery [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
- **Cardiovascular Thrombotic Events**: NSAIDs cause an increased risk of serious cardiovascular (CV) thrombotic events, including myocardial infarction and stroke, which can be fatal. This risk may occur early in treatment and increase with duration of use.
- **Gastrointestinal (GI) Risk**: NSAIDs cause an increased risk of serious GI adverse events including bleeding, ulceration, and perforation of the stomach or intestines, which can be fatal. These can occur at any time without warning. Elderly patients are at greater risk.

### General Warnings
- **Hepatotoxicity**: Borderline elevations of liver tests may occur in up to 15% of patients. Notable elevations (3x ULN) occur in ~1%. Discontinue if clinical signs of liver disease develop [US].
- **Hypertension**: NSAIDs can lead to the onset of new hypertension or worsening of pre-existing hypertension [US].
- **Heart Failure and Edema**: Fluid retention and edema have been observed; use with caution in patients with severe heart failure [US].
- **Renal Effects**: Long-term use may result in renal papillary necrosis. Use is not recommended in patients with advanced renal disease [US].
- **Anaphylactoid Reactions**: May occur even without prior exposure to **etodolac** [US].
- **Serious Skin Reactions**: May cause exfoliative dermatitis, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN). Discontinue at the first appearance of skin rash [US].
- **Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS)**: Reported with NSAID use; discontinue if suspected [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- Check hemoglobin or hematocrit in patients on long-term treatment if signs of anemia appear.
- Monitor blood pressure closely during initiation and throughout therapy.
- Monitor renal function in patients with heart failure, liver dysfunction, or those taking diuretics/ACE inhibitors [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common (1% to 10%) [US]
- **Gastrointestinal**: Abdominal pain, constipation, diarrhea, dyspepsia, flatulence, nausea, vomiting, GI ulcers (gastric/duodenal).
- **Body as a whole**: Asthenia, infection, edema.
- **Nervous System**: Dizziness, headaches.
- **Respiratory**: Pharyngitis, rhinitis.
- **Skin**: Pruritus, rashes.
- **Other**: Blurred vision, tinnitus, dysuria, hypertension.

### Uncommon/Rare (< 1%) [US]
- **Serious GI**: Gross bleeding/perforation.
- **Hematologic**: Anemia, increased bleeding time, agranulocytosis, hemolytic anemia, leukopenia, neutropenia, pancytopenia, thrombocytopenia.
- **Hepatic**: Elevated liver enzymes, hepatitis, jaundice, liver failure.
- **Renal**: Abnormal renal function, renal failure, renal insufficiency, renal papillary necrosis.
- **Allergic**: Anaphylactic/anaphylactoid reactions, angioedema.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **ACE Inhibitors/ARBs**: NSAIDs may diminish the antihypertensive effect of these agents [US].
- **Aspirin**: Concomitant use increases GI risk. **Etodolac** protein binding is reduced when given with aspirin [US].
- **Diuretics (Furosemide/Thiazides)**: Reduced natriuretic effect due to inhibition of renal prostaglandin synthesis [US].
- **Lithium**: Increased plasma lithium levels and reduced renal lithium clearance [US].
- **Methotrexate**: Potential for increased methotrexate toxicity [US].
- **Warfarin**: Synergistic effects on GI bleeding; use with extreme caution [US].
- **Cyclosporine/Digoxin**: Increased serum levels and potential for enhanced nephrotoxicity [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **General**: No adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women [US].
    - **Fetal Toxicity (≥20 Weeks)**: Use of NSAIDs at ~20 weeks gestation or later may cause fetal renal dysfunction leading to oligohydramnios [US].
    - **Fetal Toxicity (≥30 Weeks)**: Avoid use; risk of premature closure of the fetal ductus arteriosus [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: It is not known if **etodolac** is excreted in human milk. A decision should be made whether to discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the mother's need for the medication [US].
- **Fertility**: Based on animal data, may delay or prevent rupture of ovarian follicles (reversible upon discontinuation) [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Pediatric**: Safety and effectiveness in children < 6 years not established. Approved for JRA in patients 6–16 years [US].
- **Geriatric**: Age does not significantly alter pharmacokinetics, but the elderly may be more sensitive to GI and renal side effects. Exercise caution [US].
- **Renal Impairment**: PK of extended-release not studied in renal insufficiency. Use caution in severe deficiency; not significantly removed by hemodialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Clearance is dependent on liver function and may be reduced in severe failure [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Inhibition of prostaglandin synthetase, leading to anti-inflammatory, analgesic, and antipyretic activities [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Bioavailability > 80%. Tmax for extended-release is ~6.7 hours [US].
    - **Distribution**: > 99% protein bound (primarily to albumin) [US].
    - **Metabolism**: Extensive hepatic metabolism; metabolites do not contribute significantly to pharmacological activity [US].
    - **Elimination**: Renal excretion (72% of dose). Terminal half-life is ~8.4 hours for extended-release [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Arthritis Trials**: Double-blind, randomized trials in 1552 patients demonstrated that once-daily extended-release tablets provided efficacy comparable to immediate-release **etodolac** for OA and RA [US].
- **JRA Trial**: An open-label, 12-week study in 72 patients (ages 6–16) established safety and efficacy at weight-based doses up to 20 mg/kg [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F) [US].
- **Conditions**: Protect from excessive heat and humidity. Dispense in tight, light-resistant containers [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States**: Rx-only [US].
- **Other Jurisdictions**: Not specified in available sources.
