# Editorial Product Information: estradiol + dydrogesterone

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **estradiol + dydrogesterone**
- Chemical class: Estrogen (estradiol) combined with a progestogen (dydrogesterone)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources

- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Hormone replacement therapy for estrogen deficiency associated with natural or surgically-induced menopause in women with an intact uterus.
- Prevention of postmenopausal bone mineral density loss in women. When used solely for prevention of osteoporosis, restrict use to women at high risk of fracture who are intolerant of, or contraindicated for, non-estrogen products; discuss lifestyle measures and alternative options with the patient before prescribing.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
General dosing (adult)
- Typical regimen described in available sources: one oral tablet daily containing estradiol 1 mg plus dydrogesterone 5 mg. [If other regional strengths or regimens exist, they are not specified in available sources.]

Dosing by indication
- Menopausal symptom relief (women with intact uterus): continuous daily combined therapy as above.
- Prevention of postmenopausal bone mineral density loss: same continuous regimen; protection exists while treatment continues. Reassess benefit–risk for treatments beyond 5–10 years.

Administration instructions
- Take one tablet orally once daily without a tablet-free interval; when a pack is finished, start the next pack immediately.
- Women with natural menopause should generally commence therapy 12 months after their last menstrual bleed. For surgically-induced menopause, therapy may be initiated immediately.
- Changing from sequential HRT: switch at the end of the estrogen-plus-progestogen phase of the sequential regimen, without a tablet-free interval.

Titration and special initiation notes
- FEMOSTON-CONTI (regulated formulation reference—strengths noted above) is intended to prevent endometrial stimulation in non-hysterectomised women; unpredictable bleeding patterns may occur if endogenous estrogen remains (e.g., recent sequential HRT or prior combined oral contraceptive use).
- Normally intended for women >12 months post-menopause; if menopausal status is uncertain, counsel regarding possible irregular bleeding.

Missed dose instructions
- If a dose is remembered within 12 hours, take it as soon as possible. If more than 12 hours have elapsed, omit the missed tablet and resume the next scheduled tablet; do not double-dose. Missed doses may increase the likelihood of breakthrough bleeding or spotting.

## 5. Contraindications
The following are contraindications to use of **estradiol + dydrogesterone** (all items are from available source material and should be applied as absolute contraindications unless specified otherwise):
- Women who have had a hysterectomy.
- Known or suspected carcinoma of the breast.
- Known or suspected endometrial carcinoma or other estrogen‑dependent neoplasia.
- Known or suspected progestogen‑dependent neoplasms.
- Untreated endometrial hyperplasia.
- Active liver disease, or a history of liver disease where liver function tests have not returned to normal.
- Cerebrovascular accident, or past history of cerebrovascular disease associated with previous estrogen use.
- Previous idiopathic or current venous thromboembolism (deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism).
- Known thrombophilic disorders (e.g., protein C deficiency, protein S deficiency, antithrombin deficiency).
- Active or recent arterial thromboembolic disease (e.g., unstable angina, myocardial infarction).
- Abnormal genital/urinary tract bleeding of unknown aetiology.
- Porphyria.
- Known or suspected pregnancy.
- Lactation.
- Known hypersensitivity to any component of the product.
- Meningioma or history of meningioma.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- **Critical warning:** Estrogens and progestogens should not be used for the prevention of cardiovascular disease or dementia. Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration consistent with treatment goals and individual risk profile.

Medical assessment and monitoring
- Obtain a complete medical and family history and perform a baseline physical examination before initiating or reinstituting therapy; include blood pressure, breast, abdominal and pelvic examination.
- Arrange appropriate breast cancer surveillance (regular clinical breast exams and mammography). Mammography frequency: every 1–2 years is advised in available material; individualise based on age, risk factors and prior results.
- In cases of undiagnosed, persistent or recurring abnormal vaginal bleeding, perform adequate diagnostic measures (including endometrial sampling) to exclude malignancy.
- Re-evaluate the need for continued therapy at regular intervals; a full physical/gynaecological examination (including pelvic examination) should be performed at least annually if therapy continues.

Meningioma
- Reports of meningioma occurrence (single and multiple) associated with estrogens/progestogens have been observed. Monitor for signs/symptoms of meningioma according to clinical practice. If meningioma is diagnosed, discontinue estradiol + dydrogesterone; tumour shrinkage has been observed after treatment cessation.

Endometrial effects
- In women with an intact uterus, unopposed estrogen increases risk of endometrial hyperplasia and carcinoma; addition of a progestogen (as in this combination) reduces this excess risk. Breakthrough bleeding/spotting is common in early treatment; new-onset or persistent bleeding warrants investigation.

Cardiovascular, thromboembolic and cerebrovascular risks
- Combined estrogen–progestogen therapy is associated with an increased risk of coronary heart disease events, ischaemic stroke, and venous thromboembolism (VTE; deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism). The baseline absolute risk increases with age.
- The risk of VTE is increased, particularly in the first year of therapy. Consider withholding estrogens for 4–6 weeks prior to surgery associated with increased thromboembolic risk and during prolonged immobilisation; resume only after full mobilisation.
- Manage conventional cardiovascular risk factors (hypertension, diabetes, smoking, hyperlipidaemia, obesity) appropriately when considering HRT.
- Patients with known thrombophilic states: HRT is contraindicated. For women with a family history of VTE at a young age, consider offering counselling about limited thrombophilia testing (limitations of testing should be explained).

Breast cancer
- Combined estrogen–progestogen therapy increases the risk of breast cancer in a duration-dependent manner; risk becomes apparent after a few years of use and declines after cessation, depending on prior duration of use. Combined regimens that use dydrogesterone have been associated with a lower risk than some other synthetic progestogens in epidemiological data, but a risk remains and increases with longer durations of use.

Other considerations
- HRT may increase mammographic density and the frequency of abnormal mammograms requiring further evaluation.
- Progestogen addition may adversely affect lipoprotein metabolism (e.g., lower HDL, raise LDL) and may impair glucose tolerance in some patients.
- Use with caution in women on chronic anticoagulant therapy; carefully consider benefit–risk.

Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and periodic clinical evaluation (including breast and pelvic examinations).
- Mammography per local guidance and individual risk.
- Monitor for signs/symptoms of thromboembolism; advise patients to seek immediate medical attention for leg swelling, chest pain, sudden dyspnoea, or other thromboembolic symptoms.
- Liver function tests and evaluation if history of liver disease.

Additional observational data (selected)
- Nested case–control analysis (UK General Practice Research Database): incidence rates for estradiol/dydrogesterone users — myocardial infarction 0.40 per 1000 person‑years (95% CI 0.18–0.76); thrombotic stroke 0.27 per 1000 person‑years (95% CI 0.10–0.58). Adjusted OR for MI in estradiol/dydrogesterone users vs non‑users: 1.06 (95% CI 0.48–2.36); for thrombotic stroke: 0.50 (95% CI 0.21–1.22).
- Comparative data: Estradiol plus dydrogesterone showed lower VTE risk estimates in one large nested case‑control UK analysis compared with conjugated equine estrogen plus medroxyprogesterone acetate; exact clinical interpretation should be cautious (see Clinical Trials / Epidemiology section).

## 11. Pharmacology
Mechanism of action
- Estradiol: provides estrogen replacement to alleviate vasomotor and other symptoms of estrogen deficiency in menopause and contributes to maintenance of bone mineral density.
- Dydrogesterone: a progestogen added to protect the endometrium from estrogen‑induced hyperplasia when the uterus is intact.

## 12. Clinical Trials
Key trial and epidemiological findings described in available sources:

- Women's Health Initiative (WHI) — estrogen plus progestogen sub-study (randomised, placebo‑controlled):
  - Reported increased risks with conjugated equine estrogens plus medroxyprogesterone acetate for several outcomes versus placebo:
    - Coronary heart disease events: 37 vs 30 per 10,000 women‑years (treated vs placebo).
    - Stroke: 29 vs 21 per 10,000 women‑years.
    - Venous thromboembolism: 34 vs 16 per 10,000 women‑years.
  - Results emphasize that observed risks emerged early and persisted during follow-up. These data were used in available material to contextualise risks for estrogen/progestogen HRT broadly.

- Heart and Estrogen/progestin Replacement Study (HERS; secondary prevention trial):
  - Population: postmenopausal women with established coronary heart disease.
  - Finding: No cardiovascular benefit for combined estrogen–progestogen therapy in secondary prevention; early increase in CHD events noted in year 1.

- Cochrane meta-analyses and meta-analyses of prospective epidemiological studies:
  - Reported no protective effect of hormone therapy for all‑cause mortality or cardiovascular death; showed increased risks for stroke and venous thromboembolism with hormone therapy.
  - Reported effect sizes include RR for stroke ~1.32 (CI 1.12–1.56) in primary prevention analyses (absolute increase ~6 per 1000 women over mean follow‑up ~4.2 years) and RR for VTE 1.92 (CI 1.36–2.69) (absolute increase ~8 per 1000 women over ~6 years).

- Observational cohort and case‑control data:
  - French E3N cohort: associations between hormone therapy and breast cancer varied by type of progestogen; estrogen + dydrogesterone had a relative risk that was lower than most other synthetic progestogens in that cohort (exact figures reported in source material).
  - Nested case–control analyses (UK): suggested estradiol + dydrogesterone may have lower VTE risk compared with some other estrogen–progestogen combinations (e.g., conjugated equine estrogen + medroxyprogesterone acetate), but absolute and relative risks varied and confidence intervals included no effect for some comparisons.
