# Editorial Product Information: Esketamine Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)**. 
- At the time of this publication, only the Australian jurisdiction's documentation was available for review. Consequently, jurisdictional comparisons (e.g., with US or European labels) are not presented, and all specific regulatory classifications and pregnancy categories are reflective of the Australian therapeutic context.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals (HCPs). It is intended as a clinical reference and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Esketamine hydrochloride (the S-enantiomer of racemic ketamine).
- **Chemical Class:** Antidepressant; non-selective, non-competitive N-methyl-D-aspartate (NMDA) receptor antagonist.
- **Brand Name:** **SPRAVATO** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N06AX27.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Nasal spray, solution. Each single-use device contains 32.3 mg of esketamine hydrochloride, equivalent to 28 mg of esketamine base in 0.2 mL of solution (delivered in two actuations, one per nostril).
- **Route of Administration:** Intranasal.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Janssen-Cilag Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Treatment-Resistant Depression (TRD):** Indicated for Major Depressive Disorder in adults who have not achieved an adequate response to at least two different antidepressants of sufficient dose and duration for the current moderate to severe episode. 
- **Requirement for Combination Therapy:** Must be initiated in conjunction with a newly initiated oral antidepressant [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults < 65 years)
- **Induction Phase (Weeks 1–4):** 56 mg on Day 1. Subsequent doses are 56 mg or 84 mg administered twice weekly.
- **Maintenance Phase (Weeks 5–8):** 56 mg or 84 mg administered once weekly.
- **Maintenance Phase (Week 9 onwards):** 56 mg or 84 mg administered every 2 weeks or once weekly. Dosing frequency should be individualised to the lowest frequency required to maintain response or remission.

### General Dosing (Adults $\ge$ 65 years)
- **Induction Phase (Weeks 1–4):** 28 mg on Day 1. Subsequent doses may be increased in 28 mg increments (to 56 mg or 84 mg) administered twice weekly.
- **Maintenance Phase:** Similar titration logic to younger adults, beginning at Week 5.

### Administration Instructions
- **Supervision:** Esketamine must be self-administered by the patient under the direct supervision of an HCP. A treatment session includes administration and a post-dose observation period.
- **Device Handling:** The device is single-use and must **not** be primed. If a dose requires multiple devices (e.g., 56 mg or 84 mg), a 5-minute rest is required between the use of each device.
- **Fastening:** Patients should avoid food for $\ge$ 2 hours and liquids for $\ge$ 30 minutes before administration to reduce nausea/vomiting risk.
- **Nasal Decongestants:** If required, these should be administered at least 1 hour before esketamine.

### Missed Dose Instructions
- If one or two sessions are missed, the next session should occur as originally scheduled. If more than two sessions are missed, the dose or frequency may require adjustment based on clinical judgment [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Patients for whom an increase in blood pressure or intracranial pressure poses a serious risk, specifically:
    - Known aneurysmal vascular disease (intracranial, thoracic, abdominal aorta, or peripheral arterial vessels).
    - Known history of intracerebral haemorrhage.
- Known hypersensitivity to esketamine, ketamine, or any excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Patients must be monitored for blood pressure, sedation, and dissociation at every session until clinically stable.
- **Blood Pressure (BP) Increases:** Transient increases in systolic and diastolic BP peak approximately 40 minutes post-dose and last 1–2 hours.
    - **Baseline assessment:** If BP is $> 140/90$ mmHg ($< 65$ years) or $> 150/90$ mmHg ($\ge$ 65 years), consider the risk/benefit of delaying treatment.
    - **Post-dose:** Reassess BP at 40 minutes and as clinically warranted.
- **Suicide Risk:** Closely monitor for clinical worsening and suicidal ideation, particularly in early treatment and following dose changes.
- **Respiratory Depression:** Rare cases observed; risk is higher with concomitant CNS depressants or comorbidities (obesity, respiratory disease).
- **Cognitive and Motor Impairment:** Esketamine impairs attention, judgment, and motor skills. Patients must not drive or operate machinery until the next day following a restful sleep.
- **Bladder Effects:** While no cases of interstitial cystitis were found in esketamine clinical trials, it has been reported with long-term ketamine use/abuse.
- **Drug Abuse and Dependence:** Individuals with a history of substance use disorder require careful monitoring [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Most adverse effects are mild-to-moderate and resolve on the day of administration.

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common ($\ge$ 10%)** | Dissociation, dizziness, nausea, sedation, headache, vertigo, dysgeusia, hypoesthesia, increased blood pressure, anxiety, vomiting. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Mental impairment, tremor, lethargy, dysarthria, tachycardia, nasal discomfort, dry mouth, hyperhidrosis, pollakiuria, feeling abnormal/drunk. |
| **Rare ($< 0.1$)** | Seizures, bradycardia, respiratory depression (post-marketing). |
| **Very Rare ($< 0.01$)** | Hypotension. |

*Note: Dissociation typically resolves by 1.5 hours post-dose and tends to diminish in severity over time with repeated treatment.*

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **CNS Depressants:** Concomitant use with benzodiazepines, opioids, or alcohol may increase sedation.
- **Psychostimulants and MAOIs:** May increase blood pressure; monitor BP closely.

### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Hepatic Enzyme Inhibitors:** Ticlopidine (CYP2B6 inhibitor) increased esketamine AUC by ~29%; Clarithromycin (CYP3A4 inhibitor) had a minimal effect.
- **Hepatic Enzyme Inducers:** Rifampicin (potent inducer) decreased esketamine Cmax and AUC by ~17% and 28% respectively.
- **Effect of Esketamine on Other Drugs:** Nasal administration of 84 mg twice weekly for 2 weeks reduced midazolam (CYP3A4 substrate) AUC by ~16% [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. Esketamine should not be used during pregnancy. Data are limited; animal studies with ketamine show potential for developmental neurotoxicity. Highly effective contraception is required during treatment and for 6 weeks after the last dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. Esketamine is expected to be excreted in human milk.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed maternal/paternal toxicity at high doses, but no adverse effects on fertility or reproductive capacity were noted at clinically relevant doses.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in patients $\le$ 17 years.
- **Elderly Use ($\ge$ 65 years):** Higher Cmax and AUC observed; starting dose is lower (28 mg).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for mild (Child-Pugh A) or moderate (Child-Pugh B). Use in severe impairment (Child-Pugh C) is not recommended.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild-to-severe impairment. Not studied in patients on dialysis.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Antagonist at the ionotropic NMDA receptor. It transiently increases glutamate release, stimulating AMPA receptors and neurotrophic signalling, which may restore synaptic function in brain regions regulating mood.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Bioavailability ~48%; Tmax 20–40 minutes.
    - **Distribution:** Steady-state volume (IV) is 709 L; protein binding 43–45%.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively hepatic (primary pathway: N-demethylation via CYP2B6 and CYP3A4 to noresketamine).
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life 7–12 hours; clearance ~89 L/h (IV). Excreted primarily in urine ($>$ 78%) as metabolites.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Short-term Efficacy (TRANSFORM-1, 2, 3):** Esketamine plus a new oral antidepressant showed statistically significant and clinically meaningful reduction in MADRS total scores compared to placebo spray plus a new oral antidepressant. Onset of effect was noted as early as 24 hours post-dose.
- **Long-term Efficacy (SUSTAIN-1):** In patients achieving stable remission, continuing esketamine reduced the risk of relapse by 51% compared to those switched to placebo spray. In stable responders, the risk of relapse was reduced by 70%.
- **Comparative Study (ESCAPE-TRD):** Esketamine demonstrated superiority over extended-release quetiapine (both with an SSRI/SNRI) in achieving remission at Week 8.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C.
- **Handling:** Devices must be disposed of as per local requirements for controlled substances. Patients must never be in possession of the device outside the treatment site [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 8 (Controlled Drug)** [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only Medicine.
