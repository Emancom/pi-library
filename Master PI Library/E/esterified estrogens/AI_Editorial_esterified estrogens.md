# Editorial Product Information: **Esterified Estrogens and Methyltestosterone**

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official product information documents from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Important Regulatory Note:** The provided source indicates that this specific combination product has not been found by the FDA to be safe and effective, and its labelling has not been approved by the FDA [US].
- This document is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** **Esterified Estrogens** (a mixture of sodium salts of sulfate esters of estrogenic substances, primarily estrone) and **Methyltestosterone** (a synthetic C-19 steroid and androgen derivative).
- **Brand Name(s):** Not specified (marketed as generic) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Full Strength Tablet:** 1.25 mg **Esterified Estrogens** and 2.5 mg **Methyltestosterone**. Oval, light green, biconvex, debossed "640" and "E" [US].
    - **Half Strength (H.S.) Tablet:** 0.625 mg **Esterified Estrogens** and 1.25 mg **Methyltestosterone**. Oval, dark green, biconvex, debossed "639" and "E" [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** ECI Pharmaceuticals; Repackaged by Bryant Ranch Prepack [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Vasomotor Symptoms:** Treatment of moderate to severe vasomotor symptoms associated with menopause in patients who have not achieved adequate improvement with estrogen therapy alone [US].
- **Note:** This combination is not indicated for the treatment of nervous symptoms or depression that occur without associated vasomotor symptoms [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** Therapy should be initiated at the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration necessary to achieve treatment goals.
- **Usual Dosage Range:** 1 full-strength tablet or 1 to 2 half-strength tablets daily [US].
- **Administration Schedule:** Dosing should be cyclic (e.g., three weeks of daily administration followed by one week off) [US].
- **Monitoring/Titration:** Patients should be re-evaluated at 3- to 6-month intervals to determine if tapering or discontinuation of therapy is appropriate [US].
- **Special Considerations:** In women with an intact uterus, the addition of a progestin should be considered to mitigate the risk of endometrial cancer associated with unopposed estrogen [US].

## 4. Contraindications
The use of this product is contraindicated in women with:
- Undiagnosed abnormal genital bleeding [US].
- Known, suspected, or a history of breast cancer [US].
- Known or suspected estrogen-dependent neoplasia [US].
- Active deep vein thrombosis (DVT), pulmonary embolism (PE), or a history of these conditions [US].
- Active or recent (within the past year) arterial thromboembolic disease (e.g., stroke, myocardial infarction) [US].
- Liver dysfunction or disease, including severe liver damage [US].
- Known hypersensitivity to any ingredients [US].
- Known or suspected pregnancy [US].
- Breastfeeding [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
- **Endometrial Cancer:** Unopposed estrogen increases the risk of endometrial cancer. Clinical surveillance and diagnostic measures (endometrial sampling) are required for undiagnosed persistent or recurring abnormal vaginal bleeding.
- **Cardiovascular Risks:** Estrogens (with or without progestins) should not be used for the prevention of cardiovascular disease. Increased risks of MI, stroke, DVT, and PE have been reported.
- **Dementia:** An increased risk of probable dementia has been reported in postmenopausal women aged 65 years or older.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Malignant Neoplasms:** Increased risk of breast and ovarian cancer has been associated with estrogen plus progestin therapy [US].
- **Gallbladder Disease:** A 2- to 4-fold increase in the risk of gallbladder disease requiring surgery has been reported [US].
- **Visual Abnormalities:** Retinal vascular thrombosis has been reported. Discontinue medication if there is sudden partial or complete loss of vision, proptosis, diplopia, or migraine [US].
- **Hypercalcemia:** Estrogens and androgens may lead to severe hypercalcemia in patients with breast cancer and bone metastases [US].
- **Virilization:** Women should be monitored for signs of virilization (deepening voice, hirsutism, acne, clitoromegaly). Discontinue if mild virilization occurs to prevent irreversibility [US].
- **Hepatic Effects:** Prolonged high-dose androgen use is associated with peliosis hepatis and hepatic neoplasms, which may be life-threatening [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- Blood pressure should be monitored regularly [US].
- Diabetic patients require close observation for worsening glucose tolerance [US].
- Thyroid function should be monitored in patients on thyroid replacement therapy [US].
- Periodic liver function tests, hemoglobin, and hematocrit are recommended during androgen therapy [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Associated with Estrogens
- **Genitourinary:** Breakthrough bleeding, spotting, dysmenorrhea, vaginal candidiasis, endometrial hyperplasia [US].
- **Breasts:** Tenderness, enlargement, nipple discharge, galactorrhea [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, abdominal cramps, bloating, cholestatic jaundice [US].
- **Skin:** Chloasma/melasma, erythema multiforme, loss of scalp hair, hirsutism [US].
- **CNS:** Headache, migraine, dizziness, mental depression [US].

### Associated with Methyltestosterone
- **Endocrine/Urogenital:** Amenorrhea, virilization (deepening voice, clitoral enlargement) [US].
- **Skin:** Acne, male pattern baldness [US].
- **Metabolic:** Retention of sodium, water, potassium, and calcium; increased serum cholesterol [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Estrogen Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** (e.g., St. John’s Wort, phenobarbital, carbamazepine, rifampin) may reduce estrogen plasma concentrations, potentially decreasing therapeutic effect or changing bleeding profiles [US].
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** (e.g., erythromycin, ketoconazole, ritonavir, grapefruit juice) may increase estrogen plasma concentrations and increase side effects [US].

### Androgen Interactions
- **Anticoagulants:** C-17 substituted derivatives of testosterone may decrease anticoagulant requirements. Monitor INR/prothrombin time closely [US].
- **Oxyphenbutazone:** Concurrent use may result in elevated serum levels of oxyphenbutazone [US].
- **Insulin:** Androgens may decrease blood glucose and insulin requirements in diabetic patients [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **US Category X**. Androgens cause masculinization of the female fetus. The product is contraindicated during pregnancy [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Estrogen may decrease the quantity and quality of breast milk. Androgens and estrogens are excreted in human milk and may cause serious adverse reactions (e.g., virilization) in the nursing infant [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not indicated for use in children [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Cautious dose selection is required, typically starting at the low end of the range due to higher frequencies of hepatic, renal, or cardiac dysfunction. Data from the WHIMS study suggests increased dementia risk in women >65 years [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Androgens and estrogens may cause fluid retention; use with caution in patients with compromised renal function [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in active liver disease. Estrogens and androgens are poorly metabolized in patients with impaired liver function [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Estrogens act by binding to nuclear receptors in estrogen-responsive tissues, reducing pituitary secretion of LH and FSH. Androgens are responsible for the growth and development of male sex organs and secondary sex characteristics and promote protein anabolism [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Metabolism:** Estrogens undergo significant first-pass metabolism in the liver and enterohepatic recirculation. Methyltestosterone is less extensively metabolized by the liver than testosterone, providing a longer half-life suitable for oral use [US].
    - **Excretion:** Estrogens are excreted primarily in the urine. Testosterone metabolites are excreted 90% in urine and 6% in feces [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Women’s Health Initiative (WHI):** Reported increased risks of MI, stroke, invasive breast cancer, and VTE in postmenopausal women using conjugated estrogens (CE) with medroxyprogesterone acetate (MPA) [US].
- **WHI Memory Study (WHIMS):** Demonstrated a 2.05 relative risk for probable dementia in women >65 years using CE/MPA [US].
- **HERS/HERS II:** Showed no cardiovascular benefit for secondary prevention in postmenopausal women with established heart disease using CE/MPA [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15° to 30°C (59° to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only. **DEA Schedule III (CIII)** due to the methyltestosterone component [US].
- **Regulatory Status:** As of the last update, the FDA has not found this combination drug to be safe and effective (Unapproved Drug) [US].
