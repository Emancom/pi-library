# Editorial Product Information: Eflapegrastim

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of eflapegrastim for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for legal and regulatory compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Eflapegrastim-xnst.
- **Chemical Class:** Leukocyte growth factor; recombinant human granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (rhG-CSF) analog. 
- **Brand Name(s):** **ROLVEDON** [US].
- **ATC Code:** L03AA (Proposed/Similar class).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Injection: 13.2 mg/0.6 mL solution in a single-dose prefilled syringe [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Spectrum Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Febrile Neutropenia Prophylaxis:** Indicated to reduce the incidence of infection, manifested as febrile neutropenia, in adult patients with non-myeloid malignancies receiving myelosuppressive anti-cancer drugs associated with a clinically significant incidence of febrile neutropenia [US].
- **Limitations of Use:** Not indicated for the mobilization of peripheral blood progenitor cells for hematopoietic stem cell transplantation [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Recommended Dose:** 13.2 mg administered subcutaneously once per chemotherapy cycle [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Timing:** Administer approximately 24 hours after cytotoxic chemotherapy [US].
- **Prohibited Window:** Do not administer during the period starting 14 days before and ending 24 hours after the administration of cytotoxic chemotherapy [US].
- **Preparation:** 
    - Remove from refrigerator and allow to reach room temperature for at least 30 minutes prior to use [US].
    - Do not shake the syringe.
    - Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration; the solution should be clear and colorless [US].
    - The syringe includes an automatic needle guard to prevent needle-stick injuries [US].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Patients or caregivers who miss a dose should be instructed to contact their healthcare provider immediately [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** History of serious allergic reactions to eflapegrastim, pegfilgrastim, or filgrastim products [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Splenic Rupture:** Rare but potentially fatal cases have occurred with G-CSFs. Evaluate patients reporting left upper abdominal or shoulder pain [US].
- **Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS):** Monitor for fever, lung infiltrates, or respiratory distress. Discontinue therapy if ARDS occurs [US].
- **Serious Allergic Reactions:** Anaphylaxis can occur. Permanently discontinue therapy for serious reactions [US].
- **Sickle Cell Crisis:** Severe and potentially fatal crises may occur in patients with sickle cell disorders. Discontinue therapy if a crisis occurs [US].
- **Glomerulonephritis:** If suspected (based on azotemia, hematuria, or proteinuria), evaluate for cause and consider dose reduction or interruption [US].
- **Leukocytosis:** Monitor CBC. Discontinue treatment if WBC counts reach $\ge$ 100 x $10^9$/L [US].
- **Thrombocytopenia:** Monitor platelet counts during therapy [US].
- **Capillary Leak Syndrome (CLS):** Characterized by hypotension, hypoalbuminemia, edema, and hemoconcentration. Requires close monitoring and symptomatic treatment [US].
- **MDS and AML:** Risk is increased in patients with breast and lung cancer receiving G-CSFs in conjunction with chemotherapy and/or radiotherapy. Monitor for signs and symptoms [US].
- **Aortitis:** May present with fever, abdominal pain, and increased inflammatory markers (e.g., CRP). Discontinue if suspected [US].
- **Nuclear Imaging:** Response to growth factors causes increased bone marrow hematopoietic activity, which may lead to transient positive bone imaging findings [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No formal drug interaction studies have been conducted [US].
- **General Caution:** Due to the potential sensitivity of rapidly dividing myeloid cells to cytotoxic chemotherapy, eflapegrastim should not be administered in the period 14 days before to 24 hours after chemotherapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned under current labeling rules, but data from other G-CSF products in pregnancy have not identified a drug-associated risk of major birth defects or miscarriage [US].
    - **Animal Data:** In rabbits, eflapegrastim caused embryofetal lethality and reduced fetal weight at 6 times the clinical exposure. No malformations were observed in rats or rabbits [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on the presence of eflapegrastim in human milk. Endogenous G-CSF is present in human milk. Consider the developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding alongside the mother’s clinical need for the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats showed no adverse effects on fertility or reproductive performance at doses up to 7 times the clinical exposure [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established in paediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** In clinical studies, 39% of patients were $\ge$ 65 years and 6% were $\ge$ 75 years. No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed compared to younger patients [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Specific dosing adjustments are not provided in available sources, though patient information notes the importance of disclosing kidney problems to the prescriber [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Eflapegrastim-xnst is a recombinant human G-CSF that binds to G-CSF receptors on myeloid progenitor cells and neutrophils. This triggers signaling pathways that control the differentiation, proliferation, migration, and survival of neutrophils [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Increases absolute neutrophil counts (ANC) in a dose-dependent, linear (but less than dose-proportional) manner [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Median $T_{max}$ is 25 hours (range: 6 to 144 hours) [US].
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is 1.44 L [US].
    - **Elimination:** Geometric mean half-life is 36.4 hours in Cycle 1. Clearance decreases as the dose increases, suggesting target-mediated clearance by neutrophils [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Expected to undergo endogenous protein degradation following receptor-mediated internalization [US].
    - **Excretion:** Not detected in urine [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** Two randomized, open-label, active-controlled, non-inferiority studies (Study 1 and Study 2) enrolled 643 patients with early-stage breast cancer.
- **Design:** Patients received TC chemotherapy (docetaxel/cyclophosphamide). Eflapegrastim (13.2 mg) was compared to pegfilgrastim (6 mg) [US].
- **Primary Outcome:** Mean Duration of Severe Neutropenia (DSN) in Cycle 1.
- **Results:** Eflapegrastim was found to be non-inferior to pegfilgrastim in both studies. In Study 1, the mean DSN was 0.20 days for eflapegrastim vs. 0.35 days for pegfilgrastim [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Keep in the original carton to protect from light. Do not freeze; discard if frozen. Do not shake [US].
- **Shelf Life/Room Temperature Stability:** Syringes left at room temperature (20°C to 25°C) must be discarded after 72 hours [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only (Rx-only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
