# Editorial Product Information: Ergotamine Tartrate and Caffeine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial document has been developed based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a concise reference format.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidelines required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Ergotamine tartrate and Caffeine.
- **Chemical Class:** Ergot alkaloid (ergotamine) and methylxanthine (caffeine).
- **Brand Name(s):** Cafergot [US].
- **ATC Code:** N02CA52 (Ergotamine, combinations excl. psycholeptics).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Oral film-coated tablets containing 1 mg ergotamine tartrate and 100 mg caffeine [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Trifluent Pharma, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Vascular Headache:** Indicated for use as therapy to abort or prevent vascular headaches, including migraine, migraine variants, or "histaminic cephalalgia" (cluster headaches) [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** Therapy should be initiated at the first sign of a headache attack [US].
- **Standard Regimen:** Initial dose of 2 tablets (2 mg ergotamine/200 mg caffeine). If relief is not obtained, additional 1-tablet doses may be taken at 30-minute intervals [US].
- **Maximum Dosages [US]:**
    - **Per Attack:** 6 tablets.
    - **Per Week:** 10 tablets.
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets are for acute treatment and should not be used for chronic daily administration [US].
- **Preventive Use:** In specifically selected patients, administration at bedtime may be used as a short-term preventive measure, provided the maximum dosage limits are respected [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Potent CYP 3A4 Inhibitors:** Coadministration with potent cytochrome P450 3A4 inhibitors (e.g., protease inhibitors such as ritonavir, nelfinavir, indinavir; macrolide antibiotics such as erythromycin, clarithromycin, troleandomycin; and azole antifungals such as ketoconazole, itraconazole) [US].
- **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated due to the risk of fetal harm and oxytocic effects [US].
- **Cardiovascular Disease:** Peripheral vascular disease, coronary heart disease, and hypertension [US].
- **Organ Impairment:** Severely impaired hepatic or renal function [US].
- **Other Conditions:** Sepsis [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to ergot alkaloids, caffeine, or any inactive ingredients (including tartrazine) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning (US):** Serious and/or life-threatening peripheral ischemia has been associated with the coadministration of ergotamine with potent CYP 3A4 inhibitors. Inhibition of CYP 3A4 increases serum ergotamine levels, elevating the risk of vasospasm leading to cerebral or peripheral ischemia [US].
- **Fibrotic Complications:** Rare reports of retroperitoneal and/or pleuropulmonary fibrosis have occurred. Long-term continuous use has also been associated with fibrotic thickening of cardiac valves (aortic, mitral, tricuspid, and/or pulmonary) [US].
- **Ergotism:** Characterized by intense arterial vasoconstriction. Symptoms include numbness, tingling, muscle pain in extremities, cyanosis, and loss of peripheral pulses. If untreated, this may progress to gangrene [US].
- **Tartrazine Sensitivity:** This product contains FD&C Yellow No. 5 (tartrazine), which may cause allergic-type reactions (including bronchial asthma) in susceptible individuals, particularly those with aspirin hypersensitivity [US].
- **Drug Abuse and Dependence:** Reports of psychological dependence and withdrawal symptoms (rebound headache) exist for patients using the medication indiscriminately over long periods [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Cardiovascular:** Serious vasoconstrictive complications (ischemia, cyanosis, absence of pulse, cold extremities, gangrene, precordial pain), EKG changes, muscle pain, transient tachycardia or bradycardia, and hypertension [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea and vomiting [US].
- **Neurological:** Paresthesias, numbness, weakness, and vertigo [US].
- **Allergic/Dermatological:** Localized edema and itching [US].
- **Fibrotic:** (See Warnings/Precautions) [US].
- **Frequency Data:** Specific frequency percentages are not specified in the available source [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic (CYP 3A4):** Potent inhibitors (e.g., ritonavir, erythromycin) significantly increase ergotamine exposure, risking toxicity. Moderate/less potent inhibitors (e.g., saquinavir, fluconazole, grapefruit juice) should also be used with caution [US].
- **Vasoconstrictors:** Should not be administered with other vasoconstrictors to avoid cumulative pressor effects [US].
- **Sympathomimetics:** Concomitant use may lead to extreme elevation of blood pressure [US].
- **Beta-blockers:** Propranolol has been reported to potentiate the vasoconstrictive action of ergotamine by blocking the vasodilating properties of epinephrine [US].
- **Nicotine:** May provoke vasoconstriction in some patients, increasing the ischemic response to ergot therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category X [US].** Ergotamine has oxytocic effects, particularly in the third trimester. It is contraindicated in pregnancy and during labor/delivery. While ergotamine crosses the placenta in small amounts, prolonged vasoconstriction of uterine vessels may cause fetal growth retardation [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Ergotamine is excreted in breast milk and may cause symptoms of vomiting, diarrhea, weak pulse, and unstable blood pressure in nursing infants. Ergot drugs may also inhibit prolactin. A decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the mother's clinical need [US].
- **Fertility:** Not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in paediatric patients have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed, though contraindications for cardiovascular and renal/hepatic disease are highly relevant to this population [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Ergotamine is an alpha-adrenergic blocking agent with a direct stimulating effect on the smooth muscle of peripheral and cranial blood vessels. It also exhibits serotonin antagonism. Caffeine acts as a cranial vasoconstrictor, enhancing the effect of ergotamine [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Metabolism:** Ergotamine is extensively metabolized by the cytochrome P450 3A (CYP 3A4) system [US].
    - **Absorption/Distribution:** Caffeine is known to cross the placenta [US]. Ergotamine crosses the placenta in small amounts and is excreted in breast milk [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Ergotamine depresses central vasomotor centers. Compared to hydrogenated ergotamine, it has less pronounced adrenergic blocking and greater vasoconstrictive actions [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Container:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container [US].
- **Safety:** Keep out of reach of children [US].
