# Editorial Product Information: Eletriptan

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **eletriptan** product information for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this writing, only the Australian PI was provided for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Eletriptan hydrobromide (5-HT$_{1B/1D}$ receptor agonist).
- **Brand Names:** **Relpax** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N02CC06.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - 40 mg film-coated tablets: Orange, round, debossed with "REP 40" and "VLE".
    - 80 mg film-coated tablets: Orange, round, debossed with "REP 80" and "VLE".
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Viatris Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Migraine Headache:** Acute treatment of migraine headache with or without aura [Australian-approved]. 
- **Exclusions:** Not indicated for the prophylactic management of migraine or the treatment of hemiplegic, ophthalmoplegic, or basilar migraine.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults 18–65 years):**
    - **Recommended Initial Dose:** 40 mg.
    - **Dose Range:** 20 mg, 40 mg, and 80 mg were found effective in clinical trials; however, higher response rates are seen with 40 mg and 80 mg.
    - **Maximum Single Dose:** 80 mg.
    - **Maximum Daily Dose:** 160 mg.
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Tablets should be swallowed whole with water as early as possible after the onset of a migraine, though they remain effective if taken later.
- **Repeat Dosing:**
    - **Ineffective First Dose:** If the initial dose fails to provide a response, a second dose should not be taken for the same attack.
    - **Recurrent Headache:** If the headache returns after initial improvement, a second dose may be taken, provided there is at least a 2-hour interval from the first dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** To **eletriptan** or any excipients (including lactose).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Severe hepatic impairment [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Disease:**
    - Confirmed coronary heart disease (including ischaemic heart disease, angina pectoris, history of myocardial infarction, or silent ischaemia).
    - Coronary artery vasospasm (e.g., Prinzmetal’s angina).
    - Peripheral vascular disease.
    - Uncontrolled hypertension.
- **Cerebrovascular Disease:** History of cerebrovascular accident (CVA) or transient ischaemic attack (TIA).
- **Concomitant Medications:**
    - Use of ergotamine or ergotamine derivatives (e.g., methysergide) within 24 hours of **eletriptan**.
    - Concomitant use of other 5-HT$_1$ receptor agonists.
    - Potent CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, itraconazole, erythromycin, clarithromycin, and certain protease inhibitors) within 48 hours of treatment [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Diagnosis:** Only for use where a clear diagnosis of migraine has been established.
- **Atypical Headaches:** Do not use for "atypical" headaches related to potentially serious conditions such as stroke or aneurysm rupture.
- **Cardiovascular Risk:** Evaluation is recommended prior to use in patients with a high likelihood of underlying cardiovascular disease. **Eletriptan** is not recommended for use in patients with heart failure.
- **Blood Pressure:** Transient increases in blood pressure have been observed, particularly in the elderly and renally impaired.
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Potential risk when co-administered with SSRIs or SNRIs.
- **Medication Overuse Headache (MOH):** Excessive use of triptans can lead to daily chronic headaches.
- **Lactose Intolerance:** Tablets contain lactose; use with caution in patients with rare hereditary galactose intolerance or glucose-galactose malabsorption.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Most reactions are mild and transient. Frequency data is based on controlled clinical trials [Australian]:

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (>5%)** | Asthenia, dizziness, nausea, somnolence |
| **Infrequent (1–5%)** | Chest symptoms (tightness/pain), dry mouth, dyspepsia, paraesthesia, headache, pharyngitis, sweating |
| **Rare/Post-marketing** | Ischaemic colitis, angioedema, syncope, hypertension, coronary artery vasospasm, myocardial infarction |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Potent inhibitors significantly increase **eletriptan** exposure (e.g., ketoconazole increased AUC by 5.9-fold). **Eletriptan** is contraindicated within 48 hours of such agents.
- **Ergotamine/Ergot-type Medications:** Increased risk of prolonged vasospastic reactions. Maintain a 24-hour gap between dosing.
- **Serotonergic Drugs:** Increased risk of serotonin syndrome when used with SSRIs, SNRIs, or St John’s Wort.
- **Propranolol:** Increases **eletriptan** AUC by 33%, but this is not considered clinically significant.
- **MAO Inhibitors:** **Eletriptan** is not a substrate for MAO; no interaction expected.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B1]. Safety in pregnant women has not been established. Use only if the expected benefit to the mother outweighs the potential risk to the foetus. 
- **Breastfeeding:** **Eletriptan** is excreted in human milk. In a study, the mean total amount in milk over 24 hours was 0.02% of an 80 mg dose. Infant exposure can be minimised by avoiding breastfeeding for 24 hours post-dose.
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility observed in animal studies at doses resulting in exposure 3 times the human maximum recommended dose.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use in children (<18 years) is not recommended. Efficacy has not been proven in this age group [Australian].
- **Elderly (>65 years):** Use with caution, especially doses >40 mg, due to potentially more marked effects on blood pressure.
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - Mild to Moderate: No dose adjustment required.
    - Severe: Doses >40 mg should be used with caution due to observed increases in blood pressure.
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - Mild to Moderate: No dose adjustment required.
    - Severe: Contraindicated.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A potent and selective agonist at vascular 5-HT$_{1B}$ and neuronal 5-HT$_{1D}$ receptors. It mediates intracranial blood vessel constriction and inhibits neurogenic inflammation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed (≥81%). Bioavailability is ~50%. T$_{max}$ is ~1.5 hours. Absorption is reduced by 30% during a migraine attack.
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is 138 L; 85% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by CYP3A4. The N-demethylated metabolite is active but circulates at low levels (10-20% of parent).
    - **Elimination:** Mean plasma half-life is approximately 4 hours. Primarily eliminated via metabolism (90%).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adult Efficacy:** Eight randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled studies demonstrated that **eletriptan** (20 mg, 40 mg, and 80 mg) was significantly superior to placebo for headache response at 2 hours.
- **Comparative Efficacy:** In head-to-head trials, **eletriptan** 80 mg showed significantly higher response rates at 2 hours compared to sumatriptan (25 mg, 50 mg, and 100 mg).
- **Onset:** Significant response rates were observed as early as 30 minutes post-dose.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Packaging:** Al/Al or PVC/Aclar/Al blister packs.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** 
    - [Australian]: Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **Classification:** Rx-only.
