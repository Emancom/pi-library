# Editorial Product Information: Eslicarbazepine Acetate

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesised from the following source documents:
    - **United States (FDA)**: Prescribing Information for Eslicarbazepine Acetate Tablets (Jubilant Cadista Pharmaceuticals Inc., Revised 04/2026).
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of eslicarbazepine acetate for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis intended for educational and reference purposes. It does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Eslicarbazepine acetate (a dibenz[b,f]azepine-5-carboxamide derivative).
- **Brand Names**: **Aptiom** (Note: Document provided is a generic version by Jubilant Cadista Pharmaceuticals Inc.) [US].
- **ATC Code**: N03AF04 (Not explicitly in source, but standard for class).
- **Dosage Form and Strengths**: 
    - **Oral Tablets**: 200 mg, 400 mg, 600 mg, and 800 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Jubilant Cadista Pharmaceuticals Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Partial-Onset Seizures**: Indicated for the treatment of partial-onset seizures in patients 4 years of age and older [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing (Monotherapy and Adjunctive Therapy)
- **Initial Dose**: 400 mg once daily.
- **Titration**: Increase in weekly increments of 400 mg to 600 mg based on clinical response and tolerability.
- **Maintenance Dose**: 800 mg to 1,600 mg once daily. 
    - For monotherapy, 800 mg/day is the suggested maintenance dose for those unable to tolerate 1,200 mg/day.
    - For adjunctive therapy, 1,600 mg/day should be considered for those not achieving satisfactory response at 1,200 mg/day [US].

### Pediatric Dosing (Ages 4 to 17 Years)
Dosing is weight-based and administered once daily [US]:
- **11 to 21 kg**: 200 mg initial; 400–600 mg maintenance.
- **22 to 31 kg**: 300 mg initial; 500–800 mg maintenance.
- **32 to 38 kg**: 300 mg initial; 600–900 mg maintenance.
- **>38 kg**: 400 mg initial; 800–1,200 mg maintenance.
- **Titration**: Weekly increments should not exceed the initial dose for that weight category.

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets may be taken whole or crushed, with or without food [US].

### Discontinuation
- To minimize the risk of increased seizure frequency or status epilepticus, the dose should be tapered gradually [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity**: Known hypersensitivity to **eslicarbazepine acetate**, **oxcarbazepine**, or any component of the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Suicidal Behavior and Ideation**: Antiepileptic drugs (AEDs) increase the risk of suicidal thoughts or behavior. Monitor patients for the emergence or worsening of depression or unusual mood changes [US].
- **Serious Dermatologic Reactions**: Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS) and Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN) have been reported. Discontinue immediately if a skin reaction is suspected unless it is clearly not drug-related [US].
- **DRESS/Multiorgan Hypersensitivity**: Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS) may be fatal. Discontinue if an alternative etiology for fever, rash, or lymphadenopathy cannot be established [US].
- **Anaphylaxis and Angioedema**: Rare but potentially fatal (laryngeal edema) reactions reported. Discontinue treatment immediately if these occur [US].
- **Hyponatremia**: Clinically significant hyponatremia (sodium <125 mEq/L) can occur, typically within the first 8 weeks. Monitor sodium levels in patients at risk or if symptoms (nausea, confusion, increased seizures) develop [US].
- **Neurological Adverse Reactions**: High incidence of dizziness, gait disturbance, somnolence, fatigue, and cognitive dysfunction. Patients should avoid hazardous activities (e.g., driving) until the drug's effects are known [US].
- **Drug-Induced Liver Injury (DILI)**: Rare but serious hepatic effects. Discontinue if jaundice or evidence of significant liver injury occurs [US].
- **Thyroid and Hematologic Effects**: Dose-dependent decreases in T3/T4 (usually without clinical hypothyroidism) and rare cases of pancytopenia/agranulocytosis have been observed [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Enzyme Inducers**: **Carbamazepine**, **phenytoin**, **phenobarbital**, and **primidone** may decrease the plasma concentration of the active metabolite, eslicarbazepine. Higher doses of eslicarbazepine acetate may be required [US].
- **CYP2C19 Inhibition**: Eslicarbazepine acetate inhibits CYP2C19, potentially increasing levels of **phenytoin**, **clobazam**, and **omeprazole**. Phenytoin dose reduction may be necessary [US].
- **CYP3A4 Induction**: Eslicarbazepine acetate induces CYP3A4, which may decrease concentrations of **simvastatin**, **lovastatin**, and **hormonal contraceptives** [US].

### Specific Management Notes
- **Oral Contraceptives**: Females of reproductive potential should use additional or alternative non-hormonal birth control during and for one menstrual cycle after treatment [US].
- **Carbamazepine**: Concomitant use increases the risk of diplopia, dizziness, and ataxia. A dose adjustment of either drug may be needed [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: [US: No specific letter category, but source notes potential fetal harm based on animal data]. Animal studies showed malformations, embryolethality, and growth retardation at clinically relevant doses. Enrollment in the North American Antiepileptic Drug (NAAED) Pregnancy Registry is encouraged [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: Eslicarbazepine is excreted in human milk. The effects on the infant are unknown; the benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need of the mother [US].
- **Fertility**: Animal data showed impairment of female fertility in rats [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Approved for partial-onset seizures in patients 4 years and older [US].
- **Geriatric Use**: Limited data (N=15 in trials). Dose selection should be cautious, primarily due to the higher frequency of renal impairment in the elderly [US].
- **Renal Impairment**: 
    - **CrCl <50 mL/min**: Reduce all doses (initial, titration, and maintenance) by 50% [US].
    - **End-Stage Renal Disease**: Hemodialysis removes metabolites; repeated doses may require adjustment [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**:
    - **Mild to Moderate**: No adjustment required.
    - **Severe**: Not studied; use is not recommended [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: A pro-drug extensively converted to eslicarbazepine. It is thought to exert anticonvulsant effects by inhibiting voltage-gated sodium channels [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Highly bioavailable (>90%); peak levels in 1–4 hours. Unaffected by food [US].
    - **Distribution**: Low protein binding (<40%) [US].
    - **Metabolism**: Rapid hydrolytic first-pass metabolism to eslicarbazepine (91% of systemic exposure) [US].
    - **Elimination**: Primarily renal (90%); apparent half-life is 13–20 hours in adults [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Monotherapy (Studies 1 & 2)**: Two historical control trials (N=365) in patients ≥16 years demonstrated efficacy. The 1,200 mg and 1,600 mg once-daily doses met primary endpoints for seizure exit rates compared to historical controls [US].
- **Adjunctive Therapy (Studies 3, 4, & 5)**: Randomized, double-blind trials in adults with refractory partial-onset seizures. Doses of 800 mg and 1,200 mg once daily significantly reduced standardized seizure frequency over 28 days compared to placebo [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Packaging**: Bottles of 30, 60, 90, 500, or 1,000 tablets depending on strength [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification**: 
    - **United States**: Prescription Only (Rx Only). Not a controlled substance [US].
