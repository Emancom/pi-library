# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a clinically relevant format.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the United States PI was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Name:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
  - **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with an inborn error of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
  - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
  - Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
  - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
  - Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours (q3h or q4h), and never less than every 6 hours (q6h).
  - Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
  - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
  - Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
  - Dose adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations [US].

### Monitoring and Titration
- It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels before therapy.
- Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations.
- Target plasma free carnitine concentration: 35 to 60 µmol/L [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate risks before re-administration [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulation Note):** While this document concerns the injection, clinicians should note that high-dose chronic oral levocarnitine is avoided in severely compromised renal function due to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].
- **Monitoring:** Clinical condition and plasma carnitine levels must be monitored to ensure efficacy and avoid toxicity [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Neurologic:** Seizures.
- **Immune System:** Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity.

### Common Adverse Effects (≥5%)
In clinical trials of ESRD patients on hemodialysis, the following events occurred at a frequency ≥5%, regardless of causality [US]:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), flu syndrome (25%), headache (22%), chest pain (12%), fever (7%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), abdominal pain (9%), nausea (8%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), rhinitis (9%), dyspnea (7%).

### Other Reported Effects
- Transient nausea and vomiting.
- Body odor and gastritis.
- **Note:** Frequency of body odor and gastritis is difficult to estimate due to underlying pathology [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. 
  - **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned a letter, but the label states it should be used "only if clearly needed."
  - Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. There are no adequate/well-controlled studies in pregnant women [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows suggest carnitine concentrations in milk increase following exogenous administration. The risks to the infant of excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy for metabolic disorders are established; dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg). See Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources; generally, dose selection should be cautious, reflecting the greater frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function.
- **Renal Impairment:** In ESRD patients on dialysis, dosage is adjusted based on post-dialysis weight and trough carnitine levels (10–20 mg/kg). See Section 3 [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
  - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
  - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), the mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg TID after dialysis), trough levocarnitine levels increased from a baseline of 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Studies:** In ESRD patients, doses of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg increased trough concentrations proportionally over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing.
- **Stability:** Compatible with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservative [US].

- **Legal Classification:** 
  - **US:** Rx-only (Prescription Only Medicine).
