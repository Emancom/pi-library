# Editorial Product Information: Ethambutol Hydrochloride

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ethambutol hydrochloride (an oral chemotherapeutic agent).
- **Chemical Class:** Ethylenediimino-dibutanol.
- **Brand Names:** Ethambutol Lupin [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J04AK02.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 400 mg film-coated tablets [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Lupin Australia Pty Ltd (Distributed by Generic Health Pty Ltd) [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Ethambutol is indicated for the treatment of:
- **Pulmonary tuberculosis.**
- **Extrapulmonary tuberculosis**, including primary tuberculosis, miliary tuberculosis, tuberculous meningitis, tuberculosis of the bones and joints, genitourinary tuberculosis, tuberculosis of the skin, and tuberculous eye diseases [Australian-approved].

**Note on Usage:** Ethambutol must never be used as monotherapy. It must be administered in conjunction with at least one other antituberculosis agent (e.g., isoniazid, rifampicin, streptomycin, or pyrazinamide) based on clinical experience and susceptibility studies.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
Ethambutol is administered once every 24 hours. Absorption is not significantly affected by food intake. Therapy should continue until bacteriological conversion is permanent and maximal clinical improvement is achieved.

### Initial Treatment
- **Dose:** 15 mg/kg of body weight once daily.

### Re-treatment
- **Dose:** 25 mg/kg of body weight once daily.
- **Schedule:** After 60 days of administration, the dose should be reduced to 15 mg/kg once daily.
- **Monitoring:** Monthly eye examinations are advised while the patient is on the 25 mg/kg dose [Australian].

### Intermittent Therapy
- Following an initial daily phase (e.g., two months or until sputum conversion), ethambutol may be administered at **50 mg/kg twice weekly** [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- If isoniazid is used concurrently in intermittent therapy, the usual adult dose is 14 mg/kg twice weekly (with 10 mg pyridoxine for every 100 mg isoniazid) [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to ethambutol hydrochloride.
- Known optic neuritis, unless clinical judgement determines that the benefits outweigh the risks [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Visual Acuity Changes:** Ethambutol may cause dose-related and duration-related decreases in visual acuity due to optic neuritis. This is generally reversible upon prompt discontinuation, though recovery may take months. Rare cases of irreversible blindness have occurred.
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Post-marketing reports include Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), and drug reaction with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms (DRESS). Ethambutol must be permanently discontinued if these reactions occur.

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Ocular Monitoring:** Baseline ophthalmoscopy, finger perimetry, and colour discrimination testing are required. Visual acuity should be checked monthly in patients receiving >15 mg/kg/day. Each eye must be tested separately and both eyes together.
- **Organ Function:** Periodical assessment of renal, hepatic, and haematopoietic systems is recommended during long-term therapy.

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Paediatrics:** Physicians should be alert to the fact that rashes and fever in children may be mistaken for underlying infection rather than a drug reaction.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common (1–10%)
- **Ocular:** Decreased visual acuity, scotoma, colour blindness (red-green), and visual defects. These occur in approximately 2% to 3% of patients at recommended doses [Australian].

### Frequency Not Specified/Post-Marketing
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, anorexia, abdominal pain, and gastrointestinal upset.
- **Neurological:** Peripheral neuritis (numbness and tingling of extremities), dizziness, headache, mental confusion, disorientation, and hallucinations.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylactoid reactions, dermatitis, pruritus, and joint pain.
- **Metabolic:** Elevated serum uric acid levels and precipitation of acute gout.
- **Hepatic:** Transient impairment of liver function (abnormal LFTs).
- **Haematologic:** Rare cases of reversible thrombocytopenia and eosinophilia.
- **Respiratory:** Pulmonary infiltrates.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Notes:** Ethambutol has been shown to reduce the incidence of mycobacterial resistance to isoniazid when used concurrently [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category A:** Drugs which have been taken by a large number of pregnant women and women of childbearing age without any proven increase in the frequency of malformations or other direct or indirect harmful effects on the fetus having been observed.
    - High-dose animal studies have shown minor abnormalities (cleft palate, exencephaly, vertebral column abnormalities), but these were at doses far exceeding human therapeutic levels.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety data is not specified in available sources. 
- **Fertility:** Slight, insignificant decreases in fertility and litter size were noted in high-dose rat studies [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Limited data available; dosage for isoniazid in children is noted as 5 to 20 mg/kg [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available in provided sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosage reduction is required as the primary route of excretion is renal. Adjustments should be based on ethambutol serum levels [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Although transient LFT abnormalities occur, recommended doses are generally considered not to cause hepatic damage [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Ethambutol is a bacteriostatic agent that inhibits the synthesis of one or more metabolites in actively growing mycobacteria, leading to impaired cell metabolism, arrest of multiplication, and cell death. It is specifically active against *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*.

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; peak serum levels (2–5 µg/mL) are reached 2 to 4 hours after a 25 mg/kg dose.
- **Distribution:** Intracellular concentrations in erythrocytes reach approximately twice the plasma levels.
- **Metabolism:** Partially metabolised via oxidation of alcohol to an aldehydic intermediate, then to a dicarboxylic acid.
- **Elimination:** Approximately 50% is excreted unchanged in the urine within 24 hours; 20–22% is excreted unchanged in the faeces.
