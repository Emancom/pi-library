## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** This product is a multi-component kit containing:
    - **Diphenhydramine Hydrochloride** (Antihistamine) [US]
    - **Ibuprofen** (NSAID) [US]
    - **Hydrocortisone** (Corticosteroid/Anti-itch) [US]
    - **Bacitracin Zinc, Neomycin Sulfate, and Polymyxin B Sulfate** (First Aid Antibiotics) [US]
    - **Benzalkonium Chloride** (Antiseptic) and **Lidocaine HCl** (Topical Analgesic) [US]
    - **Ethyl Alcohol** 66.5% and **Isopropyl Alcohol** 70% (Antiseptics) [US]
- **Brand Name:** Care Science All-Purpose First Aid Kit [US]
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Diphenhydramine HCl:** 25 mg oral caplets [US].
    - **Ibuprofen:** 200 mg oral tablets [US].
    - **Hydrocortisone Cream:** 1.0% topical cream [US].
    - **Triple Antibiotic Ointment:** Bacitracin Zinc (400 units), Neomycin Sulfate (3.5 mg), Polymyxin B Sulfate (5000 units) per gram [US].
    - **First Aid and Burn Cream:** Benzalkonium Chloride (0.13%) and Lidocaine HCl (0.5%) [US].
    - **Antiseptic Wipes/Prep Pads:** Ethyl Alcohol 66.5% or Isopropyl Alcohol 70% [US].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral and Topical.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Distributed by ASO LLC (Sarasota, FL) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Respiratory/Allergy Relief:** Temporary relief of symptoms due to hay fever, other respiratory allergies, or the common cold (runny nose, sneezing, itchy/watery eyes, itching of the nose or throat) [US-approved].
- **Analgesia and Antipyresis:** Temporary relief of minor aches and pains associated with headache, toothache, backache, menstrual cramps, common cold, muscular aches, and minor arthritis pain; temporary reduction of fever [US-approved].
- **Dermatological Relief:** Temporary relief of itching associated with minor skin irritations, inflammation, and rashes (due to eczema, insect bites, poison ivy/oak/sumac, soaps, detergents, cosmetics, jewelry, seborrheic dermatitis, psoriasis, and scrapes) [US-approved].
- **Infection Prevention:** First aid to help prevent infection in minor cuts, scrapes, and burns [US-approved].
- **Topical Pain Relief:** Temporary relief of pain associated with minor burns [US-approved].
- **Antisepsis:** Cleansing of skin/face/hands to decrease bacteria; preparation of the skin prior to injection [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Oral Administration
- **Diphenhydramine HCl (25 mg):**
    - **Adults and children (12 years and over):** 1 to 2 caplets every 4–6 hours. Do not exceed 6 doses in 24 hours [US].
    - **Children under 12 years:** Do not use [US].
- **Ibuprofen (200 mg):**
    - **Adults and children (12 years and older):** 1 tablet every 4 to 6 hours while symptoms persist. If symptoms do not respond to 1 tablet, 2 tablets may be used. Do not exceed 6 tablets in 24 hours unless directed by a physician [US].
    - **Children under 12 years:** Consult a physician [US].

### Topical Administration
- **Hydrocortisone Cream 1.0%:** Apply to the affected area 3 to 4 times daily [US].
- **Triple Antibiotic Ointment:** Clean affected area; apply a small amount (finger-tip surface area) 1 to 3 times daily. May be covered with a sterile bandage [US].
- **First Aid and Burn Cream:** Clean affected area; apply a small amount not more than 3 times daily [US].
- **Antiseptic Wipes/Prep Pads:** Wipe the intended area and discard [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Diphenhydramine:** Do not use to make a child sleepy or in combination with any other product containing diphenhydramine (including topical forms) [US].
- **Ibuprofen:**
    - Known allergy to ibuprofen or any other pain reliever/fever reducer [US].
    - Immediately before or after heart surgery [US].
- **Triple Antibiotic Ointment:** Known allergy to any component; do not use internally or in the eyes [US].
- **Hydrocortisone:** Do not use for the treatment of diaper rash or for feminine itching [US].
- **Isopropyl Alcohol:** Do not use with electrocautery procedures [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
- **Cardiovascular Risk (Ibuprofen):** NSAIDs (except aspirin) increase the risk of heart attack, heart failure, and stroke, which can be fatal. Risk is higher with use that is longer or more frequent than directed [US].
- **Gastrointestinal Risk (Ibuprofen):** Contains an NSAID, which may cause severe stomach bleeding. Risk factors include: age ≥60, history of stomach ulcers, concurrent use of anticoagulants/steroids/other NSAIDs, or consumption of ≥3 alcoholic drinks daily [US].
- **Allergy Alert (Ibuprofen):** May cause severe allergic reactions (hives, facial swelling, asthma, shock, skin reddening, rash, blisters) [US].
- **CNS Effects (Diphenhydramine):** Marked drowsiness may occur. Alcohol, sedatives, and tranquilizers may increase this effect [US].
- **Flammability:** Antiseptic wipes and prep pads (containing alcohols) are flammable; keep away from fire or flame [US].
- **Proposition 65 (California):** The outer bag contains Di(2-ethylhexyl)phthalate (DEHP), known to cause cancer and birth defects or other reproductive harm [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Nervous System:** Marked drowsiness, excitability (especially in children) [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Stomach bleeding, stomach pain, upset stomach [US].
- **Dermatological/Allergic:** Rash, hives, skin reddening, blisters, facial swelling, irritation, or redness at the application site [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects (Ibuprofen):** Heart attack, stroke, shock, asthma (wheezing), vomiting blood, or bloody/black stools [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **Diphenhydramine:** Alcohol, sedatives, and tranquilizers may increase the sedative effect [US].
    - **Ibuprofen:** 
        - Concurrent use with other NSAIDs (prescription or non-prescription) increases bleeding risk [US].
        - Concurrent use with aspirin for heart attack/stroke prevention: Ibuprofen may decrease the cardioprotective benefit of aspirin [US].
        - Use with diuretics or steroids may require medical supervision [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Ibuprofen:** Consult a healthcare professional before use. It is especially important not to use **Ibuprofen** at 20 weeks or later in pregnancy unless directed by a doctor, as it may cause problems in the unborn child or complications during delivery [US].
- **Diphenhydramine:** Consult a healthcare professional before use [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Consult a healthcare professional before use [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:**
    - **Diphenhydramine:** Do not use in children under 12 years [US].
    - **Hydrocortisone:** Do not use in children under 2 years; for external anal itching, consult a doctor for children under 12 [US].
    - **First Aid/Burn Cream:** Consult a doctor for use in children under 2 [US].
    - **Antiseptic Wipes:** Supervise children under 6 years of age [US].
- **Elderly (Ibuprofen):** Increased risk of stomach bleeding in individuals aged 60 and older [US].
- **Hepatic/Renal Impairment:** Consult a doctor before using **Ibuprofen** if the patient has liver cirrhosis or kidney disease [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Diphenhydramine:** Store at room temperature 20°–25°C (68°–77°F). Protect from light [US].
- **Ibuprofen:** Store at 20°–25°C (68°–77°F). Avoid excessive heat above 40°C (104°F) [US].
- **Alcohol Prep Pads:** Store at 15°–30°C (59°–86°F) [US].
- **General Kit Items:** Store at room temperature. Do not freeze topicals. Do not use if individual packets are opened or torn [US].
