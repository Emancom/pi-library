# Editorial Product Information: Enzalutamide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Enzalutamide (an androgen receptor signalling inhibitor).
- **Chemical Name:** 4-{3-[4-cyano-3-(trifluoromethyl)phenyl]-5,5-dimethyl-4-oxo-2-thioxoimidazolidin-1-yl}-2-fluoro-N-methylbenzamide.
- **Brand Name:** **Xtandi** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 40 mg liquid-filled soft gelatin capsules [Australian]. 
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Astellas Pharma Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
[Australian-approved indications]:
- Treatment of patients with non-metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (nmHSPC) with high-risk biochemical recurrence (BCR).
- Treatment of metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC).
- Treatment of non-metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (nmCRPC).
- Treatment of metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC) following failure of androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) where chemotherapy is not yet indicated.
- Treatment of mCRPC in patients previously treated with docetaxel.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** 160 mg (four 40 mg capsules) administered as a single oral daily dose.
- **Administration:** Swallow capsules whole with water; do not chew, dissolve, or open. May be taken with or without food, preferably at the same time each day.
- **nmHSPC with High-risk BCR:** Treatment may be suspended if PSA is undetectable (< 0.2 ng/mL) after 36 weeks of therapy. Reinitiate treatment if PSA increases to ≥ 2.0 ng/mL (post-radical prostatectomy) or ≥ 5.0 ng/mL (post-primary radiation therapy).
- **Concomitant ADT:** Unless surgically castrated, medical castration with an LHRH analogue must be continued in CRPC and mHSPC populations.
- **Dose Adjustments for Toxicity:** If Grade 3+ toxicity or intolerable reactions occur, withhold dose for one week or until symptoms improve to ≤ Grade 2. Resume at the same dose or a reduced dose (120 mg or 80 mg) if warranted.
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed at the usual time, take it as close as possible. If missed for an entire day, resume the following day with the regular dose. Do not double the dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to enzalutamide or any excipients (including sorbitol and caprylocaproyl macrogolglycerides) [Australian].
- Women who are or may become pregnant [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Seizure:** Use is associated with a risk of seizure. Use with caution in patients with predisposing factors (history of seizures, brain injury, stroke, brain tumours, or alcoholism). **Enzalutamide** should be permanently discontinued in patients who develop a seizure during treatment.
- **Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES):** Rare reports exist. Symptoms include seizure, headache, confusion, and visual disturbances. Diagnosis requires MRI confirmation; discontinuation is recommended if PRES occurs.
- **Falls and Fractures:** Falls occurred in 12% of patients treated with **enzalutamide** (vs 5.5% placebo). These may lead to non-pathologic fractures.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Reactions including face, tongue, lip, and pharyngeal oedema and rash have been reported. 
- **QT Prolongation:** As ADT can prolong the QT interval, assess the benefit-risk ratio (including the risk of Torsade de pointes) in patients with a history of QT prolongation or those taking concurrent QT-prolonging medications.
- **Cardiovascular Disease:** Phase 3 trials excluded patients with recent MI (6 months), unstable angina (3 months), or NYHA Class III/IV heart failure (unless LVEF ≥ 45%). Use caution in these populations.
- **Hypertension:** Reported in 14.2% of patients. Monitor blood pressure during therapy.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are classified by frequency [Australian]:

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction (Preferred Term) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Fatigue, Asthenia, Hot flush, Hypertension, Fractures, Fall |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Headache, Ischaemic heart disease, Cognitive disorders, Dysgeusia, Restless legs syndrome, Anxiety, Gynaecomastia, Pruritus, Dry skin |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Neutropenia, Leukopenia, Seizure, Visual hallucinations |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Not specified in available sources |
| **Not Known** | Nausea, Diarrhoea, Vomiting, Dysphagia, Face/Tongue/Lip/Pharyngeal oedema, PRES, Rash, Decreased appetite |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Ischaemic heart disease and seizure are highlighted as clinically significant risks.
- **Laboratory Abnormalities:** Very common (>10%) findings include decreased haemoglobin, decreased neutrophil count, decreased white blood cell count, hyponatremia, and hyperglycaemia.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Effect of Other Drugs on Enzalutamide:**
    - **Strong CYP2C8 Inhibitors (e.g., gemfibrozil):** Avoid if possible. If co-administration is necessary, **reduce enzalutamide dose to 80 mg once daily**.
    - **CYP3A4 Inhibitors/Inducers:** No dose adjustment is required when co-administered with inhibitors (e.g., itraconazole) or inducers (e.g., rifampicin).
- **Effect of Enzalutamide on Other Drugs (Enzyme Induction):**
    - Enzalutamide is a **potent inducer of CYP3A4** and a **moderate inducer of CYP2C9 and CYP2C19**. 
    - Interactions are expected with substrates of these enzymes, potentially reducing their clinical effect (e.g., fentanyl, warfarin, midazolam, omeprazole, statins, and certain anticonvulsants). 
    - Induction may take 1 month to reach steady state and may persist for 1 month after stopping **enzalutamide**.
- **Transporter Interactions:** 
    - **P-gp:** Enzalutamide may inhibit P-gp. Use caution with narrow therapeutic index P-gp substrates (e.g., digoxin, colchicine).
- **Laboratory Test Interference:** Can cause falsely elevated digoxin levels when using the Chemiluminescent Microparticle Immunoassay (CMIA). Use alternative assay methods for monitoring digoxin.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category X** [Australian]. Enzalutamide is contraindicated in women of childbearing potential and those who are pregnant. Animal studies showed reproductive toxicity and embryo-fetal lethality.
- **Contraception:**
    - **Males:** Must use a condom during treatment and for 3 months after the last dose if engaged in sexual activity with a pregnant woman or a woman of childbearing potential.
- **Breastfeeding:** Not indicated for use in women. It is unknown if enzalutamide is excreted in human milk; however, it is secreted in rat milk.
- **Fertility:** Based on animal data, male fertility may be impaired.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in children (< 18 years); use is not recommended.
- **Elderly:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy between elderly (≥ 65 years) and younger patients. No dose adjustment is required.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - Mild to Moderate: No dose adjustment necessary.
    - Severe or End-stage: Not studied; caution is advised.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for patients with mild, moderate, or severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class A, B, or C).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Androgen receptor (AR) signalling inhibitor. It competitively inhibits androgen binding to ARs, inhibits AR nuclear translocation, and inhibits AR-DNA binding. This results in decreased proliferation and induced cell death in prostate cancer cells.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Oral bioavailability ≥ 84.2%. $T_{max}$ is 1–2 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Apparent volume of distribution is 110 L. 97–98% protein bound (primarily albumin).
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised via CYP2C8 and CYP3A4/5. The primary active metabolite is N-desmethyl enzalutamide.
    - **Elimination:** Mean terminal half-life is 5.8 days. Primarily excreted in urine (71%) as metabolites.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **AFFIRM (mCRPC post-docetaxel):** Demonstrated superior overall survival (OS) vs placebo (18.4 vs 13.6 months; HR 0.63).
- **PREVAIL (Chemo-naive mCRPC):** Demonstrated significant improvement in OS and radiographic progression-free survival (rPFS) vs placebo.
- **PROSPER (nmCRPC):** Showed 71% reduction in the risk of radiographic progression or death (MFS) vs placebo.
- **ARCHES/ENZAMET (mHSPC):** Demonstrated significant reduction in the risk of rPFS events and improved OS when added to ADT.
- **EMBARK (nmHSPC with high-risk BCR):** Demonstrated a statistically significant reduction (58%) in the risk of metastasis or death when used with ADT compared to ADT alone.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging. 
- **Handling:** Women who are or may become pregnant should not handle damaged or opened capsules without protection (e.g., gloves).
