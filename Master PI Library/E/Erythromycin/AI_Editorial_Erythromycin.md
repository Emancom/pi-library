# Editorial Product Information: Erythromycin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Erythromycin (as ethyl succinate).
- **Chemical Class:** Macrolide antibiotic.
- **Brand Names:** E-MYCIN [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J01FA01 (Erythromycin).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Film-coated tablets:** 400 mg [Australian].
    - **Powder for oral liquid (granules):** 200 mg/5 mL and 400 mg/5 mL when reconstituted [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Alphapharm Pty Ltd trading as Viatris [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Erythromycin is indicated for the treatment of mild to moderate infections caused by susceptible organisms:
- **Respiratory Tract Infections:** Upper and lower respiratory infections due to *Streptococcus pyogenes*, *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Mycoplasma pneumoniae*, *Haemophilus influenzae* (upper tract only), and *Legionella pneumophila* (Legionnaires' disease).
- **Skin and Skin Structure Infections:** Infections of mild to moderate severity caused by *S. pyogenes* or *Staphylococcus aureus* (noting resistance may emerge during treatment).
- **Sexually Transmitted and Genitourinary Infections:**
    - Chlamydial infections (*Chlamydia trachomatis*) including urethritis, trachoma, and pneumonia in infants.
    - Non-gonococcal urethritis caused by *Ureaplasma urealyticum*.
    - Primary syphilis (*Treponema pallidum*) as an alternative in penicillin-allergic patients.
    - Acute uncomplicated gonorrhoea (*Neisseria gonorrhoeae*) in female patients allergic to penicillin (in conjunction with erythromycin lactobionate for injection).
- **Specific Bacterial Infections:**
    - Diphtheria (*Corynebacterium diphtheriae*): As an adjunct to antitoxin and to prevent carrier states.
    - Erythrasma (*Corynebacterium minutissimum*).
    - Pertussis (*Bordetella pertussis*): Early elimination of the organism from the nasopharynx.
    - Infections due to *Listeria monocytogenes* or *Campylobacter jejuni*.
- **Prophylaxis:**
    - Prevention of bacterial endocarditis prior to dental or upper respiratory surgical procedures in penicillin-hypersensitive patients with valvular heart disease.
    - Continuous prophylaxis for recurrences of streptococcal infections in patients with a history of rheumatic heart disease.
    - Prophylaxis of tetanus (*Clostridium tetani*) in penicillin-hypersensitive patients following contaminated wounds.
- **Dermatology:** Adjunctive therapy for moderate to severe acne (*Cutibacterium acnes*).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Usual Dose:** 400 mg every six hours.
- **Maximum Dose:** Up to 4 g/day depending on infection severity.
- **Twice-Daily Dosing:** If desired, half the total daily dose may be given every 12 hours, provided the total daily dose does not exceed 1.6 g [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing
- **Standard Range:** 30 to 50 mg/kg/day in equally divided doses.
- **Severe Infections:** Dosage may be doubled but should not exceed the adult maximum.
- **Weight-based Guide (Mild-Moderate Infection):**
    - < 4.5 kg: 30 to 50 mg/kg/day.
    - 4.5 to 6.8 kg: 200 mg total daily dose.
    - 6.8 to 11 kg: 400 mg total daily dose.
    - 11 to 23 kg: 800 mg total daily dose.
    - 23 to 45 kg: 1,200 mg total daily dose.

### Specific Indication Dosing
- **Primary Syphilis:** 48 to 64 g total, administered in divided doses over 15 days [Australian].
- **Legionnaires' Disease:** 0.8 to 1.6 g every six hours for 14 days (optimal doses not established).
- **Endocarditis Prophylaxis:** 1.6 g (Adults) or 20 mg/kg (Children) 1.5 to 2 hours before the procedure, followed by 800 mg every 6 hours for 6 doses.
- **Severe Acne:** 400 mg four times daily for two weeks, followed by response-based adjustment for at least three months.

### Administration Instructions
- May be administered without regard to meals.
- Tablets are intended for adult use; granules are primarily for paediatric use [Australian].
- Streptococcal infections require a minimum 10-day treatment duration.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to erythromycin, other macrolides, or any formulation excipients.
- **Hepatic:** Severely impaired hepatic function [Australian].
- **Cardiac:**
    - Congenital or acquired QT interval prolongation.
    - History of clinically relevant cardiac arrhythmias (e.g., ventricular arrhythmias) or severe congestive heart failure (NYHA IV).
    - Electrolyte disturbances (hypokalaemia, hypomagnesaemia).
- **Drug Interactions:**
    - Concurrent use of ergotamine or dihydroergotamine.
    - Concurrent use of HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors: simvastatin, lovastatin, or atorvastatin.
    - Concurrent use of QT-prolonging agents (e.g., cisapride, pimozide, terfenadine, astemizole, domperidone, Class IA and III antiarrhythmics).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Cardiovascular Events
- Erythromycin is associated with QT prolongation and ventricular arrhythmias, including torsades de pointes. Fatalities have been reported.
- Observational studies indicate a rare short-term risk of myocardial infarction and cardiovascular mortality associated with macrolides.

### Hepatotoxicity
- Hepatic dysfunction, including cholestatic hepatitis and jaundice, has been reported.
- Patients should be advised to stop treatment and seek medical attention if symptoms of liver disease (e.g., dark urine, jaundice, pruritus) occur.

### Infantile Hypertrophic Pyloric Stenosis (IHPS)
- A significant risk of IHPS exists in infants following erythromycin exposure, particularly for pertussis prophylaxis. A dose-response effect has been observed (up to 10% risk with 15-21 days of therapy) [Australian].

### Clostridium difficile-Associated Disease
- Antibiotic-associated colitis, ranging from mild diarrhoea to fatal pseudomembranous colitis, may occur during or up to two months after therapy.

### Muscle and Nervous System
- Rhabdomyolysis has been reported in patients taking erythromycin concomitantly with certain statins (simvastatin, lovastatin, atorvastatin).
- Erythromycin may aggravate muscle weakness in patients with myasthenia gravis.

### Visual Impairment
- Exposure may lead to visual impairments; patients with pre-existing mitochondrial metabolism dysfunctions (e.g., Leber’s hereditary optic neuropathy) may be at higher risk.

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | System Organ Class / Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** | Decreased appetite, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, flatulence, diarrhoea, muscle spasms. |
| **Common** | Not specifically categorised in available source. |
| **Uncommon** | Oral/vaginal candidiasis, urticaria, rash, elevation of liver enzymes. |
| **Rare** | Pseudomembranous colitis, anaphylaxis, seizures, QT prolongation, palpitations, pancreatitis, cholestatic jaundice, SJS/TEN. |
| **Very Rare** | Tinnitus, reversible hearing loss, hepatic failure, tubulointerstitial nephritis, worsening myasthenia gravis. |
| **Not Known** | Eosinophilia, hallucinations, confusional state, headache, dizziness, visual impairment, hypotension, dyspnoea. |

**Note:** Hearing loss and tinnitus are concentration-dependent and more likely with high doses, overdose, or in patients with renal/hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions (CYP3A4)
- **Erythromycin as Inhibitor:** Erythromycin is a potent inhibitor of CYP3A4. Co-administration increases levels of:
    - **Statins:** (Simvastatin, lovastatin, atorvastatin) - **Contraindicated** due to rhabdomyolysis risk.
    - **Ergot Derivatives:** (Ergotamine, dihydroergotamine) - **Contraindicated** due to ergot toxicity (vasospasm).
    - **Immunosuppressants:** Ciclosporin, tacrolimus.
    - **Benzodiazepines:** Triazolam, alprazolam, midazolam.
    - **Others:** Theophylline, digoxin, warfarin (increased INR), sildenafil, carbamazepine, colchicine (toxicity risk).
- **Erythromycin as Substrate:** CYP3A4 inducers (e.g., rifampicin, phenytoin, St John's Wort) may lead to sub-therapeutic erythromycin levels.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **QT-prolonging Drugs:** Concomitant use with Class IA/III antiarrhythmics, certain neuroleptics, or fluoroquinolones is contraindicated.
- **Antibacterial Antagonism:** In vitro antagonism occurs between erythromycin and clindamycin, lincomycin, or chloramphenicol.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category A** [Australian]. (Large numbers of pregnant women have taken the drug without proven increases in malformations). Observational studies have reported cardiovascular malformations following early pregnancy exposure. Use only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Erythromycin is concentrated in breast milk (approximately 50% of plasma concentration). Risks to the infant include IHPS, gastrointestinal disturbance, and fungal colonisation. Exercise caution [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No apparent effects on fertility observed in animal studies (rats) at 9x human dose.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing must be based on clinical picture and disease course to avoid liver damage from overdose. Benefit-risk must be weighed due to IHPS risk.
- **Elderly Use:** Increased susceptibility to drug-associated QT interval effects.
- **Renal Impairment:** Half-life increases from 1.4 to 6 hours in anuric patients. Dosage adjustment is generally not required. Not removed by dialysis.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Caution is required as erythromycin is primarily excreted by the liver. Contraindicated in severe impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Erythromycin inhibits bacterial protein synthesis by binding to the 50S ribosomal subunits of susceptible organisms. It does not affect nucleic acid synthesis.

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Erythromycin ethyl succinate is absorbed intact and hydrolysed to erythromycin base. Peak serum levels occur within 1–2 hours. Absorption is unaffected by food.
- **Distribution:** Approximately 75% protein bound. Diffuses into most body fluids; does not readily cross into CSF or synovial fluid. Crosses the placental barrier.
- **Metabolism/Excretion:** Primarily concentrated in the liver and excreted in the bile. Approximately 5% is recovered in urine.
- **Half-life:** Approximately 1.4 hours in normal renal function.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Tablets:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Granules:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Reconstituted Suspension:** Refrigerate at 2°C to 8°C. Use within 10 days; do not freeze [Australian].
