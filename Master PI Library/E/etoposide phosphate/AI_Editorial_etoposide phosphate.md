# Editorial Product Information: Etoposide Phosphate (ETOPOPHOS)

**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been compiled by integrating clinical data and regulatory guidelines from the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). In instances of varying recommendations, the most comprehensive or conservative guidance has been prioritized, with the source agency noted in brackets.

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** ETOPOPHOS  
**Active Ingredient:** Etoposide phosphate

ETOPOPHOS is a water-soluble prodrug of etoposide, a semi-synthetic derivative of podophyllotoxin. It is supplied as a sterile lyophilized powder for intravenous infusion. Following administration, etoposide phosphate is rapidly and completely converted in vivo to the active moiety, etoposide.

## 2. Approved Indications
ETOPOPHOS is indicated for the treatment of the following malignancies:
*   **Small Cell Lung Cancer (SCLC):** As first-line treatment in combination with cisplatin [FDA] and other oncolytic agents [TGA].
*   **Testicular Tumors:** Specifically for refractory tumors in combination with other approved chemotherapeutic agents.
*   **Leukemias:** Acute monocytic and myelomonocytic leukemia [TGA].
*   **Lymphomas:** Hodgkin’s disease and non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma [TGA].

## 3. Contraindications
ETOPOPHOS is contraindicated in:
*   Patients with a history of severe hypersensitivity to etoposide, etoposide phosphate, or any components of the formulation (including Dextran 40 and sodium citrate).
*   Patients with severe hepatic dysfunction [TGA].
*   Patients with severe renal dysfunction [TGA].
*   Cases of severe bone marrow failure (defined as WBC < 2,000 cells/mm³ or platelet count < 75,000 cells/mm³) not caused by the underlying malignancy [TGA].
*   **Administration Note:** ETOPOPHOS must not be administered by intra-cavity injection [TGA].

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Myelosuppression
The dose-limiting toxicity of ETOPOPHOS is bone marrow suppression, which may be fatal. Frequent monitoring of complete blood counts (CBC) including differential is required before, during, and after therapy. Treatment should be withheld if platelets fall below 50,000/mm³ or absolute neutrophil counts (ANC) fall below 500/mm³ until hematopoietic recovery occurs.

### Secondary Malignancies
The development of acute leukemia, with or without a preleukemic phase, has been observed in patients receiving etoposide, typically in combination with other antineoplastics. This occurs with a median latency of approximately 32 months [TGA].

### Hypersensitivity and Infusion Reactions
Anaphylactic-type reactions, including chills, fever, tachycardia, bronchospasm, and hypotension, may occur and can be fatal. If such a reaction occurs, the infusion must be terminated immediately and symptomatic treatment initiated. Cardiac arrest has been reported during infusion.

### Extravasation and Administration
ETOPOPHOS should only be administered by slow intravenous infusion; **bolus injection is strictly prohibited**. The infusion site must be monitored for infiltration. Extravasation can lead to severe soft tissue toxicity, swelling, cellulitis, and skin necrosis.

### Tumor Lysis Syndrome
Patients with bulky, treatment-sensitive tumors or renal insufficiency should be monitored for signs of tumor lysis syndrome, which may be fatal.

### Specific Excipient Warnings
The formulation contains Dextran 40, which has been associated with acute renal failure when administered in high doses [TGA]. Renal function should be assessed before and after high-dose therapy.

## 5. Dosage and Administration
### Standard Dosing
Dosage should be adjusted based on the myelosuppressive effects of concurrent medications or prior radiation/chemotherapy.
*   **Testicular Tumors:** 50 to 100 mg/m²/day on days 1–5, or 100 mg/m² on days 1, 3, and 5 [FDA].
*   **Small Cell Lung Cancer:** 35 mg/m²/day for 4 days or 50 mg/m²/day for 5 days [FDA].
*   **General Range [TGA]:** 50 to 100 mg/m²/day for 5 days or 100 to 150 mg/m²/day on days 1, 3, and 5 every 3 to 4 weeks.

### Special Populations
*   **Renal Impairment:** For patients with a creatinine clearance (ClCr) of 15–50 mL/min, the initial dose should be reduced to 75% of the standard dose. Data for ClCr < 15 mL/min is unavailable; further reduction should be considered.
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution in mild-to-moderate impairment. Contraindicated in severe hepatic dysfunction (bilirubin > 85 micromol/L) [TGA].

### Preparation
Reconstitute to a concentration of 10 mg/mL or 20 mg/mL using Sterile Water, 5% Glucose, or 0.9% Saline. The solution may be further diluted to as low as 0.1 mg/mL. Infusion duration can range from 5 to 210 minutes [TGA].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*   **Hematologic:** Neutropenia (most common), leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, and anemia. Nadirs typically occur 7–14 days after administration.
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea and vomiting (managed with antiemetics), mucositis, anorexia, diarrhea, and abdominal pain.
*   **Dermatologic:** Reversible alopecia is very common. Rare reactions include Stevens-Johnson Syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, and radiation recall dermatitis.
*   **Cardiovascular:** Transient hypotension or hypertension. Myocardial infarction and arrhythmia have been reported.
*   **Neurological:** Dizziness, peripheral neuropathy, and seizures (rarely associated with allergic reactions).
*   **Other:** Fever, chills, hepatotoxicity (elevated liver enzymes), and interstitial pneumonitis/pulmonary fibrosis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Concurrent use may lead to a significantly elevated INR; frequent monitoring is required.
*   **Cisplatin:** May reduce the total body clearance of etoposide.
*   **Cyclosporin:** High-dose concentrations (>2000 ng/mL) may increase etoposide exposure (AUC) by 80% [TGA].
*   **Antiepileptics:** Phenytoin, phenobarbital, and carbamazepine may increase etoposide clearance and reduce efficacy.
*   **Protein Binding:** Aspirin, sodium salicylate, and phenylbutazone can displace etoposide from plasma proteins, potentially increasing toxicity.
*   **Vaccinations:** Live virus vaccines should be avoided due to the risk of severe infection in immunosuppressed patients.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
ETOPOPHOS is classified as Category D [TGA] and can cause fetal harm. It is teratogenic based on animal studies. Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during treatment and for **6 months** after the final dose [TGA/FDA]. 

### Fertility and Males
Etoposide may cause irreversible infertility and testicular atrophy. Male patients with female partners of reproductive potential should use effective contraception for **4 months** [FDA] to **6 months** [TGA] after the final dose. Sperm preservation should be discussed prior to treatment.

### Lactation
It is unknown if etoposide is excreted in human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants, breastfeeding is not recommended during therapy.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** Etoposide is 97% protein-bound (primarily to albumin). It enters the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) poorly.
*   **Metabolism:** Etoposide phosphate is a prodrug converted to etoposide via dephosphorylation. Metabolism occurs in the liver via the CYP3A4 pathway.
*   **Excretion:** Approximately 56% of a dose is recovered in urine (45% as unchanged drug) and 44% in feces at 120 hours. The terminal elimination half-life ranges from 4 to 11 hours.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Etoposide phosphate acts as a topoisomerase inhibitor. It interacts with DNA-topoisomerase II, inducing DNA strand breaks and preventing repair. This lead to cell cycle arrest, primarily at the G2 stage, ultimately resulting in cell death.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Unopened Vials:** Store under refrigeration at 2°C to 8°C. Keep in the outer carton to protect from light.
*   **Reconstituted/Diluted Solutions:** Chemically stable at room temperature (20–25°C) for 24 hours (if using non-bacteriostatic diluents) or 48 hours (if using bacteriostatic diluents). 
*   **Microbiological Integrity:** To minimize infection risk, reconstituted products should be used immediately. If storage is required, maintain at 2°C to 8°C for no more than 24 hours [TGA]. Do not freeze.
