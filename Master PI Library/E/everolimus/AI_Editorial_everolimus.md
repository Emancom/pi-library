# Editorial Product Information: everolimus

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **everolimus**
- Chemical class: mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) inhibitor (rapamycin derivative)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 2010 [US]
- Most recent revision of the U.S. product information: February 2024 [US]
- Recent major change noted in source PI: Cannabidiol drug interactions updated September 2023 [US]
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources
- Registration/approval dates for jurisdictions other than the United States: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Prophylaxis of organ rejection in adult kidney transplant recipients at low–moderate immunologic risk, administered in combination with basiliximab induction, reduced-dose cyclosporine, and corticosteroids. [US]
- Prophylaxis of allograft rejection in adult liver transplant recipients when initiated no earlier than 30 days posttransplant, administered in combination with reduced-dose tacrolimus and corticosteroids. [US]
- Limitations of use:
  - Safety and efficacy not established in kidney transplant patients at high immunologic risk. [US]
  - Safety and efficacy not established for recipients of transplanted organs other than kidney or liver (use in other organ transplant recipients not established; heart transplantation is not recommended due to increased mortality observed in a clinical trial). [US]
  - Pediatric patients (<18 years): safety and efficacy not established. [US]

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Target therapeutic whole blood trough concentration (everolimus): 3–8 ng/mL measured by LC/MS/MS assay (note assay variability; communicate with the laboratory to identify assay used).
- Dose adjustments should be guided by trough concentrations measured 4–5 days after a dosing change.

### Dosing by indication
- Kidney transplant (adult, low–moderate immunologic risk): start everolimus 0.75 mg orally twice daily (1.5 mg/day) as soon as possible after transplantation; administer concurrently with basiliximab induction, reduced-dose cyclosporine, and corticosteroids. [US]
- Liver transplant (adult): start everolimus 1 mg orally twice daily (2 mg/day) no earlier than 30 days after transplantation; administer concurrently with reduced-dose tacrolimus and corticosteroids. [US]

### Therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) and titration
- If trough <3 ng/mL: dose adjustment required; total daily dose should be doubled using available strengths.
- If trough >8 ng/mL on two consecutive measures: decrease dose by 0.25 mg twice daily.
- Everolimus trough concentrations depend on assay methodology; recommendations are based on LC/MS/MS assay; values from different assays may not be interchangeable.
- Prior to reduction of cyclosporine or tacrolimus exposure, ensure steady-state everolimus trough concentration is at least 3 ng/mL.

### Cyclosporine (kidney transplant) therapeutic ranges when coadministered with everolimus [US]
- Through Month 1: 100–200 ng/mL
- Months 2–3: 75–150 ng/mL
- Month 4: 50–100 ng/mL
- Months 6–12: 25–50 ng/mL
- Cyclosporine should be initiated as soon as possible and no later than 48 hours after reperfusion; dose adjustments from Day 5 onward to achieve target troughs. Cyclosporine, USP Modified capsules twice daily preferred when possible. Prior to reducing cyclosporine, confirm everolimus trough ≥3 ng/mL. Everolimus concentrations may decrease if cyclosporine exposure is reduced. [US]

### Tacrolimus (liver transplant) therapeutic ranges when coadministered with everolimus [US]
- By ~3 weeks after first everolimus dose (approximately Month 2) and through Month 12: whole blood trough concentrations 3–5 ng/mL.
- Note: tacrolimus does not substantially affect everolimus trough concentrations; therefore everolimus concentrations do not decrease if tacrolimus exposure is reduced. [US]

### Hepatic impairment
- Mild hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh A): reduce initial daily dose by ~one-third.
- Moderate or severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh B or C): reduce initial daily dose by ~one-half.
- Monitor trough concentrations closely and adjust to achieve 3–8 ng/mL. [US]

### Administration instructions
- Swallow orally with a glass of water; do not crush.
- Administer approximately 12 hours apart, consistently with or without food.
- Give at the same time as cyclosporine or tacrolimus to minimize variability in absorption. [US]

### Missed dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to everolimus, sirolimus, or to any component of the formulation. [US]
- No additional region-specific contraindications are specified in the available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed/major warnings (presented in source PI) [US]
- **Malignancies and serious infections:** Immunosuppression increases susceptibility to infections and may increase the risk of malignancies (e.g., lymphoma, skin cancer). Only physicians experienced in immunosuppressive therapy and transplant management should prescribe and follow patients. Facilities should have appropriate laboratory and supportive resources.
- **Kidney graft thrombosis:** Increased risk of renal arterial and venous thrombosis, mostly within the first 30 days posttransplant, potentially resulting in graft loss.
- **Nephrotoxicity:** Combined use with standard-dose cyclosporine increases risk of nephrotoxicity; reduced cyclosporine doses are required when used with everolimus to reduce renal dysfunction. Monitor everolimus and calcineurin inhibitor trough concentrations.
- **Mortality in heart transplantation:** Increased mortality observed in a trial of de novo heart transplant patients treated with regimens including everolimus; use in heart transplantation is not recommended.

### General warnings and precautions
- Management of immunosuppression: risk of over-immunosuppression and acute rejection if calcineurin inhibition is completely eliminated; combination regimens should be used with caution. Only experienced physicians should manage therapy. [US]
- Lymphomas and other malignancies: monitor and counsel regarding skin cancer risk and sun exposure.
- Serious infections: bacterial, viral, fungal, protozoal, and opportunistic infections (including CMV and Pneumocystis jirovecii) can occur; prophylaxis for Pneumocystis jirovecii and CMV is recommended in transplant recipients. [US]
- Hepatic artery thrombosis (HAT): mTOR inhibitors associated with increased HAT risk, mostly within first 30 days posttransplant leading to graft loss or death; do not initiate before day 30 post-liver transplant. [US]
- Angioedema: increased risk with concomitant angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitors; monitor and treat promptly. [US]
- Delayed wound healing and fluid accumulation: monitor post-surgical wound healing; manage fluid accumulation to minimize complications. [US]
- Interstitial lung disease (ILD)/non-infectious pneumonitis: monitor for respiratory symptoms and radiologic changes; manage by dose reduction or discontinuation and consider corticosteroids. [US]
- Hyperlipidemia: elevations in serum cholesterol and triglycerides are common; monitor lipids and consider lipid-lowering therapy. [US]
- Proteinuria: risk increases with higher trough concentrations; monitor urine protein and adjust therapy as needed. [US]
- Polyoma virus (BK) infections and BK virus–associated nephropathy: monitor and consider reducing immunosuppression if BK virus infection suspected. [US]
- Thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA)/thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP)/hemolytic uremic syndrome (HUS): may occur and concomitant use with cyclosporine may increase risk; monitor hematologic parameters and symptoms. [US]
- New onset diabetes after transplantation: monitor glucose. [US]
- Male infertility: azoospermia or oligospermia may occur; counsel male patients of reproductive potential. [US]
- Immunizations: avoid live vaccines in patients receiving everolimus. [US]
- Interaction with grapefruit juice: avoid grapefruit juice (interaction noted). [US]
- Cannabidiol: therapeutic drug monitoring and dose reduction of everolimus should be considered when coadministered with cannabidiol. [US]

### Monitoring requirements
- Routine monitoring of everolimus whole blood trough concentrations (target 3–8 ng/mL by LC/MS/MS) for all patients.
- Monitor calcineurin inhibitor (cyclosporine, tacrolimus) trough concentrations and adjust dosing to recommended reduced targets when coadministered.
- Monitor renal function (serum creatinine, GFR), urine protein, complete blood counts, lipid profile, blood glucose, signs of infection, hepatic artery patency (post-liver transplant early period), respiratory symptoms for ILD, wound healing status, and for signs of thrombotic microangiopathy. [US]

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Everolimus is metabolized via CYP3A4 and is a substrate for P-glycoprotein; strong and moderate inhibitors and inducers of CYP3A4 and P-gp may affect everolimus concentrations. Examples include:
  - Strong or moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors: ketoconazole, erythromycin, verapamil (may increase everolimus concentrations; consider dose adjustment). [US]
  - Strong CYP3A4 inducers: rifampin (may decrease everolimus concentrations; consider dose adjustment). [US]
- Cyclosporine: cyclosporine affects everolimus pharmacokinetics; everolimus concentrations may decrease if cyclosporine exposure is reduced. Coadministration requires reduced cyclosporine target trough concentrations to reduce nephrotoxicity. Monitor both everolimus and cyclosporine troughs and adjust doses. [US]
- Tacrolimus: little to no pharmacokinetic interaction of tacrolimus on everolimus; everolimus concentrations do not decrease if tacrolimus exposure is reduced. Nevertheless, reduced tacrolimus doses and monitoring are recommended to minimize nephrotoxicity when used with everolimus. [US]
- Cannabidiol: coadministration may interact with everolimus; therapeutic drug monitoring and dose reduction of everolimus should be considered. [US]
- Grapefruit juice: interaction noted—avoid grapefruit juice. [US]

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Concomitant use with calcineurin inhibitors (cyclosporine or tacrolimus) increases nephrotoxicity risk; reduce calcineurin inhibitor exposure when coadministered with everolimus and monitor renal function closely. [US]
- Concomitant use with ACE inhibitors may increase risk of angioedema; monitor. [US]

### Other interactions and notes
- Specific interactions with statins, midazolam, octreotide, and other substrates/inhibitors/inducers are listed in the full PI; details in the available excerpt are limited to the interaction classes and select examples. For drugs that are strong/moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors/inducers or P-gp substrates/inhibitors, consider therapeutic drug monitoring and dose adjustments as clinically indicated. [US]

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Embryo-fetal toxicity: based on animal data, everolimus may cause maternal and fetal harm. Females of reproductive potential should be advised of potential fetal risk and to use effective contraception during treatment and for 8 weeks after the final dose. [US]
- Pregnancy category/classifications in jurisdictions other than the United States: Not specified in available sources.

### Breastfeeding
- Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment with everolimus. [US]

### Fertility
- Everolimus may impair fertility; male infertility manifested as azoospermia or oligospermia has been reported. Counsel patients of reproductive potential accordingly. [US]

## 10. Use in Special Populations

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Everolimus is an inhibitor of mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) and acts as an immunosuppressant.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: oral administration; give consistently with or without food to reduce variability.
- Metabolism: primarily via CYP3A4 and is a substrate of P-glycoprotein.
- Distribution and elimination: specific numeric parameters (bioavailability, half-life, volume of distribution, clearance) are not specified in the available sources.
- Assay considerations: recommended therapeutic range (3–8 ng/mL) is based on LC/MS/MS assay; other assay types (chromatographic or immunoassay) may yield non-interchangeable values. [US]
- Effect of hepatic impairment: hepatic impairment increases exposure; dosing reductions recommended (see Section 4). [US]

### Pharmacodynamics
- Immunosuppressive activity via inhibition of mTOR signaling pathways involved in lymphocyte proliferation; details of dose–response relationships not specified in the available sources.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Key clinical program context:
  - Kidney transplant: indicated for prophylaxis of organ rejection in adult patients at low–moderate immunologic risk, in combination with basiliximab induction, reduced-dose cyclosporine, and corticosteroids. Clinical trials informed recommended dosing and cyclosporine trough target ranges; median cyclosporine troughs observed in trials are described in the PI (example ranges: 161–185 ng/mL through Month 1; 111–140 ng/mL at Months 2–3; median 99 ng/mL at Month 4; 46–75 ng/mL during Months 6–12). [US]
  - Liver transplant: indicated for prophylaxis of allograft rejection when initiated ≥30 days posttransplant with reduced-dose tacrolimus and corticosteroids; median tacrolimus trough concentrations observed in trials are reported (examples: 8.6–9.5 ng/mL at Weeks 2–4 prior to everolimus; ~7–8.1 ng/mL Weeks 5–6; 5.2–5.6 ng/mL at Months 2–3; 4.3–4.9 ng/mL Months 4–12). [US]
  - Heart transplant: a de novo heart transplant clinical trial showed increased mortality (often associated with serious infections) within the first 3 months posttransplant in regimens including everolimus; use in heart transplantation is not recommended. [US]
