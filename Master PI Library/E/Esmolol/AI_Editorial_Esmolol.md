# Editorial Product Information: Esmolol Hydrochloride

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Esmolol hydrochloride
- **Chemical Class:** Beta₁-selective (cardioselective) adrenoceptor blocking agent.
- **Brand Names:** **Brevibloc** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** C07AB09 [Not explicitly listed in source; derived from class standard]
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Injection:** 100 mg/10 mL (10 mg/mL) ready-to-use vial [Australian].
    - **Powder for Injection:** 2.5 g vial (concentrate for infusion after reconstitution and dilution) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Phebra Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Supraventricular Tachycardia (SVT):** Rapid control of ventricular rate in patients with atrial fibrillation or atrial flutter in perioperative, postoperative, or other emergent settings where short-term control with a short-acting agent is preferred [Australian].
- **Non-compensatory Sinus Tachycardia:** Indicated when the physician determines the rapid heart rate requires specific intervention [Australian].
- **Note:** Esmolol is intended for short-term use (typically <24 hours) and is not for use in chronic settings [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Esmolol must be individualised via titration. Each step involves a loading dose followed by a maintenance dose.

### Titration Schedule (SVT)
1. **Initial Loading Dose:** 500 microgram/kg/min (0.5 mg/kg/min) administered over 1 minute.
2. **Maintenance Infusion:** 50 microgram/kg/min (0.05 mg/kg/min) for 4 minutes.
3. **Assessment:** If the therapeutic effect is adequate, maintain the 50 microgram/kg/min rate. If inadequate after 5 minutes, repeat the loading dose (500 microgram/kg/min for 1 minute) and increase the maintenance rate to 100 microgram/kg/min.
4. **Escalation:** Continue titration by repeating the loading dose and increasing the maintenance infusion in 50 microgram/kg/min increments. 
5. **Limits:** Maintenance doses above 200 microgram/kg/min (0.2 mg/kg/min) are generally not recommended as they provide little added benefit but increase adverse effect risks. Dosages up to 300 microgram/kg/min have been used but safety beyond this is not established [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Ready-to-use (10 mg/mL):** May be used for loading doses via hand-held syringe and for maintenance.
- **2.5 g Powder:** **MUST NOT BE GIVEN BY DIRECT IV INJECTION.** It must be reconstituted and then diluted to a final concentration of 10 mg/mL (e.g., 2.5 g in 250 mL) before infusion [Australian].
- **Transitioning to Oral Agents:** Once heart rate is controlled, esmolol should be tapered. Thirty minutes after the first dose of an alternative agent (e.g., propranolol, digoxin), reduce the esmolol rate by 50%. Following the second dose of the oral agent, monitor response; if stable, discontinue esmolol after one hour [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to esmolol hydrochloride or any excipients.
- Severe sinus bradycardia (less than 50 beats per minute).
- Heart block greater than first degree (second or third-degree AV block).
- Cardiogenic shock or overt heart failure.
- Sick sinus syndrome.
- Severe AV-nodal conductance disorders (without a pacemaker).
- Severe hypotension.
- Untreated phaeochromocytoma.
- Pulmonary hypertension.
- Acute asthma attack.
- Metabolic acidosis.
- Concomitant IV verapamil or use in close proximity (risk of fatal cardiac arrest) [Australian].
- Requirement for inotropic agents or vasopressors to maintain blood pressure/output [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypotension:** Occurs in 20–50% of patients. It is dose-related and usually resolves within 30 minutes of dose reduction or discontinuation. Continuous ECG and blood pressure monitoring are mandatory [Australian].
- **Cardiac Failure:** Beta-blockade may precipitate or worsen heart failure. Discontinue at the first sign of impending failure.
- **Bronchospastic Diseases:** Generally, patients with asthma or COPD should not receive beta-blockers. Due to its beta₁-selectivity, esmolol may be used with extreme caution and careful titration, but should be stopped immediately if bronchospasm occurs [Australian].
- **Diabetes and Hypoglycaemia:** Use with caution; beta-blockers may mask tachycardia associated with hypoglycaemia [Australian].
- **Infusion Site Reactions:** Concentrations >10 mg/mL are associated with significant venous irritation, thrombophlebitis, and skin necrosis from extravasation. Avoid small veins and butterfly needles [Australian].
- **Abrupt Discontinuation:** While withdrawal effects are less common due to short half-life, caution is advised in patients with coronary artery disease [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Most effects are mild and transient.
- **Very Common (>10%):** Asymptomatic hypotension (~25%), symptomatic hypotension (12%; dizziness, diaphoresis).
- **Common (1–10%):** Dizziness (3%), somnolence (3%), nausea (7%), infusion site reactions (~8%), confusion, headache, agitation, fatigue, paraesthesia, asthenia.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Peripheral ischaemia, bradycardia (<50 bpm), chest pain, pulmonary oedema, heart block, vomiting, dyspnoea, bronchospasm, wheezing.
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Seizures, syncope, asystole, severe bradycardia, sinus pause.
- **Post-Marketing Reports:** Death, cardiac arrest, hypertension, idioventricular rhythm, renal failure, and metabolic acidosis [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Calcium Channel Blockers:** Verapamil and diltiazem increase risks of depression in contractility and AV conduction. Fatal arrests have occurred with IV verapamil [Australian]. 
- **Dihydropyridines (e.g., nifedipine):** Increase hypotension risk.
- **Catecholamine-depleting agents (e.g., reserpine):** Additive effects; monitor for marked bradycardia or vertigo.
- **Digoxin:** Esmolol may increase digoxin blood levels by 10–20%; digoxin may increase AV conduction time.
- **Morphine:** IV morphine increases esmolol steady-state levels by 46% [Australian].
- **Succinylcholine:** Esmolol prolongs the duration of neuromuscular blockade (from ~5 to 8 minutes).
- **Clonidine:** Increases risk of rebound hypertension upon withdrawal.
- **Sympathomimetics:** May counteract beta-blocking effects [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C [Australian].** No adequate studies in pregnant women. Use only if potential benefit outweighs risk to the foetus. Beta-blockers may cause foetal/neonatal bradycardia.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if esmolol is excreted in human milk; lactation is not recommended during use [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No specific human studies available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children (<18 years) have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Use with caution, typically starting at the lower end of the dosing range; however, tolerance is generally good [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** The acid metabolite is primarily excreted by the kidneys. In end-stage renal disease, the metabolite's half-life increases ten-fold, and plasma levels are significantly elevated. Use with caution [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Metabolism is mediated by red blood cell esterases, not the liver; therefore, hepatic impairment does not significantly affect esmolol pharmacokinetics [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selectively blocks beta₁-adrenergic receptors in cardiac muscle. It has no significant intrinsic sympathomimetic or membrane-stabilising activity at therapeutic doses [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Rapid onset of beta-blockade with substantial recovery within 10–20 minutes of discontinuation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** 55% protein bound; acid metabolite is 10% bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly hydrolysed by esterases in the cytosol of red blood cells (not plasma cholinesterases).
    - **Elimination:** Distribution half-life ~2 minutes; elimination half-life ~9 minutes. Less than 2% is excreted unchanged in urine; the acid metabolite is excreted via the kidneys with a half-life of ~3.7 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Supraventricular Tachycardia:** Clinical trials involving 369 patients with SVT and over 600 surgical patients demonstrated rapid control of heart rate. Responses typically occurred within the 50–200 microgram/kg/min range [Australian].
- **Cardioselectivity:** Studies in mildly asthmatic patients showed no significant increase in airway resistance at therapeutic doses, unlike propranolol [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Reconstituted/Diluted Solution:** Physiochemically stable for 24 hours at room temperature; however, for microbiological reasons, use immediately. If necessary, store at 2–8°C for no more than 24 hours [Australian].
