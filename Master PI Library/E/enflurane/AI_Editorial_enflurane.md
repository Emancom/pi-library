# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is intended for informational purposes and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule involved in long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative [(R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt].
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 or 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours) over the first 24 hours [US].
    - Subsequent daily doses typically remain in the 50 mg/kg range, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
    - Administration should occur as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].

### Monitoring and Titration
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Dosing should be guided by weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine level: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].
- **ESRD:** Therapy may be initiated if trough (pre-dialysis) plasma carnitine levels are below the normal range (40 to 50 µmol/L). Dose adjustments (e.g., downward to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of treatment [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use [US].
- Discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. In the event of a severe reaction, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine has been associated with reports of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with an established history, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Caution):** While this document focuses on the injectable form, clinicians should note that chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disorders.

| System Organ Class | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Transient nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, diarrhea, gastritis, and body odor [US]. |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, arrhythmia, chest pain [US]. |
| **Neurological** | Headache, dizziness, paresthesia, seizures (post-marketing) [US]. |
| **General/Injection Site** | Injection site reaction (common), fever, asthenia, pain [US]. |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia, hyperkalemia [US]. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels upon initiation or dose adjustment [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specifically lettered in provided text]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after exogenous administration. Benefits to the mother should be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg) for metabolic disorders; safety and efficacy follow adult parameters for indicated conditions [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically in ESRD patients post-dialysis. Accumulation of metabolites is a concern with *oral* administration in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Distribution half-life is approximately 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Study (ESRD):** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg after dialysis (3x/week) showed that trough concentrations stabilized between 54 and 180 µmol/L after one week of therapy [US].
- **Efficacy Note:** While IV levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the definitive effect on clinical outcomes in this specific population has not been fully determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Stability (Admixture):** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
