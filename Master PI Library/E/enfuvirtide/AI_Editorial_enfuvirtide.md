# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the United States regulatory documentation was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN), chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. It is a carrier molecule involved in mitochondrial fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn metabolic errors [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - **Administration:** Delivered as a slow bolus (2–3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - **Severe Crisis:** A loading dose may be given, followed by equivalent doses every 3 or 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg may be required based on clinical response [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - **Administration:** Delivered as a slow bolus (2–3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Adjustments:** Dosage should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Monitoring
- It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels.
- Monitoring should include weekly and monthly assessments of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine levels: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
- Discard any unused portion; the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, treatment must be discontinued and appropriate medical intervention initiated [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine (oral or intravenous) has been associated with reports of seizures. In patients with pre-existing seizure disorders, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Formulation in Renal Impairment:** The safety of **oral** levocarnitine has not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are usually excreted in the urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects
In clinical trials involving ESRD patients, the following effects were reported with a frequency of ≥5% (regardless of causality):
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (21%), injection site reaction (38%), pain (35%), fever (12%), and asthenia (12%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (21%), hypotension (19%), and tachycardia (9%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (35%), vomiting (21%), nausea (12%), and anorexia (6%).
- **Neurologic:** Headache (37%), dizziness (18%), and paresthesia (12%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (27%), cough (18%), and dyspnea (11%).

### Other Reported Effects
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (15%), hypervolemia (12%).
- **Special Senses:** Taste perversion (9%).
- **General:** Transient nausea and vomiting; body odor and gastritis occur less frequently [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels in patients on warfarin therapy after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not assigned to a specific letter category in provided text]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. While studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine levels after administration, the risks to the infant from excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug should be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders are applicable to paediatric patients (see Section 3).
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the available source [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Primary use is indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. Note the warning regarding toxic metabolite accumulation with **oral** dosing in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly after dialysis showed mean pre-dialysis trough levocarnitine levels increased from 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L at baseline to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** In ESRD patients, doses of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg were studied over 24 weeks. Trough concentrations increased in a dose-proportional manner, reaching 148 ± 50, 240 ± 60, and 456 ± 162 µmol/L respectively [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s in PVC bags [US].
