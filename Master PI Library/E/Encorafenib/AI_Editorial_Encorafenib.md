# Editorial Product Information: Encorafenib

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the official Product Information (PI) for **encorafenib**, specifically structured for use by healthcare professionals. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial summary and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the local approved PI for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Encorafenib
- **Chemical Class:** ATP-competitive small molecule RAF kinase inhibitor
- **Brand Name:** **BRAFTOVI** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** L01EC03
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
  - Hard capsules: 50 mg and 75 mg [Australian]
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pierre Fabre Australia Pty Limited [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
**Encorafenib** is indicated for use in adult patients for the following conditions:

- **Unresectable or Metastatic Melanoma:** In combination with binimetinib for patients with a *BRAF* V600E or V600K mutation, as confirmed by a validated test [Australian].
- **Metastatic Colorectal Cancer (mCRC):** In combination with cetuximab for patients with a *BRAF* V600E mutation who have received prior systemic therapy [Australian].
- **Metastatic Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC):** In combination with binimetinib for patients with a *BRAF* V600E mutation [Australian].

*Note: Encorafenib is not indicated for patients with wild-type BRAF tumours.*

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Melanoma and NSCLC:** The standard dose is 450 mg (six 75 mg capsules) once daily, administered in combination with binimetinib [Australian].
- **Colorectal Cancer:** The standard dose is 300 mg (four 75 mg capsules) once daily, administered in combination with cetuximab [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Capsules must be swallowed whole with water and may be taken with or without food.
- **Dispersion Option:** For patients with swallowing difficulties, capsules may be opened and the contents dispersed in approximately 20 mL of apple sauce, to be consumed immediately [Australian].
- **Avoidance:** Concomitant administration with grapefruit juice must be avoided [Australian].

### Missed Doses and Vomiting
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, it should only be taken if the next scheduled dose is more than 12 hours away.
- **Vomiting:** If vomiting occurs after administration, an additional dose should not be taken; the patient should resume with the next scheduled dose [Australian].

### Dose Modifications
- Management of adverse reactions (e.g., QTc prolongation, uveitis, liver abnormalities) may require dose reduction, temporary interruption, or permanent discontinuation.
- For Melanoma/NSCLC, the sequential reduction levels are 300 mg, then 225 mg. Discontinue if 225 mg is not tolerated.
- For mCRC, the sequential reduction levels are 225 mg, then 150 mg. Discontinue if 150 mg is not tolerated [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to **encorafenib** or any of its excipients (e.g., gelatin, microcrystalline cellulose, copovidone) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **New Primary Malignancies:** Cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma (cuSCC) and new primary melanomas have been observed. Dermatological evaluation is required before starting therapy, every 2 months during treatment, and for 6 months post-discontinuation. Non-cutaneous malignancies (RAS mutation-positive) may also occur [Australian].
- **Haemorrhage:** Major haemorrhagic events, including gastrointestinal and intracranial bleeds, have been reported. Risk increases with concurrent anticoagulants or antiplatelet agents [Australian].
- **Ocular Toxicities:** Uveitis, iritis, and iridocyclitis may occur. Patients should be monitored for visual disturbances at every visit [Australian].
- **QT Prolongation:** **Encorafenib** may increase the QTc interval. Electrolytes (Mg, K, Ca) must be corrected before and during treatment. ECG monitoring is required at baseline, one month after initiation, and then every 3 months [Australian].
- **Left Ventricular Dysfunction (LVD):** Reported when used with binimetinib. LVEF should be assessed before and during treatment [Australian].
- **Tumour Lysis Syndrome (TLS):** Risk is higher in patients with high tumour burden or renal insufficiency. Prophylactic hydration should be considered [Australian].
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Liver function abnormalities are common. LFTs should be monitored monthly for the first 6 months [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequencies vary by indication and combination regimen.

### Very Common (>10%)
- **General:** Fatigue, pyrexia, peripheral oedema.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation, abdominal pain.
- **Skin:** Hyperkeratosis, rash, alopecia, dry skin, pruritus, Palmar-plantar erythrodysaesthesia syndrome (PPES).
- **Musculoskeletal:** Arthralgia, myalgia, pain in extremity.
- **Neurological:** Headache, dizziness, dysgeusia, peripheral neuropathy.
- **Investigations:** Increased blood creatinine, increased CK, increased transaminases (AST/ALT), increased GGT, anaemia [Australian].

### Common (1–10%)
- Facial paresis, uveitis, visual impairment, hypertension, venous thromboembolism (VTE), pancreatitis, skin papilloma [Australian].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Haemorrhage (including fatal intracranial/gastric events), cardiac failure (LVD), renal failure, and TLS [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Strong inhibitors (e.g., ritonavir, itraconazole, clarithromycin, grapefruit juice) increase **encorafenib** exposure and should be avoided. If essential, monitor safety closely [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Strong inducers (e.g., rifampicin, carbamazepine, St. John's Wort) may decrease efficacy and should be avoided.
- **Effect of Encorafenib on other drugs:** **Encorafenib** is a strong inducer of CYP3A4 (e.g., may reduce midazolam AUC by 82%) and can reduce the efficacy of hormonal contraceptives [Australian].
- **Transporters:** **Encorafenib** inhibits OATP1B1, OATP1B3, and BCRP. Use caution with substrates such as rosuvastatin, atorvastatin, and methotrexate [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category D. **Encorafenib** has demonstrated embryo-foetal toxicity and malformations in animal studies. It should not be used in pregnancy unless the benefit outweighs the risk to the foetus [Australian].
- **Contraception:** Women of childbearing potential must use effective non-hormonal contraception (e.g., barrier methods) during and for at least 1 month after the last dose [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if **encorafenib** is excreted in human milk. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug is recommended [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal data suggest potential impaired spermatogenesis in males. Men should be informed of this risk [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients <18 years have not been established; no data are available [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment is required for patients ≥65 years. Data for those ≥75 years are limited [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
  - **Mild (Child-Pugh A):** Use with caution at a reduced dose of 300 mg once daily.
  - **Moderate/Severe (Child-Pugh B/C):** No dosing recommendation; use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild-to-moderate impairment. Use with caution in severe renal impairment as no clinical data exist [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
**Encorafenib** is an ATP-competitive RAF kinase inhibitor targeting *BRAF* V600E, *BRAF* V600D, and *BRAF* V600K. It inhibits the RAF/MEK/ERK pathway, suppressing tumour cell proliferation in *BRAF*-mutated cancers. It has a long dissociation half-life (>30 hours), leading to prolonged target inhibition [Australian].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; median Tmax is 1.5 to 2 hours. High-fat meals reduce Cmax but do not change AUC.
- **Distribution:** 86.1% plasma protein bound.
- **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP3A4 (approx. 83%), with minor contributions from CYP2C19 and CYP2D6.
- **Elimination:** Excreted equally in faeces and urine; mean terminal half-life is 6.32 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **COLUMBUS (Melanoma):** Phase III trial showed that **encorafenib** (450 mg) + binimetinib significantly improved median PFS (14.9 months) compared to vemurafenib (7.3 months). Median OS was 33.6 months for the combination [Australian].
- **BEACON CRC (Colorectal):** Phase III trial demonstrated that **encorafenib** (300 mg) + cetuximab significantly improved OS (median 9.3 months) compared to the control arm (median 5.9 months) in previously treated *BRAF* V600E mCRC [Australian].
- **PHAROS (NSCLC):** Phase II trial showed an Objective Response Rate (ORR) of 75% in treatment-naïve patients and 46% in previously treated patients when used with binimetinib [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C.
- Keep in the original container to protect from moisture [Australian].
