# Editorial Product Information: Enasidenib

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of enasidenib for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis designed for reference and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Enasidenib (as mesylate).
- **Chemical Class:** Isocitrate dehydrogenase-2 (IDH2) inhibitor.
- **Brand Name(s):** **IDHIFA** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
  - Film-coated tablets: 50 mg and 100 mg [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Celgene Corporation (a Bristol Myers Squibb company) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML):** Treatment of adult patients with relapsed or refractory AML who have an isocitrate dehydrogenase-2 (IDH2) mutation, as confirmed by an approved diagnostic test [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The standard dose is 100 mg taken orally once daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
  - Tablets should be swallowed whole; do not chew, split, or crush [US].
  - May be taken with or without food [US].
  - Administer at approximately the same time each day [US].
  - For patients without disease progression or unacceptable toxicity, treatment should continue for a minimum of 6 months to allow for a clinical response [US].
- **Missed Dose Instructions:**
  - If a dose is missed or vomited, it should be taken as soon as possible on the same day [US].
  - The patient should return to the normal schedule the following day. Do not administer two doses at once to make up for a missed dose [US].
- **Dosage Modifications for Toxicity:**
  - **Differentiation Syndrome:** If suspected, initiate systemic corticosteroids and hemodynamic monitoring. Interrupt enasidenib if severe pulmonary symptoms (requiring intubation/ventilator) or renal dysfunction persist >48 hours after starting steroids. Resume at 100 mg daily once symptoms improve to Grade 2 or lower [US].
  - **Noninfectious Leukocytosis (WBC >30 × 10⁹/L):** Initiate hydroxyurea. Interrupt enasidenib if not improved; resume at 100 mg daily once WBC <30 × 10⁹/L [US].
  - **Elevated Bilirubin (>3x ULN for ≥2 weeks without transaminase elevation):** Reduce dose to 50 mg daily. Resume 100 mg daily if bilirubin resolves to <2x ULN [US].
  - **Other Grade 3+ Toxicities (including Tumor Lysis Syndrome):** Interrupt until resolved to Grade 2 or lower. Resume at 50 mg daily; may escalate to 100 mg if toxicity stays at Grade 1 or lower. Discontinue if Grade 3+ toxicity recurs [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Differentiation Syndrome [US]:** 
  - Approximately 14% of patients treated with enasidenib experience differentiation syndrome, which can be fatal. 
  - **Symptoms:** Fever, dyspnea, acute respiratory distress, pulmonary infiltrates, pleural/pericardial effusions, rapid weight gain, peripheral edema, lymphadenopathy, bone pain, and multi-organ dysfunction.
  - **Management:** High-dose corticosteroids (e.g., dexamethasone 10 mg every 12 hours) must be initiated immediately upon suspicion. Taper only after resolution.
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Enasidenib can cause fetal harm. Females of reproductive potential must use effective non-hormonal contraception during treatment and for 2 months after the final dose [US].
- **Tumor Lysis Syndrome (TLS):** Rapid reduction in tumor cells may induce TLS. Monitor blood chemistries (especially uric acid) at least every 2 weeks for the first 3 months [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Very Common (>10%)
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea (50%), diarrhea (43%), vomiting (34%), decreased appetite (34%) [US].
- **Hepatobiliary:** Elevated bilirubin (81%; Grade 3-4 in 15%) [US].
- **Metabolic/Laboratory:** Decreased calcium (74%), decreased potassium (41%), decreased phosphorus (27%) [US].
- **Other:** Differentiation syndrome (14%), noninfectious leukocytosis (12%), dysgeusia (12%) [US].

### Common (1–10%)
- Tumor lysis syndrome (6%) [US].
- Other reported effects: Neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, anemia, stomatitis, renal failure, fatigue, dyspnea, and QT prolongation [US].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Serious reactions occurred in 77% of patients. Most frequent: leukocytosis (10%), diarrhea (6%), nausea (5%), and differentiation syndrome (8%) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP1A2 Substrates:** Enasidenib is an inhibitor; monitor for increased toxicity of substrates (e.g., caffeine) [US].
- **CYP2C19 Substrates:** Enasidenib is an inhibitor; monitor for increased substrate exposure [US].
- **CYP3A Substrates:** Enasidenib is an inducer. Co-administration may reduce substrate efficacy (e.g., hormonal contraceptives, antifungals). Avoid concomitant use of sensitive CYP3A substrates [US].
- **Transporters:** Enasidenib inhibits OATP1B1, OATP1B3, BCRP, and P-gp. Monitor for increased toxicity of substrates (e.g., rosuvastatin, digoxin) and consider dose reductions for the substrate if necessary [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** No human data available. Animal studies show embryo-fetal toxicity at 0.1 times clinical exposure. Pregnancy testing is required before initiation [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Avoid breastfeeding during treatment and for at least 2 months after the final dose due to potential serious adverse reactions in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal data suggests enasidenib may impair male and female fertility; reversibility is unknown [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment required. No significant differences in safety/efficacy were observed between patients ≥65 years and younger patients [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No clinically meaningful effect on pharmacokinetics was observed in patients with mild-to-moderate renal impairment (CrCl ≥30 mL/min) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No clinically meaningful effect on pharmacokinetics was observed in patients with mild (Child-Pugh A), moderate (Child-Pugh B), or severe (Child-Pugh C) hepatic impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selectively inhibits mutated IDH2 enzymes (R140Q, R172S, R172K), reducing levels of the oncometabolite 2-hydroxyglutarate (2-HG) and promoting myeloid differentiation [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** 57% absolute bioavailability; Tmax is 4 hours [US].
  - **Distribution:** 98.5% protein bound; Vd is 55.8 L [US].
  - **Metabolism:** Primary metabolism via multiple CYP enzymes (1A2, 2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 3A4) and UGTs (1A1, 1A3, 1A4, 1A9, 2B7, 2B15) [US].
  - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is 7.9 days. Excretion is primarily fecal (89%) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study AG221-C-001:** An open-label, single-arm trial of 199 adults with relapsed/refractory IDH2-mutated AML.
- **Outcomes:** The complete response (CR) rate was 19% with a median duration of 8.2 months. The rate of CR plus CR with partial hematologic recovery (CRh) was 23%. Among patients dependent on transfusions at baseline, 34% achieved transfusion independence [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Keep the bottle tightly closed and store in the original container with the desiccant canister to protect from moisture [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only Medicine (Rx-only) [US].
