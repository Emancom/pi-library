# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview designed for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical and regulatory data into a concise reference format.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official, full Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labelling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** A naturally occurring carrier molecule (amino acid derivative) required for mammalian energy metabolism.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Administer via the intravenous route only.
- Parenteral products should be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
- **Severe Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the next 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours.
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are recommended at 50 mg/kg or as clinically required. The maximum recorded dose is 300 mg/kg.

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### Monitoring
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations.
- Monitor weekly and monthly, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target free carnitine level: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including **anaphylaxis**, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, particularly in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If severe reactions occur, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate medical management.
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Periodic monitoring of plasma carnitine levels and clinical status is essential to guide dosing.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Analysis (Hemodialysis Population)
In clinical trials of ESRD patients, the following events occurred at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality):

| Body System | Adverse Event (Frequency ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%), Flu syndrome (25%), Headache (22%), Infection (15%), Abdominal pain (9%), Asthenia (9%), Fever (7%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%), Palpitations (5%) |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%), Nausea (8%), Anorexia (5%), Dyspepsia (5%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (23%), Cough increase (9%), Rhinitis (9%), Dyspnea (7%) |
| **Neurologic** | Dizziness (13%), Paresthesia (5%), Insomnia (4%) |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia (9%), Anemia (6%), Hyperkalemia (6%), Hypervolemia (5%) |

### Serious and Notable Reactions
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm.
- **Neurologic:** Seizures.
- **General:** Transient nausea, vomiting, and body odor have been observed.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not assigned a specific letter category in the current text, but classified by old standards as follows]: Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate human studies exist. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations after exogenous administration. Weigh the benefits of maternal supplementation against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant. Discontinuation of nursing or treatment may be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders apply to paediatric patients (see Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the available source; however, general monitoring of renal function is advised in older populations.
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Injectable Form:** Used specifically for ESRD patients on dialysis.
    - **Oral Form Precaution:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in renal impairment may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites: trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, a dose of 20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine from 21.3 µmol/L (baseline) to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks.
- **Clinical Outcomes:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the impact on specific clinical outcomes (e.g., malaise, muscle weakness) has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives.
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
