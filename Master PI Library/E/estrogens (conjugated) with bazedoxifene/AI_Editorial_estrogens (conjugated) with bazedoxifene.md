# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory requirements to support safe and effective use.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of preparation, only the United States (FDA) source document was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP). It is a carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane.
- **Chemical Class:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US]. 
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the next 24 hours. Administration should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though therapy may require higher doses. The maximum recorded dose is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight, administered as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of treatment [US].

### General Administration
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Monitor weekly and monthly, including blood chemistry and vital signs. Target plasma free carnitine concentrations are between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Inspection:** Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, particularly in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of IV administration. If severe reactions occur, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizures:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment & Oral Metabolites:** The safety of *oral* levocarnitine has not been established in patients with renal insufficiency. High doses of oral formulations in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in urine [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not assigned a specific letter category in the provided source]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is naturally present in human milk. Supplemental levocarnitine in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. In dairy cows, levocarnitine concentrations in milk increase following exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg body weight (refer to Section 3). Specific age-related approval ranges are not further specified in available sources [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** IV levocarnitine is indicated for ESRD patients. However, high-dose *oral* therapy is cautioned due to metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Excretion:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Carnitine deficiency is biochemically defined as plasma concentrations <20 µmol/L at one week post-term [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis showed that trough levocarnitine levels increased from ~20 µmol/L to between 54 and 180 µmol/L within one week. While IV levocarnitine effectively increases plasma carnitine concentrations, the direct effect on clinical outcomes (signs/symptoms of deficiency) in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservative [US].
