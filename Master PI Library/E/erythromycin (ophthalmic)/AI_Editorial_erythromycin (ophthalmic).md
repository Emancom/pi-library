# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises available regulatory data to provide a concise clinical reference.
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis and should not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the United States (FDA) source documentation was provided for this synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt)
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Not specified in source [US: Levocarnitine Injection, USP; generic available via Baxter Healthcare].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic management of patients with metabolic defects resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients undergoing chronic hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
    - A loading dose is often employed in severe metabolic crises, followed by equivalent dosing over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours (q3h or q4h) and never less frequent than every 6 hours (q6h) [US].
    - Maximum reported dose: 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - Administration: Slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - Dose adjustments (e.g., reduction to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of IV administration. Discontinue immediately if severe reactions occur [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in both patients with and without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known epilepsy, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** Chronic administration of high oral doses in patients with severely impaired renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted renally [US]. Note: This precaution specifically highlights oral administration risks in renal impairment.
- **Monitoring:** In metabolic disorders, monitor plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L), blood chemistries, and vital signs weekly/monthly [US]. In ESRD, monitor trough concentrations (normal range: 40 to 50 µmol/L) to guide dosing [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **General/Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting are common. Gastritis and a "fishy" body odor have also been reported [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. Monitor INR levels following the initiation of treatment or dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not specified by letter, but stated "should be used during pregnancy only if clearly needed"]. Animal reproductive studies (rats/rabbits) at 3.8 times the human dose revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. There are no adequate/controlled studies in pregnant women [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine should be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No mutagenic effects were observed in standard tests (*Salmonella*, *Saccharomyces*, *Schizosaccharomyces*). Long-term animal carcinogenicity studies have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg) as detailed in Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Safely used in ESRD patients on dialysis (primary indication). Safety/efficacy of chronic oral levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound; mean distribution half-life of 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Excretion:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
    - **Bioavailability:** Oral tablets and solution are bioequivalent (approx. 15% absolute bioavailability) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** Clinical data demonstrated that 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean plasma levocarnitine levels from ~20 µmol/L (pre-dialysis) to steady-state trough levels of ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) showed dose-proportional increases in trough concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
