# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Note:** Information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, UK, Canadian, Japanese) was not provided in the source material. Where information is absent across the provided source, it is noted as "Not specified in available sources."
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a synthesis for clinical reference and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/generic name); a carrier molecule involved in mitochondrial fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative [(R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium].
- **Brand Name(s):** Not specified in source [US: Levocarnitine Injection, USP].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose: 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
    - In severe metabolic crises: A loading dose may be given, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - Administration frequency: Every 3 to 4 hours (q3h or q4h), and never less than every 6 hours (q6h) [US].
    - Maximum recorded dose: 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - Administration: Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - Dose Adjustments: Should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products should be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue treatment immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Neurologic Reactions (Seizures):** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine level: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Oral Formulation Caution:** The safety/efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal impairment. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events reported in clinical trials for ESRD patients (occurring at ≥5% frequency, regardless of causality) include:

| Body System | Adverse Event (Incidence ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain, flu syndrome, headache, injection site reaction, abdominal pain, fever, accidental injury |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, palpitations |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, dyspepsia, anorexia |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia, hyperkalemia, peripheral edema |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, paresthesia, depression, insomnia |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis |
| **Other** | Body odor, gastritis, anemia, pruritus, eye disorder |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm, and seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine therapy or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned under current labeling, but reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8x human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm]. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake by the infant must be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is consistent with adult weight-based recommendations (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD patients on dialysis. Note the risk of metabolite accumulation if oral forms are used in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, it restores plasma carnitine levels which are often depleted due to dialysis losses and reduced renal synthesis.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are TMAO and γ-butyrobetaine [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (in patients with normal renal function). Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (n=12), 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) demonstrated dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) [US].
