# Editorial Product Information: Epinastine Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of epinastine hydrochloride ophthalmic solution, synthesised for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Epinastine hydrochloride (HCl), a direct H1-receptor antagonist and mast cell stabiliser.
- **Chemical Class:** Dibenzepin derivative.
- **Brand Names:** Somerset Therapeutics Epinastine HCl (Generic) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile ophthalmic solution 0.05% (0.5 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Topical ophthalmic.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Somerset Therapeutics, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Allergic Conjunctivitis:** Indicated for the prevention of ocular itching associated with allergic conjunctivitis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Children ≥2 years):** One drop instilled into each affected eye twice daily (BID) [US].
- **Duration of Treatment:** Therapy should be maintained throughout the period of allergen exposure (e.g., until the conclusion of the pollen season or termination of exposure to the offending allergen), even in the absence of active symptoms [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - For topical ophthalmic use only; not for injection or oral use.
    - To prevent contamination, patients must not touch the dropper tip to the eye, surrounding structures, or any other surface.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None:** There are no listed contraindications in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **General Warnings:**
    - **Contamination Risk:** Proper aseptic technique is required to maintain the sterility of the dropper tip. Contaminated solutions may cause serious ocular damage and subsequent vision loss [US].
    - **Topical Use Only:** Product is strictly for ophthalmic administration [US].
- **Contact Lens Precautions:**
    - **Irritation:** This medication should not be used to treat irritation related to contact lens wear [US].
    - **Ocular Hyperemia:** Patients should be advised not to wear contact lenses if their eyes are red [US].
    - **Preservative Absorption:** The formulation contains benzalkonium chloride, which may be absorbed by soft contact lenses. Lenses must be removed prior to instillation and may be reinserted 10 minutes following administration [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions observed in clinical trials are categorised by frequency below [US]:

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | **Ocular:** Burning sensation, folliculosis, hyperemia, pruritus. <br> **Non-Ocular:** Headache, rhinitis, sinusitis, increased cough, pharyngitis (approx. 1–3%). |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | **Non-Ocular:** Infection (presented as cold symptoms and upper respiratory infections). |

- **Post-marketing Experience:** Increased lacrimation has been reported. Because these reports are voluntary and from a population of uncertain size, frequency cannot be reliably estimated [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources. No formal drug interaction studies were reported in the provided documentation [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Classification:** Not explicitly assigned a letter category in this source, but recommended for use only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus.
    - **Animal Data:** No teratogenic effects were observed in rats or rabbits at oral doses up to 150,000 and 55,000 times the maximum recommended ocular human dose (MROHD), respectively. However, at 90,000 times the MROHD, reduced pup body weight gain was observed in rats [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Epinastine is excreted in the milk of lactating rats. It is unknown if it is excreted in human milk; caution is advised when administering to nursing mothers [US].
- **Fertility:** No effect on male fertility in rats. Decreased female fertility was observed at oral doses approximately 90,000 times the MROHD [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have been established in children aged 2 years and older. Safety and efficacy in infants under 2 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness have been observed between geriatric and younger patients [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Epinastine is a topically active, direct H1-receptor antagonist and an inhibitor of histamine release from mast cells [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The drug is selective for the H1-receptor and has affinity for the H2-receptor. It also possesses affinity for α1, α2, and 5-HT2 receptors [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Systemic exposure is low. Following BID dosing, average peak plasma concentrations (0.04 ± 0.014 ng/mL) were reached at approximately two hours. No systemic accumulation was observed after multiple dosing [US].
    - **Distribution:** 64% bound to plasma proteins [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Less than 10% is metabolised [US].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal plasma half-life is approximately 12 hours. Primarily excreted unchanged; approximately 55% of an intravenous dose is recovered in urine and 30% in feces. Renal elimination occurs mainly via active tubular secretion [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Ocular Itching Models:** Epinastine 0.05% demonstrated significant superiority over vehicle in conjunctival antigen challenge (CAC) models and environmental field studies [US].
- **Onset and Duration:** Action begins within 3 to 5 minutes after antigen challenge. The duration of effect is approximately 8 hours [US].
- **Safety Profile:** Trials up to 8 weeks in duration showed the drug to be safe and effective without evidence of tachyphylaxis [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F). Excursions are permitted between 15° and 30°C (59° and 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Keep the bottle tightly closed and out of reach of children [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only (Prescription Only) [US].
