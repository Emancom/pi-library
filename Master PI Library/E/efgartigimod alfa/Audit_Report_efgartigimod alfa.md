## Close Paraphrases
*   **Missed Dose Instructions:** The editorial states, *"If a dose is missed, it may be administered up to 3 days after the scheduled date. If more than 3 days have passed, the dose should be skipped, and the original schedule resumed."* This is a very close paraphrase of Document 2 (FDA), Section 2.3: *"If a scheduled dose is missed... may be administered up to 3 days after... Thereafter, resume the original dosing schedule."*
*   **Infection Warnings:** The phrasing regarding delaying administration until infections resolve closely mirrors the "Warnings and Precautions" sections of both source documents.

## Originality Strengths
The editorial excels in **Synthesis of Divergent Regulatory Data**. It does not simply copy one PI; it actively compares the TGA and FDA guidelines. For instance, in Section 5 (Dosage for gMG), it correctly identifies and contrasts the TGA's 7-week minimum cycle interval against the FDA's 28-day interval. This requires an active reading of both documents to create a unified instruction set that notes regional variances.

## Differences from Source PIs
*   **Harmonization:** The editorial merges two distinct documents into one "Unified" document, which is not how either source is presented.
*   **Regional Tagging:** The editorial adds bracketed labels (e.g., [TGA], [FDA]) to denote which regulatory body provides specific information, a feature entirely absent from the source documents.
*   **Structural Reorganization:** The editorial uses a simplified 11-point structure that differs from the standard 17-section FDA format or the 10-section TGA format.
*   **Omissions:** Clinical trial specifics (Study 1, Study 2, Study ARGX-113-1704), which occupy several pages of the source documents, are condensed into high-level summaries.

## Recommendation
**PASS**

**Reasoning:** While certain technical definitions and safety warnings are verbatim, this is expected and often required in regulatory writing to ensure clinical accuracy (e.g., the biochemical description of the molecule or specific reported adverse events like "hypotension leading to syncope"). The editorial provides significant "value-add" through the synthesis of two different sets of national guidelines into a single, cohesive document. No further revision is required.
