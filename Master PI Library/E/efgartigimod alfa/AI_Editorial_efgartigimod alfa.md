# Editorial Product Information: Efgartigimod Alfa (with Hyaluronidase)

**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been compiled by integrating clinical and regulatory data from the Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) of Australia and the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) of the United States. Where regional guidelines or available dosage forms differ, the most comprehensive information has been provided, with specific source agencies noted in brackets.

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** Vyvgart (Australia), Vyvgart Hytrulo (United States)  
**Active Ingredients:** 
*   **Efgartigimod alfa:** A human recombinant immunoglobulin G1 (IgG1)-derived Fc fragment.
*   **Hyaluronidase:** Specifically vorhyaluronidase alfa [TGA] or hyaluronidase-qvfc [FDA], an endoglycosidase included to facilitate subcutaneous absorption.

## 2. Approved Indications
Efgartigimod alfa is indicated for the treatment of:

*   **Generalized Myasthenia Gravis (gMG):** 
    *   As an add-on to standard therapy in adult patients who are anti-acetylcholine receptor (AChR) antibody positive [TGA].
    *   The FDA indication encompasses adult patients with gMG more broadly, including those who are anti-AChR antibody negative, based on evidence from clinical studies [FDA].
*   **Chronic Inflammatory Demyelinating Polyneuropathy (CIDP):** 
    *   For the treatment of adult patients with progressive or relapsing active CIDP. 
    *   In Australia, this is specified as monotherapy following prior treatment with corticosteroids or immunoglobulins [TGA].

## 3. Contraindications
This product is contraindicated in patients with a history of serious hypersensitivity to efgartigimod alfa, hyaluronidase, or any of the excipients in the formulation. Reported reactions include anaphylaxis and hypotension leading to syncope [FDA].

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Infections
Because efgartigimod alfa reduces circulating IgG levels, the risk of infection is increased. The most frequently reported infections include upper respiratory tract and urinary tract infections. Administration should be delayed in patients with active clinical infections until resolved. If a serious infection occurs during treatment, withholding the drug should be considered.

### Hypersensitivity and Injection Reactions
Hypersensitivity reactions, including rash, angioedema, and dyspnea, have been observed. Anaphylaxis has been reported in post-marketing settings with the intravenous formulation. Patients should be monitored for at least 30 minutes following administration for signs of systemic or local reactions.

### Immunizations
The safety of live or live-attenuated vaccines during treatment is unknown; therefore, vaccination with live vaccines is not recommended during therapy. All required vaccines should be administered according to guidelines at least 4 weeks prior to starting a new treatment cycle or at least 2 weeks after the final dose of a cycle.

### Neuromuscular Specifics
*   **MGFA Class V:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients in myasthenic crisis (intubated patients).
*   **CIDP Transition:** There have been reports of worsening neurological symptoms when transitioning from intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) to efgartigimod alfa [TGA/FDA].

## 5. Dosage and Administration
**Route:** Subcutaneous (SC) injection only. Do not administer intravenously.

### Dosage for gMG (Cyclic Regimen)
*   **Standard Dose:** 1,000 mg efgartigimod alfa / 10,000 units hyaluronidase.
*   **Schedule:** Once-weekly injections for 4 weeks (one cycle). Subsequent cycles are determined by clinical evaluation, but should not be initiated sooner than 7 weeks [TGA] or 28 days [FDA] from the start of the previous cycle.
*   **Vial Variant:** A 1,008 mg / 11,200 unit single-dose vial is available for healthcare professional administration only [FDA].

### Dosage for CIDP (Continuous Regimen)
*   **Standard Dose:** 1,000 mg once weekly.
*   **Adjustment:** Dosing may be adjusted to every other week based on clinical response, though weekly dosing should resume if symptoms worsen [TGA].

### Administration Instructions
*   **Self-Administration:** The pre-filled syringe (PFS) may be administered by the patient or caregiver after appropriate training. The first injection and first dose of the second cycle must be supervised by a healthcare professional [TGA].
*   **Site:** Inject into the abdomen, rotating sites between injections. Avoid areas with scars, moles, or bruised/tender skin.
*   **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, it may be administered up to 3 days after the scheduled date. If more than 3 days have passed, the dose should be skipped, and the original schedule resumed.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most common adverse reactions (occurring in ≥10% of patients) include:
*   Local injection site reactions (e.g., rash, erythema, pruritus, pain, bruising).
*   Upper respiratory tract infections.
*   Urinary tract infections.
*   Headaches (including procedural headache).

**Laboratory Abnormalities:** Reductions in white blood cell counts, specifically lymphocytes and neutrophils, have been observed [FDA]. Allergic reactions and myalgia are also reported.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### FcRn-Binding Products
Efgartigimod alfa may reduce the concentrations of other medications that bind to the human neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn), such as monoclonal antibodies, immunoglobulin products, or antibody derivatives. It is recommended to postpone the initiation of these products until 2 weeks after the last dose of efgartigimod alfa.

### Plasma Exchange/Immunoadsorption
The use of plasmapheresis or immunoadsorption may significantly reduce the circulating levels of efgartigimod alfa, potentially diminishing its therapeutic effect.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
**Category C [TGA].** There are no adequate data on the use of efgartigimod alfa in pregnant women. Human IgG is known to cross the placental barrier, particularly after 30 weeks of gestation. Treatment may lead to reduced passive immunity in the newborn; clinicians should weigh the risks/benefits of live vaccines in infants exposed *in utero*.

### Breastfeeding
It is unknown if efgartigimod alfa or hyaluronidase is excreted in human milk. Because maternal IgG is present in breast milk, the developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be considered alongside the mother’s clinical need for the drug.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Bioavailability is approximately 77% following subcutaneous injection.
*   **Distribution:** Volume of distribution ranges from 15 to 20 L.
*   **Metabolism:** Expected to be degraded via proteolytic enzymes into small peptides and amino acids.
*   **Elimination:** The terminal half-life is 80 to 120 hours (3 to 5 days).
*   **Special Populations:** No dose adjustments are required for age, gender, race, or mild renal impairment. Data are insufficient for patients with moderate-to-severe renal impairment.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Efgartigimod alfa is an FcRn blocker. By binding to the neonatal Fc receptor, it prevents FcRn from recycling IgG back into the circulation, leading to the targeted degradation of all IgG subtypes, including pathogenic autoantibodies (e.g., anti-AChR). It does not affect levels of IgA, IgD, IgE, IgM, or albumin. 

The inclusion of hyaluronidase increases the permeability of subcutaneous tissue by temporarily depolymerizing hyaluronan, facilitating the absorption of the large efgartigimod alfa molecule.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Refrigeration:** Store at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze or shake.
*   **Light Protection:** Keep in the original carton to protect from light.
*   **Room Temperature Excursions:** 
    *   **PFS:** May be stored at room temperature (up to 30°C) for a single period of up to 30 days.
    *   **Vial:** May be stored at room temperature (20°C to 25°C) for up to 3 days [FDA].
*   **Post-Warming:** Once warmed to room temperature, the product must not be returned to the refrigerator.
