# Editorial Product Information: Epoprostenol

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following jurisdictional Product Information (PI): 
    - **Australian (TGA)**: Product Information — FLOLAN (epoprostenol) powder for injection.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Epoprostenol sodium (chemical class: prostaglandins/prostacyclins).
- **Brand Names:** **Flolan** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** B01AC09.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Sterile freeze-dried powder for injection: 500 micrograms or 1.5 mg vials.
    - Supplied with a specific sterile buffer Diluent (pH 11.7 to 12.3).
- **Route of Administration:** Continuous intravenous infusion (via central venous catheter for long-term use).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** GlaxoSmithKline Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Epoprostenol is indicated for the long-term treatment of pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) via continuous intravenous infusion in patients with WHO functional Class III or Class IV symptoms:
- Idiopathic pulmonary arterial hypertension [Australian-approved].
- Familial pulmonary arterial hypertension [Australian-approved].
- Pulmonary arterial hypertension associated with the scleroderma spectrum of diseases [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Treatment involves two distinct phases: short-term dose-ranging to determine the optimal rate and long-term continuous maintenance.

### Short-term (Acute) Dose-ranging
- **Purpose:** To determine the long-term infusion rate based on maximum haemodynamic benefit or dose-limiting effects.
- **Initial Rate:** 2 ng/kg/min.
- **Increments:** Increase by 2 ng/kg/min every 15 minutes or longer.

### Long-term Continuous Infusion
- **Initial Chronic Rate:** Should be initiated at 4 ng/kg/min less than the maximum tolerated rate identified during acute ranging.
- **Low Tolerance Adjustment:** If the maximum tolerated rate was ≤ 5 ng/kg/min, the long-term infusion should start at 1 ng/kg/min.
- **Administration:** Must be delivered through a permanent indwelling central venous catheter using a portable infusion pump. Temporary peripheral access may be used only until central access is established.

### Titration and Adjustments
- **Dose Increases:** Based on persistence or recurrence of PAH symptoms. Increments of 1 to 2 ng/kg/min at intervals of at least 15 minutes.
- **Dose Decreases:** For dose-limiting pharmacological effects (e.g., flushing, headache, hypotension). Decrements of 2 ng/kg/min every 15 minutes or longer.
- **Abrupt Withdrawal:** **Strictly avoid.** Sudden interruption or large reductions can induce rebound PAH, resulting in dizziness, asthenia, increased dyspnoea, and potentially death.

### Reconstitution and Administration Instructions
- **Diluent:** Use only the provided pH 12 sterile buffer. Physiological saline is incompatible.
- **Filtration:** The final solution must be filtered (0.22 or 0.20 micron filter) during or prior to administration.
- **Incompatibilities:** Prepared solutions must not contact materials containing polyethylene terephthalate (PET) or polyethylene terephthalate glycol (PETG).
- **In-use Stability:** Once reconstituted/diluted, solutions are stable for up to 8 days at 2°C to 8°C. At room temperature, use within:
    - 48 hours at ≤ 25°C.
    - 24 hours at ≤ 35°C.
    - 12 hours at ≤ 40°C.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to epoprostenol or any excipients.
- Congestive heart failure (CHF) due to severe left ventricular dysfunction (associated with increased mortality).
- Chronic use in patients who develop pulmonary oedema during the initial dose-ranging phase (potential pulmonary veno-occlusive disease).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Abrupt Withdrawal:** Sudden cessation of infusion may lead to fatal rebound pulmonary hypertension.
- **Clinical Setting:** Initial dose-ranging must occur in a hospital setting with facilities for haemodynamic monitoring and emergency care.
- **Sepsis Risk:** Constant risk of catheter-related bloodstream infections; strict aseptic technique is mandatory.
- **High pH/Extravasation:** The infusion solution has a high pH (approx. 12); avoid extravasation to prevent tissue damage.
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** Use with extreme caution in patients with coronary artery disease. Epoprostenol typically increases heart rate and may cause profound hypotension in overdose.
- **Haemorrhagic Risk:** As a potent inhibitor of platelet aggregation, there is an increased risk for bleeding, especially in patients with other risk factors.
- **Pulmonary Oedema:** Development during dose-ranging may indicate pulmonary veno-occlusive disease; discontinue if this occurs.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Acute Dose-ranging Phase
- **Very Common (>10%):** Flushing (52%), headache (44%), nausea/vomiting (28%), hypotension (14%), anxiety/agitation (13%), chest pain (10%).
- **Common (1–10%):** Dizziness, abdominal pain, bradycardia, back pain, jaw pain.

### Chronic Administration Phase
- **General:** Jaw pain (54-75%), headache, flushing, diarrhoea, nausea, myalgia, dizziness.
- **Serious Effects:** Sepsis and septicaemia (common, related to delivery system), pulmonary embolism, high output cardiac failure (very rare).
- **Delivery System Related:** Local infection (up to 21%), injection site pain (up to 13%), catheter occlusion.

### Laboratory Changes
- **Common:** Decreased platelet count (thrombocytopenia), elevated serum glucose levels.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Anticoagulants/Antiplatelets:** Increased risk of bleeding when used with NSAIDs, warfarin, or other agents affecting coagulation. Regular monitoring is advised.
- **Digoxin:** Epoprostenol may decrease the clearance of digoxin by 15% in the short term; monitor for digoxin toxicity.
- **Vasodilators:** Potential for additive or synergistic hypotensive effects when co-administered with other vasodilators or diuretics.
- **Tissue Plasminogen Activator (t-PA):** Epoprostenol may reduce the thrombolytic efficacy of t-PA by increasing its hepatic clearance.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B1.** 
    - Animal studies (rats/rabbits) showed no effects on the foetus at doses up to 2.2 times the human dose. 
    - Use only if the potential benefit to the mother outweighs the risk to the foetus; human data are lacking.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if epoprostenol is excreted in human milk. A risk to the infant cannot be excluded; clinical decisions should weigh the benefits of breastfeeding against the mother's need for therapy.
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility observed in animal studies.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Limited information available for use in children with PAH.
- **Elderly Use:** Limited information for patients > 65. Dose selection should be cautious, starting at the lower end of the range, reflecting the higher frequency of hepatic, renal, or cardiac impairment.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Specific dose adjustments are not provided in available sources, though caution is advised due to the frequency of decreased organ function in the elderly.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    1. Direct vasodilation of pulmonary and systemic arterial vascular beds.
    2. Potent inhibition of platelet aggregation.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Reduces right and left ventricular afterload, increases cardiac output and stroke volume. Cardiovascular effects typically disappear within 30 minutes of ending the infusion.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Metabolism:** Spontaneously degrades at physiological pH to 6-oxo-prostaglandin F1α. 
    - **Half-life:** Extremely short (estimated ≤ 6 minutes; likely 2–3 minutes).
    - **Excretion:** Primarily renal (82%) and faecal (4%).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Idiopathic/Familial PAH (PPH):** Two trials (8 and 12 weeks) showed significant improvements in cardiac index, stroke volume, and 6-minute walk distance. A 12-week study demonstrated a significant survival benefit (0 deaths in the epoprostenol group vs. 8 deaths in the standard therapy group).
- **PAH/Scleroderma (SSD):** A 12-week trial showed significant improvements in haemodynamics (CI, PAPm, PVR) and exercise capacity. No statistical difference in survival was observed in the scleroderma population over 12 weeks.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Vials:** Store in a dry place below 25°C. Protect from light. Do not freeze.
- **Diluent:** Store below 25°C. Protect from light. Do not freeze.
- **Reconstituted Solution:** See Section 3 for temperature-specific shelf life.
