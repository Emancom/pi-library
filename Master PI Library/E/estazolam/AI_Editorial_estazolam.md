# Editorial Product Information: Estazolam

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official PI for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory guidance before prescribing. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Estazolam (a triazolobenzodiazepine derivative).
- **Brand Names:** Estazolam (Generic) [US].
- **ATC Code:** N05CD04 (Note: Not explicitly listed in source but standard for estazolam).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Oral tablets: 1 mg (white, scored, diamond-shaped) and 2 mg (dark pink, scored, diamond-shaped) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Actavis Pharma, Inc. (Teva Pharmaceuticals) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Short-term management of insomnia:** Indicated for patients characterized by difficulty falling asleep, frequent nocturnal awakenings, and/or early morning awakenings [US].
- **Clinical Note:** Treatment is generally intended for short-term use (up to 12 weeks), as insomnia is often transient. Failure of insomnia to remit after 7 to 10 days of treatment may indicate a primary psychiatric or medical illness requiring further evaluation [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:** The recommended initial dose is 1 mg at bedtime. Some patients may require titration to 2 mg [US].
- **Geriatric Dosing:** 
    - Healthy elderly patients: 1 mg is the appropriate starting dose, with increases initiated with care [US].
    - Small or debilitated elderly patients: A starting dose of 0.5 mg should be considered, although this may be only marginally effective [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** Take immediately before retiring or after being in bed and experiencing difficulty falling asleep. Should not be taken with or immediately after a meal [US].
- **Discontinuation:** To avoid withdrawal reactions, use a gradual taper to discontinue or reduce the dosage. If withdrawal symptoms occur, pause the taper or return to the previous dose level and decrease more slowly [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Co-administration with ketoconazole and itraconazole is contraindicated due to significant impairment of estazolam's oxidative metabolism [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to estazolam or other benzodiazepines [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings [US]:**
    - **Risks from Concomitant Use with Opioids:** May result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death. Reserve concomitant use for patients with inadequate alternative treatment options.
    - **Abuse, Misuse, and Addiction:** Use exposes users to risks of addiction, which can lead to overdose or death. Assess risk before prescribing and monitor throughout treatment.
    - **Dependence and Withdrawal:** Continued use may lead to physical dependence. Abrupt discontinuation or rapid reduction may precipitate life-threatening withdrawal reactions.
- **Complex Sleep Behaviors:** Reports of "sleep-driving," preparing/eating food, making phone calls, or having sex while not fully awake. Discontinue immediately if a patient reports such an episode [US].
- **CNS Depression:** Patients should be cautioned against engaging in hazardous occupations requiring mental alertness (e.g., operating machinery, driving) the day following ingestion [US].
- **Respiratory Depression:** Caution in patients with compromised respiratory function [US].
- **Depression/Suicidality:** Use with caution in patients exhibiting signs of depression; suicidal tendencies may be present and require protective measures [US].
- **Severe Anaphylaxis:** Rare cases of angioedema involving the tongue, glottis, or larynx have been reported. Do not rechallenge patients who develop these reactions [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Somnolence (42%), Headache (16%), Asthenia (11%) [US].
- **Common (1–10%):** Hypokinesis (8%), Nervousness (8%), Dizziness (7%), Abnormal coordination (4%), Nausea (4%), Hangover (3%), Cold symptoms (3%) [US].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Confusion, depression, abnormal dreams, thinking abnormal, pharyngitis, pruritus, muscle spasm, myalgia, and various gastrointestinal disturbances (constipation, dry mouth) [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Respiratory depression, anaphylaxis, complex sleep behaviors, seizures (upon withdrawal), and syncope (rare) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Opioids:** Increased risk of fatal respiratory depression and profound sedation [US].
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Potent inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, itraconazole) significantly increase estazolam levels (contraindicated). Moderate inhibitors (e.g., nefazodone, fluvoxamine, cimetidine, erythromycin) require caution and possible dose reduction [US].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Compounds like carbamazepine, phenytoin, and rifampin are expected to decrease estazolam plasma concentrations [US].
- **CNS Depressants:** Potentiation of effects occurs with alcohol, barbiturates, antihistamines, and psychotropic medications [US].
- **Cigarette Smoking:** Clearance of estazolam is increased in smokers compared to non-smokers [US].
- **Fluoxetine:** No clinically significant pharmacokinetic interaction has been observed [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly lettered in current labeling; however, warnings are provided for Neonatal Sedation and Withdrawal Syndrome [US].
    - **Risk Summary:** Use in late pregnancy can cause respiratory depression, lethargy, and hypotonia in the neonate. Withdrawal symptoms (hyperreflexia, irritability, tremors) may also occur [US].
    - **Registry:** Clinicians are encouraged to register pregnant patients in the National Pregnancy Registry for Psychiatric Medications [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. Estazolam is excreted in animal milk; potential for accumulation due to long half-life and risk of sedation in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility observed in rat studies at doses up to 30 times the human dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 18 years have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of falls and excessive sedation. Lower starting doses (0.5 mg to 1 mg) are required [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Pharmacokinetics not specifically studied; caution and monitoring recommended [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Pharmacokinetics not specifically studied; caution and monitoring recommended [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A triazolobenzodiazepine that exerts its effects by binding to stereospecific benzodiazepine receptors on the GABA-A receptor complex, enhancing the inhibitory effects of GABA.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak plasma concentrations reached within 2 hours (range 0.5 to 6 hours) [US].
    - **Distribution:** 93% protein bound [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolized, primarily by CYP3A4. The major metabolite is 4-hydroxy-estazolam (minimal contribution to hypnotic effect) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Mean elimination half-life ranges from 10 to 24 hours. Primarily renal excretion (87% in urine) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Insomnia Efficacy:** In 7-night trials, 2 mg was consistently superior to placebo for sleep induction and maintenance. In a 12-week study, estazolam remained superior to placebo for sleep duration, though tolerance/rebound insomnia was noted upon withdrawal [US].
- **Geriatric Trials:** In patients with chronic insomnia, 1 mg was consistently superior to placebo for sleep induction [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Container:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container with a child-resistant closure [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:**
    - **United States:** Controlled substance, Schedule IV (C-IV) [US].
    - **Other Regions:** Not specified in available sources.
