# Editorial Product Information: Etonogestrel and Ethinyl Estradiol Vaginal Ring

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States** (Food and Drug Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, clinical pharmacists, and medical writers. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to the local approved labeling for legal and regulatory compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Etonogestrel (a progestin) and Ethinyl Estradiol (an estrogen).
- **Chemical Class:** Combination Hormonal Contraceptive (CHC).
- **Brand Name(s):** Not specified in the primary clinical text, but labeled under the manufacturer **AvKARE** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Polymeric vaginal ring containing 11.7 mg etonogestrel and 2.7 mg ethinyl estradiol. It releases an average of 0.120 mg/day of etonogestrel and 0.015 mg/day of ethinyl estradiol over a 3-week period [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Vaginal use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AvKARE [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Contraception:** Indicated for use by females of reproductive age to prevent pregnancy [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** One ring is inserted into the vagina and remains in place continuously for three weeks. This is followed by a one-week ring-free interval, during which withdrawal bleeding typically occurs [US].
- **Cycle Timing:**
    - **No hormonal contraceptive use in the preceding cycle:** Insert on Day 1 of the menstrual cycle. If inserted on Days 2–5, use a barrier method (e.g., male condoms with spermicide) for the first seven days [US].
    - **Switching from a CHC:** Insert on any day, but no later than the day following the usual hormone-free interval of the previous method [US].
    - **Switching from Progestin-only methods:** Switch from a progestin-only pill on any day; from an implant/IUS on the day of removal; or from an injectable on the day the next dose is due. In all cases, use a barrier method for the first seven days [US].
- **Deviations and Missed Doses:**
    - **Out of vagina < 3 hours:** Efficacy is not reduced. Rinse with lukewarm water and reinsert immediately [US].
    - **Out of vagina > 3 hours (Weeks 1 & 2):** Reinsert as soon as remembered; use barrier contraception for seven days [US].
    - **Out of vagina > 3 hours (Week 3):** Discard the ring. Either insert a new ring immediately (starting a new 3-week cycle) or insert a new ring within 7 days of the previous ring's removal (provided it was used for at least 7 days prior). Use a barrier method for seven days [US].
- **Extended Use:** If the ring is left in for up to 4 weeks total, the user remains protected. If used for > 4 weeks, rule out pregnancy before re-starting and use barrier protection for 7 days [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- High risk of arterial or venous thrombotic (VTE) diseases (e.g., smokers >35 years, history of DVT/PE, cerebrovascular disease, coronary artery disease, thrombogenic valvular or rhythm diseases, inherited hypercoagulopathies, or uncontrolled hypertension) [US].
- Current diagnosis or history of breast cancer (hormone-sensitive) [US].
- Benign or malignant liver tumors or active liver disease [US].
- Undiagnosed abnormal uterine bleeding [US].
- Pregnancy [US].
- Hypersensitivity (including anaphylaxis/angioedema) to any components [US].
- Co-administration with Hepatitis C drug combinations containing ombitasvir/paritaprevir/ritonavir, with or without dasabuvir [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [US]:** **Cigarette smoking increases the risk of serious cardiovascular events from CHC use. This risk increases with age (particularly >35 years) and the number of cigarettes smoked. CHCs, including this vaginal ring, should not be used by women over 35 who smoke.**
- **Thromboembolic Disorders:** Discontinue if an arterial or venous thrombotic event occurs. Stop use at least 4 weeks before and through 2 weeks after major surgery or prolonged immobilization [US].
- **Toxic Shock Syndrome (TSS):** Cases have been reported. Evaluate patients presenting with high fever, vomiting, diarrhea, or sunburn-like rash [US].
- **Liver Disease:** Discontinue if jaundice develops. Hepatic adenomas are associated with CHC use (attributable risk ~3.3 cases/100,000 users) [US].
- **High Blood Pressure:** Monitor blood pressure; discontinue if it rises significantly during use [US].
- **Metabolic Effects:** Monitor prediabetic and diabetic women for glucose intolerance. CHCs may increase risks in women with uncontrolled dyslipidemia or hypertriglyceridemia (pancreatitis risk) [US].
- **Vaginal Use:** May cause tissue irritation or ulceration. In some cases, the ring may adhere to vaginal tissue, requiring professional removal [US].
- **Headache:** Evaluate new, recurrent, persistent, or severe headaches; discontinue if migraine frequency or severity increases [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Enzyme Inducers:** Drugs that induce CYP3A4 (e.g., phenytoin, barbiturates, carbamazepine, rifampicin, St. John's wort) decrease plasma concentrations and contraceptive efficacy. Use back-up contraception during and for 28 days after use of these inducers [US].
- **Substances Increasing Concentrations:** Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, itraconazole, clarithromycin) and vaginal miconazole nitrate (suppository or cream) may increase etonogestrel/ethinyl estradiol concentrations by up to 40% [US].
- **HCV Combination Therapy:** Co-administration with ombitasvir/paritaprevir/ritonavir is contraindicated due to potential ALT elevations [US].
- **Effect on Other Drugs:** CHCs may decrease lamotrigine levels (induction of glucuronidation), potentially reducing seizure control [US]. They may also increase concentrations of cyclosporine, theophylline, and tizanidine [US].
- **Thyroid/Cortisol Replacement:** Estrogens may increase binding globulins, necessitating dose adjustments for thyroid hormone or cortisol therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated. Epidemiologic studies have not shown an increased risk of birth defects following accidental exposure to low-dose CHCs in early pregnancy [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. CHCs can reduce milk production. Small amounts of steroids are transferred to human milk; however, harmful effects on infants have not been established [US].
- **Fertility:** Return of ovulation and spontaneous menstrual cycles typically occurs within one month after discontinuation [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established in postpubertal adolescents. Use before menarche is not indicated [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Not studied in postmenopausal women; not indicated for this population [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Pharmacokinetics not specifically studied [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in active liver disease. Steroid hormones may be poorly metabolized in these patients [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Primarily acts by suppressing gonadotropins, which inhibits ovulation. Secondary mechanisms include increasing cervical mucus viscosity (hindering sperm entry) and altering the endometrium (reducing implantation likelihood) [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Etonogestrel bioavailability is ~100%; ethinyl estradiol is ~56% [US].
    - **Distribution:** Etonogestrel is 32% bound to SHBG and 66% to albumin. Ethinyl estradiol is 98.5% bound to albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Both are metabolized in the liver via CYP3A4 [US].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily eliminated via urine, bile, and feces [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pearl Index:** In pooled data from three trials (N=2,356 women <35 years), the Pearl Index was 1.28 (95% CI [0.8, 1.9]) per 100 women-years [US].
- **US-Specific Data:** The Pearl Index in the US-only cohort was 2.02 (95% CI [1.1, 3.4]) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Prior to Dispensing:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) [US].
- **After Dispensing:** Can be stored at room temperature (25°C; excursions permitted 15–30°C) for up to 4 months. Protect from direct sunlight [US].
- **Disposal:** Place the used ring in the original foil sachet and discard in household trash out of reach of children/pets. Do not flush [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as a Human Prescription Drug (Rx-only) [US].
- **Other Jurisdictions:** Not specified in available sources.
