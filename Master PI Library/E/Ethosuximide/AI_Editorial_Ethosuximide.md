## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Ethosuximide
*   **Chemical Class:** Succinimide anticonvulsant
*   **Brand Name(s):** Zarontin [Australian]
*   **ATC Code:** N03AD01
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    *   Soft gelatin capsules: 250 mg [Australian]
    *   Syrup: 250 mg/5 mL [Australian]
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Clinect Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Petit mal epilepsy:** Indicated for the control of absence (petit mal) seizures [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Children)
*   **Initial Dose:** Approximately 20–30 mg/kg per day, administered in two divided doses.
*   **Titration:** Dosage should be increased in small increments due to a potential curvilinear dose-serum level relationship. A standard method involves increasing the daily dose by 250 mg every four to seven days until seizure control is achieved with minimal adverse effects.
*   **Maximum Dose:** Dosages exceeding 1.5 g daily must be administered under strict medical supervision.

### Therapeutic Drug Monitoring
*   **Therapeutic Range:** 40–100 mg/L (mcg/mL).
*   **Optimum Level:** 75 mg/L.
*   Periodic plasma level monitoring is recommended to ensure efficacy and safety.

### Administration Instructions
*   Ethosuximide may be administered in combination with other anticonvulsants if other forms of epilepsy (e.g., grand mal) coexist with petit mal.
*   **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
*   Hypersensitivity to ethosuximide, other succinimides, or any excipients in the formulation (including hydroxybenzoates and sulfites in capsules; benzoates and saccharin in syrup) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Precautions
*   **Mixed Epilepsy:** When used alone in mixed types of epilepsy, ethosuximide may increase the frequency of grand mal (tonic-clonic) seizures in some patients.
*   **Abrupt Withdrawal:** As with all antiepileptic drugs (AEDs), abrupt discontinuation may precipitate petit mal status.

### Haematopoietic Effects
*   **Blood Dyscrasias:** Serious and sometimes fatal blood dyscrasias (e.g., aplastic anaemia, agranulocytosis) have been reported. Periodic blood counts are mandatory. Patients should report any signs of infection (fever, sore throat) immediately.

### Dermatologic and Autoimmune Reactions
*   **Serious Skin Reactions:** Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS) and Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS) have been reported and can be fatal. Ethosuximide must be discontinued immediately if a rash occurs for which no alternative etiology is found.
*   **Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE):** Cases of SLE have been reported; clinicians should monitor for related symptoms.

### Suicidal Behaviour and Ideation
*   AEDs increase the risk of suicidal thoughts or behaviour. This risk may be observed as early as one week after starting treatment. Patients should be monitored for depression, mood changes, or self-harming thoughts.

### Mental/Physical Impairment
*   May impair the ability to perform hazardous tasks (e.g., driving or operating machinery).

## 6. Adverse Effects

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Phenytoin:** Ethosuximide may elevate serum levels of phenytoin.
*   **Valproic Acid:** Has been reported to both increase and decrease ethosuximide levels.
*   **General Note:** Periodic serum level determinations of concurrent AEDs are recommended as interactions are common.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
*   **Australian Category D:** Ethosuximide crosses the placenta. Birth defect risks are approximately three times higher than the general population for mothers with epilepsy taking AEDs.
*   **Recommendations:**
    *   AEDs should be continued if necessary, as uncontrolled epilepsy poses a greater risk to the mother and fetus.
    *   Monotherapy at the lowest effective dose is preferred.
    *   Folic acid supplementation (5 mg daily) is recommended starting four weeks before conception through the first trimester.
    *   Detailed mid-trimester ultrasound is advised.

### Breastfeeding
*   Ethosuximide is excreted in human breast milk. Due to unknown effects on the nursing infant, it should only be used if the benefits clearly outweigh the risks.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (20–30 mg/kg) [Australian].
*   **Elderly Use:** No specific data available [Australian].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with extreme caution. Ethosuximide can cause morphological and functional liver changes. Periodic liver function studies are required.
*   **Renal Impairment:** Use with extreme caution. Periodic urinalysis is advised.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Ethosuximide suppresses the paroxysmal 3-cycle-per-second spike and wave activity associated with absence seizures. It reduces the frequency of attacks by depressing the motor cortex and elevating the CNS threshold to convulsive stimuli.

### Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption/Distribution:** Crosses the placenta and enters breast milk.
*   **Metabolism/Elimination:** Not specified in available sources.

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Suicidality Meta-analysis:** A pooled analysis of 199 placebo-controlled trials across 11 AEDs showed an adjusted Relative Risk of 1.8 (95% CI: 1.2, 2.7) for suicidal ideation/behaviour. The estimated incidence was 0.43% in AED patients vs 0.24% in placebo groups.

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Capsules:** Store below 30°C. Protect from moisture and keep container tightly closed [Australian].
*   **Syrup:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
