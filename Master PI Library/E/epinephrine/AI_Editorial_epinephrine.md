# Editorial Product Information: Epinephrine (Adrenaline)

**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been synthesized from official regulatory documents provided by the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). It combines clinical data for epinephrine used as a primary emergency treatment and as an adjunct in local dental anesthesia. Where clinical guidance differs between regions, the most comprehensive or conservative recommendation has been included with the source agency noted in brackets.

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
*   **Active Ingredient:** Epinephrine (also known as Adrenaline).
*   **Formulations Covered:** 
    *   Epinephrine Injection USP (1 mg/mL).
    *   Lidocaine hydrochloride monohydrate with Epinephrine (Adrenaline) acid tartrate (e.g., 2% lidocaine with 1:80,000 epinephrine) [TGA].

## 2. Approved Indications
Epinephrine is a non-selective alpha- and beta-adrenergic agonist indicated for:
*   **Anaphylaxis:** Emergency treatment of Type 1 allergic reactions, including anaphylactic shock, in adult and pediatric populations [FDA].
*   **Septic Shock:** To increase mean arterial blood pressure in adults with hypotension associated with septic shock [FDA].
*   **Local Anesthesia (Adjunct):** Production of local anesthesia for routine dental procedures and oral surgery via infiltration or nerve block. The addition of epinephrine provides prolonged anesthesia and local hemostasis [TGA].

## 3. Contraindications
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to epinephrine, sympathomimetic amines, or any excipients (e.g., sodium metabisulfite) [TGA].
*   **Vascular Disease:** Most patients with cerebral arteriosclerosis [TGA].
*   **Local Factors:** Local anesthetic techniques involving epinephrine must not be used in the presence of inflammation or sepsis at the injection site [TGA].
*   **Emergency Context:** The FDA notes no absolute contraindications for the use of epinephrine in life-threatening allergic reactions [FDA].

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Administration Site:** Do not inject into the digits, hands, feet, or ears, as the potent vasoconstriction may result in tissue necrosis. Avoid injection into the buttock due to the risk of clostridial infection (gas gangrene) [FDA].
*   **Vascular Integrity:** Avoid extravasation during intravenous infusion, which can cause local necrosis. Monitor the infusion site for free flow and blanching [FDA].
*   **Cardiovascular Effects:** Use with extreme caution in patients with heart block, rhythm disturbances, severe bradycardia, or ischemic heart disease, as epinephrine may induce anginal pain or arrhythmias [TGA, FDA].
*   **Systemic Conditions:** Extreme caution is advised in patients with severe hypertension, thyrotoxicosis, advanced diabetes, or cerebral vascular insufficiency [TGA].
*   **Sulfite Sensitivity:** This product contains sodium metabisulfite. While this may cause allergic-type reactions in susceptible individuals, the presence of sulfites should not deter use for serious allergic or emergency situations [FDA, TGA].
*   **Dental-Specific Caution:** Patients should be advised to avoid trauma to the lips and tongue (e.g., avoid hot liquids or chewing) until normal sensation returns [TGA].

## 5. Dosage and Administration
### 5.1 Anaphylaxis [FDA]
*   **Adults and Pediatrics (≥30 kg):** 0.3 mg to 0.5 mg administered intramuscularly (IM) or subcutaneously (SC) into the anterolateral thigh.
*   **Pediatrics (<30 kg):** 0.01 mg/kg.
*   **Frequency:** May be repeated every 5 to 15 minutes if symptoms persist or recur.

### 5.2 Septic Shock [FDA]
*   **Administration:** Intravenous infusion via a large vein (avoid leg veins in the elderly).
*   **Titration:** Start at 0.05 mcg/kg/min to 2 mcg/kg/min, titrated to achieve target mean arterial pressure. 
*   **Weaning:** Gradually decrease the infusion rate every 30 minutes over a 12-to-24-hour period.

### 5.3 Dental Anesthesia (1:80,000 Solution) [TGA]
*   **Infiltration:** 1–2 mL.
*   **Nerve Block:** 1.5–5 mL.
*   **Maximum Dose:** Should be tailored to the patient, but lidocaine with epinephrine should generally not exceed 7 mg/kg of body weight.

## 6. Adverse Effects
*   **Common:** Nervousness, tremor, anxiety, headache, dizziness, palpitations, sweating, pallor, nausea, and vomiting [FDA, TGA].
*   **Serious:**
    *   **Cardiovascular:** Fatal ventricular fibrillation, rapid rise in blood pressure leading to cerebral hemorrhage, and pulmonary edema [FDA].
    *   **Neurological:** Disorientation, impaired memory, or psychomotor agitation [FDA].
    *   **Injection Site:** Rare cases of necrotizing fasciitis or myonecrosis [FDA].
    *   **Dental:** Prolonged numbness or paraesthesia following nerve blocks [TGA].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Potentiating Agents:** The effects of epinephrine are increased by tricyclic antidepressants, MAO inhibitors, COMT inhibitors (e.g., entacapone), and non-selective beta-blockers (which may lead to severe hypertension and bradycardia) [FDA, TGA].
*   **Antagonizing Agents:** Alpha-blockers, vasodilators, and diuretics may counter the pressor effects [FDA].
*   **Arrhythmogenic Agents:** Halogenated hydrocarbon anesthetics (e.g., halothane), cyclopropane, and cardiac glycosides increase the risk of cardiac arrhythmias [FDA, TGA].
*   **Antidiabetics:** Epinephrine-induced hyperglycemia may necessitate dose adjustments of hypoglycemic agents [TGA].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   **Category A [TGA]:** Extensive use during pregnancy has not shown an increase in the frequency of malformations or direct harmful effects on the fetus.
    *   **Clinical Considerations [FDA]:** Epinephrine may cause uterine vasoconstriction and inhibit uterine contractions, potentially delaying labor. However, in life-threatening emergencies (anaphylaxis/septic shock), treatment should not be withheld.
*   **Breastfeeding:** Epinephrine is excreted in breast milk. Due to its very short half-life and poor oral bioavailability, it is unlikely to affect the nursing infant [FDA, TGA].

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Onset/Duration:** Rapid onset when given parenterally. Following IV injection, the half-life is less than 5 minutes [FDA].
*   **Metabolism:** Rapidly inactivated in the body. Metabolism occurs primarily in the liver, kidneys, and other tissues via the enzymes monoamine oxidase (MAO) and catechol-O-methyltransferase (COMT) [FDA, TGA].
*   **Excretion:** Metabolites (including vanillylmandelic acid) are excreted via the kidneys [FDA].

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Epinephrine acts on both alpha- and beta-adrenergic receptors.
*   **Alpha-receptors:** Induces vasoconstriction, which increases blood pressure and reduces mucosal edema [FDA]. In local anesthesia, it slows the absorption of the anesthetic, increasing the duration of action [TGA].
*   **Beta-1 receptors:** Increases heart rate and myocardial contractility [FDA].
*   **Beta-2 receptors:** Induces bronchial smooth muscle relaxation, alleviating bronchospasm and wheezing during anaphylaxis [FDA].

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Standard Injection:** Store at room temperature (20°C to 25°C). Protect from light, alkalis, and oxidizing agents. Do not refrigerate or freeze [FDA].
*   **Dental Cartridges:** Store refrigerated (2°C to 8°C) and protect from light. Once removed for use, store below 25°C and use within 4 weeks. Do not return to the refrigerator [TGA].
*   **Visual Inspection:** Discard if the solution is discolored (pinkish or brownish) or contains particulate matter [FDA, TGA].
