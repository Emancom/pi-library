# Originality Audit Report: epinephrine

## Audit Summary
- **Originality Score:** 45/100  
- **Verbatim Copied Passages Found:** 9
- **Overall Assessment:** Concern

## Verbatim Copied Passages
The editorial contains several instances where technical descriptions and clinical indications are copied word-for-word from the source regulatory documents.

1.  **Indications (Anaphylaxis):** 
    - **Editorial:** "indicated for: Anaphylaxis: Emergency treatment of Type 1 allergic reactions, including anaphylactic shock, in adult and pediatric populations."
    - **Source (FDA, p. 3):** "indicated for emergency treatment of type I allergic reactions, including anaphylaxis, in adults and pediatric patients."
    - **Location:** Section 2.

2.  **Indications (Dental):**
    - **Editorial:** "Production of local anesthesia for routine dental procedures and oral surgery via infiltration or nerve block."
    - **Source (TGA, p. 1):** "...indicated for the production of local anaesthesia in routine dental procedures and oral surgery by means of infiltration and nerve block techniques."
    - **Location:** Section 2.

3.  **Contraindications (Vascular):**
    - **Editorial:** "Most patients with cerebral arteriosclerosis."
    - **Source (TGA, p. 3):** "Solutions with adrenaline (epinephrine) should not be used in most patients with cerebral arteriosclerosis."
    - **Location:** Section 3.

4.  **Warnings (Administration Site):**
    - **Editorial:** "Do not inject into the digits, hands, feet, or ears... Potent vasoconstriction may result in tissue necrosis."
    - **Source (FDA, p. 5):** "Do not inject into digits, hands, or feet... Accidental injection into the digits, hands or feet may result in loss of blood flow to the affected area and tissue necrosis."
    - **Location:** Section 4.

5.  **Warnings (Infection Risk):**
    - **Editorial:** "Avoid injection into the buttock due to the risk of clostridial infection (gas gangrene)."
    - **Source (FDA, p. 5):** "Injection into the buttock... has been associated with the development of Clostridial infections (gas gangrene)."
    - **Location:** Section 4.

6.  **Dosage (Anaphylaxis):**
    - **Editorial:** "Adults and Pediatrics (≥30 kg): 0.3 mg to 0.5 mg administered intramuscularly (IM) or subcutaneously (SC) into the anterolateral thigh."
    - **Source (FDA, p. 3):** "Adult and Pediatric Patients Weighing 30 kg or Greater: 0.3 mg to 0.5 mg... Administer... intramuscularly or subcutaneously in the anterolateral aspect of the thigh."
    - **Location:** Section 5.1.

7.  **Dosage (Septic Shock):**
    - **Editorial:** "Start at 0.05 mcg/kg/min to 2 mcg/kg/min, titrated to achieve target mean arterial pressure."
    - **Source (FDA, p. 4):** "...suggested dosing infusion rate... is 0.05 mcg/kg/min to 2 mcg/kg/min, and is titrated to achieve a desired mean arterial pressure (MAP)."
    - **Location:** Section 5.2.

8.  **Storage (Dental):**
    - **Editorial:** "Store refrigerated (2°C to 8°C) and protect from light. Once removed for use, store below 25°C and use within 4 weeks. Do not return to the refrigerator."
    - **Source (TGA, p. 9):** "...should be stored at 2°C to 8°C... and protected from light. Once removed from refrigeration for use, store below 25°C and use within 4 weeks. Do not return to refrigerator."
    - **Location:** Section 11.

9.  **Visual Inspection:**
    - **Editorial:** "Discard if the solution is discolored (pinkish or brownish) or contains particulate matter."
    - **Source (FDA, p. 11/TGA p. 1/4):** "epinephrine solution deteriorates rapidly... turning pink... and brown... Do not use if... it contains particulate matter."
    - **Location:** Section 11.

## Close Paraphrases
- **Mechanism of Action (Dental):** The phrase "slows the absorption of the anesthetic, increasing the duration of action" is a very close paraphrase of the TGA source (p. 7) "reduces the rate of absorption... thereby increasing the duration of action."
- **Pregnancy (TGA):** The description of Category A is taken nearly directly from the standard TGA definition used in the source (p. 5), which is difficult to rewrite but remains unoriginal.
- **Drug Interactions:** The list of agents "increased by tricyclic antidepressants, MAO inhibitors, COMT inhibitors" follows the exact bulleted structure of the FDA PI (p. 7).

## Originality Strengths
- **Section 1 (Formulations Covered):** Successfully identifies and distinguishes between the USP injection and the lidocaine/epinephrine combination found in the TGA document.
- **Section 8 (Pregnancy Clinical Considerations):** The writer does a good job of synthesizing the FDA's warning about uterine contractions with the TGA's safety category into a coherent paragraph.
- **Structural Organization:** The editorial uses a logical structure that does not strictly follow either single PI but creates a "Unified PI" format.

## Differences from Source PIs
- **Harmonization:** The editorial adds bracketed source tags ([FDA], [TGA]) to clarify where specific regulatory guidance originates, which is not in either source.
- **Terminology:** The editorial uses "Epinephrine (also known as Adrenaline)" throughout to bridge the gap between US and Australian nomenclature.
- **Omissions:** The editorial omits highly specific pharmacokinetic data (e.g., the 45-minute infusion study on healthy men) in favor of a high-level clinical summary.
- **Merged Context:** It combines storage instructions for two very different product forms (vials vs. dental cartridges) into one section.

## Recommendation
**CONCERN / NEEDS REVISION**

While the document effectively synthesizes data from two disparate sources, it relies too heavily on "patchwriting"—taking whole phrases and sentences from the regulatory text and stitching them together. For a strict originality audit, the following revisions are required:

1.  **Rewrite Indications:** Use original phrasing for the dental and anaphylaxis sections (e.g., "Indicated for use in managing acute Type 1 allergic emergencies" instead of the verbatim FDA text).
2.  **Paraphrase Warnings:** The warnings regarding injection sites (digits/buttocks) and storage conditions are currently verbatim. These should be summarized in the author's own voice.
3.  **Refine Contraindications:** Rewrite the vascular and dental contraindications to avoid matching the TGA text word-for-word.

The editorial is an excellent *technical* summary, but as a piece of *original writing*, it remains too close to the source PIs.
