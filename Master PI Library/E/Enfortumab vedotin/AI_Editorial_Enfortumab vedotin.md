# Editorial Product Information: Enfortumab vedotin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following official Product Information (PI) source:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: PADCEV (Enfortumab vedotin) Product Information.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a clinically relevant format.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved PI before initiating treatment.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Enfortumab vedotin; a Nectin-4 directed antibody-drug conjugate (ADC).
- **Chemical Class:** It consists of a fully human IgG1 kappa monoclonal antibody conjugated to monomethyl auristatin E (MMAE), a microtubule-disrupting agent, via a protease-cleavable linker.
- **Brand Name:** PADCEV [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01FX13.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Powder for injection (lyophilized) in 20 mg and 30 mg single-dose vials. Following reconstitution, the concentration is 10 mg/mL [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Astellas Pharma Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **First-line Treatment of Urothelial Cancer:** Indicated in combination with pembrolizumab for adult patients with locally advanced or metastatic urothelial cancer (mUC) [Australian-approved].
- **Monotherapy for Urothelial Cancer:** Indicated for the treatment of adult patients with locally advanced or metastatic urothelial cancer who have previously received platinum-containing chemotherapy and a programmed death receptor-1 (PD-1) or programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) inhibitor [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
The recommended dose is **1.25 mg/kg** (maximum dose of **125 mg** for patients weighing ≥100 kg).

### Dosing by Indication
- **Monotherapy:** Administered on Days 1, 8, and 15 of a 28-day cycle.
- **Combination Therapy (with pembrolizumab):** Administered on Days 1 and 8 of a 21-day cycle. When administered on the same day, pembrolizumab should be given after enfortumab vedotin [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Reconstitution:** Reconstitute with Sterile Water for Injection (SWFI). Direct the stream along the vial walls, not directly onto the powder. Swirl gently; **do not shake**.
- **Dilution:** Further dilute in an infusion bag containing 5% Dextrose, 0.9% Sodium Chloride, or Lactated Ringer’s. Final concentration should be between 0.3 mg/mL to 4 mg/mL.
- **Infusion:** Administer over 30 minutes. Do **not** administer as an IV push or bolus. Do **not** co-administer other drugs through the same line.

### Dose Modifications for Toxicity
- **Dose Reduction Schedule:**
    - First reduction: 1.0 mg/kg (max 100 mg).
    - Second reduction: 0.75 mg/kg (max 75 mg).
    - Third reduction: 0.5 mg/kg (max 50 mg).
- **Specific Toxicities:**
    - **Skin Reactions (Grade 2 worsening):** Withhold until Grade ≤1.
    - **Skin Reactions (Grade 3/Severe):** Withhold until Grade ≤1, then resume at the same or reduced dose.
    - **Hyperglycaemia (>13.9 mmol/L):** Withhold until ≤13.9 mmol/L.
    - **Peripheral Neuropathy (Grade 2):** Withhold until Grade ≤1. Resume at the same dose (first occurrence) or reduced dose (recurrence).
    - **Permanent Discontinuation:** Required for Grade 4 or recurrent Grade 3 skin reactions; confirmed SJS or TEN; Grade ≥3 pneumonitis/ILD; or Grade ≥3 peripheral neuropathy [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to enfortumab vedotin or any of the formulation excipients [Australian].
- **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated due to the potential for fetal harm [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning [Australian]
**SERIOUS SKIN REACTIONS:**
**PADCEV** can cause severe and fatal cutaneous reactions, including Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) and Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN). Monitor patients closely. Withhold for suspected SJS/TEN or severe reactions. Permanently discontinue for confirmed SJS/TEN, Grade 4, or recurrent Grade 3 reactions.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Hyperglycaemia:** Severe and fatal cases, including diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA), have occurred. Monitor blood glucose regularly, particularly in patients with pre-existing diabetes or high BMI (≥30 kg/m²).
- **Pneumonitis/Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD):** Severe, life-threatening, or fatal events have been reported. Monitor for new or worsening respiratory symptoms (e.g., hypoxia, cough, dyspnea).
- **Peripheral Neuropathy:** Primarily sensory; monitor for new or worsening symptoms.
- **Ocular Disorders:** Predominantly dry eye. Prophylactic artificial tears should be considered; refer for ophthalmologic evaluation if symptoms persist.
- **Infusion Site Extravasation:** Ensure good venous access. Extravasation can lead to skin and soft tissue injury.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect profiles differ between monotherapy and combination therapy. Frequencies are derived from the Integrated Safety Set [Australian].

| Adverse Effect | Frequency (Monotherapy) | Frequency (with Pembrolizumab) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Alopecia** | Very Common (47.7%) | Very Common (38.5%) |
| **Fatigue** | Very Common (46.8%) | Very Common (53.7%) |
| **Diarrhoea** | Very Common (39.1%) | Very Common (39.2%) |
| **Peripheral Neuropathy** | Very Common (38.5%) | Very Common (66.7%) |
| **Rash (multiple terms)** | Very Common (11.6% - 23.6%) | Very Common (68.1%) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Diarrhoea, hyperglycaemia, and pneumonitis/ILD are the most common serious reactions (≥2%) [Australian].
- **Skin Reactions:** Occur at a higher rate in combination therapy (70%) compared to monotherapy (57%) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Concomitant use with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole) is predicted to increase unconjugated MMAE exposure (Cmax by 15% and AUC by 38%). Closely monitor for toxicity when used together [Australian].
- **CYP Substrates:** MMAE is unlikely to inhibit or induce major CYP450 enzymes at clinical concentrations.
- **Transporters:** MMAE is a P-gp substrate but not an inhibitor.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category D.** Enfortumab vedotin can cause fetal harm based on its mechanism of action and animal data (embryofetal lethality and malformations).
    - **Contraception:** Females of reproductive potential should have a pregnancy test within 7 days of starting. Use effective contraception during treatment and for **7 months** after the final dose.
    - **Males:** Advised to use effective contraception and not father a child during treatment and for **6 months** after the final dose [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety is not established. Discontinue breastfeeding during treatment and for at least **6 months** after the last dose [Australian].
- **Fertility:** MMAE is aneugenic. Animal studies indicate potential for impaired male fertility. Men should consider sperm cryopreservation before starting therapy [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients <18 years [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy were observed in patients ≥65 years compared to younger patients [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for mild, moderate, or severe impairment. It has not been evaluated in end-stage renal disease (CrCL <15 mL/min) [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for mild impairment. Use with caution in moderate or severe impairment (limited/no data available) [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** The ADC binds to Nectin-4 on the cell surface. The complex is internalised, and MMAE is released via proteolytic cleavage. MMAE disrupts the microtubule network, leading to cell cycle arrest and apoptosis.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Steady-state volume of distribution for ADC is approx. 12.8 L. MMAE is 68%–82% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily catabolism of the antibody and peptide; MMAE is metabolised mainly by oxidation via CYP3A4.
    - **Elimination:** MMAE is excreted mainly in faeces (72%) and to a lesser extent in urine (24%) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **EV-301 (Monotherapy):** A Phase 3 study in patients with mUC previously treated with platinum and PD-1/L1 inhibitors. Enfortumab vedotin showed superior OS (median 12.9 vs 9.0 months; HR 0.702) and PFS (median 5.6 vs 3.7 months; HR 0.615) compared to investigator-chosen chemotherapy [Australian].
- **EV-302 (Combination):** A Phase 3 study in previously untreated mUC. Enfortumab vedotin + pembrolizumab showed significant improvement in OS (median 31.5 vs 16.1 months; HR 0.468) and PFS (median 12.5 vs 6.3 months; HR 0.450) compared to platinum-based chemotherapy [Australian].
- **EV-103 (Cisplatin-ineligible):** A Phase 2 study confirming the efficacy of the combination in patients ineligible for cisplatin, showing a high Objective Response Rate (ORR) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Vials:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C. Do **not** freeze. Keep the vial in the outer carton to protect from light [Australian].
- **Reconstituted Solution:** If not used immediately, store in the vial for up to 24 hours at 2°C to 8°C.
- **Diluted Infusion Bag:** Store for no longer than 16 hours (including infusion time) at 2°C to 8°C. Do **not** freeze [Australian].
