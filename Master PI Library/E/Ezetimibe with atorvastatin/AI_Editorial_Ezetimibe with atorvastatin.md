# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on Product Information (PI) documentation provided by the following regulatory jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a coherent reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or SmPC required for prescribing in specific jurisdictions. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved labeling for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It should be administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
- **Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours. Daily maintenance doses typically remain at 50 mg/kg but may be adjusted based on clinical response. The highest recorded dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

### Dosing by Indication (ESRD)
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
- **Titration:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial; the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, occasionally within minutes of administration. These are reported most frequently in ESRD patients on dialysis. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate risks before considering re-administration [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:**
    - Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels before parenteral therapy.
    - Conduct weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations.
    - **Target Range:** Plasma free carnitine concentrations should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions in ESRD patients are often confounded by the underlying pathology of renal failure.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category B (Historical)]: Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate, well-controlled studies exist in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine levels after administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against maternal benefit. Consideration may be given to discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies show no evidence of impaired fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg for metabolic disorders; 10–20 mg/kg for ESRD). See Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65+ to determine if they respond differently than younger subjects [US].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Intravenous:** Specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis.
    - **Oral (Safety Note):** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in urine [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Efficacy:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis carnitine levels from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L by week 8.
- **Clinical Outcomes Note:** While IV levocarnitine effectively increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the impact on specific clinical outcomes or signs/symptoms of carnitine deficiency in this population has not been definitively determined in the provided source [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
