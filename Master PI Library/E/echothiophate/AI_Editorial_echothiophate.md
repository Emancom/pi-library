# Editorial Product Information: Echothiophate Iodide

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
*   **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview of echothiophate iodide, synthesised for healthcare professionals. It integrates clinical data, regulatory warnings, and administration guidelines from the provided official Product Information (PI).
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing in any particular region. At the time of this writing, only the United States (FDA) PI was available for review. Information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European) is noted as "Not specified in available sources" where applicable.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Echothiophate iodide (a long-acting cholinesterase inhibitor).
*   **Chemical Class:** Quaternary ammonium compound; (2-mercaptoethyl) trimethylammonium iodide O,O-diethyl phosphorothioate.
*   **Brand Name(s):** **Phospholine Iodide** [US].
*   **ATC Code:** S01EB03 (Antiglaucoma preparations and miotics, parasympathomimetics).
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    *   **Powder for Ophthalmic Solution:** 6.25 mg per bottle. 
    *   **Reconstituted Strength:** 0.125% (1/8%) solution when mixed with the provided 5 mL diluent [US].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical ophthalmic.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Fera Pharmaceuticals, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Reduction of Elevated Intraocular Pressure (IOP):** Indicated for the management of conditions associated with increased IOP [US].
*   **Accommodative Esotropia:** Indicated for concomitant esotropias with a significant accommodative component [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 General Dosing (Adult)
*   **Elevated IOP:** One drop instilled into the affected eye(s) twice daily (morning and immediately before retiring). 
*   **Optimization:** A twice-daily regimen is preferred to maintain a smooth diurnal tension curve. However, some patients may be managed with a single dose daily or every other day [US]. 
*   **Inconvenience Minimization:** To mitigate the impact of miosis-induced blurred vision, at least one of the daily doses should be administered shortly before bedtime [US].

### 3.2 Dosing by Indication (Pediatric)
*   **Accommodative Esotropia (Diagnosis):** One drop instilled daily in both eyes at bedtime for two to three weeks. A favourable response (reduction in deviation) typically occurs within hours [US].
*   **Accommodative Esotropia (Treatment):** Use the lowest effective frequency. After the diagnostic phase, the schedule may be reduced to one drop every other day. The maximum recommended dose is one drop daily [US].

### 3.3 Administration Instructions
*   **Nasolacrimal Occlusion:** Digital compression of the nasolacrimal ducts for one to two minutes following instillation is recommended to minimize systemic absorption via the nasal mucosa [US].
*   **Hygiene:** Wash hands immediately after instillation to prevent accidental skin absorption. Excess solution around the eye should be removed with a tissue [US].
*   **Reconstitution Procedure:** Use aseptic technique. Pour the provided diluent into the drug powder container, attach the dropper assembly, and shake for several seconds to ensure complete dissolution [US].

### 3.4 Titration and Duration
*   **Tolerance:** Long-term use may lead to a decrease in efficacy (tolerance). A "rest period" from the drug may restore its original activity [US].
*   **Withdrawal:** In esotropia, the drug may be gradually withdrawn after one to two years of successful management. If deviation recurs, surgery should be considered [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   **Active Uveal Inflammation:** Presence of active iritis or uveitis [US].
*   **Angle-Closure Glaucoma:** Most cases of angle-closure glaucoma where an iridectomy has not been performed, due to the risk of increasing pupillary/angle block [US].
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to echothiophate iodide or any component of the formulation (e.g., chlorobutanol, mannitol, boric acid) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
*   **Anaesthesia Interaction:** **Succinylcholine** must be administered with extreme caution, or avoided, in patients using echothiophate. Cholinesterase inhibition can significantly prolong the muscle-relaxant effects, potentially leading to respiratory or cardiovascular collapse [US].
*   **Systemic Additive Effects:** Caution is required in patients concurrently using systemic anticholinesterase medications (e.g., for Myasthenia Gravis) [US].
*   **Vagotonic Sensitivity:** Use with caution in patients with bronchial asthma, spastic gastrointestinal disorders, peptic ulcer, bradycardia, hypotension, epilepsy, or Parkinsonism [US].
*   **Retinal Detachment:** Use with caution in patients with a history of retinal detachment [US].
*   **Environmental Exposure:** Patients exposed to organophosphate or carbamate insecticides/pesticides should be warned of additive systemic toxicity via inhalation or skin absorption. Protective equipment (masks/clothing) may be necessary [US].
*   **Discontinuation Criteria:** Therapy should be temporarily suspended if systemic toxicity signs appear, including profuse sweating, urinary incontinence, diarrhoea, muscle weakness, or cardiac irregularities [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are listed by clinical significance; frequencies are not explicitly quantified in the source document.

### Ocular Effects
*   **Common/Expected:** Stinging, burning, increased lacrimation, lid muscle twitching, conjunctival/ciliary redness, and browache.
*   **Vision-Related:** Induced myopia and visual blurring (due to accommodative spasm).
*   **Structural:**
    *   **Iris Cysts:** Formation of cysts at the pupillary margin (more frequent in children). These usually regress upon dose reduction or discontinuation [US].
    *   **Lens Opacities:** Development of cataracts has been reported with echothiophate use [US].
    *   **Conjunctival/Nasolacrimal:** Prolonged use may cause conjunctival thickening or obstruction of the nasolacrimal canals [US].
*   **Serious Ocular:** Retinal detachment has been reported, even in patients without a prior history [US].
*   **Paradoxical Reaction:** A paradoxical increase in IOP may occur; this may be managed with sympathomimetic mydriatics like phenylephrine [US].

### Systemic Effects
*   **Cardiac:** Cardiac irregularities (arrhythmias) [US].
*   **Cholinergic Overstimulation:** Salivation, urinary incontinence, diarrhoea, and respiratory distress [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Succinylcholine:** Potentiation of neuromuscular blockade; risk of prolonged apnoea [US].
*   **Systemic Anticholinesterases:** Additive pharmacological effects and increased risk of systemic toxicity [US].
*   **Cholinesterase-Inhibiting Pesticides:** (e.g., organophosphates/carbamates) Additive systemic toxicity through environmental exposure [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   **US Classification:** Not specified by letter category in the current PI; however, the manufacturer states that animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. It is unknown if the drug causes fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants, a decision should be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the clinical importance of the medication to the mother [US].
*   **Fertility:** No data available regarding the impact on human fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness for the treatment of accommodative esotropia are established. Children are more prone to the development of iris cysts; regular eye examinations are required [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy have been observed between elderly and younger patients [US].
*   **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Echothiophate iodide is a long-acting (irreversible) cholinesterase inhibitor. It prevents the breakdown of acetylcholine, enhancing its effect on the iris sphincter (causing miosis) and the ciliary muscle (causing accommodative spasm and increasing the facility of aqueous humor outflow). This results in a significant reduction in IOP [US].
*   **Pharmacodynamics:** Topical administration causes a drop in both plasma and erythrocyte cholinesterase levels in most patients after several weeks of therapy [US].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    *   **Absorption:** Absorbed systemically through the conjunctiva and nasolacrimal mucosa.
    *   **Distribution/Metabolism/Elimination:** Not specified in available sources.

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Prior to Reconstitution:** Store under refrigeration at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) [US].
*   **After Reconstitution:** 
    *   Store upright at room temperature (approximately 25°C / 77°F).
    *   **Do not refrigerate** the reconstituted solution.
    *   **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused solution after 4 weeks [US].
