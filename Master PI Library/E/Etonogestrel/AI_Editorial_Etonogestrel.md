# Editorial Product Information: Etonogestrel

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from the official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of etonogestrel for healthcare professionals. It serves as a clinically accurate synthesis of the available regulatory data.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and is not intended to replace the official Product Information required for prescribing within any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the local PI before making prescribing decisions.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Etonogestrel (a 19-nortestosterone derivative).
- **Chemical Class:** Progestagen.
- **Brand Names:** **Implanon NXT** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** G03AC08.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Radiopaque subdermal implant containing 68 mg of etonogestrel. The implant is a non-biodegradable, white to off-white flexible rod (4.0 cm length, 2.0 mm diameter) preloaded in a sterile, disposable applicator [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Subdermal.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Organon Pharma Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Contraception:** Indicated for long-term prevention of pregnancy. The implant must be removed and may be replaced every three years to maintain efficacy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** A single implant is inserted subdermally and can remain for up to three years. In women with higher body weight, clinicians may consider earlier replacement if efficacy concerns arise in the third year of use [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Insertion must be performed by a qualified healthcare professional (HCP) familiar with the procedure.
    - **Location:** Subdermally on the inner side of the non-dominant upper arm, approximately 8-10 cm from the medial epicondyle of the humerus, avoiding the sulcus between the biceps and triceps to prevent neurovascular damage.
    - **Verification:** Presence of the implant must be verified by palpation by both the HCP and the patient immediately following insertion. If not palpable, imaging (X-ray, CT, ultrasound, or MRI) or etonogestrel blood level testing must be performed to confirm placement [Australian].
- **Timing of Insertion:**
    - **No preceding hormonal contraception:** Insert between Day 1 and Day 5 of the menstrual cycle.
    - **Switching from Combined Hormonal Contraceptives (COC, ring, patch):** Preferably the day after the last active tablet or on the day of removal of the ring/patch.
    - **Switching from Progestagen-only methods:** Insert on the day of the next injection (if using injectables) or the day of removal (if using an IUS or another implant).
    - **Post-abortion/Miscarriage:** First trimester (immediate insertion); Second trimester (21–28 days post-event).
    - **Postpartum:** Non-breastfeeding (21–28 days postpartum); Breastfeeding (after the fourth postpartum week) [Australian].
- **Missed Dose/Replacement:** If the 3-year window is exceeded, contraceptive efficacy cannot be guaranteed.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known or suspected pregnancy.
- Active thromboembolic disorders (venous or arterial).
- Known or suspected sex steroid-sensitive malignancies (e.g., breast cancer).
- Current or history of severe hepatic disease (until liver function tests normalize).
- Current or history of liver tumors (benign or malignant).
- Undiagnosed vaginal bleeding.
- Hypersensitivity to etonogestrel or any excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
- **Thrombotic Events:** Use of etonogestrel has been associated with postmarketing reports of serious arterial and venous thromboembolic events, including pulmonary emboli (some fatal), deep vein thrombosis, myocardial infarction, and strokes. The implant should be removed in the event of a thrombosis [Australian].
- **Carcinoma of the Breast:** The risk of breast cancer increases with age and may be slightly higher in users of hormonal contraceptives; however, the risk gradually disappears within 10 years of discontinuation.
- **Insertion/Removal Complications:** Deep insertion may lead to neural or vascular damage. Implants may migrate within the arm or, in rare cases, to the pulmonary vasculature. If the implant is not palpable, it must be localized before removal [Australian].
- **Ectopic Pregnancy:** While etonogestrel inhibits ovulation, ectopic pregnancy should be considered in the differential diagnosis for users presenting with amenorrhea or abdominal pain.
- **Metabolic Effects:** Diabetic women and those with hyperlipidemia should be monitored closely, as progestagens may affect insulin resistance and lipid profiles.
- **Blood Pressure:** Use should be discontinued if a significant or sustained increase in blood pressure occurs that does not respond to therapy [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Headache (13.3%), Acne (14.8%), Weight increase (10.4%) [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Breast pain (9.8%), Abdominal pain (4.7%), Dysmenorrhea (4.4%), Injection site pain (4.1%), Dizziness (3.8%), Depression (3.2%), Libido decreased (3.2%), Nervousness (2.9%), Flatulence (2.6%), Alopecia (2.5%), Nausea (2.5%).
- **Bleeding Patterns:** Approximately 1 in 5 women experience amenorrhea, while another 1 in 5 experience frequent or prolonged bleeding [Australian].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Thromboembolism, implant migration (including to the pulmonary artery), and idiopathic intracranial hypertension [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Hepatic Enzyme Inducers (CYP450):** Drugs that induce microsomal enzymes (e.g., phenytoin, barbiturates, carbamazepine, rifampicin, rifabutin, efavirenz, and St. John's Wort) may increase the clearance of etonogestrel, potentially leading to contraceptive failure. A barrier method is recommended during use and for 28 days after discontinuing these drugs [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Strong (e.g., ketoconazole) or moderate (e.g., diltiazem) inhibitors may increase serum concentrations of etonogestrel.
- **Effects on Other Drugs:** Etonogestrel may interfere with the metabolism of other medications, potentially increasing concentrations of cyclosporine or decreasing concentrations of lamotrigine [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. Contraindicated. If pregnancy occurs, the implant must be removed. Animal studies show potential masculinization of female fetuses at high doses, though clinical data in humans have not confirmed birth defect risks from accidental use.
- **Breastfeeding:** Small amounts of etonogestrel are excreted in breast milk (approx. 0.2% of maternal dose). Studies indicate no adverse effects on milk production/quality or the growth and development of the child. It may be used during lactation after the 4th postpartum week [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Ovulation typically returns shortly after removal (within days to weeks). Fertility is restored rapidly upon removal of the implant [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in women under 18 years of age [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Not studied in women over 65 years of age [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in the presence of severe hepatic disease until liver function returns to normal [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Primarily inhibits ovulation. It also increases the viscosity of cervical mucus, hindering sperm passage [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Does not completely suppress ovarian activity; estradiol levels remain above those seen in the early-follicular phase. It has no clinically relevant effect on bone mineral density [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; ovulation-inhibiting levels reached within 24 hours. Peak concentrations (400–3,000 pg/mL) occur within 14 days.
    - **Distribution:** 95.5–99% protein-bound (primarily albumin).
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolized by CYP3A4 via hydroxylation, reduction, and conjugation.
    - **Elimination:** Mean half-life is approximately 25 hours. Excreted in urine and feces (ratio 1.5:1) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** Three pivotal trials involved 1,286 women over 2,093 woman-years. 
- **Efficacy:** No pregnancies occurred during the treatment period in these trials. The **Pearl Index was 0.00** (95% CI 0.00–0.18) [Australian].
- **Bioequivalence:** A study confirmed that the current radiopaque implant (Implanon NXT) is bioequivalent to the non-radiopaque version [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 30°C.
- Do not insert after the expiry date indicated on the primary package [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
