# Editorial Product Information: Etrasimod

## Editorial Note
*   **Jurisdictions Contributed:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration).
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the Product Information (PI) for **etrasimod**, intended for use by healthcare professionals. It serves as a clinically accurate reference for prescribing and monitoring.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local regulatory documentation before initiating therapy.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Etrasimod (present as etrasimod arginine). 
*   **Chemical Class:** Sphingosine 1-phosphate (S1P) receptor modulator.
*   **Brand Name(s):** **VELSIPITY** [Australian].
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 2 mg film-coated tablets (green, round, approximately 6 mm diameter, debossed with "ETR" and "2") [Australian].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Ulcerative Colitis (UC):** Treatment of adults with moderately to severely active UC who have had an inadequate response, loss of response, or intolerance to conventional, biologic, or Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitor therapies [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
*   The recommended dose is 2 mg taken orally once daily [Australian].
*   Treatment should be initiated under the supervision of a physician experienced in the management of UC [Australian].

### Pre-Dosing Assessments
Before initiation, the following must be performed:
*   **Electrocardiogram (ECG):** To screen for pre-existing conduction abnormalities [Australian].
*   **Complete Blood Count (CBC):** Must include a recent lymphocyte count (within 6 months or after discontinuing prior therapy) [Australian].
*   **Liver Function Tests (LFTs):** Obtain recent transaminase and bilirubin levels (within 6 months) [Australian].
*   **Ophthalmic Assessment:** Evaluation of the fundus (including the macula) is required for patients with a history of diabetes, uveitis, retinal disease, or uncontrolled hypertension, and in elderly patients [Australian].
*   **Vaccinations:** Update immunisations per current guidelines. Live attenuated vaccines must be administered at least 4 weeks prior to initiation [Australian].
*   **Pregnancy Test:** Exclude pregnancy in women of childbearing potential [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
*   Tablets should be swallowed whole, with or without food [Australian].

### First-Dose Monitoring
*   A 4-hour monitoring period (hourly pulse and blood pressure) is recommended after the first dose **only** in patients with specific cardiac risks: resting heart rate <50 bpm, second-degree [Mobitz type I] AV block, or a history of myocardial infarction or heart failure [Australian].
*   ECG is recommended at the start and end of this 4-hour period [Australian].

### Missed Dose
*   If a dose is missed, take the next dose at the scheduled time. Do not double the dose [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
**Etrasimod** is contraindicated in patients with:
*   **Recent Cardiovascular Events (last 6 months):** Myocardial infarction, unstable angina pectoris, stroke, transient ischaemic attack (TIA), or decompensated heart failure requiring hospitalisation [Australian].
*   **Heart Failure:** NYHA Class III/IV [Australian].
*   **Cardiac Conduction Disorders:** History or presence of Mobitz type II second-degree AV block, third-degree AV block, sick sinus syndrome, or sino-atrial block, unless a functioning pacemaker is present [Australian].
*   **Pregnancy and Breastfeeding:** See Section 8 [Australian].
*   **Malignancy:** Active malignancies [Australian].
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to the active substance or any excipients (including tartrazine) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Bradyarrhythmia and Conduction Delays
*   Initiation may cause a transient decrease in heart rate and AV conduction delays. The greatest mean decrease occurs 2–3 hours post-dose [Australian].
*   Caution is advised in patients taking beta-blockers or calcium channel blockers due to additive heart rate effects [Australian].

### Infections
*   **Etrasimod** causes reversible sequestration of lymphocytes, reducing peripheral counts to approximately 45% of baseline. This increases susceptibility to infections [Australian].
*   Delay initiation in patients with active infections [Australian].
*   Interrupt treatment if absolute lymphocyte count (ALC) is confirmed <0.2 × 10⁹/L; re-initiation may be considered once ALC >0.5 × 10⁹/L [Australian].
*   **PML:** While no cases were reported in the development program, Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy (PML) has occurred with other S1P modulators. Suspend treatment if PML is suspected; discontinue if confirmed [Australian].

### Macular Oedema
*   Risk is increased in patients with diabetes, uveitis, or retinal disease.
*   An ophthalmic evaluation is recommended for all patients 3–4 months after starting treatment, and at any time if visual changes occur [Australian].

### Liver Injury
*   Elevations in transaminases may occur. Discontinue if significant liver injury is confirmed [Australian].

### Cutaneous Malignancies
*   There is a potential risk of malignant skin growths. Patients should undergo periodic skin examinations and limit exposure to sunlight/UV radiation [Australian].

### Respiratory Effects
*   Reductions in FEV1 and FVC have been observed. Use with caution in patients with severe respiratory disease (e.g., pulmonary fibrosis, COPD) [Australian].

### Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES)
*   Though not reported for etrasimod, PRES is a class effect of S1P modulators. Discontinue if neurological symptoms suggestive of PRES (e.g., cognitive deficits, visual disturbances) occur and are confirmed [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most common adverse drug reactions are lymphopenia (11%) and headache (7%) [Australian].

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Lymphopenia |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Headache, dizziness, urinary tract infection, hypertension, bradycardia, elevated hepatic enzymes, nausea, pyrexia, visual impairment, hypercholesterolaemia |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Macular oedema, atrioventricular block (first-degree and Mobitz type I) |

### Serious Adverse Effects
*   Serious infections.
*   Macular oedema.
*   Clinically significant bradyarrhythmias.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
*   **CYP Inhibitors:** Co-administration with moderate-to-strong inhibitors of two or more of CYP2C8, 2C9, or 3A4 (e.g., fluconazole) increases etrasimod exposure and is not recommended [Australian].
*   **CYP Inducers:** Co-administration with moderate-to-strong inducers of these enzymes (e.g., rifampicin) decreases exposure and is not recommended [Australian].
*   **Transporters:** Etrasimod is not a substrate of P-gp, BCRP, or OATP1B1/3; inhibitors of these transporters are unlikely to affect its kinetics [Australian].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
*   **Anti-neoplastic/Immune-modulating therapies:** Risk of additive immune effects. Use caution when switching from or co-administering these agents [Australian].
*   **Heart Rate Lowering Drugs:** Caution with beta-blockers, Class Ia and III anti-arrhythmics, and QT-prolonging drugs [Australian].
*   **Vaccines:** Avoid live attenuated vaccines during treatment and for 2 weeks after discontinuation [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category D]. Etrasimod is contraindicated during pregnancy. Animal studies demonstrate embryolethality and fetal malformations (aortic vessel anomalies, septal defects) at clinically relevant exposures [Australian].
*   **Contraception:** Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during treatment and for 7 days after the last dose [Australian].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. It is unknown if etrasimod is excreted in human milk, but it was detected in the milk of lactating rats [Australian].
*   **Fertility:** No adverse effects on male or female fertility were observed in animal models [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children <16 years have not been established [Australian].
*   **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment is required for patients >65 years, though caution is advised due to a higher frequency of comorbidities [Australian].
*   **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required in patients with renal impairment, including severe impairment [Australian].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild-to-moderate impairment (Child-Pugh A/B). **Not recommended** in patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C) [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Etrasimod is an S1P receptor modulator that binds with high affinity to S1P receptors 1, 4, and 5. It partially and reversibly blocks the egress of lymphocytes from lymphoid organs, reducing the number of activated lymphocytes in peripheral blood and tissue [Australian].

### Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Tmax is approximately 4 hours (range 2–8 hours). Steady state is reached within 7 days [Australian].
*   **Distribution:** High protein binding (97.9%). Vd is approximately 66 L [Australian].
*   **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised via CYP2C8, 2C9, and 3A4, with minor contributions from UGTs and sulfotransferases [Australian].
*   **Elimination:** Half-life is approximately 30 hours. Primarily eliminated via faeces (82%) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
The efficacy of etrasimod 2 mg daily was demonstrated in two pivotal Phase 3 trials:
*   **ELEVATE UC 52:** A treat-through study. Clinical remission was significantly higher with etrasimod vs. placebo at Week 12 (27% vs. 7%) and Week 52 (32% vs. 7%) [Australian].
*   **ELEVATE UC 12:** An induction study. Clinical remission at Week 12 was significantly higher with etrasimod vs. placebo (25% vs. 15%) [Australian].
*   Key secondary endpoints including endoscopic improvement, symptomatic remission, and mucosal healing were significantly improved in both studies compared to placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
*   **Packaging:** Supplied in HDPE bottles (30 tablets) or aluminum strips (7, 28, or 98 tablets) [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **Australia:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
