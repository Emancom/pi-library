## 5. Contraindications
Ethynodiol + ethinyl is contraindicated in females who are known to have or develop any of the following conditions (items listed exactly as specified in available sources):

- A past history of deep vein thrombophlebitis or thromboembolic disorders.
- Cerebrovascular disease, myocardial infarction, or coronary artery disease, or a past history of these conditions.
- Current diagnosis of, or history of, breast cancer, which may be hormone‑sensitive.
- Known or suspected carcinoma of the female reproductive organs or suspected estrogen‑dependent neoplasia, or a history of these conditions.
- Undiagnosed abnormal genital bleeding.
- History of cholestatic jaundice of pregnancy or jaundice with prior oral contraceptive use.
- Past or present, benign or malignant liver tumors.
- Known or suspected pregnancy.
- Concurrent use by women receiving Hepatitis C drug combinations containing ombitasvir/paritaprevir/ritonavir, with or without dasabuvir, due to the potential for marked ALT elevations.

(If additional contraindications are present in other jurisdictions' PIs, they are not specified in the available sources provided.)

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- Boxed warning text not explicitly specified as such in the available sources. The following critical warning is emphasized in the provided material:  
  - **Cigarette smoking increases the risk of serious cardiovascular side effects from oral contraceptive use; the risk increases with age and with heavy smoking (≥15 cigarettes/day) and is markedly increased in women over 35 years. Women who use oral contraceptives should be strongly advised not to smoke.**

### General warnings and precautions
- Venous and arterial thromboembolism: Use is associated with an increased risk of venous and arterial thromboembolic events (deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism, thrombophlebitis) and postoperative thromboembolic complications. The relative risk estimates described in available sources indicate substantially increased risk compared with nonusers; the risk is higher in women with predisposing conditions.
- Myocardial infarction: Increased risk associated with oral contraceptive use, particularly in smokers and women with other coronary risk factors (hypertension, obesity, diabetes, hyperlipidemia). Relative risk estimates reported for current users range from approximately 2 to 6 in some studies; risk is low under age 30 but not absent.
- Cerebrovascular disease (stroke): Increased risk of both thrombotic and hemorrhagic strokes has been reported in oral contraceptive users, particularly older women, hypertensive women and smokers. Reported relative risks vary widely depending on underlying blood pressure status and smoking.
- Persistence of vascular risk: Some studies (notably with higher‑estrogen formulations) showed persistence of increased vascular risk for years after discontinuation in certain age groups or with long prior use.
- Dose-related vascular risk: Risk of vascular disease has been associated with the amount of estrogen and progestogen in the preparation. Minimizing estrogen and progestogen exposure is recommended in clinical decision making.
- Malignant neoplasms:
  - Breast cancer: Oral contraceptives are contraindicated in women with current or past breast cancer (hormonally sensitive). Epidemiologic studies have not consistently shown an association between ever use and breast cancer; some studies report a small increase in risk among current or recent users.
  - Cervical neoplasia: Some studies suggest an association between oral contraceptive use and increased risk of cervical intraepithelial neoplasia or carcinoma in some populations, though causality is uncertain.
- Hepatic neoplasia: Benign hepatic adenomas and other liver lesions have been associated with oral contraceptive use; rupture can cause intraabdominal hemorrhage and death. Long-term use (>8 years) has been associated with rare increases in hepatocellular carcinoma risk, though absolute risk is very low.
- Risk of elevated liver enzymes with certain Hepatitis C therapies: Women receiving ombitasvir/paritaprevir/ritonavir-containing regimens (with or without dasabuvir) experienced ALT elevations; concomitant use with ethinyl estradiol-containing medications is contraindicated. Ethynodiol + ethinyl should be discontinued prior to starting those Hepatitis C regimens and may be resumed approximately 2 weeks after completion of Hepatitis C therapy.
- Ocular lesions: Reports of retinal thrombosis and other ocular vascular lesions exist. Discontinue oral contraceptive use if unexplained, gradual or sudden partial or complete loss of vision, proptosis, diplopia, papilledema, or any evidence of retinal vascular lesions occur.
- Use before or during pregnancy: Extensive epidemiologic studies show no increased risk of birth defects when OCs have been used prior to pregnancy; inadvertent exposure in early pregnancy has not been shown to be teratogenic in the majority of studies. Oral contraceptives should not be used to treat threatened or habitual abortion. If pregnancy is suspected, pregnancy should be ruled out before continuing use; discontinue if pregnancy is confirmed.
- Gallbladder disease: Earlier studies suggested increased lifetime relative risk of gallbladder surgery; more recent studies indicate minimal relative risk among current lower‑dose formulations.
- Carbohydrate and lipid metabolic effects: Oral contraceptives can decrease glucose tolerance (effect related to estrogen dose) and different progestogens may increase insulin secretion and insulin resistance. Some progestogens decrease HDL cholesterol. Prediabetic and diabetic women should be carefully observed while taking oral contraceptives.

### Monitoring requirements and clinical actions (as stated in available sources)
- Advise all women not to smoke, especially those over 35.
- Discontinue oral contraceptives at least 4 weeks prior to and for 2 weeks after elective surgery associated with increased thromboembolism risk, if feasible.
- Avoid starting oral contraceptives earlier than 4 to 6 weeks postpartum in women who elect not to breastfeed (immediate postpartum period is associated with increased thromboembolic risk).
- Carefully observe prediabetic and diabetic women for changes in glucose tolerance.
- Evaluate women with abdominal pain/tenderness, abdominal mass, or shock for possible hepatic adenoma.
- Discontinue immediately and evaluate if unexplained visual changes, proptosis, diplopia, papilledema, or retinal vascular signs occur.
- Rule out pregnancy in women with two consecutive missed periods before continuing oral contraceptive use; with nonadherence consider pregnancy at the first missed period.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic interactions (as specified in available sources)
- Hepatitis C direct-acting antiviral combinations:
  - Concurrent use with regimens containing ombitasvir/paritaprevir/ritonavir, with or without dasabuvir, is contraindicated due to risk of clinically significant ALT elevations. Management: discontinue **ethynodiol + ethinyl** prior to initiating such Hepatitis C therapy; **ethynodiol + ethinyl** may be restarted approximately 2 weeks after completion of the Hepatitis C regimen.
- Other drug–drug interactions with clinical significance: Not specified in available sources.

### Management notes
- Discontinue **ethynodiol + ethinyl** prior to starting the specified Hepatitis C regimen; resume approximately 2 weeks after completion.

(Other interactions commonly considered for combined oral contraceptives — e.g., enzyme inducers that may reduce contraceptive efficacy — are not specified in the available sources and therefore are not listed here.)

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Pregnancy category systems (jurisdictional pregnancy categories, e.g., Australian or US letter categories): Not specified in available sources.
- Key information:
  - Extensive epidemiological studies reported in the available sources have not demonstrated an increased risk of birth defects in women who used oral contraceptives prior to pregnancy.
  - Most recent studies do not suggest teratogenic effects when oral contraceptives are taken inadvertently during early pregnancy.
  - Oral contraceptives should not be used to treat threatened or habitual abortion.
  - If pregnancy is suspected (e.g., two consecutive missed periods), pregnancy should be ruled out before continuing contraceptive use; discontinue if pregnancy is confirmed.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Combination oral contraceptives containing ethynodiol diacetate and ethinyl estradiol act primarily by suppression of gonadotropins. The principal mechanism is inhibition of ovulation. Additional contraceptive effects include changes in cervical mucus that impede sperm entry and changes in the endometrium that may reduce likelihood of implantation.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption, distribution, metabolism, elimination: Not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Primary pharmacodynamic effect is suppression of ovulation via negative feedback on gonadotropin secretion; estrogens and progestogens exert additional effects on cervical mucus and the endometrium.
