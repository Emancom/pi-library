# Editorial Product Information: Ergometrine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, intended to consolidate clinical and regulatory information into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidelines required for prescribing in specific jurisdictions. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ergometrine maleate (an amine ergot alkaloid).
- **Brand Names:** DBL Ergometrine Injection [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Injection: 500 micrograms/1 mL in a glass ampoule [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intramuscular (IM) or Intravenous (IV) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Prophylaxis of postpartum and postabortion haemorrhage:** Administered after the delivery of the placenta to prevent uterine atony.
- **Treatment of postpartum and postabortion haemorrhage:** Administered after the delivery of the placenta to promote uterine involution and manage haemorrhage.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Intramuscular (IM):** The standard dose for both prophylaxis and treatment is 200 micrograms.
- **Intravenous (IV):** In emergency situations involving excessive bleeding, 200 micrograms may be administered.

### Administration Instructions
- **Timing:** **Ergometrine must not be administered until delivery of the placenta is confirmed** and the possibility of a multiple pregnancy (second twin) has been excluded.
- **IV Rate:** IV doses must be administered slowly over at least one minute to minimize the risk of severe adverse effects, such as sudden hypertension or cerebrovascular accidents.
- **Dilution:** It is recommended by some clinicians to dilute the 200 microgram IV dose to a volume of 5 mL using 0.9% sodium chloride.
- **Refractory Cases:** If a patient does not respond, clinicians should consider hypocalcaemia. Cautious IV administration of calcium may restore uterine responsiveness, though this must be avoided in patients receiving digitalis.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity or idiosyncratic reactions to ergometrine, other ergot alkaloids, or any excipients.
- Induction of labour or use during the first and second stages of labour.
- Suspicion of a retained placenta.
- Eclampsia or pre-eclampsia.
- Threatened spontaneous abortion.
- Severe or persistent sepsis.
- Pre-existing cardiovascular conditions: Peripheral vascular disease, heart disease, or a history of hypertension.
- Impaired hepatic or renal function [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Intravenous Use:** Limited to life-threatening emergencies due to the high risk of severe adverse effects (hypertension, seizures) if injected too rapidly or undiluted.
- **Cardiovascular Effects:**
    - **Hypertension:** May occur suddenly, particularly with IV use or concurrent administration of regional anaesthetics or vasoconstrictors.
    - **Coronary Artery Disease:** Patients are susceptible to angina or myocardial infarction due to drug-induced vasospasm.
    - **Raynaud’s Phenomenon:** Use with caution; discontinue if signs of vasoconstriction appear.
- **Uterine Tetany:** High doses or administration prior to delivery can cause tetanic contractions, leading to uterine rupture, cervical laceration, amniotic fluid embolism, or foetal trauma/hypoxia.
- **Prolonged Therapy:** Continued use may lead to ergotism and gangrene. Treatment must stop if numbness or tingling of extremities occurs.
- **Porphyria:** Ergometrine is associated with clinical exacerbations of porphyria.
- **Monitoring:** Blood pressure and heart rate should be monitored closely, especially following IV administration.

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Note: Precise frequency data (e.g., % occurrence) is not specified in the available source; however, the following reactions have been identified.*

- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (sometimes severe/sudden), hypotension, coronary artery vasospasm, myocardial infarction, ventricular arrhythmias, palpitations, bradycardia, thrombophlebitis, and peripheral vasospasm.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal pain, and oesophageal spasm. Rare reports of mesenteric ischaemia and large bowel infarction.
- **Nervous System:** Headache, dizziness, vertigo, hallucinations, and seizures (associated with hypertension).
- **Respiratory:** Dyspnoea, nasal congestion, pulmonary oedema, and pleural thickening.
- **General/Allergic:** Allergic phenomena including anaphylactic shock; sweating, unpleasant taste, and tinnitus.
- **Musculoskeletal:** Leg cramps; single case report of unmasking myasthenia gravis.
- **Urogenital:** Haematuria and severe uterine cramping.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **Vasoconstrictors/Sympathomimetics:** Potentiate ergometrine’s effects, increasing the risk of severe hypertension and cerebral haemorrhage.
    - **Beta-blockers:** Additive peripheral vasoconstriction.
    - **Anaesthetics:** Halothane (>1%) may interfere with oxytocic action; general anaesthetics may potentiate vasoconstriction.
    - **Dopamine:** Concurrent use may lead to excessive vasoconstriction and gangrene.
- **Specific Drug Interactions:**
    - **Antianginal agents (e.g., Glyceryl Trinitrate):** Ergometrine may reduce the efficacy of these agents by inducing vasospasm.
    - **Bromocriptine:** Increased risk of hypertension, strokes, and myocardial infarction in the postpartum period.
    - **Sumatriptan:** Theoretical risk of additive vasospastic reactions.
    - **Antibiotics:** Erythromycin and tetracyclines (e.g., doxycycline) may increase the risk of ergot toxicity/ergotism.
    - **Nicotine:** Enhanced vasoconstriction in heavy smokers.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C [Australian].** Ergometrine induces uterine contractions and is contraindicated during pregnancy except for the specified indications after delivery. It may cause premature labour or foetal distress if used inappropriately.
- **Breastfeeding:** Ergometrine is secreted in breast milk. While a single postpartum dose is generally considered acceptable and should not prevent breastfeeding, repeated doses are **contraindicated** due to the risk of chronic ergot poisoning in the infant and the suppression of prolactin/lactation.
- **Fertility:** No data available in source documents.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Ergometrine is not indicated for paediatric use. Accidental administration to neonates (sometimes mistaken for Vitamin K) is a serious risk, leading to symptoms such as respiratory failure, convulsions, acute renal failure, and gangrene. One neonatal death was reported following accidental oral administration [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Use is contraindicated in patients with impaired renal or hepatic function [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Ergometrine is a direct stimulant of uterine and vascular smooth muscle. It increases the amplitude, frequency, and basal tone of uterine contractions. It acts as a partial agonist/antagonist at alpha-adrenergic, dopaminergic, and tryptaminergic receptors.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** At therapeutic doses, it produces rhythmic contractions; higher doses cause sustained tetanic contractions. It causes vasoconstriction primarily of capacitance vessels.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Onset:** IV (immediate to 1 minute); IM (2 to 5 minutes).
    - **Duration:** IV (approximately 45 minutes); IM (3 hours or longer).
    - **Metabolism/Elimination:** Primarily metabolised in the liver. Elimination may be prolonged in neonates.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze) [Australian].
- **Handling:** Protect ampoules from light.
