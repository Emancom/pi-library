# Editorial Product Information: Etoricoxib

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of etoricoxib, intended for use by healthcare professionals as a clinically accurate reference. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidelines required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority for specific legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Etoricoxib (a selective cyclooxygenase-2 [COX-2] inhibitor).
- **Chemical Class:** Coxibs.
- **Brand Names:** **ARCOXIA** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** M01AH05.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 30 mg, 60 mg, 90 mg, and 120 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Organon Pharma Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Etoricoxib is indicated for the following conditions [Australian]:
- **Osteoarthritis (OA):** Symptomatic treatment of signs and symptoms.
- **Acute Gouty Arthritis:** Management of acute flares.
- **Acute Pain:** Including primary dysmenorrhoea and minor dental procedures.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Principles
As cardiovascular (CV) risks may increase with higher doses and longer exposure, the lowest effective daily dose should be utilized for the shortest duration necessary. 

### Adults
- **Osteoarthritis:** Recommended dose is 30 mg once daily. If relief is insufficient, the dose may be increased to 60 mg once daily. If no benefit is seen after 4 weeks at 60 mg, alternative therapies should be considered [Australian].
- **Acute Gouty Arthritis:** 120 mg once daily for the symptomatic period, limited to a maximum of 8 days.
- **Primary Dysmenorrhoea:** 120 mg once daily for the symptomatic period, limited to a maximum of 8 days.
- **Dental Pain (Minor Procedures):** 90 mg once daily for the symptomatic period, limited to a maximum of 8 days.

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets are administered orally and may be taken with or without food.
- Doses exceeding the recommended maximums for each indication have not demonstrated additional efficacy or have not been studied [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
Etoricoxib is contraindicated in patients with [Australian]:
- Hypersensitivity to the active ingredient or any excipients (e.g., lactose).
- A history of asthma, urticaria, or allergic-type reactions following the use of aspirin or other Non-Steroidal Anti-inflammatory Drugs (NSAIDs).
- Congestive heart failure (NYHA II-IV).
- Uncontrolled hypertension (persistently elevated blood pressure >140/90 mmHg).
- Established ischaemic heart disease, peripheral arterial disease, and/or cerebrovascular disease.
- Active peptic ulceration or gastrointestinal (GI) bleeding.
- Severe hepatic dysfunction (Child-Pugh score $\ge$ 10 or serum albumin < 25 g/L).
- Estimated creatinine clearance < 30 mL/min.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** Selective COX-2 inhibitors are associated with an increased risk of serious thrombotic events (myocardial infarction, stroke). This risk may increase with dose and duration. Use with caution in patients with significant CV risk factors (diabetes, hypertension, hypercholesterolaemia, smoking) [Australian].
- **Hypertension:** Etoricoxib may cause more frequent and severe hypertension than other NSAIDs. Blood pressure should be monitored within two weeks of initiation and periodically thereafter.
- **Gastrointestinal Effects:** Upper GI ulcers, bleeding, and perforations can occur without warning. Risk is higher in patients >65 years or those with a history of GI disease. Concomitant use with low-dose aspirin further increases GI risk.
- **Renal Effects:** Long-term administration may result in renal papillary necrosis. Use caution in patients with compromised renal perfusion, heart failure, or cirrhosis. Rehydrate dehydrated patients before starting therapy.
- **Hepatic Effects:** Elevations in ALT/AST (approx. 3x upper limit of normal) occur in ~1% of patients. Discontinue if signs of hepatic insufficiency appear or if abnormal liver tests persist.
- **Serious Skin Reactions:** Reactions such as DRESS syndrome, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, and toxic epidermal necrolysis have been reported. Discontinue at the first sign of rash or mucosal lesions.
- **Infection:** Etoricoxib may mask fever and other signs of inflammation/infection.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequencies from clinical trials in Osteoarthritis [Australian]:

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Nasopharyngitis, Upper Respiratory Tract Infection, Urinary Tract Infection, Dizziness, Headache, Hypertension, Diarrhoea, Dyspepsia, Nausea, Peripheral Oedema, Abdominal Pain (upper). |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Anxiety, depression, somnolence, vertigo, palpitations, flushing, cough, dyspnoea, constipation, gastritis, mouth ulceration, pruritus, muscle cramp, proteinuria, fatigue. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Jaundice, hepatic failure, angioedema, bronchospasm, thrombocytopenia. |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Confirmed thrombotic CV events, gastrointestinal perforation/bleeding, and serious skin reactions (DRESS, SJS).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** INR should be monitored, particularly in the first few days after initiation or change in etoricoxib therapy [Australian].
- **Diuretics, ACE Inhibitors, and Angiotensin II Antagonists (AIIAs):** NSAIDs may diminish the antihypertensive effect of these drugs. In elderly or volume-depleted patients, co-administration may lead to acute renal failure.
- **Lithium:** Etoricoxib may increase plasma lithium levels by reducing renal clearance.
- **Methotrexate:** Standard monitoring for toxicity is required; etoricoxib doses >90 mg may increase methotrexate plasma concentrations.
- **Oral Contraceptives:** Etoricoxib (60–120 mg) can increase ethinyl oestradiol exposure (AUC) by 37–60%. This should be considered when selecting a contraceptive dose.
- **Rifampicin:** A potent inducer of hepatic metabolism; co-administration leads to a 65% decrease in etoricoxib plasma concentrations.
- **Digoxin:** No dosage adjustment is generally required.
- **Aspirin:** Can be used concomitantly at low doses for CV prophylaxis; however, etoricoxib is not a substitute for aspirin antiplatelet therapy. Concurrent use significantly increases GI ulceration rates.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy [Australian Category C]:** Etoricoxib is not recommended for use in pregnancy. In the third trimester (from 20 weeks onwards), use may cause foetal renal dysfunction leading to oligohydramnios and premature closure of the ductus arteriosus. Use during the first two trimesters only if the potential benefit outweighs the risk.
- **Breastfeeding:** Etoricoxib is excreted in the milk of lactating rats. It is unknown if it is excreted in human milk. A decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the importance of the medication to the mother.
- **Fertility:** Based on its mechanism (prostaglandin inhibition), etoricoxib may impair female fertility and is not recommended in women attempting to conceive.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly:** No dosage adjustment is generally required, but caution is advised due to increased sensitivity to adverse effects.
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild (Child-Pugh 5-6):** Do not exceed 60 mg once daily.
    - **Moderate (Child-Pugh 7-9):** Do not exceed 60 mg every other day (or 30 mg once daily).
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh >9):** Contraindicated.
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **CrCl $\ge$ 30 mL/min:** No dosage adjustment required.
    - **CrCl < 30 mL/min:** Use is not recommended/Contraindicated.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Etoricoxib is a selective inhibitor of cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2) within the clinical dose range. It does not inhibit COX-1 at doses up to 150 mg daily, thereby sparing prostaglandin-mediated gastric cytoprotection and platelet function [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** 100% oral bioavailability; $T_{max}$ is ~1 hour (fasted). Food has no clinically significant effect on absorption.
    - **Distribution:** ~92% protein bound; $V_{dss}$ is ~120 L. Crosses the blood-brain barrier and placenta.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolized in the liver, primarily via CYP3A4.
    - **Elimination:** Excreted mainly as metabolites in the urine (70%). Elimination half-life is approximately 22 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **MEDAL Program:** A large-scale CV outcomes program (n=34,701) comparing etoricoxib (60 mg or 90 mg) to diclofenac (150 mg). Results showed similar rates of confirmed thrombotic CV events between the two drugs, though etoricoxib was associated with higher rates of hypertension-related discontinuations [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal Safety:** Endoscopy studies demonstrated a significantly lower incidence of gastroduodenal ulcers with etoricoxib 120 mg compared to naproxen 1000 mg or ibuprofen 2400 mg.

## 12. Storage Conditions
