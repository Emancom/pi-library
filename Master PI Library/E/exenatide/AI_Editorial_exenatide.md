# Editorial Product Information: Exenatide

## Editorial Note
- **Jurisdictions Contributed:** US (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of exenatide for healthcare professionals, synthesising data from the provided official Product Information. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s current PI for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Exenatide.
- **Chemical Class:** Glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonist; a 39-amino acid synthetic peptide amide [US].
- **Brand Name(s):** Not specified in available sources (Editorial note: Generic exenatide injection by Amneal Pharmaceuticals is the source material).
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Prefilled pen: 5 mcg per dose (300 mcg/1.2 mL) [US].
    - Prefilled pen: 10 mcg per dose (600 mcg/2.4 mL) [US].
    - Concentration: 250 mcg/mL [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) injection [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Amneal Pharmaceuticals LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM):** Indicated as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycaemic control in adults with T2DM [US-approved].
- **Limitations of Use:** 
    - Not recommended for co-administration with other exenatide-containing products [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):**
    - **Initial Dose:** 5 mcg administered twice daily [US].
    - **Maintenance Dose:** Based on clinical response, the dose may be increased to 10 mcg twice daily after one month of therapy [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Administer via SC injection in the thigh, abdomen, or upper arm [US].
    - Timing: Within 60 minutes prior to the morning and evening meals (or the two main meals of the day, ≥6 hours apart) [US].
    - **Do not** administer after a meal [US].
    - Injection sites must be rotated for each dose [US].
    - If used with insulin, administer as separate injections; do not mix [US].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, resume the treatment with the next scheduled dose. Do not take an extra dose [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known history of severe hypersensitivity to **exenatide** or any of the product excipients [US].
- **Thrombocytopenia:** History of drug-induced immune-mediated thrombocytopenia resulting from **exenatide** products [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Acute Pancreatitis:** Observed in patients treated with GLP-1 receptor agonists, including fatal and non-fatal hemorrhagic or necrotizing cases. Discontinue immediately if pancreatitis is suspected [US].
- **Pen Sharing:** **Exenatide** pens must never be shared between patients, even if the needle is changed, due to the risk of blood-borne pathogen transmission [US].
- **Hypoglycaemia:** Increased risk when used in combination with insulin secretagogues (e.g., sulfonylureas) or insulin. A dose reduction of the concomitant agent may be required [US].
- **Acute Kidney Injury (AKI):** Post-marketing reports of AKI and worsened chronic renal failure, sometimes requiring haemodialysis. Monitor renal function in patients with severe gastrointestinal (GI) adverse effects who may experience volume depletion [US].
- **Gastrointestinal Disease:** Associated with severe GI adverse reactions. Not recommended for use in patients with severe gastroparesis [US].
- **Immunogenicity:** Patients may develop anti-exenatide antibodies, which may result in an attenuated glycaemic response in a small percentage of cases [US].
- **Drug-Induced Thrombocytopenia:** Serious, potentially fatal bleeding can occur due to immune-mediated platelet destruction. Discontinue immediately and avoid re-exposure [US].
- **Gallbladder Disease:** Acute cholelithiasis or cholecystitis may occur. If suspected, gallbladder studies are indicated [US].
- **Pulmonary Aspiration:** Rare reports of pulmonary aspiration during general anaesthesia or deep sedation due to delayed gastric emptying. Healthcare providers should be informed of **exenatide** use prior to elective surgery [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Nausea (44% when combined with metformin/sulfonylurea), vomiting (13%), diarrhoea (13%) [US].
- **Common (1–10%):** Hypoglycaemia (incidence varies significantly by background therapy), feeling jittery (9%), dizziness (9%), headache (9%), dyspepsia (6%), asthenia (4%), constipation (10% with insulin glargine), GERD (3%), hyperhidrosis (3%) [US].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%) / Post-marketing:** 
    - **Blood:** Drug-induced thrombocytopenia.
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Abdominal pain, eructation, flatulence, ileus, intestinal obstruction, fecal impaction.
    - **Hepatobiliary:** Cholecystitis, cholelithiasis.
    - **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, angioedema, macular/papular rash, generalized pruritus/urticaria.
    - **Neurologic:** Dysgeusia, somnolence, dysesthesia.
    - **Renal:** Increased serum creatinine, renal impairment, kidney transplant dysfunction.
    - **Skin:** Alopecia [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Delayed Gastric Emptying:** **Exenatide** slows gastric emptying, potentially reducing the rate and extent of absorption of oral medications [US].
- **Oral Contraceptives and Antibiotics:** Should be taken at least 1 hour before **exenatide** injection to ensure adequate absorption. If these must be taken with food, take them during a meal when **exenatide** is not being administered [US].
- **Warfarin:** Post-marketing reports of increased INR and bleeding. Monitor INR frequently upon initiation or dose alteration of **exenatide** [US].
- **HMG-CoA Reductase Inhibitors (Statins):** Lovastatin AUC and Cmax were reduced in studies, but no consistent changes in lipid profiles were observed in clinical trials [US].
- **Acetaminophen:** AUC decreased by approximately 20% and Tmax delayed when taken after **exenatide**; no significant change when taken 1 hour before **exenatide** [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - [US: Category not specified; current FDA labeling uses Risk Summary].
    - Limited human data. Use during pregnancy only if the potential benefit outweighs the risk to the foetus [US].
    - Animal studies (mice/rabbits) showed skeletal variations and increased neonatal deaths at doses significantly higher than the human dose [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Presence in human milk is unknown. Exenatide was present in the milk of lactating mice (up to 2.5% of maternal plasma concentration). Consider the developmental/health benefits of breastfeeding alongside the mother’s clinical need for the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in mouse models at systemic exposures 390 times the human dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established. A 120-patient study (ages 10–17) failed to demonstrate effectiveness [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy compared to younger adults, but caution is advised due to the higher likelihood of decreased renal function in this population [US].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - Mild to Moderate (CrCl 30–80 mL/min): No dose adjustment required [US].
    - Severe or End-Stage Renal Disease (CrCl <30 mL/min): Not recommended.
    - Renal Transplant: Use with caution [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No pharmacokinetic studies performed. As clearance is primarily renal, hepatic dysfunction is not expected to significantly impact blood levels [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** GLP-1 receptor agonist. Enhances glucose-dependent insulin secretion from pancreatic beta-cells, suppresses inappropriately elevated glucagon secretion, and slows gastric emptying [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Median Tmax of 2.1 hours. Exposure (AUC) is dose-proportional from 5 mcg to 10 mcg [US].
    - **Distribution:** Mean apparent volume of distribution is 28.3 L [US].
    - **Metabolism/Elimination:** Predominantly eliminated by glomerular filtration with subsequent proteolytic degradation. Mean terminal half-life is 2.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Restores first-phase insulin response in T2DM patients. Reduces fasting and postprandial glucose concentrations [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Monotherapy:** A 24-week trial showed a significant HbA1c reduction (-0.7% to -0.9%) compared to placebo (-0.2%) [US].
- **Combination Therapy:** 
    - **With Metformin/Sulfonylurea:** Significant reductions in HbA1c (up to -1.0% vs. placebo) observed in 30-week trials [US].
    - **With Thiazolidinediones:** 16-week trial showed an HbA1c reduction of -0.9% compared to placebo [US].
    - **With Insulin Glargine:** Significant improvement in glycaemic control when added to titrated insulin glargine compared to placebo (-1.7% vs. -1.0% change in HbA1c) [US].
    - **Non-inferiority:** **Exenatide** (10 mcg BID) demonstrated non-inferiority to insulin lispro (TID) on a background of glargine and metformin [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unused Pens:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F). Do not freeze. Protect from light [US].
- **In-use Pens:** May be kept at room temperature not exceeding 25°C (77°F) for up to 30 days. Discard 30 days after first use [US].
- **Handling:** Remove the needle immediately after each injection. Store without the needle attached to prevent leakage and air bubbles [US].
