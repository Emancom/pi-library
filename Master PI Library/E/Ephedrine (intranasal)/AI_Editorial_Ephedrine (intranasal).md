# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised clinical overview of **Levocarnitine** for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises regulatory data into a coherent reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal compliance. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Intravenous Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with metabolic errors resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) on Hemodialysis:** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Metabolic Disorders:**
    - **Adult and Paediatric Dosing:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - **Loading Dose:** Often utilised in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Administered every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    - **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus (2–3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Dose Adjustment:** Should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations (normal range: 40–50 µmol/L). Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].
- **General Instructions:** Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- **Monitoring:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported. These often occur within minutes of administration, primarily in ESRD patients. Treatment should be discontinued if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Formulation Precaution:** Chronic high-dose oral administration in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide), which are normally excreted in urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disorders.
- **Common (>10%) [US]:**
    - **General:** Injection site reaction (27–38%), pain (21–35%), headache (3–37%), flu syndrome (15–29%), chest pain (6–15%).
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%).
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), abdominal pain (5–21%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%).
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%).
- **Other Reported Effects:** Transient nausea and vomiting, body odor, gastritis, and seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended to monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not explicitly lettered in source, but stated as "use only if clearly needed."
    - **Data:** Animal studies (rats/rabbits) at 3.8 times the human surface area dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. There are no adequate/controlled studies in pregnant women [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. While studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations, the risks to the infant must be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is consistent with adult weight-based dosing (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Oral levocarnitine safety/efficacy has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic oral doses may lead to metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO) in patients with ESRD; however, intravenous levocarnitine is specifically indicated for use post-dialysis in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, facilitating oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Carnitine deficiency is defined biochemically by plasma concentrations <20 µmol/L. Administration to ESRD patients increases plasma carnitine concentrations [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), the mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours and terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients showed that 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis maintained trough concentrations between 54–180 µmol/L (normal: 40–50 µmol/L).
- **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial as it contains no preservative [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only. Poison/Schedule classification not applicable as a controlled substance [US].
