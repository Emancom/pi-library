# Editorial Product Information: Esomeprazole

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of esomeprazole for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data to facilitate quick reference and ensure clinical safety.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this writing, only the Australian (TGA) PI was available for review; therefore, regional variations from other authorities (e.g., FDA, EMA) are not reflected in this version.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Esomeprazole (as magnesium)
- **Chemical Class:** Proton pump inhibitor (PPI); substituted benzimidazole.
- **Brand Names:** **APO-ESOMEPRAZOLE** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Enteric-coated tablets: 20 mg and 40 mg [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
[All indications listed below are Australian-approved]:
- **Gastro-Oesophageal Reflux Disease (GORD):**
    - Treatment of erosive reflux oesophagitis.
    - Long-term maintenance therapy to prevent relapse in healed oesophagitis.
    - Symptomatic treatment of GORD.
- **NSAID-Associated Therapy:**
    - Short-term treatment of upper gastrointestinal symptoms.
    - Healing of gastric ulcers associated with NSAID use (non-selective and COX-2 selective).
    - Prevention of gastric and duodenal ulcers in at-risk patients requiring continuous NSAID therapy.
- **Prevention of Peptic Ulcer Rebleeding:** Following initial treatment with intravenous esomeprazole.
- **Pathological Hypersecretory Conditions:** Including Zollinger-Ellison syndrome and idiopathic hypersecretion.
- **Helicobacter pylori Eradication:** In combination with appropriate antibiotics for healing duodenal ulcers and preventing relapse of peptic ulcers.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Erosive Reflux Oesophagitis:** 40 mg once daily for 4 to 8 weeks.
- **Maintenance of Healed Oesophagitis:** 20 mg once daily.
- **Symptomatic GORD (without oesophagitis):** 20 mg once daily for 4 weeks. If symptoms resolve, 20 mg may be taken "on-demand" [Australian].
- **NSAID-Associated Symptoms/Healing:** 20 mg once daily for 4 to 8 weeks.
- **NSAID-Associated Prevention:** 20 mg once daily.
- **Hypersecretory Conditions:** Starting dose is 40 mg twice daily, subsequently adjusted to individual needs. Doses up to 120 mg twice daily have been used.
- **H. pylori Eradication:** 20 mg twice daily for 7 days (as part of triple therapy with amoxicillin and clarithromycin).

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets should be swallowed whole with liquid and must not be crushed or chewed.
- **Alternative for Swallowing Difficulty:** Tablets may be dispersed in half a glass of non-carbonated water (no other liquids). Stir until disintegrated and drink the liquid with pellets immediately or within 30 minutes. Rinse the glass and drink. Pellets must not be chewed.
- **Gastric Tube:** Disperse in water and administer via a large syringe.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **esomeprazole**, substituted benzimidazoles, or any excipients in the formulation.
- Concomitant administration with **atazanavir** (due to significantly reduced atazanavir exposure).
- Concomitant use with **cilostazol** [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Malignancy Exclusion:** Response to therapy does not preclude gastric malignancy. Exclude malignancy if "alarm symptoms" occur (e.g., significant weight loss, recurrent vomiting, dysphagia, haematemesis).
- **Gastrointestinal Infections:** Decreased acidity increases the risk of GI infections such as *Salmonella*, *Campylobacter*, and possibly *Clostridium difficile*.
- **Concomitant Therapy:** Avoid use with **clopidogrel** due to reduced exposure to clopidogrel’s active metabolite and reduced antiplatelet efficacy.
- **Acute Tubulointerstitial Nephritis:** Has been reported; discontinue therapy if it develops.
- **Long-term Use Risks:**
    - **Cyanocobalamin (B12) Deficiency:** Malabsorption may occur with treatment exceeding 3 years.
    - **Hypomagnesaemia:** Reported rarely, usually after >3 months (mostly >1 year) of therapy. May lead to tetany, seizures, or arrhythmias.
    - **Osteoporotic Fractures:** Increased risk of hip, wrist, or spine fractures, particularly with high-dose or long-term (>1 year) therapy.
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** SJS, TEN, and DRESS have been reported. Discontinue immediately at first sign of skin rash or mucosal lesions.
- **Subacute Cutaneous Lupus Erythematosus (SCLE):** Reported rarely; consider discontinuation if lesions occur in sun-exposed areas.
- **Laboratory Interferences:** PPIs increase Chromogranin A (CgA) levels, which may interfere with neuroendocrine tumour investigations. Stop esomeprazole at least 5–14 days before CgA measurements.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Esomeprazole is generally well-tolerated. Frequencies are based on [Australian] clinical data:

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Headache, abdominal pain, diarrhoea, flatulence, nausea/vomiting, constipation. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Peripheral oedema, insomnia, dizziness, paraesthesia, somnolence, vertigo, dry mouth, increased liver enzymes, dermatitis, pruritus, urticaria, rash. |
| **Rare (0.01–0.1%)** | Leukopaenia, thrombocytopaenia, hypersensitivity (angioedema, anaphylaxis), hyponatraemia, agitation, confusion, depression, taste disturbance, blurred vision, bronchospasm, stomatitis, GI candidiasis, hepatitis (with/without jaundice), alopecia, photosensitivity, arthralgia, myalgia, malaise, hyperhidrosis. |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Agranulocytosis, pancytopaenia, aggression, hallucinations, hepatic failure, encephalopathy, SCARs (SJS, TEN, DRESS, AGEP), muscular weakness, tubulointerstitial nephritis, gynaecomastia. |

- **Post-marketing Note:** Withdrawal of long-term therapy may result in rebound acid hypersecretion.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP2C19 Interactions:** Esomeprazole inhibits CYP2C19. It may increase plasma levels of **diazepam**, **phenytoin**, **citalopram**, **imipramine**, **clomipramine**, and **warfarin** (monitoring of INR and phenytoin levels recommended).
- **pH-Dependent Absorption:**
    - **Decreased Absorption:** Ketoconazole, itraconazole, erlotinib, mycophenolate mofetil.
    - **Increased Absorption:** Digoxin (toxicity risk).
- **Antiretrovirals:** Contraindicated with **atazanavir**. Concomitant use with **nelfinavir** is not recommended. **Saquinavir** levels may be increased.
- **Methotrexate:** PPIs may increase methotrexate levels; consider temporary withdrawal of esomeprazole during high-dose methotrexate therapy.
- **Clopidogrel:** Avoid concomitant use due to significant reduction in clopidogrel efficacy.
- **CYP3A4 Interactions:** Clarithromycin and voriconazole may double esomeprazole exposure; however, dose adjustment is typically not required unless high doses are used.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B3]. Limited clinical data. Use only if essential. Animal studies showed reduced foetal weights and minor skeletal anomalies at maternally toxic doses.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if esomeprazole is excreted in human milk. Do not use during breastfeeding.
- **Fertility:** No specific human data; animal studies on omeprazole showed no impairment of fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:**
    - **12–18 years:** Adult dosing and indications apply for GORD.
    - **1–11 years:** Approved for GORD. Weight-based dosing (10 mg or 20 mg).
    - **<1 year:** Not approved.
- **Elderly:** No dose adjustment required.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required in mild-to-moderate impairment. Use with caution in severe renal insufficiency due to limited data.
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild to Moderate (Child-Pugh A/B):** No dose adjustment required.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Do not exceed a maximum dose of 20 mg once daily.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A specific inhibitor of the H+, K+-ATPase (proton pump) in the parietal cell, providing dose-dependent inhibition of both basal and stimulated gastric acid secretion.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid; peak plasma levels in 1–2 hours. Bioavailability is 64% (single 40 mg dose) to 89% (repeated dosing). Food delays and decreases absorption but does not significantly affect the acid inhibitory effect.
    - **Distribution:** 97% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Completely metabolised by the cytochrome P450 system, primarily CYP2C19 (hydroxy and desmethyl metabolites) and CYP3A4 (sulphone metabolite).
    - **Elimination:** Plasma half-life is ~1.3 hours. 80% excreted as metabolites in urine, the rest in faeces.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Erosive Oesophagitis:** Large trials (n=15,120) demonstrated esomeprazole 40 mg achieved higher healing rates (approx. 93% at 8 weeks) compared to omeprazole 20 mg, lansoprazole 30 mg, and pantoprazole 40 mg.
- **Maintenance Therapy:** Studies showed esomeprazole (10 mg, 20 mg, 40 mg) was significantly more effective than placebo in preventing relapse over 6 months.
- **H. pylori:** Triple therapy (esomeprazole 20 mg BID, amoxicillin 1 g BID, clarithromycin 500 mg BID) for 7 days achieved eradication rates of approximately 90% [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C.
- Keep in the original container to protect from moisture.
