# Editorial Product Information: Etonogestrel and Ethinyl Estradiol Vaginal Ring

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (Food and Drug Administration)
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of the product information for etonogestrel and ethinyl estradiol vaginal rings, intended for use by healthcare professionals.
*   **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredients:** Etonogestrel (a progestin) and Ethinyl Estradiol (an estrogen).
*   **Chemical Class:** Combination Hormonal Contraceptive (CHC).
*   **Brand Names:** Not specified in available sources (Generic version: AvKARE) [US].
*   **ATC Code:** G03AL01 (Inferred; not specified in source).
*   **Dosage Form and Strength:** Non-biodegradable, flexible, transparent polymeric vaginal ring. Each ring contains 11.7 mg etonogestrel and 2.7 mg ethinyl estradiol. It releases an average of 0.120 mg/day of etonogestrel and 0.015 mg/day of ethinyl estradiol over a 3-week period [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Vaginal.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AvKARE [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Pregnancy Prevention:** Indicated for use by females of reproductive potential to prevent pregnancy [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 General Dosing
*   **Cycle:** One ring is used per 4-week cycle.
*   **Schedule:** The ring must remain in the vagina continuously for three consecutive weeks (21 days), followed by a one-week (7-day) ring-free interval. A withdrawal bleed usually occurs during this interval.
*   **Insertion/Removal:** A new ring is inserted exactly one week after the previous ring was removed, regardless of whether menstrual bleeding has ceased [US].

### 3.2 Initiation Schedules
*   **No Hormonal Contraceptive Use in Preceding Cycle:** Insert on Day 1 of the menstrual cycle. If started on Days 2–5, a barrier method (e.g., male condoms with spermicide) must be used for the first seven days of use.
*   **Switching from Combined Hormonal Contraceptives (CHC):** Insert on any day, but no later than the day following the usual hormone-free interval of the previous method.
*   **Switching from Progestin-Only Methods:**
    *   *Minipill:* Switch any day; start the ring the day after the last pill.
    *   *Implant/IUS:* Insert the ring on the day of removal.
    *   *Injection:* Insert the ring on the day the next injection is due.
    *   *Note:* A barrier method is required for the first seven days in all progestin-only switches [US].

### 3.3 Deviations and Missed Doses
*   **Inadvertent Expulsion/Removal:**
    *   *<3 hours:* Effectiveness is not reduced. Rinse with cool/lukewarm water and reinsert immediately.
    *   *>3 hours (Weeks 1 & 2):* Reinsert as soon as remembered. Use back-up contraception for 7 days.
    *   *>3 hours (Week 3):* Discard the ring. Either insert a new ring immediately (starting a new 3-week cycle) or allow the withdrawal bleed to occur and insert a new ring no later than 7 days after the expulsion (only if the ring was used continuously for the 7 days prior to expulsion). Use back-up contraception for 7 days.
*   **Prolonged Ring-Free Interval:** If the interval exceeds 7 days, insert a new ring immediately and use back-up contraception for 7 days [US].
*   **Prolonged Ring Use:**
    *   *Up to 4 weeks:* Efficacy is maintained. Remove and follow with a 1-week interval.
    *   *>4 weeks:* Rule out pregnancy. Re-start with back-up contraception for 7 days [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   High risk of arterial or venous thrombotic diseases (e.g., smokers >35 years, history of DVT/PE, cerebrovascular disease, CAD, thrombogenic valvular disease, or inherited hypercoagulopathies) [US].
*   Uncontrolled hypertension or hypertension with vascular disease [US].
*   Diabetes mellitus with vascular involvement [US].
*   Headaches with focal neurological symptoms or migraine with aura (any age), or migraines without aura in women >35 years [US].
*   Current or history of breast cancer or other hormone-sensitive malignancies [US].
*   Liver tumors (benign or malignant) or active liver disease [US].
*   Undiagnosed abnormal uterine bleeding [US].
*   Pregnancy [US].
*   Hypersensitivity to any components of the ring [US].
*   Co-administration with Hepatitis C drug combinations containing ombitasvir/paritaprevir/ritonavir, with or without dasabuvir [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### 5.1 Boxed Warning (United States)
*   **Cigarette Smoking and Serious Cardiovascular Events:** Cigarette smoking increases the risk of serious cardiovascular events from CHC use. This risk increases with age (particularly in women >35 years) and with the number of cigarettes smoked. CHCs are contraindicated in women over 35 who smoke [US].

### 5.2 Clinical Cautions
*   **Thromboembolic Disorders:** Discontinue if an arterial or venous thrombotic event occurs. Stop at least 4 weeks before and 2 weeks after major surgery or prolonged immobilisation.
*   **Toxic Shock Syndrome (TSS):** Cases have been reported. Monitor for symptoms (fever, rash, vomiting, dizziness) [US].
*   **Liver Disease:** Discontinue if jaundice develops. Hepatic adenomas and a very rare risk of hepatocellular carcinoma are associated with CHC use.
*   **Hypertension:** Monitor blood pressure; discontinue if significant elevations occur [US].
*   **Metabolic Effects:** Monitor prediabetic and diabetic women. CHCs may decrease glucose tolerance and affect lipid levels.
*   **Headache:** New onset or worsening of migraines requires evaluation and potential discontinuation.
*   **Bleeding Irregularities:** Breakthrough bleeding and spotting may occur, especially during the first three months. Evaluate persistent or irregular bleeding for malignancy or pregnancy.
*   **Vaginal Use Issues:** The ring may not be suitable for women with conditions predisposing them to vaginal irritation or ulceration. Cases of the ring becoming stuck to vaginal tissue have been reported [US].
*   **Inadvertent Bladder Insertion:** Ensure correct vaginal placement; assess for bladder insertion if persistent urinary symptoms occur and the ring cannot be located [US].

### 6.2 Serious Adverse Effects
*   Venous Thromboembolism (VVT) and Arterial Thromboembolism (ATE).
*   Toxic Shock Syndrome.
*   Anaphylaxis and angioedema.
*   Gallbladder disease.
*   Hepatic adenomas.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Enzyme Inducers (e.g., CYP3A4):** Drugs such as phenytoin, carbamazepine, rifampin, and St. John's wort can decrease plasma concentrations of CHCs, potentially leading to contraceptive failure or breakthrough bleeding. Use back-up contraception during and for 28 days after stopping the inducer [US].
*   **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Strong inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, clarithromycin) may increase estrogen/progestin levels. Vaginal miconazole nitrate has been shown to increase serum concentrations of etonogestrel and ethinyl estradiol by up to 40% [US].
*   **HCV Combination Therapy:** Co-administration with certain Hepatitis C antivirals (e.g., ombitasvir/paritaprevir/ritonavir) is contraindicated due to the risk of significant ALT elevations [US].
*   **Effect on Other Drugs:** CHCs may decrease plasma levels of lamotrigine (requiring dosage adjustment) and may increase levels of drugs like cyclosporine or theophylline [US].
*   **Thyroid/Cortisol Replacement:** Estrogens may increase serum concentrations of binding globulins, necessitating increased doses of thyroid or cortisol replacement therapy.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated. There is no reason to use CHCs during pregnancy. Epidemiologic data have not shown an increased risk of birth defects with accidental exposure to low-dose CHCs in early pregnancy [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended until the child is weaned. CHCs can reduce milk production and small amounts of steroids are transferred to breast milk [US].
*   **Fertility:** Return to ovulation typically occurs within one month after discontinuation [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established in post-pubertal adolescents. Not indicated before menarche [US].
*   **Geriatric Use:** Not studied in postmenopausal women; not indicated for this population [US].
*   **Renal Impairment:** Not specifically studied [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in active liver disease. Steroid hormones may be poorly metabolised in these patients [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Primary action is the suppression of gonadotropins, resulting in the inhibition of ovulation. Secondary mechanisms include increased cervical mucus viscosity (hindering sperm penetration) and alterations in the endometrium (reducing implantation likelihood) [US].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   *Absorption:* Etonogestrel is rapidly absorbed (bioavailability ~100%). Ethinyl estradiol is rapidly absorbed (bioavailability ~56%).
    *   *Metabolism:* Both are metabolised in the liver by CYP3A4.
    *   *Elimination:* Excreted via urine, bile, and faeces [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   In three large 1-year trials (N=2,834), the pooled Pearl Index was 1.28 per 100 women-years (95% CI: 0.8–1.9).
*   In a specific North American study, the Pearl Index was 2.02 (95% CI: 1.1–3.4) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Prior to Dispensing:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) [US].
*   **After Dispensing:** Can be stored at room temperature (up to 25°C / 77°F) for a maximum of 4 months. Excursions are permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F).
*   **Protection:** Avoid direct sunlight and temperatures above 30°C (86°F) [US].
