# Editorial Product Information: Estriol

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory information into a single, clinically focused reference.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource. It is not a substitute for the official PI used for prescribing in specific jurisdictions.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Estriol (a natural female estrogen).
- **Chemical Class:** Natural and semisynthetic estrogens.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Ovestin** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** G03C-A04.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 1 mg/g homogenous cream [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravaginal.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aspen Pharmacare Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
[All indications listed below are Australian-approved]
- Treatment of vulvo-vaginal complaints associated with estrogen deficiency during the climacteric, postmenopause, or following ovariectomy, including:
    - Atrophic vaginitis.
    - Pruritus vulvae.
    - Dyspareunia related to vulvo-vaginal atrophy.
- Auxiliary therapy for the management of vaginal infections.
- Pre-operative and post-operative therapy for women undergoing vulvo-vaginal surgery.
- Treatment of ulcers associated with uterine or vaginal prolapse.
- Diagnostic aid to improve the interpretation of cytological smears in postmenopausal women.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Standard Postmenopausal Symptoms:** 0.5 mg (one applicator-dose) daily for the first 3 weeks.
- **Maintenance Dose:** 0.5 mg twice weekly. 
- **Treatment Duration:** For postmenopausal symptoms, the lowest effective dose should be used for the shortest possible duration. Treatment should be discontinued every 2 to 3 months for a 4-week period to reassess the ongoing necessity of therapy [Australian].

### Indication-Specific Dosing
- **Pre-surgery Therapy:** 0.5 mg daily starting 2 weeks prior to the scheduled operation.
- **Diagnostic Use (Suspect Cytological Smear):** 0.5 mg daily for 7 days before the next smear evaluation.

### Administration Instructions
- Administer intravaginally using the provided calibrated applicator.
- Application should ideally occur at night before retiring.
- **Switching from other HRT:** 
    - From continuous combined HRT: May start any day.
    - From cyclic HRT: Start one week after the completion of the previous cycle.

### Missed Dose
- Administer as soon as remembered. If remembered on the day of the next dose, skip the missed dose and resume the regular schedule. Do not double the dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy** [Australian Category B1].
- Known, past, or suspected breast cancer.
- Known or suspected estrogen-dependent malignant tumours (e.g., endometrial carcinoma).
- Undiagnosed genital bleeding.
- Untreated endometrial hyperplasia.
- Previous or current active venous thromboembolism (VTE), such as deep vein thrombosis or pulmonary embolism.
- Known thrombophilic disorders (e.g., protein C, protein S, or antithrombin deficiency).
- Active or recent arterial thromboembolic disease (e.g., angina, myocardial infarction).
- Thrombophlebitis or a history of this condition.
- History of endometriosis.
- Porphyria.
- Severe liver dysfunction or history of liver disease where liver function tests have not returned to normal.
- Disturbed lipid metabolism.
- History of otosclerosis deterioration during pregnancy or previous steroid use.
- Hypersensitivity to estriol or any excipients.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Precautions
- **Endometrial Stimulation:** To prevent endometrial stimulation, the daily dose must not exceed 0.5 mg, and this maximum dose should not be used for more than several weeks [Australian].
- **Medical Monitoring:** Before initiation, a full medical and family history is required. Periodic check-ups and breast examinations/mammography should be conducted according to current clinical practice.
- **Fluid Retention:** Estrogens may cause fluid retention; patients with cardiac or renal dysfunction require close monitoring.

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Breast Cancer:** HRT is associated with an increased risk of breast cancer, which returns to baseline within approximately five years of stopping. Clinical data suggest the risk may be lower with estriol than with other estrogens, but caution is advised.
- **Venous Thromboembolism (VTE):** HRT increases the risk of VTE 2- to 3-fold, particularly in the first year of use.
- **Hepatitis C:** ALT elevations ( >5x ULN) have been reported when using certain HCV combination regimens (e.g., ombitasvir/paritaprevir/ritonavir) with ethinylestradiol; caution is warranted when co-administered with other estrogens like estriol.
- **Hereditary Angioedema:** Exogenous estrogens may induce or exacerbate symptoms.

### Reasons for Immediate Withdrawal
- Jaundice or deterioration in liver function.
- Significant increase in blood pressure.
- New onset of migraine-type headaches.
- Discovery of a contraindication or pregnancy.

### Serious/Systemic Risks (Associated with HRT class)
- Endometrial and ovarian cancer.
- Myocardial infarction and stroke.
- Gall bladder disease.
- Skin disorders: Chloasma, erythema multiforme, erythema nodosum, vascular purpura.
- Probable dementia in patients over 65.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP450 Inducers:** Substances such as anticonvulsants (phenytoin, phenobarbital, carbamazepine) and anti-infectives (rifampicin, rifabutin, nevirapine, efavirenz) may increase estrogen metabolism, potentially decreasing clinical efficacy.
- **St John’s Wort:** May induce the metabolism of estrogens.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Corticosteroids, Succinylcholine, Theophyllines:** Estriol may increase the pharmacological effects of these agents; dose reduction may be necessary.
- **Anticoagulants:** Estriol may alter the effectiveness of oral anticoagulants.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated. **Category B1** [Australian]. If pregnancy occurs, withdraw treatment immediately. Epidemiological data show no evidence of teratogenic effects from inadvertent exposure.
- **Breastfeeding:** There are insufficient data to assess harm to the infant. Estriol is excreted in breast milk and may reduce milk production.
- **Fertility:** Estriol is intended for postmenopausal women only. It is a weak gonadotrophin inhibitor and is not intended for contraceptive use.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No data available [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available; note risk of dementia in those starting HRT over age 65.
- **Renal Impairment:** No specific data available, but close observation is required in terminal renal insufficiency due to expected increases in circulating active levels.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe liver dysfunction or where liver function tests remain abnormal.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Estriol is a natural estrogen with a short duration of action due to its brief retention time in the nuclei of endometrial cells. It treats urogenital symptoms by substituting for lost estrogen, normalising urogenital epithelium, restoring vaginal pH, and improving resistance to infection and inflammation.

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed via the vaginal route. $C_{max}$ is reached within 1–2 hours. Systemic levels of unconjugated estriol (approx. 70 pg/mL) bypass the first-pass hepatic metabolism.
- **Distribution:** Approximately 90% is bound to albumin. Unlike other estrogens, estriol has minimal binding to sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG).
- **Metabolism:** Primarily undergoes conjugation and deconjugation via enterohepatic circulation.
- **Elimination:** Primarily excreted in the urine in conjugated form (98%); only 2% is excreted in faeces. Half-life is approximately 5 to 7 hours.
