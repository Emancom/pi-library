# Editorial Product Information: Etomidate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Etomidate (an imidazole-5-carboxylate derivative).
- **Chemical Class:** Non-barbiturate hypnotic/general anesthetic.
- **Brand Name:** AMIDATE [US].
- **ATC Code:** N01AX07 (Not explicitly listed in source, but standard for etomidate).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injection (solution), 2 mg/mL. Available in 10 mL (20 mg) and 20 mL (40 mg) single-dose fliptop vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) injection only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Hospira, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Induction of General Anesthesia:** For the initiation of anesthesia in adult and pediatric patients (aged 10 years and older) [US].
- **Maintenance of Anesthesia:** Supplementation of subpotent anesthetic agents (e.g., nitrous oxide in oxygen) during short surgical procedures, such as cervical conization or dilation and curettage [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Pediatrics >10 years):** Dose ranges from 0.2 mg/kg to 0.6 mg/kg, individualized based on patient response [US].
- **Standard Induction Dose:** 0.3 mg/kg administered intravenously over 30 to 60 seconds [US].
- **Maintenance/Supplementation:** Smaller increments may be used for short procedures to supplement other agents. Doses for maintenance must be individualized and are typically smaller than induction doses [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
    - Discard any unused portion as the product contains no preservatives.
    - Intra-arterial injection is strictly discouraged due to the potential for complications, though it is less likely than barbiturates to cause tissue necrosis [US].
- **Titration:** Dosing must be individualized. Premedication with fentanyl (approx. 0.1 mg) 1 to 2 minutes before induction may reduce the required dose of etomidate and shorten the recovery period [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to etomidate or any components of the formulation (e.g., propylene glycol) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Administration Setting:** Must only be administered by healthcare professionals trained in general anesthesia and the management of associated respiratory/cardiovascular complications [US].
- **Adrenal Suppression:** Etomidate inhibits 11-beta-hydroxylation in the adrenal cortex, leading to reduced production of cortisol and aldosterone for 6 to 8 hours following a single induction dose. It is **not** intended for administration by prolonged infusion due to the risk of significant adrenal suppression [US].
- **Pediatric Neurotoxicity:** Exposure to anesthetic drugs, including etomidate, during the third trimester of pregnancy or in the first 3 years of life may result in widespread neuronal apoptosis and long-term cognitive or behavioral deficits. Use should be balanced against clinical necessity [US].
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** While hemodynamically stable in many, etomidate may cause cardiac depression (decreased heart rate, cardiac index, and blood pressure) in geriatric patients, particularly those with hypertension [US].
- **Monitoring:** Vital signs must be monitored continuously. Renal function monitoring should be considered in elderly patients [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):**
    - **Transient Skeletal Muscle Movements:** Observed in approximately 32% of patients. These are primarily myoclonic but may include tonic or averting movements. Incidence is reduced by premedication with fentanyl [US].
    - **Injection Site Pain:** Reported in roughly 20% of patients; incidence is higher when administered into small, distal veins (e.g., hand/wrist) compared to larger, proximal veins [US].
- **Common (1–10%):**
    - Postoperative nausea and/or vomiting (PONV), particularly when used without sufficient analgesia [US].
- **Other Observations:**
    - **Respiratory:** Brief apnea (5–90 seconds), hyperventilation, hypoventilation, laryngospasm, and hiccups [US].
    - **Circulatory:** Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, and bradycardia. Anaphylactoid reactions have been rarely reported [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Neuroactive Drugs:** The use of intravenous fentanyl or other anesthetics/sedatives may reduce the required dose of etomidate [US].
- **Neuromuscular Blocking Agents:** Etomidate does not significantly alter the dosage requirements of muscle relaxants used for intubation (e.g., succinylcholine or non-depolarizing agents) [US].
- **Analgesics:** Etomidate lacks analgesic activity; therefore, appropriate analgesics must be used concurrently for painful procedures [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not assigned a letter category in the provided source, but classified with standard risk summaries. There are no adequate/controlled studies in pregnant women [US]. 
    - **Animal Data:** Fetal deaths and reduced pup survival were noted in rats at sub-therapeutic doses. Primate studies suggest neurotoxicity in offspring exposed for >3 hours during the period of peak brain development [US].
- **Labor and Delivery:** Use in obstetrics, including Caesarean section, is not recommended due to insufficient data [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if etomidate is excreted in human milk. Caution is advised when administering to nursing mothers [US].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were noted in rat studies at doses up to 2.7 times the human induction dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Recommended only for patients aged 10 years and older. Data are inadequate to support dosing recommendations for children under 10 [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Elderly patients may demonstrate decreased initial distribution volume and total clearance. Dose reduction is often required, and care should be taken due to increased risk of cardiac depression [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Etomidate is substantially excreted by the kidneys. Caution is advised as the risk of toxic reactions may be higher in patients with impaired renal function [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** In patients with cirrhosis and esophageal varices, the volume of distribution and elimination half-life are approximately doubled compared to healthy individuals [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Etomidate is a short-acting hypnotic that appears to act by potentiating GABA-mediated neurotransmission. It lacks analgesic properties [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:**
    - **Onset:** Rapid (usually within one minute).
    - **Duration:** 3 to 5 minutes for a 0.3 mg/kg dose.
    - **Cerebral Effects:** Reduces cerebral blood flow (20–30%) and intracranial pressure while maintaining cerebral perfusion pressure [US].
    - **Intraocular Pressure:** Moderately lowers intraocular pressure [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly metabolised in the liver via hydrolysis to its inactive carboxylic acid metabolite [US].
    - **Distribution:** Large volume of distribution; protein binding to albumin is approximately 76% (decreased in the elderly) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Half-life is approximately 75 minutes. About 75% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Clinical Summary:** Provided data highlights that etomidate provides a rapid induction period comparable to or slightly faster than thiopental. Cardiovascular stability is a hallmark in patients with severe heart disease, although this advantage must be weighed against the high frequency of myoclonic movements [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Use only if the solution is clear and the container is undamaged. Discard any unused portion [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only Medicine (Rx-only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available US source (typically handled under institutional anesthetic protocols).
