# Editorial Product Information: Entecavir

## Editorial Note
- **Jurisdictions Contributed:** Australian (TGA). 
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of the Product Information for entecavir. It is intended as a clinical reference for healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial is a professional summary and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the Australian regulatory source was provided for analysis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Entecavir (as monohydrate).
- **Chemical Class:** Guanosine nucleoside analogue.
- **Brand Names:** Entecavir RBX [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J05AF10.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 0.5 mg (white to off-white, triangular) and 1 mg (light pink to pink, triangular) [Australian].
    - Oral solution: 0.05 mg/mL (available in other brands, not specified in this source) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sun Pharma ANZ Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Hepatitis B (CHB):** Treatment of chronic hepatitis B virus infection in adults and adolescents 16 years or older with evidence of active liver inflammation [Australian]. 
    - Approval is based on data from nucleoside-naive and lamivudine-resistant adult patients with HBeAg-positive or HBeAg-negative CHB and compensated liver disease [Australian].
    - Also studied in patients with decompensated liver disease and HIV/HBV co-infection [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:**
    - **Nucleoside-Naive Patients:** 0.5 mg once daily [Australian].
    - **Lamivudine-Refractory Patients:** 1 mg once daily (for patients with a history of viremia while on lamivudine or known YMDD mutations) [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Must be taken on an **empty stomach** (at least 2 hours before or 2 hours after a meal) [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment Adjustment:** Recommended for patients with CrCl < 50 mL/min [Australian]:

| Creatinine Clearance (mL/min) | Nucleoside-Naïve Dose | Lamivudine-Refractory Dose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\ge$ 50 | 0.5 mg once daily | 1 mg once daily |
| 30 to < 50 | 0.25 mg daily* OR 0.5 mg every 48h | 0.5 mg daily OR 1 mg every 48h |
| 10 to < 30 | 0.15 mg daily* OR 0.5 mg every 72h | 0.3 mg daily* OR 1 mg every 72h |
| < 10, Haemodialysis, or CAPD | 0.05 mg daily* OR 0.5 mg every 5–7 days | 0.1 mg daily* OR 1 mg every 5–7 days |
*\*Note: Doses < 0.5 mg require oral solution; do not split tablets. Administer after haemodialysis on dialysis days [Australian].*

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Demonstrated hypersensitivity to entecavir or any excipient in the formulation (e.g., lactose monohydrate) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **HIV/HBV Co-infection:** Not recommended for patients co-infected with HIV who are not receiving Highly Active Antiretroviral Therapy (HAART), due to the risk of developing HIV resistance [Australian].
- **Lactic Acidosis and Severe Hepatomegaly with Steatosis:** Reported with nucleoside analogues; cases can be fatal. Use with caution in patients with known risk factors for liver disease [Australian].
- **Exacerbations of Hepatitis:** Acute, potentially severe or fatal exacerbations have been reported after discontinuing anti-HBV therapy. Hepatic function should be monitored for several months post-discontinuation [Australian].
- **Liver Transplant Recipients:** Limited data; renal function must be monitored closely if the patient is taking cyclosporin or tacrolimus [Australian].
- **Lactose Intolerance:** Tablets contain lactose (112.5 mg in the 0.5 mg dose; 225 mg in the 1 mg dose). Contraindicated in patients with rare hereditary galactose intolerance or Lapp lactase deficiency [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (1–10%):** Headache (9%), fatigue (6%), dizziness (4%), and nausea (3%) [Australian].
- **Moderate to Severe (Grades 2–4):**
    - Diarrhoea, dyspepsia, vomiting (<1% to 1%).
    - Insomnia (<1% in naive patients).
    - Somnolence (<1%).
- **Laboratory Abnormalities:**
    - **ALT Elevations:** >10x ULN and >2x baseline (2% in compensated; 0% in decompensated trials).
    - **Lipase Elevations:** >3x ULN (12% in naive; 18% in refractory patients).
    - **Platelets:** <50,000/mm³ (up to 20% in decompensated liver disease).
    - **Albumin:** <2.5 g/dL (up to 30% in decompensated liver disease).
- **Postmarketing Experience:** Anaphylactoid reaction, alopecia, rash, increased transaminases, and lactic acidosis (often associated with hepatic decompensation) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Primarily eliminated via the kidneys. Coadministration with drugs that reduce renal function or compete for active tubular secretion may increase serum concentrations of entecavir or the coadministered drug [Australian].
- **Studied Interactions (No significant impact):** Lamivudine, adefovir dipivoxil, and tenofovir disoproxil fumarate [Australian].
- **Management:** Monitor for adverse effects when coadministering with renally excreted medicines [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B1/B3:** [Australian PI specifically cites Category B3].
    - Use only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus. No data exist on maternal-infant HBV transmission [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Excreted in the milk of rats. It is unknown if it is excreted in human milk; mothers should be instructed not to breastfeed [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No effect on rat fertility. However, testicular degeneration was observed in rodents and dogs at high exposure levels (10–30x human exposure) [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children under 16 years of age have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific age-related dose adjustments required, but caution is advised due to the higher likelihood of decreased renal function in this population [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dose adjustment required for CrCl < 50 mL/min (see Section 3) [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dosage adjustment necessary for patients with hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A guanosine nucleoside analogue that inhibits HBV polymerase. It targets three functional activities: (1) base priming, (2) reverse transcription of the negative strand, and (3) synthesis of the positive strand of HBV DNA [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid; peak plasma concentrations (Tmax) between 0.5–1.5 hours. Bioavailability is 100% relative to oral solution.
    - **Distribution:** Estimated volume of distribution exceeds total body water; protein binding ~13%.
    - **Metabolism:** Not a substrate, inhibitor, or inducer of the CYP450 system.
    - **Elimination:** Predominantly renal (62–73% unchanged in urine). Terminal half-life is 128–149 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:**
    - **AI463022 & AI463027 (Nucleoside-Naive):** Demonstrated superiority to lamivudine in histologic improvement and virologic suppression at 48 weeks.
    - **AI463026 (Lamivudine-Refractory):** 1 mg dose showed superior histologic and virologic outcomes compared to continued lamivudine at 48 weeks.
    - **AI463048 (Decompensated Liver Disease):** 1 mg dose showed higher rates of undetectable HBV DNA compared to adefovir at 24 weeks.
- **Resistance:** Resistance requires primary lamivudine resistance substitutions (M204V/I ± L180M) plus additional substitutions at T184, S202, or M250 [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C.
- **Handling:** Store in the original package [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Not specified in the provided editorial source; refer to packaging or the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG) [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
