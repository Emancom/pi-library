# Editorial Product Information: Efavirenz, Emtricitabine, and Tenofovir Disoproxil Fumarate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: United States (FDA). 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single, coherent reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and must not be used as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information when prescribing or dispensing in a particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Efavirenz (EFV), Emtricitabine (FTC), and Tenofovir Disoproxil Fumarate (TDF).
- **Chemical Class:** 
  - Efavirenz: Non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NNRTI).
  - Emtricitabine: Nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NRTI).
  - Tenofovir Disoproxil Fumarate: Nucleotide reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NRTI).
- **ATC Code:** J05AR06 (Efavirenz, emtricitabine and tenofovir disoproxil).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Film-coated tablets containing 600 mg of efavirenz, 200 mg of emtricitabine, and 300 mg of tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (equivalent to 245 mg of tenofovir disoproxil) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Distributed by Aurobindo Pharma USA, Inc.; Repackaged by Bryant Ranch Prepack [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Treatment of HIV-1 Infection:** Indicated as a complete regimen or in combination with other antiretroviral agents for the treatment of HIV-1 infection in adults and pediatric patients weighing at least 40 kg [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric $\ge$ 40 kg):** One tablet taken once daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** Must be taken orally on an empty stomach. Administration at bedtime is preferred to improve the tolerability of nervous system symptoms [US].
- **Dose Adjustments (Rifampin Coadministration):** In patients weighing $\ge$ 50 kg, an additional 200 mg/day of efavirenz (as a single agent) is recommended when coadministered with rifampin [US].
- **Testing Prior to Initiation:** 
  - Test for Hepatitis B Virus (HBV) infection.
  - Assess serum creatinine, estimated creatinine clearance (CrCl), urine glucose, and urine protein in all patients.
  - Perform pregnancy testing in individuals of childbearing potential.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known clinically significant hypersensitivity (e.g., Stevens-Johnson syndrome, erythema multiforme, or toxic skin eruptions) to any component of the formulation [US].
- **Drug Coadministration:**
  - **Voriconazole:** Coadministration is contraindicated due to significantly reduced voriconazole levels and increased efavirenz levels.
  - **Elbasvir/Grazoprevir:** Coadministration is contraindicated due to potential loss of virologic response to the HCV regimen [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Post-treatment Acute Exacerbation of Hepatitis B [US]:** Severe acute exacerbations of HBV have been reported in patients coinfected with HIV-1 and HBV who discontinued products containing FTC and/or TDF. Hepatic function should be monitored closely for several months following discontinuation in coinfected patients.
- **Rash:** Discontinue treatment if severe rash occurs (e.g., blistering, desquamation, mucosal involvement, or fever). The incidence of rash is higher in pediatric patients [US].
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Monitor liver enzymes before and during treatment, particularly in patients with underlying liver disease or HBV/HCV coinfection.
- **Psychiatric Symptoms:** Serious psychiatric adverse experiences, including severe depression, suicide attempts, and psychosis-like behavior, have been reported. Immediate medical evaluation is required for these symptoms [US].
- **Nervous System Symptoms (NSS):** Symptoms such as dizziness, insomnia, and impaired concentration are common, typically beginning 1–2 days after initiation and resolving within 2–4 weeks [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** New onset or worsening renal impairment, including acute renal failure and Fanconi syndrome, has been reported with TDF use. Avoid use with concurrent or recent nephrotoxic agents [US].
- **Bone Effects:** Decreases in bone mineral density (BMD) and mineralization defects (e.g., osteomalacia) may occur. Consider monitoring BMD in patients with high fracture risk [US].
- **Lactic Acidosis/Severe Hepatomegaly with Steatosis:** Discontinue treatment if clinical or laboratory findings suggest lactic acidosis or pronounced hepatotoxicity [US].
- **QTc Prolongation:** Consider alternatives in patients taking medications with a known risk of Torsade de Pointes or those at higher risk [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common ($\ge$ 10%):** Diarrhea, nausea, fatigue, headache, dizziness, depression, insomnia, abnormal dreams, and rash [US].
- **Common (1–10%):** Vomiting, anxiety, sinusitis, upper respiratory tract infections, nasopharyngitis, and increased creatine kinase [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Stevens-Johnson syndrome, liver failure, acute renal failure, Fanconi syndrome, suicidal ideation, and lactic acidosis [US].
- **Pediatric-Specific:** Anemia and skin hyperpigmentation were observed in pediatric patients receiving emtricitabine [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Efavirenz Interactions:** EFV is an inducer of CYP3A and CYP2B6. It may decrease plasma concentrations of substrates for these enzymes (e.g., atorvastatin, clarithromycin, hormonal contraceptives, methadone) [US].
- **Tenofovir/Emtricitabine Interactions:** Primarily eliminated by the kidneys. Coadministration with drugs that reduce renal function or compete for active tubular secretion (e.g., acyclovir, NSAIDs) may increase serum concentrations of FTC or tenofovir [US].
- **Protease Inhibitors:**
  - **Atazanavir:** Coadministration is not recommended; EFV/TDF significantly reduces atazanavir concentrations while tenofovir levels increase [US].
  - **Lopinavir/Ritonavir:** Dose increase of lopinavir/ritonavir is required; monitor for tenofovir-associated toxicities [US].
- **Hormonal Contraceptives:** EFV decreases progestin levels; a reliable barrier method of contraception must be used in addition to hormonal methods [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not specified in current labeling, but use in the first trimester is not recommended]. Retrospective reports link first-trimester efavirenz exposure to neural tube defects. Avoid pregnancy during treatment and for 12 weeks after discontinuation [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended due to the risk of HIV-1 transmission and the potential for serious adverse reactions in the infant. EFV, FTC, and tenofovir are excreted in human milk [US].
- **Contraception:** Use effective barrier contraception in combination with other methods during and for 12 weeks after treatment [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for patients weighing at least 40 kg. Not recommended for those < 40 kg as it is a fixed-dose tablet [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Use caution due to the greater frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not recommended for patients with estimated CrCl < 50 mL/min as the fixed-dose combination cannot be dose-adjusted [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not recommended for moderate or severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh B or C). Use with caution in mild impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
  - **EFV:** Non-competitive inhibition of HIV-1 reverse transcriptase.
  - **FTC:** Phosphorylated to emtricitabine 5'-triphosphate, which competes with deoxycytidine 5'-triphosphate for incorporation into viral DNA, causing chain termination.
  - **TDF:** Hydrolyzed to tenofovir and phosphorylated to tenofovir diphosphate; competes with deoxyadenosine 5'-triphosphate to terminate the DNA chain [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** EFV exposure increases with high-fat meals (avoid food). FTC and TDF are rapidly absorbed.
  - **Distribution:** EFV is highly protein-bound (> 99%).
  - **Metabolism:** EFV is metabolized by CYP3A and CYP2B6.
  - **Elimination:** EFV has a long terminal half-life (40–55 hours after multiple doses). FTC and tenofovir are primarily excreted renally [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study 934:** A 144-week randomized, open-label trial in treatment-naïve patients comparing FTC + TDF + EFV to zidovudine/lamivudine + EFV. At 144 weeks, the FTC + TDF + EFV arm showed significantly higher virologic response (71% vs. 58%) and fewer discontinuations due to adverse events [US].
- **Study 073:** Evaluated patients switching to the fixed-dose combination from a stable regimen. 89% of switch patients maintained virologic suppression at 48 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F and 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Keep the container tightly closed and dispense only in the original container [US].
