# Editorial Product Information: Eptinezumab

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial is based on the Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. No other regional documents (e.g., US, European, Japanese) were provided for this synthesis.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a clinically relevant format.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official PI used for prescribing in specific jurisdictions. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved PI for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Eptinezumab (humanised monoclonal immunoglobulin G1 (IgG1) antibody).
- **Chemical Class:** Calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) antagonist.
- **Brand Name(s):** **VYEPTI** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N02CD05.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Concentrated injection for dilution for infusion; 100 mg/mL in a single-dose vial [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Lundbeck Australia Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Preventive treatment of migraine in adults:** [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The standard recommended dose is **100 mg** administered via IV infusion every 12 weeks.
- **Dose Escalation:** Some patients may derive additional clinical benefit from a dose of **300 mg** every 12 weeks.
- **Assessment of Benefit:** Clinical response should be evaluated 3–6 months after starting treatment. The requirement for dose escalation should be reviewed within 12 weeks of the first dose.
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - **Preparation:** Must be diluted prior to use. Withdraw the required volume (1.0 mL for 100 mg; 3.0 mL for 300 mg) and add to a 100 mL bag of 0.9% sodium chloride.
    - **Mixing:** Invert the bag gently; do not shake.
    - **Infusion:** Administer over approximately 30 minutes. An infusion set with a 0.2 or 0.22 micron in-line or add-on sterile filter must be used.
    - **Line Flush:** Following the infusion, flush the line with 20 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride.
    - **Restrictions:** Do not administer as a bolus. Do not mix with other medications or use other diluents [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known serious hypersensitivity to eptinezumab or any of the excipients (histidine, histidine hydrochloride monohydrate, polysorbate 80, sorbitol).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
- **Serious Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylactic reactions have been reported, occasionally occurring within minutes of infusion. Symptoms may include angioedema, urticaria, facial flushing, and rash. If a serious reaction occurs, discontinue the infusion immediately and initiate appropriate therapy.
- **Hypertension:** New-onset hypertension or worsening of pre-existing hypertension has been reported with CGRP antagonists. Blood pressure should be monitored, especially within the first 7 days of therapy. Discontinuation may be necessary if blood pressure cannot be adequately controlled.
- **Cardiovascular Risk:** Safety in patients with a history of cardiovascular disease is limited, as these patients were generally excluded from pivotal clinical trials.
- **Traceability:** To ensure biological traceability, the name and batch number of the administered product must be recorded.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are classified by frequency: Very common ($\ge$10%), Common ($\ge$1% to <10%), Uncommon ($\ge$0.1% to <1%).

| System Organ Class | Adverse Reaction | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Infections** | Nasopharyngitis | Common |
| **Immune System** | Hypersensitivity reactions | Common |
| | Anaphylactic reactions | Uncommon |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Constipation | Uncommon |
| **General Disorders** | Infusion-related reactions | Common |
| | Fatigue | Common |
| **Vascular** | Hypertension | Unknown (Post-marketing) |

- **Nasopharyngitis:** Most frequent after the initial dose (approx. 6–8%) and decreases in incidence with subsequent doses.
- **Immunogenicity:** Anti-drug antibodies (ADA) were detected in 18–20% of patients in pivotal trials, with neutralizing antibodies in 6.1–8.3%. There was no evidence of ADA-related impacts on efficacy or safety.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Cytochrome P450:** Eptinezumab is not metabolised by P450 enzymes; interactions with P450 substrates, inducers, or inhibitors are unlikely.
- **Sumatriptan:** Co-administration of 300 mg IV eptinezumab and 6 mg SC sumatriptan showed no significant pharmacokinetic alterations for either drug.
- **Other Interactions:** No other specific drug interaction studies have been conducted.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category B1 [Australian]:** Limited clinical data. Animal studies (rats/rabbits) at 25 times the MRHD showed no embryofetal toxicity. 
    - **Note:** IgG is known to cross the placental barrier. Use during pregnancy only if the benefit to the mother outweighs the potential risk to the fetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** No data on presence in human milk or effects on the infant. Human IgG is excreted in breast milk; the developmental benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need for the drug.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no adverse effects on male or female fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients <18 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use ($\ge$65 years):** Limited data available. No specific dose adjustments are recommended, though clinical experience is insufficient to determine if they respond differently than younger patients.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required based on population pharmacokinetic analysis.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment required; population PK analysis showed no impact of hepatic biomarkers on eptinezumab exposure.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Eptinezumab is a humanised IgG1 monoclonal antibody that binds with high affinity to both $\alpha$- and $\beta$- forms of the human calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) ligand. By binding the ligand, it prevents CGRP from activating its receptors, thereby inhibiting the neurogenic vasodilation associated with migraine pathophysiology.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Reduces capsaicin-induced neurogenic vasodilation by 41% on the day following a 100 mg dose.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Bioavailability:** 100% (IV administration).
    - **Distribution:** Central volume of distribution is approximately 3.7 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Degraded by proteolytic enzymes into peptides and amino acids.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 27 days; apparent clearance is 0.15 L/day.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **PROMISE 1 (Episodic Migraine):** A 48-week study (n=888) in patients with $\ge$4 to $\le$14 headache days/month. Both 100 mg and 300 mg doses significantly reduced mean monthly migraine days (MMD) from baseline compared to placebo over weeks 1–12.
- **PROMISE 2 (Chronic Migraine):** A 24-week study (n=1072) in patients with $\ge$15 to $\le$26 headache days/month. Significant reductions in MMD were observed for both doses. This trial included patients with Medication Overuse Headache (MOH), who showed similar reductions in MMD compared to those without MOH.
- **PREVAIL (Long-term Safety):** An 84-week open-label study (n=128) using 300 mg doses confirmed a safety profile consistent with placebo-controlled trials.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C. Keep in the original carton to protect from light. Do not freeze or shake.
- **Excursions:** If removed from refrigeration, vials may be kept at room temperature ($\le$25°C) for up to 7 days. If not used within this window, they must be discarded.
- **Diluted Solution:** To reduce microbiological risk, use as soon as practicable. If necessary, the diluted solution is stable for up to 8 hours at room temperature or refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C.
