# Editorial Product Information: ertugliflozin + sitagliptin

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **ertugliflozin + sitagliptin**
- Chemical class:
  - Ertugliflozin: sodium–glucose co-transporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitor
  - Sitagliptin: dipeptidyl peptidase‑4 (DPP‑4) inhibitor
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 2017.
- Date of last revision/update of the available product information: Revised 6/2026.
- Withdrawals, suspensions, restrictions: Not specified in available sources.
- Other jurisdictions' approval dates or PI revision dates: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- As an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycemic control in adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus.
- Limitations of use:
  - Not recommended for glycemic control in patients with type 1 diabetes mellitus.
  - Has not been studied in patients with a history of pancreatitis; effect in such patients is unknown.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General adult dosing
- Recommended starting dose: **5 mg ertugliflozin / 100 mg sitagliptin once daily**, taken in the morning, with or without food.
- For patients tolerating therapy who require additional glycemic lowering, increase to **15 mg ertugliflozin / 100 mg sitagliptin once daily**.

### Renal function considerations
- Assess renal function (e.g., eGFR) before initiating and periodically during therapy.
- Use is **not recommended** in patients with estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) < 45 mL/min/1.73 m².
- Use is **contraindicated** in patients with severe renal impairment (eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m²), end‑stage renal disease (ESRD), or on dialysis.

### Switching considerations
- Patients already receiving ertugliflozin may have ertugliflozin component continued when switching to the combination product (dose equivalence maintained).

### Interruption for procedures/surgery
- Withhold ertugliflozin + sitagliptin for at least 4 days, if possible, prior to surgery or procedures associated with prolonged fasting. Resume when clinically stable and oral intake resumed.
- Urinary glucose excretion attributable to the SGLT2 inhibitor component may persist for approximately 4 days after stopping; longer persistence (greater than 6 days and up to ~2 weeks) has been reported post‑marketing.

### Correction of volume depletion
- Assess and correct intravascular volume depletion prior to initiating therapy in patients suspected to be volume depleted.

### Missed dose
- Take the missed dose as soon as remembered. Do not double the next dose.

### Titration
- Initial dose: 5 mg ertugliflozin / 100 mg sitagliptin once daily.
- Increase to 15 mg ertugliflozin / 100 mg sitagliptin once daily if additional glycemic control is needed and the product is tolerated.

## 5. Contraindications
- Severe renal impairment (eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m²), end‑stage renal disease, or patients on dialysis.
- Known hypersensitivity to ertugliflozin, sitagliptin, or any excipient in the product (including anaphylaxis and angioedema reported).

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- Not specified as a boxed (black box) warning in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions (all clinically important items included)
- Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) and ketoacidosis:
  - Use in type 1 diabetes mellitus is contraindicated (not indicated) because SGLT2 inhibitors markedly increase DKA risk. Ketoacidosis may occur with lower-than-expected glucose values ("euglycemic" ketoacidosis).
  - Precipitating factors include insulin dose reduction or missed insulin, acute illness, reduced caloric intake, ketogenic diet, surgery, volume depletion, and alcohol abuse.
  - Consider ketone monitoring in patients at risk. If ketoacidosis is suspected, discontinue product, evaluate and treat, and do not restart until resolution.
  - Withhold therapy during temporary situations that predispose to ketoacidosis; resume when clinically stable with oral intake.
- Pancreatitis:
  - Postmarketing reports of acute pancreatitis (including hemorrhagic and necrotizing) have been reported with sitagliptin. Discontinue if pancreatitis suspected and manage appropriately.
  - Risk in patients with prior pancreatitis is unknown.
- Lower‑limb amputation:
  - Non‑traumatic lower‑limb amputations were observed in a long‑term cardiovascular outcomes study with higher event rates in ertugliflozin arms compared with placebo (see Section 7 and Section 12 Clinical Trials).
  - Monitor for foot infections, ulcers, new pain/tenderness, and institute preventive foot care.
- Acute renal failure / worsening renal function:
  - Postmarketing reports of acute renal failure and worsening renal function (some requiring dialysis) have been reported with sitagliptin and with SGLT2 inhibitors; some cases involved inappropriate sitagliptin dosing in renal impairment.
  - Assess renal function prior to initiation and monitor thereafter; consider temporary discontinuation if acute renal deterioration occurs and another cause is suspected.
- Volume depletion / symptomatic hypotension:
  - SGLT2 inhibition can cause intravascular volume contraction, sometimes leading to symptomatic hypotension or acute kidney injury.
  - Patients at increased risk: those with impaired renal function, elderly, low systolic blood pressure, or on diuretics (especially loop diuretics).
  - Correct volume depletion prior to initiation and monitor for signs/symptoms during therapy.
- Genitourinary infections:
  - Increased risk of genital mycotic infections and urinary tract infections due to increased urinary glucose excretion.
  - Serious infections reported postmarketing: urosepsis, pyelonephritis; and rare reports of necrotizing fasciitis of the perineum (Fournier’s gangrene). Evaluate and treat promptly; discontinue if necrotizing fasciitis suspected and institute urgent surgical/medical care.
- Heart failure:
  - An association between DPP‑4 inhibitor therapy and heart failure was observed with two other DPP‑4 inhibitors in cardiovascular outcomes trials. Consider risks and benefits in patients at risk for heart failure (history of heart failure, renal impairment) and monitor for symptoms.
- Hypoglycemia with insulin or insulin secretagogues:
  - Concomitant use with insulin or insulin secretagogues (e.g., sulfonylureas) increases risk of hypoglycemia. Consider lowering insulin or secretagogue dose and counsel patients on hypoglycemia recognition.
- Hypersensitivity reactions:
  - Postmarketing reports include anaphylaxis, angioedema, and exfoliative skin reactions including Stevens‑Johnson syndrome. Reactions commonly occurred within first 3 months, sometimes after first dose. Discontinue if suspected.
  - Use caution if history of angioedema with another DPP‑4 inhibitor.
- Severe and disabling arthralgia:
  - Postmarketing reports of severe, sometimes disabling arthralgia associated with DPP‑4 inhibitors; symptom onset ranged from 1 day to years; symptoms often resolved after discontinuation.
- Bullous pemphigoid:
  - Postmarketing cases requiring hospitalization reported with DPP‑4 inhibitor use. Discontinue if suspected and refer to dermatologist.
  
### Monitoring requirements
- Renal function (eGFR/serum creatinine) prior to initiation and periodically during therapy.
- Volume status and blood pressure prior to and after initiation (particularly in elderly, those with renal impairment, low systolic blood pressure, or on diuretics).
- Signs/symptoms of ketoacidosis (nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, malaise, dyspnea).
- Signs/symptoms of pancreatitis (persistent severe abdominal pain).
- Signs/symptoms of genitourinary infection and Fournier’s gangrene (genital/perineal pain, swelling, erythema, fever).
- Foot examinations for infection, ulcers, or signs of ischemia.
- Signs/symptoms of heart failure.
- Hypoglycemia monitoring when used with insulin or insulin secretagogues.
- Monitor for severe skin reactions, hypersensitivity, arthralgia, and bullous eruptions.

## 7. Adverse Effects
- The following adverse reactions are reported from clinical trials and postmarketing surveillance. Frequencies are stated where available in the provided sources; where not specified, this is noted.

### Summary of key adverse reactions (high‑level)
- Most common adverse reactions (documented in the available sources):
  - Female genital mycotic infections (associated with the ertugliflozin component) — reported at ≥5% incidence in clinical trials.
  - Upper respiratory tract infection, nasopharyngitis, and headache (associated with the sitagliptin component) — reported at ≥5% incidence in clinical trials.
  - Hypoglycemia more frequently reported when sitagliptin was added to sulfonylurea or insulin therapy in clinical studies.
- Important serious adverse reactions (postmarketing reports and trial observations):
  - Diabetic ketoacidosis (esp. in type 1 diabetes, and reported in type 2 with SGLT2 inhibitors)
  - Acute pancreatitis (including fatal/hemorrhagic/necrotizing)
  - Acute renal failure and worsening renal function (some requiring dialysis)
  - Volume depletion and symptomatic hypotension
  - Serious genitourinary infections: urosepsis, pyelonephritis; Fournier’s gangrene (necrotizing fasciitis of perineum)
  - Lower‑limb amputations (non‑traumatic; toe and foot most frequent)
  - Hypersensitivity reactions including anaphylaxis, angioedema, Stevens‑Johnson syndrome
  - Severe and disabling arthralgia
  - Bullous pemphigoid

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Insulin and insulin secretagogues (e.g., sulfonylureas):
  - Additive risk of hypoglycemia. Consider lowering dose of insulin or secretagogue when initiating combination therapy and counsel patients regarding hypoglycemia.
- Diuretics (particularly loop diuretics) and agents that reduce intravascular volume:
  - Concomitant use may increase risk of volume depletion and symptomatic hypotension. Assess and correct volume status before initiation; monitor closely after initiation.
- Other agents that may predispose to ketoacidosis (e.g., agents causing reduced caloric intake or dehydration):
  - Use caution and monitor.

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Specific pharmacokinetic drug–drug interaction data (e.g., CYP interactions, transporters) for the combination product are not specified in available sources. See individual component full prescribing information for detailed interaction data not reproduced here.
- Reports of worsening renal function have in some cases involved inappropriate sitagliptin dosing in renal impairment; observe standard dose adjustments for sitagliptin in renal impairment per individual product guidance (not specified in current available sources for the combination).

### Management notes
- Review concomitant medications before initiation for agents that increase risk of volume depletion or hypoglycemia.
- Adjust doses of insulin/secretagogues as clinically appropriate.
- Monitor renal function and electrolytes as indicated.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **Ertugliflozin**: Inhibits SGLT2 in the proximal renal tubules, reducing renal glucose reabsorption and increasing urinary glucose excretion, thereby lowering plasma glucose.
- **Sitagliptin**: Inhibits dipeptidyl peptidase‑4 (DPP‑4), increasing endogenous incretin levels (GLP‑1 and GIP), which increase insulin release and decrease glucagon levels in a glucose‑dependent manner.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Combination results in complementary glucose‑lowering actions: increased urinary glucose excretion (ertugliflozin) and enhanced incretin activity (sitagliptin).
- Urinary glucose excretion due to the SGLT2 inhibitor effect persists for approximately 4 days after discontinuation; postmarketing reports indicate glucosuria and ketoacidosis persisting beyond 6 days and up to about 2 weeks in some cases.

### Pharmacokinetics

## 12. Clinical Trials
### Trials of the combination
- Safety of concomitantly administered ertugliflozin and sitagliptin was evaluated in clinical studies comprising 990 patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus treated for 26 weeks across three studies:
  - A factorial study comparing ertugliflozin 5 mg or 15 mg in combination with sitagliptin 100 mg versus individual components.
  - A placebo‑controlled study of ertugliflozin 5 mg or 15 mg added to sitagliptin 100 mg and metformin.
  - A placebo‑controlled study of initial therapy with ertugliflozin 5 mg or 15 mg in combination with sitagliptin 100 mg.
- The adverse reaction profile observed with the combination in these trials was similar to that of the individual components.

### Ertugliflozin pooled data
- Pooled placebo‑controlled trials exposed 1,029 patients to ertugliflozin (mean exposure ~25 weeks), with ertugliflozin doses of 5 mg and 15 mg evaluated.

### Cardiovascular outcomes study
- In a long‑term cardiovascular outcomes study of patients with type 2 diabetes and established cardiovascular disease, non‑traumatic lower‑limb amputations were reported with event rates of:
  - Placebo: 4.7 events per 1000 patient‑years
  - Ertugliflozin 5 mg: 5.7 events per 1000 patient‑years
  - Ertugliflozin 15 mg: 6.0 events per 1000 patient‑years
- Toe and foot amputations were most frequent; many patients who underwent amputation had predisposing factors (peripheral arterial disease, prior amputation, diabetic foot, concomitant diuretics or insulin).

### Hypoglycemia in add‑on studies
- In add‑on to sulfonylurea and add‑on to insulin studies, hypoglycemia was more commonly reported in patients treated with sitagliptin compared with placebo.

### Trial design and outcomes
- Detailed primary efficacy outcomes (e.g., A1C reduction magnitude, cardiovascular endpoints other than amputation rates), subgroup analyses, and statistical results are not specified in the available sources beyond the safety observations noted above.
