# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA).
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. 
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis of the provided regulatory documentation and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. At the time of preparation, only the United States (FDA) documentation was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 [Not explicitly specified in source; provided for clinical context].
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg, administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - Frequency: Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less frequently than every 6 hours.
    - Subsequent daily doses should typically range from 50 mg/kg to as high as 300 mg/kg if clinically required [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Adjustment:** Dose adjustments (e.g., downward to 5 mg/kg) should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations and may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Monitoring and Administration Instructions
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations prior to therapy.
- Conduct weekly and monthly monitoring, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in patients with ESRD on dialysis. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate medical management [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulation Note):** While this editorial concerns the injection, clinicians should note that chronic high-dose oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disorders.

### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
The following events occurred at a frequency of ≥5% in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials, regardless of causality [US]:

| Body System | Adverse Event | Frequency (All Doses) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain | 7% – 33% |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia | 6% – 20% |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea | 8% – 16% |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia | 5% – 9% |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia | 4% – 13% |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis | 7% – 23% |

### Serious or Notable Adverse Effects
- **Neurologic:** Seizures.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent: gastritis and body odor.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis and bronchospasm.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating treatment or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified [US uses descriptive text]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant should be weighed against the benefit of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg for metabolic disorders); see Section 3.
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in provided sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. The safety of the *oral* form is not established in renal insufficiency [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Excretion:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), the distribution half-life is 0.585 hours and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Bioequivalence:** A study in 15 healthy volunteers confirmed bioequivalence between levocarnitine tablets and oral solution (Cmax ~80 µmol/L).
- **ESRD Efficacy:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg post-dialysis showed that trough concentrations (initially ~20 µmol/L) increased to 54–180 µmol/L after one week of therapy.
- **Clinical Outcomes:** While levocarnitine effectively increases carnitine plasma concentrations in ESRD patients, the impact on specific signs, symptoms, or clinical outcomes in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) when mixed in Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's in PVC bags (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial; contains no preservative.
