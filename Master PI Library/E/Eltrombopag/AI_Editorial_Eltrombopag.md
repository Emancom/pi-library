# Editorial Product Information: Eltrombopag

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (TGA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of eltrombopag for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory information to support clinical decision-making.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis prepared by a clinical pharmacist and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the local approved PI for full prescribing details and legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Eltrombopag (as olamine).
- **Chemical Class:** Thrombopoietin receptor (TPO-R) agonist; small molecule.
- **Brand Names:** Eltrombopag-RZ [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** B02BX05.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 25 mg and 50 mg [Australian].
    - Other dosage forms (e.g., powder for oral suspension) are referenced in pharmacokinetic data but specific local strengths were not specified in the primary source document.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Dr Reddy’s Laboratories (Australia) Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Immune Thrombocytopaenia (ITP):**
    - Adult patients with an inadequate response or intolerance to corticosteroids and immunoglobulins [Australian].
    - Paediatric patients (aged 1 to 17 years) who have failed other treatments and require increased platelet counts for a planned procedure or are at high risk of bleeding [Australian].
- **Chronic Hepatitis C (HCV):**
    - Treatment of thrombocytopenia in adult patients to allow the initiation and maintenance of interferon-based therapy [Australian].
- **Note:** Safety and efficacy have not been established for other thrombocytopenic conditions, such as chemotherapy-induced thrombocytopenia or Myelodysplastic Syndromes (MDS) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Principles
Dosing must be individualised based on platelet count. The goal is to achieve and maintain a count ≥ 50 x 10⁹/L to reduce bleeding risk; do not use eltrombopag to normalise platelet counts. Response typically takes 1–2 weeks.

### Chronic ITP
- **Adults and Paediatrics (6–17 years):** Starting dose is 50 mg once daily.
- **Paediatrics (1–5 years):** Starting dose is 25 mg once daily (12.5 mg may be considered) [Australian].
- **East/Southeast Asian Ancestry:** Initiate at a reduced dose of 25 mg once daily [Australian].
- **Maintenance/Titration:** Adjust in 25 mg increments. Maximum dose is 75 mg daily. Discontinue if counts do not reach a level to avoid bleeding after 4 weeks at 75 mg/day.

### Chronic Hepatitis C
- **Adults:** Initiate at 25 mg once daily for all patients, including those of East/Southeast Asian ancestry [Australian].
- **Titration:** Adjust in 25 mg increments every 2 weeks to reach the target count required to start antiviral therapy. Maximum dose is 100 mg daily.
- **Discontinuation:** Terminate eltrombopag when antiviral therapy is discontinued.

### Administration Instructions
- **Chelation:** Swallow tablets with water. Administer at least 2 hours before or 4 hours after polyvalent cations (e.g., antacids, dairy products, mineral supplements containing calcium, magnesium, iron, aluminium, zinc, or selenium) [Australian].
- **Food:** May be taken with food containing very little (< 50 mg) or preferably no calcium.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to eltrombopag olamine or any excipients (e.g., lactose) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Eltrombopag can cause severe hepatotoxicity and fatal liver injury. Monitor ALT, AST, and bilirubin before initiation, every 2 weeks during titration, and monthly thereafter. Discontinue if ALT increases to ≥ 3 x ULN (in patients with normal baseline) or meets specific criteria for progression/persistence [Australian].
- **Hepatic Decompensation:** In HCV patients with cirrhosis, concomitant use with interferon increases the risk of fatal hepatic decompensation (e.g., ascites, encephalopathy). Monitor closely if albumin is ≤ 35 g/L or MELD score is ≥ 10 [Australian].
- **Thrombotic/Thromboembolic Events (TEEs):** Risk of TEEs (including portal vein thrombosis, PE, DVT, and MI) exists even at low or normal platelet counts. Use caution in patients with known risk factors (e.g., Factor V Leiden, smoking, oral contraceptives) [Australian].
- **Bone Marrow Fibrosis:** TPO-R agonists may increase the risk of reticulin fibre deposition. Perform baseline and monthly peripheral blood smears; consider biennial bone marrow examinations [Australian].
- **Cataracts:** Eltrombopag is associated with cataract development or worsening. Baseline and regular ocular examinations are recommended [Australian].
- **MDS/Malignancy:** Theoretical risk of stimulating progression of existing haematological malignancies (e.g., MDS). Not indicated for MDS outside clinical trials [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Chronic ITP (Adults)
- **Very Common (>10%):** Diarrhoea, nausea, increased ALT, back pain.
- **Common (1–10%):** Vomiting, pharyngitis, increased AST, increased bilirubin, alopecia, rash, musculoskeletal pain, paraesthesia, menorrhagia, cataract, and dry eye.

### Chronic ITP (Paediatrics)
- **Very Common (>10%):** Nasopharyngitis, upper respiratory tract infection, cough, abdominal pain, pyrexia.

### Chronic Hepatitis C
- **Very Common (>10%):** Anaemia, decreased appetite, insomnia, headache, cough, nausea, diarrhoea, hyperbilirubinaemia, pruritus, alopecia, myalgia, fatigue, pyrexia, and peripheral oedema.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Thromboembolic events, drug-induced liver injury, hepatic failure, and thrombotic microangiopathy with acute renal failure (rare/post-marketing) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Polyvalent Cations:** Significant reduction in absorption due to chelation. Adhere to the 2-hour before/4-hour after dosing window.
- **Rosuvastatin:** Eltrombopag (an OATP1B1 and BCRP inhibitor) increases rosuvastatin exposure by ~55%. Consider reducing rosuvastatin dose by 50% [Australian].
- **Lopinavir/Ritonavir:** May decrease eltrombopag concentrations by ~17%. Monitor platelet counts closely [Australian].
- **Ciclosporin:** May decrease eltrombopag exposure; adjust eltrombopag dose based on platelet response [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category B3. Animal studies showed increased pre- and post-implantation loss and reduced foetal weight at maternally toxic doses. Use only if benefits clearly outweigh risks; human data are absent [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Secretion into human milk is unknown. Not recommended for nursing mothers [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No effect on fertility observed in animal models [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for chronic ITP in patients ≥ 1 year. Safety/efficacy in children < 1 year with ITP or any child with chronic HCV is not established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Limited data in patients ≥ 65 years. No overall differences in safety/efficacy observed, though greater sensitivity in some individuals cannot be ruled out. Exposure (AUC) is ~36% higher in patients > 60 years [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment necessary. Use with caution and monitor creatinine/urinalysis closely [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Chronic ITP:** Starting dose 25 mg once daily for any degree of impairment (Child-Pugh ≥ 5).
    - **Chronic HCV:** Starting dose 25 mg once daily.
    - **General:** Contraindicated for Child-Pugh ≥ 5 unless benefits outweigh the risk of portal vein thrombosis [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Eltrombopag is an oral TPO-R agonist that interacts with the transmembrane domain of the human TPO receptor. It initiates signalling cascades (similar but not identical to endogenous TPO) to induce proliferation and differentiation of megakaryocytes from bone marrow progenitor cells [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak concentrations in 2–6 hours. Bioavailability is at least 52%. High-fat/high-calcium meals significantly reduce exposure (AUC by 59%, Cmax by 65%) [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Highly plasma protein bound (> 99.9%). Substrate for BCRP.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via cleavage, oxidation (CYP1A2, CYP2C8), and glucuronidation (UGT1A1, UGT1A3).
    - **Elimination:** Faecal (59%) and urinary (31%) excretion. Half-life is 21–32 hours in healthy subjects and 26–35 hours in ITP patients [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **RAISE (Adult ITP):** Eltrombopag showed 8-fold higher odds of achieving platelet counts 50–400 x 10⁹/L vs placebo (p < 0.001) over 6 months [Australian].
- **PETIT 2 (Paediatric ITP):** 40% of eltrombopag patients achieved sustained response (platelets ≥ 50 x 10⁹/L for 6 of 8 weeks) vs 3% in placebo (p < 0.001) [Australian].
- **ENABLE 1 & 2 (Chronic HCV):** Significantly more patients achieved sustained virologic response (SVR) with eltrombopag compared to placebo, primarily by reducing the need for interferon dose reductions due to thrombocytopenia [Australian].
