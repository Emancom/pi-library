# Editorial Product Information: Eliglustat

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals (HCPs). 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI or local prescribing guidelines in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved labeling for definitive prescribing decisions.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Eliglustat (as eliglustat tartrate).
- **Chemical Class:** Synthetic small molecule; glucosylceramide synthase inhibitor.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Cerdelga** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A16AX10.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Hard capsules containing 84 mg of eliglustat (equivalent to 100 mg of eliglustat tartrate) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** sanofi-aventis australia pty ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Gaucher Disease Type 1 (GD1):** Indicated for the long-term treatment of adult patients with GD1 [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
Therapy must be initiated and supervised by a physician experienced in the management of Gaucher disease. **Patient genotyping for CYP2D6 is mandatory prior to treatment initiation** to determine metaboliser status [Australian].

- **CYP2D6 Intermediate Metabolisers (IMs) and Extensive Metabolisers (EMs):** 84 mg twice daily.
- **CYP2D6 Poor Metabolisers (PMs):** 84 mg once daily.
- **CYP2D6 Ultra-Rapid Metabolisers (URMs):** Should NOT be treated with eliglustat as adequate therapeutic concentrations may not be achieved.
- **Indeterminate Metabolisers:** Use is not recommended if the genotype cannot be determined.

### Administration Instructions
- Capsules must be swallowed whole, preferably with water. Do not crush, dissolve, or open capsules.
- May be taken with or without food.
- **Avoid** consumption of grapefruit or grapefruit juice (strong CYP3A inhibitor).

### Missed Dose Instructions
- If a dose is missed, take the prescribed dose at the next scheduled time. Do **not** double the dose to make up for a missed one [Australian].

### Prior Treatment with Enzyme Replacement Therapy (ERT)
- Patients switching from ERT to eliglustat may do so on the day following their last ERT dose. Monitor for disease stability in all domains (e.g., at 6 months and regularly thereafter) [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
Eliglustat is contraindicated in the following scenarios [Australian]:
- **Hypersensitivity** to the active substance or any excipients (e.g., lactose).
- **CYP2D6 IMs or EMs** taking a strong or moderate CYP2D6 inhibitor *concomitantly* with a strong or moderate CYP3A inhibitor.
- **CYP2D6 PMs** taking any strong CYP3A inhibitor.
- **CYP2D6 EMs with moderate/severe hepatic impairment.**
- **CYP2D6 EMs with mild hepatic impairment** who are also taking a strong or moderate CYP2D6 inhibitor.
- **CYP2D6 IMs or PMs with any degree of hepatic impairment.**

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **CYP2D6 Genotyping:** Mandatory before initiation. Dosing is strictly phenotype-dependent.
- **Drug-Drug Interactions:** Significant potential for elevations in plasma concentration leading to ECG interval prolongation.
- **ECG Interval Prolongation:** While not significant at therapeutic doses, concentrations 11-fold above expected levels are predicted to cause mild increases in PR, QRS, and QTc intervals. Avoid in patients with pre-existing cardiac conditions (e.g., congestive heart failure, recent MI, bradycardia, heart block, long QT syndrome) or in combination with Class IA or III antiarrhythmic medications [Australian].

### Monitoring Requirements
- Regular monitoring of disease stability (spleen/liver volume, haemoglobin, platelets) is required, particularly when switching from ERT.
- Adverse event monitoring is recommended in PMs as 84 mg QD dosing was predicted by PBPK modelling but not directly studied in this population.

### Lactose Intolerance
- Contains lactose; patients with rare hereditary problems of galactose intolerance, Lapp lactase deficiency, or glucose-galactose malabsorption should not take this medicine [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The majority of adverse reactions observed in clinical trials were mild and transient. 

### Common Adverse Reactions (1–10%)
| System Organ Class | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Dyspepsia (6%), upper abdominal pain, diarrhoea, nausea, constipation, gastro-oesophageal reflux disease, abdominal distension, gastritis |
| **Nervous System** | Headache, dizziness |
| **Cardiac** | Palpitations |
| **Musculoskeletal** | Arthralgia |
| **General** | Fatigue |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Syncope (0.8%):** The most frequently reported serious adverse reaction, often associated with pre-existing risk factors and appearing vasovagal in nature [Australian].

### Post-Marketing Experience
- **Respiratory:** Cough (frequency unknown).

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Agents Increasing Eliglustat Exposure
Eliglustat is a substrate of CYP2D6 and CYP3A4.
- **Strong CYP2D6 Inhibitors (e.g., paroxetine, fluoxetine, quinidine, bupropion):** Reduce eliglustat to 84 mg once daily in IMs/EMs.
- **Moderate CYP2D6 Inhibitors (e.g., duloxetine, terbinafine):** Use with caution.
- **Strong CYP3A Inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, clarithromycin, itraconazole):** Contraindicated in PMs; use with caution in IMs/EMs.

### Agents Decreasing Eliglustat Exposure
- **Strong CYP3A Inducers (e.g., rifampicin, carbamazepine, St. John’s wort):** Concomitant use is **not recommended** as it substantially reduces eliglustat exposure (up to 95% in PMs) [Australian].

### Impact of Eliglustat on Other Drugs
Eliglustat is an inhibitor of P-gp and CYP2D6 in vitro.
- **P-gp Substrates (e.g., digoxin, colchicine, dabigatran):** Exposure may be increased; monitor and consider dose reduction of the substrate.
- **CYP2D6 Substrates (e.g., metoprolol, tricyclic antidepressants, flecainide):** Exposure may be increased; dose reduction of these agents may be required.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B1]. Limited human data. Animal studies showed no embryofoetal toxicity at 4x clinical exposure but skeletal malformations at maternally toxic doses (>16x exposure). Use only if benefit outweighs risk.
- **Breastfeeding:** Unknown if excreted in human milk. A risk to newborns cannot be excluded based on animal data (reduced pup weight gain). A decision must be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Reversible inhibition of spermatogenesis observed in male rats at 10-20x human exposure. No impairment of female fertility in animal models [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children <18 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly (≥65 years):** Limited data; no significant differences in safety/efficacy observed compared to younger adults [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:**
  - **CYP2D6 EMs:** No adjustment for mild, moderate, or severe impairment. Not recommended in end-stage renal disease (ESRD).
  - **CYP2D6 IMs/PMs:** Not recommended for use in any degree of renal impairment [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
  - **CYP2D6 EMs:** Mild (Child-Pugh A): No adjustment (84 mg BID) unless taking inhibitors. Moderate (Child-Pugh B) or Severe (Child-Pugh C): **Contraindicated**.
  - **CYP2D6 IMs/PMs:** **Contraindicated** in any degree of hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Eliglustat is a substrate reduction therapy (SRT). It is a potent, specific inhibitor of glucosylceramide synthase, reducing the synthesis of glucosylceramide to balance the impaired catabolism in GD1 patients.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Low oral bioavailability (<5%); Tmax 1.5–3 hours. High-fat meals decrease Cmax (15%) but do not affect AUC.
  - **Distribution:** Vd 816 L; plasma protein binding 76–83%.
  - **Metabolism:** Primarily CYP2D6, secondary CYP3A4.
  - **Excretion:** Primarily via metabolism. Half-life is 4–7 hours in non-PMs and ~9 hours in PMs.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ENGAGE (Treatment-Naïve):** 9-month Phase 3 study. Eliglustat showed statistically significant improvements vs. placebo in spleen volume (-28% vs +2%), haemoglobin (+0.69 g/dL vs -0.54 g/dL), and liver volume [Australian].
- **ENCORE (Switch from ERT):** 12-month non-inferiority study vs. imiglucerase. Eliglustat met the primary composite endpoint for stability in 84.8% of patients (imiglucerase 93.6%), falling within the pre-specified -25% margin.
- **Study 304 (Long-term):** Open-label study showing sustained efficacy in visceral and haematological parameters for up to 8 years, alongside improvements in bone marrow infiltration and bone mineral density [Australian].
