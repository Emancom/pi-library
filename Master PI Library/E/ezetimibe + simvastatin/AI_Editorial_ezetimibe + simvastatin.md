## 3. Approved Indications
- Adults (≥18 years)
  - Prevention of cardiovascular disease in patients with coronary heart disease (CHD) and history of acute coronary syndrome (ACS) who are on the maximum tolerated dose of simvastatin and require additional LDL-C lowering to achieve modest further reduction in cardiovascular risk after at least one year of therapy.
  - Primary hypercholesterolaemia (heterozygous familial and non-familial) or mixed hyperlipidaemia as adjunctive therapy to diet where a combination product is appropriate:
    - Patients not adequately controlled with a statin or ezetimibe alone.
    - Patients already treated with a statin and ezetimibe.
  - Homozygous familial hypercholesterolaemia (HoFH) — as adjunct to other lipid-lowering treatments (e.g., LDL apheresis) or when such treatments are unavailable.
- Children and adolescents (10–17 years), pubertal status specified (boys Tanner Stage II and above; girls at least 1 year post-menarche)
  - Heterozygous familial hypercholesterolaemia (HeFH) — adjunctive therapy to diet where combination product use is appropriate (patients not controlled on statin or ezetimibe alone; patients already on both).
  - Homozygous familial hypercholesterolaemia (HoFH) — adjunctive therapy; may be used with other treatments (e.g., LDL apheresis).
- Notes:
  - Use in children <10 years: not recommended due to limited safety and efficacy data.
  - Use in pre-pubertal children and pre-menarcheal girls: not studied; not recommended.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Usual daily regimen: one dose once daily in the evening.
- Available fixed-dose combinations described in source: tablets containing ezetimibe 10 mg combined with simvastatin 10, 20, 40 or 80 mg. (Dose selection refers to the combined ezetimibe 10 mg with the indicated simvastatin dose.)
- Typical starting dose: ezetimibe 10 mg + simvastatin 10/20/40 mg once daily (commonly 10/10 mg to 10/40 mg/day as starting doses).
- Dose range: ezetimibe 10 mg + simvastatin 10 mg to ezetimibe 10 mg + simvastatin 80 mg daily.
- The ezetimibe 10 mg + simvastatin 80 mg dose should only be used in patients at high cardiovascular risk who have not achieved treatment goals on lower doses and when benefits are expected to outweigh potential risks (notably increased myopathy/rhabdomyolysis risk).

### Dosing by indication
- Prevention of cardiovascular events (post-ACS/CHD): In the IMProved Reduction of Outcomes: EZETIMIBE/SIMVASTATIN Efficacy International Trial (IMPROVE-IT), the starting dose used was ezetimibe 10 mg + simvastatin 40 mg once daily in the evening.
- Homozygous familial hypercholesterolaemia (HoFH): recommended dose ezetimibe 10 mg + simvastatin 40 mg/day or 10 mg + simvastatin 80 mg/day in the evening; 10/80 mg only when benefits outweigh risks. Use as adjunct to other lipid-lowering measures.
- Renal impairment / chronic kidney disease:  
  - eGFR ≥ 60 mL/min/1.73 m2 (mild renal insufficiency): no dosage adjustment required.
  - eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73 m2: recommended dose is ezetimibe 10 mg + simvastatin 20 mg once daily in the evening; efficacy and safety of higher doses not evaluated in this population.
- Concomitant therapy considerations:
  - With lomitapide in HoFH: do not exceed ezetimibe 10 mg + simvastatin 40 mg/day.
  - With certain interacting agents (see Section 8): dose limits apply (e.g., max ezetimibe 10 mg + simvastatin 20 mg or 40 mg depending on the interacting drug).
- Paediatric (10–17 years, pubertal): recommended usual starting dose ezetimibe 10 mg + simvastatin 10 mg once daily in the evening; dosing range 10/10 mg to a maximum of 10/40 mg/day. Initiation should be under specialist review. Not recommended in children <10 years or in pre-pubertal children and pre-menarcheal girls.

### Administration instructions
- Take once daily in the evening, with or without food.
- If co-administered with a bile acid sequestrant, administer the ezetimibe + simvastatin dose either ≥2 hours before or ≥4 hours after the bile acid sequestrant.

### Titration and monitoring
- After initiation or dose titration, lipid levels may be checked after ≥2 weeks and dose adjusted accordingly.
- When titrating to higher simvastatin-containing doses (especially 10/80 mg), consider periodic CK determinations and closer clinical monitoring for myopathy; see Section 6.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to ezetimibe, simvastatin, or any excipient.
- Active liver disease or unexplained persistent elevations of serum transaminases.
- Pregnancy and lactation.
- History of myopathy secondary to other lipid-lowering agents.
- Concomitant administration of potent CYP3A4 inhibitors at therapeutic doses (examples listed in interactions).
- Concomitant administration of gemfibrozil, ciclosporin, or danazol.
- Concomitant use with systemic fusidic acid (fusidic acid must not be co-administered with statins).
- Additional contraindicated combinations as specified under Interactions (Section 8).

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Myopathy and rhabdomyolysis:
  - Statins (including simvastatin) can rarely cause myopathy, which may present as muscle pain, tenderness, or weakness with CK elevation (CK > 10 × ULN commonly used to define myopathy). Rhabdomyolysis with or without acute renal failure secondary to myoglobinuria has occurred and can be fatal.
  - Risk of myopathy is increased with higher simvastatin exposure (e.g., higher simvastatin doses or drug interactions that increase simvastatin plasma levels) and in patients with predisposing factors (advanced age ≥65 years, female sex, uncontrolled hypothyroidism, renal impairment).
  - The risk of myopathy is dose-related for simvastatin. Clinical trial data indicate increasing incidence at higher simvastatin doses (see Section 7 Adverse Effects for numerical data).
  - Patients should be advised to promptly report unexplained muscle pain, tenderness or weakness, especially if accompanied by malaise or fever.
  - Discontinue ezetimibe + simvastatin immediately if myopathy is diagnosed or suspected.
  - Periodic CK measurements may be considered in patients starting therapy or whose dose is being increased; periodic CK monitoring is recommended when titrating to 10/80 mg. There is no assurance that monitoring will prevent myopathy.
  - Temporarily stop ezetimibe + simvastatin a few days prior to elective major surgery and when any major medical or surgical condition supervenes.

- Hepatic impairment:
  - Not recommended in patients with moderate or severe hepatic dysfunction (Child-Pugh score 7–9 and >9 respectively). No dose adjustment required in mild hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh 5–6).
  - Active liver disease or unexplained persistent transaminase elevations is a contraindication.

- Renal impairment:
  - Patients with severe CKD or complex medical histories (e.g., longstanding diabetes with renal insufficiency) merit closer monitoring due to higher risk of myopathy.

- Use in specific populations:
  - The safety and efficacy in children <10 years are not established; not recommended.
  - Experience in adolescents is limited and long-term (>1 year) safety data are lacking.

- Ethnicity considerations:
  - An increased risk of myopathy has been identified in Chinese patients in clinical trials; caution and use of the lowest effective dose are recommended when treating Asian patients.

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline liver function tests (transaminases) prior to initiating therapy and thereafter as clinically indicated.
- Baseline CK may be considered in patients at increased risk of myopathy; monitor CK if muscle symptoms occur.
- Lipid profile assessment after ≥2 weeks following initiation or dose change to assess response and guide titration.
- Additional monitoring as clinically indicated for renal function and signs of myopathy or hepatic dysfunction.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Temporarily discontinue before systemic fusidic acid therapy; do not co-administer systemic fusidic acid with ezetimibe + simvastatin.
- Review concomitant medications for known interactions that increase simvastatin exposure (see Section 8). Adjust ezetimibe + simvastatin dose or choose alternative lipid-lowering regimen when necessary.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- General principle: Because the combination contains simvastatin, interactions that increase simvastatin exposure raise the risk of myopathy/rhabdomyolysis. Review concomitant medications carefully and apply dose limits or avoid combinations as specified.

### Contraindicated concomitant medicines (do not co-administer)
- Potent CYP3A4 inhibitors at therapeutic doses (examples provided in source): examples include (but are not limited to) certain azole antifungals, some macrolide antibiotics, certain HIV protease inhibitors, boceprevir, telaprevir, nefazodone, and drugs containing cobicistat. If short-term treatment with a potent CYP3A4 inhibitor is unavoidable, suspend ezetimibe + simvastatin during that treatment.
- Gemfibrozil.
- Ciclosporin.
- Danazol.
- Fusidic acid (systemic): do not co-administer; if systemic fusidic acid is essential, discontinue ezetimibe + simvastatin for the duration of fusidic acid therapy and reintroduce ezetimibe + simvastatin seven days after the last fusidic acid dose. Advise patients to seek medical attention promptly for muscle symptoms if fusidic acid is used.

### Concomitant medicines requiring dose limits or caution (adjust ezetimibe + simvastatin dose)
- Amiodarone: do not exceed ezetimibe 10 mg + simvastatin 20 mg daily when co-administered.
- Verapamil or diltiazem: do not exceed ezetimibe 10 mg + simvastatin 20 mg daily when co-administered.
- Amlodipine: do not exceed ezetimibe 10 mg + simvastatin 40 mg daily when co-administered.
- Niacin (≥1 g/day, lipid-modifying doses): do not exceed ezetimibe 10 mg + simvastatin 20 mg daily when co-administered. Note: coadministration with lipid-modifying doses of niacin-containing products is not recommended in Asian patients due to higher incidence of myopathy in Chinese patients.
- Products that inhibit Breast Cancer Resistance Protein (BCRP) (e.g., elbasvir and grazoprevir): do not exceed ezetimibe 10 mg + simvastatin 20 mg daily when co-administered.
- Moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors: co-administration may increase simvastatin exposure especially at higher ezetimibe + simvastatin doses; a dose adjustment may be necessary (specific dose adjustments not universally specified beyond those listed).
- Lomitapide (in HoFH): do not exceed ezetimibe 10 mg + simvastatin 40 mg daily when co-administered.
- Fibrates (other than gemfibrozil): safety and effectiveness when co-administered have not been studied; concomitant use should be avoided. (Gemfibrozil is specifically contraindicated.)
- Bile acid sequestrants: administer ezetimibe + simvastatin either ≥2 hours before or ≥4 hours after a bile acid sequestrant.

### Management notes
- If an interacting agent that is contraindicated is required, discontinue ezetimibe + simvastatin for the duration of the interacting therapy (e.g., potent CYP3A4 inhibitors, fusidic acid) or choose an alternative therapy.
- Consider alternative statin-ezetimibe regimens with lower interaction potential or use lower simvastatin doses when interacting drugs cannot be avoided.
- Closely monitor for signs and symptoms of myopathy and check CK if muscle symptoms occur.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Adolescents (10–17 years), pubertal (boys Tanner Stage II and above; girls at least 1 year post-menarche):
  - Indicated for HeFH and HoFH as adjunct to diet where combination product is appropriate.
  - Recommended starting dose: ezetimibe 10 mg + simvastatin 10 mg once daily in the evening.
  - Dosing range: ezetimibe 10/10 mg to a maximum of ezetimibe 10/40 mg/day.
  - Initiation should be by or under review of a specialist.
  - Clinical experience limited; long-term safety (>1 year) not established.
- Children <10 years: not recommended; not studied in pre-pubertal children or pre-menarcheal girls.

### Elderly
- No dosage adjustment required solely on the basis of age. Exercise caution in elderly patients given increased baseline risk for myopathy.

### Renal impairment
- Mild renal insufficiency (eGFR ≥60 mL/min/1.73 m2): no dosage adjustment.
- eGFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m2: recommended dose ezetimibe 10 mg + simvastatin 20 mg once daily in the evening; safety and efficacy of higher doses not evaluated in this population. Monitor closely for myopathy.

### Hepatic impairment
- Mild hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh score 5–6): no dosage adjustment required.
- Moderate to severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh score ≥7): treatment not recommended.
- Active liver disease or unexplained persistent elevations of serum transaminases: contraindicated.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- IMProved Reduction of Outcomes: EZETIMIBE/SIMVASTATIN Efficacy International Trial (IMPROVE-IT)
  - Design: Randomised trial in patients with coronary heart disease post-acute coronary syndrome.
  - Population: 18,144 patients randomized to ezetimibe + simvastatin 10/40 mg daily (n=9,067) or simvastatin 40 mg daily (n=9,077).
  - Follow-up: Median 6.0 years.
  - Safety outcomes reported in source:
    - Myopathy incidence: 0.2% in ezetimibe + simvastatin 10/40 mg group vs 0.1% in simvastatin 40 mg group (myopathy defined as unexplained muscle weakness or pain with serum CK ≥ 10 × ULN or two consecutive CK ≥ 5 and <10 × ULN).
    - Rhabdomyolysis incidence: 0.1% in ezetimibe + simvastatin group vs 0.2% in simvastatin group (trial-specific definitions applied).
  - Note: IMPROVE-IT served as a key trial for cardiovascular outcomes and safety comparisons between combination therapy and simvastatin alone.

- Chronic kidney disease trial
  - Design: Randomised trial in patients with chronic kidney disease.
  - Population: Over 9,000 patients randomized to ezetimibe + simvastatin 10/20 mg daily (n ≈ 4,650) or placebo (n ≈ 4,620).
  - Follow-up: Median 4.9 years.
  - Safety outcomes reported:
    - Myopathy/rhabdomyolysis incidence: 0.2% with ezetimibe + simvastatin vs 0.1% with placebo.

- Simvastatin clinical trial database and SEARCH trial
  - Clinical trial database: 41,413 patients treated with simvastatin; in trials with median follow-up ≥4 years (n=24,747), incidence of myopathy approximately 0.03% at 20 mg/day, 0.08% at 40 mg/day, and 0.61% at 80 mg/day. These trials often excluded certain interacting medicines and included careful monitoring.
  - SEARCH trial: large long-term trial in post-myocardial infarction patients comparing simvastatin 80 mg/day vs 20 mg/day (mean follow-up 6.7 years). Reported incidence of myopathy ~1.0% at 80 mg/day vs 0.02% at 20 mg/day; rhabdomyolysis incidence ~0.1–0.2% at 80 mg/day.
  - Note: These simvastatin data are included to illustrate dose-related risk of myopathy associated with simvastatin and to contextualise risk when using combination products containing simvastatin.

- Additional trial observations
  - An increased risk of myopathy was identified in Chinese patients in a trial of simvastatin 40 mg/day: incidence of myopathy ~0.05% in non-Chinese patients vs ~0.24% in Chinese patients. Caution advised when prescribing to Asian patients and to use the lowest effective dose.

- Trial definitions and thresholds:
  - Myopathy and rhabdomyolysis definitions varied between trials, often using CK thresholds (e.g., CK ≥10 × ULN for myopathy; subsets for rhabdomyolysis included CK thresholds plus evidence of end-organ damage).

- Other efficacy or safety trial details beyond those summarised above: Not specified in available sources.
