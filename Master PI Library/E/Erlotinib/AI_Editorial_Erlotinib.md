# Editorial Product Information: Erlotinib

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the local approved PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Erlotinib (as hydrochloride).
- **Chemical Class:** Antineoplastic agent; epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) tyrosine kinase inhibitor.
- **Brand Names:** Erlotinib Cipla [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01EB02.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Film-coated tablets in 25 mg, 100 mg, and 150 mg strengths [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Cipla Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
### Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)
- **First-line treatment:** Patients with advanced (Stage IIIB) or metastatic (Stage IV) NSCLC with activating EGFR mutations [Australian].
- **Maintenance therapy:** Patients with locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC with activating EGFR mutations whose disease has not progressed following first-line chemotherapy [Australian].
- **Second/Third-line therapy:** Treatment of patients with locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC after failure of at least one prior chemotherapy regimen [Australian].

### Pancreatic Cancer
- **First-line treatment:** In combination with gemcitabine for patients with locally advanced, unresectable, or metastatic pancreatic cancer [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Administration:** Erlotinib must be taken at least one hour before or two hours after food ingestion (empty stomach). 
- **Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer:** The recommended daily dose is 150 mg.
- **Pancreatic Cancer:** The recommended daily dose is 100 mg, administered in combination with gemcitabine.

### Dose Adjustments
- When dose reduction is necessary, it should be managed in 50 mg decrements [Australian].
- **Concomitant Medications:** Dose adjustments may be required when used with CYP3A4 inhibitors or inducers (see Section 7).
- **Smoking:** Cigarette smoking reduces erlotinib exposure. If a patient continues to smoke, a dose increase to 300 mg may be considered with close monitoring, though data on efficacy and long-term safety of this dose are limited [Australian].

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Erlotinib is contraindicated in patients with severe hypersensitivity to erlotinib or any of its excipients (e.g., lactose) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **EGFR Mutation Status:** Testing for activating EGFR mutations is recommended prior to initiating first-line or maintenance therapy in NSCLC patients [Australian].
- **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD):** Uncommon but potentially fatal ILD-like events have been reported. Erlotinib should be interrupted pending evaluation of new or progressive pulmonary symptoms (dyspnoea, cough, fever) and discontinued if ILD is diagnosed [Australian].
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Serious drug-induced liver injury (DILI), including fatal hepatic failure, has occurred. Periodic liver function tests (LFTs) are required. Treatment should be interrupted or discontinued for severe changes in LFTs [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal Perforation:** Increased risk in patients receiving concomitant anti-angiogenic agents, corticosteroids, NSAIDs, or those with a history of peptic ulcers. Erlotinib must be permanently discontinued if perforation occurs [Australian].
- **Bullous and Exfoliative Skin Conditions:** Rare cases suggestive of Stevens-Johnson syndrome or toxic epidermal necrolysis have been reported. Treatment should be interrupted or discontinued for severe bullous or blistering conditions [Australian].
- **Ocular Disorders:** Rare cases of corneal perforation or ulceration. Keratitis and abnormal eyelash growth are risk factors. Therapy should be interrupted for acute/worsening ocular symptoms [Australian].
- **Renal Failure/Hypokalaemia:** Rare reports of renal failure, often secondary to severe dehydration from diarrhoea or vomiting. Monitor renal function and electrolytes in at-risk patients [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Summary (Monotherapy)
- **Very Common (>10%):** Rash (75%), Diarrhoea (54%), Fatigue (52%), Anorexia (52%), Nausea (33%), Vomiting (23%), Stomatitis (17%), Pruritus (13%), Dry skin (12%), Conjunctivitis (12%), Keratoconjunctivitis sicca (12%), and Dyspnoea [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Epistaxis, paronychia, and infection [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD), Gastrointestinal perforation, and Gastrointestinal bleeding [Australian].
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Hepatic failure, Stevens-Johnson syndrome/Toxic epidermal necrolysis, corneal ulceration, and pneumatosis intestinalis [Australian].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- ILD-like events (fatalities reported).
- Gastrointestinal perforation and bleeding.
- Severe Drug-Induced Liver Injury (DILI).
- Myocardial infarction/ischaemia and cerebrovascular accidents (observed in pancreatic cancer trials in combination with gemcitabine) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Potent inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, clarithromycin, voriconazole) increase erlotinib exposure. Reduce erlotinib dose if toxicity is observed [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Potent inducers (e.g., rifampicin, phenytoin, carbamazepine, St. John’s Wort) significantly decrease erlotinib exposure. Alternative treatments should be considered; if rifampicin is required, increase erlotinib dose up to 450 mg with close monitoring [Australian].
- **Gastric pH-Altering Agents:** Erlotinib solubility is pH-dependent. Omeprazole (PPI) and ranitidine (H2RA) significantly reduce exposure. Avoid PPIs where possible. If using H2RAs, erlotinib must be taken 2 hours before or 10 hours after the H2RA dose [Australian].
- **Anticoagulants:** Elevated INR and bleeding events have been reported with warfarin. Monitor prothrombin time/INR regularly [Australian].
- **Statins:** Potential for increased risk of statin-induced myopathy or rhabdomyolysis [Australian].
- **Smoking:** Induces CYP1A1/1A2, reducing erlotinib exposure by 50–60%. Advise patients to quit [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category C. Studies in animals show reproductive toxicity and embryo-foetal lethality. Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during treatment and for at least 2 weeks after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if erlotinib is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended during therapy and for at least 2 weeks after the final dose [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal data suggests erlotinib may affect ovulation in females, potentially reducing fertility; however, no impairment of fertility was observed in male rats [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in patients <18 years [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No meaningful differences in safety or pharmacokinetics; no specific dose adjustments required [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not studied in patients with renal compromise; however, <9% of a dose is excreted renally [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Erlotinib is primarily cleared by the liver. Use with caution and monitor LFTs closely. Treatment is not recommended in patients with severe hepatic dysfunction [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Potent inhibitor of the intracellular phosphorylation of HER1/EGFR (Human Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor Type 1) tyrosine kinase. This inhibition prevents downstream signalling pathways involved in cell proliferation and apoptosis.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** ~59% bioavailable; Tmax ~4 hours. Exposure is increased by food.
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (~95%), primarily to albumin and alpha-1 acid glycoprotein (AAG). Apparent volume of distribution ~232 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic via CYP3A4/CYP3A5; minor pathways via CYP1A2 and CYP1A1.
    - **Elimination:** Predominantly faecal (>90%); renal elimination <9%. Mean half-life is ~36.2 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **EURTAC (ML20650):** Demonstrated superior progression-free survival (PFS) for erlotinib (10.4 months) vs. platinum-based chemotherapy (5.4 months) as first-line treatment in NSCLC patients with EGFR mutations [Australian].
- **SATURN (BO18192):** Showed PFS benefit as maintenance therapy in NSCLC, particularly in the EGFR mutation-positive subgroup [Australian].
- **BR.21:** Pivotal Phase III study in second/third-line NSCLC. Erlotinib improved median survival to 6.7 months vs. 4.7 months for placebo [Australian].
- **PA.3:** In pancreatic cancer, erlotinib + gemcitabine improved median survival to 6.4 months vs. 6 months for gemcitabine alone [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].
