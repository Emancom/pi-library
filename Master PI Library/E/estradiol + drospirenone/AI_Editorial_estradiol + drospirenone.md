# Editorial Product Information: estradiol + drospirenone

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **estradiol + drospirenone**
- Chemical class:
  - Estradiol: natural estrogen (estrogen receptor agonist)
  - Drospirenone: synthetic progestogen (anti-mineralocorticoid and anti-androgenic properties described in product literature)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Hormone replacement therapy (HRT) for short-term treatment of climacteric (menopausal) symptoms caused by deficient endogenous estrogen production in postmenopausal women with an intact uterus (indication presented in available sources).
- Use in other indications: Not specified in available sources
- Jurisdictional differences in indications: Not specified in available sources

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Typical regimen described in available sources: one oral tablet once daily containing estradiol 1.0 mg and drospirenone 2.0 mg (continuous combined regimen).
- Treatment is continuous: one tablet daily, next pack follows immediately without a break.
- Duration: HRT should be used at the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration consistent with treatment goals and individual risk profile; continued use should be regularly reassessed.

### Dosing by indication
- Short-term management of menopausal climacteric symptoms in postmenopausal women with intact uterus: one tablet daily as above.
- Other indications or dose adjustments: Not specified in available sources

### Administration instructions
- Tablets to be swallowed whole with some liquid; can be taken irrespective of food intake.
- Preferably take at the same time each day.
- If changing from a sequential combined HRT, start at the end of the scheduled bleeding; women not currently on estrogens or changing from a continuous combination product may start at any time.

### Missed dose instructions
- If a tablet is missed, it should be taken as soon as possible.
- If more than 24 hours have elapsed since the missed dose, no extra tablet is required; however, if several tablets are missed, breakthrough bleeding may occur.

### Titration schedules
- Not specified in available sources

## 5. Contraindications
(Complete list from available sources)
- Undiagnosed vaginal bleeding
- Known or suspected breast cancer
- Known or suspected premalignant conditions or malignancies if sex steroid‑influenced
- Presence or history of liver tumours (benign or malignant)
- Severe hepatic disease
- Presence or history of severe renal disease as long as renal function values have not returned to normal
- Acute arterial thromboembolism (e.g. myocardial infarction, stroke)
- Active deep venous thrombosis, thromboembolic disorders, or a documented history of these conditions
- A high risk of venous or arterial thrombosis
- Severe hypertriglyceridemia
- Pregnancy
- Lactation
- Known hypersensitivity to estradiol, drospirenone, or any excipient in the product

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed / Prominent warning
- [Australian] Estrogens, with or without progestogens, should not be used for the prevention of cardiovascular disease or dementia. Clinical trials (notably the Women’s Health Initiative and its Memory Study) reported increased risks of stroke, venous thromboembolism, myocardial infarction, pulmonary embolism, and probable dementia in older postmenopausal women receiving certain estrogen-containing regimens. Because of these risks, prescribe estrogens with or without progestogens at the lowest effective dose and for the shortest duration consistent with treatment goals and individual risk.

### General warnings and precautions
- Risk–benefit assessment: Initiate HRT only after careful consideration of the individual woman’s risks and treatment goals. Reassess periodically and discontinue if risks outweigh benefits.
- Use only for women with menopausal symptoms; ordinarily not for long-term preventive use.
- Combination continuous HRT with estradiol + drospirenone should not be used in women who have had a hysterectomy.
- ANGELIQ product name removed per instruction — generic name used.

### Thromboembolic disease
- Venous thromboembolism (VTE): Increased relative risk of deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism reported in randomized and epidemiologic studies. Risk factors (personal or family history of VTE, severe obesity, increasing age) increase risk; recent immobilisation, major surgery, trauma may temporarily increase VTE risk. Consider temporary discontinuation for prolonged immobilisation or major surgery. Stop treatment immediately if thrombotic events or suspicion occur.
- Observational data summarized in available sources: one case‑control study reported adjusted OR for VTE in current users of oral estrogen replacement therapy versus non‑users 3.5 (95% CI 1.8–6.8); for transdermal estrogen users OR 0.9 (95% CI 0.5–1.6); estimated OR for oral vs transdermal 4.0 (1.9–8.3).

### Arterial thromboembolism and cardiovascular disease
- Large trials with other estrogen preparations reported increased or no benefit in coronary heart disease and increased risk of stroke (secondary outcome) with some regimens. Uncertainty remains whether findings apply to all HRT products or non‑oral routes; therefore caution is warranted.

### Malignancy risks
- Breast cancer: Epidemiologic meta-analysis reported increased risk of breast cancer diagnosis with several years of HRT use; relative risk increases approximately 2.3% per year of use (meta-analysis cited in available sources). Risk decreases after stopping HRT. HRT increases mammographic density which may impair radiologic detection.
- Ovarian cancer: Meta-analysis cited reported a small increased risk for ovarian cancer in HRT users (prospective studies RR 1.20, 95% CI 1.15–1.26; current users RR 1.43, 95% CI 1.31–1.56). Absolute excess risk in women 50–54 taking 5 years of HRT estimated in source text as about 1 extra case per 2000 users (source data).
- Endometrial cancer: Unopposed estrogens increase risk of endometrial hyperplasia/carcinoma; addition of a progestogen (drospirenone) opposes the development of endometrial hyperplasia.

### Central nervous system / Dementia
- The Women’s Health Initiative Memory Study reported increased risk of probable dementia in women aged 65 years and older treated with conjugated estrogens with or without progestogen. It is unknown whether this applies to other HRT products. Consider age-related dementia risks when initiating HRT in older women.

### Hepatobiliary
- Estrogens increase bile lithogenicity; gallbladder disease may be predisposed during estrogen therapy.
- Liver tumours (rare benign, very rarely malignant) have been observed after hormonal therapy; consider hepatic tumour in differential diagnosis for upper abdominal pain, hepatomegaly, or intra‑abdominal hemorrhage.
- Contraindicated in presence/history of liver tumours and severe hepatic disease. Monitor liver function and discontinue if deterioration.

### Metabolic / Lipids / Pancreatitis
- Women with moderately elevated triglycerides require special surveillance; HRT may further increase triglycerides and risk of acute pancreatitis.
- Small increases in blood pressure reported; clinically relevant hypertension is rare but consider withdrawal if sustained clinically significant hypertension develops.
- HRT may affect peripheral insulin resistance and glucose tolerance; diabetics should be carefully monitored.

### Renal / Potassium risk
- Drospirenone: theoretical risk of hyperkalemia in patients with limited renal potassium excretion capacity and in those concomitantly using potassium‑sparing drugs; in a study no serum potassium change observed in mild or moderate renal impairment, but caution advised if baseline potassium in upper reference range or concomitant potassium‑sparing drugs are used.
- Contraindicated in severe renal disease.

### Other clinical precautions
- Abnormal uterine bleeding: frequent or persistent bleeding indicates need for endometrial assessment.
- Uterine fibroids may increase in size; discontinue if observed.
- Reactivation of endometriosis: discontinue if occurs.
- If prolactinoma is suspected, exclude prior to initiation.
- Chloasma (melasma) may occur; patients with history should avoid sun/UV exposure.
- Conditions reported to occur or deteriorate with HRT and requiring careful monitoring: epilepsy, benign breast disease, asthma, migraine, porphyria, otosclerosis, systemic lupus erythematosus, chorea minor.
- Hereditary angioedema: exogenous estrogens may induce or exacerbate symptoms.

### Monitoring requirements
- Prior to initiation and periodically thereafter: complete medical history and physical examination, including pelvic organs, routine cervical cytology, abdominal exam, breast exam, and blood pressure.
- Laboratory monitoring as clinically indicated: liver function tests, lipid profile (triglycerides), glucose control in diabetics, electrolytes (serum potassium) in at‑risk patients, and other tests affected by sex steroids (thyroid, adrenal, renal function, carrier proteins).
- Frequency and extent of monitoring: follow established clinical practice guidelines and individualize.

### Key serious adverse outcomes (summarized)
- Venous thromboembolism: deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism (increased risk with oral estrogen-containing therapy; study ORs provided in text).
- Arterial thromboembolism: myocardial infarction, stroke (reported increased risk with some estrogen/ progestogen regimens in clinical trials).
- Breast cancer: increased incidence with prolonged HRT use (relative risk increase approx. 2.3% per year of use reported in meta-analysis).
- Ovarian cancer: small increased risk (prospective studies RR 1.20; current users RR 1.43 per meta-analysis cited).
- Endometrial hyperplasia/cancer if estrogen unopposed (progestogen addition opposes hyperplasia).
- Hepatic tumours (rare), gallbladder disease, pancreatitis secondary to hypertriglyceridemia.
- Probable dementia in women ≥65 years in one trial population (WHIMS) with certain estrogen regimens.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Enzyme inducers (may increase clearance of sex hormones, reduce efficacy and alter bleeding profile):
  - Examples given: phenytoin, barbiturates, primidone, carbamazepine, rifampicin; possibly oxcarbazepine, topiramate, felbamate, griseofulvin, St John’s wort.
  - Enzyme induction can be observed after a few days; maximal effect within weeks and may persist ≈4 weeks after stopping inducer.
- HIV/HCV protease inhibitors and non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors:
  - Variable effects reported; co‑administration can increase or decrease estrogen/progestin plasma concentrations; changes may be clinically relevant in some cases.
- Enzyme inhibitors (may increase concentrations of estrogen/progestin):
  - Strong/moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors (azole antifungals e.g. ketoconazole, itraconazole, voriconazole, fluconazole), verapamil, macrolides (clarithromycin, erythromycin), diltiazem, grapefruit juice can increase steroid concentrations; clinical relevance unclear.
- Alcohol:
  - Acute alcohol ingestion during HRT use may lead to elevations of circulating estradiol levels.
- Drospirenone effects on CYPs:
  - In vitro drospirenone inhibited CYP1A1, CYP2C9, CYP2C19 and CYP3A4 at concentrations ~100–1000x higher than free drospirenone steady‑state levels.
  - Clinical interaction studies:
    - Drospirenone 3 mg for 14 days with omeprazole 40 mg: no significant influence on CYP2C19 substrate AUC or CYP3A4 marker AUC in the studied genotypes.
    - In vivo studies with simvastatin or midazolam as marker substrates suggest clinically relevant CYP‑mediated interactions with drospirenone 3 mg are unlikely.
- Anticoagulants:
  - Antagonism of anticoagulant effectiveness may occur; monitor coagulation parameters if clinically indicated.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Antihypertensive medications:
  - Concomitant use may produce an additional blood pressure lowering effect.
- NSAIDs plus antihypertensive drugs with ANGELIQ (estradiol + drospirenone) may cause a small increase in serum potassium, more pronounced in diabetic women.
- Potassium‑sparing drugs:
  - Combined use may increase risk of hyperkalemia (theoretical risk); monitor serum potassium in at‑risk patients.

### Clinically significant interactions and management
- When concomitant enzyme inducers are required, consider alternative contraception/HRT strategies or additional monitoring for reduced efficacy; consider adjusting therapy after inducer discontinuation when enzyme activity returns to baseline.
- When co‑administering strong CYP3A4 inhibitors, consider monitoring for increased steroid-related adverse effects.
- Monitor coagulation in patients on anticoagulants and HRT.
- Monitor blood pressure, serum potassium (in at‑risk patients), lipids and glucose as clinically indicated.

- Jurisdiction-specific interaction differences: Not specified in available sources

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Contraindicated in pregnancy.
- [Australian] Pregnancy category: B3 (as stated in available sources).
- Animal data (from available sources): drospirenone and/or metabolites cross the placenta in rats and rabbits; combinations of drospirenone with ethinylestradiol in animal studies produced dose‑dependent embryolethality (increased pre‑ and post‑implantation losses) and sex‑specific fetal effects (feminisation of male fetuses and virilisation of female fetuses in some studies). No specific data for the estradiol + drospirenone combination in pregnancy were provided in the available sources.

### Breastfeeding
- Contraindicated during lactation (product listed pregnancy or lactation as contraindication).
- Effects on milk production or infant from estradiol + drospirenone: Not specified in available sources beyond contraindication.

### Fertility
- Effects on fertility: Not specified in available sources (section in source referred to mechanism but details not provided).

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Not indicated for use in children or adolescents.

### Elderly use
- Use in elderly women: special risks noted from clinical trials (Women’s Health Initiative / WHIMS) include increased risks of cardiovascular events, VTE and probable dementia in older age groups; HRT is not indicated for dementia prevention. Use with caution; benefits versus risks should be carefully considered in older women.

### Renal impairment
- Mild to moderate renal impairment: a slight increase in drospirenone exposure was observed but not expected to be clinically relevant; drospirenone well tolerated in mild/moderate renal impairment per available sources.
- Severe renal disease: contraindicated.

### Hepatic impairment
- Mild to moderate hepatic impairment: drospirenone was reported to be well tolerated in mild/moderate hepatic impairment in available sources.
- Severe hepatic disease: contraindicated; presence/history of liver tumours contraindicates use. Monitor liver function tests periodically and discontinue if deterioration occurs.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Estradiol: provides estrogenic activity to replace deficient endogenous estrogen in postmenopausal women and relieve vasomotor and other menopausal symptoms.
- Drospirenone: progestogenic activity added to oppose estrogen-induced endometrial proliferation; drospirenone addition is reported to oppose development of endometrial hyperplasia caused by estrogens (as stated in available sources). Drospirenone also has anti‑mineralocorticoid properties (the product literature implies a potential to affect potassium balance).

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: Not specified in available sources.
- Distribution: Not specified in available sources.
- Metabolism:
  - Drospirenone: in vitro data show potential to inhibit several CYP enzymes at supra‑physiologic concentrations; however, clinical interaction studies at 3 mg/day did not demonstrate significant CYP2C19 or CYP3A4 inhibition.
- Elimination: Not specified in available sources.
- Special population pharmacokinetics:
  - Mild/moderate renal impairment: slight increase in drospirenone exposure (not expected to be clinically relevant).
  - Mild/moderate hepatic impairment: drospirenone reportedly well tolerated.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Women’s Health Initiative (WHI): large randomized controlled trials with conjugated estrogens (CEE) alone and combined with medroxyprogesterone acetate (MPA) reported increased risks of stroke and VTE (CEE alone showed increased stroke risk as secondary outcome in some analyses), and increased risks of myocardial infarction, stroke, invasive breast cancer, pulmonary emboli and deep vein thrombosis with combined CEE+MPA over ~5 years. These findings prompted conservative use of HRT at lowest effective dose for shortest duration.
- Women’s Health Initiative Memory Study (WHIMS): sub-study of WHI in women aged ≥65 years showed a two‑fold increase in probable dementia in women treated with conjugated estrogens with medroxyprogesterone acetate over ~4 years; applicability to other estrogen/progestogen regimens is unknown.
- Case–control study (Scarabin et al.): conducted in France (1999–2002), 155 first‑episode VTE cases vs 381 controls: current user OR for VTE with oral estrogen replacement therapy vs non‑users 3.5 (95% CI 1.8–6.8); transdermal estrogen current use OR 0.9 (95% CI 0.5–1.6); estimated risk for oral vs transdermal users OR 4.0 (1.9–8.3).
- Hodis et al.: double‑blind randomized placebo‑controlled angiographic trial in menopausal women with at least one coronary artery lesion showed no significant effect of estradiol (with or without medroxyprogesterone acetate) on progression of atherosclerosis (quantitative coronary angiography endpoints).
- Epidemiologic meta-analyses:
  - Breast cancer: meta‑analysis of 51 epidemiologic studies reported increased risk of breast cancer diagnosis with several years of HRT use; relative risk increases with duration (~2.3% per year).
  - Ovarian cancer: meta‑analysis of 52 epidemiologic studies reported a small increased risk of ovarian cancer with estrogen‑only and combined HRT (prospective RR 1.20; current users RR 1.43).
- Other trial and study details: Not all trial design specifics, dosing comparisons, or full outcome tables are provided in the available sources; where summarized above, only the principal outcomes reported in the available sources are included.

- Additional clinical trial data specific to the estradiol + drospirenone fixed combination (pivotal randomized controlled trials, safety and efficacy endpoints specific to this combination product) are Not specified in available sources.
