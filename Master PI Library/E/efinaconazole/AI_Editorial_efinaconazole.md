# Editorial Product Information: Efinaconazole

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **United States** (Food and Drug Administration).
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory information into a single reference.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference and does not replace the official PI or prescribing requirements within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Efinaconazole (azole antifungal).
- **Brand Names:** **JUBLIA** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Topical solution, 10% (100 mg of efinaconazole per gram of solution) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Topical (for use on toenails and immediately adjacent skin).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Bausch Health US LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Onychomycosis of the toenail(s):** Indicated for the topical treatment of fungal infections of the toenails caused by *Trichophyton rubrum* and *Trichophyton mentagrophytes* [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** Apply to the affected toenails once daily for a duration of 48 weeks [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Ensure toenails are clean and dry before application.
    - Use the integrated flow-through brush applicator to cover the entire toenail, toenail folds, toenail bed, hyponychium, and the undersurface of the toenail plate.
    - For the hallux (big toenail), apply the solution twice [US].
    - Patients should wait at least 10 minutes after washing or bathing before applying the medication.
    - The treated area should be allowed to dry completely before being covered by socks, bedding, or other clothing [US].
    - Wash hands with soap and water following application.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Special Instructions:** The impact of nail polish or cosmetic nail products on efficacy has not been evaluated [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None identified in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **General Precautions:** For external use only. Not for ophthalmic, oral, or intravaginal administration [US].
- **Flammability:** The solution is flammable. Patients should avoid heat, open flames, and smoking during and immediately after application [US].
- **Local Irritation:** If persistent irritation (e.g., redness, swelling, or itching) occurs at the application site, the patient should be advised to contact a healthcare professional [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Not specified in available sources.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions reported in clinical trials (at an incidence of >1%) and through postmarketing experience include:

| Adverse Event | Frequency [US] |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Ingrown toenail** | 2.3% |
| **Application site dermatitis** | 2.2% |
| **Application site vesicles** | 1.6% |
| **Application site pain** | 1.1% |

**Postmarketing Experience:**
The following reactions have been identified during post-approval use:
- **General/Site Disorders:** Application site erythema and exfoliation.
- **Skin and Subcutaneous Tissue:** Onychomadesis (nail shedding) and nail discoloration.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** *In vitro* studies indicate that at therapeutic concentrations, efinaconazole does not inhibit or induce major Cytochrome P450 (CYP450) enzymes (including CYP1A2, 2A6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 2E1, and 3A4) [US].
- **Clinically Significant Interactions:** No significant systemic drug interactions are expected due to low systemic absorption following topical application.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:**
    - **US Category:** Not assigned (PLLR format). 
    - **Risk Summary:** No human data are available to inform drug-associated risks. Animal studies using subcutaneous administration showed no malformations at doses significantly higher than the Maximum Recommended Human Dose (MRHD). Embryolethality was only observed in rats at 559 times the MRHD in the presence of maternal toxicity [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if efinaconazole is excreted in human milk. It has been detected in the milk of nursing rats. Exercise caution and consider the clinical need for the mother alongside potential risks to the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No effects on fertility were observed in rats at subcutaneous doses up to 25 mg/kg/day (279 times the MRHD). A delay in the estrous cycle was noted in females at high doses [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have been established in patients aged 6 years and older. Use in patients under 6 years of age has not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials (11.3% of subjects ≥65 years) showed no overall differences in safety or effectiveness compared to younger subjects, though increased sensitivity in some older individuals cannot be ruled out [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources; however, systemic exposure is minimal.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Efinaconazole is an azole antifungal. It acts by inhibiting fungal lanosterol 14α-demethylase, an enzyme essential for the biosynthesis of ergosterol, a critical component of fungal cell membranes [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The clinical pharmacodynamics are unknown [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Systemic absorption is low. In adults with severe onychomycosis, the mean maximum plasma concentration ($C_{max}$) was 0.67 ± 0.37 ng/mL after 28 days of daily application [US].
    - **Distribution/Elimination:** The plasma half-life is approximately 29.9 hours following repeated daily applications to all 10 toenails [US].
- **Microbiology:** Exhibits *in vitro* minimum inhibitory concentrations (MICs) of ≤0.06 mcg/mL against most isolates of *T. rubrum* and *T. mentagrophytes* [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Design:** Two 52-week, multicenter, randomized, double-blind trials in subjects (18–70 years) with 20% to 50% clinical involvement of the target toenail [US].
- **Primary Outcomes:** Efficacy was assessed at Week 52 (4 weeks post-treatment). 
    - **Complete Cure** (0% clinical involvement plus negative KOH and fungal culture):
        - Trial 1: 17.8% (Jublia) vs. 3.3% (Vehicle) [US].
        - Trial 2: 15.2% (Jublia) vs. 5.5% (Vehicle) [US].
    - **Mycologic Cure** (Negative KOH and negative culture):
        - Trial 1: 55.2% (Jublia) vs. 16.8% (Vehicle) [US].
        - Trial 2: 53.4% (Jublia) vs. 16.9% (Vehicle) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** 
    - Protect from freezing.
    - Store the bottle tightly closed in an upright position.
    - **Warning:** Solution is flammable; keep away from heat or flame [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** **Rx-only** (Prescription only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
