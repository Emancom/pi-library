# Editorial Product Information: Ethynodiol Diacetate and Ethinyl Estradiol

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States** (Food and Drug Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document serves as a harmonized editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. It integrates clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single, cohesive reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for final prescribing decisions.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Ethynodiol diacetate (progestin) and Ethinyl estradiol (estrogen).
- **Chemical Class:** Combination Oral Contraceptive (COC).
- **Brand Names:** Distributed as generic formulations by various sponsors, including Mylan Pharmaceuticals Inc. [US].
- **ATC Code:** G03AA01 (Ethinylestradiol and ethynodiol).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Tablets containing 1 mg ethynodiol diacetate and 35 mcg ethinyl estradiol.
    - Supplied in a 28-day regimen package: 21 light orange active tablets and 7 green inert placebo tablets [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Mylan Pharmaceuticals Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Prevention of Pregnancy:** Indicated for use by females of reproductive potential to prevent pregnancy [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** One tablet must be taken daily at the same time every day for 28 consecutive days. Maximum efficacy is achieved only when taken at 24-hour intervals.
- **Cycle Initiation:**
    - **Day 1 Start:** The first active tablet is taken on the first day of menstrual flow. No backup contraception is required.
    - **Sunday Start:** The first active tablet is taken on the first Sunday after the onset of menstruation (if menstruation begins on Sunday, start that day). A non-hormonal backup method (e.g., condoms) must be used for the first 7 days of the initial cycle [US].
- **Missed Dose Instructions [US]:**
    - **One active tablet missed:** Take as soon as remembered; take the next tablet at the regular time (may result in 2 tablets in one day).
    - **Two active tablets missed (Week 1 or 2):** Take 2 tablets daily for the next 2 days, then resume the regular schedule. Use backup contraception for 7 days.
    - **Two active tablets missed (Week 3) or three+ tablets missed:** 
        - *Day 1 Starters:* Discard the pack and start a new pack the same day.
        - *Sunday Starters:* Take one tablet daily until Sunday, then discard the pack and start a new pack.
        - *Both:* Use backup contraception for 7 days.

## 4. Contraindications
Ethynodiol diacetate and ethinyl estradiol are contraindicated in patients with:
- Current or history of thrombophlebitis or thromboembolic disorders [US].
- Cerebral vascular or coronary artery disease [US].
- Current or suspected carcinoma of the breast or other estrogen-dependent neoplasia [US].
- Undiagnosed abnormal genital bleeding [US].
- Cholestatic jaundice of pregnancy or jaundice with prior pill use [US].
- Hepatic adenomas or carcinomas (benign or malignant) [US].
- Known or suspected pregnancy [US].
- **Hepatitis C Treatment:** Concomitant use with drug combinations containing ombitasvir/paritaprevir/ritonavir, with or without dasabuvir, due to the risk of ALT elevations [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning (Cigarette Smoking and Cardiovascular Events):** Cigarette smoking increases the risk of serious cardiovascular side effects from COC use. This risk increases with age (particularly over 35 years) and with heavy smoking (15+ cigarettes/day). COC use is strongly discouraged in women who smoke [US].
- **Thromboembolism:** Increased risk of myocardial infarction, stroke, and venous thromboembolism (VTE). COCs should be discontinued at least 4 weeks prior to and 2 weeks after elective surgery associated with increased risk of thromboembolism [US].
- **Liver Disease:** Discontinue if jaundice develops. Hepatic neoplasia (rarely) may cause life-threatening intra-abdominal hemorrhage [US].
- **Gallbladder Disease:** Early studies suggest an increased risk of gallbladder surgery in COC users; however, risk may be lower with modern low-dose formulations [US].
- **Metabolic Effects:** COCs may decrease glucose tolerance and increase serum triglycerides. Monitor prediabetic and diabetic women closely [US].
- **Hypertension:** Blood pressure may increase; if significant elevation occurs, discontinue use [US].
- **Headache:** Onset or exacerbation of migraine or new-pattern recurrent/severe headaches requires evaluation and potential discontinuation [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Thrombophlebitis, arterial thromboembolism, pulmonary embolism, myocardial infarction, cerebral hemorrhage/thrombosis, hypertension, gallbladder disease, and hepatic tumors [US].
- **Commonly Reported Drug-Related Effects:**
    - Nausea and vomiting.
    - Gastrointestinal symptoms (cramping, bloating).
    - Breakthrough bleeding and spotting.
    - Changes in menstrual flow or amenorrhea.
    - Edema and chloasma/melasma (may persist).
    - Breast changes (tenderness, enlargement, secretion).
    - Weight changes [US].
- **Other Reported Effects:** Migraine, allergic rash, mental depression, vaginal candidiasis, and intolerance to contact lenses [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Reduced COC Efficacy:** Drugs that induce hepatic enzymes (e.g., CYP3A4) may decrease plasma concentrations of COCs. Examples include rifampin, barbiturates, phenytoin, carbamazepine, and potentially griseofulvin and certain antibiotics (ampicillin, tetracyclines) [US].
- **Troglitazone:** May reduce hormone concentrations by approximately 30% [US].
- **HCV Combinations:** Contraindicated with ombitasvir/paritaprevir/ritonavir due to potential for severe ALT elevations [US].
- **Laboratory Tests:** COCs may affect levels of prothrombin and factors VII-X, thyroid-binding globulin (increasing total T4), and glucose tolerance [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category X [US].** COCs are contraindicated during pregnancy. Epidemiological studies show no increased risk of birth defects when taken inadvertently during early pregnancy [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Small amounts of contraceptive steroids are excreted in breast milk. Reported effects in infants include jaundice and breast enlargement. COCs may also decrease the quantity and quality of milk. If possible, defer use until the child is completely weaned [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy are established in women of reproductive age. Use is expected to be the same for postpubertal adolescents under 16 years. Use is not indicated before menarche [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not indicated for postmenopausal women [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with active liver disease or history of cholestatic jaundice [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution in patients with conditions aggravated by fluid retention [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** COCs prevent pregnancy primarily by suppressing gonadotropins, leading to the inhibition of ovulation. Secondary mechanisms include thickening cervical mucus (to inhibit sperm entry) and altering the endometrium (to reduce the likelihood of implantation) [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Estrogens increase HDL and may cause hyperinsulinism; progestins may decrease HDL and cause glucose intolerance [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy Data:** Clinical trials and epidemiological studies indicate that COCs are highly effective. Perfect use failure rate is estimated at 0.1%, while typical use failure rate is approximately 5% during the first year of use [US].
- **Cardiovascular Studies:** Data indicate an increased relative risk of MI (2 to 6 times higher than non-users) and stroke, particularly in smokers and hypertensive patients [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Keep out of reach of children.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources (refer to local state/national schedules).
