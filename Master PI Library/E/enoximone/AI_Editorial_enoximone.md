# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for educational and professional reference. It does not replace the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing or dispensing in any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN).
- **Chemical Class:** A naturally occurring carrier molecule (amino acid derivative) required for mammalian energy metabolism.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources (Note: Traditionally A16AA01).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
  - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. 
  - Administer as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
  - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours.
  - Daily maintenance doses should be in the range of 50 mg/kg or as clinically required.
  - The highest dose administered to date is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
  - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
  - Administration: Slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
  - Initiation of therapy may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations <40 µmol/L to 50 µmol/L [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect parenterals visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

### Titration and Monitoring
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations before starting parenteral therapy. Monitor weekly and monthly thereafter, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target free carnitine level: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Dose Adjustments (ESRD):** Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and initiate appropriate medical treatment [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with known seizure disorders, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of metabolic disorders or ESRD.

### Common to Very Common Adverse Effects (≥5% in ESRD Trials)
| System Organ Class | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Injection site reaction (33-59%), Pain (30-49%), Flu syndrome (25-40%), Chest pain (12-15%), Fever (5-12%), Headache (3-37%), Abdominal pain (5-21%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (18-21%), Hypotension (3-19%), Tachycardia (5-9%) |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea (9-35%), Vomiting (9-21%), Nausea (5-12%), Anorexia (3-6%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (15-27%), Cough increase (10-18%), Dyspnea (3-11%), Rhinitis (6-11%) |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness (10-18%), Paresthesia (3-12%), Insomnia (3-6%) |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia (6-15%), Hypervolemia (3-12%) |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis and laryngeal edema [US].
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is used concurrently with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating treatment or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified by letter, but source states:] Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. There are no adequate/controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. While specific studies in nursing mothers have not been conducted, studies in dairy cows indicate increased carnitine concentrations in milk after exogenous administration. Risks to the child from excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefits to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Refer to Section 3 for dosing. In premature infants and newborns, secondary deficiency is defined as plasma concentrations below age-related normal levels [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosage adjustments for ESRD patients are required post-dialysis (see Section 3). Oral levocarnitine should be used with caution due to metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This delivers substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
  - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and [³H]-γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
  - **Elimination:** Following a single IV dose (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Study (ESRD):** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis showed that trough levocarnitine levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L by week 8 [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** In ESRD patients, doses of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg were shown to increase carnitine concentrations in a dose-dependent manner over 12 and 24 weeks. However, the effect of this supplementation on clinical outcomes in this population has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing [US].
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC plastic bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].
