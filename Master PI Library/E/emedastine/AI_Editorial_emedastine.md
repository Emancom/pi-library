# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   **Jurisdictions contributing to this editorial:** US (FDA).
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for use by healthcare professionals. 
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis of official product information and is not a substitute for the specific, legally approved Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any particular jurisdiction. The clinical data contained herein is derived from the provided US regulatory source.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP).
*   **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule for fatty acid transport; a naturally occurring quaternary ammonium compound.
*   **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
*   **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    *   Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with genetic metabolic defects resulting in secondary deficiency [US].
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) on Hemodialysis:** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
*   **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg, administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion. 
    *   **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
    *   **Maintenance:** Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours). 
    *   **Maximum Dose:** Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].
*   **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** 
    *   **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    *   **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line immediately following each dialysis session.
    *   **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
*   Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
*   **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s in concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported. These may occur within minutes of intravenous administration, particularly in ESRD patients. Treatment should be discontinued immediately upon signs of severe hypersensitivity [US].
*   **Seizures:** Levocarnitine may increase the frequency or severity of seizures in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a history of epilepsy [US].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
*   **Oral Levocarnitine in Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been established in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are usually renally excreted [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
*   **Plasma Carnitine Levels:** Monitoring of plasma free carnitine levels is recommended (target range: 35 to 60 µmol/L).
*   **Clinical Monitoring:** Monitoring should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and overall clinical status [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common and Serious Adverse Effects
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea and vomiting (transient), abdominal pain, diarrhea, and gastritis.
*   **Neurological:** Seizures [Serious], dizziness, headache, and paresthesia.
*   **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, and arrhythmias.
*   **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (noted in 9% of ESRD patients), hyperkalemia, and hypervolemia.
*   **Body as a Whole:** Body odor, injection site reactions (reported in up to 38% of patients in some trials), chest pain, and fever [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concurrently with warfarin. 
*   **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [US: Category C]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. However, there are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use only if clearly needed [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations following exogenous administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake to the infant must be weighed against the benefits to the mother [US].
*   **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests were negative; however, long-term animal carcinogenicity studies have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Refer to metabolic disorder dosing (50 mg/kg). In premature infants and newborns, carnitine deficiency is defined as plasma concentrations below age-related normal levels [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources [US].
*   **Renal Impairment:** The injection is indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. High-dose **oral** formulations are cautioned against in this population due to metabolite accumulation [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for beta-oxidation and energy production.
*   **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with organic acidemias, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly cleared.
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    *   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    *   **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ESRD Dialysis Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV levocarnitine three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis trough concentrations from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. 
*   **Outcome Note:** While IV levocarnitine effectively increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the impact on clinical outcomes (e.g., malaise, muscle weakness) has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
*   **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
*   **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
