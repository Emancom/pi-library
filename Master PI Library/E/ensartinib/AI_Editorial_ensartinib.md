# Editorial Product Information: Ensartinib

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial product information is based on official documents from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a clinically relevant format.
- **Note:** This synthesis is an editorial resource and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing within any specific jurisdiction. At the time of this publication, only the United States PI was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ensartinib (as ensartinib hydrochloride).
- **Chemical Class:** Kinase inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** ENSACOVE [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
  - **Capsules:** 25 mg (white opaque) and 100 mg (blue opaque cap, yellow opaque body) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Xcovery Holdings, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Anaplastic Lymphoma Kinase (ALK)-positive Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC):** Indicated for the treatment of adult patients with locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC, as detected by an approved test, who have not previously received an ALK-inhibitor (TKI-naive) [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 225 mg taken orally once daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
  - May be taken with or without food [US].
  - Capsules must be swallowed whole; do not crush, chew, or dissolve [US].
  - Dose should be taken at approximately the same time each day [US].
- **Duration of Therapy:** Continue treatment until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity [US].
- **Pre-treatment Requirements:** Evaluate liver function tests (LFTs) and fasting blood glucose prior to initiation [US].
- **Dose Modifications for Adverse Reactions:**
  - **First reduction:** 200 mg once daily [US].
  - **Second reduction:** 150 mg once daily [US].
  - **Discontinuation:** If unable to tolerate 150 mg daily, the drug should be permanently discontinued [US].
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as remembered. If the next dose is due within 12 hours, the missed dose should be skipped. Do not take two doses at the same time [US].
- **Vomiting:** If vomiting occurs after administration, do not take an additional dose; take the next scheduled dose at the regular time [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known severe hypersensitivity to ensartinib or any of its excipients [US].
- **Excipient Specificity:** Contraindicated in patients with a known hypersensitivity to **FD&C Yellow No. 5 (tartrazine)**, which is contained in the 100 mg capsules [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD)/Pneumonitis:** Severe or life-threatening ILD/pneumonitis can occur. Monitor for new/worsening respiratory symptoms (e.g., dyspnoea, cough, fever). Permanently discontinue ensartinib if ILD/pneumonitis is confirmed [US].
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Drug-induced liver injury has been reported. Monitor LFTs (ALT, AST, bilirubin) at baseline, every 2 weeks for the first month, and monthly thereafter. Dose modification or permanent discontinuation may be required based on severity [US].
- **Dermatologic Adverse Reactions:** Includes rash, pruritus, photosensitivity, and Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS). Patients should be advised to limit sun exposure and use protective measures. Severe reactions require dose interruption or discontinuation [US].
- **Bradycardia:** Ensartinib can cause symptomatic bradycardia. Monitor heart rate and blood pressure regularly. Avoid concomitant use of other drugs known to cause bradycardia [US].
- **Hyperglycaemia:** Fasting serum glucose should be assessed at baseline and monitored periodically. Anti-hyperglycaemic therapy may need to be initiated or adjusted [US].
- **Visual Disturbances:** Reported symptoms include blurred vision, diplopia, and vitreous floaters. Any new or worsening visual symptoms require an ophthalmologic evaluation [US].
- **Creatine Phosphokinase (CPK) Elevation:** Monitor CPK levels periodically. Advise patients to report unexplained muscle pain or weakness [US].
- **Hyperuricemia:** Serum uric acid should be monitored at baseline and periodically. Initiate urate-lowering medication as clinically indicated [US].
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Based on animal data and mechanism of action, ensartinib can cause fetal harm [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are presented based on the pooled safety population (n=458) [US].

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Rash (72%), musculoskeletal pain (36%), pruritus (32%), constipation (31%), cough (31%), nausea (28%), oedema (27%), pyrexia (22%), fatigue (21%), vomiting (16%), decreased appetite (15%), dizziness (12%), alopecia (11%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Visual disturbances (8%), hyperuricemia (6%), bradycardia (6%), ILD/pneumonitis (5%) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Occurred in 23% of patients. The most frequent (≥1%) were pneumonia, haemorrhage, rash, and cellulitis. One fatal case of bronchopneumonia was reported [US].
- **Laboratory Abnormalities (Grade 3-4, ≥2%):** Increased uric acid (39%), decreased lymphocytes (7%), increased ALT (5%), decreased phosphate (7%), increased gamma-glutamyl transferase, increased magnesium, increased amylase, and decreased sodium [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Metabolism:** Ensartinib is a primary substrate of CYP3A.
- **Strong or Moderate CYP3A Inhibitors:** Avoid concomitant use, as these may increase ensartinib exposure and the risk of toxicity [US].
- **Strong or Moderate CYP3A Inducers:** Avoid concomitant use, as these may significantly decrease ensartinib exposure and efficacy [US].
- **P-gp Inhibitors:** Avoid concomitant use, as ensartinib is a P-gp substrate [US].
- **Transporters:** Ensartinib does not inhibit BCRP, P-gp, OATP1B1, OATP1B3, OAT1, OAT3, OCT1, or OCT2 in vitro [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **Category:** Not assigned a letter category in US PI; however, it is stated to cause fetal harm [US].
  - **Risk Summary:** No human data. Animal studies in rats showed embryo-fetal mortality and structural malformations (aortic dislocation, septal defects) at exposures equivalent to human therapeutic doses [US].
  - **Contraception:** Females of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for 1 week after the final dose. Males with female partners of reproductive potential should also use effective contraception for 1 week after the final dose [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on the presence of ensartinib in human milk. Due to potential serious adverse reactions in breastfed children, advise patients not to breastfeed during treatment and for 1 week after the last dose [US].
- **Fertility:** Dedicated fertility studies have not been conducted. No adverse effects on reproductive organs were noted in 3-month toxicology studies in rats and dogs [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in paediatric patients have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use (≥65 years):** No overall differences in effectiveness were observed compared to younger patients. However, exploratory analyses suggest a higher incidence of serious adverse events (43% vs 27%) and dose modifications (34% vs 16%) in patients aged 65 and older [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
  - **Mild to Moderate (eGFR 30–89 mL/min):** No dose adjustment required [US].
  - **Severe or ESRD (eGFR <30 mL/min):** Pharmacokinetics have not been studied [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
  - **Mild:** No dose adjustment required [US].
  - **Moderate (Bilirubin >1.5 to 3x ULN):** Monitor for increased adverse reactions and adjust dose as clinically indicated [US].
  - **Severe (Bilirubin >3x ULN):** Avoid use; not studied in this population [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Ensartinib is a tyrosine kinase inhibitor targeting ALK, ROS1, and MET. It inhibits ALK-mediated signalling pathways (AKT, ERK, S6), leading to inhibited proliferation in cell lines harboring ALK fusions or mutations [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Median Tmax is 3 hours. Steady state is reached within 15 days [US].
  - **Food Effect:** No clinically significant difference when taken with a high-fat meal [US].
  - **Distribution:** Highly protein bound (91.6%). Apparent volume of distribution (Vz/F) is 1,720 L [US].
  - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by CYP3A [US].
  - **Elimination:** Mean steady-state half-life is 30 hours. Excretion is primarily faecal (91%), with 10% in urine [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **eXALT3 Study:** An open-label, randomised (1:1), multicenter trial comparing ensartinib (225 mg QD) to crizotinib (250 mg BID) in TKI-naive ALK-positive NSCLC patients (n=290) [US].
- **Efficacy Outcomes:** 
  - **Progression-Free Survival (PFS):** Median PFS was 25.8 months for ensartinib vs 12.7 months for crizotinib (HR 0.51; 95% CI: 0.40, 0.79; p=0.0008) [US].
  - **Overall Response Rate (ORR):** 74% for ensartinib vs 67% for crizotinib [US].
  - **Intracranial Response:** Among patients with measurable CNS disease at baseline, the intracranial ORR was 59% for ensartinib compared to 21% for crizotinib [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at controlled room temperature, 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Excursions are permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Dispense in the original bottle. The bottle contains a desiccant to protect against moisture; do not remove the desiccant [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription-only (Rx-only) [US].
