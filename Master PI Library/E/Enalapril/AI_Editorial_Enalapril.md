# Editorial Product Information: Enalapril

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following jurisdictional Product Information (PI) documents:
    - **Australian (TGA):** Acetec (enalapril maleate) tablets.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official PI. Healthcare professionals must refer to the current approved PI in their specific jurisdiction before prescribing or dispensing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Enalapril maleate
- **Chemical Class:** Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme (ACE) inhibitor; prodrug of enalaprilat.
- **Brand Names:** Acetec [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** C09AA02.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - Tablets: 2.5 mg, 5 mg, 10 mg, and 20 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Alphapharm Pty Limited trading as Viatris [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypertension:** All grades of essential hypertension and renovascular hypertension [Australian].
- **Congestive Heart Failure:** Treatment of all degrees of symptomatic heart failure (usually in combination with diuretics and digitalis) [Australian].
- **Left Ventricular Dysfunction (LVD):** All degrees of LVD where the left ventricular ejection fraction is <35%, regardless of the presence of overt heart failure symptoms [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Essential Hypertension:**
    - Initial dose (patients not on diuretics): 5 mg once daily.
    - Usual maintenance: 10 to 40 mg daily, administered as a single dose or in two divided doses.
- **Renovascular Hypertension:**
    - Initial dose: 5 mg or less. Most patients respond to 20 mg once daily.
- **Congestive Heart Failure:**
    - Initial dose: 2.5 mg or less, administered under close medical supervision to monitor for hypotension.
    - Usual maintenance: 10 to 20 mg daily in single or divided doses.

### Special Population Dosing
- **Elderly (over 65 years):** Starting dose of 2.5 mg [Australian].
- **Left Ventricular Dysfunction (Asymptomatic):** Initial dose of 2.5 mg twice daily, titrated to a maintenance dose of 20 mg daily in divided doses.

### Administration Instructions
- If a patient is on diuretic therapy, the diuretic should be discontinued 2–3 days before starting enalapril if possible.
- If diuretics cannot be discontinued, an initial enalapril dose of 2.5 mg must be used under medical supervision for at least one hour [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to enalapril maleate or any excipients.
- History of angioedema (hereditary, idiopathic, or related to previous ACE inhibitor therapy).
- **Pregnancy:** Enalapril is contraindicated during pregnancy [Australian].
- **Dual Blockade:** Concomitant use with aliskiren-containing products in patients with diabetes [Australian].
- **Neprilysin Inhibitors:** Concomitant use with sacubitril/valsartan or other neprilysin inhibitors. Enalapril must not be started within 36 hours of the last dose of sacubitril/valsartan [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Angioedema:** May occur at any time during treatment. Risk is higher in Black patients and those taking concomitant mTOR inhibitors (e.g., sirolimus), vildagliptin, or neprilysin inhibitors [Australian].
- **Hypotension:** Excessive hypotension may occur in volume-depleted patients (diuretics, salt restriction, dialysis, diarrhea/vomiting) or those with heart failure.
- **Neutropenia/Agranulocytosis:** Use with caution in patients with collagen vascular disease or renal impairment; periodic hematologic monitoring is recommended.
- **Hyperkalaemia:** Risk factors include renal insufficiency, diabetes, and use of potassium-sparing diuretics or potassium supplements. Serum potassium >5.7 mmol/L was observed in ~1% of patients [Australian].
- **Cough:** A persistent, non-productive cough is common, particularly in women (representing ~2/3 of reported cases) [Australian].
- **Aortic Stenosis/Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy:** Use with caution in patients with left ventricular outflow tract obstruction.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Enalapril is generally well-tolerated. Discontinuation due to adverse effects occurred in 6% of patients in clinical trials [Australian].

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Angioedema (0.2%), myocardial infarction or cerebrovascular accident (secondary to hypotension), renal failure, hepatic failure/hepatitis, and agranulocytosis.
- **Symptom Complex:** Rare reports of a syndrome involving fever, serositis, vasculitis, myalgia, arthralgia, positive ANA, and elevated ESR [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Diuretics:** Potentiates antihypertensive effect; risk of excessive hypotension.
- **Agents increasing serum potassium:** Potassium-sparing diuretics (e.g., spironolactone, eplerenone), potassium supplements, or trimethoprim may cause hyperkalaemia.
- **Lithium:** Reduced lithium clearance; risk of lithium toxicity.
- **NSAIDs (including COX-2 inhibitors):** May attenuate antihypertensive effect and increase risk of renal impairment, especially in the elderly or volume-depleted.
- **Dual Blockade (RAAS):** Use with ARBs or aliskiren increases risk of hypotension, syncope, and hyperkalaemia.
- **Antidiabetics:** Increased risk of hypoglycaemia during the first weeks of treatment.
- **Gold:** Rare nitritoid reactions (flushing, nausea, vomiting) with injectable sodium aurothiomalate.
- **mTOR inhibitors/Vildagliptin:** Increased risk of angioedema [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. ACE inhibitors can cause fetal and neonatal morbidity (hypotension, renal failure, skull hypoplasia) and death when used in the 2nd and 3rd trimesters. If pregnancy is detected, enalapril must be discontinued immediately.
- **Breastfeeding:** Enalapril is found in rat milk; its presence in human milk is unknown. Use is not recommended [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been studied in children [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** More sensitive to effects; start at 2.5 mg.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosage must be adjusted based on Creatinine Clearance (CrCl):
    - CrCl >30 mL/min: Initial dose 5 mg/day.
    - CrCl ≤30 mL/min: Initial dose 2.5 mg/day.
    - Dialysis patients: 2.5 mg on dialysis days [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits ACE, preventing the conversion of Angiotensin I to Angiotensin II. This results in decreased vasopressor activity and decreased aldosterone secretion.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** ~60% absorbed; not affected by food.
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly hydrolyzed in the liver to the active metabolite, enalaprilat.
    - **Peak Plasma Levels:** ~1 hour for enalapril; 3–4 hours for enalaprilat.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal. Effective half-life for accumulation of enalaprilat is 11 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **SOLVD-Treatment:** 2,569 patients with symptomatic heart failure (LVEF ≤35%). Enalapril reduced hospitalisations and improved survival.
- **SOLVD-Prevention:** 4,228 patients with asymptomatic LVD (LVEF ≤35%). Enalapril retarded the progression to overt heart failure and reduced heart failure-related hospitalisations [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Handling:** Keep in original container (Al/Al blisters).

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Poison Schedule S4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
