# Editorial Product Information: Elagolix

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of elagolix for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical and regulatory information into a single reference point.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes for healthcare professionals. It does not replace the official Product Information (PI) or prescribing data approved in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians must consult local regulatory guidelines before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Elagolix (as elagolix sodium).
- **Chemical Class:** Nonpeptide gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) receptor antagonist.
- **Brand Name(s):** **ORILISSA** [US].
- **ATC Code:** H01CC03.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Oral Tablets:** 150 mg (light pink, oblong, debossed with "EL 150") and 200 mg (light orange, oblong, debossed with "EL 200") [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AbbVie Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Endometriosis-Associated Pain:** Management of moderate to severe pain associated with endometriosis [US].
- **Limitations of Use:** Duration of use should be limited based on the dose administered and the presence of coexisting conditions (e.g., hepatic impairment) [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Initiation:** Exclude pregnancy prior to starting treatment or initiate within 7 days of the onset of menses [US].
- **Dosing Regimens:**
    - **Standard Dose:** 150 mg once daily for up to 24 months [US].
    - **Severe Symptoms (e.g., Dyspareunia):** 200 mg twice daily for up to 6 months [US].
- **Administration:** Take at approximately the same time each day, with or without food [US].

### Hepatic Impairment Adjustments
- **Mild (Child-Pugh A):** No dosage adjustment required [US].
- **Moderate (Child-Pugh B):** 150 mg once daily limited to 6 months of use. The 200 mg twice daily regimen is not recommended [US].
- **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Contraindicated [US].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Take the missed dose on the same day as soon as remembered; otherwise, skip the dose and resume the regular schedule.
- **150 mg QD:** Maximum of 1 tablet per day.
- **200 mg BID:** Maximum of 2 tablets per day [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy:** Exposure in early pregnancy may increase the risk of early pregnancy loss [US].
- **Known Osteoporosis:** Due to the risk of further bone mineral density (BMD) loss [US].
- **Severe Hepatic Impairment:** (Child-Pugh C) [US].
- **Concomitant use of Strong OATP1B1 Inhibitors:** Specifically those known or expected to significantly increase elagolix plasma concentrations (e.g., cyclosporine, gemfibrozil) [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to elagolix or any inactive components (reactions have included anaphylaxis and angioedema) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Bone Loss:** Elagolix causes dose- and duration-dependent decreases in BMD. This loss may not be completely reversible. Assessment of BMD should be considered in patients with risk factors for bone loss or a history of low-trauma fractures [US].
- **Suicidal Ideation and Mood Disorders:** Clinical trials reported cases of suicidal ideation and behavior, including one completed suicide. Patients with a history of depression or suicidality are at higher risk. Professionals should monitor for new-onset or worsening depression, anxiety, or mood changes [US].
- **Hepatic Transaminase Elevations:** Dose-dependent asymptomatic elevations in ALT (>3x ULN) occurred. Use the lowest effective dose and monitor for signs of liver injury [US].
- **Reduced Ability to Recognize Pregnancy:** Elagolix may alter menstrual bleeding (amenorrhea or irregular bleeding), potentially delaying the recognition of pregnancy. Perform pregnancy testing if suspected and discontinue if confirmed [US].
- **Interactions with Hormonal Contraceptives:** Use effective non-hormonal contraception during treatment and for 28 days following discontinuation. Estrogen-containing contraceptives may reduce elagolix efficacy and increase estrogen-related risks [US].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Bone Mineral Density Loss:** Significant decreases (e.g., >8% decrease in BMD in 21% of users on 200 mg BID after 12 months) [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis and angioedema [US].
- **Suicidality:** Suicidal ideation and behavior [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **OATP1B1 Substrate:** Inhibitors of OATP1B1 (e.g., cyclosporine) can significantly increase elagolix exposure and are contraindicated [US].
- **CYP3A4 Induction:** Elagolix is a weak-to-moderate inducer of CYP3A. Co-administration may decrease concentrations of CYP3A substrates (e.g., midazolam, rosuvastatin) [US].
- **CYP2C19 Inhibition:** Elagolix is a weak inhibitor of CYP2C19. It may increase concentrations of CYP2C19 substrates (e.g., omeprazole) [US].
- **P-gp Inhibition:** Elagolix is an inhibitor of P-glycoprotein. Monitoring of digoxin is recommended when initiating or discontinuing elagolix [US].

### Clinically Significant Interactions
- **Rifampin:** Strong CYP3A/OATP1B1 inducer. Concomitant use with 200 mg BID is not recommended; limit use with 150 mg QD to 6 months [US].
- **Combined Oral Contraceptives (COCs):** Elagolix 200 mg BID increases ethinyl estradiol levels (2.2-fold), increasing thromboembolic risks [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Classification:** Contraindicated.
    - **Australian Category:** Not specified in available sources.
    - **Clinical Data:** Animal studies showed post-implantation loss and spontaneous abortion at 7–20 times human exposure. In humans, exposure early in pregnancy may increase the risk of pregnancy loss [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** No data are available regarding the presence of elagolix in human milk or its effects on the breastfed child. The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need for the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** Rat studies showed no effect on fertility, though relevance to humans is low due to poor GnRH receptor binding in rats [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Elagolix is not indicated in postmenopausal women [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for patients with any degree of renal impairment or end-stage renal disease (including those on dialysis) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - Mild: No adjustment. 
    - Moderate: 150 mg QD maximum for 6 months. 
    - Severe: Contraindicated [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Competitive antagonist of the GnRH receptor in the pituitary gland. It suppresses luteinizing hormone (LH) and follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), leading to dose-dependent decreases in estradiol and progesterone [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ ~1 hour. High-fat meals decrease AUC by 24% and $C_{max}$ by 36% [US].
    - **Distribution:** 80% bound to human plasma proteins [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic via CYP3A; minor pathways include CYP2D6, CYP2C8, and UGTs [US].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life ($t_{1/2}$) of 4–6 hours. Predominantly eliminated in feces (90%) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy (EM-1 and EM-2):** Two 6-month, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in 1,686 premenopausal women. 
- **Primary Outcomes:** Statistically significant reductions in dysmenorrhea and non-menstrual pelvic pain scores at Month 3 compared to placebo. 
- **Extension Studies:** Continued bone loss was observed in extension trials (EM-3 and EM-4) for up to 12 months [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 2°C to 30°C (36°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Handling:** Do not flush unused medication down the toilet; use take-back programs or dispose of in household trash as per local guidance [US].
