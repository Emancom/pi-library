# Editorial Product Information: estetrol + drospirenone

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **estetrol + drospirenone**
- Chemical class:
  - Estetrol (E4): a synthetic analogue of a native human estrogen.
  - Drospirenone: a synthetic progestational compound with antimineralocorticoid and antiandrogenic properties.
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration: Oral.

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources.
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources.
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Prevention of pregnancy in women of reproductive potential.
  - If jurisdictional differences in indications exist in other sources, they are not specified in the available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Each active tablet contains 14.2 mg estetrol (equivalent to 15 mg estetrol monohydrate) and 3 mg drospirenone.
- Regimen: One tablet daily for 28 consecutive days. The established cycle consists of 24 consecutive days of active tablets followed by 4 consecutive days of inactive tablets.

### Starting or switching
- Initiation in women not currently using hormonal contraception:
  - Preferably begin on day 1 of the natural menstrual cycle (the first day of menstrual bleeding).
  - If initiation is not on day 1, advise use of a non‑hormonal backup method (e.g., condom) until active tablets have been taken for 7 consecutive days.
  - Take the first active tablet corresponding to the day of week and continue in sequence daily.
- Switching from other contraceptives:
  - From combined oral contraceptives (COC): start the day after the last active tablet of the previous COC.
  - From transdermal patch: start on the day the next application would have been due.
  - From vaginal ring: start on the day of ring removal.
  - From injection: start on the day the next injection would have been scheduled.
  - From hormone‑medicated intrauterine system or implant: start on removal day.
  - From progestin‑only tablet: start the day after the last tablet was taken.

### After abortion, miscarriage, or childbirth
- After first‑trimester abortion or miscarriage:
  - May be started immediately. If started within 5 days after termination, additional contraception is not required. If started later than 5 days, use a non‑hormonal backup until 7 consecutive days of active tablets have been taken.
- After second‑trimester abortion or miscarriage:
  - Do not start until 4 weeks after the event due to increased thromboembolic risk. Rule out pregnancy before starting; use non‑hormonal backup until 7 consecutive days of active tablets have been taken.
- After childbirth:
  - Do not start until 4 weeks postpartum because of increased thromboembolic risk. Rule out pregnancy before starting; use non‑hormonal backup until 7 consecutive days of active tablets have been taken.
  - If breastfeeding, recommend use of non‑hormonal contraception until breastfeeding has ceased.

### Method of administration
- Swallow one tablet once daily at approximately the same time each day.

### Missed dose instructions
- If one active (pink) tablet missed:
  - Take it as soon as possible and continue taking subsequent tablets at the usual time (may result in taking two active tablets in one day).
- If two or more active tablets missed in Week 1 or Week 2:
  - Take one missed tablet as soon as possible and take the tablet scheduled for that day (two in one day), discard other missed tablets. Continue daily. Use non‑hormonal backup until 7 consecutive days of active tablets have been taken.
- If two or more active tablets missed in Week 3:
  - Take one missed tablet as soon as possible and take the tablet scheduled for that day (two in one day). Discard the remaining missed active tablets and discard the inactive tablets in the pack; start a new pack the next day. Use non‑hormonal backup until 7 consecutive days of active tablets have been taken.
- If one or more inactive (white) tablets missed:
  - Skip missed inactive tablets and continue daily dosing; no additional contraceptive measures needed.
- Gastrointestinal disturbances:
  - If vomiting or severe diarrhoea occurs within 3–4 hours after taking an active tablet, take a replacement tablet as soon as possible (within 12 hours of usual dosing time if possible). If more than two active tablets are missed due to GI disturbances, follow the missed‑tablet guidance above.

## 5. Contraindications
(Include all stated contraindications; stop product immediately if any of these conditions are present or arise.)
- Current venous thromboembolism (VTE) (on anticoagulants), or history of deep vein thrombosis (DVT) or pulmonary embolism (PE).
- Known hereditary or acquired predisposition for VTE (e.g., APC‑resistance including factor V Leiden, antithrombin‑III deficiency, protein C deficiency, protein S deficiency).
- Major surgery with prolonged immobilisation.
- Current arterial thromboembolism (ATE) or history of ATE (e.g., myocardial infarction or stroke) or prodromal conditions (e.g., angina, transient ischemic attack).
- Known hereditary or acquired predisposition for ATE (e.g., hyperhomocysteinaemia, antiphospholipid antibodies such as anticardiolipin or lupus anticoagulant).
- History of migraine with focal neurological symptoms.
- High risk of ATE due to multiple combined risk factors (e.g., heavy cigarette smoking [around 15 or more/day], positive family history of ATE at relatively early age [<50 years], age >35 years).
- High risk of ATE due to one serious risk factor (e.g., diabetes mellitus with vascular symptoms, severe hypertension, severe dyslipoproteinaemia).
- Pancreatitis, or history of pancreatitis if associated with severe hypertriglyceridaemia.
- Presence or history of severe hepatic disease as long as liver function has not returned to normal.
- Severe renal insufficiency or acute renal failure.
- Presence or history of liver tumours (benign or malignant).
- Adrenal insufficiency.
- Known or suspected sex‑steroid‑influenced malignancies (e.g., of genital organs or breasts).
- Undiagnosed vaginal bleeding.
- Known or suspected pregnancy.
- Hypersensitivity to any component of the product.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed/critical warnings
- **Thromboembolism risk:** Combined hormonal contraceptives increase the risk of venous and arterial thromboembolic events; stop product immediately if a thrombotic event occurs.

### General warnings and precautions
- Evaluate personal and family history for thrombotic or thromboembolic disorders before initiation; consider inherited or acquired hypercoagulable states.
- Discontinue during prolonged immobilisation. If feasible, stop at least 4 weeks before and through 2 weeks after major surgery or other procedures with elevated thromboembolic risk.
- Start no earlier than 4 weeks postpartum in non‑breastfeeding women because postpartum thromboembolic risk is increased; consider breastfeeding status prior to initiation.
- Stop if unexplained visual disturbances, proptosis, diplopia, papilledema, or retinal vascular lesions occur and evaluate for retinal vein thrombosis.
- Cigarette smoking increases the cardiovascular risk associated with combined hormonal contraceptives; risk increases with age and smoking intensity, particularly in women over 35 years.
- Monitor for symptoms of arterial events (e.g., sudden focal neurological signs, severe prolonged headache, visual disturbances, chest pain) and advise urgent medical evaluation if suspected.
- Venous event symptoms (unilateral leg swelling, pain, increased warmth, sudden unexplained dyspnoea, chest pain, haemoptysis) require immediate evaluation.
- Hyperkalaemia:
  - Drospirenone has antimineralocorticoid activity. Monitor serum potassium in patients with renal impairment, known hyperkalaemia, or when concomitant use of potassium‑sparing medicines or other agents that increase potassium is anticipated.
  - Clinical development studies reported no meaningful trend in serum potassium; most observed elevations were mild and transient; no discontinuations due to hyperkalaemia in pivotal trials (n = 3,632).
- Hypertension:
  - Contraindicated in uncontrolled hypertension or hypertension with vascular disease.
  - Monitor blood pressure at routine visits; discontinue if clinically significant increases occur.
  - Clinical trials showed no clinically meaningful mean changes in blood pressure or heart rate.
- Metabolic effects:
  - May decrease glucose tolerance. Contraindicated in women with diabetes and vascular complications (e.g., hypertension, nephropathy, retinopathy, neuropathy).
  - Monitor glucose control in prediabetic and diabetic patients.
  - Consider alternative contraception in uncontrolled dyslipidaemia. Women with hypertriglyceridaemia or family history of hypertriglyceridaemia may have increased pancreatitis risk.
- Hepatic disorders:
  - Contraindicated in severe hepatic disease or presence/history of liver tumours. Discontinue if significant deterioration occurs.
- Breast cancer and other malignancies:
  - Do not use in known or suspected sex‑steroid‑dependent malignancies; evaluate unexplained vaginal bleeding for malignancy or pregnancy.
- Age considerations:
  - Assess cardiovascular risk factors before initiating in women >35 years (e.g., hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidaemia, obesity).
- Restarting after a break:
  - The risk of VTE is highest in the first year of COC use and when restarting after a break of ≥4 weeks.

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and periodic assessment of blood pressure.
- Consider baseline potassium in patients at risk for hyperkalaemia and monitor potassium during therapy if risk factors or interacting drugs are present.
- Monitor glucose in patients with diabetes or prediabetes.
- Evaluate for signs/symptoms of thromboembolism at each visit.
- Investigate unexplained vaginal bleeding and absence of scheduled withdrawal bleeding to exclude pregnancy or other pathology.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Pharmacodynamic interactions:
  - Concomitant use of potassium‑sparing diuretics, potassium supplements, ACE inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers, aldosterone antagonists, or other agents that can increase serum potassium may increase the risk of hyperkalaemia due to drospirenone’s antimineralocorticoid activity. Monitor potassium if such combinations are necessary.
- Pharmacokinetic interactions:
  - Specific enzyme‑mediated pharmacokinetic interaction data (e.g., CYP interactions, effects of hepatic enzyme inducers/inhibitors) are not specified in the available sources.
- Interactions requiring clinical management:
  - Use caution and monitor potassium when combining with medications that raise serum potassium (see above).
  - For agents that may alter coagulation or thrombosis risk, exercise clinical judgment; the product should be discontinued if a thrombotic event occurs.
- Jurisdiction‑specific interaction guidance: Not specified in the available sources.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Known or suspected pregnancy is a contraindication to use.
- Starting after pregnancy loss:
  - After first‑trimester abortion or miscarriage: may start immediately; no additional contraception needed if started within 5 days.
  - After second‑trimester abortion or miscarriage: delay initiation until 4 weeks post‑event; use non‑hormonal backup until 7 consecutive active tablets taken.
- Pregnancy categories by jurisdiction (e.g., Australian category, US letter category): Not specified in the available sources.

### Breastfeeding
- If breastfeeding, non‑hormonal contraception is recommended until breastfeeding is discontinued.
- Specific statements regarding secretion into breast milk, effects on lactation, or infant safety: Not specified in the available sources.

### Fertility
- Fertility return after discontinuation: Not specified in the available sources.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Clinical trial populations included females aged 16–50 years in pivotal studies. Specific approvals, dosing, or safety for adolescents <16 years are not specified in the available sources.

### Elderly
- Not applicable; contraceptive use is intended for women of reproductive potential. Dose adjustments or recommendations for elderly patients are not specified.

### Renal impairment
- Severe renal insufficiency or acute renal failure: contraindicated.
- Mild or moderate renal impairment: in one clinical study drospirenone did not show an effect on serum potassium; however, theoretical risk for hyperkalaemia exists in patients with elevated baseline potassium or concomitant potassium‑raising drugs. Monitor potassium as indicated.

### Hepatic impairment
- Presence or history of severe hepatic disease with unresolved LFT abnormalities: contraindicated.
- Presence or history of liver tumours: contraindicated.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Estetrol: a synthetic analogue of endogenous human estrogen.
- Drospirenone: a synthetic progestational agent with antimineralocorticoid and antiandrogenic properties.
- A detailed mechanism of contraceptive action (e.g., ovulation inhibition, cervical mucus changes) is not specified in the available sources.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption, distribution, metabolism, elimination (specific parameters such as Tmax, half‑life, clearance): Not specified in the available sources.
- Effects of renal or hepatic impairment on pharmacokinetics: Not specified in the available sources, except clinical comments regarding potassium in renal impairment and contraindication in severe hepatic disease.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Hemostatic effects: In a small Phase 2 study, estetrol + drospirenone produced smaller increases from baseline in some pro‑coagulant markers (e.g., ETP‑based activated protein C resistance and prothrombin fragments 1+2) compared to ethinylestradiol/levonorgestrel; overall impact on haemostatic markers was less than with ethinylestradiol/drospirenone in that study. Clinical relevance for thrombotic risk requires cautious interpretation and continued vigilance.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Two large prospective clinical studies in women aged 16–50 years were described (pooled n = 3,632; referred to as C301 [Europe/Russia] and C302 [North America]):
  - Indication studied: prevention of pregnancy.
  - Safety and bleeding pattern data were collected using daily diaries.
  - VTE: one VTE reported in the Europe/Russia study, corresponding to an incidence of 3.66 per 10,000 women‑years in that study population.
  - Bleeding profile:
    - Unscheduled bleeding/spotting incidence: 27.1% in Cycle 1 (pooled); 15–20% per cycle from Cycle 2 through Cycle 12.
    - Most unscheduled episodes were spotting only; when unscheduled bleeding did occur, the majority did not require sanitary protection.
    - Absence of scheduled withdrawal bleeding: occurred in 9.7–11.3% of cycles in trials.
  - Hemostasis and metabolic comparisons (Phase 2 study, small sample):
    - Compared estetrol + drospirenone to ethinylestradiol/levonorgestrel and ethinylestradiol/drospirenone over 6 cycles.
    - Estetrol + drospirenone showed smaller percentage changes from baseline in certain pro‑coagulant markers compared with ethinylestradiol/levonorgestrel.
    - Lipid parameters: estetrol + drospirenone had a neutral effect on lipid parameters in a small Phase 2 comparison; specific median percentage changes and between‑treatment differences were reported in the source material.
    - Glucose metabolism: no notable changes in oral glucose tolerance, fasting HbA1c, or fasting glucose at 6 months in the Phase 2 trial; small increases in fasting insulin and C‑peptide were observed but were smaller than comparative ethinylestradiol regimens.
- Additional trial details (randomisation, blinding, primary efficacy outcomes such as Pearl Index, pregnancy incidence, or full safety/tolerability listings) are not specified in the available sources.
