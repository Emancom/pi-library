# Editorial Product Information: Edaravone

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises available regulatory data into a single clinical reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the local approved labelling for definitive legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Edaravone (Chemical name: 3-Methyl-1-phenyl-2-pyrazolin-5-one).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Radicava** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Concentrated solution for injection. Each 20 mL clear glass ampoule contains 30 mg of edaravone [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only (requires dilution) [Australian].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Teva Pharma Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Edaravone** is indicated for the treatment of adults diagnosed with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) who meet the following clinical criteria [Australian]:
- Independent in activities of daily living (ADL).
- Possess normal respiratory function.
- Treatment initiation occurs within two years of disease onset.

*Note: Efficacy has not been demonstrated in patient populations falling outside these specific parameters [Australian].*

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:** The standard dose is 60 mg (two 30 mg ampoules) [Australian].
- **Dosing Schedule:**
    - **Initial Cycle:** Daily 60 mg infusion for 14 consecutive days, followed by a 14-day drug-free interval [Australian].
    - **Subsequent Cycles:** Daily 60 mg infusion for 10 days within a 14-day period, followed by a 14-day drug-free interval [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Must be diluted with 100 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride [Australian].
    - Administer via intravenous infusion over a 60-minute period [Australian].
    - Use as soon as practicable after dilution; if stored, refrigerate at 2°C–8°C for no more than 24 hours [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **edaravone** or any of the inactive ingredients, including sodium bisulfite [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None listed in provided sources [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity and Anaphylaxis:** Cases of redness, wheals, erythema multiforme, and full anaphylaxis (decreased blood pressure, dyspnoea) have occurred. Patients must be monitored closely. Infusion must be stopped immediately at the first sign of hypersensitivity [Australian].
- **Sulfite Sensitivity:** This product contains sodium bisulfite. It may cause allergic-type reactions or life-threatening asthmatic episodes, particularly in patients with a history of asthma [Australian].
- **Sodium Content:** Each ampoule contains 135 mg of sodium chloride. This should be considered for patients on sodium-restricted diets [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Clinical monitoring for hypersensitivity is required during and after the infusion [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity reactions [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Profile:** Edaravone is not expected to significantly inhibit or induce major CYP450 enzymes (1A2, 2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 3A4) or UGT isoforms (1A1, 2B7) [Australian].
- **Transporter Interactions:** Edaravone and its metabolites do not significantly inhibit P-gp, BCRP, OATP1B1, OATP1B3, OAT1, or OCT2. However, the metabolite edaravone sulfate is a substrate for OAT1 and OAT3. Inhibitors of these transporters may increase exposure to the sulfate metabolite [Australian].
- **Incompatibilities:** Do not mix with total parenteral nutrition, amino-acid infusions, potassium canrenoate, or anticonvulsants (e.g., diazepam, phenytoin), as cloudiness may occur [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. There are no adequate data in pregnant women. Animal studies showed lower fetal weights and embryofetal death at maternally toxic doses. Use is generally discouraged; patients should be advised of potential fetal risks [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Edaravone and its metabolites are excreted in the milk of lactating rats. There is no human data regarding excretion in human milk or effects on the infant. The maternal clinical need should be weighed against the potential risks to the breastfed child [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal data showed disrupted oestrus cycles and altered mating behaviour at very high doses (3 times the human equivalent dose), but no effects were seen at lower doses [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in paediatric patients [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials included patients up to 75 years old. No general differences in safety or efficacy were observed, though greater sensitivity in some older individuals cannot be ruled out [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for mild to moderate impairment. It has not been studied in severe renal impairment and is **not recommended** for patients undergoing renal replacement therapy [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for mild, moderate, or severe hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Edaravone is a free radical scavenger that reduces oxidative stress. The exact mechanism by which this provides a therapeutic effect in ALS remains unknown [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak plasma concentration (Cmax) is reached at the end of the 60-minute infusion. No accumulation occurs with multiple doses [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Protein binding is 92% (primarily to albumin) [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolised to inactive sulfate and glucuronide conjugates [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Excreted primarily in the urine (70–90% as glucuronide conjugate). The terminal elimination half-life is 4.5 to 6 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study MCI186-19:** A 6-month, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 137 Japanese ALS patients meeting specific criteria (independent living, disease duration ≤2 years, %FVC ≥80%). The edaravone group showed significantly less decline in ALSFRS-R scores compared to placebo (treatment difference 2.49; p=0.0013) [Australian].
- **Study MCI186-16:** A trial involving a broader ALS population that failed to reach statistical significance in its primary endpoint. Post-hoc analysis suggested the benefit was driven by the specific sub-population later identified in the approved indication [Australian].
- **Survival:** No benefit on survival has been demonstrated in clinical trials [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].
- **Single Use:** Ampoules are for single use only; discard any unused residue [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Subject to additional monitoring by the TGA to identify new safety information [Australian].
