# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a concise reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP), a carrier molecule involved in mammalian energy metabolism.
- **Chemical Class:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injection (sterile solution), 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Manufactured by Baxter Pharmaceuticals India Private Ltd for Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Administration:** Delivered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; frequency should never be less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest administered dose reported is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Delivered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Titration:** Dose adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Monitoring
- Plasma free carnitine concentrations should be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L. Monitoring should include blood chemistries and vital signs [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known:** There are currently no listed contraindications for levocarnitine injection [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks/benefits before any attempt to re-administer [US].
- **Seizures:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Oral Formulation Caution:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of the oral formulation in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **General:** Transient nausea and vomiting are common. Body odor and gastritis are reported less frequently.
- **ESRD Clinical Trial Data (≥5% Frequency):**
    - **Body as a whole:** Abdominal pain (21%), injection site reaction (38%), chest pain (15%), fever (12%), flu syndrome (29%), headache (37%), infection (24%), pain (35%).
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (21%), hypotension (19%), tachycardia (9%).
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhea (35%), vomiting (21%), nausea (12%).
    - **Nervous system:** Dizziness (18%), paresthesia (12%).
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (27%), cough increase (18%), dyspnea (11%).
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels in patients on warfarin therapy following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not specified; strictly "use only if clearly needed"]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. However, adequate, well-controlled human studies are lacking [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. In dairy cows, carnitine concentrations in milk increase following exogenous administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, Saccharomyces, Schizosaccharomyces) were negative. Long-term animal carcinogenicity studies have not been conducted [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg body weight; see Section 3 for specific metabolic disorder dosing [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety and efficacy of the **oral** formulation have not been established in renal failure. Levocarnitine is easily removed by dialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours.
    - **Elimination:** Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels).
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Bioavailability:** In healthy volunteers, levocarnitine tablets and oral solution were found to be bio-equivalent. Absolute bioavailability (corrected for endogenous levels) is approximately 15% [US].
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (n=12), 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean trough concentrations from 21.3 µmol/L at baseline to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations is proven, the effect on clinical outcomes in ESRD remains undetermined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when diluted to concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL in Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's [US].
