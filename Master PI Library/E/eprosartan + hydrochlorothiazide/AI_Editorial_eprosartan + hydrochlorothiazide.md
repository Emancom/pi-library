# Editorial Product Information: eprosartan + hydrochlorothiazide

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **eprosartan + hydrochlorothiazide**
- Chemical class:
  - Eprosartan: angiotensin II receptor (AT1) antagonist (non-biphenyl, non‑tetrazole, non‑peptide)
  - Hydrochlorothiazide: thiazide diuretic (sulfonamide derivative)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of hypertension (essential hypertension).  
- May be used alone or in combination with other antihypertensive agents (for example, calcium channel blockers).  
- Not indicated for initial therapy of hypertension (fixed-dose combination formulation).  

(If regional differences in indications were present in the source material these would be noted; none were specified beyond the statements above.)

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Fixed-dose strengths described in available sources: eprosartan 600 mg combined with hydrochlorothiazide 12.5 mg or 25 mg.  
- The fixed combination is not intended for initial therapy; use in patients already established on component therapy or when combination therapy is appropriate.

### Dosing by indication
- Hypertension: specific recommended starting doses, titration steps, alternative dose regimens for special populations, and maximum recommended doses for the fixed combination are Not specified in available sources. (Clinical trials of eprosartan monotherapy used a range of daily total eprosartan doses discussed in the clinical trials section; see Section 12.)

### Administration instructions
- May be administered with or without food. Administration with food delays eprosartan absorption and causes variable (<25%) changes in Cmax and AUC that were not considered clinically important for eprosartan.

### Titration schedules
- Not specified in available sources for the fixed-dose combination. (Eprosartan monotherapy studies evaluated multiple dose regimens; see Section 12.)

### Missed dose instructions

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to **eprosartan + hydrochlorothiazide** or any component of the product.
- Because of the hydrochlorothiazide component:
  - Anuria.
  - Hypersensitivity to other sulfonamide-derived drugs.
- Any additional region-specific contraindications: Not specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed/Highlighted warning
- **Fetal/Neonatal Risk:** Drugs that act directly on the renin–angiotensin system can cause fetal and neonatal morbidity and death when administered during pregnancy. When pregnancy is detected, discontinue **eprosartan + hydrochlorothiazide** as soon as possible. Adverse fetal/neonatal outcomes reported with renin–angiotensin system blockade include hypotension, neonatal skull hypoplasia, anuria, reversible or irreversible renal failure, oligohydramnios (with associated limb contractures, craniofacial deformation, pulmonary hypoplasia), prematurity, intrauterine growth retardation, patent ductus arteriosus, and death.

### General warnings and precautions
- Symptomatic hypotension may occur in volume- and/or salt-depleted patients (for example, those treated with high-dose diuretics). Correct volume and/or salt depletion prior to initiating therapy or monitor closely on initiation.
- Hydrochlorothiazide-specific precautions:
  - Use with caution in patients with impaired hepatic function or progressive liver disease; small fluid/electrolyte changes may precipitate hepatic coma.
  - Hypersensitivity reactions (including in patients with a history of allergy or bronchial asthma) may occur.
  - Thiazide-associated idiosyncratic reactions can cause acute transient myopia and acute angle-closure glaucoma; onset is typically hours to weeks after initiation.
  - Thiazides may exacerbate or activate systemic lupus erythematosus.
  - Lithium generally should not be given with thiazides.
- Electrolyte and metabolic disturbances associated with the hydrochlorothiazide component:
  - Increased urinary potassium loss and decreased serum potassium (hypokalemia) can occur; concomitant use of an angiotensin II receptor antagonist may attenuate potassium loss.
  - Thiazides can increase urinary excretion of magnesium (risk of hypomagnesemia).
  - Thiazides decrease urinary calcium excretion and may cause intermittent slight elevations of serum calcium; marked hypercalcemia may indicate underlying hyperparathyroidism.
  - Hyperglycemia and precipitation of latent diabetes mellitus may occur.
  - Hyperuricemia and precipitation of gout have been reported.
- Hepatic impairment: Eprosartan AUC increased by ~40% in men with decreased hepatic function after a single 100 mg dose; no adjustment was judged necessary in the populations studied.
- Renal impairment: In moderate or severe renal impairment, eprosartan AUC increased 70–90% and Cmax increased 30–50% after 600 mg once daily; unbound eprosartan fraction increased. Eprosartan is poorly removed by hemodialysis (dialytic clearance <1 L/hr). No initial dose adjustment generally necessary in moderate/severe renal impairment for eprosartan, but maximum dose should not exceed 600 mg daily.
- Hypersensitivity: Hypersensitivity reactions to hydrochlorothiazide may occur even without prior allergy history.
- Monitoring for signs of fetal harm if exposure occurs in pregnancy: serial ultrasound assessments and fetal surveillance as appropriate; discontinue drug if oligohydramnios is observed unless maternal life-saving need justifies continuation.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor blood pressure, renal function (serum creatinine, eGFR), and serum electrolytes (including potassium, sodium, calcium, magnesium) prior to and during therapy, particularly after initiation or dose changes and in patients at risk (e.g., elderly, renal impairment, concomitant diuretics).
- Monitor glucose and uric acid in patients at risk.
- In pregnant patients inadvertently exposed during the second or third trimester, monitor fetal renal function/amnio‑infusion environment (serial ultrasound), and monitor neonates for hypotension, oliguria, and hyperkalemia.
- Monitor for signs/symptoms of acute angle‑closure glaucoma (sudden ocular pain, decreased visual acuity).

### Cough — trial frequency details (explicitly reported)
- In pooled analysis of six double‑blind clinical trials (1,554 patients treated with eprosartan), spontaneously reported cough incidence: 3.5% for eprosartan versus 2.6% for placebo.
- In one study of patients with prior ACE inhibitor–associated cough:
  - Cough incidence: eprosartan 2.2% vs placebo 4.4% vs ACE inhibitor 20.5% (p = 0.008 for eprosartan vs ACE inhibitor).
- In another study comparing eprosartan and an ACE inhibitor:
  - Cough incidence: eprosartan 1.5% vs ACE inhibitor 5.4% (p = 0.018).

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Eprosartan did not affect digoxin pharmacokinetics in a single-dose study and had no effect on steady‑state INR when coadministered with warfarin in healthy volunteers.
- Eprosartan coadministration did not alter 24‑hour plasma glucose profiles with glyburide in diabetic patients.
- Eprosartan pharmacokinetics were not affected by ranitidine.
- Eprosartan did not inhibit human cytochrome P450 enzymes CYP1A, 2A6, 2C9/8, 2C19, 2D6, 2E, and 3A in vitro.
- Steady‑state eprosartan plasma concentrations were not affected by coadministration of ketoconazole or fluconazole (CYP3A and CYP2C9 inhibitors respectively).
- No pharmacokinetic interaction was observed between 600 mg eprosartan and 12.5 mg hydrochlorothiazide.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions / clinically significant interactions
- Lithium: Thiazide diuretics can increase lithium concentrations and enhance lithium toxicity; lithium generally should not be given with thiazides.
- Concomitant use with other agents that affect the renin–angiotensin system (ACE inhibitors, other ARBs) may increase risk of hypotension, hyperkalemia, and renal impairment — specific combination guidance Not specified in available sources for the fixed-dose product.
- Use with other antihypertensive agents: additive blood pressure lowering effects may occur; monitor blood pressure.
- Diuretics: Preexisting volume depletion from diuretics may increase risk of symptomatic hypotension on initiation of eprosartan; correct volume depletion prior to therapy or monitor closely.
- Agents that may alter renal function (e.g., NSAIDs): Not specified in available sources for this combination; however, renal function monitoring is advised when combining agents affecting renal hemodynamics.

### Management notes
- Monitor serum electrolytes and renal function when adding or withdrawing concomitant therapy that affects renal function or electrolytes (e.g., diuretics, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, NSAIDs, potassium supplements, potassium‑sparing diuretics).
- Avoid lithium coadministration where possible; if essential, monitor lithium levels closely and adjust dosage.

(Any jurisdiction-specific interaction differences in the source materials would be noted here; none were specified beyond the interaction summaries above.)

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Pregnancy risk: **Eprosartan + hydrochlorothiazide** exposure during pregnancy, particularly in the second and third trimesters, can cause fetal and neonatal morbidity and death due to blockade of the renin–angiotensin system.
- Reported fetal/neonatal adverse outcomes following maternal exposure to renin–angiotensin system antagonists include hypotension, neonatal skull hypoplasia, anuria, reversible or irreversible renal failure, oligohydramnios (with associated limb contractures, craniofacial deformation, pulmonary hypoplasia), prematurity, intrauterine growth retardation, patent ductus arteriosus, and death.
- If pregnancy is detected, discontinue **eprosartan + hydrochlorothiazide** as soon as possible.
- For rare cases where no suitable alternative exists, inform the mother of potential fetal hazards and perform serial ultrasound examinations to assess amniotic fluid; if oligohydramnios is observed, discontinue the product unless maternal life‑saving need exists. Appropriate fetal testing (contraction stress test, nonstress test, or biophysical profile) may be considered depending on gestational age.
- Infants with in utero exposure should be monitored for hypotension, oliguria, and hyperkalemia; exchange transfusion or dialysis may be required in neonates with severe effects.
- Pregnancy category designations (regional classification systems) are Not specified in available sources.

### Breastfeeding
- Hydrochlorothiazide is excreted in breast milk. Thiazides may decrease lactation and can cause neonatal adverse effects such as jaundice and thrombocytopenia when present in breast milk.
- Excretion of eprosartan in human milk: Not specified in available sources.
- Recommendations for breastfeeding mothers: Not specified in available sources beyond the statement that hydrochlorothiazide is excreted in breast milk.

### Fertility

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Eprosartan pharmacokinetics and safety have not been investigated in patients younger than 18 years of age. Use in pediatric patients: Not specified in available sources.

### Elderly use
- Elderly patients demonstrated increased exposure: in single-dose studies, AUC, Cmax, and Tmax for eprosartan increased approximately twofold in elderly men (aged 68–78) versus younger men (aged 20–38). Plasma protein binding not influenced by age. Clinical precautions apply; specific dose adjustments for elderly patients not specified in available sources.

### Renal impairment
- Moderate or severe renal impairment: following 600 mg once daily, eprosartan AUC increased 70–90% and Cmax increased 30–50%; unbound fraction increased by 35% (moderate) and 59% (severe).
- No initial dose adjustment is generally necessary in patients with moderate or severe renal impairment, but the maximum dose should not exceed 600 mg daily.
- Eprosartan is poorly removed by hemodialysis (dialytic clearance <1 L/hr).

### Hepatic impairment
- Eprosartan AUC increased by approximately 40% in men with decreased hepatic function after a single 100 mg dose; plasma protein binding not altered. No dosage adjustment was deemed necessary in the population studied. Specific dosing by degree of hepatic impairment not specified.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Eprosartan: a selective competitive angiotensin II receptor antagonist at the AT1 receptor, blocking angiotensin II‑mediated vasoconstriction and aldosterone secretion. It does not inhibit angiotensin‑converting enzyme nor degrade bradykinin. Eprosartan has approximately 1,000‑fold greater affinity for AT1 versus AT2 receptors and lacks partial agonist activity at AT1.
- Hydrochlorothiazide: a thiazide diuretic that inhibits renal tubular reabsorption of sodium and chloride, increasing urinary excretion of these electrolytes; diuresis reduces plasma volume and may lead to secondary increases in plasma renin activity, aldosterone secretion, and urinary potassium loss.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption:
  - Eprosartan: absolute bioavailability after a single 300 mg oral dose ≈13%. Peak plasma concentrations occur 1–2 hours post­dose (fasted). Food delays absorption and causes variable (<25%) changes in Cmax and AUC that were not considered clinically important. Plasma concentrations increase slightly less than dose-proportionally over 100–800 mg.
  - Hydrochlorothiazide: after oral administration diuresis begins within 2 hours, peaks at ~4 hours, lasts ~6–12 hours.
- Distribution:
  - Eprosartan: high plasma protein binding (~98%) across therapeutic concentrations; population mean steady‑state volume of distribution ~308 L. Oral clearance decreases with age; population mean CL/F ~48.5 L/hr for a 60‑year‑old.
  - Hydrochlorothiazide: crosses the placenta but not the blood–brain barrier; excreted in breast milk.
- Metabolism and excretion:
  - Eprosartan: eliminated by biliary and renal routes primarily as unchanged drug. Less than 2% of an oral dose excreted in urine as glucuronide. Following oral dosing, ~90% recovered in feces and ~7% in urine. Not metabolized by cytochrome P450 enzymes; no active metabolites identified in human studies.
  - Hydrochlorothiazide: not metabolized; eliminated rapidly by the kidney; ≥61% of an oral dose eliminated unchanged within 24 hours.
- Half-life:
  - Eprosartan: mean terminal elimination half‑life following multiple 600 mg oral doses ≈20 hours; does not significantly accumulate with chronic use.
  - Hydrochlorothiazide: reported plasma half‑life between ~5.6 and 14.8 hours when measured over at least 24 hours.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Eprosartan blocks angiotensin II actions, increasing plasma renin activity and angiotensin II concentrations but maintaining antihypertensive effect. Onset of blood pressure reduction is typically within 1–2 hours, with maximal effect achieved in most patients after 2–3 weeks of treatment. Eprosartan does not cause a rebound hypertension upon discontinuation.
- Co‑administration of an ARB with a thiazide diuretic may mitigate diuretic‑induced hypokalemia due to reduction of renin‑aldosterone activity.

## 12. Clinical Trials
### Eprosartan monotherapy
- Five placebo‑controlled trials (4–13 weeks) evaluated eprosartan in predominantly mild to moderate hypertension. Doses studied included:
  - 400–1200 mg once daily (two studies),
  - 25–400 mg twice daily (two studies),
  - comparison of total daily doses 400–800 mg given once daily versus twice daily (one study).
- The five studies randomized 1,111 patients to eprosartan and 395 to placebo and demonstrated dose‑related antihypertensive responses.
- At study endpoints, eprosartan total daily doses of 600–1200 mg once daily produced placebo‑adjusted reductions in sitting systolic/diastolic blood pressure of approximately 5–10/3–6 mmHg. Twice‑daily regimens (200–400 mg b.i.d.) produced similar or slightly larger trough effects.
- Onset of effect occurs within 1–2 hours; achievement of maximal BP response generally requires 2–3 weeks.

### Eprosartan–hydrochlorothiazide combination
- Four adequate and well‑controlled studies assessed the combination in 1,457 patients with mild‑to‑moderate essential hypertension.
- In a 2×2 factorial study (≈112–119 patients per arm), placebo‑subtracted mean blood pressure reductions at 8 weeks were:
  - Eprosartan 600 mg: 3.6/2.1 mmHg
  - Hydrochlorothiazide 12.5 mg: 5.6/1.9 mmHg
  - Combination (eprosartan 600 mg + hydrochlorothiazide 12.5 mg): 10.0/5.0 mmHg
  - This demonstrates additive BP reduction with combination therapy compared with either component alone.

### Other clinical observations
- In trials comparing eprosartan with ACE inhibitors, the incidence of dry persistent cough was consistently lower with eprosartan than with ACE inhibitors (see Section 7 for rates and p‑values).
- No meaningful changes in fasting triglycerides, total cholesterol, HDL‑cholesterol, LDL‑cholesterol, or fasting glucose were observed in the trials cited for eprosartan alone.
- Eprosartan increased mean effective renal plasma flow (ERPF) in salt‑restricted and salt‑replete normal subjects; effects on ERPF were not observed in hypertensive patients on normal salt diets.
