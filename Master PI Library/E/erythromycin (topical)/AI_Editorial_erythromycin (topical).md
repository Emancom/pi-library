# Editorial Product Information: Erythromycin (Topical)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of topical erythromycin for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Erythromycin (a macrolide antibiotic produced by *Saccaro-polyspora erythraea*).
- **Brand Names:** Erythromycin Topical Solution [US].
- **ATC Code:** D10AF02.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Topical solution: 2% (20 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical (dermatologic) use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Saptalis Pharmaceuticals, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acne Vulgaris:** Indicated for the topical treatment of acne vulgaris [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Adolescents):**
    - Apply a thin layer to the affected areas twice daily, once in the morning and once in the evening [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Thoroughly wash the skin with warm water and soap, and pat dry before application [US].
    - Acne lesions on the face, neck, shoulders, chest, and back are suitable for treatment [US].
    - Use the provided applicator top. If fingertips are used, wash hands immediately after application [US].
- **Titration/Dose Adjustment:**
    - Frequency of application may be reduced if excessive drying or peeling occurs [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Contraindicated in individuals with a history of hypersensitivity to erythromycin or any other component of the formulation (including alcohol, citric acid, or propylene glycol) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Pseudomembranous Colitis:** Although rare with topical use, colitis has been reported with nearly all antibacterial agents. Clinicians should consider this diagnosis in patients who develop diarrhea during or following treatment. Mild cases may respond to discontinuation; moderate to severe cases require fluid/electrolyte management and appropriate antibacterial therapy against *C. difficile* [US].
- **Cumulative Irritancy:** Use with caution alongside other topical acne therapies (e.g., peeling, desquamating, or abrasive agents) as a cumulative irritant effect may occur [US].
- **Microbial Overgrowth:** Prolonged use may result in the overgrowth of non-susceptible or antibiotic-resistant organisms. If superinfection occurs, discontinue use [US].
- **Application Cautions:** Avoid contact with eyes and all mucous membranes [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following local adverse reactions have been reported; however, specific frequency data (e.g., %) was not provided in the source documentation.

- **Dermatologic:** Peeling, dryness, itching (pruritus), erythema, and oiliness [US].
- **Ocular:** Irritation of the eyes [US].
- **Sensitization:** Tenderness of the skin and generalized urticarial reactions.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Generalized urticarial reactions possibly related to erythromycin have been reported, occasionally requiring systemic steroid therapy [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** *In vitro* antagonism has been demonstrated between erythromycin and other antibiotics, including lincomycin, chloramphenicol, and clindamycin [US].
- **Topical Combinations:** Caution is advised when used concomitantly with other abrasive or medicated soaps/cleansers and other topical acne medications due to potential cumulative irritancy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned a letter grade in the source, but the PI notes that animal reproduction studies (rats fed oral erythromycin base up to 0.25% of diet) showed no evidence of teratogenicity [US]. 
    - **Clinical Recommendation:** There are no adequate/well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Erythromycin crosses the placental barrier (though fetal levels are low). Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Erythromycin is known to be excreted in human milk following oral and parenteral administration. It is unknown if topical application results in significant systemic absorption or excretion in milk. Caution should be exercised when administered to a nursing woman [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies found no apparent effect on male or female fertility in rats [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in pediatric patients have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Erythromycin inhibits protein synthesis in susceptible organisms by reversibly binding to the 50S ribosomal subunits. This inhibits the translocation of aminoacyl transfer-RNA and inhibits polypeptide synthesis [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The clinical effect in acne is primarily attributed to its antibacterial activity, reducing the population of *Propionibacterium acnes* and potentially reducing inflammation [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Not specified in available sources for the topical route.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store at 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Keep the container tightly closed [US].
