# Editorial Product Information: Elosulfase alfa

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically focused overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory guidelines for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Elosulfase alfa (rch).
- **Chemical Class:** Purified human enzyme (N-acetylgalactosamine-6-sulfatase) produced by recombinant DNA technology in a Chinese hamster ovary cell line.
- **Brand Name:** **Vimizim** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A16AB12.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Concentrated solution for injection; 1 mg/mL. Each 5 mL vial contains 5 mg of elosulfase alfa.
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion after dilution.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** BioMarin Pharmaceutical Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Mucopolysaccharidosis type IVA (MPS IVA; Morquio A syndrome):** Indicated for the treatment of patients confirmed to have this lysosomal storage disorder [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** 2 mg/kg of body weight administered intravenously once per week.
- **Pre-treatment:** Administration of antihistamines (with or without antipyretics) is recommended 30–60 minutes before starting the infusion to reduce the risk of infusion-related reactions (IRs).
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Infusions should be delivered over approximately 4 hours.
    - **Dilution:** Must be diluted with 0.9% Sodium Chloride for injection.
    - **Total Volume:** Determined by patient weight:
        - < 25 kg: 100 mL final volume.
        - ≥ 25 kg: 250 mL final volume.
    - **In-line Filter:** Use a low-protein-binding infusion set with a 0.2 µm in-line filter.
- **Titration Schedule:**
    - **100 mL Volume:** Start at 3 mL/hr for 15 mins; increase to 6 mL/hr for 15 mins; then increase by 6 mL/hr every 15 mins to a maximum of 36 mL/hr.
    - **250 mL Volume:** Start at 6 mL/hr for 15 mins; increase to 12 mL/hr for 15 mins; then increase by 12 mL/hr every 15 mins to a maximum of 72 mL/hr.
- **Home Administration:** May be considered for patients tolerating infusions well, provided it is supervised by a healthcare professional trained in managing serious infusion reactions [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Severe or life-threatening hypersensitivity to elosulfase alfa or any excipient, particularly if the hypersensitivity cannot be adequately controlled [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Anaphylaxis and Severe Allergic Reactions:** These have been reported as early as 30 minutes into the infusion and as late as 47 infusions into treatment. Medical support must be readily available during administration. If a severe reaction occurs, the infusion must be stopped immediately and appropriate treatment initiated.
- **Infusion-Related Reactions (IRs):** Highly prevalent (96% of patients in clinical trials). Management includes slowing or interrupting the infusion and administering additional prophylactic medications.
- **Spinal/Cervical Cord Compression (SCC):** A known complication of MPS IVA. Patients should be monitored for signs such as back pain, limb paralysis, or incontinence.
- **Acute Respiratory Complications:** Patients with an acute febrile or respiratory illness at the time of infusion are at higher risk of life-threatening complications. Consider delaying treatment based on clinical status.
- **Sleep Apnoea:** Common in this population. Airway patency should be evaluated before starting treatment. Supplemental oxygen or CPAP should be available during infusion if required.
- **Sodium Restriction:** Each vial contains 8 mg of sodium; this must be considered for patients on controlled sodium diets.
- **Sorbitol:** Not recommended for patients with rare hereditary fructose intolerance.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following frequencies are based on a 24-week placebo-controlled study of 176 patients.
- **Very Common (>10%):**
    - **General:** Pyrexia (43.1%), Chills (10.3%).
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Vomiting (44.8%), Nausea (31.0%), Abdominal pain (24.1%), Diarrhoea (20.7%).
    - **Nervous System:** Headache (41.4%), Dizziness (12.1%).
    - **Infections:** Otitis media (15.5%), Gastroenteritis (12.1%).
    - **Respiratory:** Oropharyngeal pain (20.7%), Dyspnoea (12.1%).
- **Common (1–10%):**
    - Ear infection, agitation, paraesthesia, somnolence, corneal opacity, flushing, throat irritation, urticaria, neck pain, myalgia, and infusion site pain.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity are the primary serious risks. Serious IRs were observed in 7.7% of patients.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** No formal interaction studies have been performed. Because elosulfase alfa is a recombinant protein, it is unlikely to interact with cytochrome P450-mediated metabolism [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B3.** 
    - There are no adequate studies in pregnant women. Animal studies in rats showed increased perinatal pup mortality at high doses (200x human exposure). Use during pregnancy only if clearly necessary.
- **Breastfeeding:** Elosulfase alfa is excreted in rat milk; human excretion is unknown. A risk-benefit assessment regarding the continuation of breastfeeding vs. therapy is required.
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility or reproductive performance was found in rat studies at systemic exposures up to 400 times the human value.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Most clinical data come from children. Use in patients < 5 years old showed consistent safety profiles, although the 6-minute walk test (6MWT) was not feasible in very young children [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients > 65 years have not been established; it is unknown if they respond differently than younger patients.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not expected to impact the pharmacokinetics of the drug as it is a protein degraded via peptide hydrolysis.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Elosulfase alfa provides exogenous N-acetylgalactosamine-6-sulfatase. This enzyme is taken up into lysosomes via the mannose-6-phosphate (M6P) receptor, where it restores the catabolism of glycosaminoglycans (GAGs), specifically keratan sulfate (KS) and chondroitin 6-sulfate (C6S).
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Distribution:** Mean volume of distribution (Vdss) was 650 mL/kg at Week 22.
    - **Metabolism:** Expected to be degraded via peptide hydrolysis.
    - **Elimination:** Short half-life (t1/2) increased from ~7.5 minutes at Week 0 to ~35.9 minutes by Week 22.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Treatment leads to a sustained reduction in urinary KS levels, though the correlation between KS reduction and clinical outcomes is not fully established.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Study (MOR-004):** A Phase 3, 24-week, randomised, placebo-controlled trial of 176 patients (ages 5–57).
- **Outcomes:** 
    - The 2 mg/kg weekly group showed a statistically significant improvement in the 6-minute walk test (6MWT) compared to placebo (mean treatment effect of 22.5 metres; p=0.0174).
    - No significant difference was observed in the 3-minute stair climb test (MSCT) compared to placebo.
    - Extension studies (MOR-005) indicated maintenance of initial endurance improvements over 72 weeks [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store between 2°C and 8°C. Refrigerate, do not freeze. Keep the vial in the outer carton to protect it from light.
- **Shelf Life:** 3 years [Australian].
- **Diluted Solution:** Chemical and physical stability has been demonstrated for up to 24 hours at 2°C to 8°C, followed by up to 24 hours at room temperature (23°C to 27°C). However, for microbiological safety, the product should be used immediately after dilution.

- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
