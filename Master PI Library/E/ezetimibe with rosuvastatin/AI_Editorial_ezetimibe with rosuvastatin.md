# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine Injection for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory information from the provided source.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the United States (FDA) documentation was provided for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP); chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [United States].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [United States].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Metabolic Disorders:** For the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [United States].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It should be administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [United States]. 
    - **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours, and never less frequently than every 6 hours.
    - **Titration:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, adjusted based on clinical response and plasma carnitine levels. Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised.

- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** 
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [United States].
    - **Administration Timing:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are adequate.

### Administration and Monitoring
- **Inspection:** Parenteral products must be visually inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use.
- **Plasma Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain baseline carnitine levels. For metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring is advised. The target plasma free carnitine concentration is generally between 35 and 60 µmol/L [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None Known:** There are no known contraindications listed in the available source [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued and appropriate medical treatment initiated [United States].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [United States].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** The safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been established in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events observed in clinical trials of ESRD patients (occurring at ≥5%) regardless of causality [United States]:

| Body System | Adverse Event (Incidence ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain, abdominal pain, asthenia |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, palpitations |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, anorexia, dyspepsia |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia, weight decrease/increase, peripheral edema |
| **Nervous System** | Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia, depression |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis |
| **Skin** | Pruritus, rash |
| **Urogenital** | Kidney failure, urinary tract infection |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and seizures [United States].
- **Other:** Body odor and gastritis have been reported less frequently.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. Monitoring of INR is recommended upon initiation of levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [United States: Category not specified, but reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm]. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies in nursing mothers have not been conducted. Levocarnitine concentrations increase in milk in dairy cows. The risks of excess carnitine intake by the infant must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [United States].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders apply to paediatric patients (see Section 3).
- **Geriatric Use:** Not specifically detailed in provided source.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used as indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD. However, caution is advised regarding the accumulation of metabolites if oral formulations are used in this population (see Section 5).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [United States].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** It promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and [3H]-γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours. About 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (n=12) receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis, trough levocarnitine concentrations increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** A study comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses in ESRD patients (n≈30 per group) showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks.
- **Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the effect on specific clinical outcomes in this population has not been definitively determined [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservative [United States].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL).
