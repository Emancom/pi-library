# Editorial Product Information: Enalaprilat

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: US (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory information into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Enalaprilat (anhydrous equivalent), an angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitor.
- **Brand Names:** Not specified in available sources (Generic: Enalaprilat Injection USP) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strengths:** Sterile aqueous solution for intravenous (IV) injection: 1.25 mg/mL (available in 1 mL and 2 mL single-dose vials) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Dr. Reddy’s Laboratories Inc [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypertension:** Indicated for the management of hypertension in cases where oral therapy is not practical [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:** The standard dose is 1.25 mg administered every six hours [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** For intravenous use only. Administer as a slow infusion over at least five minutes. It may be given undiluted or diluted with up to 50 mL of compatible diluents (e.g., 5% Dextrose, 0.9% Sodium Chloride, or Lactated Ringer's) [US].
- **Clinical Response:** Effects typically begin within 15 minutes. Peak effects may not occur for up to four hours after the initial dose, but may be more pronounced with subsequent doses [US].
- **Duration of Therapy:** Clinical studies have generally limited use to 48 hours; however, some patients have received treatment for up to seven days [US].
- **Patients on Diuretic Therapy:** Recommended starting dose is 0.625 mg IV over five minutes. If response is inadequate after one hour, the dose may be repeated. Subsequent doses should be 1.25 mg every six hours [US].
- **Conversion to Oral Therapy:** For patients responding to 1.25 mg q6h, the suggested initial dose of oral enalapril maleate is 5 mg once daily. For those responding to 0.625 mg IV, the oral starting dose is 2.5 mg once daily [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to enalaprilat or any component of the formulation [US].
- History of angioedema related to previous ACE inhibitor treatment [US].
- Hereditary or idiopathic angioedema [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning (Fetal Toxicity):** Exposure to ACE inhibitors during the second and third trimesters can cause significant fetal injury or death. Discontinue therapy immediately upon detection of pregnancy [US].
- **Hypotension:** Excessive blood pressure reduction is rare in uncomplicated patients but may occur in those with volume or salt depletion (e.g., vigorous diuretic use, dialysis, or heart failure). Initial doses as low as 0.2 mg have caused hypotension in high-risk patients [US].
- **Angioedema:** May occur at any time during treatment and can involve the face, extremities, lips, or airway. Laryngeal edema may be fatal; involvement of the tongue or glottis requires emergency management (e.g., subcutaneous epinephrine) [US].
- **Anaphylactoid Reactions:** Reported in patients during desensitisation (hymenoptera venom) or during dialysis with high-flux membranes [US].
- **Neutropenia/Agranulocytosis:** ACE inhibitors have been associated with bone marrow depression and agranulocytosis, particularly in patients with renal impairment or collagen vascular disease [US].
- **Hepatic Failure:** Rarely, ACE inhibitors are associated with a syndrome of cholestatic jaundice progressing to fulminant hepatic necrosis. Discontinue if jaundice or significant liver enzyme elevations occur [US].
- **Monitoring:** Periodic assessment of renal function and white blood cell counts is advised in susceptible populations [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (1–10%):** Hypotension (3.4%), headache (2.9%), nausea (1.1%) [US].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Myocardial infarction, fatigue, dizziness, fever, rash, constipation [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:**
    - **Cardiovascular:** Cardiac arrest, cerebrovascular accident, pulmonary embolism, rhythm disturbances (atrial fibrillation, bradycardia) [US].
    - **Renal:** Renal failure/dysfunction, oliguria [US].
    - **Respiratory:** Bronchospasm, asthma [US].
    - **Dermatologic:** Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, exfoliative dermatitis [US].
    - **Hepatic:** Hepatitis (hepatocellular or cholestatic) [US].
- **Laboratory Findings:** Small increases in BUN and serum creatinine; hyperkalemia; hyponatremia; slight decreases in hemoglobin/hematocrit [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Diuretics:** Potential for additive hypotensive effects. Close monitoring is required for at least one hour after the first dose [US].
- **NSAIDs:** Co-administration in patients with compromised renal function may lead to further renal deterioration. NSAIDs may also blunt the antihypertensive effect of ACE inhibitors [US].
- **Lithium:** Increased serum lithium levels and risk of toxicity have been reported [US].
- **Potassium-Sparing Agents/Supplements:** Risk of significant increases in serum potassium. Use with caution and frequent monitoring [US].
- **Gold (Sodium Aurothiomalate):** Nitritoid reactions (flushing, nausea, vomiting, hypotension) have been reported rarely [US].
- **Other Agents:** Enalaprilat has been used safely with digitalis, beta-blockers, methyldopa, nitrates, and calcium channel blockers without significant interactions [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category C (First Trimester):** Use with caution. 
    - **US Category D (Second and Third Trimesters):** Known risk of fetal/neonatal injury, including skull hypoplasia, anuria, renal failure, and death [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Both enalapril and enalaprilat are excreted in human milk. Caution should be exercised, balancing the importance of the drug to the mother against the potential risk to the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on reproductive performance were observed in rat studies at doses 26 times the human daily dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in children have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Dosing should be cautious, generally starting at the low end of the range due to increased frequency of decreased organ function and concomitant disease [US].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - CrCl > 30 mL/min: Standard dose (1.25 mg q6h) [US].
    - CrCl ≤ 30 mL/min (Serum Creatinine ≥ 3 mg/dL): Initial dose of 0.625 mg. If response is inadequate after one hour, the dose may be repeated [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Enalaprilat is the active metabolite of the prodrug enalapril. It inhibits ACE, preventing the conversion of angiotensin I to the vasoconstrictor angiotensin II. This results in decreased vasopressor activity and reduced aldosterone secretion [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Reduces supine and standing blood pressure without a significant orthostatic component. Duration of action is typically around six hours [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Metabolism:** Administered as the active form (not a prodrug). 
    - **Distribution:** Poorly crosses the blood-brain barrier. 
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal; over 90% is excreted unchanged in urine within 24 hours. The effective half-life for accumulation is approximately 11 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Controlled trials involved 349 patients (hypertension, congestive heart failure, and coronary artery disease). In hypertensive patients, hypotension occurred in 0.9% and syncope in 0.5% after the initial dose [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Stability:** Once mixed with compatible diluents, the solution maintains full activity for 24 hours at room temperature [US].
- **Handling:** Inspect visually for particulate matter and discoloration before use [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** Rx-only [US].
