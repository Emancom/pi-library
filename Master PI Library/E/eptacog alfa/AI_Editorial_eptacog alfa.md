# Unified Product Information: Eptacog Alfa (Activated)

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** NovoSeven® RT
**Active Ingredient:** Eptacog alfa (activated)

Eptacog alfa (activated) is a recombinant coagulation factor VIIa (rFVIIa), a vitamin K-dependent glycoprotein. It is produced by genetic engineering using baby hamster kidney (BHK) cells. The protein is structurally and functionally similar to human plasma-derived activated Factor VII.

## 2. Approved Indications
NovoSeven RT is indicated for the treatment of bleeding episodes and the prevention of surgical complications in the following patient populations:

*   **Hemophilia A or B with Inhibitors:** Patients with inhibitors to coagulation Factors VIII or IX.
*   **Congenital Factor VII Deficiency:** Patients with a quantitative or qualitative deficiency in FVII.
*   **Glanzmann’s Thrombasthenia:** Patients with antibodies to GPIIb-IIIa and/or HLA, who have a history of refractoriness to platelet transfusions.
*   **Acquired Hemophilia:** For the treatment of bleeding and surgical prophylaxis [FDA].

## 3. Contraindications
NovoSeven RT is contraindicated in:
*   Patients with known hypersensitivity to the active substance or any of the excipients (including sodium chloride, calcium chloride dihydrate, glycylglycine, polysorbate 80, mannitol, sucrose, methionine, and histidine).
*   Patients with a known hypersensitivity to mouse, hamster, or bovine proteins, as trace amounts may be present from the manufacturing process.

## 4. Warnings and Precautions

### Thrombotic Events (Boxed Warning [FDA])
There is a significant risk of serious arterial and venous thrombotic events following administration of NovoSeven RT. 
*   **Observation:** Patients must be monitored closely for clinical signs of thrombosis or disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC). 
*   **High-Risk Groups:** Caution is required when treating patients with a history of coronary artery disease, liver disease, sepsis, crush injury, or those post-major surgery. 
*   **Elderly:** Patients with vascular disease may be at increased risk of thrombosis when treated with high doses (e.g., 270 µg/kg) [TGA].
*   **Dose Adjustment:** If signs of untoward coagulation activation occur, the dosage should be reduced or treatment discontinued.

### Hypersensitivity
Severe allergic reactions, including anaphylaxis, have been reported. Patients should be informed of early symptoms (e.g., hives, tightness of chest, wheezing, hypotension). If an anaphylactic reaction occurs, administration must cease immediately.

### Antibody Formation
Patients with Factor VII deficiency should be monitored for the development of neutralizing antibodies (inhibitors). If the expected Factor VIIa activity levels are not achieved or bleeding is not controlled at recommended doses, antibody analysis is indicated [TGA].

### Laboratory Testing
While rFVIIa shortens prothrombin time (PT) and activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT), these tests do not correlate directly with clinical hemostasis. Dosage should be guided by clinical response rather than laboratory normalization.

## 5. Dosage and Administration
NovoSeven RT is administered via intravenous bolus injection over 2–5 minutes. It must not be mixed with infusion solutions or administered as a drip.

### Recommended Dosing Regimens
| Indication | Dosage and Frequency |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Inhibitors (VIII/IX)** | **Bleeding:** 90 µg/kg every 2–3 hours until hemostasis. For mild/moderate bleeds, a single dose of 270 µg/kg may be used in patients >18 years [TGA]. <br> **Surgery:** 90 µg/kg immediately pre-op, repeated every 2 hours during surgery, then every 2–3 hours for 1–2 days. |
| **FVII Deficiency** | 15–30 µg/kg every 4–6 hours until hemostasis is achieved. Dosing must be individualized. |
| **Glanzmann’s** | 80–120 µg/kg every 2.5 hours (minimum 3 doses) to secure hemostasis. |
| **Acquired Hemophilia** | 70–90 µg/kg every 2–3 hours until hemostasis is achieved [FDA]. |

**Pediatric Use:** Children exhibit faster clearance than adults. Paediatric patients may require higher or more frequent doses to achieve therapeutic plasma concentrations [TGA].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most common and serious adverse reactions are related to the vascular system:
*   **Vascular:** Arterial thromboembolic events (myocardial infarction, cerebral infarction), venous thromboembolic events (deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism), and portal vein thrombosis.
*   **General:** Pyrexia, injection site pain, and decreased therapeutic response.
*   **Neurological:** Headache and, rarely, cerebrovascular accident.
*   **Skin:** Rash, pruritus, and urticaria.
*   **Post-marketing:** Reports of anaphylaxis, flushing, and angioedema.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Coagulation Concentrates:** Simultaneous use of prothrombin complex concentrates (activated or non-activated) should be avoided due to the risk of a synergistic thrombotic effect.
*   **rFXIII:** Combining rFVIIa with rFXIII is not recommended; non-clinical studies in primates have shown exaggerated pharmacology (thrombosis/death) with this combination [TGA].
*   **Antifibrinolytics:** Use with aminocaproic acid or tranexamic acid may reduce blood loss in surgery (especially oral/orthopedic), but clinical data on combined use is limited.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
**Pregnancy (Category B1):** Animal reproduction studies have shown no evidence of impaired fertility or harm to the fetus. However, there are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. NovoSeven RT should be used during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk.

**Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if rFVIIa is excreted in human milk. Caution should be exercised, weighing the benefits of breastfeeding for the child against the medical necessity of treatment for the mother.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** The mean steady-state volume of distribution ranges from 130 to 165 mL/kg.
*   **Elimination:** The terminal half-life is approximately 2.3 to 6 hours in adults.
*   **Special Populations:** Clearance is approximately 50% higher in pediatric patients (2–12 years) compared to adults, necessitating weight-based dosing and potentially higher frequency. Clearance is also increased in patients with FVII deficiency compared to those with Hemophilia A/B.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
NovoSeven RT works through two primary pathways:
1.  **Tissue Factor (TF) Dependent:** At the site of injury, rFVIIa complexes with exposed TF to activate Factors IX and X, initiating the coagulation cascade.
2.  **TF-Independent (Pharmacological Doses):** High doses of rFVIIa directly activate Factor X on the surface of activated platelets. This "bypass" mechanism leads to a "thrombin burst," converting fibrinogen to fibrin and forming a stable hemostatic plug, even in the absence of Factors VIII or IX.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Unopened Vials:** Store below 25°C. Do not freeze. Protect from light.
*   **Reconstituted Solution:** Should ideally be used immediately. If necessary, it can be stored for 24 hours at 2°C – 8°C (refrigerated) if kept in the vial.
*   **Shelf Life:** 3 years for the product as packed for sale [TGA]. Do not use after the expiry date.
