# Editorial Product Information: Levodopa/Carbidopa/Entacapone

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the following jurisdictional Product Information (PI) source: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- **Note:** At the time of this publication, only the Australian PI for the brand **Carlevent** was provided for review. Consequently, this document reflects Australian regulatory positions and clinical guidelines. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. It serves as a clinical synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Levodopa, Carbidopa (as monohydrate), and Entacapone.
- **Chemical Class:** Dopamine precursor (Levodopa), peripheral dopa decarboxylase inhibitor (Carbidopa), and reversible, peripherally acting catechol-O-methyltransferase (COMT) inhibitor (Entacapone).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Carlevent** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N04BA03.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets in a fixed 4:1 ratio of Levodopa/Carbidopa, each containing a fixed 200 mg dose of Entacapone [Australian]:
        - 50 mg/12.5 mg/200 mg
        - 75 mg/18.75 mg/200 mg
        - 100 mg/25 mg/200 mg
        - 125 mg/31.25 mg/200 mg
        - 150 mg/37.5 mg/200 mg
        - 200 mg/50 mg/200 mg
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- Management of patients with Parkinson’s disease who are experiencing motor fluctuations [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** Dosage must be individually titrated for each patient to achieve optimal clinical response. Patients should take only one tablet per dose [Australian].
- **Maximum Daily Dose:** 
    - The maximum recommended daily dose of entacapone is 2000 mg (10 tablets per day).
    - For the 200/50/200 mg strength, the maximum dose is 7 tablets per day (due to a 375 mg daily limit for carbidopa).
    - Total daily levodopa dose should not exceed 1500 mg [Australian].
- **Administration:** May be taken with or without food. Tablets must be swallowed whole [Australian].
- **Transferring Therapy:**
    - **From standard-release Levodopa/DDC inhibitor and Entacapone:** Patients currently on equivalent doses may be directly transferred. If doses do not match current strengths, titrate carefully to match the current total daily levodopa dose [Australian].
    - **From Levodopa/Benserazide:** Discontinue previous therapy the night before and start this combination the following morning, using an equivalent or slightly higher (5–10%) dose of levodopa [Australian].
    - **Patients not currently on Entacapone:** May be initiated at doses corresponding to current levodopa treatment. If the patient has dyskinesias or a daily levodopa dose >800 mg, it is recommended to introduce entacapone as a separate medication first before switching to the triple combination [Australian].
- **Dose Adjustments:** If less levodopa is required, decrease frequency or strength. If more is required, increase frequency or use a higher strength [Australian].
- **Discontinuation:** If treatment is stopped, other antiparkinsonian therapies (especially levodopa) must be adjusted to prevent loss of symptom control and risks associated with abrupt withdrawal (see Section 5) [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to levodopa, carbidopa, entacapone, or any excipients [Australian].
- Pregnancy and breastfeeding [Australian].
- Severe hepatic impairment [Australian].
- Narrow-angle glaucoma [Australian].
- Phaeochromocytoma (risk of hypertensive crisis) [Australian].
- Concomitant use of non-selective MAO inhibitors (e.g., phenelzine, tranylcypromine) or a combination of selective MAO-A and MAO-B inhibitors [Australian].
- Previous history of Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS) or non-traumatic rhabdomyolysis [Australian].
- Suspicious undiagnosed skin lesions or a history of malignant melanoma (levodopa may activate melanoma) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **General Precautions:** Not recommended for drug-induced extrapyramidal reactions. Use with caution in patients with ischemic heart disease, severe cardiovascular/pulmonary disease, bronchial asthma, renal/hepatic/endocrine disease, or history of peptic ulcer/convulsions [Australian].
- **NMS and Rhabdomyolysis:** Abrupt withdrawal or dose reduction may trigger a syndrome resembling NMS (rigidity, hyperthermia, mental changes, elevated CK). Rhabdomyolysis may occur secondary to NMS or severe dyskinesia [Australian].
- **Mental Health:** Monitor for depression, suicidal tendencies, and serious antisocial behaviour. Use with caution in patients with a history of psychosis [Australian].
- **Impulse Control Disorders:** Monitor for pathological gambling, increased libido, hypersexuality, compulsive spending, and binge eating [Australian].
- **Somnolence and Sleep:** Entacapone/levodopa is associated with somnolence and sudden sleep onset. Patients must exercise caution when driving or operating machinery [Australian].
- **Orthostatic Hypotension:** May induce postural hypotension; use cautiously with other antihypertensives [Australian].
- **Diarrhoea and Colitis:** Prolonged diarrhoea may be a sign of colitis. Monitor weight and discontinue if symptoms persist [Australian].
- **Fibrotic Complications:** Though more common with ergot derivatives, retroperitoneal fibrosis and pulmonary infiltrates have been reported rarely [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Periodic evaluation of hepatic, haematopoietic, cardiovascular, and renal function is recommended during extended therapy [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorized by frequency [Australian]:
- **Very Common (≥10%):** Dyskinesia (25.2%), Nausea (13.8%), Muscle/musculoskeletal pain, Diarrhoea (10.0%).
- **Common (1–10%):** Parkinsonism aggravated (13.9%), Hyperkinesia, Hypokinesia, Dizziness, Abdominal pain, Constipation, Fatigue, Hallucinations, Insomnia, Urine colour abnormal (reddish-brown), Sweating increased, Dyspepsia, Urinary tract infection.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Myocardial infarction and other ischemic heart disease events have been reported (incidence ~0.43% to 1.54%). Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome, rhabdomyolysis, and angioedema are rare but serious risks [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Antihypertensives:** Risk of symptomatic postural hypotension [Australian].
- **Antidepressants:** Hypertension and dyskinesia reported with tricyclics. No interactions observed with imipramine or moclobemide in single-dose studies [Australian].
- **MAO Inhibitors:** Non-selective inhibitors are contraindicated. Selegiline (MAO-B inhibitor) may be used, but the daily dose must not exceed 10 mg [Australian].
- **Warfarin:** Entacapone may increase R-warfarin AUC. Monitor INR when initiating therapy [Australian].
- **Dopamine D2 Antagonists:** (e.g., phenothiazines, risperidone) may reduce the therapeutic effect of levodopa [Australian].
- **Iron Preparations:** Levodopa and entacapone may chelate iron in the GI tract. Space doses by at least 2–3 hours [Australian].
- **COMT-Metabolised Drugs:** (e.g., adrenaline, dopamine, dobutamine, methyldopa) Entacapone may potentiate their action; use with caution regardless of route of administration [Australian].
- **Food:** High protein diets may impair levodopa absorption [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category B3**. Adequate data in pregnant women are unavailable. Animal studies show visceral and skeletal malformations (levodopa) and foetotoxicity (entacapone). Use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Carbidopa and entacapone are excreted in animal milk. Safety in humans is unknown. Breastfeeding is not recommended [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no effects on fertility at high exposures [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for patients under 18 years due to lack of safety/efficacy data [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific dose adjustment is required for patients under 75. Levodopa and entacapone AUC are slightly higher (10–20%) in the elderly (60–75 years) [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use cautiously in severe renal impairment, including those on dialysis. Longer dosing intervals may be required [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe hepatic impairment. Administer with caution in mild-to-moderate cases; dose reduction may be necessary [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Levodopa:** Crosses the blood-brain barrier and is converted to dopamine in the striatum. 
    - **Carbidopa:** Inhibits peripheral decarboxylation of levodopa, increasing its availability to the brain and reducing systemic side effects (nausea). 
    - **Entacapone:** A reversible COMT inhibitor that prevents the peripheral metabolism of levodopa to 3-OMD, increasing the AUC and prolonging the clinical response of levodopa [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Rapid but variable. Levodopa bioavailability is 15–33%, Entacapone 29–36%.
    - **Distribution:** Levodopa protein binding 10–30%; Entacapone is 98% bound (mainly to albumin).
    - **Metabolism:** Levodopa is metabolised via DDC and COMT. Carbidopa is metabolised to two main metabolites. Entacapone undergoes glucuronidation.
    - **Elimination:** Levodopa t½ 0.6–1.3h; Carbidopa t½ 2–3h; Entacapone t½ 0.4–0.7h [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **NOMECOMT and SEESAW Trials:** Two pivotal Phase III studies in 376 patients with Parkinson's disease and motor fluctuations.
- **Outcomes:** Entacapone significantly increased mean daily "ON" time (1.3 hours in NOMECOMT; 0.6 hours in SEESAW) and reduced daily levodopa requirements by approximately 100 mg/day compared to placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C [Australian].
- Shelf life is specified on individual product packaging [Australian].
