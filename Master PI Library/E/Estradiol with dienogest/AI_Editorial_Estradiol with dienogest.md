# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following official Product Information (PI) source:
    - **US (FDA):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine Injection for healthcare professionals. It synthesises available regulatory data into a single clinical reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine [INN], a carrier molecule involved in long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (trimethylammonium).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter). [US]
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Clear, colorless sterile solution for injection. Available as 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials. [US]
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation. [US]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients presenting with secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn errors of metabolism. [US]
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis. [US]

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Administration should be via slow intravenous bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion. [US]

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg.
- **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. 
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less frequently than every 6 hours. [US]
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically remain at 50 mg/kg or as clinically required. The highest recorded dose administered is 300 mg/kg. [US]

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). [US]
- **Timing:** Administer after each dialysis session into the venous return line. [US]
- **Adjustments:** Trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels should guide dose adjustments. Levels below 40–50 µmol/L may prompt initiation or increase. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be implemented as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations. [US]

### Monitoring
- Pre-treatment plasma carnitine levels are recommended.
- Periodic monitoring (weekly/monthly) should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L). [US]

## 4. Contraindications
- None known. [US]

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Hypersensitivity Reactions
- **Serious Reactions:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. [US]
- **Management:** Discontinue treatment immediately and initiate medical intervention. Risk-benefit analysis is required before re-administration; if re-challenged, monitor closely for recurrence. [US]

### Neurologic Reactions (Seizures)
- Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed. [US]

### General Precautions
- **Oral Formulation Note:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been established in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]), which are normally renally excreted. [US]

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. [US]
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy. [US]

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- **Risk Summary:** No evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm was observed in rat and rabbit studies at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area). There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. [US]
- **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed. [US]

### Breastfeeding
- **Risk Summary:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Animal data (dairy cows) indicate carnitine concentrations in milk increase following administration. [US]
- **Recommendation:** Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of maternal supplementation. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered. [US]

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosage and administration for infants and children are included in the general metabolic disorder recommendations (50 mg/kg). Secondary deficiency in premature infants and newborns is defined as plasma levels below age-related norms. [US]
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** See **Dosage and Administration** for ESRD-specific protocols. Safety of the *oral* formulation in renal impairment is not established. [US]
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted. [US]

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. [US]
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces). [US]
- **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. [US]
- **Bioavailability:** Absolute bioavailability of oral formulations is low (~15–16%). [US]

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** Efficacy was demonstrated in a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients where 20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis significantly increased plasma carnitine concentrations. Trough levels remained stable or increased over the study duration. Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) confirmed a dose-dependent increase in trough concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks. [US]
- **Clinical Outcomes Note:** While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations is proven, the effects on specific clinical outcomes or signs/symptoms of deficiency in ESRD populations have not been definitively determined. [US]

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). [US]
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat. [US]
- **Stability:** Once opened, discard any unused portion (preservative-free). Compatible and stable for 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL). [US]
