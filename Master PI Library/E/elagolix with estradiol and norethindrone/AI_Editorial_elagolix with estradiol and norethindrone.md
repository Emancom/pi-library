# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises the clinical and regulatory information available from the provided source.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved labelling before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP), an amino acid derivative and carrier molecule involved in mitochondrial fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources (A16AA01).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection solution: 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
    - Subsequent daily doses are typically maintained at 50 mg/kg or adjusted based on clinical requirements. Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - Therapy initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels below the normal range of 40 µmol/L to 50 µmol/L [US].

### Administration and Monitoring
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations and monitor weekly and monthly. Monitoring should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine range: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Dose Adjustments:** In ESRD patients, downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week based on trough concentrations [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable and compatible for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) when mixed in Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's in concentrations from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL in PVC bags [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** No contraindications are currently listed in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If severe reactions occur, treatment must be discontinued immediately and appropriate medical management initiated [US].
- **Oral Levocarnitine Caution:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]), which are normally excreted in urine [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is used concurrently with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating treatment or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned (narrative only) [US]. 
    - Animal reproductive studies in rats and rabbits using doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. However, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response, use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. Clinicians should weigh the benefits of maternal supplementation against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant; discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg body weight; see Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Evaluated extensively in ESRD patients on dialysis (see Section 3). Use of oral formulations is cautioned due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production (ATP). It also assists in clearing accumulated acyl-CoA esters by forming acylcarnitine, which is rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (primarily in faeces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours, and the mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
    - **Bioavailability:** Absolute bioavailability of oral formulations (tablets/solution) is approximately 15–16% [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg post-dialysis three times per week, plasma levocarnitine concentrations increased from a pre-dialysis baseline of 21.3 $\pm$ 7.7 µmol/L to 190 $\pm$ 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks. Trough levels remained stable or increased over the study period [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations at 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and avoid excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
