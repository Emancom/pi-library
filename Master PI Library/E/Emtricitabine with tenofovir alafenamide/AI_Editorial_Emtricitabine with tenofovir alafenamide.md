## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US]; Manufactured by Baxter Pharmaceuticals India Private Ltd [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow (2 to 3 minute) bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often utilized in patients presenting with severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours, and never less frequently than every 6 hours, whether by infusion or intravenous injection [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg or as clinically required. The maximum reported dose is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Monitoring
- It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. If severe, discontinue treatment and monitor for recurrence if re-administration is attempted [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** The safety/efficacy of oral levocarnitine in patients with renal insufficiency has not been established. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Visual Inspection:** Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration before use [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **General:** Transient nausea and vomiting. Less frequent effects include body odor and gastritis [US].
- **Hemodialysis Trials (Incidence ≥5%):**
    - **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (5–21%), headache (3–37%), injection site reaction (27–38%), pain (21–35%), fever (5–12%) [US].
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%) [US].
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%) [US].
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%) [US].
    - **Neurological:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concurrently with levocarnitine. Monitoring of INR is recommended upon initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine in patients on anticoagulant therapy [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine is not protein-bound [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specifically lettered in source, but stated as follows]: Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine levels after exogenous administration. Weigh the benefits to the mother against the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Refer to the "Dosage and Administration" section; specific pediatric age-bracket adjustments are not further detailed beyond weight-based dosing [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** See "Dosage and Administration" for ESRD patients. Note that oral levocarnitine carries a risk of metabolite (TMA/TMAO) accumulation in severe renal impairment [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule essential for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, it alleviates metabolic abnormalities caused by the accumulation of toxic organic acids and corrects carnitine deficiency (defined as <20 µmol/L) [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound; distribution half-life is approximately 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are TMAO (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg post-dialysis showed that trough concentrations reached 54–180 µmol/L within one week (normal range: 40–50 µmol/L).
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** In ESRD patients receiving 10, 20, or 40 mg/kg, mean trough concentrations at 24 weeks were 148, 240, and 456 µmol/L, respectively.
- **Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the impact on specific clinical outcomes in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations: 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
