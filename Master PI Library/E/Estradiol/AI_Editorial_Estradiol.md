# Editorial Product Information: Estradiol and Drospirenone

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial product information is derived from the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the clinical properties of **Angeliq 1/2** (estradiol/drospirenone) for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial summary and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI or the specific prescribing regulations within a particular jurisdiction. For precise prescribing, refer to the current approved documentation in your local region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Estradiol (as hemihydrate) and Drospirenone.
- **Chemical Class:** Estrogen and synthetic progestogen (spironolactone analogue).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Angeliq 1/2** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available source.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Film-coated tablets containing 1.0 mg estradiol and 2.0 mg drospirenone [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Bayer Australia Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hormone Replacement Therapy (HRT):** Short-term treatment of climacteric syndrome in postmenopausal women with an intact uterus, caused by deficient endogenous estrogen due to natural menopause, hypogonadism, castration, or primary ovarian failure [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** One tablet taken daily.
- **Regimen:** Continuous combined treatment. Blister packs contain 28 days of therapy; the next pack should be started immediately without a break.
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets should be swallowed whole with liquid, regardless of food intake. It is preferable to take the dose at the same time each day.
- **Starting Therapy:**
    - Women not currently on HRT or changing from a continuous combination product may start at any time.
    - Women changing from sequential HRT should start at the end of their scheduled bleeding.
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** If a tablet is forgotten, it should be taken as soon as possible. If more than 24 hours have elapsed, no extra tablet is required. If multiple tablets are missed, vaginal bleeding may occur.
- **Clinical Recommendation:** HRT should be prescribed at the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration necessary to meet treatment goals.

## 4. Contraindications
- Undiagnosed vaginal bleeding.
- Known, suspected, or history of breast cancer.
- Known or suspected sex-steroid-influenced malignancies or pre-malignant conditions.
- Presence or history of liver tumours (benign or malignant).
- Severe hepatic disease.
- Severe renal disease (until renal function returns to normal).
- Acute arterial thromboembolism (e.g., myocardial infarction, stroke).
- Active deep venous thrombosis (DVT), pulmonary embolism (PE), or a documented history of these conditions.
- High risk of venous or arterial thrombosis.
- Severe hypertriglyceridemia.
- Pregnancy or lactation.
- Known hypersensitivity to the active ingredients or any excipients.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [Australian]:** Estrogens with or without progestogens should not be used for the prevention of cardiovascular disease or dementia. Data from the Women’s Health Initiative (WHI) study indicated increased risks of stroke, DVT, MI, and invasive breast cancer. The WHI Memory Study (WHIMS) reported an increased risk of probable dementia in women aged 65 and older.
- **Venous Thromboembolism (VTE):** HRT is associated with a higher relative risk of developing DVT or PE. Risk is highest in the first year of use. Discontinue therapy if a thrombotic event is suspected.
- **Breast Cancer:** Increased risk reported with several years of use, which increases with duration and returns to baseline within a few years after stopping. HRT may increase mammographic density, potentially hindering radiological detection of cancer.
- **Endometrial Cancer:** Progestogens (like drospirenone) are added to estrogens in women with an intact uterus to reduce the risk of endometrial hyperplasia and carcinoma associated with unopposed estrogen.
- **Other Malignancies:** A slight increase in the risk of ovarian cancer has been noted in epidemiological studies with long-term use.
- **Gallbladder Disease:** Estrogens may increase the lithogenicity of bile.
- **Hepatic Function:** Discontinue if markers of liver function deteriorate or if cholestatic jaundice/pruritus recurs.
- **Hypertriglyceridemia:** Women with pre-existing elevated triglycerides require close monitoring due to the risk of pancreatitis.
- **Monitoring:** Prior to initiation and periodically thereafter, a full medical history and physical examination (including blood pressure, breasts, abdomen, and pelvic organs/cervical cytology) are required.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Breast pain (reported in approximately 20% of users).
- **Common (1–10%):**
    - **Nervous system:** Headache, mood swings, hot flushes.
    - **Digestive:** Nausea, abdominal pain/bloating.
    - **Body as a whole:** Asthenia, pain in extremities.
    - **Urogenital:** Uterine fibroid enlargement, leucorrhoea, cervical neoplasm.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):**
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension, migraine, venous thrombosis, palpitations, varicose veins.
    - **Nervous:** Dizziness, insomnia, decreased libido, anxiety, vertigo.
    - **Metabolic:** Weight gain, hyperlipidaemia, oedema.
    - **Skin:** Alopecia, hirsutism, breast carcinoma.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Arterial and venous thromboembolic events, invasive breast cancer, and probable dementia in women >65 years.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Enzyme Inducers:** Medicines that induce microsomal enzymes (e.g., phenytoin, carbamazepine, rifampicin, St. John’s wort) can increase the clearance of sex hormones, potentially reducing efficacy and changing the uterine bleeding profile.
- **Enzyme Inhibitors:** Strong/moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., azole antifungals, macrolides, verapamil, grapefruit juice) may increase plasma concentrations of estradiol and/or drospirenone.
- **Alcohol:** Acute ingestion may lead to elevated circulating estradiol levels.
- **Antihypertensives:** Drospirenone has antimineralocorticoid activity. Co-administration with antihypertensive medications may result in an additional decrease in blood pressure.
- **Potassium-Sparing Agents:** Concomitant use with NSAIDs or antihypertensives may cause a small increase in serum potassium, particularly in diabetic women.
- **Anticoagulants:** Antagonism of effectiveness may occur.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3 [Australian]**. Contraindicated. If pregnancy occurs, treatment must be discontinued immediately. Animal studies showed embryolethality and interference with sexual differentiation (feminisation of male fetuses and virilisation of female fetuses) at high doses.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Small amounts of drospirenone and estradiol are excreted in breast milk. Estrogens can decrease the quantity and quality of milk.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not indicated for use in children or adolescents.
- **Elderly Use:** Higher risk of probable dementia in women >65 years [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe renal disease. Drospirenone exposure was slightly higher in moderate impairment (37% increase) but not considered clinically relevant.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe hepatic disease or liver tumours. Drospirenone terminal half-life was 1.8 times higher in moderate impairment.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Estradiol:** Replaces deficient estrogen in postmenopausal women, alleviating vasomotor and organic symptoms.
    - **Drospirenone:** A synthetic progestogen that opposes estrogen-induced endometrial hyperplasia. It possesses antimineralocorticoid activity (1 mg is equivalent to approximately 7 mg spironolactone) and antiandrogenic properties.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Drospirenone is rapidly absorbed (Tmax ~1h); bioavailability 76-85%. Estradiol undergoes extensive first-pass metabolism; absolute bioavailability is ~5%.
    - **Distribution:** Drospirenone binds to albumin; estradiol binds to SHBG and albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Drospirenone is metabolised via CYP3A4 and acid forms. Estradiol is metabolised to estrone and estriol.
    - **Elimination:** Drospirenone half-life is ~35-39h. Estradiol half-life is ~13-20h. Both are excreted via urine and faeces.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Vasomotor Symptoms (AR98):** A Phase III trial showed that **Angeliq 1/2** was significantly more effective than placebo in reducing the frequency and severity of hot flushes over 16 weeks.
- **Bone Mineral Density (AR99):** Treatment resulted in a significant increase in hip BMD (mean change ~3.9% in osteopenic patients) compared to a loss in the placebo group over 104 weeks.
- **Endometrial Safety (A17844):** Re-evaluation of biopsy samples confirmed that the addition of drospirenone effectively protected the endometrium, with very low rates of hyperplasia compared to estrogen monotherapy.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C.
- **Shelf Life:** The expiry date is marked on the packaging.
- **Handling:** Keep out of reach of children. Unused material should be disposed of via local pharmacy programs [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian]**.
