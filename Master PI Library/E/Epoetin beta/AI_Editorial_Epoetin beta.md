# Editorial Product Information: Methoxy polyethylene glycol-epoetin beta (Mircera)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **methoxy polyethylene glycol-epoetin beta** for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single, structured reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI or prescribing information required for practice within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved documentation before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Methoxy polyethylene glycol-epoetin beta (a chemically synthesised erythropoiesis stimulating agent).
- **Brand Name:** **Mircera** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** B03XA03.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Solution for injection in pre-filled syringes.
    - Available strengths: 30 mcg/0.3 mL, 50 mcg/0.3 mL, 75 mcg/0.3 mL, 100 mcg/0.3 mL, 120 mcg/0.3 mL, 150 mcg/0.3 mL, 200 mcg/0.3 mL, 250 mcg/0.3 mL, and 360 mcg/0.6 mL [Australian]. 
    - *Note:* The 150 mcg and 250 mcg strengths may not be distributed in all regions [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) or Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Roche Products Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- Treatment of anaemia associated with chronic kidney disease (CKD) [Australian].
- **Important Limitation:** Safety and efficacy have not been established for the treatment of anaemia in the peri-surgical setting or anaemia due to cancer chemotherapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Principles
Treatment should be initiated under specialist supervision. The objective is to use the lowest dose required to provide adequate symptom control. Haemoglobin (Hb) levels should be monitored closely, aiming for a target range of 100 to 120 g/L [Australian].

### Adult Patients Not Currently Treated with an ESA
- **Patients not on dialysis:** 1.2 mcg/kg body weight administered once monthly via SC injection. Alternatively, 0.6 mcg/kg may be given once every two weeks via SC or IV injection.
- **Patients on dialysis:** 0.6 mcg/kg body weight administered once every two weeks via SC or IV injection.

### Adult Patients Converting from Another ESA
Patients may be converted from epoetin or darbepoetin alfa to **Mircera** once monthly or once every two weeks. The starting dose is determined by the previous weekly dose of the prior ESA at the time of conversion.

**Table 1: Conversion Starting Dose (Monthly)**
| Previous Weekly Darbepoetin Alfa (mcg) | Previous Weekly Epoetin (Units) | Mircera Starting Dose (mcg/month) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| < 40 | < 8,000 | 120 |
| 40–80 | 8,000–16,000 | 200 |
| > 80 | > 16,000 | 360 |

### Dose Adjustments and Titration
- **Frequency:** Adjustments should not occur more than once monthly.
- **Dose Increase:** If the Hb rise is < 10 g/L over one month, the dose may be increased by approximately 25%.
- **Dose Reduction:** If Hb exceeds 120 g/L, or the rate of rise is > 20 g/L in a month, or if Hb increases by > 10 g/L in any 2-week period, reduce the dose by approximately 25%.
- **Withholding Dose:** If Hb continues to rise despite reduction, withhold therapy until Hb begins to decrease, then restart at ~25% below the previous dose.

### Administration Notes
- SC injection can be performed in the abdomen, arm, or thigh.
- If a dose is missed, administer it as soon as possible and resume the prescribed schedule.

## 4. Contraindications
- Uncontrolled hypertension [Australian].
- Known hypersensitivity to the active substance or any excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiovascular and Thrombotic Events:** Increased risk of serious events (myocardial infarction, stroke, heart failure, and graft occlusion) particularly when ESAs are used to target Hb > 120 g/L or when Hb rises too rapidly (> 10 g/L in 2 weeks).
- **Hypertension:** Blood pressure must be controlled prior to and during therapy. Hypertensive encephalopathy and seizures have been reported.
- **Pure Red Cell Aplasia (PRCA):** Neutralising anti-erythropoietin antibodies can cause PRCA. If PRCA is suspected, ESA therapy must be discontinued immediately. Patients should not be switched to other erythropoietic proteins as antibodies cross-react.
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** SJS and TEN have been reported, primarily with long-acting epoetins. If SCARs occur, **Mircera** must be permanently discontinued.
- **Oncology Use:** **Mircera** is not indicated for cancer patients. ESAs have been shown to shorten time to tumour progression and/or survival in various malignancies when Hb targets exceed 120 g/L.
- **Supplementation:** Adequate iron stores are required for effective erythropoiesis. Supplemental iron is recommended if serum ferritin is < 100 mcg/L or transferrin saturation is < 20% [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Approximately 8% of CKD patients treated with **Mircera** experience adverse reactions.

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common** (≥1/100 to <1/10) | Hypertension |
| **Uncommon** (≥1/1,000 to <1/100) | Headache, vascular access thrombosis |
| **Rare** (≥1/10,000 to <1/1,000) | Hypersensitivity, hypertensive encephalopathy, hot flush, maculo-papular rash |
| **Unknown** | Anaphylactic reaction, SJS/TEN |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Thromboembolic events, PRCA, and severe cutaneous reactions. A slight decrease in platelet counts (within normal range) is common (~9% of patients fall below 100 x 10⁹/L) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- No formal interaction studies have been performed.
- Clinical trial data and population pharmacokinetic analyses indicate no significant interactions with concomitant medications commonly used in CKD patients [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **[Australian Category: B3]**. Animal studies indicate reduced foetal weight and increased post-implantation loss at exposures 1–3 times clinical levels. Use only if potential benefit outweighs foetal risk.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if the drug is excreted in human milk. Animal data show excretion in rat milk with associated adverse effects on offspring (reduced weight gain, increased mortality). A decision must be made to either discontinue breastfeeding or the drug.
- **Fertility:** No effect on fertility or sperm parameters was observed in animal models at 6 times human exposure.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for patients < 18 years of age due to lack of safety and efficacy data [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment is required for patients aged 65 years or older.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment of starting dose or modification rules is required for patients with any degree of hepatic impairment [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosing is specifically for CKD; however, the pharmacokinetic profile is similar between dialysis and non-dialysis patients.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A continuous erythropoietin receptor activator. It has a lower affinity for the erythropoietin receptor compared to recombinant epoetin, resulting in slower association and faster dissociation. This, combined with reduced receptor-mediated endocytosis, leads to a significantly longer half-life.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability of 62% (dialysis) and 54% (non-dialysis) after SC administration. Peak serum levels occur at 72–95 hours post-SC dose.
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is approximately 5 L.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is ~134 hours (IV) and ~139–142 hours (SC). Clearance is not affected by haemodialysis.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Onset of Hb increase is observed 7 to 15 days following a single dose.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Correction of Anaemia:** Pivotal trials (CORDATUS, ARCTOS, AMICUS) demonstrated that **Mircera** was non-inferior to darbepoetin alfa and epoetin in achieving Hb response in patients not previously on ESAs. Time to response was typically longer with **Mircera**.
- **Maintenance of Hb:** Trials (MAXIMA, PROTOS, STRIATA, RUBRA) showed that patients switching from other ESAs successfully maintained Hb levels within the target range (110–130 g/L in study protocols) when using once-fortnightly or once-monthly **Mircera** [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (refrigerate). Do not freeze.
- **Light:** Keep the syringe in the outer carton to protect from light.
- **Stability:** The end user may keep the product at room temperature (up to 30°C) for a single period of up to 1 month. Once removed from refrigeration, it must not be returned to the fridge and must be used within that month [Australian].
- **Handling:** Do not shake. Allow the product to reach room temperature before injection.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
