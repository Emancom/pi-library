# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial was developed based on official Product Information from the United States (US Food and Drug Administration). Note: While the user instructions referenced Australian sources for glycopyrronium, the provided source documentation pertains exclusively to **Levocarnitine Injection** as approved in the US.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a single clinical reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (L-carnitine).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Generic/Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2- to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the next 24 hours. Dosing should be administered every 3 or 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) [US].
    - Subsequent daily doses range typically around 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** 
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow 2- to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - Dosing initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations <40–50 µmol/L [US].

### Administration and Monitoring
- **Monitoring:** Plasma carnitine concentrations should be obtained prior to therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Dose Adjustments:** In ESRD, downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough levels [US].
- **Preparation:** Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of intravenous administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and do not re-administer without careful risk-benefit assessment and monitoring [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Accumulation in Renal Impairment:** While IV levocarnitine is used in ESRD, chronic high-dose **oral** levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalised Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels in patients on warfarin therapy following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category B equivalent]: Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. However, adequate, well-controlled studies in humans are absent. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations following exogenous administration. In nursing mothers, the risk of excess carnitine intake by the infant must be weighed against the benefit to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is similar to adult dosing (50 mg/kg). Safety and efficacy in ESRD pediatric patients have not been specifically detailed in provided sources beyond general clinical use [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific precautions noted in available sources [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** IV administration is indicated for ESRD; however, oral formulations are generally avoided in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria. This provides substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, a dose of 20 mg/kg results in a significant increase in plasma carnitine concentrations (Trough levels increase from ~21 µmol/L at baseline to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks of therapy) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly after dialysis successfully increased trough carnitine levels. While biochemical efficacy is established, the impact on specific clinical symptoms or outcomes (e.g., malaise, muscle weakness) in this population has not been definitively determined [US].
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s; stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
