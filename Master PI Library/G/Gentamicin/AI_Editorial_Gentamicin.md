# Editorial Product Information: Gentamicin

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (TGA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of gentamicin product information, synthesized for use by healthcare professionals. It integrates clinical guidance, regulatory specifications, and safety data from the available source(s).
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Gentamicin (as sulphate).
- **Chemical Class:** Aminoglycoside antibiotic.
- **Brand Names:** DBL Gentamicin Injection BP [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Sterile solution for injection/infusion: 10 mg/mL and 40 mg/mL (as 80 mg/2 mL) [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Intramuscular (IM) or Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Hospira Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Gentamicin is indicated for the treatment of severe infections caused by susceptible pathogens, including *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, *Proteus* species (indole-positive and indole-negative), *Escherichia coli*, *Klebsiella-Enterobacter-Serratia* species, and *Staphylococcus* species (coagulase-positive and coagulase-negative). Specific indications include:
- Septicaemia [Australian].
- Respiratory tract infections [Australian].
- Complicated skin, bone, and soft tissue infections (e.g., peritonitis, septic abortion, infected burns) [Australian].
- Recurrent or complicated urinary tract infections (UTI) [Australian].
- **Note:** Gentamicin is not recommended as first-line therapy for uncomplicated UTI unless the pathogen is resistant to less toxic agents [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Systemic and Severe UTI:** 3 mg/kg/day administered in divided doses every 8 hours. 
    - *Typical doses:* 80 mg for patients >60 kg; 60 mg for patients <60 kg [Australian].
- **Life-Threatening Infections:** Initial dose of 5 mg/kg/day, reduced to 3 mg/kg/day as clinically indicated, divided every 6 to 8 hours [Australian].
- **Duration:** Usually 7–10 days. Auditory, vestibular, and renal monitoring is required for longer courses [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing
Dosing is based on age and infection severity, typically divided into 8- or 12-hour intervals [Australian]:
- **0–7 days:** 5 mg/kg/day initially.
- **1 week to 1 year:** 6 mg/kg/day initially (7.5 mg/kg/day for life-threatening).
- **1 year to 12 years:** 4.5 mg/kg/day (6 mg/kg/day for life-threatening).

### Administration Instructions
- **Intramuscular:** Preferred route; dosage is identical to IV [Australian].
- **Intravenous:** Used when IM is not feasible. Dilute in 100–200 mL of 0.9% saline or 5% glucose (concentration ≤1 mg/mL). Infuse over 30 minutes to 2 hours [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Peak levels should be measured ~1 hour post-IM or 30 minutes post-IV infusion. Trough levels should be measured immediately prior to the next dose [Australian].
- **Target Levels:** Desired peaks are 4–10 µg/mL; troughs should remain below 2 µg/mL to minimize toxicity [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to gentamicin, other aminoglycosides, or any excipients (e.g., disodium edetate) [Australian].
- History of severe toxic reactions (nephrotoxicity or ototoxicity) to gentamicin or other aminoglycosides [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Neurotoxicity and Ototoxicity:** May cause irreversible vestibular and auditory damage (bilateral). Symptoms include tinnitus, dizziness, and hearing loss. Risk increases with high doses, prolonged use, and renal impairment [Australian].
- **Nephrotoxicity:** Aminoglycosides are inherently nephrotoxic. Renal function must be monitored closely, particularly in those with pre-existing impairment [Australian].
- **Neuromuscular Blockade:** Potential for respiratory paralysis, especially when used with anaesthetics or neuromuscular blockers (e.g., succinylcholine). This may be reversed by calcium salts [Australian].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Periodic assessment of renal function (BUN, creatinine, urinalysis) and auditory function (audiograms) is recommended [Australian].
- **Hydration:** Patients must maintain adequate hydration during therapy [Australian].
- **Cross-Allergenicity:** Known to occur among aminoglycosides [Australian].
- **Muscular Disorders:** Use with extreme caution in patients with myasthenia gravis or parkinsonism due to potential aggravation of muscle weakness [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
| System | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Renal** | Nephrotoxicity (casts, proteinuria, oliguria, rising BUN/creatinine) [Australian]. |
| **Neurological** | Vestibular/auditory ototoxicity, numbness, skin tingling, muscle twitching, convulsions [Australian]. |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea, vomiting, stomatitis, decreased appetite, weight loss [Australian]. |
| **Hypersensitivity** | Rash, itching, urticaria, laryngeal oedema, anaphylactoid reactions [Australian]. |
| **Laboratory** | Increased ALT/AST/bilirubin; decreased Ca, Mg, Na, K; anaemia, thrombocytopenia [Australian]. |
| **Other** | Respiratory depression, lethargy, confusion, visual disturbances, alopecia, joint pain [Australian]. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Ototoxic/Nephrotoxic Agents:** Avoid concurrent or sequential use with ethacrynic acid, frusemide, cisplatin, vancomycin, amphotericin B, or other aminoglycosides [Australian].
- **Neuromuscular Blockers:** Gentamicin enhances the effect of succinylcholine and tubocurarine, increasing the risk of apnoea [Australian].
- **Penicillins:** In vitro and in vivo inactivation of gentamicin occurs when mixed with penicillins. Do not combine in the same IV bag; stagger administration times, especially in renal failure [Australian].
- **Potent Diuretics:** May enhance toxicity by altering antibiotic concentrations in serum and tissue [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category D]. Aminoglycosides cross the placenta and can cause foetal harm, including selective uptake in the foetal kidney and potential eighth cranial nerve damage. Use only if potential benefits outweigh risks [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Gentamicin is excreted in breast milk in small amounts. While GI absorption in the infant is low, risk of ototoxicity/nephrotoxicity to the neonate cannot be excluded. Use with caution [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatrics:** Use with caution in neonates and premature infants due to renal immaturity and prolonged half-life [Australian].
- **Elderly:** Caution required due to age-related decline in renal function, which may not be fully reflected in BUN/creatinine levels. Dose reduction may be necessary [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dose intervals must be adjusted based on creatinine clearance (CrCl).
    - *Example Guidelines:* CrCl >70 mL/min (8h); 35–70 mL/min (12h); 24–34 mL/min (18h); 16–23 mL/min (24h); 10–15 mL/min (36h); 5–9 mL/min (48h) [Australian].
- **Haemodialysis:** An 8-hour session may reduce gentamicin levels by 50%. Suggested post-dialysis dose: 1 to 1.7 mg/kg [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal; inhibits protein synthesis in susceptible bacteria [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak serum levels 30–90 minutes post-IM. Poorly absorbed orally [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Detected in tissues and fluids (pleural, peritoneal, sputum). Crosses placental and peritoneal membranes; low CSF penetration [Australian].
    - **Metabolism/Elimination:** Not metabolised; excreted unchanged via glomerular filtration. Renal clearance correlates with endogenous creatinine [Australian].
