**Editorial Note:** This Unified Product Information document integrates clinical data and regulatory requirements from the Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) of Australia and the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) of the United States. It covers both parenteral (intravenous) glucose and oral formulations containing glucose, fructose, and phosphoric acid. Source-specific mandates are indicated in brackets throughout the text.

# Unified Product Information: Glucose (Dextrose)

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** Glucose (Dextrose)
**Active Ingredients:** 
*   **Parenteral:** Glucose (as $\alpha$-D(+)glucopyranose) 50% w/v [TGA].
*   **Oral:** Dextrose (Glucose) 1.87 g, Levulose (Fructose) 1.87 g, and Phosphoric Acid 21.5 mg per 5 mL [FDA].

## 2. Approved Indications
Glucose is indicated for the following clinical scenarios:
*   **Acute Hypoglycemia:** Management of severe hypoglycemic episodes, particularly those resulting from insulin overdose [TGA].
*   **Cerebral Edema and Pressure:** Reduction of intracranial pressure and cerebral edema associated with acute alcohol intoxication or delirium tremens [TGA].
*   **Nausea Relief:** Oral relief of upset stomach and nausea [FDA].
*   **Caloric Support:** Provision of a simple carbohydrate energy source [TGA].

## 3. Contraindications
Administration is contraindicated in the following conditions:
*   Diabetic coma or states of significantly elevated blood glucose [TGA].
*   Anuria, intracranial hemorrhage, or intraspinal hemorrhage [TGA].
*   Dehydrated patients suffering from delirium tremens [TGA].
*   Ischemic stroke [TGA].
*   **Hereditary Fructose Intolerance (HFI):** Must not be used in patients with HFI due to the fructose content in specific oral formulations [FDA].
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to corn, corn products [TGA], or any active/inactive ingredients in the formulation [FDA].

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
**General Metabolic Precautions:**
*   **Diabetes Mellitus:** Use with extreme caution. Patients using oral formulations should consult a physician prior to use [FDA/TGA].
*   **Nutritional Deficiencies:** Use with caution in patients with severe malnutrition or thiamine deficiency. The administration of glucose without adequate B-vitamins may precipitate Wernicke's encephalopathy in susceptible patients, such as alcoholics [TGA].
*   **Electrolyte Imbalance:** Utilization of glucose may cause intracellular shifts of potassium, magnesium, and phosphate. Monitoring and replacement may be necessary in patients with existing electrolyte deficits [TGA].

**Administration-Specific Precautions:**
*   **Hypertonicity (IV):** Concentrated glucose is strongly hypertonic and may cause dehydration if not monitored. Clinicians should observe skin turgor and hematocrit levels [TGA].
*   **Infusion Site Integrity:** To prevent venous thrombosis and phlebitis, injections should be administered slowly via a small-bore needle into a large vein. Avoid extravasation as it may lead to tissue damage [TGA].
*   **Fluid Overload:** Excessive intravenous administration can lead to fluid overload, causing electrolyte dilution and acid-base disturbances [TGA].
*   **Oral Dilution:** For maximum efficacy, oral anti-nausea liquids should not be diluted, and fluids should not be consumed immediately before or after dosing [FDA].

## 5. Dosage and Administration
### Intravenous Administration (50% Solution) [TGA]
*   **Hypoglycemic Coma (Adults):** 25–50 mL administered intravenously. Response should be evaluated after the first 50 mL. Supplemental oral carbohydrates should follow recovery to prevent relapse.
*   **Acute Alcoholism:** 50 mL administered intravenously, typically supplemented with 100 mg thiamine and 20 units of unmodified insulin.
*   **Pediatrics:** For neonates and infants, use a 10–25% glucose solution at a dose of 2 mL/kg, administered slowly.
*   **Elderly:** Dosing is consistent with adult recommendations.
*   **Administration Rate:** Should not exceed 800 mg/kg/hour to avoid glycosuria and osmotic diuresis. A recommended slow injection rate is 3 mL/minute.

### Oral Administration (Nausea Relief) [FDA]
*   **Adults and Children (12+ years):** 15 mL or 30 mL.
*   **Children (2 to 11 years):** 5 mL or 10 mL.
*   **Frequency:** Doses may be repeated every 15 minutes until symptoms subside. Do not exceed 5 doses within one hour without medical consultation.

## 6. Adverse Effects
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Rare anaphylactoid reactions have been noted, particularly in patients with comorbid asthma and diabetes [TGA].
*   **Injection Site Reactions:** Fever, infection at the site, venous thrombosis, and phlebitis. A generalized flush may occur if 25–50 g is infused too rapidly (within 3 minutes); this typically resolves within 10 minutes [TGA].
*   **Metabolic Effects:** Exacerbation of pre-existing diabetes, glycosuria, and osmotic diuresis. Severe overdosage can lead to hyperosmolar coma, mental confusion, and death [TGA].
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Worsening or persistence of nausea symptoms [FDA].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Blood Products:** Glucose solutions must not be administered through the same infusion set as blood, as this may lead to pseudo-agglutination or hemolysis [TGA].
*   **Thiamine:** As glucose metabolism exhausts thiamine stores, these agents are often administered together in alcohol-related presentations [TGA].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** Safety during pregnancy has not been definitively established. The clinical benefits must be weighed against potential fetal risks [TGA]. Patients using OTC oral products should consult a healthcare professional before use [FDA].
*   **Lactation:** The effect of concentrated glucose on breastfeeding is unknown [TGA]; consultation with a healthcare provider is recommended [FDA].

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
Glucose is a simple carbohydrate that is readily absorbed and utilized by the body. It is distributed throughout the tissues and can be stored as glycogen in the liver and muscles or converted into fat. The metabolism of one gram of glucose yields approximately 3.8 calories (16 J). The final metabolic products are carbon dioxide and water, which are excreted via the lungs and kidneys, respectively [TGA].

## 10. Mechanism of Action
*   **Metabolic:** Glucose serves as the primary energy source for cellular metabolism. It also exerts a protein-sparing effect, preventing the deamination of amino acids for energy [TGA].
*   **Osmotic:** Hypertonic glucose (50%) increases the osmotic pressure of the blood and glomerular filtrate, facilitating the movement of fluid from the tissues (e.g., cerebral tissue) into the vascular compartment and promoting diuresis [TGA].
*   **Antiemetic:** The combination of glucose, fructose, and phosphoric acid in oral liquid form acts locally to relieve symptoms of gastric upset and nausea [FDA].

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Parenteral:** Store below 25°C and protect from light. Discard any unused portion of pre-filled syringes [TGA].
*   **Oral:** Store at room temperature. Keep away from excessive heat and direct light. Do not freeze. Do not use if the protective seal is damaged [FDA].
