# Editorial Product Information: Ganaxolone

## Editorial Note
- The following Jurisdictional Product Information (PI) document contributed to this editorial: US (FDA) for **ZTALMY**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of ganaxolone for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a single clinical reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labelling for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ganaxolone (a neuroactive steroid; gamma-aminobutyric acid [GABA] A receptor positive modulator).
- **Brand Name(s):** **ZTALMY** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Oral suspension, 50 mg/mL [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Immedica Pharma US Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Seizures associated with cyclin-dependent kinase-like 5 (CDKL5) deficiency disorder (CDD):** Indicated for the treatment of seizures in patients 2 years of age and older [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Administration:** Must be administered three times daily **with food**. 
- **Preparation:** Shake the bottle for at least 1 minute and wait 1 minute before measuring the dose. Use the provided oral syringe; household spoons are not adequate.
- **Titration:** Dosage must be increased gradually (no more frequently than every 7 days) based on tolerability.

### Weight-Based Dosing [US]
For patients with normal hepatic function or mild-to-moderate hepatic impairment:

| Patient Weight | Starting Dosage | Maximum Maintenance Dosage |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **$\le$ 28 kg** | 2 mg/kg three times daily (6 mg/kg/day) | 21 mg/kg three times daily (63 mg/kg/day) |
| **$> 28$ kg** | 50 mg three times daily (150 mg daily) | 600 mg three times daily (1800 mg daily) |

### Missed Dose and Discontinuation
- **Missed Dose:** Instructions not explicitly detailed in the summary highlights; follow institutional protocol.
- **Discontinuation:** Withdraw gradually to minimise the risk of increased seizure frequency and status epilepticus. If a serious adverse event occurs, rapid discontinuation may be considered [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None:** There are no listed contraindications in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Somnolence and Sedation:** Highly prevalent (44% in trials). Patients should avoid operating heavy machinery or driving until the effects of the drug are known. Concurrent use of alcohol or other CNS depressants may potentiate these effects [US].
- **Suicidal Behavior and Ideation:** As with all antiepileptic drugs (AEDs), monitor for new or worsening depression, suicidal thoughts, or unusual changes in mood or behavior [US].
- **Withdrawal Seizures:** Abrupt discontinuation increases the risk of status epilepticus.
- **Reporting:** Suspected adverse reactions should be reported to the manufacturer or the national regulatory authority (e.g., FDA MedWatch in the US).

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects observed at an incidence of $\ge$ 5% and at least twice the rate of placebo include:
- **Very Common (>10%):** Somnolence (38%), Pyrexia (18%), Upper respiratory tract infection (10%).
- **Common (1–10%):** Sedation (6%), Salivary hypersecretion/drooling (6%), Seasonal allergy (6%).
- **Other observed effects:** Bronchitis, influenza, gait disturbance, and nasal congestion (all 4%).

**Serious Adverse Effects:**
- Significant CNS depression (somnolence/sedation).
- Potential for suicidal ideation.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **UGT Inhibitors (e.g., Valproic Acid):** May increase ganaxolone exposure. A reduction in ganaxolone maintenance dose should be considered when initiating a UGT inhibitor [US].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers (Strong/Moderate):** Significant decrease in ganaxolone exposure (e.g., Rifampin decreases AUC by 68%). Avoid concomitant use; if unavoidable, consider increasing the ganaxolone dose (not exceeding the maximum recommended daily dose) [US].
- **CNS Depressants and Alcohol:** Potentiate the sedative effects of ganaxolone.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: No category assigned under current labelling rules]. No available human data. Animal studies showed fetal malformations (mice) and neurobehavioral impairment (rats) at exposures lower than the Maximum Recommended Human Dose (MRHD). Use only if the potential benefit justifies the risk. A pregnancy registry is available (North American Antiepileptic Drug Pregnancy Registry) [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Ganaxolone is excreted in human milk. Infant exposure is estimated to be $< 1\%$ of the maternal dose. The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need and potential adverse effects on the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal data showed altered estrous cyclicity in female rats at high doses; no effects on spermatogenesis were noted [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for ages 2 years and older. Safety and efficacy below age 2 have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of patients aged 65 and over, as CDD is primarily a paediatric/young adult condition [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild (Child-Pugh A) or Moderate (Child-Pugh B):** No dosage adjustment required.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Requires a modified titration schedule. Maximum dose for $\le$ 28 kg is 7 mg/kg tid (21 mg/kg/day); for $> 28$ kg, maximum is 200 mg tid (600 mg/day) [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No clinically significant changes in exposure were observed in patients with creatinine clearance $< 90$ mL/min [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Positive allosteric modulator of the GABA-A receptor in the central nervous system.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Tmax is 2 to 3 hours. Food significantly increases absorption (Cmax increases 3-fold and AUC 2-fold with a high-fat meal) [US].
    - **Distribution:** Approximately 99% protein-bound in serum.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolized via CYP3A4/5, CYP2B6, CYP2C19, CYP2D6, and various UGT enzymes (1A3, 1A6, 1A9, 2B7, 2B15).
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 34 hours. Excretion is primarily faecal (55%) and urinary (18%) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study 1 (NCT03572933):** A double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 101 patients (ages 2–19).
- **Primary Outcome:** The median reduction in 28-day major motor seizure frequency was 31% for the ganaxolone group compared to 7% for the placebo group (p=0.0036).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C.
- **Handling:** Store upright with the cap tightly closed.
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion 30 days after first opening the bottle [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** [US: Schedule CV (Controlled Substance)].
- **Abuse Potential:** Ganaxolone has a potential for abuse; human abuse potential studies showed "drug liking" at high doses (2000 mg) similar to or slightly higher than placebo, but lower than lorazepam [US].
