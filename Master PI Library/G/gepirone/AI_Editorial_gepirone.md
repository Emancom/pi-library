# Editorial Product Information: Gepirone

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of the official Product Information for gepirone, designed for use by healthcare professionals as a clinically accurate reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial is a summary and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this writing, only the United States Product Information was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Gepirone hydrochloride (a pyrenylpiperazine derivative).
- **Chemical Class:** Selective 5-HT1A receptor agonist.
- **Brand Name:** **EXXUA** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Extended-Release Tablets [US]:** 18.2 mg, 36.3 mg, 54.5 mg, and 72.6 mg (equivalent to 20 mg, 40 mg, 60 mg, and 80 mg of gepirone HCl, respectively).
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aytu Therapeutics, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Major Depressive Disorder (MDD):** Indicated for the treatment of MDD in adults [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Starting Dose:** 18.2 mg once daily.
- **Titration:** Based on clinical response and tolerability, the dose may be increased to 36.3 mg daily on Day 4. Further titration to 54.5 mg daily may occur after Day 7, and to 72.6 mg daily after an additional week.
- **Maximum Dose:** 72.6 mg once daily [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Food Requirement:** Must be administered with food at approximately the same time each day. Systemic exposure is significantly higher in the fed state [US].
- **Tablet Integrity:** Tablets must be swallowed whole; do not split, crush, or chew.

### Pre-treatment and Monitoring Requirements
- **ECG Monitoring:** Perform an electrocardiogram (ECG) prior to initiation. Do not initiate if QTc is >450 msec. Conduct follow-up ECGs during titration and periodically during treatment [US].
- **Electrolytes:** Correct any potassium or magnesium abnormalities before starting therapy.

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Take the missed dose as soon as remembered. If it is nearly time for the next dose, skip the missed dose and resume the regular schedule. Do not double the dose to compensate [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to gepirone or its components [US].
- Baseline prolonged QTc interval (>450 msec) [US].
- Congenital long QT syndrome [US].
- Severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C) [US].
- Concomitant use of strong CYP3A4 inhibitors [US].
- **MAOIs:** Concomitant use with Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs) or use within 14 days of discontinuing an MAOI. Conversely, allow 14 days after stopping gepirone before starting an MAOI [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning [US]
- **Suicidal Thoughts and Behaviors:** Antidepressants increase the risk of suicidal thinking and behavior in pediatric and young adult patients (up to age 24). Closely monitor all patients for clinical worsening and emergence of suicidality, particularly during the first months of therapy or following dose adjustments. Gepirone is not approved for pediatric use.

### General Warnings
- **QT Prolongation:** Gepirone causes dose-dependent QTc prolongation. Risk is increased in patients with electrolyte imbalances, those taking other QT-prolonging drugs, or those with hepatic/renal impairment. Monitor more frequently if QTc ≥450 msec develops; do not escalate dose if QTc exceeds this threshold [US].
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Potentially life-threatening; risk increases with concomitant use of other serotonergic agents (e.g., SSRIs, SNRIs, triptans, tricyclic antidepressants). Discontinue immediately if symptoms (agitation, hallucinations, tachycardia, hyperreflexia) occur.
- **Activation of Mania/Hypomania:** Antidepressants can precipitate manic episodes. Screen patients for bipolar disorder history prior to initiation [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most common adverse reactions (incidence ≥5% and twice the rate of placebo) include dizziness, nausea, insomnia, abdominal pain, and dyspepsia [US].

| Adverse Reaction | Gepirone (18.2–72.6 mg) % | Placebo % |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| Dizziness | 49 | 10 |
| Nausea | 35 | 13 |
| Headache | 31 | 20 |
| Insomnia | 14 | 5 |
| Abdominal Pain | 7 | 3 |
| Dyspepsia | 6 | 2 |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** QT interval prolongation, serotonin syndrome, and treatment-emergent suicidality [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Contraindicated; increases gepirone exposure ~5-fold [US].
- **Moderate CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Reduce gepirone dose by 50% [US].
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., Rifampin):** Avoid concomitant use; significantly reduces gepirone exposure (up to 29-fold) [US].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **MAOIs:** Increased risk of hypertensive crisis and serotonin syndrome; see Contraindications.
- **Other Serotonergic Drugs:** Increased risk of serotonin syndrome.
- **QT-Prolonging Drugs:** Increased risk of arrhythmias; requires frequent ECG monitoring [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned (PLLR format used).
    - **Clinical Data:** Use in the third trimester may increase the risk of persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn (PPHN) and neonatal adaptation syndrome (e.g., respiratory distress, irritability). Animal studies show increased mortality in early postnatal life and decreased fetal growth at doses exceeding the maximum human dose [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on the presence of gepirone in human milk. Monitoring infants for sedation and poor feeding is recommended if used during lactation [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal data showed increased latency to mating at high doses [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established. Not approved for use in pediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly Use (≥65 years):** Higher exposure (AUC and Cmax) observed. Recommended starting dose is 18.2 mg, with a maximum dose of 36.3 mg daily after 7 days [US].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **CrCl <50 mL/min:** Starting dose 18.2 mg; maximum dose 36.3 mg daily after 7 days [US].
    - **CrCl ≥50 mL/min:** No dosage adjustment required.
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Moderate (Child-Pugh B):** Starting dose 18.2 mg; maximum dose 36.3 mg daily after 7 days [US].
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Contraindicated.
    - **Mild (Child-Pugh A):** No dosage adjustment required.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Modulation of serotonergic activity in the CNS via selective agonist activity at 5-HT1A receptors [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is 14–17%. Tmax is 6 hours (3 hours if taken with a high-fat meal).
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolized via CYP3A4 into major active metabolites 1-PP and 3’-OH-gepirone [US].
    - **Elimination:** Mean terminal half-life is approximately 5 hours. Primary excretion via urine (81%) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
The efficacy of gepirone was demonstrated in two 8-week, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, flexible-dose studies in adults (Study 1 and Study 2). The primary endpoint was the change from baseline in the Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HAMD-17). Both studies showed statistically significant improvement compared to placebo at week 8 [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from high humidity and moisture.
