# Editorial Product Information: gilteritinib fumarate

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **gilteritinib fumarate**
- Chemical class: protein tyrosine kinase inhibitor (FLT3 inhibitor); pharmacotherapeutic group: protein kinase inhibitors
- ATC code: L01EX13
- Route(s) of administration: oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources.
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources.
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of adult patients with relapsed or refractory acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) with a documented FLT3 mutation (internal tandem duplication [ITD] or tyrosine kinase domain [TKD]).

(If additional jurisdiction-specific indications exist, they are not specified in the available sources.)

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General dosing (adult)
- Recommended starting dose: **120 mg once daily** (composition described in sources as three 40 mg tablets).
- In the absence of a response (patient does not achieve a composite complete remission [CRc]) after approximately 4 weeks of treatment, the dose may be increased to **200 mg once daily** if tolerated and clinically warranted (described in available sources).

### Dose modification, interruption and discontinuation
- Dose reductions permitted:
  - From 120 mg daily → 80 mg daily.
  - From 200 mg daily → 120 mg daily.
- Specific interruption/reduction/discontinuation recommendations:
  - Differentiation syndrome:
    - If suspected, initiate systemic corticosteroids and hemodynamic monitoring until symptom resolution; corticosteroids should be given for a minimum of 3 days and tapered after resolution.
    - Interrupt gilteritinib if severe signs/symptoms persist >48 hours after starting corticosteroids.
    - Resume at the same dose when signs/symptoms improve to Grade ≤2.
  - Posterior reversible encephalopathy syndrome (PRES):
    - Discontinue gilteritinib if PRES is suspected.
  - QTc interval >500 msec:
    - Interrupt gilteritinib.
    - Resume at a reduced dose (80 mg or 120 mg) when QTc returns to within 30 msec of baseline or ≤480 msec.
  - QTc increase >30 msec on ECG on day 8 of cycle 1:
    - Confirm with ECG on day 9; if confirmed, consider dose reduction to 80 mg or 120 mg.
  - Pancreatitis:
    - Interrupt until resolved; may resume at a reduced dose (80 mg or 120 mg).
  - Other Grade ≥3 toxicity considered related to treatment:
    - Interrupt until toxicity resolves or improves to Grade 1; may resume at a reduced dose (80 mg or 120 mg).
  - Planned haematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT):
    - Interrupt gilteritinib one week prior to conditioning regimen.
    - May resume 30 days after HSCT if engraftment was successful, patient did not have grade ≥2 acute graft versus host disease, and was in CRc.

### Administration instructions
- Take once daily, at about the same time each day.
- Can be taken with or without food.
- Swallow whole with water; do not break or crush tablets.
- If a dose is missed: administer as soon as possible on the same day and return to the usual schedule the following day.
- If vomiting occurs after dosing: do not re-dose; return to the normal schedule the next day.

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to **gilteritinib fumarate** or to any of the excipients.
- Anaphylactic reactions have been observed in clinical trials; prior severe hypersensitivity to the active substance or excipients is a contraindication.

(Any additional jurisdiction-specific contraindications are not specified in the available sources.)

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Differentiation syndrome:
  - Has been associated with treatment; may be life‑threatening or fatal if not treated promptly. Typical findings include fever, dyspnoea, pleural/pericardial effusion, pulmonary oedema, hypotension, rapid weight gain, peripheral oedema, rash and renal dysfunction. Initiate systemic corticosteroids and hemodynamic monitoring if suspected.
- Posterior reversible encephalopathy syndrome (PRES):
  - Rare cases reported; presents with seizure, headache, confusion, visual/neurological disturbances with or without hypertension. Confirm by brain imaging (preferably MRI) and discontinue gilteritinib if PRES suspected.
- QT prolongation:
  - Gilteritinib is associated with QT interval prolongation, especially during the first 3 months of treatment. Correct hypokalaemia and hypomagnesaemia prior to and during treatment. Interrupt for QTcF >500 msec and follow dose modification guidance above.
- Pancreatitis:
  - Cases reported; evaluate and monitor patients with suggestive signs/symptoms and interrupt therapy until resolution.
- Hepatic impairment:
  - No dose adjustment required for mild (Child-Pugh A) or moderate (Child-Pugh B) hepatic impairment. Not studied in severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C).
- Renal impairment:
  - No dose adjustment necessary for most degrees of renal impairment. Exposure may be increased in severe renal impairment or end stage renal disease; closely monitor for toxicities and QT prolongation.
- Use in paediatrics:
  - Safety and efficacy not established in <18 years. Do not use in children from birth to <6 months due to potential impact on cardiac development related to 5HT2B binding observed in vitro.
- Fertility and reproductive risks:
  - Embryofetal toxicity observed in animal studies; advise effective contraception (see Section 9).
- Drug interactions:
  - Strong CYP3A/P‑gp inducers can markedly reduce exposure and should be avoided. Strong CYP3A and/or P‑gp inhibitors can increase exposure—use with caution and monitor for toxicity. Gilteritinib may inhibit intestinal transporters (P‑gp, BCRP) and OCT1 in vitro; use caution with substrates (see Section 8).
- Anaphylaxis and hypersensitivity:
  - Anaphylactic reactions reported; monitor patients and discontinue for severe hypersensitivity.

### Monitoring requirements
- Blood chemistries, including creatine phosphokinase (CPK), should be assessed:
  - Prior to initiation, on Day 15 of Cycle 1, and monthly thereafter for the duration of treatment.
- Electrocardiograms (ECG):
  - Prior to initiation, on Day 8 and Day 15 of Cycle 1, and prior to the start of the next three subsequent months of treatment.
  - When dose is increased, repeat ECG monitoring following the same schedule after dose escalation.
- Monitor electrolytes (potassium, magnesium) and correct as necessary to mitigate QT prolongation risk.
- Monitor for signs/symptoms of differentiation syndrome, PRES, pancreatitis, liver injury, and cardiac toxicity.
- Monitor for signs of infection, bleeding, and other haematologic complications as clinically indicated.

- Additional safety notes from clinical experience:
  - Differentiation syndrome: occurred in 11 of 319 patients (≈3%); onset ranged from 1 to 82 days after initiation; 9 of 11 recovered after treatment or dose interruption.

## 8. Drug Interactions

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Metabolism: primarily metabolised by CYP3A enzymes.
- Strong CYP3A/P‑gp inducers (e.g., rifampin, phenytoin, St John’s Wort) can substantially reduce gilteritinib exposure and should be avoided:
  - Example from healthy subjects: rifampicin (600 mg) reduced single-dose gilteritinib Cmax by ~27% and AUCinf by ~70%.
- Strong CYP3A and/or P‑gp inhibitors (e.g., itraconazole, voriconazole, posaconazole, clarithromycin, ritonavir) can increase gilteritinib exposure; use with caution and monitor for toxicity:
  - Example: itraconazole (200 mg daily × 28 days) co-administered with a single 10 mg gilteritinib dose in healthy subjects increased mean Cmax by ~20% and AUCinf by ~2.2-fold.
  - In patients with relapsed/refractory AML, co-administration with a strong CYP3A and/or P‑gp inhibitor increased exposure approximately 1.5‑fold.
- Gilteritinib is a substrate of P‑gp and BCRP in vitro; intestinal inhibition of these transporters is possible.
- Gilteritinib is not a clinically meaningful inhibitor or inducer of CYP3A4 in vivo (midazolam PK changed by ≈10% after repeated gilteritinib), and is not an in vivo inhibitor of MATE1 (cephalexin PK changed by <10%).
- Gilteritinib may inhibit transporters in vitro (P‑gp, BCRP, OCT1); potential to affect drugs that are substrates of these transporters.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Gilteritinib may reduce the effects of agents targeting 5‑HT2B receptors or sigma nonspecific receptors (e.g., certain SSRIs such as escitalopram, fluoxetine, sertraline). Avoid concomitant use unless essential.
- Because gilteritinib can prolong QT interval, concomitant use with other QT‑prolonging drugs may increase risk of torsades de pointes and should be used cautiously.

### Clinically significant interaction examples and management
- Avoid concomitant use with strong CYP3A/P‑gp inducers due to risk of loss of efficacy.
- Use caution when co-prescribing strong CYP3A and/or P‑gp inhibitors (e.g., voriconazole, itraconazole, posaconazole, clarithromycin); consider alternative agents that do not strongly inhibit CYP3A/P‑gp when possible and monitor closely for toxicity if co‑administration is unavoidable.
- Monitor concentrations/effects and adverse events when co-administering with P‑gp substrates (e.g., digoxin, dabigatran etexilate), BCRP substrates (e.g., mitoxantrone, rosuvastatin), and OCT1 substrates (e.g., metformin) because gilteritinib may inhibit these transporters.
- Consider electrolyte monitoring and correction when combining with agents known to cause hypokalaemia or hypomagnesaemia.

(Region-specific interaction recommendations beyond the above are not specified in the available sources.)

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

### Pregnancy
- **[Australian] Pregnancy category: D.**
- Gilteritinib can cause fetal harm. In animal studies (rats), gilteritinib crossed the placenta and caused embryofetal lethality and teratogenicity at doses resulting in subclinical exposures (≥20 mg/kg/day PO).
- Females of reproductive potential should have a pregnancy test within seven days prior to initiating treatment.
- Contraception:
  - Females of reproductive potential: use effective contraception during treatment and for at least **6 months** after stopping treatment. Women using hormonal contraceptives should add a barrier method.
  - Males with female partners of reproductive potential: use effective contraception during treatment and for at least **4 months** after the last dose.

### Breastfeeding
- It is unknown whether gilteritinib or its metabolites are excreted in human milk.
- Animal data: gilteritinib and/or metabolites detected in milk of lactating rats at levels higher than maternal plasma; distribution to infant tissues via milk observed.
- Recommendation: discontinue breastfeeding during treatment and for at least **2 months** after the last dose.

### Fertility
- No human data available on the effect of gilteritinib on fertility.

## 10. Use in Special Populations

### Paediatric use
- Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients <18 years.
- Not recommended in children from birth to <6 months due to potential effects on cardiac development related to 5‑HT2B binding observed in vitro.

### Elderly
- No dose adjustment required for patients ≥65 years of age based on available pharmacokinetic data.

### Renal impairment
- No dose adjustment required for most degrees of renal impairment.
- Severe renal impairment or end-stage renal disease may increase exposure; monitor closely for toxicity and QT prolongation.

### Hepatic impairment
- No dose adjustment required for mild (Child‑Pugh A) or moderate (Child‑Pugh B) hepatic impairment.
- Not studied in severe hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh C); use in severe hepatic impairment: Not specified in available sources — monitor closely if used.

## 11. Pharmacology

### Mechanism of action
- **Gilteritinib fumarate** is a small molecule inhibitor of FLT3 tyrosine kinase (and other relevant kinases within its class), resulting in inhibition of FLT3 phosphorylation and downstream signalling. In patients receiving 120 mg, >90% inhibition of FLT3 phosphorylation was observed rapidly (within 24 hours) and was sustained.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: Oral administration; can be taken with or without food (detailed bioavailability parameters not specified in available sources).
- Distribution:
  - Crosses placenta in animal studies; detected in milk of lactating rats at levels higher than maternal plasma.
  - In vitro, substrate of P‑gp and BCRP; may inhibit P‑gp and BCRP in the intestine and OCT1 in the liver at clinically relevant concentrations.
- Metabolism: Primarily by CYP3A enzymes.
- Elimination:
  - Long terminal half‑life estimated at **~113 hours** (used to inform overdose management).
- Drug interaction PK effects:
  - Rifampicin (strong CYP3A/P‑gp inducer) decreased single-dose gilteritinib Cmax by ~27% and AUCinf by ~70% in healthy subjects.
  - Itraconazole (strong CYP3A/P‑gp inhibitor) with a single 10 mg gilteritinib dose increased Cmax ~20% and AUCinf ~2.2‑fold in healthy subjects; in patients, strong CYP3A/P‑gp inhibitors increased exposure ~1.5‑fold.
  - Repeated gilteritinib administration (300 mg) had minimal effect on midazolam PK (≈10% increase).
  - Cephalexin PK was not meaningfully altered (<10% change) after repeated dosing of gilteritinib (200 mg).

### Pharmacodynamics
- Rapid and sustained inhibition of FLT3 phosphorylation in patients with FLT3‑mutated relapsed/refractory AML. Associated pharmacodynamic effects include potential rapid proliferation/differentiation of myeloid cells (differentiation syndrome) and effects on cardiac repolarization (QT prolongation).

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Safety data described were obtained from clinical studies in which **319** patients with relapsed or refractory AML who received at least one dose of 120 mg gilteritinib were evaluated.
- Key safety findings from the clinical population:
  - Differentiation syndrome occurred in 11 of 319 patients (≈3%); onset ranged from 1 to 82 days after initiation; majority recovered after treatment or dose interruption.
  - PRES occurred in ≈0.6% of patients; symptoms resolved after discontinuation in reported cases.
  - QTcF >500 msec observed in a small proportion of patients (4/317 at 120 mg; additional cases across all doses yielding 12 patients with QTcF >500 msec).
  - Common laboratory and clinical adverse events as listed in Section 7.
