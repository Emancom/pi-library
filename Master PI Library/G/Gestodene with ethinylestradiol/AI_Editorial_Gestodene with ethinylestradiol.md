# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine Injection, synthesised for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Prescribing Information approved in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved labelling before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. A naturally occurring carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn metabolic errors resulting in carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) on Hemodialysis:** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours.
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, adjusted based on clinical response and carnitine levels. Maximum reported dose is 300 mg/kg.

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2–3 minute bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may occur as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter/discoloration prior to use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature (25°C).

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known:** No specific contraindications are listed in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. If severe, discontinue treatment immediately and do not re-administer without careful risk-benefit analysis [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In those with a history of epilepsy, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Oral Levocarnitine in Renal Impairment:** The safety/efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with renal insufficiency or ESRD has not been evaluated. Chronic high doses may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO) [US].
- **Monitoring:** 
    - **Plasma Carnitine:** Target free carnitine levels between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
    - **ESRD Monitoring:** Trough levels should be monitored weekly (initially) and monthly thereafter.
    - **Warfarin:** Monitor INR closely in patients co-administered warfarin.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse event frequencies are based on clinical trials in patients with ESRD on dialysis.

| System Organ Class | Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | >10% | Hypertension, Hypotension, Headache, Nausea, Vomiting, Diarrhea, Abdominal pain, Fever, Pharyngitis, Cough, Chest pain, Injection site reaction. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | 1–10% | Asthenia, Back pain, Arrhythmia, Palpitations, Tachycardia, Hypercalcemia, Hyperkalemia, Peripheral edema, Anemia, Dizziness, Insomnia, Paresthesia, Pruritus, Rash, Eye disorder. |
| **Postmarketing** | Frequency Not Specified | Anaphylaxis, Laryngeal edema, Bronchospasm, Seizures, Body odor, Gastritis. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and other severe hypersensitivity reactions [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is used concomitantly. Frequent INR monitoring is recommended following initiation or dose adjustment [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not lettered]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations after supplementation. Risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models at 3.8 times human dosage [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is based on mg/kg body weight (refer to Section 3). 
- **Elderly Use:** Specific dose adjustments for the elderly are not detailed in the available source [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically for ESRD on dialysis. Note that *oral* forms are not recommended for chronic use in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic/fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Bioavailability:** Absolute bioavailability of oral forms is ~15%.
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) and γ-butyrobetaine.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (n=12), IV levocarnitine (20 mg/kg post-dialysis) increased pre-dialysis trough concentrations from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While carnitine levels increased, the effect on clinical outcomes in this specific population was not determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to expiry date on packaging.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial. Contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources for other jurisdictions.
