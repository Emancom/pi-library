# Editorial Product Information: Ganirelix

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Ganirelix for healthcare professionals. It is intended for informational purposes and serves as a clinically accurate synthesis of the provided regulatory data.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial is not a substitute for the official PI or local prescribing guidelines. Clinicians should consult the approved PI in their specific jurisdiction before prescribing or administering this medication.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ganirelix acetate (a synthetic decapeptide).
- **Chemical Class:** Gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) antagonist.
- **Brand Names:** **ARX Ganirelix** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** H01CC01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Solution for injection in a pre-filled syringe; 250 µg ganirelix (as acetate) in 0.5 mL.
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Controlled Ovarian Stimulation (COS):** For the prevention of premature luteinisation and ovulation (premature LH surges) in patients undergoing controlled ovarian stimulation, followed by oocyte pick up and assisted reproductive techniques (ART) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** 0.25 mg injected subcutaneously once daily.
- **Initiation of Therapy:** Treatment with **ganirelix** should typically begin on day 5 or day 6 of follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) administration, depending on the individual ovarian response.
- **Duration:** Daily treatment should continue until sufficient follicles of adequate size are present. Final follicular maturation is then induced by administering human chorionic gonadotrophin (hCG).
- **Timing Requirements:** Due to the half-life of **ganirelix**, the interval between two injections, and between the final **ganirelix** dose and the hCG injection, must not exceed 30 hours to prevent a premature LH surge.
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Inspect the solution for clarity and particulates; use only if clear.
    - Administer via subcutaneous injection, preferably in the thigh. 
    - Rotate injection sites to prevent lipoatrophy.
    - Air bubbles in the pre-filled syringe are expected and do not require removal before injection.
- **Missed Dose:** Not explicitly detailed, though the 30-hour maximum interval is emphasized as critical for efficacy [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to the active substance or any excipients (glacial acetic acid, mannitol, water for injections).
- Hypersensitivity to GnRH or any other GnRH analogue.
- Pregnancy and lactation.
- Moderate to severe renal impairment.
- Moderate to severe hepatic impairment.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Cases of anaphylaxis, angioedema, and urticaria have been reported, sometimes with the first dose. Use with caution in women with signs of active allergic conditions. **Ganirelix** is not advised for patients with severe active allergic symptoms.
- **Ovarian Hyperstimulation Syndrome (OHSS):** OHSS is an intrinsic risk of gonadotrophin stimulation and must be monitored and treated symptomatically.
- **Ectopic Pregnancy:** Women undergoing ART have an increased incidence of tubal abnormalities; early ultrasound confirmation of intrauterine pregnancy is recommended.
- **Congenital Abnormalities:** The incidence of malformations after ART may be slightly higher than spontaneous conception (likely due to parental characteristics and multiple gestations), but data suggest **ganirelix** carries a risk comparable to GnRH agonists.
- **Body Weight:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in women weighing <50 kg or >90 kg [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Local skin reactions at the injection site (redness, with or without swelling). These typically disappear within 4 hours of administration.
- **Common (1–10%):** Not specified as distinct from very common in the primary source.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** 
    - Nausea (0.5%)
    - Headache (0.4%)
    - Malaise (0.3%)
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Very rare cases of post-marketing hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylactic shock and angioedema.
- **Note:** Other reported events (pelvic pain, abdominal distension, OHSS, spontaneous abortion) are generally associated with the controlled ovarian stimulation procedure rather than **ganirelix** itself.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Formal drug interaction studies have not been performed. Interactions with commonly used medicinal products cannot be excluded.
- **Incompatibilities:** In the absence of compatibility studies, **ganirelix** must not be mixed with other medicinal products.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category D.** **Ganirelix** is contraindicated in pregnancy. Animal studies show increased fetal resorptions at doses lower than or near human equivalent doses, attributed to the drug's hormonal properties.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. It is unknown if **ganirelix** is excreted in human milk.
- **Fertility:** In animal models (rats), treatment resulted in reversible impairment of mating and fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No relevant use in the paediatric population.
- **Elderly Use:** No data available.
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in moderate to severe renal impairment.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in moderate to severe hepatic impairment.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Competitive antagonist at GnRH receptors in the pituitary gland. It induces rapid, reversible suppression of endogenous gonadotropins (LH and FSH) without the initial "flare" effect seen with GnRH agonists. The suppression of LH is more pronounced than FSH.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid absorption following SC injection; bioavailability is approximately 91%. Peak serum levels (Cmax ~15 ng/mL) are reached within 1 to 2 hours (tmax).
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is approximately 44 L. Plasma protein binding is 82%.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily circulating as the parent compound (ganirelix).
    - **Elimination:** Excreted via faeces (~75%) and urine (~22%). The elimination half-life is approximately 13 hours. Steady-state levels are reached within 2 to 3 days.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- The efficacy of **ganirelix** was established in three pivotal trials (Protocols 38602, 38607, and 103-001). 
- In these trials, **ganirelix** (0.25 mg) was initiated on day 6 of FSH treatment.
- Results across studies showed that the incidence of premature LH surges (LH ≥ 10 mIU/mL) occurred in less than 1% of subjects.
- Ongoing pregnancy rates per attempt ranged from 20.3% to 33.8%, which was found to be clinically comparable to regimens using GnRH agonists [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C.
- Do not freeze.
- Store in the original package and protect from light.
- **Shelf life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4) [Australian].
