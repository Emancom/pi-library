# Editorial Product Information: Gatifloxacin (Ophthalmic)

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information documents contributed to this editorial: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of gatifloxacin ophthalmic solution 0.5% for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial resource and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Gatifloxacin anhydrous.
- **Chemical Class:** 8-methoxyfluoroquinolone (quinolone antimicrobial).
- **Brand Names:** Gatifloxacin Ophthalmic Solution (Caplin Steriles Limited) [US].
- **ATC Code:** S01AE01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile preserved aqueous ophthalmic solution 0.5% (5 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Topical Ophthalmic.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Caplin Steriles Limited [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Bacterial Conjunctivitis:** Indicated for the treatment of conjunctivitis caused by susceptible strains of the following aerobic organisms [US]:
    - **Gram-positive:** *Staphylococcus aureus*, *Staphylococcus epidermidis*, *Streptococcus mitis* group, *Streptococcus oralis*, *Streptococcus pneumoniae*.
    - **Gram-negative:** *Haemophilus influenzae*.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Pediatrics $\ge$ 1 year)
- **Day 1:** Instill one drop into the affected eye(s) every two hours while awake, up to a maximum of 8 times.
- **Days 2 through 7:** Instill one drop into the affected eye(s) two to four times daily while awake.

### Administration Instructions
- For topical ophthalmic use only.
- Patients should be instructed to avoid contaminating the applicator tip with material from the eye, fingers, or other surfaces to maintain sterility.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** History of hypersensitivity to **gatifloxacin**, other quinolone antibiotics, or any component of the formulation (including the preservative benzalkonium chloride) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious and occasionally fatal hypersensitivity (anaphylactic) reactions, angioedema (including laryngeal, pharyngeal, or facial edema), and dermatological emergencies such as Stevens-Johnson Syndrome have been reported, even following a single dose. Therapy must be discontinued immediately at the first sign of a rash or allergic reaction.
- **Corneal Endothelial Cell Injury:** This product is intended for topical use only. It may cause injury to the corneal endothelium if introduced directly into the anterior chamber of the eye.
- **Microbial Overgrowth:** As with other anti-infectives, prolonged use may lead to the overgrowth of non-susceptible organisms, including fungi. If superinfection occurs, the drug should be discontinued and alternative therapy initiated.
- **Monitoring:** Clinical judgment, assisted by magnification (e.g., slit-lamp biomicroscopy) and fluorescein staining, should be employed if symptoms do not improve.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects ($\ge$ 1%)
- Worsening of conjunctivitis
- Eye irritation
- Dysgeusia (bitter taste)
- Eye pain

### Other Ocular Adverse Effects
- Chemosis, conjunctival haemorrhage, dry eye, eye discharge, eyelid oedema, increased lacrimation, keratitis, red eye, papillary conjunctivitis, and reduced visual acuity.

### Postmarketing and Serious Reactions
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, angioedema, allergic dermatitis, rash, urticaria, and pruritus.
- **Ocular:** Blepharitis, eye swelling (corneal and conjunctival), blurred vision, and eye pruritus.
- **Systemic:** Nausea and dyspnoea.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Systemic exposure following topical ophthalmic administration is negligible (serum levels <5 ng/mL); therefore, systemic drug-drug interactions are unlikely [US].
- **Clinically Significant Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US]: There are no adequate data on the use of 0.5% ophthalmic gatifloxacin in pregnant women. Animal studies using high oral doses (600x the maximum recommended human ophthalmic dose) showed teratogenic effects (skeletal malformations, fetal weight reduction). However, no effects were observed at doses 200–400x the human ophthalmic dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** [US]: Gatifloxacin is excreted in the milk of lactating rats following oral administration. It is unknown if gatifloxacin is present in human milk. Because systemic absorption is low, the risk to the infant is likely minimal, but the benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the potential risks.
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in rats at oral doses up to 800x the human ophthalmic dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have been established in children aged 1 year and older. Safety has not been established in infants under 1 year of age [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy have been observed between geriatric and younger adult populations.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Given the minimal systemic absorption of the ophthalmic solution, dosage adjustments are generally not required. Specific data for these populations regarding the ophthalmic formulation are not provided in the source documentation.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Gatifloxacin is a fluoroquinolone that inhibits DNA gyrase and topoisomerase IV. DNA gyrase is essential for bacterial DNA replication, transcription, and repair; topoisomerase IV is critical for chromosomal DNA partitioning during cell division.
- **Microbiology:** Exhibits activity against various Gram-positive and Gram-negative pathogens. There is generally no cross-resistance with aminoglycosides, macrolides, or tetracyclines.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Following intensive topical dosing (up to 8 times daily), serum concentrations remain below the limit of quantification (5 ng/mL) in healthy subjects.
- **Physicochemical Properties:** The 0.5% solution is clear, pale yellow, sterile, and aqueous, with a pH of 5.1–5.7 and an osmolality of 260–330 mOsm/kg.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy in Conjunctivitis:** In two randomized, double-masked, multicenter trials (N=717), gatifloxacin 0.5% was superior to vehicle on Day 6.
- **Clinical Success:** 58% for gatifloxacin vs. 45% for vehicle.
- **Microbiological Eradication:** 90% eradication rate for gatifloxacin vs. 70% for vehicle.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store between 15°C to 25°C (59°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Keep the bottle tightly closed when not in use.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription only (Rx-only).
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available US sources.
