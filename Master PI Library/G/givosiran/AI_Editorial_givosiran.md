# Product Information: Givlaari (givosiran)

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** Givlaari  
**Active Ingredient:** Givosiran (as givosiran sodium)  
**Concentration:** 189 mg/mL solution for injection.

Givosiran is a double-stranded small interfering ribonucleic acid (siRNA) designed for subcutaneous administration.

## 2. Approved Indications
Givlaari is indicated for the treatment of acute hepatic porphyria (AHP) in adults. In Australia, the indication extends to include adolescents aged 12 years and older [TGA]. 

*Editorial Note: Clinical data for AHP subtypes other than acute intermittent porphyria (AIP)—specifically hereditary coproporphyria (HCP), variegate porphyria (VP), and ALA dehydratase-deficient porphyria (ADP)—are limited. Prescribers should weigh individual benefit-risk in these rarer subtypes.*

## 3. Contraindications
Givlaari is strictly contraindicated in patients with a history of severe hypersensitivity (e.g., anaphylaxis) to the active substance or any of the excipients (sodium hydroxide, phosphoric acid, and water for injections).

## 4. Warnings and Precautions

### Anaphylactic Reactions
Anaphylaxis has occurred in less than 1% of patients during clinical trials. Healthcare providers should ensure immediate medical support is available during administration. Patients must be monitored for signs of hypersensitivity; if a reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued immediately and appropriate therapy initiated.

### Hepatic Toxicity (Transaminase Elevations)
Substantial elevations in alanine aminotransferase (ALT) have been observed, typically between 3 to 5 months after starting treatment. 
*   **Monitoring:** Liver function tests (LFTs) should be conducted at baseline, repeated monthly for the first 6 months, and then as clinically indicated.
*   **Management:** Treatment should be interrupted or discontinued for significant elevations. Resumption of treatment at a lower dose (1.25 mg/kg) may be considered once transaminases normalize.

### Renal Toxicity
Increases in serum creatinine and decreases in estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) have been reported. Renal function should be monitored throughout treatment as clinically indicated. Use with caution in patients with pre-existing renal disease.

### Blood Homocysteine Increased
Givosiran can cause elevations in blood homocysteine levels. 
*   **Monitoring:** Homocysteine levels should be measured at baseline and monitored during treatment. 
*   **Management:** If levels are elevated, consider supplementation with Vitamin B6 (pyridoxine) [TGA/FDA].

### Pancreatitis
Acute pancreatitis, including severe cases, has been reported in patients receiving givosiran. Clinical symptoms such as acute upper abdominal pain and elevations in pancreatic enzymes should prompt immediate evaluation. Interruption or discontinuation should be considered for confirmed cases [FDA].

## 5. Dosage and Administration
Therapy must be initiated under the supervision of a healthcare professional experienced in managing porphyria.

*   **Recommended Dose:** 2.5 mg/kg administered via subcutaneous injection once monthly.
*   **Dosing Calculation:** Based on actual body weight.
*   **Dose Modification:** In the event of clinically relevant transaminase elevations, the dose may be reduced to 1.25 mg/kg once monthly.
*   **Missed Dose:** Administer as soon as possible. Resume the monthly schedule thereafter.

### Administration Instructions
*   For subcutaneous use only.
*   **Volume Limits:** The maximum single injection volume is 1.5 mL. If the calculated dose exceeds 1.5 mL, divide the dose equally across multiple syringes and injection sites.
*   **Injection Sites:** Rotate sites (abdomen, thighs, or upper arms). Avoid injecting into scar tissue or areas that are inflamed, reddened, or swollen.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most common adverse reactions (occurring in ≥20% of patients) are nausea and injection site reactions (ISRs).

*   **Injection Site Reactions:** Reported in 25–36% of patients. Symptoms include erythema, pain, pruritus, and rash. These are generally mild/moderate and transient. Recall reactions at previous injection sites have been noted.
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea (up to 32%) and potential acute pancreatitis.
*   **Other common reactions:** Fatigue, rash, and headache.
*   **Laboratory Abnormalities:** Transaminase elevations, increased serum creatinine, and increased blood homocysteine.
*   **Immunogenicity:** Anti-drug antibodies (ADA) were detected in approximately 0.9% of patients in clinical trials, though no significant impact on efficacy or safety was observed.

## 7. Drug Interactions
Givosiran has the potential to reduce the activity of certain hepatic CYP450 enzymes.

*   **CYP1A2 and CYP2D6 Substrates:** Givosiran may increase the concentration of drugs that are substrates of these enzymes (e.g., caffeine, dextromethorphan). Avoid concomitant use with substrates where small concentration changes could lead to severe toxicity. If use is unavoidable, consider a dose reduction of the substrate.
*   **Other Enzymes:** Weak effects have been observed on CYP2C19 and CYP3A4 substrates.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

### Pregnancy
**TGA Category B3.** There are insufficient data regarding the use of givosiran in pregnant women. Animal studies have shown embryofetal toxicity (increased post-implantation loss and skeletal variations) at doses that were also toxic to the mother. Use during pregnancy should only be considered if the expected benefit to the mother outweighs the potential risk to the fetus.

### Breastfeeding
It is unknown if givosiran is excreted in human milk. A risk to the infant cannot be excluded. The decision to continue breastfeeding or therapy should consider the importance of the drug to the mother and the benefits of breastfeeding to the child.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed after subcutaneous injection; peak plasma concentration ($T_{max}$) is reached within 0.5 to 2 hours.
*   **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (>90%). Givosiran primarily distributes to the liver.
*   **Metabolism:** Metabolized by nucleases into shorter oligonucleotides. The active metabolite, AS(N-1)3' givosiran, possesses similar potency to the parent drug.
*   **Excretion:** Eliminated primarily via metabolism with a terminal half-life of approximately 5 to 6 hours. A small percentage (5–14%) is recovered in urine.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Givosiran is a liver-targeted siRNA that causes the degradation of aminolevulinic acid synthase 1 (ALAS1) messenger RNA (mRNA) through RNA interference. By reducing the levels of ALAS1 mRNA, givosiran lowers the production of neurotoxic heme intermediates, specifically aminolevulinic acid (ALA) and porphobilinogen (PBG). These intermediates are the primary drivers of porphyria attacks and chronic disease manifestations in AHP.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [TGA] or between 2°C to 25°C [FDA].
*   **Light:** Keep the vial in the original outer carton to protect it from light.
*   **Shelf Life:** Use immediately once the vial is opened. Discard any unused portion as the product contains no preservatives.
