# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction:
    - **US** (Food and Drug Administration)
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing or dispensing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg, administered as a slow bolus injection (over 2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion. In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose may be given, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours). Subsequent daily doses typically remain at 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilized [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). Administration should occur as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect parenteral products for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- The formulation contains no preservatives; discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial.

### Monitoring and Titration
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Dosing should be guided by clinical response and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine levels: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].
- **ESRD:** Therapy initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels below 40 to 50 µmol/L. Downward dose adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be implemented as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough levels [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions manifest within minutes of intravenous administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue the drug immediately and initiate appropriate medical management [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In patients with a known history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Administration Precaution):** While the injection is indicated for ESRD, the safety of *oral* levocarnitine has not been established in renal impairment. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].
- **Monitoring:** Monitor International Normalised Ratio (INR) in patients receiving concomitant warfarin therapy [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **General:** Transient nausea, vomiting, and abdominal pain are common. Body odor and gastritis are reported less frequently.
- **ESRD Clinical Trial Experience (Frequency ≥5%):**
    - **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), flu syndrome (25%), headache (22%), infection (15%), abdominal pain (9%).
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), chest pain (12%).
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%).
    - **Nervous:** Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), paresthesia (5%).
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%).
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Increased INR has been reported when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients on anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned; however, text states animal studies at 3.8 times the human dose revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm.] There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk; however, supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations following exogenous administration. Weigh the benefits of maternal supplementation against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is provided on a mg/kg basis for metabolic disorders and ESRD [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. Monitor for metabolite accumulation if transitioning to oral forms [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, it replenishes carnitine stores lost to dialysis and reduced renal synthesis.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x/week post-dialysis), trough levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. While levocarnitine injection effectively increases carnitine concentrations, the impact on specific clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) when mixed in Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's in concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8 mg/mL in PVC bags [US].
