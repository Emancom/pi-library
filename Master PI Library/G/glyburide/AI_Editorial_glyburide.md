# Editorial Product Information: Glyburide and Metformin Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction:
    - **United States**: US Food and Drug Administration (FDA)
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a single clinical reference.
- **Disclaimer**: This document is an editorial synthesis and intended for educational and informational use by healthcare professionals. It is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information approved in a specific jurisdiction for the purposes of prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients**: Glyburide (a second-generation sulfonylurea) and Metformin Hydrochloride (a biguanide).
- **Brand Names**: Glucovance (original brand) [US]; generic formulations available [US].
- **ATC Code**: A10BD02
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**:
    - **Oral Film-Coated Tablets [US]**: 
        - 1.25 mg glyburide / 250 mg metformin HCl
        - 2.5 mg glyburide / 500 mg metformin HCl
        - 5 mg glyburide / 500 mg metformin HCl
- **Route of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: USV Private Limited (India); Distributed by Avet Pharmaceuticals Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus**: Indicated as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycemic control in adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Administration**: Must be administered in divided doses, twice daily, with meals to reduce gastrointestinal side effects and the risk of hypoglycemia [US].
- **Maximum Dose**: 20 mg glyburide / 2,000 mg metformin HCl daily [US].

### Dosing by Patient History [US]
- **Treatment-naïve patients**: Start with 1.25 mg/250 mg once or twice daily with meals.
- **Patients inadequately controlled on sulfonylurea or metformin monotherapy**: Recommended starting dose of 2.5 mg/500 mg or 5 mg/500 mg twice daily with meals.
- **Patients switching from combination therapy (glyburide + metformin)**: The starting dose should not exceed the daily dose of glyburide (or equivalent sulfonylurea) and metformin already being taken.

### Titration
- Increase dose gradually based on glycemic control and tolerability [US].

### Renal Impairment Thresholds [US]
- **eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m²**: Contraindicated.
- **eGFR 30–45 mL/min/1.73 m²**: Initiation is not recommended.
- **eGFR falls below 45 mL/min/1.73 m² during therapy**: Assess the benefit/risk of continuing.
- **eGFR falls below 30 mL/min/1.73 m² during therapy**: Discontinue immediately.

### Iodinated Contrast Imaging Procedures [US]
- Discontinue at the time of, or prior to, the procedure in patients with an eGFR between 30 and 60 mL/min/1.73 m², or those with a history of liver disease, alcoholism, or heart failure. Re-evaluate eGFR 48 hours after the procedure; restart only if renal function is stable.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Renal Impairment**: Severe renal impairment (eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m²) [US].
- **Hypersensitivity**: Known hypersensitivity to metformin or glyburide [US].
- **Metabolic Acidosis**: Acute or chronic metabolic acidosis, including diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA), with or without coma [US].
- **Drug-Specific**: Concomitant administration of **bosentan** (increased risk of liver enzyme elevations) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning: Lactic Acidosis [US]
- **Risk**: Post-marketing cases of metformin-associated lactic acidosis have resulted in death, hypothermia, hypotension, and resistant bradyarrhythmias.
- **Presentation**: Often subtle with non-specific symptoms (malaise, myalgia, respiratory distress, somnolence, abdominal pain). 
- **Risk Factors**: Renal impairment, age ≥ 65 years, radiological studies with contrast, surgery, hypoxic states (e.g., heart failure), excessive alcohol, and hepatic impairment.
- **Management**: Discontinue immediately if suspected. Prompt hemodialysis is recommended.

### General Warnings
- **Hypoglycemia**: Glyburide can cause severe hypoglycemia. Proper patient selection and dosing are critical, particularly in the elderly and renally impaired [US].
- **Cardiovascular Mortality**: Historically, oral sulfonylurea drugs have been reported to be associated with increased cardiovascular mortality compared to treatment with diet alone or diet plus insulin [US].
- **Hemolytic Anemia**: Risk in patients with glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency; consider non-sulfonylurea alternatives [US].
- **Vitamin B12 Deficiency**: Metformin may lower serum B12 levels. Monitor hematological parameters annually and B12 every 2 to 3 years [US].
- **Macrovascular Outcomes**: No clinical studies have established conclusive evidence of macrovascular risk reduction [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%)**: Diarrhea (17%), nausea/vomiting (8%), abdominal pain (7%) [US].
- **Common (1–10%)**: Headache (9%), dizziness (6%), hypoglycemia (incidence varies by dose and patient history: 11% to 38% in initial therapy) [US].
- **Dermatologic**: Allergic skin reactions (pruritus, erythema, urticaria) occur in ~1.5% of glyburide-treated patients [US].
- **Serious Post-marketing Reports**: Cholestatic jaundice, hepatitis, liver failure, leukopenia, agranulocytosis, thrombocytopenia, and angioedema [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Bosentan**: Concomitant use increases the risk of hepatotoxicity; contraindicated [US].
- **Carbonic Anhydrase Inhibitors (e.g., topiramate, acetazolamide)**: May increase the risk of lactic acidosis [US].
- **OCT2/MATE Inhibitors (e.g., cimetidine, dolutegravir, ranolazine)**: May increase metformin systemic exposure [US].
- **Alcohol**: Potentiates the effect of metformin on lactate metabolism; warn patients against excessive intake [US].
- **Colesevelam**: Reduces glyburide absorption. Administer glyburide/metformin at least 4 hours **prior** to colesevelam [US].
- **Drugs affecting Glycemic Control**: Thiazides, corticosteroids, and estrogens may lead to loss of glycemic control (hyperglycemia) [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **US Category**: Not specified (Current US labeling uses a risk summary format). 
    - **Clinical Note**: Discontinue at least two weeks before expected delivery due to the risk of prolonged severe neonatal hypoglycemia [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: Metformin is present in human milk (approximately 0.11% to 1% of maternal weight-adjusted dose). Monitor breastfed infants for symptoms of hypoglycemia [US].
- **Fertility**: Treatment may result in ovulation in some anovulatory, premenopausal women, increasing the risk of unintended pregnancy [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and effectiveness have not been established in patients under 18 years of age [US].
- **Elderly Use**: Higher risk of hypoglycemia and lactic acidosis. Use cautious dose selection (starting at the low end) and monitor renal function more frequently [US].
- **Renal Impairment**: Metformin is renally excreted. Accumulation risk increases with the severity of renal impairment. Contraindicated in severe impairment (eGFR <30) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Avoid use; impaired lactate clearance may increase the risk of lactic acidosis [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**:
    - **Glyburide**: Stimulates insulin release from pancreatic beta cells by binding to sulfonylurea receptors and closing ATP-sensitive potassium channels [US].
    - **Metformin**: Decreases hepatic glucose production, decreases intestinal absorption of glucose, and improves insulin sensitivity by increasing peripheral glucose uptake [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Glyburide is significantly absorbed within 1 hour. Metformin bioavailability is 50–60%. Food delays metformin absorption but increases the peak concentration of the glyburide component [US].
    - **Distribution**: Glyburide is extensively protein-bound (non-ionic). Metformin is negligibly bound to plasma proteins [US].
    - **Metabolism**: Glyburide is metabolised by the liver (major metabolite 4-trans-hydroxy derivative). Metformin is not metabolised [US].
    - **Elimination**: Glyburide is excreted 50% in bile and 50% in urine. Metformin is excreted unchanged in the urine (90% within 24 hours) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Initial Therapy Study**: A 20-week study of 806 drug-naïve patients showed significant reductions in HbA1c and Fasting Plasma Glucose (FPG) with combination therapy compared to monotherapy or placebo. Mean HbA1c change from baseline: -1.48% (1.25/250 mg) and -1.53% (2.5/500 mg) vs -1.24% (glyburide alone) and -1.03% (metformin alone) [US].
- **Second-line Therapy Study**: A 16-week study of 639 patients inadequately controlled on sulfonylureas showed that glyburide/metformin 2.5/500 mg and 5/500 mg resulted in significantly greater HbA1c reductions (-1.7% to -1.9%) compared to glyburide or metformin monotherapy [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Container**: Dispense in light-resistant, tight containers [US].
