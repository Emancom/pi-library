# Editorial Product Information: Gallium Ga 68 Gozetotide

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of gallium Ga 68 gozetotide, synthesised for use by healthcare professionals. 
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis based on available regulatory documentation and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the United States (FDA) PI was provided for synthesis; references to other jurisdictions are noted as "not specified" where data was absent.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Gallium Ga 68 gozetotide (also known as PSMA-11).
- **Chemical Class:** Radioconjugate consisting of a prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA)-targeting ligand peptide conjugated via an acyclic radiometal chelator (HBED-CC) to the radioisotope Gallium-68.
- **Brand Name(s):** **ILLUCCIX** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Kit for the preparation of injection. 
    - Each kit contains: 
        - Vial 1: 25 mcg gozetotide (lyophilised powder).
        - Vial 2 (A or B): 150 mg anhydrous sodium acetate (buffer solution).
        - Vial 3: Sterile vacuumed reaction vial for radiolabelling.
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous bolus injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Telix Pharmaceuticals (US) Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
Gallium Ga 68 gozetotide is a radioactive diagnostic agent indicated for positron emission tomography (PET) of PSMA-positive lesions in men with prostate cancer for the following purposes [US]:
- Evaluation of suspected metastasis in patients who are candidates for initial definitive therapy.
- Evaluation of suspected recurrence based on elevated serum prostate-specific antigen (PSA) levels.
- Identification and selection of patients for whom PSMA-directed therapy is indicated.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** The recommended radioactivity dose is 111 MBq to 259 MBq (3 mCi to 7 mCi) administered as an intravenous bolus injection [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Use aseptic technique and standard radiation safety measures.
    - Inspect the final solution visually; it should be clear, colorless to slightly yellow, and free of particulate matter.
    - The dose must be assayed in a dose calibrator immediately prior to administration.
    - Follow the injection with an intravenous flush of sterile 0.9% Sodium Chloride to ensure full dose delivery.
- **Patient Preparation:** 
    - Patients should be well-hydrated prior to the procedure and encouraged to void immediately before imaging and frequently for the first hour post-administration to reduce radiation exposure [US].
    - A diuretic may be administered at the time of the radiotracer injection to reduce artifact from urinary accumulation, unless contraindicated.
- **Image Acquisition:** PET scanning should begin 50 to 100 minutes post-injection. The scan typically proceeds cranially from the proximal thighs to the skull base/vertex [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None listed [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Risk for Misinterpretation:** PET imaging with gallium Ga 68 gozetotide is subject to interpretation errors. Uptake is not pathognomonic for prostate cancer and may occur in other malignancies or non-malignant conditions (e.g., Paget’s disease, fibrous dysplasia, osteophytosis). Conversely, tumors that do not express PSMA will not be visualised. Clinical correlation with histopathology or other diagnostic modalities is recommended [US].
- **Radiation Risks:** Gallium Ga 68 gozetotide contributes to cumulative radiation exposure. Ensure strict adherence to safety handling protocols to protect patients and healthcare workers [US].
- **Monitoring:** Performance of the PET scan may be affected by serum PSA levels (in recurrence settings) or Gleason scores (in metastatic settings) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions reported in clinical trials occurred at low frequencies.
- **Common Adverse Effects (0.5% – 10%):**
    - Fatigue (1.2%)
    - Nausea (0.8%)
    - Constipation (0.5%)
    - Vomiting (0.5%)
- **Uncommon Adverse Effects (< 0.5%):** Diarrhea, dry mouth, injection site reactions (hematoma, warmth), and chills [US].
- **Postmarketing Experience:** Injection site pain has been reported voluntarily [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Androgen Deprivation Therapy (ADT):** ADT and other therapies targeting the androgen pathway (e.g., androgen receptor antagonists) may alter PSMA expression and subsequently change the uptake of gallium Ga 68 gozetotide in prostate cancer lesions. The precise clinical effect of these therapies on PET scan performance has not been established [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** There is no indicated use for this product in females. While no human or animal data are available, radiopharmaceuticals have the potential to cause fetal harm depending on the radiation dose and gestational stage [US].
- **Breastfeeding/Lactation:** Not indicated for use in females. There are no data regarding the presence of the drug in human milk or its effects on the breastfed infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No animal studies have been conducted to evaluate effects on fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in paediatric populations have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies included a high proportion of patients aged 65 and over (up to 75%). No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed between elderly and younger adult patients [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Specific dosing adjustments for renal or hepatic impairment are not specified in available sources. However, the drug is primarily cleared renally (14% in the first 2 hours), and hydration is advised to facilitate clearance [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Gallium Ga 68 gozetotide binds to cells expressing PSMA, including malignant prostate cancer cells. The Gallium-68 isotope is a $\beta+$ emitter, allowing for PET imaging.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The relationship between plasma concentrations and imaging efficacy was not explored in clinical trials [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Following intravenous injection, the drug is cleared from the blood and accumulates preferentially in the liver (15%), kidneys (7%), spleen (2%), and salivary glands (0.5%). There is minimal uptake in the cerebral cortex or heart.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 14% of the dose is excreted in the urine within the first 2 hours post-injection [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study 1 (PSMA-PreRP):** A prospective study of 325 patients with biopsy-proven prostate cancer scheduled for prostatectomy. PET imaging was evaluated for detecting pelvic lymph node metastasis against a histopathology reference standard. Sensitivity was 47% and specificity was 90% [US].
- **Study 2 (PSMA-BCR):** A prospective study of 635 patients with biochemical recurrence. 74% of patients had at least one positive region detected on PET, with a higher likelihood of detection as PSA levels increased [US].
- **Study 3 (NCT03511664):** Evaluated 1,003 patients with metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC) to determine eligibility for PSMA-directed therapy [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Kit Storage:** Store refrigerated between 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) in the original packaging. Do not freeze [US].
- **Post-Preparation Shelf Life:** The radiolabelled injection must be stored upright with appropriate lead shielding at room temperature (up to 25°C) and used within 4 hours of preparation [US].
