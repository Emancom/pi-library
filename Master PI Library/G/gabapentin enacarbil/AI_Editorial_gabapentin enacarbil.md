# Editorial Product Information: Gabapentin Enacarbil

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of gabapentin enacarbil for healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, regulatory warnings, and dosing strategies into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not intended to replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for the most current legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Gabapentin enacarbil (a prodrug of gabapentin).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid; carbamate.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Horizant** [US].
- **ATC Code:** N03AX (Gabapentin).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Extended-release tablets: 300 mg and 600 mg [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Azurity Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Restless Legs Syndrome (RLS):** Treatment of moderate-to-severe primary RLS in adults [US-approved].
- **Postherpetic Neuralgia (PHN):** Management of PHN in adults [US-approved].

*Note: Gabapentin enacarbil is not recommended for patients who must sleep during the day and remain awake at night [US].*

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Instructions
- **Administration:** Tablets must be swallowed whole; do not cut, crush, or chew.
- **Food Requirement:** Must be taken with food to ensure optimal absorption [US].
- **Substitutability:** **Gabapentin enacarbil is NOT substitutable with other gabapentin products** on a milligram-per-milligram basis due to differing pharmacokinetic profiles [US].

### Restless Legs Syndrome (Adults)
- **Standard Dose:** 600 mg once daily at approximately 5:00 PM.
- **Note on Higher Doses:** Doses of 1,200 mg daily provide no additional clinical benefit but increase the incidence of adverse reactions.

### Postherpetic Neuralgia (Adults)
- **Titration Schedule:** 600 mg once daily in the morning for 3 days.
- **Maintenance Dose:** Increase to 600 mg twice daily (1,200 mg/day) starting on day 4.
- **Note on Higher Doses:** Doses exceeding 1,200 mg/day have not shown additional benefit in 12-week clinical trials.

### Missed Dose Instructions
- **RLS:** If a dose is missed at the scheduled time, the next dose should be taken the following day as originally prescribed.
- **PHN:** If a dose is missed, skip that dose and take the next dose at the next scheduled time.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None listed** in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Driving Impairment:** Gabapentin enacarbil may cause significant driving impairment. Patients should not drive until they have sufficient experience with the medication to gauge their level of impairment. Note that a patient's self-assessment of their driving competence may be unreliable [US].
- **Somnolence and Dizziness:** These are the most common adverse effects and can impair the ability to operate complex machinery.
- **Suicidal Behavior and Ideation:** As a prodrug of an antiepileptic drug (AED), gabapentin enacarbil increases the risk of suicidal thoughts. Monitor patients for new or worsening depression and unusual mood changes.
- **Abrupt Discontinuation:** Rapid withdrawal may increase seizure risk, even in patients without a history of epilepsy. Withdrawal symptoms (agitation, confusion, etc.) have been reported with gabapentinoids.
- **Respiratory Depression:** Serious, life-threatening, or fatal respiratory depression may occur, particularly when co-administered with CNS depressants (e.g., opioids) or in patients with underlying respiratory impairment.
- **DRESS/Multiorgan Hypersensitivity:** Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS) has been reported. Discontinue immediately if fever, rash, or lymphadenopathy occurs without an alternative etiology.
- **Tumorigenic Potential:** In animal studies (rats), an increased incidence of pancreatic acinar cell adenomas and carcinomas was observed. The clinical significance in humans is unknown [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Alcohol:** Ethanol causes a more rapid release of the drug from the extended-release formulation. Consumption of alcohol is not recommended [US].
- **Morphine:** Co-administration results in increased CNS effects (somnolence, dizziness, nausea). No significant changes in the pharmacokinetics of gabapentin or morphine were observed, suggesting a pharmacodynamic interaction.
- **Cimetidine:** A minor increase in gabapentin AUC (24%) and a decrease in renal clearance (20%) occur, but these are generally not considered clinically significant.
- **Probenecid:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:**
    - **US Category:** Not specified as a lettered category in the revised labeling, but the source notes that based on animal data, it may cause fetal harm.
    - **Clinical Data:** No adequate data in pregnant women. Animal studies in rats and rabbits at doses exceeding the MRHD showed developmental toxicity (mortality, decreased weight, skeletal ossification delays).
    - **Neonatal Withdrawal:** There are reports of neonatal withdrawal syndrome in infants exposed to gabapentin in utero, particularly with co-exposure to opioids.
- **Breastfeeding:** Gabapentin (derived from the prodrug) is known to be secreted in human milk following oral administration of other gabapentin products. The effect on the breastfed infant is unknown.
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in rats at doses up to 39 times the MRHD [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients below 18 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed between elderly (≥65 years) and younger patients in PHN trials. However, because elderly patients are more likely to have reduced renal function, dosage adjustment is required based on creatinine clearance (CrCl).
- **Renal Impairment:** Gabapentin is cleared renally. Dosage must be adjusted as follows:

#### RLS Renal Dosing [US]
| CrCl (mL/min) | Target Dose Regimen |
| :--- | :--- |
| ≥60 | 600 mg once daily |
| 30–59 | 300 mg daily, increase to 600 mg as needed |
| 15–29 | 300 mg once daily |
| <15 | 300 mg every other day |
| <15 (Hemodialysis) | Not recommended |

#### PHN Renal Dosing [US]
| CrCl (mL/min) | Titration | Maintenance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ≥60 | 600mg AM x 3 days | 600mg twice daily |
| 30–59 | 300mg AM x 3 days | 300mg twice daily (may inc. to 600mg BID) |
| 15–29 | 300mg AM on Day 1 & 3 | 300mg once daily (may inc. to 300mg BID) |
| <15 | None | 300mg every other day (may inc. to 300mg daily) |
| <15 (HD) | None | 300mg after each dialysis (may inc. to 600mg) |

- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources (gabapentin is not significantly metabolised by the liver).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Gabapentin enacarbil is a prodrug of gabapentin. Gabapentin binds with high affinity to the $\alpha_2\delta$ subunit of voltage-activated calcium channels. The exact mechanism for RLS and PHN efficacy is unknown.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absorbed throughout the GI tract via the MCT-1 transporter. Bioavailability is ~75% when taken with food.
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is <3%. Volume of distribution is 76 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Undergoes extensive first-pass hydrolysis by carboxylesterases (primarily in enterocytes) to form gabapentin. It is not a substrate/inhibitor for major CYP enzymes.
    - **Elimination:** Excreted unchanged by the kidneys. The elimination half-life is 5.1 to 6.0 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **RLS:** Two 12-week trials demonstrated significant improvement in IRLS (International Restless Legs Syndrome) Rating Scale scores and CGI-I (Clinical Global Impression of Improvement) responders at doses of 600 mg and 1,200 mg daily compared to placebo [US].
- **PHN:** A 12-week study showed significant reduction in mean pain scores at doses of 1,200 mg, 2,400 mg, and 3,600 mg daily. Benefit was observed as early as Week 1 [US].
- **Driving Studies:** Simulated driving trials showed significant impairment at doses of 1,200 mg and 1,800 mg. At 600 mg, no significant difference in lane position variability was noted, though some patients experienced simulated crashes.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C (59°F–86°F).
- **Special Handling:** Protect from moisture. Keep in the original bottle and do not remove the desiccant canister.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only. Not a controlled substance.
