# Unified Product Information: Gadoteridol

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** Gadoteridol (marketed as ProHance)
**Active Ingredient:** Gadoteridol

Gadoteridol is a non-ionic, paramagnetic contrast agent used in Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI). It is supplied as a sterile, non-pyrogenic, clear, colorless to slightly yellow solution for intravenous injection. Each mL contains 279.3 mg of gadoteridol (0.5 mmol/mL).

## 2. Approved Indications
Gadoteridol is indicated for use in MRI to facilitate visualization of:

*   **Central Nervous System (CNS):** Enhancement of images of the brain, spine, and associated tissues in adults and pediatric patients to identify lesions with abnormal vascularity or a compromised blood-brain barrier.
    *   *Note:* In the United States, this includes term neonates [FDA]. In Australia, this indication is approved for children from 2 years of age [TGA].
*   **Head and Neck:** Visualization of extracranial or extraspinal lesions in the head and neck in adults [FDA].
*   **Whole Body:** Enhancement of MRI images for the whole body, including the liver, breast, musculoskeletal system, and soft tissues [TGA].

## 3. Contraindications
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Gadoteridol is contraindicated in patients with a known history of severe hypersensitivity or allergic reactions to gadoteridol, its excipients (such as calteridol calcium or trometamol), or other gadolinium-based contrast agents (GBCAs).
*   **Age Restrictions:** Use is contraindicated in children under 6 months of age [TGA].

## 4. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed Warning: Intrathecal Use and Nephrogenic Systemic Fibrosis (NSF)
*   **Intrathecal Administration:** Gadoteridol must **not** be administered intrathecally. Reports indicate that intrathecal use of GBCAs can result in death, coma, seizures, and encephalopathy.
*   **Nephrogenic Systemic Fibrosis (NSF):** GBCAs increase the risk of NSF in patients with impaired elimination. This is a potentially fatal or debilitating condition characterized by fibrosis of the skin, muscle, and internal organs. The risk is highest in patients with:
    *   Chronic, severe kidney disease (GFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m²).
    *   Acute kidney injury.
    *   Acute renal insufficiency of any severity due to hepato-renal syndrome or during the perioperative liver transplantation period.

### Gadolinium Retention
Evidence suggests that gadolinium may be retained in various organs—including the brain, bone, and skin—for months or years following administration. While the clinical significance of brain accumulation is currently unknown, higher retention is associated with linear GBCAs compared to macrocyclic agents like gadoteridol. Clinicians should use the lowest effective dose and carefully assess the risk-benefit ratio for repeated imaging studies.

### Hypersensitivity Reactions
Anaphylactic and anaphylactoid reactions, including fatalities, have occurred. Patients with a history of asthma or prior reactions to contrast media are at increased risk. Facilities must be equipped with emergency resuscitation equipment and trained personnel. Patients should be monitored for at least 30 to 120 minutes post-injection [FDA/TGA].

### Central Nervous System Disorders
Caution should be exercised in patients with a low seizure threshold or pre-existing neurological disorders, as GBCAs may trigger convulsions [TGA].

## 5. Dosage and Administration

### Standard Dosing
*   **Adults and Pediatrics:** The recommended dose is **0.1 mmol/kg (0.2 mL/kg)** administered as a rapid intravenous infusion or bolus.
*   **Supplemental Dosing (Adults Only):** In adult patients with normal renal function, if the initial 0.1 mmol/kg dose results in equivocal or poor visualization of CNS lesions, a supplemental dose of **0.2 mmol/kg (0.4 mL/kg)** may be administered up to 30 minutes after the first injection [FDA/TGA].

### Administration Instructions
*   **Preparation:** Visually inspect the solution for particulate matter and discoloration. Discard if found.
*   **Flush:** Follow the injection with a minimum 5 mL normal saline flush to ensure complete delivery of the contrast medium.
*   **Imaging Window:** The MRI procedure should ideally be completed within one hour of injection.
*   **Mixing:** Gadoteridol should not be physically mixed with any other drugs or parenteral nutrition.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The overall incidence of adverse reactions is approximately 5.8%. The most frequently reported effects (≥0.4%) include:
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea (1.4%), dry mouth, vomiting.
*   **Nervous System:** Headache (0.7%), dysgeusia (taste perversion/metallic taste, 0.9%), dizziness, paraesthesia.
*   **Skin:** Urticaria (0.4%), pruritus, rash.
*   **Injection Site:** Coldness, pain, or erythema.

**Post-Marketing/Rare Events:** Severe reactions such as cardiac arrest, arrhythmia, laryngospasm, pulmonary edema, and acute renal failure have been reported rarely (<0.1%).

## 7. Drug Interactions
No formal drug-to-drug interaction studies have been conducted. Gadoteridol is not known to interfere with common laboratory tests, although transitory changes in serum iron have been observed without clinical significance [TGA].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

### Pregnancy
*   **Risk Summary:** Gadoteridol crosses the placenta. Human data on fetal risk are limited and inconclusive. Animal studies showed increased post-implantation loss at high doses (16–19 times the human dose).
*   **Clinical Advice:** Use gadoteridol during pregnancy only if the imaging is essential and cannot be delayed.

### Breastfeeding
*   **Excretion:** Small amounts of gadoteridol are excreted in human milk.
*   **Clinical Advice:** While the risk to the infant is considered low due to poor gastrointestinal absorption of GBCAs, a conservative approach suggests that nursing may be temporarily discontinued for at least 24 hours following administration [TGA].

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** Gadoteridol rapidly distributes into the extracellular space. It does not bind to plasma proteins.
*   **Metabolism:** No detectable biotransformation or decomposition occurs.
*   **Elimination:** The drug is eliminated exclusively by the kidneys via glomerular filtration. In patients with normal renal function, approximately 94% of the dose is excreted within 24 hours.
*   **Half-Life:** The elimination half-life is approximately 1.57 hours. In patients with severe renal impairment (not on dialysis), the half-life may extend to over 9 hours.
*   **Dialysis:** Gadoteridol is dialyzable; roughly 72% is removed after one session and 98% after three sessions.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Gadoteridol is a paramagnetic contrast agent. When placed in a magnetic field, it creates a local magnetic field that shortens the T1 (spin-lattice) relaxation time of water protons in the target tissues. This increase in relaxation rate enhances the signal intensity (brightness) of the image, particularly in T1-weighted sequences, allowing for better visualization of tissues where the blood-brain barrier is disrupted.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C. Excursions between 15°C and 30°C are permitted.
*   **Protection:** Protect from light and secondary x-rays. Do not freeze.
*   **Freezing:** If the product freezes, bring it to room temperature for at least 60 minutes. If the solution becomes clear and all solids redissolve, it may be used. If solids persist, discard the vial.
*   **Usage:** Gadoteridol is for single use only. Any unused portion must be discarded.
