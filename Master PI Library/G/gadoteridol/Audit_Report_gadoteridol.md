## Close Paraphrases
1.  **Storage/Freezing Instructions:** 
    *   **Editorial:** "If the product freezes, bring it to room temperature for at least 60 minutes. If the solution becomes clear and all solids redissolve, it may be used. If solids persist, discard the vial."
    *   **Source:** Doc 2 (FDA) Page 16: "Should freezing occur... brought to room temperature before use... stand... for a minimum of 60 minutes... return to a clear... all solids are redissolved... Should solids persist, discard vial."

    *   **Source:** Doc 1 (TGA) Page 9 and Doc 2 (FDA) Page 13: "94.4 ±4.8%... excreted within 24 hours post injection."
    *   **Risk:** Technical data points must remain accurate, so high similarity is expected here.

## Originality Strengths
*   **The "Editorial Note":** Completely original framing that explains the methodology of the document.
*   **Indication Harmonisation:** The writer did an excellent job of synthesizing the geographic differences in Section 2, specifically contrasting the FDA approval for neonates against the TGA's "from 2 years of age" restriction.
*   **Adverse Effects:** Section 6 successfully aggregates data from the clinical trial tables in the source documents into a readable narrative format with original categorization (Gastrointestinal, Nervous System, etc.).
*   **Breastfeeding Section:** The editorial provides a clear, practical synthesis of the conservative TGA advice versus the more general risk summary in the FDA document.

## Differences from Source PIs
*   **Structural Consolidation:** The editorial merges the "Highlights" of the FDA PI with the detailed "Clinical Particulars" of the TGA PI to create a more efficient single-page reference.
*   **Terminology Updates:** The editorial uses the clinical term "dysgeusia," whereas the TGA document primarily uses "taste disturbance" and the FDA uses "dysgeusia" in tables; the editorial chose the more professional medical term consistently.
*   **Omissions:** The editorial wisely omitted the extensive chemical formulas (e.g., [10-(2-hydroxypropyl-O)-1,4,7,10...]) and detailed clinical study results (trial sizes/demographics) to focus on "Product Information" relevant to a prescribing physician.
*   **Formatting:** Use of bolding and bulleted lists for "Warnings and Precautions" makes the editorial significantly more scannable than the dense paragraph blocks found in the source documents.

## Recommendation
**PASS**

The editorial is a high-quality synthesis. It successfully avoids the "copy-paste" trap often found in regulatory writing while maintaining the precision required for medical compliance. The few instances of verbatim or near-verbatim text are limited to essential safety warnings and technical physical descriptions where deviation could lead to clinical ambiguity. No revisions are required.
