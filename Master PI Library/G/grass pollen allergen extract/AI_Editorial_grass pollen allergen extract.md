# Editorial Product Information: Timothy Grass Pollen Allergen Extract

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial synthesis: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Timothy grass pollen allergen extract for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a clinically focused reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes. It does not replace the official PI or summary of product characteristics required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Timothy Grass (*Phleum pratense*) pollen allergen extract.
- **Chemical Class:** Standardised allergen extract.
- **Brand Name(s):** **GRASTEK** [US].
- **ATC Code:** V01AA02.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sublingual tablet containing 2800 Bioequivalent Allergy Units (BAU) [US]. 
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Sublingual.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** ALK-Abelló A/S [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Allergic Rhinitis/Conjunctivitis:** Indicated as immunotherapy for the treatment of grass pollen-induced allergic rhinitis, with or without conjunctivitis. 
- **Diagnostic Requirement:** Diagnosis must be confirmed by positive skin testing or *in vitro* testing for pollen-specific IgE antibodies to Timothy grass or cross-reactive grass pollens [US].
- **Approved Age Range:** Approved for use in patients aged 5 through 65 years [US].
- **Limitation of Use:** Not indicated for the immediate relief of acute allergic symptoms [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Children ≥5 years):** One 2800 BAU tablet administered sublingual once daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Place the tablet immediately under the tongue and allow it to dissolve completely.
    - The patient must not swallow for at least 1 minute after application.
    - Avoid food and beverages for at least 5 minutes following administration.
    - Wash hands after handling the tablet [US].
- **Supervision Requirements:** The initial dose must be administered in a healthcare setting under the supervision of a physician experienced in treating allergic diseases. The patient must be observed for at least 30 minutes to monitor for serious systemic or local allergic reactions [US].
- **Pediatric Administration:** Children must be supervised by an adult during administration [US].
- **Treatment Timeline:** 
    - **Seasonal:** Initiate treatment at least 12 weeks before the expected start of the grass pollen season and continue throughout the season. 
    - **Sustained Effect:** For efficacy that persists for one season after treatment cessation, daily administration for three consecutive years is recommended [US].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, do not double the next dose. Resume the regular schedule the following day. If more than one dose is missed, the patient should contact their healthcare provider [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Asthma:** Severe, unstable, or uncontrolled asthma [US].
- **History of Severe Reactions:** A history of any severe systemic allergic reaction or severe local reaction following sublingual allergen immunotherapy [US].
- **Eosinophilic Esophagitis (EoE):** A known history of EoE [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to inactive ingredients, including gelatin, mannitol, or sodium hydroxide [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning (Severe Allergic Reactions):** **GRASTEK** can cause life-threatening allergic reactions, including anaphylaxis and severe laryngopharyngeal restriction. Prescribers must provide an epinephrine auto-injector and train patients on its use [US].
- **Epinephrine Suitability:** The product may not be appropriate for patients with medical conditions (e.g., compromised lung function, unstable angina, recent MI) that reduce their ability to survive a serious allergic reaction. It may also be unsuitable for patients taking medications that interfere with epinephrine, such as beta-blockers [US].
- **Upper Airway Compromise:** Severe local reactions (laryngopharyngeal swelling) may occur. Discontinuation should be considered if local reactions escalate [US].
- **Eosinophilic Esophagitis:** Patients experiencing severe or persistent gastro-esophageal symptoms (dysphagia, chest pain) should be evaluated for EoE and treatment discontinued if confirmed [US].
- **Asthma Management:** Treatment should be withheld during acute asthma exacerbations. Patients with recurrent exacerbations require re-evaluation and potential discontinuation [US].
- **Oral Inflammation/Wounds:** Treatment should be interrupted to allow complete healing following oral surgery, dental extractions, or in cases of oral inflammation (e.g., thrush, mouth ulcers) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Oral pruritus (26.7%), throat irritation (22.6%), ear pruritus (12.5%), and mouth edema (11.1%) [US].
- **Common (1–10%):** Tongue pruritus, lip swelling, paraesthesia oral, pharyngeal edema, glossitis, stomatitis, and nausea [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:**
    - Systemic allergic reactions (anaphylaxis).
    - Laryngopharyngeal swelling.
    - Asthma exacerbations.
    - Eosinophilic esophagitis [US].
- **Postmarketing Reports [European/US]:** Anaphylactic shock, ulcerative colitis exacerbation, altered state of consciousness, tachycardia, hypotension, and angioedema.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **Beta-Adrenergic Blockers:** May render patients unresponsive to standard doses of epinephrine used to treat anaphylaxis [US].
    - **Alpha-Blockers/Ergot Alkaloids:** May antagonise the vasoconstricting effects or reverse the pressor effects of epinephrine [US].
    - **TCAs, MAOIs, and Certain Antihistamines:** May potentiate the adverse effects of epinephrine [US].
    - **Cardiac Glycosides/Diuretics:** May increase the risk of cardiac arrhythmias if epinephrine is administered [US].
- **Concomitant Immunotherapy:** Use with other allergen immunotherapies has not been studied and may increase the risk of local or systemic reactions [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified under the current PLLR system, but animal data (mice) using 6.7 times the human dose showed no evidence of fetal harm or impaired fertility [US].
    - **Clinical Consideration:** Available human data are insufficient to establish drug-associated risks.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if the extract is excreted in human milk. The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the mother's clinical need and potential effects on the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies revealed no treatment-related post-implantation losses or malformations [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Established in children 5 through 17 years. Safety and efficacy in children <5 years have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** There is no clinical trial experience in patients over 65 years of age [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** The precise mechanism of allergen immunotherapy for Timothy grass pollen is not fully established [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Immunotherapy typically involves inducing immunological tolerance by modifying the T-cell response and increasing specific IgG4 antibodies.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **First Season Efficacy:** In a trial of 1,501 subjects (ages 5-65), **GRASTEK** demonstrated a 23% reduction in the Total Combined Score (symptoms plus medication use) over the entire grass pollen season compared to placebo [US].
- **Pediatric Efficacy:** A trial of 344 pediatric subjects (ages 5-17) showed a 26% reduction in the Total Combined Score compared to placebo [US].
- **Sustained Effect:** A 5-year study showed that three years of daily treatment provided a sustained reduction in symptoms for one year after treatment was discontinued, though the effect was not maintained into the second post-treatment year [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at controlled room temperature, 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Excursions are permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Store in the original package until use to protect from moisture [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Not specified in available sources; refer to the "EXP" date on the carton.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** 
    - **US:** Prescription-only (Rx-only).
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
