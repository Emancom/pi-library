# Editorial Product Information: Columvi (glofitamab)

**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been synthesized from official regulatory sources: the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). Where clinical recommendations or approved indications differ between these agencies, the most comprehensive or conservative guidance has been prioritized, with the source agency noted in brackets (e.g., [TGA] or [FDA]).

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** COLUMVI®  
**Active Ingredient:** Glofitamab (glofitamab-gxbm in the US)  
**Therapeutic Class:** Antineoplastic agent; Bispecific CD20-directed CD3 T-cell engager.

---

## 2. Approved Indications
Columvi is indicated for the treatment of adult patients with relapsed or refractory (R/R) diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL), not otherwise specified (NOS), or large B-cell lymphoma (LBCL) arising from follicular lymphoma, after two or more lines of systemic therapy.

**Specific Agency Provisions:**
*   **Accelerated/Provisional Approval:** In both the US and Australia, this indication is approved under accelerated/provisional pathways based on response rate and durability of response. Continued approval may be contingent upon verification of clinical benefit in confirmatory trials.
*   **Combination Therapy:** Columvi is also indicated in Australia in combination with gemcitabine and oxaliplatin (following obinutuzumab pretreatment) for adult patients with R/R DLBCL NOS who are not candidates for autologous stem cell transplant (ASCT) [TGA].
*   **Exclusions:** Columvi is not indicated for the treatment of patients with primary central nervous system (CNS) lymphoma [TGA].

---

## 3. Contraindications
Columvi is contraindicated in patients with a known hypersensitivity to glofitamab or any of its excipients [TGA]. The FDA PI lists no specific contraindications.

---

## 4. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed Warning: Cytokine Release Syndrome (CRS) and Neurologic Toxicity
Columvi can cause serious and life-threatening CRS and Neurologic Toxicity, including Immune Effector Cell-Associated Neurotoxicity Syndrome (ICANS). 
*   **CRS:** Manifestations include fever, tachycardia, hypotension, and hypoxia. Risk is highest during the step-up dosing phase.
*   **ICANS:** Manifestations include confusional state, delirium, somnolence, and cognitive impairment.

### Serious Infections
Severe, including fatal, bacterial, fungal, or viral infections have occurred. Columvi should not be initiated in patients with active infections. Prophylaxis for *Pneumocystis jirovecii* pneumonia (PJP) and herpes virus is recommended for patients at increased risk. Cases of Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy (PML) have been reported [FDA].

### Haemophagocytic Lymphohistiocytosis (HLH)
Life-threatening HLH has been reported. This should be considered if CRS is atypical, prolonged, or refractory to standard management. If HLH is suspected, Columvi must be interrupted [TGA].

### Tumor Flare
Manifestations include localized pain and swelling at the site of lymphoma lesions. Bulky tumors near airways or vital organs require close monitoring for mass-effect complications.

### Tumor Lysis Syndrome (TLS)
TLS has been reported, particularly in patients with high tumor burden or renal insufficiency. Hydration and anti-hyperuricemics are recommended prior to infusion.

### Immunization
The safety of live vaccines during or following therapy has not been studied; their use is not recommended during treatment.

## 5. Dosage and Administration

### Pre-treatment and Prophylaxis
To reduce the risk of CRS, all patients must receive:
1.  **Obinutuzumab Pre-treatment:** A single 1,000 mg dose on Cycle 1, Day 1 (7 days prior to the first Columvi dose).
2.  **Premedication:** Dexamethasone (20 mg IV), an anti-pyretic, and an anti-histamine must be administered prior to each dose in the step-up phase and subsequent doses if CRS occurred previously.

### Recommended Dosing Schedule (21-Day Cycles)
*   **Cycle 1, Day 8 (Step-up Dose 1):** 2.5 mg
*   **Cycle 1, Day 15 (Step-up Dose 2):** 10 mg
*   **Cycle 2 onwards, Day 1:** 30 mg
*   **Duration:** Maximum of 12 cycles or until disease progression/unacceptable toxicity.

### Monitoring and Hospitalization
*   **2.5 mg Dose:** Patients should be hospitalized during the infusion and for 24 hours following completion.
*   **10 mg Dose:** Hospitalization is required for 24 hours post-infusion if any grade CRS occurred with the 2.5 mg dose [FDA/TGA]. 
*   **30 mg Dose:** Hospitalization is required for 24 hours post-infusion if Grade ≥2 CRS occurred with the previous dose.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most common adverse reactions (≥20%) across clinical trials include:
*   Cytokine Release Syndrome (reported in up to 70% of patients)
*   Musculoskeletal pain
*   Rash
*   Fatigue
*   Gastrointestinal disturbances (nausea, constipation, diarrhea)

**Laboratory Abnormalities:** Very common Grade 3–4 abnormalities include decreased lymphocyte count, decreased phosphate, decreased neutrophil count (neutropenia), and increased uric acid.

---

## 7. Drug Interactions
No formal drug-drug interaction studies have been conducted. The transient release of cytokines at the start of treatment may suppress CYP450 enzymes. 
*   **Clinical Management:** Monitor patients receiving concomitant CYP450 substrates with a narrow therapeutic index (e.g., warfarin) during the first week of Cycle 1 (Days 8 and 15) and during episodes of CRS.

---

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

### Pregnancy
Columvi may cause fetal harm, including B-cell depletion and increased risk of opportunistic infections in the neonate. 
*   **Pregnancy Testing:** Verify status in females of reproductive potential prior to initiation.
*   **Contraception:** Females must use highly effective contraception during treatment and for **2 months** following the last dose [TGA—most conservative; FDA suggests 1 month].

### Breastfeeding
It is unknown if glofitamab is excreted in human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in the infant, patients should discontinue breastfeeding during treatment and for **2 months** after the final dose [TGA—most conservative; FDA suggests 1 month].

---

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** Glofitamab exhibits a bi-exponential decline. The total volume of distribution is approximately 5.6 L.
*   **Elimination:** The terminal half-life at steady state is approximately 7.6 to 7.9 days. Clearance is approximately 0.6 L/day.
*   **Special Populations:** No clinically significant PK differences were observed based on age (21–90 years), mild-to-moderate renal impairment, or mild hepatic impairment. Data for severe renal or moderate-to-severe hepatic impairment are unavailable.

---

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Glofitamab is a bispecific antibody designed with a 2:1 molecular format (bivalent for CD20, monovalent for CD3). It simultaneously binds to CD20 on the surface of B-cells and the CD3 epsilon subunit of the T-cell receptor complex. This dual binding induces an immunological synapse, leading to T-cell activation, proliferation, and the subsequent release of cytolytic proteins that cause the lysis of CD20-expressing B-cells.

---

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Vials:** Store in the original carton to protect from light at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze or shake.
*   **Diluted Solution:** If not used immediately, the diluted infusion solution can be stored for up to 64 hours at 2°C to 8°C or for up to 4 hours at room temperature (up to 25°C).
