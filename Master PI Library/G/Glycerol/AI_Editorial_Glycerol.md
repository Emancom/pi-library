# Editorial Product Information: Glycerol Phenylbutyrate

- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis intended for healthcare professionals. It provides a clinically accurate overview of the product’s regulatory and clinical profile.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Glycerol phenylbutyrate (a nitrogen-binding agent).
- **Chemical Class:** Phenylbutyric acid triglyceride.
- **Brand Names:** Not explicitly named in the primary text (Generic: Glycerol Phenylbutyrate) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Oral liquid, 1.1 g/mL (delivers 1.02 g/mL of phenylbutyrate) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral; may also be administered via nasogastric or gastrostomy tube.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Navinta LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Management of Urea Cycle Disorders (UCDs):** Indicated for use as a nitrogen-binding agent in patients with UCDs who cannot be managed by dietary protein restriction and/or amino acid supplementation alone. It must be used in conjunction with dietary protein restriction and, in some cases, dietary supplements (e.g., essential amino acids, arginine, citrulline) [US].
- **Limitations of Use:** 
    - Not indicated for the treatment of acute hyperammonemia in patients with UCDs, as more rapid interventions are required [US].
    - Safety and efficacy for the treatment of N-acetylglutamate synthase (NAGS) deficiency have not been established [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Administration:** Should be prescribed by a physician experienced in UCD management. Must be taken with food or formula. For infants, administer just prior to breastfeeding [US].
- **Initial Dosage (Phenylbutyrate-Naïve Patients):**
    - Range: 4.5 to 11.2 mL/m²/day (5 to 12.4 g/m²/day).
    - Starting dose for patients with residual enzyme activity: 4.5 mL/m²/day.
    - Factors for determination: Urea synthetic capacity, dietary protein intake, and diet adherence [US].
- **Switching from Sodium Phenylbutyrate (Tablets/Powder):**
    - Patients should receive a dose containing the same amount of phenylbutyric acid.
    - **Conversion (Tablets):** Total daily sodium phenylbutyrate (g) x 0.86 = Total daily glycerol phenylbutyrate (mL).
    - **Conversion (Powder):** Total daily sodium phenylbutyrate (g) x 0.81 = Total daily glycerol phenylbutyrate (mL) [US].
- **Dosing Schedule:**
    - **≥ 2 years:** 3 equally divided doses, rounded up to the nearest 0.5 mL.
    - **< 2 years:** 3 or more equally divided doses, rounded up to the nearest 0.1 mL.
    - **Maximum Dosage:** 17.5 mL (19 g) per day [US].
- **Monitoring and Adjustment:**
    - Monitor plasma ammonia levels to guide titration.
    - Target (≥ 6 years): Maintain fasting plasma ammonia < 0.5 times the upper limit of normal (ULN).
    - Target (< 6 years): Keep the first morning ammonia below the ULN [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** Contraindicated in patients with known hypersensitivity to phenylbutyrate. Signs include wheezing, dyspnea, coughing, hypotension, flushing, nausea, and rash [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Neurotoxicity:** Phenylacetate (PAA), the active moiety, may be toxic. Symptoms include somnolence, fatigue, headache, dysgeusia, hypoacusis, disorientation, and impaired memory. If these occur in the absence of high ammonia, consider reducing the dosage [US].
- **Pancreatic Insufficiency or Malabsorption:** Low or absent pancreatic enzymes (required to hydrolyze the drug) may result in reduced absorption and poor ammonia control. Monitor ammonia levels closely in these patients [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Regular assessment of plasma ammonia, and if available, PAA/PAGN (phenylacetylglutamine) ratios and urinary PAGN measurements [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Data (Adults)
| Adverse Reaction | Glycerol Phenylbutyrate (%) | Sodium Phenylbutyrate (%) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| Diarrhea | 16% | 7% |
| Headache | 14% | 9% |
| Flatulence | 14% | 2% |
| Abdominal Pain | 7% | 4% |
| Vomiting | 7% | 4% |
| Fatigue | 7% | 2% |

### Pediatric Adverse Effects
- **Ages 2 to 17 years:** Common (≥10%) reactions include upper abdominal pain, rash, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, decreased appetite, and headache.
- **Ages 2 months to < 2 years:** Neutropenia, vomiting, constipation, diarrhea, pyrexia, hypophagia, cough, and rash.
- **Ages < 2 months:** Vomiting, rash, gastroesophageal reflux, increased hepatic enzymes, feeding disorder, anemia, and thrombocytopenia [US].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Neurotoxicity (reversible) at high PAA concentrations.
- Hyperammonemic crises (though the drug is intended for chronic management) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Corticosteroids, Valproic Acid, and Haloperidol:** May induce hyperammonemia or protein breakdown; monitor ammonia levels closely [US].
- **Probenecid:** May inhibit renal excretion of metabolites (PAA and PAGN) [US].
- **CYP3A4 Substrates:** Glycerol phenylbutyrate is a weak inducer. Concomitant use may decrease exposure to CYP3A4 substrates with a narrow therapeutic index (e.g., alfentanil, quinidine, cyclosporine) and **midazolam** [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: No specific category letter provided, but data is insufficient to inform drug-associated risk]. Animal studies showed maternal toxicity and skeletal variations at high doses (≥ 5.7 times the human dose) [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Breastfeeding is not recommended due to the potential for serious adverse reactions (neurotoxicity and tumorigenicity) in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No effect on fertility in rats at standard doses; however, nonviable embryos increased at doses ~7 times the human dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Established for use in patients from birth through 17 years of age [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies included insufficient numbers of subjects ≥ 65 years. Start at the lower end of the dosing range [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety and efficacy are unknown; monitor ammonia levels closely [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** For moderate to severe impairment, start at the lower end of the dosing range (4.5 mL/m²/day) and maintain the lowest dose necessary for ammonia control [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A pro-drug containing 3 molecules of PBA. Pancreatic lipases release PBA, which undergoes $\beta$-oxidation to PAA. PAA conjugates with glutamine to form PAGN, which is excreted in the urine, providing an alternative nitrogen disposal pathway [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** PBA is released in the GI tract by lipases.
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding for PBA is 81-98%; PAA is 37-66%; PAGN is 7-12%.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 69% of PBA is excreted as urinary PAGN in adults [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study 1 (Adults):** A randomized, double-blind, crossover non-inferiority study (n=45) compared glycerol phenylbutyrate to sodium phenylbutyrate. Glycerol phenylbutyrate was non-inferior based on 24-hour ammonia AUC (866 vs 977 micromol·h/L) [US].
- **Pediatric Studies:** Open-label studies confirmed efficacy and safety in children from birth to 17 years, showing comparable ammonia control to sodium phenylbutyrate [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15° to 30°C (59° to 86°F) [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Discard bottle 28 days after opening [US].
- **Handling:** If moisture enters the bottle, the liquid will become cloudy; cloudy contents should be discarded [US].
