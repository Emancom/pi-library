# Editorial Product Information: Glyceryl trinitrate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Glyceryl trinitrate (GTN) for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource only. It is not a substitute for the official, legally approved Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Glyceryl trinitrate (GTN).
- **Chemical Class:** Organic nitrate; Vasodilator.
- **Brand Name(s):** DBL Glyceryl Trinitrate Concentrate Injection [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** C01DA02.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 50 mg/10 mL (5 mg/mL) sterile concentrate for injection [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous infusion (following dilution).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Glyceryl trinitrate concentrate injection is indicated for the following clinical objectives [Australian]:
- **Perioperative Hypertension Control:** Management of blood pressure during surgical procedures, particularly cardiovascular surgery (e.g., during intubation, anaesthesia, sternotomy, or cardiac bypass).
- **Congestive Heart Failure:** Specifically when associated with acute myocardial infarction.
- **Refractory Angina Pectoris:** Treatment of patients who have not responded to standard doses of organic nitrates or beta-blockers.
- **Controlled Hypotension:** Induction of hypotensive states during orthopaedic or neurosurgical procedures.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
There is no fixed optimum dose; therapy must be individualised through continuous monitoring of physiological parameters (BP, heart rate, and pulmonary capillary wedge pressure).

- **Initial Dose:** 5 micrograms/min delivered via an infusion pump capable of exact delivery [Australian].
- **Titration:**
    - Increase in increments of 5 micrograms/min every 3 to 5 minutes until a clinical response is observed.
    - If no response occurs at 20 micrograms/min, increments of 10 to 20 micrograms/min may be utilised.
    - Once a partial response is achieved, the titration interval should be lengthened and increments reduced.
- **Dose Reduction Note:** Doses must be reduced if using non-absorbing (non-PVC) tubing, as higher doses are typically reported in studies using PVC sets due to drug adsorption [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **NOT FOR DIRECT INTRAVENOUS INJECTION.** The concentrate must be diluted before use.
- **Compatible Diluents:** 0.9% sodium chloride or 5% glucose [Australian].
- **Standard Dilution:** 50 mg (10 mL) added to 490 mL of diluent provides 100 micrograms/mL.
- **Maximum Concentration:** 400 micrograms/mL.
- **Compatibility:** Do not admix with other medications.
- **Equipment:** Use non-PVC (polyethylene-lined) tubing to prevent drug loss through adsorption. Avoid filters.

## 4. Contraindications
Glyceryl trinitrate is contraindicated in the following conditions [Australian]:
- Known hypersensitivity to glyceryl trinitrate, other organic nitrates, or any excipients (including ethanol and propylene glycol).
- Hypotension or uncorrected hypovolaemia (risk of severe shock).
- Increased intracranial pressure (e.g., cerebral haemorrhage or head trauma).
- Constrictive pericarditis and pericardial tamponade.
- Severe anaemia or arterial hypoxaemia.
- Hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy (HOCM) or angina caused by HOCM.
- Acute circulatory failure (shock or collapse).
- Pronounced hypotension (systolic BP < 90 mmHg).
- Cardiogenic shock (unless adequate filling pressure is maintained by inotropic agents or intra-aortic counterpulsation).
- **Concomitant Therapies:**
    - Phosphodiesterase inhibitors (e.g., sildenafil) used for erectile dysfunction or pulmonary hypertension (due to risk of fatal hypotension).
    - Soluble guanylate cyclase (sGC) stimulators (e.g., riociguat).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Adsorption to Plastics:** 40% to 80% of GTN may be absorbed by standard PVC infusion sets. Use only glass containers and non-PVC administration sets. 
- **Severe Hypotension:** Even low doses may cause profound hypotension. Use with extreme caution in patients with severe coronary or cerebral arteriosclerosis.
- **Myocardial Infarction:** In acute MI, use only for definite left ventricular failure. Monitor hemodynamics to avoid reflex tachycardia and sudden BP drops, which can extend ischaemic injury.

### Specific Precautions
- **Tolerance:** Tolerance or cross-tolerance to other nitrates may develop with prolonged use.
- **Nitrate Dependence:** Withdrawal syndromes (increased angina) can occur; taper doses carefully in patients with coronary arteriosclerosis who have received high-dose infusions.
- **Hypoxaemia:** GTN can worsen ventilation-perfusion mismatch, leading to decreased arterial oxygen tension, particularly in patients with pulmonary disease or cor pulmonale.
- **Methaemoglobinaemia:** Reported with GTN therapy; risk is higher in those with methaemoglobin reductase deficiencies.
- **Excipients:** Contains propylene glycol (risk of hyperosmolality, haemolysis, and lactic acidosis) and absolute ethanol [Australian].
- **Closed-Angle Glaucoma:** Use with caution in predisposed patients.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are typically dose-related and stem from the drug's vasodilatory action.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Not specified in available source |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Headache (approx. 2%; dose-dependent) |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Tachycardia, nausea, vomiting, apprehension, restlessness, muscle twitching, retrosternal discomfort, palpitations, dizziness, abdominal pain |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Cyanosis, exfoliative dermatitis |

- **Serious Effects:** Profound hypotension, paradoxical bradycardia, increased angina, methaemoglobinaemia, and syncope.
- **Solvent-related Effects:** Alcohol intoxication (with high-dose infusions) and hyperosmolarity due to propylene glycol [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Potentiation of Hypotension:** Alcohol, levodopa, tricyclic antidepressants, antihypertensives (beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors, diuretics, calcium antagonists), and major tranquillisers.
- **Contraindicated Interactions:** PDE inhibitors (sildenafil) and sGC stimulators (riociguat) cause life-threatening hypotension.
- **Sympathomimetics:** May reduce the antianginal effect of nitrates; nitrates may counteract the pressor effect of sympathomimetics.
- **Ergot Alkaloids:** May precipitate angina; avoid concomitant use if possible.

### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Heparin:** The propylene glycol solvent may decrease the anticoagulant effect of heparin; monitor coagulation parameters closely.
- **Pancuronium:** GTN may increase the duration of neuromuscular blockade.
- **Morphine:** GTN may slow morphine metabolism, potentially leading to over-sedation.
- **NSAIDs/Aspirin:** May diminish the therapeutic response to GTN.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B2** [Australian]. Limited human data available; animal studies do not show fetal damage but show testicular degeneration at very high doses in rats. Use only if the potential benefit outweighs the risk.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if GTN or its metabolites are excreted in human milk. Exercise caution.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies (rats) showed severe testicular atrophy and aspermatogenesis at high doses (230 mg/kg/day) [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended; safety and efficacy have not been established in children [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased sensitivity to side effects (e.g., hypotension). Dose reductions may be necessary.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution and perform additional monitoring in patients with severe hepatic insufficiency or severe renal failure [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** GTN is an organic nitrate that relaxes vascular smooth muscle. It causes dose-related dilation of both arteries and veins, though venous dilation (venous pooling) predominates, reducing preload. Arteriolar relaxation reduces systemic vascular resistance (afterload). It also provides transient coronary artery dilation.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Reduces myocardial oxygen demand by lowering the pressure-rate product and tension-time index. Systemic BP and pulmonary capillary wedge pressure decrease, while heart rate may slightly increase as a reflex.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Widely distributed; volume of distribution approx. 200 L in adults. 60% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly hydrolysed in the liver and plasma by glutathione-organic nitrate reductase to dinitrates and mononitrates.
    - **Elimination:** Very short half-life (1 to 4 minutes). Metabolised ultimately to glycerol and carbon dioxide.

## 11. Clinical Trials
Pivotal trial data were not detailed in the provided source document. However, clinical studies established that starting doses using PVC sets were typically 25 micrograms/min, whereas non-absorbing tubing requires lower initial dosing [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Protection:** Protect from light [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Expiry date is located on the packaging. Following dilution, infusion should commence as soon as possible and must be completed within 24 hours.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
- **Legal Status:** Registered as a prescription medicine in Australia (Date of first approval: 28 September 2000).
