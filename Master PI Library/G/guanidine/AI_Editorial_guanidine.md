# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This unified editorial document is based on the Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction:
    - **United States** (Food and Drug Administration)
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a concise, structured format.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidelines required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN).
- **Chemical Class:** A naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism; chemically (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Names:** Not specified for this generic formulation; however, it is manufactured as Levocarnitine Injection, USP by Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available source.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Intravenous Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    - **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
    - **Dose Adjustments:** Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].

### Monitoring
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- **ESRD:** Initiation of therapy may be prompted by pre-dialysis plasma concentrations below 40–50 µmol/L [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have occurred, occasionally within minutes of intravenous administration. These are primarily reported in patients with ESRD on dialysis. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks/benefits before re-administration [US].
- **Seizures:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** While not the primary route for the provided source, it is noted that chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported at a frequency of ≥5% (regardless of causality) in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials include:

| Body System | Adverse Event (Incidence ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, back pain, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), palpitations (5%) |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), anorexia (5%), abdominal pain (9%) |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%), hypervolemia (5%) |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), depression (5%), paresthesia (5%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), rhinitis (9%), dyspnea (7%) |

### Other Adverse Effects
- **Common:** Transient nausea and vomiting [US].
- **Less Frequent:** Body odor and gastritis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating treatment or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not specified by letter category in source, but animal reproductive studies (rats/rabbits) at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm [US]. 
    - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. While supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied, dairy cow studies show increased milk concentrations after exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess intake should be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing guidelines for metabolic disorders are applicable to paediatric patients; specifically, the 50 mg/kg dose [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety and efficacy of high-dose oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency; accumulation of metabolites (TMA/TMAO) may occur [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Carnitine deficiency is biochemically defined as plasma levels <20 µmol/L at one week post-term [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Excretion:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Dialysis Efficacy:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean trough carnitine concentrations from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L by week 8 [US].
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg showed dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and avoid excessive heat [US].
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only [US].
