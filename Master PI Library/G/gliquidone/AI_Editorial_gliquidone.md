# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical and regulatory data into a single, concise reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis for informational purposes and does not substitute for the official PI or local clinical guidelines required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion. 
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    - Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - Administer as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - Initiation may be prompted by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levels below 40–50 µmol/L [US].

### Titration and Monitoring
- **Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine level: 35 to 60 µmol/L).
- **ESRD Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by trough plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known seizure disorder, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures has been observed following levocarnitine administration [US].
- **Monitoring:** Monitor International Normalized Ratio (INR) in patients co-administered with warfarin [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects
In clinical trials of ESRD patients on hemodialysis, the following adverse events were reported at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) [US]:

| Body System | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, abdominal pain, vomiting, nausea |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, paresthesia |
| **Respiratory** | Cough increase, dyspnea, pharyngitis, rhinitis |

### Other Adverse Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent reports of gastritis.
- **Neurologic:** Seizures.
- **Other:** Body odor (reported with chronic use) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased INR have been observed when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. There are no adequate/well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant should be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders are weight-based (50 mg/kg) and apply to paediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may result in the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]), which are normally excreted in the urine [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also facilitates the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, levocarnitine injection increases plasma carnitine concentrations and may alleviate metabolic abnormalities associated with inborn errors [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. 
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Excretion:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis trough levels from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** A study of ~90 ESRD patients compared 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses. Trough concentrations increased dose-dependently over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
