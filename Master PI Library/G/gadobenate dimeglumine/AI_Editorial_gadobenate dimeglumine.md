# Editorial Product Information: Gadobenate Dimeglumine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction:
    - **United States (FDA)**: MultiHance (gadobenate dimeglumine) Injection.
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the United States PI was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Gadobenate dimeglumine (a paramagnetic gadolinium-based contrast agent [GBCA]).
- **Brand Name(s)**: MultiHance [US].
- **ATC Code**: V08CA08.
- **Dosage Form and Strength**: Sterile, nonpyrogenic, aqueous solution for intravenous injection. Each mL contains 529 mg of gadobenate dimeglumine [US].
- **Route of Administration**: Intravenous injection (rapid bolus).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Bracco Diagnostics Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Central Nervous System (CNS) Imaging**: Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the CNS in adult and paediatric patients (including term neonates) to visualise lesions with abnormal blood-brain barrier or abnormal vascularity of the brain, spine, and associated tissues [US].
- **Magnetic Resonance Angiography (MRA)**: Evaluation of adults with known or suspected renal or aorto-ilio-femoral occlusive vascular disease [US-approved; paediatric MRA not specified].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing**: 0.2 mL/kg (0.1 mmol/kg) administered as a rapid intravenous bolus [US].
- **Paediatric Dosing (CNS MRI)**: 
    - Patients ≥2 years: 0.2 mL/kg (0.1 mmol/kg) [US].
    - Patients <2 years (including term neonates): 0.1 to 0.2 mL/kg [US].
- **Administration Instructions**:
    - Follow injection with a saline flush (at least 5 mL for CNS MRI; at least 20 mL for MRA) [US].
    - Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use; do not use if discolored [US].
    - **Pharmacy Bulk Package**: Used for filling multiple syringes via a transfer device. Must be used in a laminar flow hood. Once punctured, the contents must be used or discarded within 8 hours. The container temperature must not exceed 25°C [US].
- **Imaging Timing**:
    - **CNS**: Imaging may begin immediately after bolus injection [US].
    - **MRA**: Start imaging immediately; use of a test bolus (1-2 mL) or automatic bolus detection is recommended to determine the optimal scan delay [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity**: History of known allergic or hypersensitivity reactions to gadolinium-based contrast agents (GBCAs) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Intrathecal Use**: GBCAs administered intrathecally can cause serious reactions, including death, seizures, encephalopathy, and coma. Gadobenate dimeglumine is NOT approved for intrathecal administration [US].
- **Boxed Warning: Nephrogenic Systemic Fibrosis (NSF)**: GBCAs increase the risk of NSF in patients with impaired elimination. Risk is highest in patients with:
    - Chronic, severe kidney disease (GFR <30 mL/min/1.73m²)
    - Acute kidney injury (AKI)
    - Screen patients for renal dysfunction prior to administration [US].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions**: Anaphylactic/anaphylactoid reactions, including fatalities, may occur. Monitor patients for at least 2 hours post-injection. Ensure immediate availability of resuscitation equipment [US].
- **Gadolinium Retention**: Gadolinium may be retained for months or years in various organs (bone, brain, skin, kidney, liver, spleen). Linear GBCAs (like gadobenate) generally result in higher retention than macrocyclic agents. Clinical consequences are unknown, but higher risk populations include those requiring multiple lifetime doses, pregnant patients, and children [US].
- **Acute Renal Failure**: Risk increases with higher doses. Monitor renal function in patients with pre-existing insufficiency [US].
- **Cardiac Arrhythmias**: QTc prolongations (average <5 msec) have been observed. Use caution in patients predisposed to arrhythmias [US].
- **Extravasation**: May lead to local tissue reactions, including pain, swelling, and rarely, necrosis/eschar [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions in clinical trials were generally mild to moderate.
- **Common (1–10%)**: Nausea (1.3%), Headache (1.2%), Injection site reaction (1.1%), Feeling hot (1.0%) [US].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%)**: Dysgeusia, Paresthesia, Dizziness [US].
- **Paediatric Specific**: Vomiting (1.0%), Pyrexia (0.7%), Hyperhidrosis (0.7%) [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects**: Convulsions, pulmonary edema, acute necrotizing pancreatitis, and anaphylactoid reactions (reported in ~0.1% of patients) [US].
- **Postmarketing Experience**: Acute pancreatitis (within 48 hours), acute respiratory distress syndrome, and gadolinium-associated plaques [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Transporter-Based Interactions**: Gadobenate dimeglumine may compete for the canalicular multispecific organic anion transporter (MOAT/MRP2). Caution and monitoring for prolonged exposure are advised when co-administered with MOAT substrates, such as:
    - Cisplatin, anthracyclines (doxorubicin, daunorubicin), vinca alkaloids, methotrexate, etoposide, tamoxifen, and paclitaxel [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **US Category**: Not specified (Current labeling advises use only if imaging is essential and cannot be delayed).
    - **Clinical Data**: GBCAs cross the placenta. Animal data (rabbits) showed teratogenicity (microphthalmia, retinal folds) and increased intrauterine deaths at doses 6-10 times the human dose. No such effects were seen in rats at 3 times the human dose [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: Gadobenate dimeglumine is excreted in human milk (0.001% to 0.04% of maternal dose). Due to limited gastrointestinal absorption of GBCAs by the infant, the risk is considered low, but the clinical need of the mother should be weighed against potential risks to the infant [US].
- **Fertility**: No effect on fertility was observed in rats at 3 times the human dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Approved for CNS MRI from birth (including term neonates). Safety in preterm neonates is not established. No age-based dose adjustment is required for patients ≥2 years [US].
- **Geriatric Use**: No overall differences in efficacy or safety compared to younger adults; however, increased vigilance regarding renal function is advised due to the higher prevalence of renal impairment in this population [US].
- **Renal Impairment**: Elimination half-life is significantly prolonged (from ~1–2 hours in healthy volunteers to ~9.5 hours in severe renal impairment). Gadobenate is dialyzable; ~72% of a dose is removed over a 4-hour hemodialysis session [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: No significant effect on pharmacokinetics; no dosage adjustment required [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Gadobenate dimeglumine is a paramagnetic contrast agent that shortens T1 and T2 relaxation times in target tissues, enhancing signal intensity (brightness).
- **Pharmacodynamics**: Demonstrates weak and transient interactions with serum proteins, resulting in higher relaxivity (r1 = 9.7; r2 = 12.5 mM⁻¹s⁻¹ in human plasma) compared to other gadolinium chelates [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Distribution**: Rapid; volume of distribution is equivalent to extracellular water. No appreciable protein binding [US].
    - **Metabolism**: No detectable biotransformation [US].
    - **Elimination**: Primarily renal (78%–96% recovered in urine); small biliary component (0.6%–4% in feces) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **CNS MRI**: Two trials in adults (Study A and B) and supporting paediatric data showed that 0.1 mmol/kg gadobenate provided statistically significant improvement in lesion border delineation, internal morphology, and contrast enhancement compared to non-contrast MRI [US].
- **MRA**: Two multicenter trials demonstrated superior sensitivity and non-inferior specificity compared to non-contrast MRA for detecting clinically significant steno-occlusive disease (≥51% stenosis) in renal and aorto-ilio-femoral arteries [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions permitted to 15–30°C [US].
- **Freezing**: Do not freeze [US].
- **Handling**: Use within 8 hours once the Pharmacy Bulk Package is opened [US].

- **Legal Classification**:
