# Editorial Product Information: Granisetron

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single, structured reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local clinical guidelines mandated for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority for the most current legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Granisetron (as hydrochloride).
- **Chemical Class:** Selective 5-hydroxytryptamine3 (5-HT3) receptor antagonist.
- **Brand Names:** Granisetron-AFT [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A04AA0.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Concentrated solution for injection: 1 mg/1 mL and 3 mg/3 mL ampoules [Australian]. 
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion (following dilution).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AFT Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chemotherapy-Induced Nausea and Vomiting (CINV):**
    - Prevention and treatment of CINV in adults [Australian].
    - Prevention of CINV in paediatric patients [Australian].
- **Radiotherapy-Induced Nausea and Vomiting (RINV):**
    - Prevention of RINV in adults [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
*Granisetron concentrated solution must be diluted before administration.*

### Chemotherapy-Induced Nausea and Vomiting (CINV)
- **Adult Prevention:** A single 3 mg dose administered as an IV infusion (diluted in 20–50 mL) over 5 minutes. Administration should be completed within 30 minutes prior to starting chemotherapy [Australian].
- **Adult Treatment:** A single 1 mg dose administered as a 5-minute infusion. If necessary, additional doses may be given at least 10 minutes apart. The maximum daily dose is 9 mg/24 hours [Australian].
- **Paediatric Prevention (Aged 2 years and older):** 20–40 µg/kg body weight (up to a maximum of 3 mg). Administer as an IV infusion (diluted in 10–30 mL) over 5 minutes, completed within 30 minutes before chemotherapy begins [Australian].

### Radiotherapy-Induced Nausea and Vomiting (RINV)
- **Adult Prevention:** A single 3 mg dose administered as an IV infusion (diluted in 20–50 mL) over 5 minutes, prior to the start of radiotherapy [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Compatible Diluents:** 0.9% sodium chloride, 0.18% sodium chloride and 4% glucose, 5% glucose, Hartmann’s solution, 1.85% sodium lactate, or Mannitol [Australian].
- **Combination Therapy:** Efficacy is enhanced when combined with IV corticosteroids (e.g., 8–20 mg dexamethasone or 250 mg methylprednisolone) [Australian].
- **Inspection:** Parenteral products should be visually inspected for particulate matter and discolouration prior to use.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to granisetron or any of the product excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Gastrointestinal Motility:** Granisetron may reduce lower bowel motility. Monitor patients with signs of sub-acute intestinal obstruction [Australian].
- **Cardiac Effects:** As with other 5-HT3 antagonists, cases of QT prolongation and ECG modifications have been reported. While usually not clinically significant, caution is advised in patients with pre-existing arrhythmias, cardiac conduction disorders, or those receiving cardiotoxic chemotherapy/electrolytes abnormalities [Australian].
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Potentially life-threatening serotonin syndrome has been reported with 5-HT3 antagonists, especially when used concomitantly with other serotonergic drugs (e.g., SSRIs, SNRIs). Symptoms include mental status changes, autonomic instability, and neuromuscular abnormalities [Australian].
- **Cross-sensitivity:** Sensitivity between different 5-HT3 antagonists has been reported [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Clinical observation is advised if concomitant use with serotonergic medicines is warranted [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Granisetron is generally well-tolerated. The most common adverse effects observed in clinical trials were headache and constipation.

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Headache (14%), Constipation |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Asthenia (5%), Somnolence (4%), Diarrhoea (4%), Fever, Hypertension, Transient increases in AST/ALT, Taste disorder, Agitation, Anxiety, Dizziness, Insomnia |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | QT prolongation, Serotonin syndrome, Hypersensitivity reactions (anaphylaxis, urticaria), Skin rashes |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Hypotension, Arrhythmias (sinus bradycardia, atrial fibrillation, AV block), Extrapyramidal syndrome (only with concomitant medication) |

*Note: In the context of chemotherapy, events such as leukopaenia, anaemia, and thrombocytopaenia are frequently reported but typically related to the underlying treatment [Australian].*

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Hepatic Enzyme Inducers:** Phenobarbital induction of hepatic enzymes resulted in a 25% increase in the total plasma clearance of IV granisetron in humans [Australian].
- **Cytochrome P450:** Granisetron does not appear to induce or inhibit the P450 system in humans; it does not modify the activity of the CYP3A4 subfamily [Australian].
- **Serotonergic Drugs:** Increased risk of Serotonin Syndrome when co-administered with other serotonergic agents [Australian].
- **General Compatibility:** Safe administration has been noted with benzodiazepines, neuroleptics, and anti-ulcer medications. No interactions with emetogenic cancer therapies were observed [Australian].
- **Physical Compatibility:** Do not mix in solution with other medicines except dexamethasone sodium phosphate [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B1** [Australian]. There is no experience in human pregnancy. Animal studies (rats/rabbits) showed no teratogenic effects at high doses, but since animal studies are not always predictive, use should be limited to cases where clearly needed [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on the excretion of granisetron in human milk. Use should be restricted to situations where the maternal benefit outweighs the potential risk to the infant [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Data not specified in available sources [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for the prevention of CINV in children aged 2 years and older. There is insufficient information to recommend its use for the *treatment* of CINV or for RINV in children [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No special precautions or dosage adjustments are required [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required for patients with renal failure, as pharmacokinetic parameters are similar to normal subjects [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Clearance may be halved in patients with hepatic impairment due to neoplastic liver involvement, but no dosage adjustment is recommended [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A potent, highly selective 5-HT3 receptor antagonist. It blocks serotonin receptors located peripherally on vagal nerve terminals and centrally in the chemoreceptor trigger zone (CTZ) of the area postrema [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Granisetron does not affect plasma levels of prolactin or aldosterone and has no consistent effect on pulse, blood pressure, or EEG in healthy subjects [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Extensively distributed (Vd ~3 L/kg); plasma protein binding is ~65% [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic via N-demethylation and aromatic ring oxidation. Metabolites generally lack significant pharmacological activity [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Mean plasma half-life is approximately 9 hours. Approximately 12% is excreted unchanged in urine within 48 hours; the remainder is excreted as metabolites in urine (47%) and faeces (34%) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Cisplatin-based Chemotherapy:** A single 40 µg/kg IV dose was significantly more effective than placebo. Dose-response studies showed 10 and 40 µg/kg doses were superior to 2 µg/kg [Australian].
- **Moderately Emetogenic Chemotherapy:** IV granisetron (40 µg/kg) was superior to a combination of chlorpromazine and dexamethasone [Australian].
- **Radiotherapy:** A 3 mg IV dose was significantly more effective than a standard regimen of metoclopramide, dexamethasone, and lorazepam in preventing RINV [Australian].
- **Paediatrics:** Doses of 10–40 µg/kg were effective in preventing vomiting in children undergoing highly emetogenic chemotherapy [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Concentrated solution:** Store below 30°C and protect from light [Australian].
- **Shelf life after dilution:** To minimize microbiological hazards, the infusion should ideally be commenced immediately. If storage is necessary, store at 2–8°C for no more than 24 hours [Australian].
- **Stability:** Granisetron is chemically stable for 24 hours at room temperature in normal light when diluted in compatible fluids [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Prescription Only Medicine (**Schedule 4**) [Australian].
