# Editorial Product Information: Gadopiclenol

**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been compiled by synthesizing official regulatory documentation from the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). While both agencies approve gadopiclenol for similar diagnostic purposes, differences in approved age groups and specific handling instructions exist. Where information differs, the most comprehensive or conservative guidance is provided with the contributing agency noted in brackets.

---

# 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Names:** ELUCIREM [TGA], VUEWAY [FDA]  
**Active Ingredient:** Gadopiclenol

Each milliliter of the solution contains 485.1 mg of gadopiclenol, which is a macrocyclic non-ionic gadolinium-based contrast agent (GBCA). This concentration is equivalent to 0.5 mmol/mL of gadopiclenol and 78.6 mg/mL of gadolinium.

# 2. Approved Indications
Gadopiclenol is a paramagnetic contrast agent intended for use with magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). It is indicated for the detection and visualization of lesions associated with abnormal vascularity or a disrupted blood-brain barrier in the following areas:

*   **Central Nervous System (CNS):** Including the brain, spine, and associated tissues.
*   **Body:** Including the head and neck, thorax (including breast), abdomen (including liver/pancreas), pelvis, and the musculoskeletal system.

**Approved Populations:**
*   **FDA:** Indicated for adults and pediatric patients, including term neonates.
*   **TGA:** Indicated for adults and children aged 2 years and older.

# 3. Contraindications
Gadopiclenol is contraindicated in patients with a known history of severe hypersensitivity reactions to the active ingredient (gadopiclenol) or any of the excipients (including tetraxetan, trometamol, hydrochloric acid, and sodium hydroxide).

# 4. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed Warning: Nephrogenic Systemic Fibrosis (NSF)
Gadolinium-based contrast agents (GBCAs) increase the risk of Nephrogenic Systemic Fibrosis (NSF) in patients with impaired elimination of the drug. The risk is highest in patients with:
*   Chronic, severe kidney disease (GFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m²).
*   Acute kidney injury (AKI).

NSF is a potentially fatal or debilitating condition characterized by fibrosis of the skin, muscle, and internal organs. Healthcare providers must screen all patients for renal dysfunction prior to administration. Use should be avoided in high-risk patients unless the diagnostic information is essential and unavailable via non-contrast MRI.

### Risk Associated with Intrathecal Use [FDA]
Gadopiclenol is not approved for intrathecal administration. Clinical data and reports of other GBCAs indicate that intrathecal administration can result in serious neurological adverse reactions, including seizures, encephalopathy, coma, and death.

### Hypersensitivity Reactions
Anaphylactic and anaphylactoid reactions, including fatalities, may occur. These reactions can be independent of dose and may occur even after the first administration. Patients with a history of asthma, drug allergies, or prior reactions to contrast media are at higher risk. Emergency resuscitation equipment and trained personnel must be immediately available during and for at least 30 minutes following the injection.

### Gadolinium Retention
Gadolinium can be retained in the body for months or years after administration, primarily in the bone, followed by the brain, skin, kidney, liver, and spleen. While the clinical significance of brain retention is unknown, linear GBCAs generally result in higher retention than macrocyclic agents like gadopiclenol. To minimize potential risks, the lowest effective dose should be used, particularly in patients requiring multiple lifetime doses and vulnerable populations (pediatrics, pregnant patients).

### Seizures [TGA]
Caution is advised when administering GBCAs to patients with a lowered seizure threshold. Facilities should be prepared to manage convulsions during the procedure.

### Extravasation and Injection Site Reactions
Injection site reactions, including pain and localized swelling, may occur. If extravasation happens, the injection must be stopped immediately. Ensure venous patency prior to administration to avoid tissue irritation.

# 5. Dosage and Administration

### Recommended Dosage
The recommended dose for both adults and pediatric patients (including term neonates [FDA]) is **0.1 mL/kg body weight**, which is equivalent to **0.05 mmol/kg**. 

### Administration Instructions
*   **Method:** Intravenous bolus injection at a rate of approximately 2 mL/second.
*   **Flush:** The injection should be followed by a flush of 0.9% sodium chloride to ensure complete delivery of the contrast.
*   **Observation:** Patients should be kept under medical supervision for at least 30 minutes post-injection to monitor for immediate hypersensitivity reactions.
*   **Dose Frequency:** Only one dose should be used per scan. Repeated administration should not occur within a 7-day interval [TGA].

### Preparation and Handling
*   Gadopiclenol should be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration. The solution should be clear and colorless to pale yellow.
*   **Vials/Syringes:** Intended for single use; discard any unused portion.
*   **Bulk Packages [FDA]:** Specific "Pharmacy Bulk Packages" and "Imaging Bulk Packages" are available for multi-patient use in conjunction with automated contrast injection systems, provided the manufacturer's strict aseptic transfer protocols are followed. These containers must be used within 24 hours of the initial puncture.

# 6. Adverse Effects
The safety profile of gadopiclenol is based on clinical trials involving over 1,000 subjects. Most adverse events are mild to moderate.

*   **Common (≥ 1/100 to < 1/10):** Headache, nausea, dizziness, injection site pain, and injection site warmth or coldness.
*   **Uncommon (≥ 1/1,000 to < 1/100):** Dysgeusia (distortion of taste), diarrhea, vomiting, abdominal pain, fatigue, and feeling hot.
*   **Laboratory Changes:** Transient increases in serum creatinine and Cystatin C have been observed.
*   **GBCA-Class Effects:** Rare cases of acute pancreatitis (occurring within 48 hours of administration) have been reported with the use of GBCAs.

# 7. Drug Interactions
No formal clinical drug-drug interaction studies have been conducted. 

*   **Laboratory Tests:** No interference with clinical laboratory tests has been observed.
*   **Cardiovascular Compensation [TGA]:** Concomitant use of beta-blockers, vasoactive substances, or ACE inhibitors may reduce the body’s ability to compensate for blood pressure changes during a hypersensitivity reaction.

# 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

### Pregnancy
There is no adequate data regarding the use of gadopiclenol in pregnant women. Animal studies show that GBCAs cross the placenta and may result in fetal gadolinium retention. Gadopiclenol should only be used during pregnancy if the clinical condition requires its use and the diagnostic benefit outweighs the potential risks to the fetus.

### Breastfeeding
Gadolinium-based contrast agents are excreted into human milk in very small amounts (approximately 0.01% to 0.04% of the maternal dose). Gastrointestinal absorption by the infant is expected to be minimal. 
*   **Recommendation:** A decision to continue or temporarily suspend breastfeeding for 24 hours after the dose should be made at the discretion of the physician and the mother.

# 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** Gadopiclenol distributes rapidly into the extracellular fluid. It does not cross an intact blood-brain barrier. Plasma protein binding is negligible (≤ 1.8%).
*   **Metabolism:** The drug is not metabolized.
*   **Excretion:** Elimination occurs primarily through the kidneys via glomerular filtration. Approximately 98% of the dose is recovered in the urine within 48 hours in patients with normal renal function.
*   **Elimination Half-life:** Approximately 1.5 hours.
*   **Renal Impairment:** Half-life increases significantly with the severity of renal impairment, reaching approximately 11.7 hours in patients with severe renal impairment (eGFR 15 to < 30 mL/min/1.73 m²).

# 10. Mechanism of Action
Gadopiclenol is a paramagnetic molecule that develops a magnetic moment when placed in a magnetic field. It enhances the relaxation rates of water protons in its vicinity, which shortens the T1 and T2 relaxation times. This results in increased signal intensity (brightness) of the targeted tissues. 

Gadopiclenol is characterized by high relaxivity (approximately two to three times that of other macrocyclic GBCAs) because its chemical structure allows two water molecules to link to the gadolinium ion simultaneously, rather than one. This allows for a lower dose of gadolinium (0.05 mmol/kg) to achieve diagnostic enhancement comparable to other agents used at 0.1 mmol/kg.

# 11. Storage Conditions
*   Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions are permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F).
*   Protect from light (keep in original carton until use).
*   **Do not freeze** pre-filled syringes.
*   Shelf life is 36 months when stored correctly [TGA].
