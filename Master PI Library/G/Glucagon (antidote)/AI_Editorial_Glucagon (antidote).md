# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**. 
- At the time of this editorial’s preparation, documentation from other major jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian, Japanese) was not provided for comparison. 
- **Purpose:** This document serves as a harmonized editorial overview for healthcare professionals to facilitate clinical decision-making. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial resource and does not replace the official, legally binding Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP), a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acids.
- **Chemical Class:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US: Baxter].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from metabolic errors [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients undergoing chronic hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 50 mg/kg as a slow intravenous bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion. 
    - *Acute Management:* A loading dose may be utilized in severe metabolic crises, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and not less than every 6 hours).
    - *Maintenance:* Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilized.
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - Dose adjustments (downward to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard unused portions of single-dose vials as the formulation lacks preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** While the source documents list "None known" in the formal contraindications section, clinicians are cautioned against use in patients with previous severe hypersensitivity to levocarnitine [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These often occur within minutes of intravenous administration, particularly in ESRD patients [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Reports indicate that seizures may occur in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with known epilepsy, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Clinical Monitoring:** 
    - **General:** Periodic monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations is recommended.
    - **Target Levels:** Plasma free carnitine concentrations should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Oral vs. IV in Renal Impairment:** The safety of **oral** levocarnitine in patients with severe renal impairment or ESRD has not been established; chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic errors.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site reaction (33–38%), Pharyngitis (15–27%), Chest pain (6–15%), Hypertension (18–21%), Diarrhea (9–35%), Vomiting (9–21%), Headache (3–37%), Dizziness (10–18%) [US] |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Abdominal pain, Fever, Infection, Nausea, Cardiovascular disorder, Tachycardia, Hypercalcemia, Anemia, Cough, Pruritus, Dyspnea [US] |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:**
    - Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity.
    - Seizures (neurological).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. 
    - **Management:** Monitor INR frequently upon initiation of levocarnitine or following any dose adjustments in patients on vitamin K antagonists [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Classification:** [US: Pregnancy Category not explicitly assigned, but reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8x human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm]. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Consider the risk-benefit ratio of excess carnitine intake in the infant versus the mother’s clinical need [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was observed in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg body weight (see Section 3).
- **Geriatric Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources; however, dose selection should generally be cautious, reflecting the greater frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function in the elderly.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. Monitoring of trough concentrations is essential to guide dosing (target 40–50 µmol/L pre-dialysis) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This provides substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly cleared [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (found in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (found in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg TIW), mean pre-dialysis carnitine levels increased from 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks. Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when diluted to concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s [US].
