# Editorial Product Information: Gemifloxacin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- Information from other major jurisdictions (e.g., Australian (TGA), European (EMA), Health Canada) was not provided for this specific harmonisation; therefore, this document primarily reflects the clinical and regulatory position of the United States.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Gemifloxacin (as mesylate).
- **Chemical Class:** Fluoroquinolone antibacterial agent (naphthyridone derivative).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Factive** [US].
- **ATC Code:** J01MA15.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 320 mg film-coated tablets (white to off-white, oval, with breaklines and debossed "GE 320").
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Merus Labs International Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Bacterial Exacerbation of Chronic Bronchitis (ABECB):** Caused by *S. pneumoniae*, *H. influenzae*, *H. parainfluenzae*, or *M. catarrhalis*. Use should be reserved for patients with no alternative treatment options [US].
- **Community-Acquired Pneumonia (CAP):** Of mild to moderate severity, caused by *S. pneumoniae* (including multi-drug resistant strains [MDRSP]), *H. influenzae*, *M. catarrhalis*, *M. pneumoniae*, *C. pneumoniae*, or *K. pneumoniae* [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing
The standard dose is **320 mg once daily**, taken with or without food.
- **ABECB:** 320 mg daily for 5 days.
- **CAP (Mild to Moderate):**
    - Due to *S. pneumoniae, H. influenzae, M. pneumoniae,* or *C. pneumoniae*: 320 mg daily for 5 days.
    - Due to MDRSP, *K. pneumoniae,* or *M. catarrhalis*: 320 mg daily for 7 days.

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets should be swallowed whole with a liberal amount of liquid.
- Do not take with dairy products or calcium-fortified juices alone, though they may be part of a larger meal.
- Administer at least 3 hours before or 2 hours after products containing magnesium, aluminum, iron, or zinc (including antacids and multivitamins) [US].
- Administer at least 2 hours before sucralfate [US].

### Missed Dose
- If a dose is missed, take it as soon as remembered. Do not exceed one dose per day.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to gemifloxacin, any member of the fluoroquinolone class, or any component of the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
- **Serious Adverse Reactions:** Fluoroquinolones are associated with disabling and potentially irreversible serious adverse reactions, including tendinitis, tendon rupture, peripheral neuropathy, and CNS effects. Discontinue the drug immediately if these occur.
- **Exacerbation of Myasthenia Gravis:** May exacerbate muscle weakness. Avoid use in patients with a known history of myasthenia gravis.
- **Usage Restriction:** For ABECB, gemifloxacin should only be used when no other treatment options are available.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **QT Prolongation:** Avoid in patients with a history of QTc prolongation, uncorrected hypokalemia/hypomagnesemia, or those taking Class IA or III antiarrhythmic agents. Use with caution alongside other QTc-prolonging drugs (e.g., antipsychotics, TCAs).
- **Hypersensitivity:** Serious and occasionally fatal anaphylactic reactions may occur, even after the first dose.
- **Clostridium difficile-Associated Diarrhea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all systemic antibacterials; severity can range from mild diarrhea to fatal colitis.
- **Photosensitivity:** Exaggerated sunburn reactions can occur. Patients should avoid excessive UV exposure and use sun protection.
- **Tendon Disorders:** Increased risk in patients over 60, those taking corticosteroids, or transplant recipients.
- **Peripheral Neuropathy:** Sensory or sensorimotor axonal polyneuropathy may occur rapidly and become permanent.
- **CNS Effects:** Risk of seizures, increased intracranial pressure, and toxic psychosis. Use with caution in patients with known CNS disorders.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The majority of adverse reactions in clinical trials were mild to moderate. Gemifloxacin was discontinued in 2.0% of patients due to adverse events [US].

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Diarrhea (5.0%), nausea (3.7%), headache (4.2%), rash (3.5%), abdominal pain (2.2%), vomiting (1.6%), dizziness (1.7%). |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Anorexia, constipation, dermatitis, dry mouth, dyspepsia, fatigue, flatulence, fungal infection, insomnia, leukopenia, pruritus, somnolence, taste perversion, urticaria, vaginitis. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Abnormal vision, anemia, arthralgia, bilirubinemia, dyspnea, eczema, facial edema, flushing, GGT increase, myalgia, thrombocytopenia, tremor, vertigo. |

- **Rash:** Incidence is notably higher in females under 40, especially those on hormone replacement therapy, and with treatment durations exceeding 5 days [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Chelating Agents:** Antacids (Al/Mg), ferrous sulfate, zinc, multivitamins, and didanosine significantly reduce gemifloxacin absorption. Space administration as noted in Section 3.
- **Probenecid:** Increases gemifloxacin systemic exposure (AUC) by approximately 45%.
- **Warfarin:** While no significant effect was seen in controlled studies, post-marketing reports indicate potential increases in INR and bleeding risk. Monitor PT/INR closely.
- **NSAIDs:** Concomitant use may increase the risk of CNS stimulation and seizures.
- **Sucralfate:** Significantly reduces bioavailability; take gemifloxacin at least 2 hours before sucralfate.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category C]. There are no adequate/controlled studies in pregnant women. Animal data indicate potential fetal growth retardation and malformations at maternally toxic doses. Use only if potential benefit outweighs risk.
- **Breastfeeding:** Gemifloxacin is excreted in the milk of rats; human data are unavailable. Use is not recommended unless the benefit to the mother outweighs the potential risk to the infant.
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility observed in animal studies at clinically relevant exposure levels.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established in patients under 18 years. Fluoroquinolones cause arthropathy in immature animals.
- **Elderly Use:** Generally no dose adjustment required. However, elderly patients are at higher risk for QT prolongation and tendon disorders.
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - CrCl >40 mL/min: No adjustment.
    - **CrCl ≤40 mL/min:** Reduce dose to **160 mg every 24 hours**. This includes patients on hemodialysis or CAPD [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild (Child-Pugh A), moderate (B), or severe (C) impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal. Inhibits DNA synthesis by targeting DNA gyrase and topoisomerase IV. It exhibits dual-targeting activity against *S. pneumoniae*.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; 71% absolute bioavailability. Peak plasma levels (Cmax) reached in 0.5–2 hours.
    - **Distribution:** 60–70% protein bound. Penetrates well into lung tissue (bronchial mucosa, epithelial lining fluid).
    - **Metabolism:** Limited hepatic metabolism via non-CYP450 pathways.
    - **Excretion:** Dual routes; approximately 61% in feces and 36% in urine as drug and metabolites. Mean elimination half-life is ~7 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ABECB:** Three pivotal trials (Studies 068, 070, 212) established that 320 mg daily for 5 days was non-inferior to 7-day courses of clarithromycin, amoxicillin/clavulanate, or levofloxacin.
- **CAP:** A 5-day course was compared to a 7-day course (Study OP-634-001), showing success rates of 95.0% and 92.1%, respectively. Additional studies confirmed efficacy against MDRSP over 7 days of treatment.
- **Rash Study:** A specific safety study in healthy females <40 years showed that gemifloxacin 320 mg for 10 days resulted in a significantly higher rash rate than ciprofloxacin, particularly between days 8 and 10 of therapy.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions permitted to 15°–30°C (59°–86°F).
- Protect from light [US].
