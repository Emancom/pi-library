# Editorial Product Information: Gliclazide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Gliclazide (Modified Release) for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidance required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the Australian PI was provided for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Gliclazide (a sulfonylurea containing an N-containing heterocyclic ring).
- **Brand Name(s):** APO-Gliclazide [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Modified-release (MR) tablets: 30 mg [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus:** Indicated for use in conjunction with dietary modifications when such measures alone are insufficient for glycaemic control [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The daily dose ranges from 30 mg to 120 mg, administered once daily [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets must be taken at breakfast. Because gliclazide MR tablets are designed for modified release, they must be swallowed whole and should not be crushed, broken, or chewed. Co-administration with food is mandatory to mitigate the risk of hypoglycaemia [Australian].
- **Titration Schedules:** The recommended starting dose is 30 mg daily (including in patients ≥ 65 years). Dose adjustments should occur in increments of 30 mg based on fasting blood glucose levels. Each titration step should be maintained for at least two weeks [Australian].
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** If a dose is missed, the patient should not increase the subsequent day's dose [Australian].
- **Switching Therapy:** Gliclazide 30 mg MR can replace gliclazide 80 mg immediate-release tablets on a tablet-for-tablet basis (for doses of 1–4 tablets/day). When switching from other sulfonylureas with long half-lives, a 1- to 2-week monitoring period is recommended to prevent overlapping hypoglycaemic effects [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to gliclazide, other sulfonylureas, sulfonamides, or any product excipients [Australian].
- Type 1 diabetes mellitus [Australian].
- Diabetic ketoacidosis, pre-coma, or coma [Australian].
- Severe renal impairment [Australian].
- Severe hepatic impairment [Australian].
- Concurrent treatment with miconazole (systemic or oromucosal gel) [Australian].
- Pregnancy and lactation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
- **Hypoglycaemia:** Sulfonylureas may cause severe or prolonged hypoglycaemia requiring hospitalisation. Risk factors include malnutrition, irregular meals, strenuous exercise, and hepatic or renal dysfunction. It may be difficult to recognise in the elderly or those on beta-blockers [Australian].
- **Stress-Related Hyperglycaemia:** In cases of fever, trauma, infection, or surgery, glycaemic control may be lost; temporary insulin therapy may be required [Australian].
- **G6PD Deficiency:** Use with caution as sulfonylureas can induce haemolytic anaemia in patients with G6PD deficiency; non-sulfonylurea alternatives should be considered [Australian].
- **Secondary Failure:** The efficacy of gliclazide may diminish over time due to disease progression or reduced treatment response [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Regular monitoring of glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c) and blood glucose is required [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events observed in clinical trials were generally mild and transient.

| Frequency | System Organ Class | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Metabolism | Hyperglycaemia (9%), Hypoglycaemia (3.5 per 100 patient months) |
| | Nervous System | Headache (8%), Dizziness (2%) |
| | Infections | Viral infection (7%), Respiratory infection (3%), UTI (6%) |
| | Gastrointestinal | Diarrhoea (5%), Nausea, Vomiting |
| | Musculoskeletal | Back pain (2%), Arthrosis (2%) |
| **Rare** (<0.1%) | Hepatobiliary | Cholestatic jaundice, Hepatitis (requires discontinuation) |
| | Haematological | Anaemia, leucopenia, thrombocytopenia, agranulocytosis |
| | Dermatological | SJS, TEN, DRESS, Angioedema, Pruritus, Urticaria |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Pancytopenia, gastrointestinal haemorrhage, and severe hypoglycaemia leading to coma or death [Australian].
- **Class Effects:** Erythrocytopenia, agranulocytosis, haemolytic anaemia, and hyponatremia have been reported with the sulfonylurea class [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Contraindicated:** **Miconazole** (increases hypoglycaemic effect, potentially leading to coma) [Australian].
- **Not Recommended:** 
    - **Phenylbutazone:** Displaces sulfonylureas from plasma proteins and reduces elimination [Australian].
    - **Alcohol:** Potentiates hypoglycaemia and may cause a disulfiram-like reaction [Australian].
    - **Danazol:** Diabetogenic effect [Australian].
- **Combinations Requiring Caution (Potentiation of Hypoglycaemia):** Other antidiabetics, beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors, fluconazole, MAOIs, H2-receptor antagonists, clarithromycin, and NSAIDs [Australian].
- **Combinations Requiring Caution (Increased Blood Glucose):** Chlorpromazine (>100 mg/day), glucocorticoids, tetracosactrin, and intravenous salbutamol or terbutaline [Australian].
- **Other Interactions:** 
    - **Fluoroquinolones:** Risk of dysglycaemia (hypo- or hyperglycaemia), especially in the elderly [Australian].
    - **Warfarin:** Sulfonylureas may potentiate the anticoagulant effect [Australian].
    - **St John’s Wort:** Decreases gliclazide exposure [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C [Australian].** Sulfonylureas may cross the placenta and cause neonatal hypoglycaemia. Gliclazide is contraindicated; insulin is the preferred agent for diabetes management during pregnancy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. It is unknown if gliclazide is excreted in human milk, and a risk of neonatal hypoglycaemia cannot be excluded [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No clinical data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established; gliclazide is intended for adult use only [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment is required for patients ≥ 65 years, though they are at higher risk for hypoglycaemia and should be monitored closely [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required for mild to moderate impairment (CrCl 15–80 mL/min). Contraindicated in severe renal failure [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe hepatic impairment due to increased risk of hypoglycaemia and reduced neoglucogenesis [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Stimulates insulin secretion from pancreatic beta-cells by binding with high affinity to $\beta$-cell $K_{ATP}$ channels. It also increases peripheral insulin sensitivity (upregulating GLUT4) and decreases hepatic glucose production [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Restores the first peak of insulin secretion and increases the second phase. It may also provide haemovascular benefits (reduced platelet aggregation and increased fibrinolytic activity), though clinical benefit of these effects is not proven [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Complete absorption; food does not affect the rate or extent.
    - **Distribution:** Approximately 95% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic; no active metabolites detected in plasma.
    - **Elimination:** Half-life is approximately 16 hours. Less than 1% is excreted unchanged in the urine [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- In controlled trials involving 955 patients, the 30 mg MR formulation (doses 30–120 mg daily) was as effective as immediate-release gliclazide 80 mg in controlling HbA1c.
- The incidence of hypoglycaemia was lower in the MR group (3.5 episodes per 100 patient months) compared to the immediate-release group (4.8 episodes per 100 patient months) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C.
- **Handling:** Protect from moisture [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to product packaging for expiry [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
