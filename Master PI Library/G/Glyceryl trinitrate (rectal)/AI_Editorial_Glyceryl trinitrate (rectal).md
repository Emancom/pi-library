# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing within a specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. 
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule for fatty acid transport).
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for both acute and chronic management of patients with inborn metabolic errors resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. This may be administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). It should be administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Dosing by Indication
- **Severe Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance (Metabolic):** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been used [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration before administration [US].
- For ESRD patients, dose adjustments (e.g., downward to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels [US].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These reactions often occur within minutes of intravenous administration, particularly in ESRD patients on dialysis. If a severe reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued immediately and appropriate medical treatment initiated [US].
- **Oral Formulation Restriction:** Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Seizure Potential:** Both oral and intravenous levocarnitine have been associated with reports of seizures. In patients with pre-existing seizure disorders, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** In metabolic disorders, it is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine levels prior to therapy. Regular weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events observed in clinical trials of ESRD patients (frequency ≥5% regardless of causality) include:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), headache (22%), chest pain (12%), fever (7%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), palpitations (5%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), abdominal pain (9%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%).
- **Neurological/Psychiatric:** Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), depression (5%), paresthesia (5%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%), hypervolemia (5%).

**Serious/Postmarketing Adverse Effects:**
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (in patients with or without prior history) [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, laryngeal edema [US].
- **Other:** Body odor and gastritis have been reported less frequently [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concurrently with warfarin. It is recommended that INR levels be monitored following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not assigned/Not specified in source text]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. However, there are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. The benefits of maternal supplementation must be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant; discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use is guided by the same weight-based dosing (50 mg/kg) as adults for metabolic disorders [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** See "ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis" in Section 3. Oral formulations are contraindicated for chronic use in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Following intravenous bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours, and the mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

### Pharmacodynamics
- In patients with defects in fatty acid metabolism, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine, which is rapidly cleared by the kidneys [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg administered three times weekly post-dialysis resulted in a significant increase in plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Trough levels remained stable or increased over 8 weeks, reaching mean concentrations of 190 ± 55 µmol/L compared to a baseline of 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses confirmed a dose-dependent increase in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservative [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
