# Editorial Product Information: Guanfacine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- Information regarding other major jurisdictions (e.g., US, European, Canadian, Japanese) was not provided for this specific review; therefore, references to these regions are marked as "Not specified in available sources" where they would typically differ.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals to support clinical decision-making. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidance required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Guanfacine hydrochloride (a selective alpha$_{2A}$-adrenergic receptor agonist).
- **Brand Name(s):** **INTUNIV** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** C02AC02 (Antihypertensive); N06BA11 (Other psychostimulants and nootropics).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Modified-release tablets: 1 mg, 2 mg, 3 mg, and 4 mg [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Takeda Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD):** Indicated for the treatment of ADHD in children and adolescents aged 6–17 years. Use is approved as:
    - **Monotherapy:** When stimulants or atomoxetine are unsuitable, not tolerated, or ineffective.
    - **Adjunctive Therapy:** To psychostimulants where there has been a sub-optimal response [Australian].
- **Note:** Must be used as part of a comprehensive management programme involving psychological, educational, and social measures [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Paediatric 6–17 years):**
    - **Initial Dose:** 1 mg once daily.
    - **Titration:** Increase in increments of no more than 1 mg per week.
    - **Maintenance Dose:** Recommended range of 0.05–0.12 mg/kg/day. 
- **Target Dosing by Weight:**
    - 25.0–33.9 kg: 2–3 mg/day.
    - 34.0–41.4 kg: 2–4 mg/day.
    - 41.5–49.4 kg: 3–5 mg/day.
    - 49.5–58.4 kg: 3–6 mg/day.
    - 58.5–91.0 kg: 4–7 mg/day.
    - $\ge$ 91.0 kg: 5–7 mg/day.
- **Maximum Doses:** 4 mg/day in children (6–12 years); 7 mg/day in adolescents (13–17 years) [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Take once daily (morning or evening).
    - **Do not crush, chew, or break** tablets (increases release rate).
    - Do **not** administer with high-fat meals (increases exposure).
    - Do **not** take with grapefruit juice.
- **Missed Dose:** Resume the next day. If $\ge$ 2 doses are missed, re-titration is recommended.
- **Discontinuation:** Do not stop abruptly. Taper in decrements of $\le$ 1 mg every 3–7 days to avoid rebound hypertension.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to guanfacine, its excipients (including lactose), or other guanfacine-containing products [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** Can cause syncope, hypotension, and bradycardia. Monitor heart rate and blood pressure prior to initiation, following dose increases, periodically during treatment, and upon discontinuation.
- **Rebound Hypertension:** Abrupt cessation can lead to clinically significant increases in blood pressure and pulse. Cases of hypertensive encephalopathy have been reported rarely.
- **Sedation and Somnolence:** Common, particularly at the start of treatment. Use caution when driving or operating machinery. Avoid alcohol.
- **Suicidal Ideation:** Post-marketing reports of ideation, attempts, and rare completions have occurred with ADHD medications. Monitor patients for emergent suicidal behaviour.
- **Aggression:** Monitor for the appearance or worsening of aggressive behaviour or hostility.
- **BMI and Growth:** Treatment may increase BMI. Monitor height, weight, and BMI every 3 months for the first year, then every 6 months.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often dose-related and most prominent at the start of therapy.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Somnolence, Headache, Fatigue, Abdominal pain (monotherapy), Insomnia (co-administration). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Decreased appetite, Anxiety, Affect lability, Nightmare, Dizziness, Sedation, Lethargy, Bradycardia, Hypotension, Vomiting, Diarrhoea, Nausea, Dry mouth, Constipation, Rash, Enuresis, Irritability, Weight increased. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Hypersensitivity, Depression, Agitation, Hallucinations, Syncope, Convulsion, Tachycardia, Sinus arrhythmia, AV block ($1^{st}$ degree), Pallor, Asthma, Dyspepsia, Pruritus, Pollakiuria, Asthenia, Chest pain, Increased ALT, Increased BP. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Hypersomnia, Hypertension. |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Hypertensive encephalopathy (post-marketing). |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Syncope, severe bradycardia, and rebound hypertension/hypertensive encephalopathy [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **CYP3A4/5 Inhibitors (e.g., Ketoconazole):** Substantial increase in guanfacine exposure (3-fold AUC increase). Use caution and consider dose reduction.
    - **CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., Rifampicin):** Significant decrease in exposure (70% AUC reduction). Consider dose increase within the recommended range.
    - **Valproic Acid:** May increase valproic acid concentrations. Monitor for additive CNS effects and serum levels.
    - **Transporters:** Guanfacine inhibits MATE1 and may inhibit OCT1; may increase concentrations of substrates like metformin.
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **CNS Depressants:** Additive sedation/somnolence (alcohol, benzodiazepines, antipsychotics).
    - **Antihypertensives:** Additive risk of hypotension and syncope.
    - **Psychostimulants:** While co-administration is approved, it may increase the risk of rebound hypertension upon guanfacine withdrawal.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. No adequate studies in pregnant women. Animal studies show guanfacine crosses the placenta. Use only if benefits outweigh risks.
- **Breastfeeding:** Non-clinical studies show excretion in rat milk. It is unknown if it enters human milk. Exercise caution [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects observed in animal studies at 22 times the MRHD.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in children < 6 years old.
- **Elderly Use:** Safety and efficacy not established; not recommended for use in this population.
- **Renal Impairment:** Clearance is approximately 30% renal. Dose reduction may be required in severe impairment (GFR 29–15 mL/min), end-stage renal disease (GFR < 15 mL/min), or dialysis. Not assessed specifically in paediatric populations [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Clearance is approximately 50% hepatic. Dose reduction may be required for various degrees of impairment. Not assessed specifically in paediatric populations [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selective alpha$_{2A}$-adrenergic receptor agonist. It is not a CNS stimulant. It is thought to modulate signalling in the prefrontal cortex and basal ganglia by modifying synaptic norepinephrine transmission.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ ~5 hours. High-fat meals increase $C_{max}$ (~75%) and AUC (~40%).
    - **Distribution:** ~70% plasma protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily CYP3A4/5-mediated oxidation, followed by sulfation and glucuronidation.
    - **Elimination:** $T_{1/2}$ ~18 hours. Eliminated via hepatic metabolism and renal excretion (OCT2 transporter).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy:** Demonstrated in multiple short-term (8–15 week) trials and one randomised withdrawal trial.
- **SPD503-316:** A trial in patients 6–17 years showed guanfacine was superior to placebo in ADHD-RS-IV total score reduction, with results comparable to atomoxetine (though not designed for head-to-head superiority) [Australian].
- **Maintenance (SPD503-315):** A 41-week study showed significant superiority over placebo in maintaining treatment response (failure rate 49.3% vs 64.9%).
- **Cognitive Impact:** Study SPD503-206 showed no evidence of speed processing impairment compared to placebo.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** 4 years [Australian].
- **Packaging:** Blister packs (7 or 28 tablets).

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (S4) – Prescription Only Medicine** [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription only [Australian].
