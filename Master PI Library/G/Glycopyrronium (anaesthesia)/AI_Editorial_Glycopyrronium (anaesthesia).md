# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for healthcare professionals, synthesising data from the provided product information.
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule involved in mitochondrial fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Class**: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name(s)**: Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code**: A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength**: Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration**: Intravenous only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism)**: For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD)**: For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders**:
    - **Recommended Dose**: 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    - **Initial Management**: A loading dose is often administered in patients with severe metabolic crisis, followed by equivalent doses over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Frequency**: Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours. 
    - **Maximum Dose**: The highest recorded dose is 300 mg/kg.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis**:
    - **Recommended Starting Dose**: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Administration**: Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Adjustment**: Dose adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
- **Compatibility**: Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature (25°C) when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions**: Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. Treatment must be discontinued immediately upon signs of severe hypersensitivity [US].
- **Seizure Activity**: Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **General Precautions**: Chronic administration of high oral doses in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are usually renally excreted [US].
- **Monitoring**: 
    - Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
    - For ESRD patients, monitor trough (pre-dialysis) concentrations.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported at a frequency of $\ge 5\%$ (regardless of causality) in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials include:
- **Body as a Whole**: Abdominal pain (9%), headache (22%), infection (15%), injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), fever (7%), asthenia (9%).
- **Cardiovascular**: Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), chest pain (12%).
- **Digestive**: Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), anorexia (5%).
- **Nervous System**: Dizziness (13%), paresthesia (5%), insomnia (4%), depression (5%).
- **Respiratory**: Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%).
- **Other**: Hypercalcemia (9%), anemia (6%), pruritus (5%).

### Post-marketing Experience
- **Neurologic**: Seizures.
- **Gastrointestinal**: Transient nausea and vomiting; body odor; gastritis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin**: Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management**: Monitoring of INR is recommended after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **Category**: Not assigned a letter category in source, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm [US].
    - **Recommendation**: Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding**: 
    - Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied.
    - Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after exogenous administration.
    - **Recommendation**: Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: See Section 3 for general metabolic dosing; specific age-range limitations are not detailed in the source.
- **Elderly Use**: Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment**: Safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. For IV use in ESRD, see Section 3 [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacodynamics**: Restores carnitine levels in primary systemic deficiency and alleviates metabolic abnormalities in inborn errors (e.g., glutaric aciduria II, propionic acidemia).
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Distribution**: Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism**: Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (primarily feces) [US].
    - **Elimination**: Mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine over 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Study (ESRD)**: A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly showed that trough concentrations (initially ~20 µmol/L) increased to between 54 and 180 µmol/L after one week of therapy [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study**: ESRD patients (N≈30 per group) received 10, 20, or 40 mg/kg three times weekly. At 24 weeks, mean trough concentrations were 148, 240, and 456 µmol/L, respectively [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions**: Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
- **Handling**: Single-dose vial; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservative [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States**: Rx-only; Poison schedule not applicable.
