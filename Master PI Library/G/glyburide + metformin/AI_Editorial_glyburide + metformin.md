# Editorial Product Information: glyburide + metformin

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **glyburide + metformin**
- Chemical class:
  - Glyburide: sulfonylurea
  - Metformin (metformin hydrochloride): biguanide
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Last revised 7/2026 [date reported in available source]
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycemic control in adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus.
- Other indications: Not specified in available sources

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General dosing (adult)
- Administer in divided doses, twice daily, with meals.
- Available marketed strengths (clinical dosing strengths reported): 1.25 mg glyburide / 250 mg metformin HCl; 2.5 mg glyburide / 500 mg metformin HCl; 5 mg glyburide / 500 mg metformin HCl.
- Maximum recommended total daily dose: 20 mg glyburide / 2,000 mg metformin HCl.

### Initiation and titration
- Patients not previously treated with either glyburide (or another sulfonylurea) or metformin HCl:
  - Initiate with a formulation at a dose of 1.25 mg glyburide / 250 mg metformin HCl once or twice daily with meals.
- Patients not adequately controlled on either glyburide (or another sulfonylurea) or metformin HCl alone:
  - Recommended starting dose: 2.5 mg glyburide / 500 mg metformin HCl OR 5 mg glyburide / 500 mg metformin HCl twice daily with meals.
- Patients already receiving combination therapy of glyburide (or another sulfonylurea) plus metformin HCl:
  - Starting dose should not exceed the daily dose of glyburide (or equivalent sulfonylurea) and metformin HCl the patient is already taking.
- Titration:
  - Increase the dose gradually based on glycemic control and tolerability up to the stated maximum daily dose.

### Administration instructions
- Take with meals; give divided doses twice daily.
- For patients taking colesevelam concomitantly: administer glyburide + metformin at least 4 hours prior to colesevelam.

### Special procedural instructions
- Iodinated contrast imaging procedures:
  - Discontinue glyburide + metformin at the time of, or prior to, iodinated contrast imaging in:
    - patients with an estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) between 30 and 60 mL/min/1.73 m2;
    - patients with a history of liver disease, alcoholism, or heart failure;
    - patients who will be administered intra-arterial iodinated contrast.
  - Re-evaluate eGFR 48 hours after the procedure; restart therapy if renal function is stable.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources

## 5. Contraindications
List includes all contraindications reported in available sources:
- Severe renal impairment (eGFR below 30 mL/min/1.73 m2).
- Known hypersensitivity to metformin or glyburide.
- Acute or chronic metabolic acidosis, including diabetic ketoacidosis, with or without coma.
- Concomitant administration of bosentan.
- Other contraindications: Not specified in available sources

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed warning
- **Lactic acidosis** — Postmarketing cases of metformin‑associated lactic acidosis have resulted in death, hypothermia, hypotension, and resistant bradyarrhythmias. Onset may be subtle with nonspecific symptoms (malaise, myalgias, respiratory distress, somnolence, abdominal pain). If lactic acidosis is suspected, immediately discontinue **glyburide + metformin** and institute general supportive measures in a hospital setting; prompt hemodialysis is recommended. [US]

### General warnings and precautions
- **Lactic acidosis**:
  - Risk factors include renal impairment, concomitant use of certain drugs (e.g., carbonic anhydrase inhibitors such as topiramate), age ≥65 years, iodinated contrast studies, surgery/other procedures with restricted intake, hypoxic states (e.g., acute congestive heart failure), excessive alcohol intake, and hepatic impairment.
  - Obtain baseline eGFR prior to initiation and periodically thereafter; metformin is substantially renally excreted and accumulation increases lactic acidosis risk.
- **Hypoglycemia**:
  - Glyburide can cause severe hypoglycemia. Risk increases with advanced age, renal impairment, concomitant antidiabetic agents, poor caloric intake, prolonged exercise, alcohol ingestion, adrenal/pituitary/hepatic insufficiency, and concomitant drugs that potentiate hypoglycemia. Counsel patients to recognize and manage hypoglycemia.
- **Potential increased cardiovascular mortality with sulfonylureas**:
  - Historical data suggest sulfonylurea therapy may be associated with increased cardiovascular mortality; inform patients of risks, benefits, and alternatives.
- **Hemolytic anemia**:
  - Sulfonylureas may cause hemolytic anemia in patients with glucose‑6‑phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency; avoid use in known G6PD deficiency. Postmarketing reports of hemolytic anemia have also occurred in patients without known G6PD deficiency.
- **Vitamin B12 deficiency**:
  - Metformin may lower vitamin B12 levels; decreases to subnormal levels were observed in clinical studies. Measure hematologic parameters annually and assess vitamin B12 at 2–3 year intervals; manage abnormalities appropriately.
- **Macrovascular outcomes**:
  - No clinical studies have established conclusive evidence of macrovascular risk reduction with glyburide + metformin.
- **Use in hepatic impairment**:
  - Avoid use in patients with clinical or laboratory evidence of hepatic disease due to increased risk of lactic acidosis.
- **Perioperative and acute illness**:
  - Temporarily discontinue when patients have restricted food/fluid intake or conditions predisposing to hypoxia, dehydration, or renal impairment.

### Monitoring requirements
- Renal function (eGFR) before initiation and periodically thereafter; at least annually in all patients and more frequently in elderly or those at risk for renal impairment.
- Reassess eGFR 48 hours after iodinated contrast imaging if therapy was withheld.
- Hematologic parameters annually.
- Vitamin B12 levels every 2–3 years, or sooner if clinically indicated.
- Monitor for signs/symptoms of lactic acidosis and hypoglycemia.
- Other laboratory monitoring: not specified in available sources

## 8. Drug Interactions

### Pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic interactions (clinically significant)
- Drugs that may increase the risk of metformin accumulation / lactic acidosis:
  - Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors (e.g., topiramate) may increase lactic acidosis risk — consider more frequent monitoring.
  - Drugs that reduce metformin clearance (examples reported): ranolazine, vandetanib, dolutegravir, cimetidine — concomitant use may increase metformin accumulation; consider benefit/risk.
- Drugs that potentiate hypoglycemia:
  - Concomitant antidiabetic medicines and other drugs that increase hypoglycemic effect may necessitate dose reduction and closer monitoring.
- Bosentan:
  - Concomitant administration with bosentan is contraindicated.
- Colesevelam:
  - Concomitant colesevelam may reduce glyburide absorption; when coadministered, give glyburide + metformin at least 4 hours before colesevelam.
- Alcohol:
  - Excessive alcohol intake (acute or chronic) potentiates the effect of metformin on lactate metabolism; warn patients against excessive alcohol consumption.
- Iodinated contrast agents:
  - Intravascular iodinated contrast can acutely reduce renal function and precipitate lactic acidosis in metformin-treated patients; withhold therapy in specified patients and re-evaluate renal function 48 hours post‑procedure.
- Other interactions:
  - Drugs that impair renal function, cause significant hemodynamic changes, interfere with acid-base balance, or otherwise increase metformin accumulation may increase risk of lactic acidosis — consider more frequent monitoring.
- For other drug interactions not specifically listed in the available excerpts: Not specified in available sources

## 10. Use in Special Populations

### Paediatric use
- Approvals, age ranges, and dosing for pediatric patients: Not specified in available sources

### Elderly use
- Increased risk: Elderly patients (≥65 years) have higher risk for lactic acidosis due to higher likelihood of renal, hepatic, or cardiac impairment.
- Recommendation: Assess renal function more frequently in geriatric patients.

### Renal impairment
- Assess eGFR prior to initiation and periodically thereafter.
- Contraindicated in patients with eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m2.
- Initiation not recommended in patients with eGFR between 30 and 45 mL/min/1.73 m2.
- If a patient taking therapy has an eGFR that falls below 45 mL/min/1.73 m2, assess benefit versus risk of continuing therapy.
- Discontinue therapy if eGFR falls below 30 mL/min/1.73 m2.

### Hepatic impairment
- Avoid use in patients with clinical or laboratory evidence of hepatic disease due to increased risk of lactic acidosis.

## 11. Pharmacology

### Mechanism of action
- Classification reported: glyburide is a sulfonylurea; metformin is a biguanide.
- Detailed molecular mechanisms (e.g., insulin release stimulation by glyburide, suppression of hepatic gluconeogenesis by metformin): Not specified in available sources

### Pharmacokinetics
- Metformin is substantially excreted by the kidney; accumulation increases with severity of renal impairment.
- Metformin is dialyzable; reported hemodialysis clearance up to approximately 170 mL/min under favorable hemodynamic conditions.
- Metformin plasma levels >5 mcg/mL have been associated with lactic acidosis in reported cases.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Clinical study overview available in excerpts:
  - Double-blind clinical studies of glyburide + metformin used as initial therapy or as second-line therapy of 20 and 14 weeks, respectively, were reported. In these trials, a total of 642 patients received glyburide + metformin, 312 received metformin HCl alone, 324 received glyburide alone, and 161 received placebo.
  - Adverse reactions reported in these trials included diarrhea, headache, nausea/vomiting, abdominal pain, and dizziness; diarrhea rates in combination-treated patients were reported as >5% in the clinical trial context.
- Macrovascular outcomes: No clinical studies showing conclusive evidence of macrovascular risk reduction with glyburide + metformin.
