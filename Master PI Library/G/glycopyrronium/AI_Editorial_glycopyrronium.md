# Editorial Product Information: glycopyrronium

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): glycopyrronium  
- Chemical class: Anticholinergic (quaternary ammonium antimuscarinic agent)  
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources  
- Route(s) of administration: Topical (axillary application) and parenteral formulations referenced in pharmacokinetic/toxicology data (intravenous data described in sources)

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources  
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Last revision stated as 11/2022 in one available source [jurisdiction not attributed here per source rules]. Other jurisdictions' revision dates: Not specified in available sources  
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Topical treatment of primary axillary hyperhidrosis in adults and pediatric patients 9 years of age and older.  
  - [US] Indicated for topical treatment of primary axillary hyperhidrosis in adults and pediatric patients ≥9 years.  
- Other indications or regional differences: Not specified in available sources

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult and pediatric ≥9 years)
- Apply once daily to both axillae using a single pre-moistened cloth containing 2.4% glycopyrronium solution (one cloth for both underarms). Do not apply more frequently than once every 24 hours.

### Administration instructions
- Apply to clean, dry skin of the underarm (axillary) areas only.  
- Tear open pouch, unfold cloth, wipe across one underarm once and then the other underarm once using the same cloth. Discard cloth in household trash, out of reach of children.  
- Wash hands immediately with soap and water after applying and discarding the cloth.  
- Avoid transfer to eyes or periocular area; inadvertent ocular exposure can cause mydriasis and blurred vision.  
- Do not apply to broken, irritated, or inflamed skin.  
- Avoid use with occlusive dressings (may increase systemic absorption and anticholinergic effects).  

### Titration / dose adjustment
- No titration schedule provided in available sources. No alternative dosing regimens specified.

### Missed dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
List includes all contraindications reported in the available sources (do not use in patients with any of the following):
- Narrow-angle or uncontrolled glaucoma (or any glaucoma that can be exacerbated by anticholinergic effects)  
- Paralytic ileus  
- Unstable cardiovascular status in acute hemorrhage  
- Severe ulcerative colitis  
- Toxic megacolon complicating ulcerative colitis  
- Myasthenia gravis  
- Sjögren’s syndrome

(Where jurisdictions list additional or differing contraindications: Not specified in available sources.)

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- No boxed/black-box warnings specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- **New or worsening urinary retention:** Cases of new or worsening urinary retention have occurred with topical use; patients with a history of urinary retention, prostatic hypertrophy, or bladder-neck obstruction should use with caution. Instruct patients to discontinue and seek medical attention if difficulty passing urine or bladder distension occurs.  
- **Control of body temperature / risk of heat illness:** Anticholinergic reduction of sweating can impair thermoregulation. In hot ambient temperatures generalized lack of sweating, hyperpyrexia, or heat stroke may occur. Patients should monitor for decreased sweating in hot/warm environments and avoid use if not sweating under these conditions.  
- **Ocular effects / operating machinery:** Transient mydriasis and blurred vision can occur if product contacts the eye or periocular area. If blurred vision occurs, discontinue until symptoms resolve and avoid operating vehicles or machinery requiring clear vision until resolution.  
- **Risk of accidental exposure (children):** Accidental ocular exposures have occurred (e.g., discarded wipes accessed by children, transfer from hands to eyes), causing mydriasis, anisocoria, and blurred vision; effects were generally temporary and resolved within about one week. Strict adherence to handwashing and disposal instructions is essential.  
- **Increased absorption with occlusion:** Application under occlusive conditions may increase systemic absorption and anticholinergic adverse effects (e.g., dry mouth, urinary hesitation, etc.).  
- **Renal impairment:** Elimination is markedly affected in renal failure (see Pharmacokinetics and Special Populations). Use caution in patients with significant renal impairment.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor for signs/symptoms of urinary retention, decreased sweating/heat intolerance, ocular symptoms (mydriasis, blurred vision), excessive systemic anticholinergic effects (dry mouth, constipation, tachycardia), and local skin reactions.  
- Advise patients to wash hands after use and to avoid touching eyes/periocular area.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Concomitant use with other anticholinergic-containing drugs may produce additive anticholinergic effects (e.g., increased dry mouth, constipation, urinary retention, blurred vision, decreased sweating). Avoid coadministration with other anticholinergic agents when possible. [No jurisdictional variation specified in available sources.]

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- In vitro studies indicated that, under conditions of clinical use, glycopyrronium is not expected to induce CYP1A2, 2B6, or 3A4, nor to inhibit CYP1A2, 2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, or 3A4. Clinical relevance: no specific clinically significant CYP-mediated interactions were identified in the available sources.

### Management notes
- Assess the patient's medication list for additive anticholinergic burden and adjust therapy accordingly. Monitor for enhanced anticholinergic adverse effects if combination therapy cannot be avoided.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Human data: No available data on glycopyrronium use in pregnant women to inform fetal risk.  
- Animal data:  
  - Rats: Oral glycopyrrolate during organogenesis produced decreased maternal weight gain and decreased fetal weight at higher doses; minor skeletal changes (reduced ossification) likely secondary to maternal toxicity; no increase in gross external or visceral malformations reported. Doses studied: up to 400 mg/kg/day.  
  - Rabbits: Intravenous glycopyrrolate during organogenesis showed no embryo‑fetal developmental adverse effects at doses up to 1.0 mg/kg/day; some maternal weight gain/food consumption reductions at higher doses.  
  - Peri- and post-natal rat studies: decreased pup weight during nursing at maternal doses up to 400 mg/kg/day, with recovery post-weaning; no notable effects on behavior, learning, memory, or reproductive function of pups.  
- Human pregnancy risk category systems (regional categories): Not specified in available sources.

### Breastfeeding
- No data on the presence of glycopyrronium or its metabolites in human milk, effects on the breastfed infant, or effects on milk production. Balance the benefits of breastfeeding against the mother's clinical need for glycopyrronium and potential infant risk.  

### Fertility
- Animal reproductive/fertility studies: Glycopyrrolate produced no treatment-related effects on fertility or reproductive parameters in rats at doses up to 100 mg/kg/day.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Safety, effectiveness, and pharmacokinetics established for topical axillary use in pediatric patients aged ≥9 years (supported by two randomized vehicle-controlled trials including pediatric subjects). Use in patients <9 years: safety and effectiveness have not been established. [US: approved ≥9 years per available source.]

### Elderly
- Clinical trials did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged ≥65 years to determine whether they respond differently from younger subjects. Use caution due to expected increased sensitivity to anticholinergic effects in elderly patients. No specific dose adjustment provided in available sources.

### Renal impairment
- Elimination markedly impaired in patients with renal failure based on intravenous glycopyrronium data: uremic subjects demonstrated greater AUC and lower clearance versus healthy subjects. Pharmacokinetics of topical glycopyrronium cloth in renal impairment were not studied. Exercise caution in patients with significant renal impairment; monitor for anticholinergic accumulation and adverse effects. No specific dosage adjustment by eGFR/stage specified in available sources.

### Hepatic impairment
- Pharmacokinetics in hepatic impairment were not evaluated in available sources. No dosage adjustment recommendations specified.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **Glycopyrronium** is a competitive antagonist of acetylcholine at muscarinic receptors located on peripheral tissues, including eccrine sweat glands; inhibition of acetylcholine action at sweat glands reduces sweating.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Pharmacodynamic relationships of topical glycopyrronium (e.g., exposure–response relationships) are not fully characterized in available sources.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption (topical axillary application): Plasma exposures measured after once‑daily topical axillary application for 5 days showed low systemic absorption with no evidence of accumulation.  
  - Adults (mean ± SD): Cmax ≈ 0.08 ± 0.04 ng/mL; AUC0–6 ≈ 0.2 ± 0.14 h·ng/mL; AUC0–24 ≈ 0.88 ± 0.57 h·ng/mL; median Tmax ≈ 1 hour (range 0–10 h).  
  - Pediatric (ages 10–17 years, mean ± SD): Cmax ≈ 0.07 ± 0.06 ng/mL; AUC0–6 ≈ 0.18 ± 0.13 h·ng/mL; median Tmax ≈ 1.5 hours (range 0–6 h).  
- Distribution: After intravenous administration in pediatric subjects (1–14 years), mean volume of distribution ≈ 1.3–1.8 L/kg (range 0.7–3.9 L/kg). In adults aged 60–75 years, reported Vd ≈ 0.42 L/kg ± 0.22.  
- Metabolism: A small proportion is metabolized after IV dosing; specific metabolic pathway(s) are not characterized.  
- Elimination: After IV dosing, approximately 85% of total radioactivity recovered in urine and <5% in bile; >80% of radioactivity in urine and bile was unchanged drug. Renal excretion is a primary route of elimination; clearance is markedly reduced in renal failure.  
- In vitro CYP interactions: Not expected to induce CYP1A2, 2B6, or 3A4; not expected to inhibit CYP1A2, 2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, or 3A4 under conditions of clinical use.

## 12. Clinical Trials
### Pivotal trials (efficacy and safety)
- Two randomized, double‑blind, vehicle‑controlled multicenter trials enrolled subjects with primary axillary hyperhidrosis aged 9–76 years (total randomized population across trials ≈ 697 subjects). Key entry criteria included gravimetric sweat production ≥50 mg per axilla over 5 minutes and mean daily patient-reported severity (ASDD item #2) ≥4 on a 0–10 scale during baseline week.

- Trial design and co‑primary endpoints:
  - Randomized to once‑daily topical glycopyrronium cloth 2.4% vs vehicle applied to axillae.
  - Co‑primary endpoints: proportion of subjects achieving ≥4‑point improvement from baseline in weekly mean ASDD item #2 score at Week 4; and mean absolute change from baseline in gravimetrically measured sweat production (mg/5 minutes) at Week 4.

- Key efficacy results (Week 4):
  - Trial 1: Proportion achieving ≥4‑point ASDD improvement — 53% glycopyrronium vs 28% vehicle. Median change in sweat production: −81 mg (glycopyrronium) vs −66 mg (vehicle).
  - Trial 2: Proportion achieving ≥4‑point ASDD improvement — 66% glycopyrronium vs 27% vehicle. Median change in sweat production: −79 mg (glycopyrronium) vs −58 mg (vehicle).
  - Baseline median sweat production per 5 minutes ranged approximately 113–127 mg across arms/trials; baseline ASDD item #2 mean ≈ 7.2.

### Long‑term safety
- An open‑label safety extension (up to an additional 44 weeks) treated 564 subjects. Adverse reactions reported ≥2% included dry mouth (16.9%), vision blurred (6.7%), nasopharyngitis (5.8%), mydriasis (5.3%), urinary hesitation (4.2%), nasal dryness (3.6%), dry eye (2.9%), pharyngitis (2.2%), and various application site reactions (pain 6.4%, dermatitis 3.8%, pruritus 3.8%, rash 3.8%, erythema 2.4%).

## 13. Overdosage and Management
- Presentation of overdose: Excessive anticholinergic activity — flushing, hyperthermia, tachycardia, ileus, urinary retention, loss of ocular accommodation, light sensitivity due to mydriasis; with severe toxicity possible but central nervous system effects less likely due to quaternary ammonium structure (limited blood–brain barrier penetration). Topical overdosing or application under occlusive conditions may increase incidence/severity of local and systemic anticholinergic effects.  
- Management (supportive and symptomatic):  
  - Manage hyperthermia, seizures, coma, arrhythmias, and airway/ventilation per standard of care.  
  - Manage severe urinary retention with catheterization if not spontaneously reversed.  
  - Provide cardiovascular support as needed.  
  - Consider administration of a quaternary ammonium anticholinesterase (e.g., neostigmine) to alleviate severe life‑threatening peripheral anticholinergic effects.  
  - Local skin reactions may be increased with topical overdose; remove exposure, wash skin, and treat symptomatically.

## 14. Nonclinical Toxicology
- Carcinogenesis: Topical glycopyrronium tosylate applied to rats for up to 24 months at concentrations up to 4% w/w showed no carcinogenic potential. Oral studies with glycopyrrolate in mice and rats up to 24 months did not demonstrate treatment‑related increases in tumor incidence.  
- Mutagenesis: Glycopyrrolate/glycopyrronium tosylate was negative in a battery of genotoxicity assays (Ames, mouse lymphoma, in vivo micronucleus).  
- Impairment of fertility: No treatment‑related effects on fertility or reproductive parameters in rats at doses up to 100 mg/kg/day.

## 15. Postmarketing Experience
- New onset urinary retention has been reported postmarketing; because of spontaneous reporting, frequency cannot be reliably estimated. Accidental ocular exposure (children or transfer from hands) reported causing mydriasis, anisocoria, and blurred vision; usually temporary and resolved within about one week.

## 16. Patient Counselling Points (for clinicians to communicate)
- Instruct patients to apply once daily to clean, dry underarm skin using one cloth for both axillae and to discard the cloth safely out of reach of children. Emphasize immediate handwashing after use to prevent accidental ocular exposure.  
- Warn patients about the risk of blurred vision and to avoid driving or operating machinery if vision is impaired.  
- Advise patients to discontinue use and seek medical attention for symptoms of urinary retention (difficulty passing urine, bladder distension) or for signs of heat intolerance/decreased sweating in hot conditions.  
- Counsel patients about common local reactions (erythema, burning/stinging, pruritus) and systemic anticholinergic effects (dry mouth, dry throat, constipation, dry eyes, nasal dryness).  
- Review concomitant medications for anticholinergic agents and counsel regarding additive effects; avoid coadministration if possible.
