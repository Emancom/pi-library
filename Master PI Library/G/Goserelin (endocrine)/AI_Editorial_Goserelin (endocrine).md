# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory guidelines and full PI documents.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - [US]: Injection solution, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials.
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** 
    - [US]: Baxter Healthcare Corporation (Manufactured by Baxter Pharmaceuticals India Private Ltd).

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion. 
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose may be given, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. 
    - Dosing frequency should be every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours. 
    - The highest recorded dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - Administration should occur as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - Dose adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- [US]: None known.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, therapy must be discontinued immediately and appropriate medical treatment initiated [US].
- **Neurologic Reactions (Seizures):** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** In patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD, chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine (not specified for IV in this context, but noted as a precaution) may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].
- **Monitoring:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Very Common (>10%) and Common (1–10%)
Based on clinical trials in ESRD patients (regardless of causality):
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33–59%), pain (21–49%), flu syndrome (15–40%), headache (3–37%), chest pain (6–15%), abdominal pain (5–21%), fever (5–12%), accidental injury (8–12%), asthenia (8–12%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (14–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%), palpitations (3–8%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%), anorexia (3–6%), dyspepsia (5–10%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–33%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–19%), rhinitis (6–11%).
- **Metabolic/Nutritional:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hypervolemia (3–17%), peripheral edema (3–6%).
- **Nervous:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%), insomnia (3–6%), depression (3–6%).

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Anaphylaxis and Severe Hypersensitivity:** Bronchospasm and laryngeal edema.
- **Neurologic:** New-onset or worsening seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. Monitoring of INR is recommended upon initiation or dose adjustment [US].
- **Other Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - [US]: Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. There are no adequate/controlled studies in pregnant women. Use only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine after exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is calculated by weight (50 mg/kg); specific age-range safety profiles are not further detailed [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** See **Dosage and Administration** for ESRD-specific protocols. Chronic administration of high oral doses should be avoided in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
    - **Bioavailability:** Absolute oral bioavailability is approximately 15% [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis trough concentrations from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While carnitine concentrations increased, the impact on clinical outcomes in this specific population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation lacks preservatives [US].
