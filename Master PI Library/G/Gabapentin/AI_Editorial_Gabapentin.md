# Editorial Product Information: Gabapentin

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information from the following regulatory authority: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of gabapentin, intended for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single, scannable reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) legally mandated for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to the current PI approved in their region for definitive prescribing guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Gabapentin (Chemical class: Cyclohexaneacetic acid derivative; structurally related to GABA).
- **Brand Names:** **ALKEM GABAPEN** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N03AX12 (Not explicitly specified in source; provided for clinical context).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Hard Capsules:** 100 mg (White), 300 mg (Yellow), 400 mg (Orange) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Excipients of Note:** Contains lactose [Australian].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pharmacor Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Epilepsy:** Indicated as add-on therapy for the treatment of partial seizures (including secondarily generalised tonic-clonic seizures) in adults and children aged 3 years and older who have not achieved adequate control with standard anti-epileptic drugs [Australian].
- **Neuropathic Pain:** Treatment of neuropathic pain in adults aged 18 years and older [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- Gabapentin may be administered with or without food.
- The maximum time between doses in a three-times-daily (TID) schedule must not exceed 12 hours [Australian].

### Epilepsy (Adults and Children >12 Years)
- **Initiation:** As add-on therapy.
- **Starting Dose:** 300 mg TID on Day 1, or titrated (300 mg Day 1; 300 mg BID Day 2; 300 mg TID Day 3).
- **Maintenance:** Typically 900 mg to 1800 mg per day in divided doses (TID). Dosages up to 2400 mg/day have been well tolerated [Australian].

### Epilepsy (Children 3–12 Years)
- **Effective Dose:** 25 mg/kg/day to 35 mg/kg/day in three divided doses.
- **Titration:** Over 3 days (10 mg/kg Day 1; 20 mg/kg Day 2; 30 mg/kg Day 3) [Australian].

### Neuropathic Pain (Adults >18 Years)
- **Starting Dose:** 900 mg/day in three divided doses.
- **Titration:** Based on clinical response to a maximum dose of 3600 mg/day [Australian].

### Renal Impairment (Adults)
Dose adjustment is mandatory based on Creatinine Clearance (CrCl):
- **CrCl $\ge$ 80 mL/min:** 900–3600 mg/day.
- **CrCl 50–79 mL/min:** 600–1800 mg/day.
- **CrCl 30–49 mL/min:** 300–900 mg/day.
- **CrCl 15–29 mL/min:** 150 mg (as 300 mg every other day) to 600 mg/day.
- **CrCl <15 mL/min:** 150 mg (as 300 mg every other day) to 300 mg/day.
- **Haemodialysis:** For patients who have never received gabapentin, a loading dose of 300–400 mg is recommended, followed by 200–300 mg after every 4 hours of dialysis [Australian].

### Discontinuation
Do not stop abruptly. Withdraw gradually over a minimum of one week to reduce the risk of status epilepticus or withdrawal symptoms [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to gabapentin or any inactive ingredients (e.g., lactose) in the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Abuse and Dependence:** [Australian Warning] Gabapentin poses risks of abuse and dependence which can lead to overdose and death, particularly when combined with opioids or other CNS depressants. Monitor patients with a history of substance abuse closely.
- **Respiratory Depression:** Severe respiratory depression may occur without concomitant opioid use. Higher risk in the elderly, those with compromised respiratory function, or neurological disease.
- **Suicidal Behaviour and Ideation:** Anti-epileptic drugs (AEDs) increase the risk of suicidal thoughts. Monitor for changes in mood, depression, or self-harming behaviour starting as early as one week after initiation.
- **CNS Depression:** Significant dizziness and somnolence may occur, increasing the risk of accidental falls.
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), and DRESS have been reported. Discontinue immediately and permanently if signs appear.
- **Anaphylaxis:** Rare but serious risk; instruct patients to seek emergency care for swelling of the lips, throat, or tongue.
- **Withdrawal Symptoms:** Anxiety, insomnia, nausea, and sweating often occur within 48 hours of discontinuation if not tapered [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events are generally mild to moderate and occur most frequently during initiation.

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Somnolence, dizziness, ataxia, fatigue, fever (paediatric), viral infection (paediatric) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Nystagmus, tremor, dysarthria, amnesia, weight increase, peripheral oedema, dyspepsia, dry mouth, constipation, hostility (paediatric), hyperkinesia (paediatric) |
| **Post-Marketing/Rare** | Rhabdomyolysis, acute kidney failure, blood glucose fluctuations (hyper/hypoglycaemia in diabetics), pancreatitis, thrombocytopenia, hallucinations, hypomania |

Serious adverse effects include respiratory failure, coma (with concomitant CNS depressants), and sudden unexplained deaths [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Opioids:** Concomitant use increases gabapentin concentrations and risks of severe sedation, respiratory depression, and death. Reduce gabapentin dose if co-prescribed.
- **Antacids:** Aluminium/magnesium antacids reduce gabapentin bioavailability by approximately 20%. Administer gabapentin at least 2 hours after antacid use.
- **Morphine:** Co-administration of controlled-release morphine (60 mg) 2 hours prior to gabapentin (600 mg) increased gabapentin AUC by 44%.
- **Cimetidine:** May slightly decrease renal excretion of gabapentin.
- **AEDs:** No clinically significant interactions observed with phenobarbital, phenytoin, valproic acid, or carbamazepine.
- **Oral Contraceptives:** No effect on norethindrone or ethinyl estradiol pharmacokinetics [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Gabapentin crosses the placenta. Data suggests a 2–3 fold increase in risk of birth defects in offspring of mothers treated with AEDs. Use only if benefits clearly outweigh risks. Newborns may experience neonatal withdrawal syndrome.
- **Breastfeeding:** Gabapentin is excreted in human milk. The effect on the nursing infant is unknown; use only if benefits outweigh risks to the infant.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies (rats) showed no adverse effects on fertility at 14 times the human dose [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy for epilepsy established in children $\ge$ 3 years. Not established for neuropathic pain in patients < 18 years.
- **Elderly:** Increased risk of CNS depression and respiratory depression. Dosage should be adjusted based on renal function.
- **Renal Impairment:** Mandatory dose reduction based on CrCl (see Section 3).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources (gabapentin is not metabolised by the liver) [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Unknown. It binds with high affinity to a novel peptide binding site (alpha-2-delta subunit) in the brain. It does not bind to GABA receptors, nor does it affect GABA uptake or metabolism.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Bioavailability is *not* dose-proportional; it decreases as the dose increases. Food has no effect.
    - **Distribution:** Largely unbound to plasma proteins (<3%).
    - **Metabolism:** Not appreciably metabolised in humans.
    - **Excretion:** Eliminated exclusively by renal excretion as unchanged drug. Elimination half-life is 5 to 7 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adult Epilepsy:** Three multicentre trials in 705 adults showed significant "responder rates" (50% reduction in seizure frequency) at doses of 900–1200 mg/day compared to placebo.
- **Paediatric Epilepsy:** Two trials in 247 children (3–12 years) demonstrated gabapentin (25–35 mg/kg/day) was significantly better than placebo in controlling partial seizures.
- **Neuropathic Pain:** Two 8-week trials in diabetic peripheral neuropathy and post-herpetic neuralgia showed significant pain score reduction (Likert scale) compared to placebo starting from week 2 [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
