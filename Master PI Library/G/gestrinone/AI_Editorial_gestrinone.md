# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory and clinical data into a single reference. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing in any particular region. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Adult Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours and never less than every 6 hours.
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Adjustment:** Dose adjustments (e.g., downward to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, often within minutes of administration. These are reported primarily in ESRD patients. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate the risks before re-administration [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known seizure disorder, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:**
    - **Plasma Carnitine Levels:** Monitoring should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations. The recommended plasma free carnitine concentration range is 35 to 60 µmol/L [US].
    - **ESRD Monitoring:** Initiation of therapy is prompted by trough levels below 40–50 µmol/L [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted renally [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions reported in clinical trials for ESRD patients (frequency ≥5% regardless of causality):

| Body System | Adverse Event (Frequency %) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%), Chest pain (12%), Headache (22%), Fever (7%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%) |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%), Nausea (8%), Abdominal pain (9%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (23%), Cough increase (9%), Dyspnea (7%) |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness (13%), Insomnia (4%) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm [US].
- **Other Effects:** Transient nausea/vomiting and body odor have been observed.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalised Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dosage adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not explicitly assigned in updated format]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after exogenous administration. Weigh the benefits to the mother against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is based on mg/kg body weight, applicable to paediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. For IV use in ESRD, refer to Section 3 [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg post-dialysis three times weekly, mean pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levels increased from ~20 µmol/L at baseline to 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg in ESRD patients demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C.
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) [US].
