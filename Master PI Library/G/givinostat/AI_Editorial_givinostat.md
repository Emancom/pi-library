# Editorial Product Information: Givinostat

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial synthesis: United States (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised clinical overview of givinostat, intended for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises available regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This editorial is a professional summary and does not replace the official, legally binding Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Givinostat (as givinostat hydrochloride monohydrate).
- **Chemical Class:** Histone deacetylase (HDAC) inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** **Duvyzat** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Oral suspension containing 8.86 mg/mL of givinostat (equivalent to 10 mg/mL of givinostat hydrochloride monohydrate) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Italfarmaco S.A. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy (DMD):** Indicated for the treatment of DMD in patients aged 6 years and older [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 Recommended Evaluation
Before commencing treatment, clinicians must obtain baseline platelet counts and fasting triglyceride levels. Initiation is contraindicated if the platelet count is below 150 x 10⁹/L [US]. In patients with pre-existing cardiac disease or those taking QTc-prolonging medications, an electrocardiogram (ECG) should be performed at baseline and periodically during treatment [US].

### 3.2 Standard Dosing (Pediatric and Adult)
Dosing is weight-based and administered twice daily with food [US].

| Patient Weight | Twice-Daily Dose (mg) | Twice-Daily Volume (mL) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 10 kg to < 20 kg | 22.2 mg | 2.5 mL |
| 20 kg to < 40 kg | 31 mg | 3.5 mL |
| 40 kg to < 60 kg | 44.3 mg | 5 mL |
| 60 kg or more | 53.2 mg | 6 mL |

### 3.3 Administration Instructions
- The suspension must be shaken for at least 30 seconds by inverting the bottle 180° before each use [US].
- Measurement should be performed using the provided graduated oral syringe.
- The product must be taken with food [US].

### 3.4 Dosage Modifications
Dose reductions or interruptions may be required for the following [US]:
- **Thrombocytopenia:** If platelet count drops below 150 x 10⁹/L (confirmed by two assessments one week apart).
- **Hypertriglyceridemia:** If fasting triglycerides exceed 300 mg/dL (confirmed by two assessments one week apart).
- **Diarrhea:** In cases of moderate or severe symptoms.
- **QTc Prolongation:** Withhold treatment if QTc interval exceeds 500 ms or increases by >60 ms from baseline.

### 3.5 Missed Dose
If a dose is missed, patients should skip the missed dose and resume the regular schedule. Do not double the dose to compensate for a missed one [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- No contraindications are listed in the available source documentation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hematological Changes:** Givinostat causes dose-related myelosuppression, most notably thrombocytopenia (33% of trial participants), but also anemia and neutropenia. Monitoring is required every 2 weeks for the first 2 months, then monthly at month 3, and every 3 months thereafter [US].
- **Increased Triglycerides:** Significant elevations in fasting triglycerides can occur. Monitoring is required at 1, 3, and 6 months post-initiation, followed by 6-monthly checks. Persistent elevation despite dietary intervention and dose adjustment may require discontinuation [US].
- **Gastrointestinal Disturbances:** Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, and abdominal pain are frequent. Diarrhea usually appears within the first few weeks. Severe cases may require antiemetics, antidiarrheals, or treatment discontinuation if symptoms persist [US].
- **QTc Prolongation:** Givinostat can prolong the QTc interval. Avoid use in patients with a high risk of ventricular arrhythmias (e.g., congenital long QT syndrome, coronary artery disease, or electrolyte imbalances) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### 6.1 Frequency Data
Based on the pivotal 18-month clinical trial, the most common adverse reactions (≥10% and greater than placebo) include [US]:

| Adverse Reaction | Frequency (%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Diarrhea** | 37% |
| **Abdominal pain** | 34% |
| **Thrombocytopenia** | 33% |
| **Nausea/Vomiting** | 32% |
| **Hypertriglyceridemia** | 23% |
| **Pyrexia** | 13% |

### 6.2 Other Adverse Effects
- **Common (1–10%):** Myalgia, rash, arthralgia, fatigue, constipation, decreased appetite, and hypothyroidism (or increased TSH) [US].
- **Serious Effects:** Serious cases of abdominal pain and severe diarrhea have been reported. Potential for serious arrhythmias due to QTc prolongation [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Substrates:** Givinostat is a weak intestinal CYP3A4 inhibitor. Monitor patients closely when co-administered with sensitive oral CYP3A4 substrates where small concentration changes cause serious toxicity [US].
- **OCT2 Transporters:** Givinostat weakly inhibits the renal transporter OCT2. Closely monitor sensitive OCT2 substrates (e.g., metformin) [US].
- **QTc-Prolonging Drugs:** Avoid concomitant use with other drugs known to prolong the QTc interval. If unavoidable, perform regular ECG monitoring [US].
- **P-gp and BCRP:** While in vitro studies suggest substrate/inhibitor potential, these are not expected to be clinically meaningful [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: No specific category letter; safety not established in humans]. Animal studies indicate givinostat may cause fetal harm, including decreased fetal body weight, skeletal/visceral variations, and increased embryofetal mortality. Use is generally not expected in the primary DMD population (young males), but caution is advised regarding reproductive potential [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on the presence of givinostat in human milk or its effects on milk production or the breastfed infant. The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need and potential risks [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal data showed no adverse effects on fertility in rats, though increased pre- and post-implantation losses were observed [US].

## 9. Use in Specific Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy are established in patients 6 years and older. Use in patients under 6 years has not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical experience is limited as DMD is primarily a disease of children and young adults [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not formally studied; however, as renal excretion is a minor pathway (<3% of dose), no dose adjustment is expected to be necessary [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not formally studied. Givinostat is primarily metabolised by the liver; therefore, increased exposure is expected in hepatic impairment. No specific dosing recommendations can be made [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Givinostat inhibits histone deacetylase (HDAC). In DMD, the precise mechanism of action remains unknown, but HDAC inhibition is thought to modulate downstream pathogenetic pathways [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Treatment has been shown to result in a smaller increase in muscle fat fraction compared to placebo, as assessed by MR spectroscopy [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Tmax is 2 to 3 hours. A high-fat meal increases exposure (AUC) by approximately 40% [US].
    - **Distribution:** Approximately 96% protein-bound [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised by the liver via pathways other than CYP450 or UGT [US].
    - **Elimination:** Plasma half-life is approximately 6 hours. Excretion is primarily biliary/fecal following hepatic metabolism; urinary excretion is <3% [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
The efficacy of givinostat was demonstrated in **Study 1** (NCT02851797), a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 18-month trial in 179 ambulant male patients with DMD on stable corticosteroids. The primary endpoint was the change from baseline in the 4-stair climb (4SC) time. At 18 months, patients receiving givinostat showed a statistically significant smaller functional decline compared to the placebo group (treatment difference -1.78 seconds; p=0.037) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Do not freeze. Store the bottle upright [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any remaining suspension 60 days after the initial opening of the bottle [US].
