# Editorial Product Information: Glimepiride

## Editorial Note
- This editorial document has been developed using the official Product Information (PI) source from:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: APO-Glimepiride (Apotex/Aspen Pharma Pty Ltd)
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of glimepiride for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved PI for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Glimepiride
- **Chemical Class:** Sulfonylurea (second or third generation)
- **Brand Names:** APO-Glimepiride [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** A10BB12
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Oral tablets: 1 mg, 2 mg, 3 mg, and 4 mg [Australian]
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aspen Pharma Pty Ltd (Sponsor), Apotex Pty Ltd (Distributor) [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus:** As an adjunct to diet, exercise, and weight loss to improve glycaemic control in patients with non-insulin-dependent (Type 2) diabetes mellitus [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The dosage must be the lowest effective dose required to achieve metabolic control. The usual maintenance range is 1 mg to 4 mg once daily. Doses exceeding 4 mg/day rarely provide additional benefit, though exceptional cases may require higher dosing [Australian].
- **Initial Dose:** 1 mg once daily [Australian].
- **Titration:** If control is inadequate after 1–2 weeks of treatment, the dose may be increased in 1 mg increments at 1–2 week intervals [Australian].
- **Administration:** Tablets should be swallowed whole with liquid immediately before or during a substantial breakfast (or the first main meal of the day if breakfast is light/skipped). It is critical not to skip meals after taking the medication [Australian].
- **Changeover from other Antidiabetics:** There is no exact dose conversion. Start with 1 mg once daily and titrate based on response. A drug-free transition period may be necessary if moving from agents with long half-lives to avoid overlapping effects [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Errors in dosing (e.g., forgetting a dose) must never be corrected by taking a larger dose subsequently [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to glimepiride, other sulfonylureas, sulfonamides, or any tablet excipients [Australian].
- **Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus:** Not suitable for insulin-dependent diabetes [Australian].
- **Acute Complications:** Diabetic ketoacidosis, pre-coma, or coma [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Severe impairment (CrCl < 30 mL/min) and patients undergoing dialysis [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Severe hepatic dysfunction [Australian].
- **Pregnancy and Lactation:** Contraindicated (refer to Section 8) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypoglycaemia:** All sulfonylureas carry a risk of severe, prolonged hypoglycaemia. Risk is higher in the first month of treatment, upon dose escalation, or in patients with caloric deficiency, heavy exercise, or alcohol use [Australian].
- **G6PD Deficiency:** Use with caution in patients with glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase deficiency; sulfonylureas can induce haemolytic anaemia. Consider non-sulfonylurea alternatives [Australian].
- **Secondary Failure:** Glycaemic control may diminish over time due to disease progression or reduced drug responsiveness [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Regular monitoring of fasting blood glucose, urine glucose, and glycosylated haemoglobin (HbA1c) is mandatory [Australian].
- **Alertness/Driving:** Alertness may be impaired due to hypo- or hyperglycaemia, particularly during treatment initiation or dose adjustment [Australian].
- **Stress Situations:** During trauma, surgery, or febrile infections, it may be necessary to temporarily switch to insulin to maintain control [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | System Organ Class / Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Hypoglycaemia (symptoms include headache, ravenous hunger, nausea, dizziness, tremor) [Australian]. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Gastrointestinal: Nausea, vomiting, sensations of pressure/fullness in the epigastrium, abdominal pain, diarrhoea [Australian]. |
| **Rare (< 0.1%)** | Haematologic: Thrombocytopaenia, leucopaenia, haemolytic anaemia, granulocytopaenia, agranulocytosis, pancytopaenia [Australian]. |
| **Isolated/Post-marketing** | Hepatic: Increased liver enzymes, cholestasis, jaundice, hepatitis, liver failure. Dermatologic: Allergic/pseudo-allergic reactions (urticaria, rashes, photosensitivity), allergic vasculitis. Electrolyte: Hyponatraemia [Australian]. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Severe hypoglycaemia (mimicking stroke), life-threatening liver failure, and severe haematologic cytopaenias [Australian].
- **Visual Effects:** Transient visual impairment may occur at the start of treatment due to changes in blood glucose levels affecting lens turgidity [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Glimepiride is metabolised by CYP2C9. Its metabolism may be affected by inducers (e.g., rifampicin) or inhibitors (e.g., fluconazole, amiodarone) of this enzyme [Australian].
- **Potentiation of Hypoglycaemic Effect:**
    - Insulin and other oral antidiabetics, ACE inhibitors, anabolic steroids, fibrates, fluconazole, MAO inhibitors, salicylates, sulfonamides, and tetracyclines [Australian].
- **Attenuation of Hypoglycaemic Effect:**
    - Corticosteroids, diuretics (thiazides), adrenaline (epinephrine), nicotinic acid (high doses), oestrogens, progestogens, phenytoin, rifampicin, and thyroid hormones [Australian].
- **Masking of Symptoms:** Beta-blockers, clonidine, and guanethidine may mask the adrenergic symptoms of hypoglycaemia [Australian].
- **Alcohol:** Acute or chronic intake may either potentiate or weaken the effect unpredictably [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category C [Australian]:** Sulfonylureas may enter foetal circulation and cause neonatal hypoglycaemia. Animal studies have shown foetotoxicity (limb deformations, resorptions). Glimepiride must not be taken during pregnancy; patients should switch to insulin [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Glimepiride is excreted in milk [Australian]. Due to the risk of hypoglycaemia in the infant, breastfeeding is contraindicated; the mother must either switch to insulin or cease breastfeeding [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no recognisable effects on fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children have not been established; use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Pharmacokinetics are similar to younger adults, but hypoglycaemia may be more difficult to recognise in this population [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - Mild to moderate (CrCl 30–50 mL/min): Initial dose must not exceed 1 mg once daily [Australian].
    - Severe (CrCl < 30 mL/min): Contraindicated [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - No experience in severe hepatic impairment; such patients should be transitioned to insulin [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Primarily stimulates insulin release from functioning pancreatic beta cells by improving their sensitivity to physiological glucose stimulus. It also exhibits extrapancreatic effects, including increased peripheral insulin sensitivity and reduced hepatic glucose production [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** 100% bioavailable after oral administration; Cmax reached in ~2.5 hours. Food does not significantly affect absorption [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (>99%); volume of distribution ~8.8 L [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Completely metabolised via oxidative biotransformation (CYP2C9) to metabolites M1 (40% active) and M2 (inactive) [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 58% in urine and 35% in faeces. Terminal half-life is 5–8 hours at steady state [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Dose-Response Studies:** Multicentre trials (n≈1,900) demonstrated that most patients achieve significant FPG reduction at 1 mg/day, with additional benefit for some at 2–4 mg/day. No significant difference in FPG reduction was noted between 4 mg and 8 mg doses [Australian].
- **Dosing Frequency:** A double-blind crossover study suggested that while twice-daily dosing may improve results for a subset of patients, once-daily dosing provides adequate control for the majority [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Not specified in available sources (refer to packaging).

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** S4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only.
