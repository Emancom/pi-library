# Editorial Product Information: Galantamine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of galantamine for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI used for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labelling for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Galantamine (as hydrobromide).
- **Chemical Class:** Tertiary alkaloid; cholinomimetic (reversible acetylcholinesterase inhibitor).
- **Brand Names:** APO-Galantamine MR [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N06DA04.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Modified-release (MR) hard gelatin capsules: 8 mg, 16 mg, and 24 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Alzheimer’s Disease:** Treatment of mild to moderately severe dementia of the Alzheimer type [Australian].
- **Note on Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI):** Galantamine is NOT indicated for individuals with MCI (isolated memory impairment not meeting Alzheimer's criteria) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults/Elderly)
- **Administration:** Once daily in the morning, preferably with food.
- **Starting Dose:** 8 mg daily for four weeks.
- **Initial Maintenance Dose:** 16 mg daily for at least four weeks.
- **Maximum Maintenance Dose:** 24 mg daily, considered after clinical evaluation of benefit and tolerability.
- **Titration:** Gradual dose escalation is required to minimise cholinergic side effects.

### Administration Instructions
- Capsules must be swallowed whole and not crushed or sprinkled on food.
- Ensure adequate fluid intake during treatment.

### Re-initiation of Therapy
- If treatment is interrupted for longer than several days, therapy should be re-initiated at the lowest daily dose and titrated gradually to the previous maintenance dose.

### Discontinuation
- No rebound effect is observed following abrupt discontinuation [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to galantamine hydrobromide or any excipients.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Severe impairment (Child-Pugh score > 9) [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Severe impairment (Creatinine clearance < 9 mL/min) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiovascular Conditions:** Use with caution in patients with "sick sinus syndrome," supraventricular conduction disturbances, or those on concomitant bradycardic agents (e.g., digoxin, beta-blockers). Potential for vagotonic effects, bradycardia, and AV block.
- **QT Prolongation:** Reports of QT prolongation at therapeutic doses and Torsades de pointes with overdose. Caution in patients with pre-existing cardiac disease or electrolyte disturbances.
- **Gastrointestinal Risks:** Potential increase in gastric acid secretion. Monitor for GI bleeding, especially in those with a history of peptic ulcers or those taking NSAIDs. Not recommended in patients with GI obstruction or recovering from GI surgery.
- **Neurological Conditions:** Cholinomimetics may lower the seizure threshold. Cholinergic stimulation may also worsen Parkinsonian symptoms.
- **Pulmonary Conditions:** Prescribe with caution in patients with severe asthma, obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), or active pneumonia.
- **Genitourinary:** Not recommended in patients with urinary outflow obstruction or recovering from bladder surgery.
- **Serious Skin Reactions:** Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) and acute generalised exanthematous pustulosis (AGEP) have been reported. Discontinue at the first sign of a skin rash.
- **Weight Monitoring:** Monitoring is required as cholinesterase inhibitors are associated with weight loss in Alzheimer's patients.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Cholinomimetics:** Avoid concomitant use (e.g., bethanechol, donepezil).
- **Anticholinergics:** Galantamine antagonises the effect of anticholinergic medications.
- **Bradycardic Agents:** Increased risk of bradycardia with beta-blockers and digoxin.
- **Anaesthesia:** Likely to exaggerate succinylcholine-type muscle relaxation during surgery.

### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP2D6/CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Potent inhibitors increase galantamine bioavailability.
    - **Paroxetine (CYP2D6):** Increases bioavailability by ~40%.
    - **Ketoconazole (CYP3A4):** Increases bioavailability by ~30%.
    - **Erythromycin (CYP3A4):** Increases bioavailability by ~12%.
- **Management:** A dose reduction of galantamine may be considered when initiating potent inhibitors of CYP2D6 (e.g., quinidine, fluoxetine, fluvoxamine) or CYP3A4.
- **Memantine:** No clinically significant effect on galantamine pharmacokinetics.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category B1. No clinical data in pregnant women. Use only if potential benefit outweighs risk to the foetus. Animal studies showed minor skeletal abnormalities at high doses.
- **Breastfeeding:** Excretion into breast milk is unknown. Women taking galantamine should not breastfeed.
- **Fertility:** No effect on rat fertility at 5x human exposure.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended; no data available.
- **Elderly Use:** Pharmacokinetics in Alzheimer's patients are 30-40% higher than in healthy young subjects, but dosing follows the standard titration.
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - Moderate (CrCl 9–51 mL/min): No dose adjustment required.
    - Severe (CrCl < 9 mL/min): Contraindicated [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - Mild: No adjustment needed.
    - Moderate (Child-Pugh 7–9): Start 8 mg every other day for 1 week (morning), then 8 mg once daily for 4 weeks. Total daily dose must not exceed 16 mg.
    - Severe (Child-Pugh > 9): Contraindicated [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    1. **Acetylcholinesterase Inhibition:** Selective, competitive, and reversible inhibition of the enzyme, increasing available acetylcholine.
    2. **Nicotinic Modulation:** Enhances the intrinsic action of acetylcholine on nicotinic receptors via allosteric binding.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid; absolute bioavailability ~88.5%. Modified-release Tmax is ~4.4 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Low plasma protein binding (17.7%).
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP2D6 and CYP3A4; major pathways include N-oxidation and O-demethylation.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life of 7–8 hours. Approximately 90–97% of a dose is recovered in urine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** GAL-INT-1, GAL-USA-1, and GAL-USA-10 involved over 2,500 patients. 
- **Outcomes:** Galantamine demonstrated statistically significant improvements in cognition (ADAS-cog), global clinical response (CIBIC-plus), and activities of daily living (ADCS-ADL/DAD) compared to placebo.
- **Modified-Release (MR):** Shown to be bioequivalent to immediate-release (IR) formulations in terms of AUC and Cmin, with a 24% lower Cmax.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Expiry date is located on the packaging.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
