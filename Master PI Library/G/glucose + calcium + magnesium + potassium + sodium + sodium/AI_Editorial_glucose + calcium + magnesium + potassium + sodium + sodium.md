# Editorial Product Information: glucose + calcium + magnesium + potassium + sodium + sodium

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **glucose + calcium + magnesium + potassium + sodium + sodium**
- Chemical class: Bicarbonate‑buffered balanced electrolyte replacement fluid (crystalloid) containing glucose and physiological electrolytes
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intravenous (infused as a replacement fluid in continuous renal replacement therapy)

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Intravenous replacement fluid for continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT) in intensive care for treatment of acute kidney injury.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- CRRT dosing is individualized by the prescribing physician according to clinical status, body weight and the CRRT system used.
- Effluent volume guidance (used as a surrogate for delivered dose):
  - Typical effluent volume: 20–35 mL/kg/h (intended to achieve a delivered dose of approximately 20–25 mL/kg/h)
  - Maximum effluent volume: 75 L/day
- Maximum intended period of use for a single CRRT line: 72 hours.

### Product strengths / composition options (ready‑to‑use mixed solution; per 1000 mL)
- Formulation variants differ by potassium and glucose content:
  - Potassium-free variant (K0)
    - Glucose: 0 mmol (no glucose)
    - Calcium (Ca2+): 1.75 mmol/L
    - Magnesium (Mg2+): 0.50 mmol/L
    - Sodium (Na+): 140 mmol/L
    - Potassium (K+): 0 mmol/L
    - Bicarbonate (HCO3−): 35 mmol/L
    - Chloride (Cl−): 109.3 mmol/L
    - Theoretical osmolarity: ~287 mOsm/L
  - Potassium 2 mmol/L variant (K2)
    - Glucose: 5.55 mmol/L (~1.0 g/L)
    - Calcium: 1.75 mmol/L
    - Magnesium: 0.50 mmol/L
    - Sodium: 140 mmol/L
    - Potassium: 2.0 mmol/L
    - Bicarbonate: 35 mmol/L
    - Chloride: 111.3 mmol/L
    - Theoretical osmolarity: ~296 mOsm/L
  - Potassium 4 mmol/L variant (K4)
    - Glucose: 5.55 mmol/L (~1.0 g/L)
    - Calcium: 1.75 mmol/L
    - Magnesium: 0.50 mmol/L
    - Sodium: 140 mmol/L
    - Potassium: 4.0 mmol/L
    - Bicarbonate: 35 mmol/L
    - Chloride: 113.3 mmol/L
    - Theoretical osmolarity: ~300 mOsm/L
- pH of the final ready-to-use solution: approximately 7.0–7.5.

### Administration instructions
- Only for use by physicians experienced in CRRT and in units with appropriate equipment and trained personnel.
- The product is supplied as separated electrolyte and bicarbonate components that must be mixed immediately prior to use; do not use before mixing both chambers.
- Additives (concomitant medications) may be introduced via the medication port in the larger electrolyte chamber prior to opening the inter‑chamber seal; compatibility must be checked before admixture.
- After mixing, visually inspect the solution for clarity, absence of particulate matter, precipitation or color change and check for leaks before connection.
- Use aseptic technique throughout preparation and administration.
- Solutions (including any additives) should be used immediately; replacement fluids without additives should be used shortly after mixing and within a maximum of 24 hours after mixing.
- Warm solution to approximately 36.5–38.0 °C immediately prior to infusion (exact temperature determined by clinical requirements and equipment). Do not infuse below room temperature.
- Single‑patient single‑use only. Do not reconnect or re‑use partially used containers; discard any unused residue.

### Titration schedules
- Titration is guided by monitoring of clinical status, fluid balance, electrolytes and acid‑base parameters; specific titration schedules are not specified in available sources.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Metabolic alkalosis (contraindicated).
- Potassium‑strength specific contraindications:
  - Potassium‑free (K0) and Potassium 2 mmol/L (K2): Contraindicated in hypokalaemia if appropriate potassium supplementation is not provided.
  - Potassium 4 mmol/L (K4): Contraindicated in hyperkalaemia if appropriate potassium management is not provided.
- CRRT‑technique dependent contraindications:
  - Renal failure with pronounced protein catabolism where uremic symptoms cannot be corrected by CRRT.
  - Inadequate blood flow or insufficient arterial pressure from vascular access.
  - Any clinical states with high risk of haemorrhage such as when systemic anticoagulation produces unacceptable bleeding risk.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- None specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Use only in appropriately equipped intensive care units by trained personnel.
- Do not infuse solutions that have not been mixed; inadvertent administration of the bicarbonate chamber alone may cause metabolic alkalosis.
- Ensure complete opening of the inter‑chamber seal and thorough mixing before administration; if the inter‑chamber seal is not opened and only the bicarbonate chamber is infused, metabolic alkalosis can occur.
- Rarely, calcium carbonate precipitation may develop several hours after therapy initiation; inspect lines and solution during use.
- Warm solution prior to infusion; do not infuse at temperatures below room temperature.
- Single‑use only; discard any damaged bags or containers.

### Monitoring requirements
- Continuous and careful monitoring of fluid balance (intake, ultrafiltration, urine output), body weight and haemodynamic status to detect hyper‑ or hypovolaemia early.
- Regular monitoring of serum electrolytes: sodium, potassium, chloride, bicarbonate, calcium, magnesium, phosphate.
- Regular monitoring of acid‑base status (arterial or venous blood gas as clinically appropriate).
- Regular monitoring of blood glucose, particularly when using the potassium‑free variant (K0) that contains no glucose, and in diabetic patients.
- Monitor for signs of precipitation/particles in solution or tubing; if present replace fluid and CRRT tubing immediately and monitor the patient.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Adjust potassium supplementation or choose appropriate K‑strength solution based on patient potassium status and trends during therapy.
- If hypokalaemia is present or develops, consider potassium supplementation and/or use of K2 or K4 formulation.
- If hyperkalaemia occurs, consider increased filtration rate and/or use of lower potassium strength (K2 or K0).
- Be aware that CRRT may reduce blood concentrations of drugs that are low protein‑bound, have small volume of distribution, low molecular weight relative to the haemofilter cut‑off, or are adsorbed by the haemofilter; dosage review may be required.
- Exercise caution in patients receiving cardiac glycosides—monitor potassium closely because hypokalaemia increases risk of arrhythmias.
- Concomitant vitamin D, vitamin D analogues or calcium‑containing medicines may increase risk of hypercalcaemia.
- Additional bicarbonate from other sources may increase risk of metabolic alkalosis.

### Serious adverse effects (highlighted)
- Air embolism (serious, procedure‑related; associated with precipitate)
- Severe metabolic alkalosis with associated symptoms (nausea, lethargy, headache, arrhythmia, respiratory depression)
- Severe fluid overload leading to congestive cardiac failure or pulmonary congestion if fluid balance not monitored
- Severe electrolyte disturbances (e.g., marked hypo/hyperkalaemia, hypocalcaemia with tetany) leading to cardiac arrhythmia or neuromuscular symptoms

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- CRRT with this replacement fluid may reduce circulating concentrations of drugs that are:
  - Low protein bound
  - Small volume of distribution
  - Of molecular weight below the haemofilter cut‑off
  - Prone to adsorption on the haemofilter
- Dose revision of affected drugs may be required while on CRRT.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Concomitant electrolyte‑containing infusions or parenteral nutrition can influence serum electrolyte and fluid status; consider when prescribing replacement fluid.
- Concomitant vitamin D, vitamin D analogues or calcium‑containing medicines increase risk of hypercalcaemia.
- Additional sources of bicarbonate/buffer may increase the risk of metabolic alkalosis.
- Potassium‑modifying therapies (supplementation, diuretics, potassium‑sparing drugs, etc.) interact with potassium content of replacement fluid; monitor potassium and adjust therapy accordingly.
- Cardiac glycosides: hypokalaemia increases risk of glycoside‑related arrhythmias — monitor potassium closely.

### Clinically significant interactions with management notes
- Monitor drug levels and clinical effect for medications likely to be cleared or adsorbed by CRRT; adjust dosing as needed.
- Coordinate electrolyte and bicarbonate sources (including concomitant IV fluids and nutrition) to avoid additive effects.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- The solution supplies sodium, potassium (depending on formulation), calcium, magnesium, chloride and bicarbonate to replace ultrafiltrate losses and to assist in correction of fluid, electrolyte and acid‑base disturbances in acute renal failure managed with CRRT.
- Bicarbonate buffering increases blood buffer capacity and corrects metabolic acidosis commonly seen with acute renal injury.
- Glucose (present in some formulations) supplies metabolic substrate and influences extracellular osmolarity and glycaemic management.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: Intravenous administration → bioavailability effectively 100%.
- Distribution: Distribution of water and electrolytes is determined by osmotic gradients and cellular requirements; glucose distributes into extracellular space and is taken up into cells via insulin‑mediated mechanisms.
- Metabolism: Electrolytes are not metabolized; glucose is metabolized by normal metabolic pathways.
- Elimination: Elimination of water and electrolytes depends on residual renal function, cellular requirements and other routes of fluid losses (gut, lungs, skin).
- Special populations: No specific pharmacokinetic studies; distribution and elimination depend on patient clinical status and residual renal function.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Rapid correction or modification of intravascular and extracellular electrolyte and bicarbonate concentrations according to infused volume and composition; CRRT technique determines net removal of solutes and fluid.

### Preclinical safety
- Genotoxicity and carcinogenicity studies were not performed; given the physiological nature of components, genotoxic and carcinogenic hazards are not considered likely.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Small clinical experience described: a trial of 16 patients requiring high‑volume CRRT for severe acute renal injury reported hypoglycaemia as an adverse event (classified as rare). The trial detail is limited in the available sources; no larger pivotal outcome trials are described.
- General statement: Use of bicarbonate‑buffered balanced replacement fluids in CRRT is an established clinical practice in management of acute kidney injury.
