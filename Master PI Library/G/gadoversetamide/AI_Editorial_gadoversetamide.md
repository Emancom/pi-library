# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** for use by healthcare professionals.
- This document is an editorial synthesis and must not be used as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information when prescribing or administering this medication in a particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It may be administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
    - **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose is often given, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered if clinically required [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - **Titration:** Dose adjustments (e.g., downward to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- None specified in available sources.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of IV administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks/benefits before re-administration [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** While this concerns the oral formulation more specifically, it is noted that chronic high doses in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Plasma Carnitine Levels:** It is recommended to obtain a baseline level prior to therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring should follow, including blood chemistries and vital signs. The target plasma free carnitine concentration is 35 to 60 µmol/L [US].
- **INR Monitoring:** Increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) has been reported when used concomitantly with warfarin. Monitor INR after initiation or dose adjustment [US].

- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting, gastritis, and body odor have been observed [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in INR when used with levocarnitine. Significant clinical management requires frequent INR monitoring in patients on anticoagulant therapy during levocarnitine initiation or titration [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified by letter, but reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm]. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. Risk to the child vs. benefit to the mother should be weighed; consideration may be given to discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, etc.) were negative. Long-term animal studies for carcinogenic potential have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use is indicated for inborn errors of metabolism; dosing is consistent with adult weight-based recommendations (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** In ESRD patients on dialysis, carnitine is lost via dialysate. IV levocarnitine is used to replace these losses. High-dose *oral* levocarnitine is cautioned against in severe renal impairment due to metabolite (TMA/TMAO) accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US]. 
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with metabolic errors involving organic acidemias, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters which are rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis (trough) carnitine levels from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. While levocarnitine effectively increases plasma concentrations, the direct impact on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, cardiomyopathy) in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial; the formulation contains no preservative [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription only medication) [US].
