# Editorial Product Information: Gadoxetate Disodium

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a single clinically relevant reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or prescribing information required for clinical practice in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved PI for the most current legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Gadoxetate disodium (a paramagnetic gadolinium-based contrast agent).
- **Chemical Class:** Gadolinium complex; (4S)-4-(4-Ethoxybenzyl)-3,6,9-tris(carboxylatomethyl)-3,6,9-triazaundecanedioic acid, disodium salt.
- **Brand Names:** Eovist [US].
- **ATC Code:** V08CA10.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injection (solution). Supplied at a concentration of 0.25 mmol/mL (181.43 mg/mL). Available in 10 mL (2.5 mmol) and 15 mL (3.75 mmol) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Bayer HealthCare Pharmaceuticals Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Liver Imaging:** Indicated for use in magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the liver to detect and characterise lesions in adult and paediatric patients, including term neonates, with known or suspected focal liver disease [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric):** The recommended dose is 0.025 mmol/kg of actual body weight [US].
- **Volume Equivalency:** This dose is equivalent to 0.1 mL/kg of the 0.25 mmol/mL solution [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Administer via intravenous injection at a rate of approximately 1 mL/sec to 2 mL/sec [US].
    - Follow the injection with a 0.9% sodium chloride flush [US].
    - Inspect the solution visually; it should be clear and colourless to pale yellow. Do not use if discolored or if particulate matter is present [US].
    - Vials are for single use; discard any unused portion [US].
- **Imaging Protocol:**
    - **Dynamic Phase:** Begin imaging approximately 15–25 seconds post-injection (arterial phase), followed by porto-venous (~60 seconds) and blood equilibrium (~120 seconds) phases [US].
    - **Hepatocyte Phase:** Begin imaging approximately 20 minutes post-injection. This phase may be performed up to 120 minutes post-injection [US].
    - **Reduced Hepatic Contrast:** In patients with elevated bilirubin (>3 mg/dL) or ferritin, hepatic contrast may be reduced. In these cases, hepatocyte imaging should be performed no later than 60 minutes post-administration [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** History of severe hypersensitivity reaction to gadoxetate disodium [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Intrathecal Use:** Intrathecal administration of gadolinium-based contrast agents (GBCAs) can cause serious adverse reactions, including death, coma, seizures, and encephalopathy. Gadoxetate disodium is NOT approved for intrathecal use [US].
- **Boxed Warning: Nephrogenic Systemic Fibrosis (NSF):** GBCAs increase the risk of NSF in patients with impaired elimination. Avoid use in patients with chronic, severe kidney disease (GFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m²) or acute kidney injury unless diagnostic information is essential and unavailable via other modalities. NSF can result in fatal or debilitating fibrosis of the skin, muscle, and internal organs [US].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Anaphylactic and anaphylactoid reactions, including shock, may occur. Patients should be monitored during and for at least 30 minutes following administration. Ensure trained personnel and resuscitation equipment are immediately available [US].
- **Gadolinium Retention:** Gadolinium is retained for months or years in the brain, bone, skin, and other organs. Retention is higher with linear agents (like gadoxetate disodium) than with macrocyclic agents. Clinical consequences are currently unestablished, but patients requiring multiple lifetime doses, pregnant patients, and children may be at higher risk [US].
- **Acute Kidney Injury:** In patients with pre-existing renal impairment, some GBCAs have been associated with acute kidney injury requiring dialysis [US].
- **Laboratory Interference:** Gadoxetate disodium can interfere with serum iron determination using complexometric methods (e.g., ferrocene complexation) for up to 24 hours. Iron tests should be conducted before or at least 24 hours after administration [US].
- **Extravasation:** May cause local tissue reactions, including inflammation and myocyte necrosis. Ensure venous patency before injection [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (1–10%):** Nausea (1.1%), headache (1.1%) [US].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Feeling hot (0.8%), dizziness (0.6%), back pain (0.6%), vomiting (0.4%), increased blood pressure (0.4%), injection site reactions (0.4%), dysgeusia (0.4%), paresthesia (0.3%), flushing (0.3%), parosmia (0.3%), pruritus (0.3%), and rash (0.3%) [US].
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Tremor, akathisia, bundle branch block, palpitation, oral discomfort, and hypersensitivity reactions [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:**
    - Nephrogenic Systemic Fibrosis (NSF) [US].
    - Anaphylactic shock [US].
    - Acute pancreatitis (postmarketing report within 48 hours of GBCA administration) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Serum Iron Tests:** Interference with complexometric iron assays occurs for 24 hours post-dose [US].
- **Other Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Risk Summary:** GBCAs cross the placenta and result in fetal exposure. Animal studies showed increased preimplantation loss in rats and post-implantation loss in rabbits at doses significantly higher than the human dose.
    - **Clinical Recommendation:** Use only if imaging is essential and cannot be delayed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:**
    - **Safety Information:** Data for other GBCAs show 0.01% to 0.04% of the maternal dose is present in breast milk.
    - **Recommendation:** A lactating woman may consider interrupting breastfeeding and discarding milk for 10 hours post-administration to minimise exposure to the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats showed no effect on fertility or reproductive performance at 6.5 times the human dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness established in paediatric patients, including term neonates. Safety/efficacy NOT established in preterm neonates [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness observed compared to younger patients; however, caution is advised due to the higher frequency of decreased renal or hepatic function [US].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - Risk of NSF is highest in severe impairment (GFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m²).
    - Exposure (AUC) is increased 6-fold in end-stage renal failure.
    - Gadoxetate disodium can be removed by haemodialysis (approx. 30% removed in one 3-hour session) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - No dose adjustment necessary [US].
    - In severe impairment, AUC may increase by 60% and elimination half-life by 49%. Imaging performance may be reduced [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Gadoxetate disodium is a paramagnetic molecule that develops a magnetic moment in a magnetic field, shortening the T1 and T2 relaxation times of water protons in its vicinity. This increases signal intensity (brightness) in the liver because it is selectively taken up by functioning hepatocytes [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Biphasic—initial distribution in extracellular space followed by selective uptake by hepatocytes via the OATP1B1/3 transporters. Protein binding is <10% [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Not metabolised [US].
    - **Elimination:** Equally eliminated via renal and hepatobiliary routes (50% each). Terminal half-life is approximately 0.9 to 1 hour [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** Effectiveness was evaluated in four non-randomised, intra-patient controlled studies (Studies 1–4).
- **Detection (Studies 1 & 2):** Evaluated patients scheduled for liver surgery using histopathology as a reference. Combined pre-contrast and gadoxetate-contrasted MRI showed improved sensitivity for lesion detection compared to pre-contrast MRI alone [US].
- **Characterisation (Studies 3 & 4):** Evaluated the ability to correctly identify lesion types (e.g., HCC, metastasis, hemangioma). Combined imaging improved the proportion of correctly characterised lesions compared to pre-contrast MRI [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F and 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Single-dose container; discard unused portion.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** Not specified in available sources.
