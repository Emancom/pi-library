# Editorial Product Information: Gefitinib

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of gefitinib for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, regulatory requirements, and safety information into a concise reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource only. It is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information or Summary of Product Characteristics required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Gefitinib (a selective epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) tyrosine kinase inhibitor).
- **Chemical Class:** Quinazoline derivative; N-(3-chloro-4-fluorophenyl)-7-methoxy-6-(3-morpholinopropoxy) quinazolin-4-amine.
- **Brand Names:** Cipla Gefitinib [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Film-coated tablet:** 250 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Cipla Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC):** Treatment of patients with locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC whose tumours express activating mutations of the EGFR tyrosine kinase [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Standard Adult Dose:** 250 mg (one tablet) once daily [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - May be taken with or without food [Australian].
    - **Dispersion Method:** For patients with swallowing difficulties, the tablet may be dispersed in half a glass of non-carbonated drinking water without crushing. Stir for approximately 15 minutes until dispersed and drink immediately. Rinse the glass with an additional half-glass of water and drink the contents.
    - **Nasogastric Tube:** The dispersion described above may also be administered via a nasogastric tube [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** 
    - If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as remembered. 
    - If it is less than 12 hours until the next scheduled dose, skip the missed dose. 
    - Do not take a double dose to make up for a forgotten dose [Australian].
- **Dose Adjustments:**
    - No adjustment is required based on age, body weight, gender, ethnicity, or renal function [Australian].
    - No adjustment is required for hepatic impairment due to liver metastases [Australian].
    - **Toxicity Management:** Poorly tolerated diarrhoea or skin reactions may be managed by a short therapy interruption (up to 14 days), followed by reinstatement of the 250 mg dose [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known severe hypersensitivity to gefitinib or any excipients in the formulation (e.g., lactose monohydrate) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **EGFR Mutation Status:** Activating mutation status should be established using robust, well-validated tests on tumour tissue or circulating tumour DNA (ctDNA) from plasma prior to initiating first-line therapy [Australian].
- **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD):** ILD-type events (some fatal) have been reported. If a patient presents with acute onset or worsening of respiratory symptoms (dyspnoea, cough, fever), gefitinib must be interrupted and investigated. If ILD is confirmed, gefitinib must be permanently discontinued [Australian]. 
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Liver function abnormalities (elevated ALT, AST, bilirubin) and isolated cases of fatal hepatic failure have occurred. Periodic liver function testing is recommended. Use with caution in patients with mild to moderate liver function changes; discontinue if changes are severe [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal Perforation:** Reported in patients taking gefitinib; often associated with risk factors such as concomitant steroids, NSAIDs, GI ulceration history, smoking, or bowel metastases [Australian].
- **Severe Cutaneous Reactions:** Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN) have been reported. Interrupt treatment for severe bullous or exfoliative conditions; discontinue if SJS or TEN is confirmed [Australian].
- **Ocular Effects:** Keratitis and corneal erosion (sometimes with aberrant eyelash growth) have been reported. Patients with signs of keratitis should be referred to an ophthalmologist. Interrupt or discontinue treatment for ulcerative keratitis [Australian].
- **QT Interval Prolongation:** While *in vitro* studies suggest potential inhibition of cardiac action potential repolarization, clinical trials have not demonstrated adverse cardiac effects [Australian].
- **Lactose Intolerance:** Tablets contain lactose monohydrate; not recommended for patients with rare hereditary galactose intolerance or glucose-galactose malabsorption [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse drug reactions (ADRs) typically occur within the first month of therapy and are generally reversible. Approximately 10% of patients experience Grade 3 or 4 ADRs [Australian].

| Frequency | Adverse Events |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (>10%) | Diarrhoea, vomiting, nausea, stomatitis, skin reactions (pustular rash, pruritus, dry skin, fissures), anorexia, elevations in ALT, asthenia. |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Keratitis, conjunctivitis, blepharitis, dry eye, haemorrhage (epistaxis, haematuria), interstitial lung disease, elevations in AST and total bilirubin, nail disorders, alopecia, allergic reactions (angioedema, urticaria), proteinuria, cystitis, pyrexia, increased creatinine. |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Pancreatitis, gastrointestinal perforation, hepatitis, corneal erosion, palmar-plantar erythrodysaesthesia syndrome. |
| **Rare** (<0.1%) | Bullous conditions (SJS, TEN, erythema multiforme), cutaneous vasculitis, haemorrhagic cystitis. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** ILD (potentially fatal), gastrointestinal perforation, and hepatic failure [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Co-administration with potent inhibitors (e.g., itraconazole) can increase gefitinib AUC by approximately 80%, potentially increasing toxicity [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Potent inducers (e.g., rifampicin, phenytoin, carbamazepine, St John’s Wort) can significantly reduce gefitinib plasma concentrations (rifampicin reduced AUC by 83%) and may decrease efficacy [Australian].
- **Gastric pH Modifiers:** Drugs that cause sustained elevation of gastric pH ≥5 (e.g., ranitidine, proton pump inhibitors) can reduce gefitinib AUC by nearly 50%, potentially reducing efficacy [Australian].
- **CYP2D6 Substrates:** Gefitinib can increase the plasma concentrations of drugs metabolised by CYP2D6 (e.g., metoprolol exposure increased by 35%) [Australian].
- **Warfarin:** Elevations in INR and/or bleeding events have been reported. Monitor prothrombin time or INR regularly [Australian].
- **Vinorelbine:** Gefitinib may exacerbate the neutropenic effect of vinorelbine [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category C** [Australian]: No data in pregnant women. Animal studies showed increased incomplete ossification in rats and reduced foetal weights in rabbits at maternally toxic doses. Women of childbearing potential should avoid pregnancy during treatment [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Mothers must be advised to discontinue breastfeeding during therapy. It is unknown if gefitinib is excreted in human milk, though it and its metabolites are excreted in rat milk [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal data indicate that gefitinib may affect ovulation and reduce the number of corpora lutea [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended; safety and effectiveness have not been established [Australian]. Haemorrhages (some fatal) were reported in small trials of paediatric patients with CNS gliomas [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available in provided sources [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment required. Monitor urea, electrolytes, and creatinine in patients at risk of dehydration due to diarrhoea or vomiting [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - Exposure is increased (average 3.1-fold) in patients with cirrhosis or hepatitis. These patients should be closely monitored for adverse events [Australian]. 
    - No adjustment is needed for impairment due to liver metastases [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selective inhibitor of the EGFR tyrosine kinase. It inhibits the growth of human tumour xenografts, inhibits angiogenesis, and increases apoptosis in cancer cell lines [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak plasma concentrations occur 3–7 hours after oral dosing. Mean bioavailability is 59%. Not significantly affected by food [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Extensive tissue distribution (Vdss 1400 L). 90% plasma protein bound [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP3A4. O-desmethyl gefitinib (formed via CYP2D6) is the major metabolite but is 14-fold less potent than the parent drug [Australian].
    - **Excretion:** Predominantly faecal; renal elimination accounts for <4% [Australian]. Elimination half-life is approximately 41 hours (oral) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **IPASS:** Demonstrated gefitinib's superiority over carboplatin/paclitaxel in progression-free survival (PFS) for first-line treatment of Asian patients with EGFR mutation-positive NSCLC [Australian].
- **INTEREST:** Demonstrated gefitinib's non-inferiority to docetaxel in terms of overall survival in pre-treated patients with locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC [Australian].
- **ISEL:** Compared gefitinib to placebo in pre-treated patients; showed higher response rates in EGFR mutation-positive tumours, though overall survival results were not statistically significant [Australian].
- **IFUM:** A single-arm study in Caucasian patients confirming that gefitinib activity (ORR 70%) is similar to that seen in Asian populations [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 30°C [Australian].
- Shelf life information is available on the public summary of the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG) [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4) [Australian].
