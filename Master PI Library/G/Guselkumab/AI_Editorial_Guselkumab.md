# Editorial Product Information: Guselkumab

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a structured format for quick reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Guselkumab, a fully human immunoglobulin G1 lambda (IgG1λ) monoclonal antibody.
- **Chemical Class:** Interleukin-23 (IL-23) inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** **TREMFYA** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L04AC16.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths [Australian]:**
    - **Subcutaneous (SC) Injection:**
        - Pre-filled Syringe: 100 mg/1 mL and 200 mg/2 mL.
        - Pre-filled Pen: 45 mg/0.45 mL, 100 mg/1 mL, and 200 mg/2 mL.
    - **Intravenous (IV) Infusion:**
        - Single-dose vial: 200 mg/20 mL (10 mg/mL) concentrated solution for infusion.
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous injection or Intravenous infusion (indication-specific).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Janssen-Cilag Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Plaque Psoriasis (Adult):** Treatment of moderate to severe plaque psoriasis in adults (≥18 years) who are candidates for systemic therapy or phototherapy [Australian].
- **Plaque Psoriasis (Paediatric):** Treatment of moderate to severe plaque psoriasis in paediatric patients (6 to 17 years) who are candidates for systemic therapy or phototherapy [Australian].
- **Psoriatic Arthritis:** Treatment of active psoriatic arthritis in adults who have had an inadequate response or intolerance to prior disease-modifying antirheumatic drug (DMARD) therapy [Australian].
- **Ulcerative Colitis:** Treatment of moderately to severely active ulcerative colitis in adults with inadequate response, loss of response, or intolerance to conventional therapy, biologics, or JAK inhibitors [Australian].
- **Crohn’s Disease:** Treatment of moderately to severely active Crohn’s disease in adults with inadequate response, loss of response, or intolerance to conventional therapy or biologics [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Plaque Psoriasis & Psoriatic Arthritis:** 100 mg SC at Week 0 and Week 4, followed by 100 mg every 8 weeks thereafter. For psoriatic arthritis, it may be used with or without conventional synthetic DMARDs (e.g., methotrexate) [Australian].
- **Ulcerative Colitis:**
    - **Induction:** 200 mg IV infusion (minimum 1 hour) at Week 0, Week 4, and Week 8.
    - **Maintenance:** 200 mg SC at Week 12, then every 4 weeks. Alternatively, 100 mg SC at Week 16 and every 8 weeks may be considered for some patients [Australian].
- **Crohn’s Disease:**
    - **Induction:** 200 mg IV infusion (minimum 1 hour) OR 400 mg SC (as two 200 mg injections) at Week 0, Week 4, and Week 8.
    - **Maintenance:** 200 mg SC at Week 12, then every 4 weeks. Alternatively, 100 mg SC at Week 16 and every 8 weeks may be considered for some patients [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing (Plaque Psoriasis, 6-17 years)
Administered SC at Week 0, Week 4, and every 8 weeks thereafter:
- **Weight ≥40 kg:** 100 mg.
- **Weight <40 kg:** Dose adjusted by weight (ranging from 20 mg for 12-15 kg to 45 mg for 31-39 kg) using the 45 mg/0.45 mL pre-filled pen [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **SC Injection:** Rotate sites; avoid psoriasis plaques. Allow to reach room temperature (30 mins) before use. Do not shake [Australian].
- **IV Infusion:** Must be diluted in 250 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride to a final concentration of 0.8 mg/mL. Use an in-line, sterile, non-pyrogenic, low protein-binding filter (0.2 micrometre) [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Serious hypersensitivity to guselkumab or any of its excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Infections:** Guselkumab may increase infection risk. Do not initiate in patients with clinically important active infections until resolved or adequately treated. Discontinue if a serious infection develops [Australian].
- **Tuberculosis (TB):** Evaluate for TB prior to treatment. Initiate treatment for latent TB before starting guselkumab. Monitor for active TB signs during and after therapy [Australian].
- **Immunisations:** Complete all required vaccinations prior to therapy. **Live vaccines** should not be used concurrently. Withhold live vaccines for at least 12 weeks after the last guselkumab dose and do not resume for at least 2 weeks post-vaccination [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Serious reactions (anaphylaxis) have occurred post-marketing, sometimes several days after treatment. Discontinue immediately if a serious reaction occurs [Australian].
- **Hepatic Enzymes:** Increased transaminases were observed in clinical trials (specifically Psoriatic Arthritis and Crohn's Disease). Most cases were transient and did not require discontinuation [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by frequency based on clinical trial data [Australian]:

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Upper respiratory tract infections (including nasopharyngitis) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Headache, arthralgia, diarrhoea, gastroenteritis, injection site reactions (erythema, bruising, pain), tinea infections, herpes simplex infections, rash |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Migraine, urticaria, candida infections |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Anaphylaxis, hypersensitivity |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Serious infections (≤0.8% in trials) and serious hypersensitivity reactions.
- **Laboratory Changes:** Elevated ALT and AST (Common in Psoriatic Arthritis and Crohn’s disease trials) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP450 Substrates:** No clinically relevant changes were observed in the pharmacokinetics of midazolam (CYP3A4), warfarin (CYP2C9), omeprazole (CYP2C19), dextromethorphan (CYP2D6), or caffeine (CYP1A2) when co-administered with a single dose of guselkumab. No dose adjustments are required [Australian].
- **Live Vaccines:** Interaction risk due to potential for infection; avoid concurrent use [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B1 [Australian]**. Use in pregnant women has not been studied. Use only if potential benefit outweighs risk. Animal studies (monkeys) showed no embryo-fetal toxicity; however, guselkumab crosses the placenta [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** No human data available. Guselkumab was not detected in the milk of lactating monkeys. Consider developmental/health benefits of breastfeeding versus mother's clinical need [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No effects on fertility parameters were identified in animal studies [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Established for plaque psoriasis in patients ≥6 years. Safety/efficacy not established for Psoriatic Arthritis, UC, or Crohn’s in patients <18 years [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment required. Population PK analysis showed no apparent changes in clearance in subjects ≥65 years [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specifically studied. No dose adjustment is considered necessary, as monoclonal antibodies are typically eliminated via intracellular catabolism rather than renal or hepatic pathways [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selective binding to the p19 subunit of the IL-23 protein. This prevents IL-23 from interacting with its cell-surface receptor, thereby inhibiting the Th17/IL-17 cytokine pathway that drives inflammatory disease [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics [Australian]:**
    - **Bioavailability:** ~49% after a single 100 mg SC dose.
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is 7–10 L (98–123 mL/kg).
    - **Elimination:** Clearance ranges from 0.288 to 0.479 L/day.
    - **Half-life:** Approximately 15 to 18 days in psoriasis patients; 17 days in UC/Crohn's patients.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Treatment leads to reduced expression of Th17 pathway genes (IL-17A, IL-17F, IL-22) and reduced levels of inflammatory markers such as C-reactive protein (CRP) and faecal calprotectin [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Plaque Psoriasis (VOYAGE 1 & 2):** Demonstrated superiority over placebo and adalimumab. At Week 16, ~84–85% of patients achieved IGA 0/1 (cleared/minimal) [Australian].
- **Psoriatic Arthritis (DISCOVER 1 & 2):** Demonstrated significant improvement in ACR20 responses at Week 24 compared to placebo [Australian].
- **Ulcerative Colitis (QUASAR):** Demonstrated significantly higher clinical remission rates (23% vs 8% placebo) at Week 12 following IV induction [Australian].
- **Crohn’s Disease (GALAXI & GRAVITI):** Demonstrated clinical remission and endoscopic response superiority over placebo at Week 12 and Week 48 with both IV and SC induction regimens [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store in a refrigerator at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze. Do not shake [Australian].
- **Handling:** Keep in the original carton to protect from light.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **S4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian]**.
