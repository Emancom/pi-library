# Editorial Product Information: glycine

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **glycine**
- Chemical class: Amino acid (aminoacetic acid)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Transurethral irrigation (urologic irrigation)
- Strengths noted in sources: 1.5% (1.5 g glycine per 100 mL; osmolarity ~200 mOsmol/L; pH ~6.0)

## 2. Regulatory History
- US first marketing start date (as stated): 07/01/2019 [US]
- Date(s) of last revision/update of source PI as stated: November 2022; Revised October 2025 (both dates appear in available source material) [US]
- Withdrawals, suspensions, or restrictions: Not specified in available sources
- If additional jurisdictional registration dates are required: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Indicated for use as irrigating fluid during transurethral prostatic resection and other transurethral surgical procedures (urologic irrigation).  
  - Where an indication is jurisdictional in source material, it is presented without additional regional restrictions; specific jurisdictional approvals beyond the above are not specified in available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Concentration commonly provided/used: 1.5% glycine (1.5 g per 100 mL; 200 mOsmol/L).

### Dosing by indication
- Transurethral prostatic resection and other transurethral procedures: total volume used for irrigation is at the discretion of the operating surgeon. No fixed systemic dose schedules are provided.

### Administration instructions
- For transurethral instillation only using appropriate urologic instrumentation.
- Use a disposable irrigation set.
- Attach the administration set promptly following aseptic technique.
- Single-dose containers: discard unused portion; do not store mixed or partially used solution.
- Do not use for parenteral injection by usual parenteral routes.
- Positioning: avoid placing the irrigation container more than approximately 60 cm (≈2 feet) above the operating table because greater height has been reported to increase intravascular absorption.
- When introducing additives: consult a pharmacist if available; use aseptic technique, mix thoroughly and do not store mixtures.

### Titration schedules
- Not specified in available sources.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not applicable / Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Not for injection by usual parenteral routes (general contraindication).
- Do not use in patients with anuria.
- No additional contraindications are specified in the available source material.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- **FOR UROLOGIC IRRIGATION ONLY.** Absorbed irrigating fluids must be regarded as systemic agents.
- Use caution in patients with severe cardiopulmonary or renal dysfunction because substantial intravascular absorption of irrigating fluid can occur during transurethral surgery and may precipitate acute volume overload and decompensated heart failure.
- Monitor cardiovascular status closely in patients with known cardiac disease before and during transurethral resection when using glycine irrigation.
- Systemic absorption may expand the extracellular fluid volume and can lower serum sodium concentration (dilutional hyponatremia) or aggravate pre-existing hyponatremia; hyponatremia may lead to coma.
- Exercise caution in patients with **impaired hepatic function**; glycine is metabolized in the liver and ammonia produced during metabolism may accumulate and cause hyperammonemia with possible encephalopathy or coma.
- Aseptic technique is essential. Do not use unless solution is clear, seal intact and container undamaged. Discard unused portion.
- Carcinogenesis, mutagenesis, impairment of fertility: studies have not been performed with the irrigation solution to evaluate these risks.
- Nursing mothers: exercise caution when administered to a nursing woman.
- Pregnancy: use only if clearly needed. (See Section 9.)
- Pediatric use: safety and effectiveness have not been established; available use in pediatric patients is limited and inadequate to fully define appropriate dosing and limitations.
- Do not heat container over 66°C (150°F).
- In the event of suspected overhydration or solute overload, reassess and institute appropriate corrective measures.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor fluid balance and signs of intravascular absorption during and after procedure.
- Monitor serum electrolytes, including sodium, when significant fluid absorption is suspected or when clinically indicated.
- Monitor respiratory and cardiovascular status for signs of pulmonary congestion, hypotension, arrhythmia or ischemia.
- Monitor for neurologic changes suggestive of hyperammonemia or encephalopathy (confusion, decreased consciousness, seizures).

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Safety and effectiveness have not been established. Limited pediatric experience is inadequate to fully define proper dosing and limitations.

### Elderly
- No specific dosing adjustments provided. Exercise caution in elderly patients with pre-existing cardiac, pulmonary, renal or hepatic disease because of risk of fluid overload and systemic effects.

### Renal impairment
- Do not use in anuric patients (contraindicated).
- Caution advised in patients with renal dysfunction because irrigating fluid absorbed intravascularly is principally excreted by the kidney; however, no specific dose adjustments or eGFR-based guidance are provided.

### Hepatic impairment
- Exercise caution in patients with impaired liver function because glycine is metabolized in the liver and ammonia produced during metabolism may accumulate; no specific dosing adjustments are provided.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **glycine** is an amino acid (aminoacetic acid) used as a nonelectrolyte irrigating solution. As a nonelectrolyte aqueous solution it is nonconductive and thus suitable for irrigation during electrosurgical procedures requiring nonconductive media.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: Irrigating solutions may be absorbed intravascularly in variable and sometimes large volumes during transurethral surgery through open prostatic or bladder venous sinuses.
- Distribution: As a hypotonic nonelectrolyte solution, systemic absorption expands extracellular fluid volume; distribution depends on body water compartments and electrolyte status.
- Metabolism: Glycine is rapidly degraded in the liver by glycine oxidase; metabolism may produce ammonia.
- Elimination: Absorbed solution and metabolites are excreted by the kidney.
- Calculated osmolarity for 1.5% solution: approximately 200 mOsmol/L (hypotonic vs extracellular fluid ~280 mOsmol/L).
- pH: approximately 6.0 (range 4.5–6.5 noted in source).

### Pharmacodynamics
- A 1.5% glycine irrigation concentration is intended to reduce the risk of intravascular hemolysis associated with absorption of plain water through prostatic veins during transurethral resection while remaining nonconductive for electrosurgical use.
