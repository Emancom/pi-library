# Editorial Product Information: Glucarpidase

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory guidance.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and intended for educational and informational reference. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Glucarpidase (recombinant bacterial enzyme; carboxypeptidase).
- **Brand Name:** **VORAXAZE** [US].
- **ATC Code:** V03AF09 (Note: Not explicitly stated in the source but classified within the detoxifying agents for antineoplastic treatment).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Lyophilized powder for injection; 1,000 Units per single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** BTG International Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Reduction of Methotrexate Toxicity:** Indicated to reduce toxic plasma methotrexate (MTX) concentrations (defined as >1 micromole per litre) in adult and paediatric patients who exhibit delayed methotrexate clearance due to impaired renal function [US].
- **Limitations of Use:** Not recommended for use in patients with expected methotrexate clearance or those with expected plasma concentrations. Use in these populations may lead to subtherapeutic methotrexate exposure [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric):** 50 Units per kg body weight [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Administer as a single intravenous injection over 5 minutes [US].
    - The intravenous line should be flushed before and after administration [US].
- **Concomitant Leucovorin (Folinic Acid) Rescue:**
    - **Timing:** Do not administer leucovorin within 2 hours before or after a dose of **VORAXAZE** [US].
    - **Initial 48 Hours:** Continue the same leucovorin dose that was being administered prior to **VORAXAZE** [US].
    - **Post-48 Hours:** Adjust leucovorin dosage based on measured methotrexate concentrations. Continue rescue therapy until MTX levels remain below the treatment threshold for at least 3 days [US].
- **Ancillary Care:** Continue intravenous hydration and urinary alkalinisation as clinically indicated [US].
- **Preparation:** Reconstitute with 1 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection, USP. Mix by gently rolling and tilting; do not shake [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None specified in available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Serious Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Occurred in <1% of patients. Clinicians should monitor for signs of infusion reactions [US].
- **Interference with Methotrexate Monitoring:**
    - **VORAXAZE** hydrolyses MTX into 4-deoxy-4-amino-N10-methylpteroic acid (DAMPA). 
    - **Immunoassay Warning:** DAMPA interferes with standard MTX immunoassays, leading to a significant overestimation of MTX concentrations [US].
    - **Requirement:** For 48 hours following **VORAXAZE** administration, MTX levels must be measured using a chromatographic method (e.g., HPLC) [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Continued monitoring of renal function and MTX concentrations is essential until clearance is confirmed [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions were evaluated in 290 patients. Evaluation is often confounded by the underlying toxicity of methotrexate (e.g., myelosuppression, mucositis, renal failure).

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** One case of a Grade 3 hypersensitivity reaction was reported in clinical trials [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Leucovorin (Folinic Acid):** **VORAXAZE** hydrolyses leucovorin, which can significantly decrease its concentration and efficacy as a rescue agent. Strict adherence to the 2-hour administration gap is required [US].
- **Other Folate Analogues:** **VORAXAZE** may reduce the concentration of other folate analogues or folate-based metabolic inhibitors [US].
- **Analytical Interaction:** As noted in section 5, the metabolite DAMPA interferes with MTX immunoassays for up to 48 hours post-dose [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not assigned a lettered category in the revised US PI, but no human or animal data are available [US].
    - **Clinical Consideration:** Glucarpidase is typically used in combination with methotrexate, which is a known teratogen (embryo-fetal harm) [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** No data are available regarding the presence of glucarpidase in human milk or its effects on the breastfed infant. Caution is advised as it is administered with methotrexate [US].
- **Fertility:** Not evaluated in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy are established in patients from 1 month to 17 years of age. No overall safety differences were observed compared to adults [US].
- **Elderly Use:** 15% of trial participants were ≥65 years. No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were noted between elderly and younger patients [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is recommended. While the half-life of glucarpidase is slightly prolonged in severe renal impairment (8.2 hours vs 5.6 hours), pharmacokinetic parameters are otherwise similar to healthy subjects [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Glucarpidase is a recombinant bacterial enzyme that hydrolyses the carboxyl-terminal glutamate residue from folic acid and classical antifolates like methotrexate. This converts MTX into inactive metabolites (DAMPA and glutamate), providing a non-renal pathway for MTX elimination [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** A single dose of 50 Units/kg reduces MTX concentrations by ≥97% within 15 minutes [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Mean volume of distribution ($V_d$) is 3.6 L [US].
    - **Elimination:** Mean elimination half-life ($t_{1/2}$) is 5.6 hours (based on serum activity) to 9 hours (based on serum concentration) [US].
    - **Clearance:** Mean systemic clearance is 7.5 mL/min [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy Data:** Based on 22 patients in an open-label study (Study 1). The primary endpoint was "Rapid and Sustained Clinically Important Reduction" (RSCIR), defined as MTX concentration ≤1 micromole/L within 15 minutes, sustained for 8 days [US].
- **Outcomes:** 10/22 (45%) achieved RSCIR. Efficiency was highest (77%) in patients with pre-dose MTX levels between 1–50 micromole/L. While 91% of patients achieved an initial >95% reduction, those with extremely high baseline MTX (>100 micromole/L) often saw levels rebound above 1 micromole/L (nadir of ~1.4 micromole/L) [US].
- **Repeat Dosing:** A second dose administered 48 hours after the first did not result in additional patients achieving RSCIR if their levels were still >1 micromole/L [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Do not freeze. Use reconstituted solution immediately or store under refrigeration for up to 4 hours [US].
- **Preservatives:** The product is preservative-free; discard any unused portion [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not applicable in the US; would be classified according to local schedules in other jurisdictions (e.g., Schedule 4 in Australia).
