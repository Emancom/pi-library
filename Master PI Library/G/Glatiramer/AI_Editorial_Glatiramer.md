# Editorial Product Information: Glatiramer Acetate

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesised from the following jurisdictional Product Information (PI) documents: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory information into a single, clinically focused reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing and dispensing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active ingredient:** Glatiramer acetate, a synthetic polypeptide composed of four naturally occurring amino acids (L-glutamic acid, L-alanine, L-tyrosine, and L-lysine).
- **Brand name(s):** **Copaxone** [Australian]
- **ATC code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage form(s) and strength(s):** 
    - Solution for injection in a pre-filled syringe: 40 mg/mL [Australian].
- **Route(s) of administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) only.
- **Manufacturer/sponsor:** Teva Pharma Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Relapsing Remitting Multiple Sclerosis (RRMS):** Indicated for the reduction of the frequency of relapses in patients with RRMS [Australian].
- **Single Clinical Event:** Treatment of patients experiencing a single clinical event suggestive of multiple sclerosis with at least two clinically silent MRI lesions characteristic of MS, provided alternative diagnoses have been excluded [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** 40 mg administered three times a week.
- **Administration Schedule:** Injections must be administered at least 48 hours apart (e.g., Monday, Wednesday, Friday) [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - For subcutaneous use only. Do **not** administer intravenously or intramuscularly.
    - Solution should be removed from the refrigerator and allowed to reach room temperature for approximately 20 minutes prior to injection.
    - Injection sites include the arms, abdomen, hips, and thighs. Sites must be rotated daily to reduce local irritation or pain.
    - Pre-filled syringes are for single use in one patient only; any residue must be discarded.
- **Titration:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **glatiramer acetate** or **mannitol** [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Immediate Post-Injection Reaction (IPIR):** Approximately 2% of patients using the 40 mg/mL strength may experience a constellation of symptoms within minutes of injection, including flushing (vasodilation), chest pain, dyspnea, palpitations, tachycardia, anxiety, throat constriction, and urticaria. These are generally transient and self-limiting but may require emergency care [Australian].
- **Chest Pain:** Transient chest pain may occur independently or as part of the IPIR. It typically begins at least one month after starting treatment and usually lacks clinical sequelae [Australian].
- **Anaphylaxis:** Rare but potentially fatal hypersensitivity reactions may occur. Treatment must be discontinued if an anaphylactic reaction occurs [Australian].
- **Hepatic Injury:** Rare cases of severe liver injury (including liver failure and hepatitis with jaundice) have been reported. 
    - **Monitoring:** Serum aminotransferase, alkaline phosphatase, and total bilirubin levels should be obtained within 6 months prior to treatment initiation and monitored during treatment as clinically relevant [Australian].
- **Injection Site Reactions:** Localised lipoatrophy and, rarely, skin necrosis may occur. Rotation of injection sites is critical to minimise these risks. Lipoatrophy is thought to be permanent [Australian].
- **Immune Function:** As an antigenic material, glatiramer may induce antibodies and potentially alter normal immune responses [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Renal function should be monitored in patients with pre-existing impairment [Australian].

**Serious/Other Significant Effects:** 
- Post-marketing reports include sepsis, LE syndrome, glaucoma, blindness, and rare severe liver injury leading to transplant [Australian].
- Eosinophilia was reported in up to 7% of patients during the first three months of treatment in some trials [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No formal evaluation of glatiramer with Interferon beta-1b has been performed, though limited data (n=10) suggests no serious adverse events when switching between these therapies [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** No significant interactions have been observed with therapies commonly used in MS patients, including corticosteroids [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category B1** [Australian]. 
    - Large observational datasets (>1000 outcomes) suggest no malformative, fetal, or neonatal toxicity. Glatiramer may be used during pregnancy if clinically required [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on the effects on milk production or presence in human milk. However, due to its physico-chemical properties and low oral absorption, infant exposure is expected to be negligible. Benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against maternal clinical need [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats showed no adverse effects on reproductive parameters at doses up to 15 times the human therapeutic dose [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for children under 12 years of age due to insufficient data. Safety in adolescents (12–18 years) is reported to be similar to adults [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically studied in the elderly, but safety data for patients up to 65 years of age are reassuring [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not determined. Monitor renal function during treatment; use with caution due to the possibility of immune complex deposition [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution. Baseline and periodic LFT monitoring is required. Discontinue if clinically significant drug-induced liver injury (DILI) is suspected [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** The precise mechanism is unknown. It is thought to modify immune processes responsible for MS pathogenesis, possibly by mimicking/competing with myelin basic protein [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Readily absorbed after SC injection.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively degraded by local hydrolysis at the SC injection site. 
    - **Distribution:** 97.5% bound to plasma proteins in vitro. 
    - **Elimination:** Excretory fate is unknown, as metabolites cannot be distinguished from naturally occurring amino acids [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **RRMS (40 mg/mL TIW):** A 12-month placebo-controlled trial of 1,404 patients showed a 34.4% relative risk reduction in the total number of confirmed relapses (p < 0.0001) compared to placebo. Significant reductions were also noted in MRI variables, including new/enlarging T2 lesions and T1-enhancing lesions [Australian].
- **Single Clinical Event:** A study of 481 patients showed that glatiramer (20 mg/mL daily) delayed progression to Clinically Definite Multiple Sclerosis (CDMS) by 45% (Hazard Ratio 0.55) over three years [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate). Do **not** freeze [Australian].
- **Excursions:** If refrigeration is unavailable, the product may be stored below 25°C on a **single occasion for up to one month**. If not used within this month, it must be returned to the refrigerator [Australian].
- **Light:** Keep the pre-filled syringe in the outer carton to protect from light [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
