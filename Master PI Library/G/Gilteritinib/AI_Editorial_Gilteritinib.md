# Editorial Product Information: Gilteritinib

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of gilteritinib product information, synthesised for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved PI for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Gilteritinib (as gilteritinib fumarate). 
- **Chemical Class:** Tyrosine kinase inhibitor; FMS-like tyrosine kinase 3 (FLT3) and AXL inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** **XOSPATA** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** L01EX13
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Film-coated tablets containing 44.2 mg gilteritinib fumarate (equivalent to 40 mg gilteritinib).
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Astellas Pharma [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Myeloid Leukaemia (AML):** Monotherapy for the treatment of adult patients with relapsed or refractory AML who possess an FLT3 mutation (internal tandem duplication [ITD] or tyrosine kinase domain [TKD]) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** The recommended starting dose is 120 mg (three 40 mg tablets) once daily.
- **Dose Escalation:** If a composite complete remission (CRc) is not achieved after 4 weeks, the dose may be increased to 200 mg once daily, provided it is tolerated and clinically appropriate. 
- **Duration of Treatment:** Therapy should continue until the patient no longer derives clinical benefit or until unacceptable toxicity occurs. Clinical response may be delayed; continuation for up to 6 months should be considered.
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Tablets must be swallowed whole with water; do not break or crush.
    - May be taken with or without food.
    - Administer at approximately the same time each day.
- **Missed Dose:** Administer as soon as possible on the same day. Resume normal scheduling the following day. Do not take a double dose.
- **Vomiting:** If vomiting occurs after administration, do not take an additional dose; resume the normal schedule the following day.
- **Dose Modifications for Toxicity:**
    - **Differentiation Syndrome:** If suspected, initiate systemic corticosteroids and haemodynamic monitoring. Interrupt gilteritinib if severe symptoms persist >48 hours despite steroids.
    - **QTc Prolongation:** Interrupt treatment if QTcF >500 msec. Resume at a reduced dose (80 mg or 120 mg) when QTcF returns to ≤480 msec or within 30 msec of baseline.
    - **Pancreatitis:** Interrupt until resolution; resume at a reduced dose.
    - **Other Grade 3/4 Toxicities:** Interrupt until recovery to Grade 1; resume at a reduced dose (80 mg or 120 mg).
- **HSCT Considerations:** Interrupt gilteritinib one week prior to haematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) conditioning. Treatment may resume 30 days post-HSCT if engraftment is successful and the patient is in CRc without significant graft-versus-host disease (GvHD).

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to gilteritinib or any excipient in the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Differentiation Syndrome:** Associated with rapid proliferation of myeloid cells; can be fatal. Symptoms include fever, dyspnoea, pleural/pericardial effusion, pulmonary oedema, hypotension, and renal dysfunction. Treat with corticosteroids and haemodynamic monitoring.
- **Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES):** Rare, reversible neurological disorder. Confirm via MRI if suspected (seizure, headache, confusion). Discontinue gilteritinib permanently if PRES develops.
- **Prolonged QT Interval:** Gilteritinib is associated with concentration-dependent QT prolongation. Correct hypokalaemia or hypomagnesaemia before and during treatment. Perform ECGs at baseline, Day 8 and 15 of Cycle 1, and monthly for the following three months.
- **Pancreatitis:** Evaluate patients with suggestive symptoms.
- **Embryofetal Toxicity:** Potential for fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status within 7 days of starting treatment. 
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Assess blood chemistries, including creatine phosphokinase (CPK), prior to initiation, on Day 15, and monthly thereafter.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A/P-gp Inducers:** Avoid concomitant use with strong inducers (e.g., phenytoin, rifampicin, St. John’s Wort) as they significantly decrease gilteritinib exposure (AUC decreased by 70%).
- **CYP3A/P-gp Inhibitors:** Use caution with strong inhibitors (e.g., voriconazole, itraconazole, clarithromycin) as they increase gilteritinib exposure (AUC increased by approximately 2.2-fold).
- **Substrates of Transporters:** Gilteritinib may inhibit P-gp, BCRP, and OCT1. Caution is advised when co-administering with substrates such as digoxin, dabigatran etexilate, and metformin.
- **Targeted Receptors:** Gilteritinib may reduce the efficacy of drugs targeting 5HT2B or sigma nonspecific receptors (e.g., escitalopram, sertraline). Avoid unless essential.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Animal studies indicate embryofetal lethality and teratogenicity at subclinical exposures. Gilteritinib should not be used in pregnant women or women of childbearing potential not using effective contraception.
- **Contraception:** 
    - **Females:** Effective contraception (including a barrier method if using hormonal types) during and for 6 months post-treatment.
    - **Males:** Effective contraception during and for 4 months post-treatment.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety not established. Animal data show excretion in milk at levels higher than maternal plasma. Breastfeeding should be discontinued during treatment and for at least 2 months after the final dose.
- **Fertility:** No human data available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients <18 years not established. Use in infants <6 months is specifically cautioned against due to potential impacts on cardiac development via 5HT2B binding.
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required for patients ≥65 years.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild or moderate impairment. In severe renal impairment or ESRD, exposure may be increased; monitor closely for toxicity and QT prolongation.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild (Child-Pugh A) or moderate (Child-Pugh B) impairment. Not studied in severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Gilteritinib inhibits FLT3 receptor signalling and proliferation in cells expressing FLT3 (including ITD and TKD mutations) and induces apoptosis in FLT3-ITD expressing cells. It also inhibits AXL.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak plasma concentrations (tmax) reached in 4–6 hours. High-fat meals decrease Cmax by 26%.
    - **Distribution:** Extensive tissue distribution; approximately 90% plasma protein bound (primarily to albumin).
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by CYP3A4.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily faecal (64.5%); renal excretion is a minor pathway (16.4%). 
    - **Half-life:** Approximately 113 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ADMIRAL Trial (2215-CL-0301):** A Phase 3, randomised, open-label trial comparing gilteritinib 120 mg to salvage chemotherapy in 371 adults with relapsed/refractory FLT3-mutated AML. 
- **Primary Outcome:** Median Overall Survival (OS) was significantly longer with gilteritinib (9.3 months) compared to chemotherapy (5.6 months) [HR 0.637; p=0.0004]. 
- **Response Rates:** The CR/CRh rate was 34% for gilteritinib versus 15.3% for chemotherapy.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Handling:** Store in the original container to protect from light/moisture. 
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].
