# Originality Audit Report: gramicidin

## Audit Summary
- **Originality Score:** 65/100
- **Verbatim Copied Passages Found:** 8
- **Overall Assessment:** Concern

## Verbatim Copied Passages

1.  **Section: 2. Approved Indications (Ophthalmic)**
    - **Editorial:** "...treatment of superficial infections of the external eye and its surrounding tissues (adnexa), including bacterial conjunctivitis, keratitis, keratoconjunctivitis, blepharitis, and blepharoconjunctivitis."
    - **Source:** Document 2 (FDA), Page 3: "...indicated for the topical treatment of superficial infections of the external eye and its adnexa caused by susceptible bacteria. Such infections encompass conjunctivitis, keratitis and keratoconjunctivitis, blepharitis and blepharoconjunctivitis."
    - **Location:** Under Ophthalmic Use.

2.  **Section: 3. Contraindications (Topical)**
    - **Editorial:** "Contraindicated in tuberculous skin lesions and viral infections, including Herpes simplex, vaccinia, and varicella."
    - **Source:** Document 1 (TGA), Page 1: "Tuberculous lesions and most viral lesions of the skin such as Herpes simplex, but particularly in vaccinia and varicella."
    - **Location:** Third bullet point of Section 3.

3.  **Section: 4. Warnings and Precautions (Superinfection)**
    - **Editorial:** "Prolonged use may lead to the overgrowth of non-susceptible organisms, including fungi. If superinfection occurs, appropriate therapy should be initiated."
    - **Source:** Document 2 (FDA), Page 3: "...prolonged use of this product may result in overgrowth of nonsusceptible organisms including fungi. If superinfection occurs, appropriate measures should be initiated."
    - **Location:** General Precautions, second bullet.

4.  **Section: 4. Warnings and Precautions (Cross-Reactivity)**
    - **Editorial:** "Allergic cross-reactions may occur, potentially preventing the future use of related aminoglycoside antibiotics such as gentamicin or streptomycin."
    - **Source:** Document 2 (FDA), Page 3: "Allergic cross-reactions may occur which could prevent the use of any or all of the following antibiotics for the treatment of future infections: ...streptomycin, and possibly gentamicin."

6.  **Section: 5. Dosage and Administration (Ophthalmic)**
    - **Editorial:** "Instill 1 or 2 drops into the affected eye(s) every 4 hours for 7 to 10 days. In cases of severe infection, dosage may be increased to 2 drops every hour."
    - **Source:** Document 2 (FDA), Page 5: "Instill one or two drops into the affected eye every 4 hours for 7 to 10 days. In severe infections, dosage may be increased to as much as two drops every hour."

    - **Source:** Document 1 (TGA), Page 5: "Once absorbed neomycin is rapidly excreted unchanged through the kidneys. The half-life is approximately 2 to 3 hours."
    - **Location:** Metabolism and Excretion.

8.  **Section: 10. Mechanism of Action (Gramicidin)**
    - **Editorial:** "...altering the permeability of the bacterial cell membrane. It forms channels through the lipid bilayer, allowing the leakage of essential inorganic cations..."
    - **Source:** Document 2 (FDA), Page 2: "...increases the permeability of the bacterial cell membrane to inorganic cations by forming a network of channels through the normal lipid bilayer of the membrane."
    - **Location:** First paragraph of Section 10.

## Close Paraphrases

*   **Section 2 (Dermatological Use):** The editorial changes "relief of the inflammatory and pruritic manifestations of dermatoses" to "relief of inflammatory and pruritic symptoms of dermatoses." This is a superficial synonym swap of the TGA PI.
*   **Section 8 (Pregnancy - Category D):** The description of aminoglycosides crossing the placenta and risk of ototoxicity/nephrotoxicity follows the TGA text (Section 4.6) very closely in structure, merely condensing the specific mention of gentamicin to aminoglycosides in general.
*   **Section 10 (Synergistic Components):** The definitions for Neomycin and Polymyxin B are direct mirrors of the "Clinical Pharmacology" section in the FDA PI, with only minor grammatical adjustments.

## Originality Strengths

*   **Unified Synthesis:** The editorial successfully creates a structural framework that didn't exist in the sources—a "Unified PI." This requires an original organizational strategy.
*   **Editorial Note:** The introductory paragraph is entirely original and provides necessary context for the document.
*   **Contrastive Analysis:** The editorial does well to separate "Formulation-Specific Warnings," clearly distinguishing between the risks of the topical ointment (steroid-related) and the ophthalmic solution.

## Differences from Source PIs

*   **Consolidation:** The editorial merges two distinct products (Kenacomb and a triple-antibiotic ophthalmic solution) into one reference document.
*   **Formatting:** The editorial uses a standardized numbering system (1-11) which aligns with modern TGA/EMA PI formats, whereas the FDA source is an older, non-numbered label format.
*   **Omissions:** The editorial omits specific chemical structural formulas and detailed list of excipients (Plastibase 50W, thimerosal) found in the sources, focusing instead on clinical data.
*   **Harmonization:** It uses the term "Gramicidin D" and clarifies its use across different clinical applications (dermatology vs. ophthalmology).

## Recommendation
**REVISION NEEDED**

While the *structure* of the document is original, several critical clinical sections (Dosage, Pharmacokinetics, and Contraindications) are copied nearly verbatim. To pass a strict compliance and originality audit, the following changes are required:
1.  **Reword Section 5 (Dosage):** Use original phrasing to describe the frequency and duration.
2.  **Rewrite Section 10 (Mechanism of Action):** The description of "channels through the lipid bilayer" is too close to the FDA text. Describe the physiological process in new language.
3.  **Paraphrase Section 3 (Contraindications):** Instead of listing the viral infections exactly as they appear in the TGA list, integrate them into a more fluid sentence structure.
4.  **Section 9 (Pharmacokinetics):** Reword the excretion data for neomycin to avoid the exact "half-life" sentence construction used by the TGA.
