# Editorial Product Information: Galsulfase

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of galsulfase for use by healthcare professionals.
- This document is an editorial synthesis intended for reference and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Galsulfase (rch).
- **Chemical Class:** Recombinant human N-acetylgalactosamine 4-sulfatase (glycosaminoglycan N-acetylgalactosamine 4-sulfatase). It is produced using recombinant DNA technology in a Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cell line.
- **Brand Name:** **NAGLAZYME** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Concentrated solution for injection. Each 5 mL vial contains 5 mg of galsulfase (1 mg/mL).
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** BioMarin Pharmaceutical Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Mucopolysaccharidosis VI (MPS VI):** Indicated as long-term enzyme replacement therapy for patients with Maroteaux-Lamy syndrome (N-acetylgalactosamine 4-sulfatase deficiency) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dosage is 1 mg/kg of body weight, administered once weekly via intravenous infusion [Australian].
- **Pretreatment:** Patients should be pretreated with antihistamines (with or without antipyretics) 30 to 60 minutes prior to starting the infusion to reduce the risk of infusion reactions [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Must be diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection to a final volume of 250 mL.
    - For patients weighing 20 kg or less, or those at risk of fluid overload, a volume of 100 mL may be considered.
    - The infusion duration should be no less than 4 hours.
    - **Infusion Rate:** Start at 6 mL/h for the first hour. If tolerated, the rate may be increased to a maximum of 80 mL/h for the remaining 3 hours.
    - **Equipment:** Use a low-protein-binding infusion set with a 0.2 µm in-line filter.
- **Preparation Notes:** Do not shake or agitate the vials, as this may denature the protein. Vials should reach room temperature before dilution but should not be heated [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Anaphylaxis and Severe Allergic Reactions:** Life-threatening reactions (including shock, respiratory distress, and laryngeal oedema) have occurred during and up to 24 hours post-infusion. Immediate discontinuation and emergency resuscitation equipment must be available [Australian].
- **Immune-mediated Reactions:** Type III reactions, including membranous glomerulonephritis, have been reported. Re-challenge should be performed under close clinical supervision if appropriate [Australian].
- **Acute Cardiorespiratory Failure:** Caution is required in patients susceptible to fluid overload (e.g., weight ≤20 kg, acute respiratory illness, or compromised cardiac/respiratory function) [Australian].
- **Acute Respiratory Complications:** Sleep apnoea is common in MPS VI; antihistamine pretreatment may exacerbate this. Airway patency should be evaluated before treatment [Australian].
- **Spinal/Cervical Cord Compression (SCC):** SCC is a known complication of MPS VI. Patients should be monitored for signs such as back pain, limb paralysis, or incontinence [Australian].
- **Sodium Restriction:** Each vial contains 0.8 mmol (18.5 mg) of sodium; this must be considered for patients on controlled sodium diets [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by frequency based on clinical trial data (n=19) [Australian]:

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Abdominal pain (47%), Ear pain (42%), Arthralgia (42%), Pain (32%), Conjunctivitis (21%), Dyspnoea (21%), Rash (21%), Chills/Rigors (21%), Chest pain (16%), Pharyngitis (11%), Areflexia (11%), Corneal opacity (11%), Gastroenteritis (11%), Hypertension (11%), Malaise (11%), Nasal congestion (11%), Umbilical hernia (11%), Hearing impairment (11%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Not specified (events <11% not listed in trial table) |

- **Infusion Reactions:** Occurred in 56% of patients despite pretreatment. Common symptoms include pyrexia, chills, rash, and nausea.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Laryngeal oedema, apnoea, pyrexia, urticaria, respiratory distress, angioedema, anaphylactoid reaction, and respiratory failure [Australian].
- **Post-marketing Experience:** Membranous nephropathy, thrombocytopenia, bradycardia, tachycardia, hypoxia, cyanosis, and paraesthesia [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- No formal drug interaction studies have been conducted [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. There are no adequate studies in pregnant women. Animal studies in rabbits showed an increased incidence of sternal fusion at high doses. Use only if benefits outweigh foetal risks.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if galsulfase is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution when administering to nursing mothers [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats showed no effect on fertility or reproductive performance at doses up to 3 mg/kg/day [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Studied in patients aged 3 months to 29 years. Safety and efficacy in infants (<1 year) were consistent with older paediatric patients [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include patients older than 29 years; response in the elderly is unknown [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** MPS VI is caused by a deficiency in N-acetylgalactosamine 4-sulfatase, leading to the accumulation of dermatan sulfate. Galsulfase provides an exogenous source of the enzyme, which is taken up by lysosomes via mannose-6-phosphate receptors to catabolise glycosaminoglycans (GAGs) [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Treatment leads to a significant reduction in urinary GAG levels (95% of patients showed $\geq$ 50% reduction after 72 weeks) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Distribution:** Large variability in parameters.
    - **Elimination:** Mean half-life ranges from 9 minutes (Week 1) to 26 minutes (Week 24). Clearance decreases over time, possibly influenced by antibody development [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Phase 3 Trial:** A double-blind, placebo-controlled study (n=39) evaluated 1 mg/kg weekly infusions over 24 weeks.
- **Primary Endpoint:** The 12-minute walk test (12MWT).
- **Outcomes:** The galsulfase group showed a mean increase of 109 metres in the 12MWT compared to 26 metres in the placebo group (p=0.025). Improvements were also noted in the 3-minute stair climb test [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store under refrigeration at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze or shake. Protect from light [Australian].
- **Diluted Solution:** Contains no preservatives. Should ideally be used immediately. If necessary, it can be stored at 2°C to 8°C for up to 48 hours from the time of dilution to the completion of administration [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
