# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Jurisdictions contributing to this editorial: US (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial resource and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. The information provided is based on the source document for **levocarnitine**; note that the introductory prompt text mentioned gadopentetate dimeglumine, but the clinical data provided and synthesised herein pertains exclusively to **levocarnitine**.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. This may be administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion. 
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically in the range of 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments (e.g., downward to 5 mg/kg) should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations and may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL. Mixed solutions are stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None Known:** There are no known contraindications for levocarnitine injection listed in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulation Caution):** The safety of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Monitoring:** In metabolic disorders, monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common and Very Common Effects (≥5%)
Based on clinical trials in ESRD patients, the following events occurred at a frequency of ≥5%, though not necessarily causally linked to the drug [US]:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), headache (22%), infection (15%), abdominal pain (9%), fever (7%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%).

### Other Reported Effects
- **Neurologic:** Seizures, dizziness, paresthesia [US].
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia [US].
- **Body Odor:** Transient nausea, vomiting, and body odor have been observed during therapy [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is used concurrently with warfarin. 
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category C]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies on levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers are unavailable. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations after exogenous administration. Weigh the benefits to the mother against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is calculated by weight (50 mg/kg); see Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the available source.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used therapeutically in ESRD patients on dialysis. Note the specific caution regarding the accumulation of metabolites if using oral formulations in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (n=12), 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While carnitine concentrations increase, the definitive effect on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been fully determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservative [US].

- **US:** Rx-only.
