# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been derived from the Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: US (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information approved in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium).
- **Brand Name(s):** Not specifically named in source (Generic: **Levocarnitine Injection**).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It should be administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the next 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Administered every 3 hours or every 4 hours; should not be administered less frequently than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are recommended in the 50 mg/kg range or as clinically required. Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** 
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Monitoring
- **Plasma Concentrations:** Recommended prior to therapy. Normal free carnitine levels should be between 35 and 60 µmol/L.
- **Frequency:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma concentrations is recommended [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These often occur within minutes of administration, particularly in ESRD patients. If a reaction occurs, discontinue the drug and initiate appropriate medical treatment [US].
- **Seizures:** Reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral vs. IV):** While this PI concerns the injectable form, clinicians should note that chronic high-dose *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Monitoring:** Patients on warfarin require increased monitoring of International Normalised Ratio (INR) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events occurring at a frequency ≥5% in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials [US]:
- **Very Common (>10%):**
    - **General:** Injection site reaction (33–38%), Pain (30–35%), Headache (3–37%), Flu syndrome (15–29%), Chest pain (6–15%), Accidental injury (8–12%).
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), Hypotension (3–19%).
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), Vomiting (9–21%), Nausea (5–12%).
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), Cough increase (10–18%), Dyspnea (3–11%), Rhinitis (6–11%).
- **Common (1–10%):** 
    - Abdominal pain, Asthenia, Fever, Infection, Tachycardia, Hypercalcemia, Hyperkalemia, Dizziness, Insomnia, Pruritus.

### Postmarketing Experience
- **Neurologic:** Seizures.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; body odor and gastritis have been noted less frequently [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, laryngeal edema.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased INR have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. Monitor INR levels after initiating treatment or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified by letter, but descriptive]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Consider the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant versus the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See **Section 3: Dosage and Administration** for weight-based dosing (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. No specific dose adjustments provided for non-dialysis renal impairment for the intravenous form, though safety of high-dose oral forms is cautioned in this group [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn metabolic errors, it promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are quickly excreted.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Study:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly showed that mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine concentrations increased from 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L (baseline) to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses three times weekly showed dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine concentrations at 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of an opened vial as the formulation is preservative-free [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's (concentration range 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poisons Schedule:** Not applicable for US jurisdiction; see local scheduling for other regions.
