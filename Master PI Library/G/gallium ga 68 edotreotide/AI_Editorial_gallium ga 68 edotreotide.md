# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for use by healthcare professionals. 
- This document is an editorial synthesis based on provided regulatory documentation and is not intended to substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP), an inner salt and a carrier molecule involved in mammalian energy metabolism.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [United States].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [United States].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for both acute and chronic treatment of patients with metabolic defects resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [United States].
- **Carnitine Deficiency in End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [United States].
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Administered every 3 to 4 hours; should not be administered less frequently than every 6 hours.
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though higher doses may be required based on clinical response. The maximum reported dose is 300 mg/kg [United States].
- **Monitoring:** Pre-therapy plasma carnitine levels should be obtained. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is recommended.

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [United States].
- **Administration Timing:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels <40 to 50 µmol/L. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations.

### General Instructions
- **Visual Inspection:** Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- **Discarding:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If severe reactions occur, discontinue treatment and initiate appropriate medical care [United States].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [United States].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in urine [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **General Reactions:** Transient nausea, vomiting, and body odor have been observed. Gastritis occurs less frequently.
- **ESRD Clinical Trial Experience:** In double-blind, placebo-controlled trials, the following events occurred at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality):

| Body System | Adverse Event (Frequency ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, back pain, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, palpitations, tachycardia |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, dyspepsia, nausea, vomiting |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia, depression |
| **Respiratory** | Cough increase, dyspnea, pharyngitis, rhinitis |
| **Skin** | Pruritus |
| **Special Senses** | Amblyopia (blurred vision) |
| **Urogenital** | Kidney failure |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended that INR levels be monitored after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [United States: Category C (prior system)]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate, well-controlled studies exist in pregnant women. Use only if clearly needed [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased carnitine concentrations in milk after exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine must be weighed against maternal benefits [United States].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal models [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders and ESRD are provided on a mg/kg basis, which applies to paediatric patients (see Section 3).
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources; however, dose selection should generally be cautious, reflecting the greater frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily used in ESRD patients on dialysis. High-dose oral levocarnitine is cautioned in this population due to metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO) [United States].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, it alleviates metabolic abnormalities related to the accumulation of toxic organic acids and restores plasma carnitine levels.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** Efficacy was demonstrated in studies where patients received 20 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis. Pre-dialysis (trough) levels increased from ~20 µmol/L at baseline to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks of therapy.
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine concentrations at 12 and 24 weeks [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
- **Stability:** Levocarnitine injection is compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [United States].
