# Editorial Product Information: Gallium Nitrate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (Food and Drug Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a concise reference format.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local clinical guidelines required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Gallium nitrate (hydrated nitrate salt of a group IIIa element).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Ganite** [United States].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources (L01XX is common for gallium nitrate, but not explicitly stated in the source).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 25 mg/mL in a 20 mL single-dose vial (500 mg per vial) [United States].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Genta Incorporated [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Cancer-Related Hypercalcemia:** Indicated for the treatment of clearly symptomatic hypercalcemia associated with malignancy that has not responded to adequate hydration [United States].
- **Clinical Context:** Generally reserved for patients with corrected serum calcium ≥12 mg/dL. Mild or asymptomatic cases should first be managed with conservative measures (e.g., saline hydration).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Standard Adult Dose:** 200 mg/m² body surface area daily for five consecutive days.
- **Dose Adjustment:** In patients with mild hypercalcemia and minimal symptoms, a lower dose of 100 mg/m²/day for five days may be considered.
- **Duration:** If serum calcium levels normalise in fewer than five days, treatment may be discontinued early.
- **Preparation and Dilution:** 
  - The daily dose should be diluted in 1,000 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection or 5% Dextrose Injection.
- **Administration Instructions:** 
  - Must be administered as a continuous intravenous infusion over 24 hours.
  - Adequate hydration must be established and maintained throughout treatment. A goal urine output of 2 L/day is recommended before starting therapy.
- **Retreatment:** Safety and efficacy of retreatment cycles have not been established in controlled studies.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Renal Impairment:** Severely impaired renal function, defined as a serum creatinine >2.5 mg/dL [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning - Nephrotoxicity:** Concurrent use with other potentially nephrotoxic drugs (e.g., aminoglycosides, amphotericin B) significantly increases the risk of severe renal insufficiency. If these drugs are required, gallium nitrate should be discontinued, and hydration should continue for several days after the nephrotoxic agent is stopped [United States].
- **Renal Monitoring:** Serum creatinine and BUN must be monitored closely. Therapy should be discontinued if serum creatinine exceeds 2.5 mg/dL.
- **Hydration Status:** Patients with cancer-related hypercalcemia are often dehydrated. Adequate oral or IV hydration must be established before and during treatment. Use caution in patients with compromised cardiovascular status to avoid overhydration.
- **Hypocalcemia/Electrolytes:** Frequent monitoring of serum calcium (daily) and phosphorus (twice weekly) is required.
- **Diuretics:** Avoid using loop diuretics (e.g., furosemide) until hypovolemia is corrected.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Acute Renal Failure:** Reported in clinical trials; the relationship to the drug is often complicated by the patient's underlying malignancy.
- **Optic Neuritis/Blindness:** Rare reports associated with high-dose use in chemotherapy trials (<1%).
- **Encephalopathy:** A single fatal case reported following high-dose therapy (300 mg/m²).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Nephrotoxic Agents:** Aminoglycosides and amphotericin B increase the risk of renal failure. This is a critical interaction [United States].
- **Diuretics:** No adverse interaction noted with furosemide in available data, provided the patient is not hypovolemic.
- **Combination Chemotherapy:** A symptom complex of dyspnea (interstitial pneumonitis), mouth soreness, and asthenia was reported when used with cyclophosphamide and prednisone in myeloma patients; however, causality is not established.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [United States]. Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. It is unknown if gallium nitrate causes fetal harm or affects reproductive capacity. Use only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is not known if the drug is excreted in human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in infants, a decision must be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug.
- **Fertility:** Long-term animal studies for carcinogenic potential or impairment of fertility have not been performed. Gallium nitrate was not mutagenic in standard tests.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in children have not been established.
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in source, though renal monitoring is critical in this population.
- **Renal Impairment:**
  - **Moderate (Creatinine 2.0–2.5 mg/dL):** Requires frequent monitoring and cautious use.
  - **Severe (Creatinine >2.5 mg/dL):** Contraindicated.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources; however, the drug is not metabolised by the liver.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Exerts a hypocalcemic effect by inhibiting calcium resorption from bone, likely by reducing increased bone turnover. It does not appear to be cytotoxic to bone cells.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Decreases serum calcium levels; peak effect typically occurs after several days of continuous infusion.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Distribution:** Steady-state plasma levels are generally achieved within 24 to 48 hours.
  - **Metabolism:** Not metabolised by the liver or kidneys.
  - **Elimination:** Significantly excreted via the kidneys. Average plasma clearance is approximately 0.15 L/hr/kg.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Comparative Study:** In a randomised double-blind trial against calcitonin (8 I.U./kg every 6 hours), gallium nitrate (200 mg/m²/day x 5 days) normalised serum calcium in 75% of patients compared to 27% in the calcitonin group (p=0.0016).
- **Duration of Effect:** The median duration of normocalcemia was 7.5 days for gallium nitrate vs. 1 day for calcitonin.
- **Dose-Response:** In open-label studies, 200 mg/m² was more effective (83% response) than 100 mg/m² (50% response) at normalising calcium levels.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store at controlled room temperature 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Diluted Solution:** 
  - Stable for 48 hours at room temperature (15°C to 30°C).
  - Stable for 7 days if stored under refrigeration (2°C to 8°C).
- **Handling:** Solution contains no preservative; discard any unused portion. Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.

- **Legal Classification:** **Rx-only** [United States].
