# Editorial Product Information: Griseofulvin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of griseofulvin for healthcare professionals. It aims to consolidate clinical data into a concise, structured format for quick reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidance required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Griseofulvin (fine particle).
- **Chemical Class:** Antifungal antibiotic derived from *Penicillium griseofulvum*.
- **Brand Names:** Grisovin [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** D01BA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Tablets: 125 mg (round, white to off-white, bevel-edged, engraved with "G125") [Australian].
    - Tablets: 500 mg (round, white to off-white, biconvex, engraved with "G500") [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aspen Pharmacare Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Dermatophyte Infections:** Treatment of fungal infections of the skin, scalp, hair, or nails caused by species of *Epidermophyton*, *Microsporum*, and *Trichophyton* [Australian].
- **Clinical Prerequisite:** Indicated only where topical therapy is considered inappropriate or has failed [Australian].
- **Note:** Griseofulvin is ineffective against *Candida* (monilia), *Malassezia* (tinea versicolor), or deep-seated mycoses [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Standard Dose:** 500 mg daily [Australian].
- **Indication-Specific (Nail Infections):** May require up to 1000 mg daily until a clinical response is observed, followed by a reduction to 500 mg daily [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing
- **Standard Dose:** 10 mg/kg body weight daily, administered in divided doses [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Food Interaction:** Tablets must be taken after meals. Absorption is significantly increased when taken with a high-fat meal; inadequate absorption may occur if taken on an empty stomach [Australian].
- **Duration of Therapy:**
    - Hair or Skin: At least 4 weeks.
    - Toenails or Fingernails: 6 to 12 months.
    - General Rule: Continue therapy for at least 2 weeks after all clinical signs of infection have resolved [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to griseofulvin or any tablet excipients [Australian].
- **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated during pregnancy and in women intending to become pregnant within one month of stopping treatment [Australian].
- **Reproductive Health (Men):** Men should not father children within six months of treatment due to risk of aneuploidy [Australian].
- **Porphyria:** Contraindicated in established porphyria [Australian].
- **Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE):** Contraindicated in patients with SLE [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Severe liver disease or hepatocellular failure [Australian].
- **Prophylaxis:** Prophylactic use is contraindicated [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Contraceptive Failure:** Griseofulvin reduces the effectiveness of oral contraceptives. Non-hormonal contraceptive measures must be used during treatment and for one month after cessation [Australian].
- **Photosensitivity:** Patients should avoid intense natural or artificial sunlight to prevent photosensitivity reactions [Australian].
- **Lupus Risk:** Rare reports of SLE precipitation, exacerbation, or lupus-like syndromes [Australian].
- **Penicillin Cross-Sensitivity:** While the drug is derived from *Penicillium*, cross-sensitivity with penicillin has not yet been clinically observed; however, caution is advised [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Periodic monitoring of renal, hepatic, and haematopoietic function is required during prolonged therapy [Australian].
- **Infection Control:** Hygienic measures should be used to prevent re-infection; concurrent topical fungicides may help prevent the spread of infective material [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (up to 15%):** Headache (may be severe, often disappears with continued therapy) [Australian].
- **Nervous System:** Peripheral neuritis, lethargy, mental confusion, vertigo, fatigue, and drowsiness. Rare cases of impaired performance of routine tasks [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, heartburn, flatulence, thirst, oral thrush, and gastrointestinal bleeding [Australian].
- **Haematological:** Leukopenia and neutropenia (often transient). Therapy should be discontinued if granulocytopenia occurs [Australian].
- **Dermatological:** Skin rashes, urticaria (cold and warm), erythema, and photosensitivity. **Serious reactions:** Toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN) and erythema multiforme have been reported [Australian].
- **Renal:** Albuminuria, proteinuria, and rare cases of nephrosis [Australian].
- **Hepatic:** Hepatotoxicity (rare) [Australian].
- **Other:** Serum-sickness syndromes, angioedema (rare), and estrogen-like effects in children [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Enzyme Inducers (e.g., Barbiturates, Phenylbutazone):** May reduce griseofulvin efficacy by interfering with absorption or inducing metabolism [Australian].
- **Coumarin Anticoagulants:** Griseofulvin may decrease the anticoagulant response; dosage adjustments of the anticoagulant may be necessary during and after therapy [Australian].
- **Oral Contraceptives:** Significant risk of breakthrough bleeding, amenorrhoea, and contraceptive failure [Australian].
- **Alcohol:** Griseofulvin may potentiate the effects of alcohol [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D [Australian]**. Griseofulvin is teratogenic in animals and suspected to cause human foetal abnormalities. It can induce aneuploidy. Strictly contraindicated [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Information regarding passage into breast milk is unavailable. Safety in infants has not been established; use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Capable of inducing aneuploidy in mammalian cells. Men must wait six months post-treatment before fathering children [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for use (10 mg/kg/day). Monitor for potential estrogen-like effects [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Data not specified in available sources [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources, though periodic monitoring of renal function is recommended [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe liver disease or hepatocellular failure [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Systemically administered griseofulvin is deposited in newly formed keratin of the skin, hair, and nails, rendering the tissue resistant to fungal invasion. As new keratin grows, the old infected keratin is shed [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Highly specific activity against dermatophytes; no antibacterial activity and no effect on gastrointestinal flora [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Variable and incomplete (<50%). Enhanced by high-fat meals and microsize/fine particle formulation [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** 84% plasma protein bound (mainly albumin); selective deposition in keratin precursor cells [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolised to 6-desmethylgriseofulvin or its glucuronide conjugate [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Excreted primarily in the urine. Terminal half-life ranges from 9.5 to 21 hours [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C in a dry place [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].
- **Disposal:** Unused medicine should be returned to a pharmacy for disposal [Australian].
