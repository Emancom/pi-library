# Editorial Product Information: Gemcitabine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of gemcitabine for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single, structured reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Always consult the current local PI before prescribing or administering this medication.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Gemcitabine hydrochloride.
- **Chemical Class:** Nucleoside analogue (antimetabolite).
- **Brand Names:** DBL™ Gemcitabine Injection [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available source.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Clear, colourless to light straw-coloured solution for intravenous use. Available as 38 mg/mL in the following vial sizes: 200 mg/5.3 mL, 1 g/26.3 mL, and 2 g/52.6 mL [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Gemcitabine is indicated for the following [Australian-approved]:
- **Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC):** Treatment of locally advanced or metastatic disease.
- **Pancreatic cancer:** Treatment of locally advanced or metastatic adenocarcinoma; also indicated for 5-Fluorouracil refractory disease.
- **Bladder cancer:** Treatment of disease, used alone or in combination with cisplatin.
- **Breast cancer:** In combination with paclitaxel for unresectable, locally recurrent, or metastatic disease following relapse after adjuvant/neoadjuvant chemotherapy (which should have included an anthracycline unless contraindicated).
- **Ovarian cancer:** In combination with carboplatin for recurrent epithelial ovarian carcinoma in patients who relapsed >6 months following platinum-based therapy.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
The standard infusion time is 30 minutes. Prolongation of infusion time or increased dosing frequency increases toxicity [Australian].

### Dosing by Indication [Australian]
- **NSCLC (Single agent):** 1,000 mg/m² weekly for 3 weeks, followed by a 1-week rest (4-week cycle).
- **NSCLC (Combination with cisplatin):**
    - 3-week schedule: 1,250 mg/m² on Days 1 and 8 (Cisplatin 75–100 mg/m² on Day 1 before gemcitabine).
    - 4-week schedule: 1,000 mg/m² on Days 1, 8, and 15 (Cisplatin 75–100 mg/m² on Day 1 after gemcitabine).
- **Pancreatic Cancer:** 1,000 mg/m² weekly for up to 7 weeks, followed by 1 week of rest. Subsequent cycles: weekly for 3 weeks out of every 4.
- **Bladder Cancer:**
    - Single agent: 1,250 mg/m² on Days 1, 8, and 15 of a 28-day cycle.
    - Combination: 1,000 mg/m² on Days 1, 8, and 15 (Cisplatin 70 mg/m² on Day 1 or 2).
- **Breast Cancer:** Paclitaxel (175 mg/m² Day 1 over 3 hours) followed by gemcitabine (1,250 mg/m² on Days 1 and 8) of a 21-day cycle.
- **Ovarian Cancer:** Gemcitabine (1,000 mg/m² on Days 1 and 8) followed by carboplatin (AUC 4.0 on Day 1) of a 21-day cycle.

### Administration Instructions
- May be administered neat or diluted with 0.9% Sodium Chloride [Australian].
- Inspect visually for particulates or discolouration prior to use.
- Do not mix with other medicines.

### Dosage Adjustments for Haematological Toxicity
Dose modifications for subsequent doses within a cycle or at the start of a new cycle are required based on absolute granulocyte and platelet counts. 
- **Full dose (100%):** Requires Granulocytes >1.0–1.5 x 10⁹/L and Platelets >75–100 x 10⁹/L depending on the regimen.
- **Dose Reduction (50–75%):** Required for moderate cytopenia.
- **Omission:** Doses should be held if Granulocytes <0.5–1.0 x 10⁹/L or Platelets <50–75 x 10⁹/L (thresholds vary by regimen) [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to gemcitabine or any excipients [Australian].
- Pregnancy [Australian].
- Breastfeeding [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in provided source.
- **Myelosuppression:** Gemcitabine suppresses bone marrow; leucopenia, thrombocytopenia, and anaemia are expected. Use with caution in patients with compromised marrow function.
- **Pulmonary Toxicity:** Severe effects including pulmonary oedema, interstitial pneumonitis, and Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS) have been reported. Permanent discontinuation is required if these occur [Australian].
- **Vascular/Renal Toxicity:** Cases of Haemolytic Uraemic Syndrome (HUS), Thrombotic Microangiopathy (TMA), and Capillary Leak Syndrome (CLS) have been reported.
- **Neurological:** Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES) has been reported and may be severe.
- **Dermatological:** Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs), including SJS, TEN, and AGEP, have been reported. Treatment must be withdrawn if these appear [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Periodic monitoring of liver and kidney function, as well as frequent blood counts, is essential.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequencies [Australian]
- **Very Common (>10%):** Nausea and vomiting; raised AST/ALT/Alkaline Phosphatase (~60%); proteinuria and haematuria (~50%); dyspnoea (10–40%); allergic skin rashes (~25%); anaemia, leucopenia, and thrombocytopenia; oedema/peripheral oedema; influenza-like symptoms (fever, headache, back pain, anorexia).
- **Common (1–10%):** Febrile neutropenia; diarrhoea; constipation; stomatitis; alopecia; pruritus; somnolence.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Interstitial pneumonitis; bronchospasm; pulmonary oedema.
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Myocardial infarction; heart failure; arrhythmia; hypotension; gangrene; adult respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS); skin scaling/vesiculation.
- **Very Rare (<0.01%):** Anaphylactoid reactions; PRES; Capillary Leak Syndrome; facial oedema; SJS/TEN.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Radiotherapy:** Concurrent use with radiotherapy is associated with significant, potentially life-threatening toxicity (oesophagitis and pneumonitis). The optimum safe regimen for concurrent use is not established [Australian].
- **Vaccinations:** Live vaccines (e.g., yellow fever) are not recommended due to the risk of systemic, potentially fatal disease in immunosuppressed patients [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** No significant pharmacokinetic interactions were observed when gemcitabine was combined with paclitaxel, cisplatin, or carboplatin [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Gemcitabine is suspected to cause human foetal malformations or irreversible damage. It must not be used during pregnancy.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. It is unknown if gemcitabine is excreted in human milk; use should be avoided due to potential hazards to the infant [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Gemcitabine caused hypospermatogenesis in animal studies. Men should be advised not to father a child during and for a period after treatment [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in children; data from limited Phase 1 and 2 trials are insufficient to support use [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Generally well tolerated in patients >65 years. No specific dose adjustments are required, though clearance and half-life are affected by age [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution. There is insufficient information from clinical studies to provide a clear dose recommendation. Renal failure and HUS have been reported [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution. Patients with liver metastases or history of hepatitis/alcoholism may experience exacerbated hepatic insufficiency. Dose reduction is recommended in patients with elevated bilirubin [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Gemcitabine (dFdC) is a pyrimidine antimetabolite. It is metabolised intracellularly to active diphosphate (dFdCDP) and triphosphate (dFdCTP) nucleosides. dFdCDP inhibits ribonucleotide reductase, reducing deoxynucleotide pools. dFdCTP competes with dCTP for incorporation into DNA, leading to "masked chain termination" and apoptosis [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is 12.4 L/m² (women) to 17.5 L/m² (men). Plasma protein binding is negligible [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly metabolised by cytidine deaminase in the liver, kidney, and blood to the inactive metabolite dFdU.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (92–98% recovered in urine within one week). Half-life ranges from 42 to 94 minutes depending on age and gender [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **NSCLC:** Phase III trials showed superior median survival (9.1 vs 7.6 months) and response rates (30.4% vs 11.1%) for gemcitabine plus cisplatin compared to cisplatin alone [Australian].
- **Pancreatic Cancer:** Gemcitabine showed a statistically significant increase in "clinical benefit response" (a composite of pain, performance status, and weight) compared to 5-FU (23.8% vs 4.8%) [Australian].
- **Breast Cancer:** Combination with paclitaxel showed a significant improvement in median survival (18.6 vs 15.8 months) compared to paclitaxel monotherapy [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store at 2°C to 8°C. Refrigerate; do not freeze [Australian].
- **Reconstituted/Diluted:** Chemically stable for 24 hours at 2°C to 8°C or room temperature (15°C to 30°C). To reduce microbiological hazard, use within 6 hours of preparation [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
