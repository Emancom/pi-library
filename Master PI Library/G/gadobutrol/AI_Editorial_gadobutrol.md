# Unified Product Information: Gadobutrol

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
*   **Product Name:** Gadovist 1.0 [TGA] / Gadavist [FDA]
*   **Active Ingredient:** Gadobutrol
*   **Concentration:** 1.0 mmol/mL (equivalent to 604.72 mg/mL)
*   **Formulation:** A sterile, clear, colorless to pale yellow, non-ionic, paramagnetic macrocyclic gadolinium-based contrast agent (GBCA) for intravenous injection. It contains no antimicrobial preservatives.

## 2. Approved Indications
Gadobutrol is a diagnostic contrast agent indicated for use with Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) in adults and pediatric patients, including term newborns, for:

*   **Central Nervous System (CNS):** Detection and visualization of areas with disrupted blood-brain barrier and/or abnormal vascularity in the brain and spine.
*   **Whole Body MRI:** Contrast enhancement in the head, neck, thoracic space, breast, abdomen (pancreas, liver, spleen), pelvis (prostate, bladder, uterus), retroperitoneal space (kidney), extremities, and musculoskeletal system [TGA].
*   **Malignant Breast Disease:** Assessment of the presence and extent of known or suspected breast cancer.
*   **Magnetic Resonance Angiography (CE-MRA):** Evaluation of known or suspected supra-aortic or renal artery disease.
*   **Cardiac MRI (CMRI):** Assessment of myocardial perfusion (under rest and pharmacological stress) and late gadolinium enhancement in patients with known or suspected coronary artery disease (CAD).
*   **Cerebral Perfusion:** Use in first-pass MRI studies to evaluate cerebral blood flow [TGA].

## 3. Contraindications
Gadobutrol is strictly contraindicated in patients with a history of severe hypersensitivity reactions to the drug or any of its excipients (including calcobutrol sodium, trometamol, and hydrochloric acid).

## 4. Warnings and Precautions

### Nephrogenic Systemic Fibrosis (NSF)
GBCAs increase the risk of NSF in patients with impaired elimination. NSF is a debilitating, potentially fatal disease affecting the skin, muscle, and internal organs.
*   **High-Risk Patients:** Those with acute or chronic severe renal insufficiency (GFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m²) or acute renal failure due to hepato-renal syndrome or the perioperative liver transplant period.
*   **Screening:** All patients must be screened for renal dysfunction via medical history and/or laboratory testing prior to administration.
*   **Dosing:** Do not exceed recommended doses and allow sufficient time (at least 7 days in neonates [TGA]) for elimination before re-administration.

### Intrathecal Use
**Gadobutrol is not approved for intrathecal administration.** Inadvertent intrathecal injection of GBCAs has resulted in serious adverse reactions, including death, coma, encephalopathy, and seizures [FDA].

### Hypersensitivity Reactions
Anaphylactoid and other idiosyncratic reactions may occur, ranging from mild cutaneous manifestations to severe shock and cardiac arrest.
*   Risks are higher in patients with a history of asthma, allergic disorders, or previous reactions to contrast media.
*   Patients on beta-blockers may be resistant to standard treatment with beta-agonists.
*   Observation for at least 30 minutes post-injection is recommended.

### Gadolinium Retention
Gadolinium may be retained for months or years in the brain, bone, skin, and other organs. While the clinical significance in patients with normal renal function is unknown, macrocyclic agents like gadobutrol generally result in lower retention than linear agents. Minimize repetitive imaging where possible [FDA].

### Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS)
ARDS has been reported following administration, characterized by severe hypoxemia. Onset may occur within 30 minutes to 24 hours. Monitor respiratory function closely in patients demonstrating distress.

## 5. Dosage and Administration

### Standard Dosage
The recommended dose for adults and pediatric patients (including term neonates) is **0.1 mmol/kg body weight** (equivalent to 0.1 mL/kg). The lowest effective dose should be utilized.

### Specific Indications and Rates
*   **CNS Imaging:** Administered at a rate of approximately 2 mL/second.
*   **Cardiac MRI:** The dose is typically divided into two separate 0.05 mmol/kg injections—one during pharmacological stress and one at rest [FDA].
*   **CE-MRA:**
    *   *One field of view:* 7.5 mL for weight < 75 kg; 10 mL for weight ≥ 75 kg.
    *   *Multiple fields of view:* 15 mL for weight < 75 kg; 20 mL for weight ≥ 75 kg [TGA].
*   **Cerebral Perfusion:** A rate of 5 mL/second using a power injector is recommended for gradient echo sequences [TGA].

### Administration Instructions
*   Gadobutrol should be drawn into the syringe or prepared for injection immediately before use.
*   If using an Imaging Bulk Package (IBP), it must be used with an automated injection system and discarded within 24 hours of initial puncture.
*   A 0.9% sodium chloride flush should follow the injection to ensure complete delivery of the contrast bolus.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The overall safety profile is based on clinical trials involving over 6,300 patients [TGA] to 7,713 patients [FDA].

*   **Common (≥ 0.5% to < 10%):** Headache, nausea, and dizziness.
*   **Uncommon (≥ 0.1% to < 0.5%):** Dysgeusia, vomiting, injection site reactions (pain, warmth, erythema), rash, pruritus, and feeling hot.
*   **Rare/Post-marketing:** Cardiac arrest, seizures, loss of consciousness, pulmonary edema, NSF, and acute pancreatitis (within 48 hours of administration) [FDA].

## 7. Drug Interactions
No formal drug interaction studies have been conducted.
*   **Beta-blockers:** May increase the severity of hypersensitivity reactions and reduce the efficacy of epinephrine/adrenaline treatment [TGA].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

### Pregnancy
*   **Category B3 [TGA].** GBCAs cross the placenta. Animal studies have shown embryolethality and retarded development at doses 8–12 times the human equivalent.
*   **Clinical Advice:** Use only if imaging is essential and cannot be delayed. The potential for fetal gadolinium retention should be considered [FDA].

### Breastfeeding
*   **Lactation:** Gadobutrol is excreted in breast milk in very small amounts (less than 0.1% of the maternal dose). GI absorption by the infant is poor.
*   **Clinical Advice:** At clinical doses, no effects on the infant are anticipated; breastfeeding may be continued.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** Rapidly distributed into the extracellular space; protein binding is negligible.
*   **Metabolism:** Gadobutrol is not metabolized in the body.
*   **Elimination:** Excreted unchanged via the kidneys through glomerular filtration. In healthy subjects, the mean terminal half-life is approximately 1.8 hours.
*   **Renal Impairment:** Elimination is significantly prolonged. The half-life increases to approximately 5.8 hours in mild-to-moderate impairment and up to 17.6 hours in severe impairment not on dialysis.
*   **Dialysis:** Gadobutrol can be removed by hemodialysis. Approximately 98% of the agent is removed after three sessions.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Gadobutrol is a paramagnetic molecule that develops a magnetic moment when placed in a magnetic field. This moment alters the relaxation rates of water protons in the vicinity. In T1-weighted sequences, this leads to an increase in signal intensity (brightness), enhancing the contrast of tissues and lesions. Its high thermodynamic stability (log K = 21.8) ensures the gadolinium ion remains securely chelated within the macrocyclic ligand.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store below 30°C [TGA] or at 25°C with excursions permitted to 15–30°C [FDA].
*   **Post-Opening:** Once the container closure is pierced or the prefilled syringe is prepared, the solution remains stable for 24 hours at 20–25°C. Any unused portion must be discarded after this period.
*   **Visual Inspection:** Solution should be inspected for particulate matter or discoloration. If solidification occurs due to cold, the product should be brought to room temperature before use.
