## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Gentian Violet (1%)
- **Chemical Class:** Triphenylmethane dye; antiseptic/anti-infective.
- **Brand Name(s):** 
    - **GNP Gentian Violet 1%** [United States]
- **ATC Code:** D01AE02 (Gentian violet)
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):**
    - **Topical Solution:** 1% (10 mg/mL) [United States]
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** 
    - AmerisourceBergen (Labeler); Pharma Nobis, LLC (Manufacturer) [United States]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **First Aid Antiseptic:** To help protect against infection in minor cuts, scrapes, and burns [United States].
- **Anti-infective:** For the treatment of abrasions, minor cuts, surface injuries, and superficial fungal infections of the skin [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Application:** Clean the affected area prior to use. Apply the solution directly to the wound or via a cotton applicator.
- **Frequency:** One to three times daily [United States]. 
- **Instructions:** 
    - Do not bandage the treated area following application [United States].
    - For external use only.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Ocular Exposure:** Do not use in or near the eyes [United States].
- **Extensive Application:** Do not apply over large areas of the body [United States].
- **Ulcerative Lesions:** Contraindicated for use on ulcerative lesions, as this may result in permanent "tattooing" of the skin [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **External Use Only:** Not for oral or mucosal ingestion.
- **Wound Complexity:** Patients should consult a physician prior to use for deep or puncture wounds, serious burns, or animal bites [United States].
- **Staining:** The solution will stain skin and clothing [United States].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Treatment Duration:** Discontinue use and consult a healthcare provider if the condition persists for longer than one week unless directed otherwise [United States].
- **Hypersensitivity/Irritation:** Stop use if redness, irritation, swelling, or pain increases, or if signs of infection occur [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Dermatological:** 
    - **Common:** Staining of the skin (expected pharmacological effect of the dye) [United States].
    - **Serious:** Permanent skin "tattooing" if applied to ulcerative or necrotising lesions [United States].
- **General:** Irritation, redness, or swelling at the site of application [United States].
