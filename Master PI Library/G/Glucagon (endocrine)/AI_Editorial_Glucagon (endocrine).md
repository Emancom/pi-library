# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The jurisdictions contributing to this editorial include: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in specific jurisdictions.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injection, solution; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Metabolic Disorders:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism which result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg given as a slow (2 to 3 minute) bolus injection or by infusion. 
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
### Administration Instructions
- **Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Hemodialysis:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations before therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine levels between 35 and 60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Visual Inspection:** Parenteral products should be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
### Titration
- **ESRD:** Dose adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** No contraindications are currently listed in the available source, though hypersensitivity warnings apply (see Section 5) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of IV administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and initiate medical treatment [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with pre-existing conditions, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been noted [US].
- **Oral vs. IV Precautions:** While this document covers the IV form, it is noted that chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. 
- **Management:** It is recommended that INR levels be monitored after initiating levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specifically lettered]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after exogenous administration. In humans, the risks of excess carnitine intake to the child must be weighed against the benefit of supplementation to the mother. Discontinuation of nursing or treatment may be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models at 3.8x human dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders (50 mg/kg) applies to paediatric patients. In premature infants and newborns, secondary deficiency is defined by plasma levels below age-related norms [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources; however, general monitoring of renal function is advised in elderly populations due to the drug's renal clearance.
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. For IV use in ESRD, see Section 3. Accumulation of TMA/TMAO metabolites is a concern with chronic high-dose oral administration [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, it alleviates metabolic abnormalities caused by the accumulation of toxic organic acids and disrupts intermediary metabolism.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and [3H]-γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US]. Total body clearance is approximately 4.00 L/h.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Trials:** In two double-blind, placebo-controlled trials, levocarnitine increased plasma carnitine concentrations. In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg post-dialysis), mean pre-dialysis concentrations rose from ~20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While the increase in concentrations is proven, the effect on specific clinical outcomes in ESRD remains to be determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL concentration) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
- **Single-use:** Discard any unused portion; the formulation contains no preservative [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources (US system uses DEA schedules, and this is not a controlled substance).
