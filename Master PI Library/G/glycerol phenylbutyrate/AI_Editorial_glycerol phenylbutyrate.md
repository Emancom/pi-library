# Editorial Product Information: Glycerol Phenylbutyrate

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory guidance and clinical data into a concise, structured format.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Glycerol phenylbutyrate (a nitrogen-binding agent).
- **Brand Names:** Noted generically as Glycerol Phenylbutyrate Oral Liquid [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Oral liquid, 1.1 g/mL (delivers 1.02 g/mL of phenylbutyrate) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral; may be administered via nasogastric (NG) or gastrostomy (G) tube.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Navinta LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Urea Cycle Disorders (UCDs):** Indicated for the chronic management of adult and paediatric patients with UCDs who cannot be managed by dietary protein restriction and/or amino acid supplementation alone [US].
- **Adjuvant Therapy:** Must be used in conjunction with dietary protein restriction and, where necessary, dietary supplements (e.g., essential amino acids, arginine, citrulline).
- **Limitations of Use:**
    - Not indicated for the treatment of acute hyperammonemia [US].
    - Safety and efficacy in N-acetylglutamate synthase (NAGS) deficiency have not been established [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 General Dosing
- **Prescriber Requirements:** Should be prescribed by a physician experienced in managing UCDs.
- **Maximum Daily Dose:** 17.5 mL (19 g) [US].
- **Administration Frequency:** 
    - **$\ge$ 2 years of age:** Total daily dose divided into 3 equal portions, rounded to the nearest 0.5 mL.
    - **< 2 years of age:** Total daily dose divided into 3 or more portions, rounded to the nearest 0.1 mL.

### 3.2 Specific Dosing Schedules
- **Initial Dosage (Phenylbutyrate-Naïve Patients):** 4.5 to 11.2 mL/m²/day (5 to 12.4 g/m²/day). Starting dose should consider residual enzyme activity, protein intake, and dietary adherence.
- **Switching from Sodium Phenylbutyrate:** Patients should receive an equivalent amount of phenylbutyric acid.
    - **From Tablets:** Sodium phenylbutyrate (g) x 0.86 = Glycerol phenylbutyrate (mL).
    - **From Powder:** Sodium phenylbutyrate (g) x 0.81 = Glycerol phenylbutyrate (mL).

### 3.3 Administration Instructions
- Administer with food or formula directly into the mouth via oral syringe. For infants, administer just prior to breastfeeding.
- **Tube Administration:** Utilize a new dry oral syringe for each dose. Flush the NG or G-tube with 10 mL of water or formula after administration.
- **Stability:** Discard the bottle 28 days after opening. If the liquid becomes cloudy due to moisture entry, discard immediately.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to phenylbutyrate [US]. Symptoms may include wheezing, dyspnoea, hypotension, and rash.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Neurotoxicity:** Phenylacetate (PAA), the active metabolite, may cause reversible neurotoxicity (e.g., somnolence, fatigue, headache, dysgeusia) at concentrations >500 µg/mL. Dosage reduction is required if symptoms appear in the absence of high ammonia.
- **Pancreatic Insufficiency/Malabsorption:** As the drug requires pancreatic lipases for hydrolysis, patients with impaired exocrine pancreatic function or intestinal malabsorption may have reduced ammonia control. Monitor ammonia levels closely.
- **Monitoring Requirements:**
    - Plasma ammonia levels for titration and ongoing management.
    - Fasting ammonia (in patients $\ge$ 6 years) should ideally be <0.5 x Upper Limit of Normal (ULN).
    - PAA and PAA:PAGN (phenylacetylglutamine) ratios may be used to guide dosing, especially if neurotoxicity is suspected.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### 6.1 Clinical Trial Experience
| Population | Common Adverse Reactions ($\ge$ 10%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Adults** | Diarrhoea, flatulence, headache, nausea, vomiting, decreased appetite, dizziness, fatigue. |
| **Paediatric (2–17 years)** | Upper abdominal pain, rash, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, decreased appetite, headache. |
| **Paediatric (< 2 years)** | Neutropenia, vomiting, constipation, diarrhoea, pyrexia, cough, rash, gastroesophageal reflux, increased hepatic enzymes. |

### 6.2 Serious and Postmarketing Effects
- **Serious:** Neurotoxicity, hyperammonemic crises (associated with the underlying condition), neutropenia (in infants).
- **Postmarketing:** Abnormal body odor, retching/gagging, burning sensation in the mouth [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### 7.1 Interactions Affecting Ammonia Levels
- **Corticosteroids:** May increase protein catabolism, raising ammonia levels.
- **Valproic Acid and Haloperidol:** May induce hyperammonemia.

### 7.2 Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Probenecid:** May inhibit renal excretion of metabolites (PAA and PAGN).
- **CYP3A4 Substrates:** Glycerol phenylbutyrate is a weak inducer of CYP3A4. It may decrease exposure to substrates with a narrow therapeutic index (e.g., alfentanil, quinidine, cyclosporine) and has been shown to decrease midazolam exposure by approximately 32% [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not specified; current FDA labeling uses descriptive risk summary]. Limited data in humans. Animal studies showed maternal toxicity and skeletal variations at high doses (approx. 5.7 times the human dose).
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. There is a potential risk of neurotoxicity and tumorigenicity in the breastfed infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal data showed decreased fertility (reduced pregnancy rates) at high doses [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for all age groups. Dosing is highly individualised based on body surface area (BSA) and protein intake [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Clinical studies were insufficient to determine if the elderly respond differently; start at the low end of the dosing range [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Efficacy and safety are unknown; monitor ammonia levels closely [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** In patients with moderate to severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh B and C), PAA metabolism is reduced. Start at the lower end of the dosing range (4.5 mL/m²/day) [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Glycerol phenylbutyrate is a pro-drug. Pancreatic lipases release phenylbutyrate (PBA), which undergoes $\beta$-oxidation to PAA. PAA conjugates with glutamine to form PAGN, which is excreted renally. Each mole of PAGN removes two moles of waste nitrogen.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Total 24-hour ammonia exposure (AUC) is comparable between glycerol phenylbutyrate and sodium phenylbutyrate.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** PBA is released by lipases; peak levels of PAA occur at approximately 4 hours [US].
    - **Distribution:** PBA is 81–98% protein bound; PAA is 37–66% bound.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal as PAGN (approx. 69% of dose in adults) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adult Study (Study 1):** A randomized, double-blind, crossover trial showed glycerol phenylbutyrate was non-inferior to sodium phenylbutyrate in maintaining ammonia control (mean 24-hour ammonia AUC 866 vs. 977 µmol·h/L) [US].
- **Paediatric Studies:** Multiple open-label studies confirmed safety and ammonia control across age groups from neonates to adolescents. In patients < 2 years, successful transition from other nitrogen binders was achieved within 3 days in the majority of subjects [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C.
- **Shelf Life:** Discard 28 days after opening [US].
- **Handling:** Keep the bottle tightly closed; protect from moisture to prevent cloudiness.
