# Editorial Product Information: Danaparoid Sodium

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australia (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Danaparoid sodium.
- **Chemical Class:** A mixture of low molecular weight sulfated glycosaminoglycuronans (heparan sulfate, dermatan sulfate, and a minor amount of chondroitin sulfate) derived from animal mucosa.
- **Brand Name:** Orgaran [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile, isotonic aqueous solution for injection. Supplied in 0.6 mL ampoules containing 750 anti-Factor Xa units (approximately 55 mg danaparoid sodium).
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) injection. (Note: Intravenous use is mentioned in PK and animal studies, but SC is the indicated route for administration in the provided source).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aspen Pharmacare Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Venous Thromboembolism (VTE) Prophylaxis:** Prevention of postoperative venous thromboembolism in patients undergoing general or orthopaedic surgery [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:** 750 anti-Factor Xa units administered twice daily via subcutaneous injection.
- **Duration of Therapy:** Typically 7 to 10 days. Safety has only been demonstrated for up to 10 postoperative days in human studies.
- **Surgical Timing:** It is recommended to administer the final preoperative dose 1–4 hours prior to surgery.
- **Monitoring:** Routine clotting assays (e.g., PT, aPTT) are unsuitable for monitoring danaparoid activity. If monitoring is required, a functional anti-Factor Xa test using a chromogenic peptide substrate should be used, with danaparoid sodium used as the standard. The steady-state plasma anti-Xa response is typically 0.15–0.35 units/mL.
- **Administration Instructions:** Must not be administered via the intramuscular route.

## 4. Contraindications
- Severe haemorrhagic diathesis (e.g., haemophilia, idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura).
- Haemorrhagic stroke in the acute phase.
- Uncontrollable active bleeding states.
- Hypersensitivity to danaparoid sodium or any excipients.
- Hypersensitivity to sulfites [Australian].
- History of heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT) with a positive *in vitro* aggregation test in the presence of danaparoid.
- Severe renal insufficiency.
- Severe hepatic insufficiency.
- Severe hypertension.
- Severe gastric or duodenal ulcer (unless it is the reason for the surgical intervention).
- Acute bacterial endocarditis.
- Diabetic retinopathy.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Interchangeability:** Danaparoid and Low Molecular Weight Heparins (LMWHs) are **not** interchangeable.
- **Spinal/Epidural Haematomas:** When neuraxial anaesthesia (epidural/spinal) or spinal puncture is employed, patients anticoagulated with heparinoids are at risk of developing an epidural or spinal haematoma, which can result in long-term or permanent paralysis. Risk is increased by indwelling catheters, concomitant use of NSAIDs, platelet inhibitors, or other anticoagulants, and traumatic or repeated punctures.
- **Sulfite Sensitivity:** Contains sodium sulfite. In asthmatic patients hypersensitive to sulfite, this may cause bronchospasm or anaphylactic shock.
- **Heparin-Induced Thrombocytopenia (HIT):** Danaparoid has low cross-reactivity with HIT antibodies (approximately 9% in *in vitro* tests). It may be used if cross-reactivity is ruled out by *in vitro* testing.
- **Osteoporosis:** While not reported at recommended doses, danaparoid (as a glycosaminoglucoronan) may potentially cause osteoporosis if inappropriate dosages are used.
- **Monitoring:** Platelet counts should be monitored at regular intervals.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequencies reported in clinical settings [Australian]:

| Adverse Event | Danaparoid (%) | Heparin (%) | Placebo (%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Increased bleeding** | 45% | 69% | 23% |
| **Injection site reaction/pain** | 15% | 14% | 10% |
| **General pain** | 1.6% | 1.4% | 0.4% |
| **Allergic reactions** | 0.3% | 0% | 0% |

- **Other Effects:** Bruising, skin rashes (local or generalised), and elevations in liver transaminases or alkaline phosphatase (without demonstrated clinical significance).
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Thrombocytopenia (potential, though not observed in initial reported data), and spinal/epidural haematoma.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **Platelet Inhibitors/NSAIDs:** Concomitant use with aspirin, other NSAIDs, or platelet inhibitors increases the risk of bleeding. Combination with aspirin may prolong bleeding time.
    - **Oral Anticoagulants:** May be used together, but caution is required.
    - **Corticosteroids:** Use caution due to potential ulcerogenic effects.
- **Laboratory Interactions:** Monitoring of oral anticoagulants (PT and Thrombotest) is unreliable within 5 hours following danaparoid administration.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category C:** Use in human pregnancy has only been studied incidentally. Animal studies showed reduced postnatal weight gain in rats at high doses (>100 anti-Xa units/kg/day IV), but no teratogenic effects were confirmed in rabbits. It should only be used if clearly indicated; no evidence currently suggests foetal abnormalities or exacerbated bleeding during delivery.
- **Breastfeeding:** No data are available regarding the secretion of danaparoid into breast milk.
- **Fertility:** No data available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No data available; safety and efficacy not established.
- **Elderly Use:** Clearance is not markedly reduced in the elderly; the usual dosage is recommended [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe renal insufficiency. Use with caution in moderate impairment as the elimination half-life of anti-Xa activity may be prolonged.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe hepatic insufficiency. Use with caution in moderate impairment, especially if haemostasis is impaired.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** An antithrombotic agent that inhibits thrombin generation. It has a high ratio of anti-Factor Xa to antithrombin (anti-IIa) activity (approximately 20:1). 
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Anti-Xa activity is mediated by antithrombin-III. It has negligible effects on platelet function, aggregability, and haemostatic plug formation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Bioavailability:** Approaches 100% after SC administration.
    - **Absorption:** Peak anti-Xa levels occur in 4–5 hours.
    - **Distribution/Metabolism:** The liver is not involved in its metabolism.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal. The elimination half-life for anti-Xa activity is approximately 25 hours; for thrombin generation inhibition, it is approximately 7 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Clinical studies indicate that danaparoid clinical activity may not directly correlate with the dose expressed in milligrams or anti-Xa units. In surgical trials, the incidence of bleeding was lower than with heparin and higher than with placebo.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C.
- **Protection:** Protect from light.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Medicine) [Australian].
