# Editorial Product Information: Dienogest

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonized, clinically focused overview for healthcare professionals (HCPs). 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Dienogest (a nortestosterone derivative).
- **Chemical Class:** Progestogen with antiandrogenic activity.
- **Brand Names:** Dinasane [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** G03D.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 2 mg oral tablets [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Endometriosis:** For the treatment of endometriosis [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** One 2 mg tablet daily.
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Tablets should be taken at approximately the same time each day with liquid.
    - Treatment can be initiated on any day of the menstrual cycle.
    - Administration must be continuous, without breaks, regardless of vaginal bleeding.
    - Subsequent packs should be started immediately after the previous pack is finished.
- **Starting Treatment:**
    - If transitioning from short-acting hormonal treatment: Start on the first day of menstrual bleeding following cessation of previous therapy.
    - If transitioning from long-acting (injectable) treatment: Start when the next injection would have been due (based on expected metabolism of the prior drug).
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** 
    - Efficacy may be reduced if tablets are missed. 
    - If a dose is missed, the patient should take only one tablet as soon as remembered and continue the next dose at the usual time.
    - In the event of vomiting or severe diarrhoea within 3–4 hours of ingestion, an additional tablet should be taken.

## 4. Contraindications
Dienogest is contraindicated in the presence of the following conditions:
- **Pregnancy:** Known or suspected pregnancy.
- **Lactation:** Current breastfeeding.
- **Thromboembolic Disorders:** Active venous thromboembolic (VTE) disorder.
- **Cardiovascular Disease:** Current or historical arterial and cardiovascular disease (e.g., myocardial infarction, cerebrovascular accident, ischemic heart disease).
- **Diabetes:** Diabetes mellitus with vascular involvement.
- **Hepatic Disease:** Current or historical severe hepatic disease (until liver function values return to normal).
- **Hepatic Tumours:** Current or historical liver tumours (benign or malignant).
- **Malignancy:** Known or suspected sex hormone-dependent malignancies.
- **Vaginal Bleeding:** Undiagnosed vaginal bleeding.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to dienogest or any excipients (e.g., lactose monohydrate).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Contraception:** Dienogest is not a contraceptive. If contraception is required, non-hormonal methods (e.g., barrier methods) must be used. Hormonal contraceptives must be stopped prior to initiation.
- **Bone Mineral Density (BMD):** 
    - Progestogens may moderately decrease endogenous oestrogen. 
    - In adolescents (12 to <18 years), a 1.2% mean decrease in lumbar spine BMD was observed over 12 months. 
    - Long-term fracture risk and impact on peak bone mass are unknown. 
    - Risk-benefit assessment is required for patients with risk factors for osteoporosis (e.g., low BMI, chronic corticosteroid use, metabolic bone disease).
- **Ectopic Pregnancy:** Users of progestogen-only preparations may have a higher likelihood of ectopic pregnancy compared to users of combined oral contraceptives.
- **Circulatory Disorders:** While evidence for VTE risk with progestogen-only pills is less than for combined pills, risk factors (obesity, age, surgery) should be considered. Discontinue 4 weeks before elective surgery; resume 2 weeks after full remobilisation.
- **Changes in Bleeding Pattern:** Most patients experience changes in menstrual bleeding (e.g., spotting, irregular, or prolonged bleeding). Severe bleeding may lead to anaemia; discontinuation should be considered if anaemia occurs.
- **Hepatic Effects:** Discontinue if cholestatic jaundice or pruritus occurs.
- **Depression:** Patients with a history of depression should be monitored closely; discontinue if serious depression recurs.
- **Ovarian Follicles:** Persistent functional ovarian cysts may occur and are usually asymptomatic, though they may cause pelvic pain.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are most frequent during the first months of therapy and typically subside with continued treatment.

### Common Adverse Effects (≥1/100 to <1/10)
| System Organ Class | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Nervous System** | Headache (9.0%), Migraine |
| **Psychiatric** | Depressed mood (5.1%), Sleep disturbance, Nervousness, Loss of libido |
| **Reproductive** | Breast discomfort (5.4%), Ovarian cysts (3.0%), Hot flushes, Uterine/Vaginal spotting |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea, Abdominal pain, Flatulence |
| **Skin** | Acne (5.1%), Alopecia |
| **General** | Weight increase (3.6%), Asthenic conditions, Irritability |

### Uncommon Adverse Effects (≥1/1000 to <1/100)
- **Blood:** Anaemia.
- **Metabolism:** Increased appetite, weight decrease.
- **Psychiatric:** Anxiety, mood swings.
- **Special Senses:** Dry eye, tinnitus.
- **Cardiovascular:** Palpitations, hypotension, circulatory disturbance.
- **Infection:** Urinary tract infection, vaginal candidiasis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
Dienogest is primarily metabolised by **CYP3A4**.
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** (e.g., Rifampicin, phenytoin, carbamazepine, St. John's Wort) may significantly reduce dienogest plasma concentrations. Rifampicin has been shown to decrease steady-state AUC of dienogest by 83% [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** (e.g., Ketoconazole, erythromycin, clarithromycin, grapefruit juice) may increase dienogest concentrations. Ketoconazole co-administration resulted in a 186% increase in dienogest AUC [Australian].

### Other Interactions
- **Laboratory Tests:** Progestogens may influence results of certain laboratory tests, including biochemical parameters of liver, thyroid, adrenal, and renal function.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. Contraindicated. If pregnancy occurs, the drug must be discontinued. There is no clinical requirement to treat endometriosis during pregnancy.
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. Dienogest is excreted in breast milk. A decision should be made to either discontinue breastfeeding or abstain from therapy based on the benefit-risk to the child and mother.
- **Fertility:** Ovulation is inhibited in most patients at the 2 mg dose, but it is not a reliable contraceptive. Menstrual cycles typically return to normal within 2 months of cessation.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not indicated in children prior to menarche. Efficacy is established in adolescents (12–18 years), but BMD concerns require careful monitoring.
- **Elderly Use:** No relevant indication for use in the geriatric population [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No data suggest the need for dose adjustment in patients with renal impairment.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with current or historical severe hepatic disease.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Reduces endogenous oestradiol production, thereby suppressing the trophic effects of oestradiol on ectopic and eutopic endometrium. It creates a hypoestrogenic, hypergestagenic environment, leading to decidualisation of endometrial tissue followed by atrophy of lesions.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Has a strong progestogenic effect with moderate antiandrogenic activity. Does not possess significant glucocorticoid or mineralocorticoid activity.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; 91% bioavailability. Tmax is approximately 1.5 hours.
    - **Distribution:** 90% bound to albumin; does not bind to SHBG or CBG. Vd = 40 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Completely metabolised via CYP3A4 into endocrinologically inactive metabolites.
    - **Elimination:** Excreted primarily as metabolites in the urine. Terminal half-life is 9–10 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Placebo-Controlled Study:** A 3-month study (n=102) demonstrated superiority over placebo in reducing endometriosis-associated pelvic pain (EAPP). Mean reduction in pain on a Visual Analog Scale (VAS) was 27.4 mm for dienogest vs. 12.3 mm for placebo.
- **Active-Controlled Study:** A 6-month study comparing dienogest to the GnRH analogue leuprorelin acetate (LA) demonstrated non-inferiority in pain reduction (dienogest 47.5 mm vs. LA 46.0 mm).
- **Long-term Safety:** An observational surveillance study (n=27,840) followed patients for up to 7 years, identifying a slightly increased risk of depression in dienogest users compared to other endometriosis treatments.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Conditions:** Protect from light.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].
