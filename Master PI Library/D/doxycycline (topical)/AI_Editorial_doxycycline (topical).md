# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory information into a clinically focused format.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not intended to substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Sponsor: Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - **Frequency:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (not less than every 6 hours) over the first 24 hours.
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses range from 50 mg/kg or as clinically required.
    - **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

### Dosing by Indication
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - **Adjustments:** Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations.

### Administration Instructions
- Monitor plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L), blood chemistry, and vital signs [US].
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion as the formulation lacks preservatives.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported. Most cases occurred in ESRD patients on dialysis, sometimes within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if reactions occur [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine may cause seizures in patients with or without a pre-existing seizure disorder. In patients with known seizure activity, an increase in frequency or severity has been reported [US].
- **Toxicity of Metabolites:** In patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD, chronic high-dose administration of *oral* levocarnitine may lead to the accumulation of trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are potentially toxic [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disorders.
- **Common (1–10%):** Abdominal pain, body odor, nausea, vomiting, gastritis, headache, and dizziness.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:**
    - Seizures.
    - Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity.
- **Dialysis-Related Observations (≥5% incidence in clinical trials):**
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), chest pain (12%).
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%).
    - **General:** Infection (15%), fever (7%), injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%).
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Classification:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. 
    - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows suggest exogenous carnitine increases milk concentrations. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant should be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is consistent with adult weight-based dosing (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the source document.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - Specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. 
    - Dose adjustments are required based on pre-dialysis trough levels (target 40–50 µmol/L).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with organic acidemias, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis), trough levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While levocarnitine effectively increases carnitine plasma concentrations, the effects on specific clinical outcomes in the ESRD population have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) at room temperature [US].
