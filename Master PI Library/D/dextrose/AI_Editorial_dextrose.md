# Editorial Product Information: Dextrose (50% Injection)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis was prepared based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory and clinical data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Dextrose monohydrate ($C_6H_{12}O_6 \cdot H_2O$).
- **Chemical Class:** Hexose sugar.
- **Brand Names:** Dextrose Injection (Hospira/Pfizer); Ansyr® II Plastic Syringe [US].
- **ATC Code:** B05BA03 (Carbohydrates).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Injection: 50% (25 g/50 mL), equivalent to 500 mg/mL.
    - Osmolarity: 2.53 mOsmol/mL (calculated) [US].
    - pH Range: 3.2 to 6.5.
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only. 
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Hospira, Inc. (a Pfizer company) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Insulin-induced Hypoglycemia:** Indicated for the treatment of acute hypoglycemia (e.g., hyperinsulinemia or insulin shock) in adults and pediatric patients aged 2 years and older [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Instructions
- **Route:** Strictly for intravenous infusion. Do **not** administer subcutaneously or intramuscularly.
- **Visual Inspection:** Parenteral products should be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration. Do not use if the solution is cloudy or the seal is compromised.
- **Blood Glucose Monitoring:** Obtain a baseline blood glucose level before administration. In emergency settings, treatment should proceed promptly without waiting for test results.
- **Co-administration:** Do not administer simultaneously with blood through the same infusion set to avoid pseudoagglutination of red blood cells.

### Adult and Pediatric Patients (12 Years and Older)
- **Administration:** Use a central vein or large peripheral vein whenever possible due to high osmolarity (>900 mOsm/L).
- **Recommended Dosage:** 10 to 25 grams (20 to 50 mL) administered as a single dose. 
- **Repeat Dosing:** May be repeated as clinically necessary based on patient response.
- **Infusion Rate:** Administer slowly to minimize venous irritation and reduce the risk of hyperglycemia.

### Pediatric Patients (2 to 11 Years)
- **Administration:** Must be administered via a **central vein**. Avoid peripheral administration due to the risk of venous irritation. If central access is unavailable, consider a lower dextrose concentration.
- **Dosage:** Individualised based on age, body weight, clinical status, and metabolic condition.
- **Maximum Rate:** 0.5 g/kg/hour to avoid producing hyperglycemia.

### Discontinuation
- To avoid rebound hypoglycemia upon abrupt cessation of 50% dextrose, patients should transition to 5% or 10% dextrose injections.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Intracranial or Intraspinal Hemorrhage:** Risk of worsening cerebral edema due to fluid shifts across the blood-brain barrier [US].
- **Severe Dehydration:** Potential to exacerbate hyperosmolar states [US].
- **Alcohol Withdrawal:** Risk of precipitating Wernicke encephalopathy (WE) or cardiomyopathy in thiamine-deficient patients [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to dextrose [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hyperglycemia and Hyperosmolar State:** Rapid administration can lead to significant hyperglycemia and hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state, characterized by mental confusion or loss of consciousness.
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Anaphylaxis has been reported. Discontinue immediately if signs of hypersensitivity appear.
- **Phlebitis and Thrombosis:** The solution is highly hypertonic (>900 mOsmol/L). Ensure the needle is correctly positioned within the vein lumen to prevent extravasation and subsequent tissue damage.
- **Electrolyte and Fluid Imbalance:** Prolonged use may result in electrolyte deficits (specifically potassium and phosphate) and fluid overload (including pulmonary edema).
- **Hyponatremia:** Dextrose infusion may cause hyponatremia, potentially leading to acute hyponatremic encephalopathy. Risk is higher in pediatric and geriatric patients, and those with SIADH or cardiac/pulmonary failure.
- **Aluminum Toxicity:** The product contains up to 600 mcg/L of aluminum. Prolonged administration can lead to toxic levels in patients with renal impairment or immature renal function (pediatrics <2 years).

## 6. Adverse Effects
Frequency data is not reliably established from postmarketing reports; however, the following reactions are documented:
- **Metabolic:** Hyperglycemia, hyperosmolar state, hyponatremia, electrolyte imbalance (hypokalemia, hypophosphatemia).
- **Injection Site:** Phlebitis, vein thrombosis, pain, erythema, blistering, extravasation, and tissue damage.
- **Immune System:** Anaphylaxis, angioedema, bronchospasm, rash, pruritus, pyrexia, and chills.
- **Cardiovascular:** Cyanosis, volume overload, hypotension.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Glycemic/Electrolyte Modulators:** Monitor patients closely when co-administering drugs that affect glycemic control or fluid/electrolyte balance.
- **Drugs Associated with Hyponatremia:** Avoid use with diuretics or drugs that cause SIADH (e.g., SSRIs, SNRIs, certain antiepileptics, and cytotoxic agents) as they increase the risk of hyponatremia.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not assigned a letter category in current US labeling.
    - **Clinical Considerations:** Not expected to cause malformations. However, maternal hyperglycemia during delivery can cause neonatal hypoglycemia. Animal reproduction studies have not been performed.
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on dextrose levels in human milk or effects on the infant. The clinical need for the mother should be weighed against potential risks.
- **Fertility:** Data not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - Indicated for $\ge$ 2 years old. 
    - **Contraindicated in neonates and children < 2 years** due to aluminum toxicity risks and the danger of intracranial hemorrhage in low birth weight infants [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Start at the low end of the dosing range. Increased risk of hyponatremia and fluid overload; caution is required due to the higher prevalence of renal impairment.
- **Renal Impairment:** Increased risk of aluminum toxicity and electrolyte imbalances. Closely monitor patients with renal failure.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Directly restores blood glucose levels and provides carbohydrate calories (3.4 kcal/gram).
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The exposure-response relationship is not fully characterized [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Dextrose is metabolized via oxidation to carbon dioxide and water.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Pivotal clinical trial data for 50% dextrose injection for acute hypoglycemia was not provided in the source documentation, as the initial US approval dates back to 1940.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Container:** Single-dose containers; discard any unused portion.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription Only). Initial approval 1940.
