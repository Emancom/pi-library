# Editorial Product Information: Dicyclomine Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- **Jurisdictions included in this editorial:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised clinical overview of dicyclomine hydrochloride, synthesised for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the United States (FDA) PI was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Dicyclomine hydrochloride (an antispasmodic and anticholinergic/antimuscarinic agent).
- **Brand Names:** Distributed by Bryant Ranch Prepack; manufactured by Lannett Company, Inc. [US].
- **ATC Code:** A03AA07 (Not specified in available source, but commonly referenced).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Capsules: 10 mg [US].
    - Tablets: 20 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Lannett Company, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Functional Bowel/Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS):** Indicated for the treatment of patients with functional bowel disorders or irritable bowel syndrome [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:**
    - **Initial Dose:** 20 mg four times daily [US].
    - **Titration:** After one week of the initial dose, the dosage may be increased to 40 mg four times daily, provided the patient tolerates the side effects [US].
- **Discontinuation Criteria:** Treatment should be ceased if clinical efficacy is not achieved within two weeks or if side effects necessitate a dosage lower than 80 mg per day [US].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, patients are advised to resume their normal schedule without doubling the next dose [US].
- **Safety Note:** Safety data for doses exceeding 80 mg daily are not available for durations longer than two weeks [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Age:** Infants under 6 months of age [US].
- **Lactation:** Nursing mothers [US].
- **Gastrointestinal Disorders:** 
    - Obstructive disease of the gastrointestinal tract [US].
    - Severe ulcerative colitis [US].
- **Urinary Disorders:** Obstructive uropathy [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Unstable cardiovascular status in the setting of acute haemorrhage [US].
- **Other Conditions:**
    - Glaucoma [US].
    - Myasthenia gravis [US].
    - Reflux esophagitis [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** Use with caution in patients with tachyarrhythmias (e.g., thyrotoxicosis, CHF, or post-cardiac surgery), coronary heart disease, and hypertension, as the drug may accelerate heart rate and worsen ischemia or infarction [US].
- **Central Nervous System (CNS) & Peripheral Effects:**
    - **Heat Prostration:** Due to decreased sweating, fever and heat stroke can occur in high environmental temperatures. Discontinue use if symptoms occur [US].
    - **Psychosis and Delirium:** Reported in sensitive individuals (elderly or those with mental illness). Symptoms typically resolve within 12–24 hours of discontinuation [US].
    - **Mental Alertness:** May cause drowsiness, dizziness, or blurred vision. Patients should avoid operating heavy machinery or driving if affected [US].
- **Myasthenia Gravis:** Overdose may cause neuromuscular blockade leading to paralysis. Use only to mitigate muscarinic effects of anticholinesterases [US].
- **Intestinal Obstruction:** Diarrhea may be an early sign of incomplete obstruction (especially in patients with ileostomies/colostomies); treatment in this context is inappropriate and potentially fatal [US].
- **Toxic Megacolon:** Risk of intestinal perforation/dilatation in patients with Salmonella dysentery or severe ulcerative colitis [US].
- **Prostatic Hypertrophy:** Caution is required as use may lead to urinary retention [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are primarily dose-related extensions of the drug's pharmacological action on muscarinic receptors.

| Frequency | Adverse Event [US] |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Dizziness (40%), Dry mouth (33%), Blurred vision (27%), Nausea (14%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Somnolence (9%), Asthenia (7%), Nervousness (6%) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Cardiovascular symptoms (tachyarrhythmias, palpitations) and severe CNS symptoms (psychosis, delirium, hallucinations, syncope) [US].
- **Post-marketing Experience:** 
    - **Immune:** Anaphylactic shock, angioedema, face edema [US].
    - **Psychiatric:** Amnesia, agitation, confusion, mania, mood alterations [US].
    - **Integumentary:** Allergic dermatitis, erythema, rash [US].
    - **Respiratory:** Dyspnoea, nasal congestion, apnea [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Antiglaucoma Agents:** Anticholinergics antagonize these agents; use in glaucoma may increase intraocular pressure [US].
- **Other Anticholinergics:** Additive effects/toxicity may occur with amantadine, Class I antiarrhythmics (e.g., quinidine), antihistamines, antipsychotics, benzodiazepines, MAOIs, narcotic analgesics, nitrates, and tricyclic antidepressants [US].
- **Antacids:** May interfere with the absorption of dicyclomine; simultaneous use should be avoided [US].
- **Digoxin:** Gastrointestinal motility changes may increase serum concentrations of slowly dissolving digoxin formulations [US].
- **GI Motility Drugs:** Interactions with agents like metoclopramide may antagonize effects on motility [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category B [US]:** No evidence of teratogenicity in animal studies at 33 times the human dose. Epidemiological studies at doses up to 40 mg/day in the first trimester showed no increased risk of malformations. Use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Dicyclomine is excreted in human milk and poses a risk of serious adverse reactions in infants [US].
- **Fertility:** Rat studies at 100 mg/kg/day showed no deleterious effects on breeding or conception [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - Contraindicated in infants <6 months [US]. 
    - Safety and efficacy not established in older children. Serious respiratory symptoms (apnea, dyspnea), seizures, and coma have been reported in infants, though no causal relationship is confirmed [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased susceptibility to adverse events (e.g., psychosis). Use caution, starting at the low end of the dosing range, and monitor renal function [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specifically studied, but as the drug is primarily excreted renally, use with caution in patients with significant impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - Dual effect: (1) Specific anticholinergic (antimuscarinic) action at acetylcholine-receptor sites (approx. 1/8th the potency of atropine). (2) Direct musculotropic effect on GI smooth muscle (antagonizing bradykinin and histamine) [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Inhibits secretions (saliva, sweat, GI), decreases GI motility, dilates pupils, and increases heart rate [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; peak values within 60–90 minutes [US].
    - **Distribution:** Extensive (Vd: 3.65 L/kg) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily urinary (79.5%). Plasma half-life is approx. 1.8 hours, followed by a secondary elimination phase [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **IBS Efficacy:** In controlled trials, 82% of patients treated with 160 mg daily (40 mg QID) showed a favorable clinical response compared to 55% in the placebo group (p<0.05) [US].
- **Tolerability:** Side effects occurred in 61% of patients at the 160 mg/day dose. Approximately 9% discontinued treatment due to side effects [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container with a child-resistant closure. Protect from light [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** Rx-only (Prescription Only) [US].
