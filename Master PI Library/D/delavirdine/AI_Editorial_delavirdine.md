# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single, concise reference.
- **Prescriber Notice:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing in any particular region. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (also known as L-carnitine).
- **Chemical Class:** A naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism; chemically designated as (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Intravenous Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **ESRD-Associated Carnitine Deficiency:** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease (ESRD) undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - **Loading Dose:** Often utilised in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the next 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours.
    - **Maximum Dose:** Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** 
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., reduction to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Monitoring
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels prior to therapy.
- Conduct weekly and monthly monitoring, including blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** While the source states "None known," serious hypersensitivity reactions have been reported; use with caution in patients with a history of allergy to carnitine components [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of injection. Discontinue immediately if a reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine may increase the frequency or severity of seizures in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a prior history [US].
- **Oral Formulation in Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been established in patients with renal insufficiency. High doses of oral carnitine in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects listed were observed in clinical trials involving patients on chronic hemodialysis.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), hypertension (20%), headache (22%), pharyngitis (23%), diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8-12%), dizziness (13%), dyspnea (7-19%) [US]. |
| **Common (1-10%)** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, allergic reaction, asthenia, fever, flu syndrome, infection, arrhythmia, tachycardia, hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, depression, pruritus, rash [US]. |

**Serious Adverse Effects:**
- Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity.
- Seizures.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels frequently after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Classification:** Not assigned a letter grade in the provided text, but animal studies (rats/rabbits) at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. 
    - **Recommendation:** Should be used during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Although carnitine is a normal component of human milk, the risks of excess intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits to the mother. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug should be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is weight-based (mg/kg) and applicable to paediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** The injection is indicated for carnitine deficiency secondary to dialysis in ESRD. Oral formulations should be avoided in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine levels from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging:** A study of doses (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) showed dose-dependent increases in trough concentrations, with the 40 mg/kg group reaching 456 ± 162 µmol/L at 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Cautions:** Protect from freezing and avoid excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].
- **Stability in IV Fluids:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **US:** Classified as Rx-only.
