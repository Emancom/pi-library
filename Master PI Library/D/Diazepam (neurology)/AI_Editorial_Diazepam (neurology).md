# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial interpretation and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for legal and regulatory compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** A carrier molecule required for long-chain fatty acid transport; chemically identified as (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injection (solution for intravenous use); 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Manufactured for Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with metabolic disorders resulting in secondary deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. This may be administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Loading Doses:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 or 4 hours, and not less than every 6 hours.
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Initial Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Titration:** Adjustments may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels (target: 40–50 µmol/L). Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be visually inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use.
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None Known:** There are no known contraindications reported in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Serious Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, often within minutes of intravenous administration. These are reported primarily in ESRD patients. Treatment should be discontinued immediately upon signs of hypersensitivity [US].

### General Precautions
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Oral Formulation Note:** Safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) has been reported when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating **levocarnitine** or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses 3.8 times the human surface area-based dose revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. However, there are no adequate clinical trials in pregnant women. **Levocarnitine** should be used during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies in lactating women are unavailable. Dairy cow studies show increased milk carnitine concentrations after administration. The benefits of supplementation to the mother must be weighed against the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg for metabolic disorders); specific age-related adjustments are not otherwise specified [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65 and over to determine if they respond differently than younger subjects [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosing for ESRD patients is specific to the post-dialysis period (10–20 mg/kg) to compensate for dialytic losses [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also facilitates the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of a single IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; the mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Bioavailability Study:** In healthy volunteers, oral tablets and solution were found to be bio-equivalent with an absolute bioavailability of approximately 15% [US].
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly showed that trough levocarnitine concentrations increased from a baseline of 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks of therapy [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Trough concentrations in ESRD patients remained dose-proportionate over 12 and 24 weeks when administered at 10, 20, or 40 mg/kg doses [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **US:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription Only Medicine). NDC 36000-368-10 (carton of 10 vials) [US].
