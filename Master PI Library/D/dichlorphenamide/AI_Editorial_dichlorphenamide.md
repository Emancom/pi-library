# Editorial Product Information: Dichlorphenamide

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA). 
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of dichlorphenamide for healthcare professionals, synthesising data from the provided regulatory source.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the local approved PI for complete prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Dichlorphenamide (a dichlorinated benzenedisulfonamide).
*   **Chemical Class:** Carbonic anhydrase inhibitor.
*   **Brand Names:** Dichlorphenamide Tablets [US].
*   **ATC Code:** S01EC02 (Note: Primarily classified under ophthalmologicals, though used systemically for periodic paralysis).
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    *   **Oral Tablet:** 50 mg. The tablet is white to off-white, round, flat-faced, beveled-edged, and scored [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Oral.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Torrent Pharmaceuticals Limited [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Primary Periodic Paralysis:** Treatment of primary hyperkalemic periodic paralysis, primary hypokalemic periodic paralysis, and related variants [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
*   **General Adult Dosing:**
    *   **Initial Dose:** 50 mg administered orally once or twice daily [US].
    *   **Titration:** Adjust the dosage up or down based on individual clinical response at weekly intervals (or more frequently if adverse reactions occur) [US].
    *   **Recommended Range:** Minimum of 50 mg daily; maximum of 200 mg daily [US].
*   **Clinical Evaluation:** Prescribers should assess the patient's response to therapy after two months to determine the benefit of continuing treatment [US].
*   **Monitoring:** 
    *   Obtain baseline serum potassium and sodium bicarbonate levels [US].
    *   Perform periodic monitoring of electrolytes during therapy [US].
*   **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
Dichlorphenamide is contraindicated in the following conditions [US]:
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to dichlorphenamide or other sulfonamides.
*   **Hepatic Insufficiency:** Due to the risk of aggravating hepatic encephalopathy.
*   **Severe Pulmonary Disease:** Specifically conditions causing severe pulmonary obstruction that limit the body’s ability to compensate for drug-induced metabolic acidosis.
*   **Concomitant Salicylate Therapy:** Use with high-dose aspirin is contraindicated.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
*   **Hypersensitivity and Severe Skin Reactions:** Sulfonamide administration has been associated with fatal reactions, including Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN). Therapy must be discontinued immediately at the first sign of a skin rash or any evidence of an immune-mediated reaction [US].
*   **Hypokalemia:** Dichlorphenamide increases potassium excretion. Risk is heightened in patients with adrenocortical excess or renal tubular acidosis (Types 1 and 2). Baseline and periodic potassium measurements are required. If hypokalemia persists, dose reduction or discontinuation should be considered [US].
*   **Metabolic Acidosis:** The drug can cause hyperchloremic non-anion gap metabolic acidosis. Caution is required in patients with respiratory acidosis (e.g., advanced lung disease) as it may lead to respiratory decompensation. Monitor serum bicarbonate; reduce dose or discontinue if acidosis develops [US].
*   **Falls:** The risk of falls is increased, particularly in elderly patients and those on higher dosages. Evaluation of fall risk is recommended [US].
*   **Aspirin Interaction:** Even low-dose aspirin requires careful monitoring when co-administered with dichlorphenamide due to the risk of salicylate toxicity [US].

*   **Serious/Postmarketing Adverse Effects:** Amnesia, cardiac failure, convulsions, hallucinations, nephrolithiasis, pancytopenia, psychotic disorder, renal tubular necrosis, stupor, syncope, and tremor [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Salicylates (Aspirin):** High-dose aspirin is contraindicated. Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors increase the risk of metabolic acidosis, which enhances salicylate toxicity (anorexia, tachypnea, lethargy, coma). Low-dose aspirin requires close monitoring [US].
*   **OAT1 Substrates:** Dichlorphenamide inhibits OAT1 *in vitro*. It may increase plasma exposure of substrates such as methotrexate, famotidine, and oseltamivir; concomitant use is not recommended [US].
*   **OAT1/OAT3 Inhibitors:** As a substrate of OAT1 and OAT3, dichlorphenamide toxicity may increase when co-administered with inhibitors of these transporters [US].
*   **Hypokalemic Agents:** Increased risk of severe hypokalemia with loop diuretics, thiazides, laxatives, and corticosteroids [US].
*   **Metabolic Acidosis Inducers:** Co-administration with other drugs that cause metabolic acidosis may increase the severity of this condition [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   **US Category:** Not assigned under current labeling rules, but animal data indicates potential fetal harm [US].
    *   **Animal Data:** Teratogenic effects (fetal limb reduction) were observed in rats at doses 17 times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) [US].
    *   **Clinical Considerations:** Metabolic acidosis in pregnancy can lead to decreased fetal growth and oxygenation. Newborns of treated mothers should be monitored for transient metabolic acidosis [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Data regarding the presence of dichlorphenamide in human milk are unavailable. The health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the mother's clinical need for the drug and potential risks to the infant [US].
*   **Fertility:** Studies assessing the effects on human fertility have not been conducted [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in pediatric patients have not been established [US].
*   **Geriatric Use:** Elderly patients are at a higher risk of metabolic acidosis and falls. Pharmacokinetic differences in this population have not been determined [US].
*   **Renal Impairment:** Dosing adjustments by eGFR are not specified in the source; however, metabolic acidosis risk suggests caution [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in hepatic insufficiency due to the risk of aggravating hepatic encephalopathy [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Dichlorphenamide is a carbonic anhydrase inhibitor. The exact mechanism by which it reduces periodic paralysis attacks is unknown [US].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ is approximately 1.5 to 3 hours [US].
    *   **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is approximately 88% [US].
    *   **Metabolism:** Not a substrate for major CYP450 isoforms (1A2, 2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 3A4) *in vitro* [US].
    *   **Elimination:** Terminal half-life ranges from 32 to 66 hours. Steady-state is reached within 10 days of twice-daily dosing [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Study 1 (9-week trial):** A double-blind, placebo-controlled study in patients with hypokalemic (n=44) and hyperkalemic (n=21) periodic paralysis.
    *   **Outcome:** Patients with hypokalemic paralysis had 2.2 fewer attacks per week vs. placebo (p=0.02). Patients with hyperkalemic paralysis had 3.9 fewer attacks per week vs. placebo (p=0.08) [US].
*   **Study 2 (35-week crossover trial):** Evaluated long-term efficacy and withdrawal due to intolerable worsening.
    *   **Outcome:** In the hyperkalemic substudy, patients had 2.3 fewer attacks per week vs. placebo (p=0.006) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
*   **Excursions:** Permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
*   **Handling:** Dispense in a tight container with a child-resistant closure [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **United States:** Prescription only (Rx-only). Schedule: Not a controlled substance.
