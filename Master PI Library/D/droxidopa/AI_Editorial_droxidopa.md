# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information contained in this editorial was synthesized from the official Product Information (PI) document provided for the **United States (FDA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonized, clinically focused editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- This synthesis is intended for educational and reference purposes and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing decisions in any specific region.
- **Note:** While the preamble of this request mentioned droxidopa, the provided source document is for **Levocarnitine Injection**. This editorial reflects the provided source.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule involved in mitochondrial fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Name:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [United States].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [United States].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for both acute and chronic management of patients with metabolic defects resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [United States].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. This should be administered as a slow bolus (over 2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion. 
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 or 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours).
    - Subsequent daily doses typically remain in the 50 mg/kg range, though individual therapy requirements may necessitate doses up to 300 mg/kg [United States].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). 
    - This should be administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - Therapy initiation may be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma carnitine levels (<40 to 50 µmol/L) [United States].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect parenteral products for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [United States].

### Titration and Monitoring
- In ESRD patients, dose adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough plasma concentrations.
- It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels and monitor weekly or monthly. Target free carnitine concentrations should generally range between 35 and 60 µmol/L [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in patients with ESRD on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued and appropriate medical treatment initiated [United States].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with reports of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a history of seizures, an increase in the frequency or severity of events has been observed [United States].
- **General Monitoring:** Monitoring should include regular assessment of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported at a frequency of ≥5% (regardless of causality) in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials include:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (9%), accidental injury (10%), asthenia (9%), chest pain (12%), fever (7%), flu syndrome (25%), headache (22%), infection (15%), injection site reaction (33%), and pain (30%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), and arrhythmia (2%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), nausea (8%), vomiting (15%), and anorexia (5%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), and dyspnea (7%).
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), and depression (5%).

### Post-Marketing and Other Reactions
- **Neurologic:** Seizures.
- **Other:** Transient nausea and vomiting, gastritis, and "fishy" body odor [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dosage adjustments in patients receiving warfarin anticoagulation [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [United States: Category not explicitly assigned in updated format, but reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm]. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is not known if levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers is safe for the infant. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. Mothers must weigh the benefits of supplementation against potential risks to the child, or consider discontinuing nursing [United States].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders are based on mg/kg and apply to pediatric patients [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. In ESRD patients on dialysis, chronic administration of high oral doses may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide). Intravenous levocarnitine is specifically indicated for use in the dialysis population [United States].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing the necessary substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [United States].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine alleviates metabolic abnormalities caused by the accumulation of toxic acyl-CoA compounds and helps normalize plasma carnitine concentrations [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following a 20 mg/kg IV bolus, the mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis showed that trough levocarnitine concentrations increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses three times weekly demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** When diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL), the solution is stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [United States].
