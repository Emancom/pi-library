# Editorial Product Information: Delafloxacin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **delafloxacin** for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single, cohesive reference.
- **Prescriber Notice:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Delafloxacin (as meglumine salt).
- **Chemical Class:** Fluoroquinolone antibacterial.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Baxdela** [US].
- **ATC Code:** J01MA23.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Oral Tablet:** 450 mg delafloxacin (equivalent to 649 mg delafloxacin meglumine) [US].
    - **Lyophilized Powder for Injection:** 300 mg delafloxacin (equivalent to 433 mg delafloxacin meglumine) in a single-dose vial for reconstitution [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral, Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Melinta Therapeutics, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Bacterial Skin and Skin Structure Infections (ABSSSI):** Treatment of adults with infections caused by susceptible isolates of *Staphylococcus aureus* (including MRSA and MSSA), *S. haemolyticus*, *S. lugdunensis*, *Streptococcus pyogenes*, *S. agalactiae*, *S. anginosus* group, *Enterococcus faecalis*, *Escherichia coli*, *Enterobacter cloacae*, *Klebsiella pneumoniae*, and *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* [US].
- **Community-Acquired Bacterial Pneumonia (CABP):** Treatment of adults with infections caused by susceptible isolates of *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Staphylococcus aureus* (MSSA only), *Klebsiella pneumoniae*, *Escherichia coli*, *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, *Haemophilus influenzae*, *H. parainfluenzae*, *Chlamydia pneumoniae*, *Legionella pneumophila*, and *Mycoplasma pneumoniae* [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
The standard regimen for both indications is:
- **Intravenous:** 300 mg via IV infusion over 60 minutes every 12 hours.
- **Oral:** 450 mg tablet every 12 hours.
- **Switch Therapy:** Patients may be initiated on IV therapy and switched to oral tablets at the clinician’s discretion [US].

### Dosing by Indication
- **ABSSSI:** Total duration of 5 to 14 days.
- **CABP:** Total duration of 5 to 10 days.

### Administration Instructions
- **Tablets:** May be taken with or without food.
- **IV Reconstitution:** Reconstitute the 300 mg vial with 10.5 mL of D5W or 0.9% Sodium Chloride. Vigorously shake until dissolved. The resulting 25 mg/mL solution must be further diluted in 250 mL of D5W or 0.9% Sodium Chloride to a final concentration of 1.2 mg/mL.
- **Compatibility:** Do not co-infuse with other medications or solutions containing multivalent cations (e.g., magnesium, calcium). If a common line is used, flush with D5W or 0.9% Sodium Chloride before and after infusion [US].

### Missed Dose Instructions
If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as possible up to 8 hours before the next scheduled dose. If less than 8 hours remain, the missed dose should be skipped [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **delafloxacin**, other fluoroquinolones, or any component of the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning [US]
**Serious Adverse Reactions:** Fluoroquinolones are associated with disabling and potentially irreversible serious adverse reactions that can occur together, including:
- **Tendinitis and Tendon Rupture:** Increased risk in all ages, but higher in patients >60 years, those taking corticosteroids, and organ transplant recipients.
- **Peripheral Neuropathy:** Sensory or sensorimotor axonal polyneuropathy may occur soon after initiation and may be permanent.
- **Central Nervous System (CNS) Effects:** Including seizures, increased intracranial pressure, and psychiatric reactions (toxic psychosis, hallucinations, anxiety, depression, suicidal ideation).
**Discontinue delafloxacin immediately if any of these reactions occur.**
- **Exacerbation of Myasthenia Gravis:** Fluoroquinolones may exacerbate muscle weakness; avoid use in patients with a known history of myasthenia gravis.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious and occasionally fatal anaphylaxis may occur after the first dose. Discontinue at the first sign of a skin rash.
- **Clostridium difficile-Associated Diarrhea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all systemic antibacterials, ranging from mild diarrhea to fatal colitis.
- **Aortic Aneurysm and Dissection:** Increased risk within two months of fluoroquinolone use, especially in the elderly. Use only when no other treatment options are available in high-risk patients.
- **Blood Glucose Disturbances:** Both symptomatic hyperglycemia and hypoglycemia have been reported, primarily in diabetic patients. Close monitoring is required.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (Incidence ≥ 2%)
| Adverse Reaction | ABSSSI Pooled Trials [US] | CABP Trial [US] |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| Diarrhea | 8% | 5% |
| Nausea | 8% | <2% |
| Transaminase Elevations | 3% | 5% |
| Headache | 3% | <2% |
| Vomiting | 2% | <2% |

### Serious and Less Common Effects (<2%)
- **Blood/Lymphatic:** Agranulocytosis, anemia, pancytopenia.
- **Cardiac:** Sinus tachycardia, palpitations, bradycardia.
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness, dysgeusia, syncope, paraesthesia.
- **Renal:** Renal impairment, renal failure.
- **Skin:** Urticaria, pruritus, dermatitis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Chelation Agents:** Antacids containing magnesium or aluminum, sucralfate, metal cations (iron, zinc), and buffered didanosine formulations interfere with oral absorption. Administer **delafloxacin** tablets at least 2 hours before or 6 hours after these agents [US].
- **Cytochrome P450:** Delafloxacin does not significantly inhibit major CYP450 isoforms at clinical concentrations. It is a mild inducer of CYP2C9 and CYP3A4 *in vitro*, though clinical studies with midazolam showed no effect [US].
- **Transporters:** Delafloxacin is a substrate for P-gp and BCRP *in vitro*; the clinical relevance of co-administration with inhibitors is unknown [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **[US]:** Data are insufficient to inform drug-associated risks. Animal studies (rats) at high oral doses showed reduced fetal weights and ossification delays but no malformations.
- **Breastfeeding:** 
    - **[US]:** No human data available. Delafloxacin is excreted in rat milk. Consider the developmental/health benefits of breastfeeding against the mother's clinical need for the drug.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended in patients <18 years due to potential for arthropathy [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment needed based on age. However, the elderly are at higher risk for tendon disorders and aortic aneurysm/dissection [US].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **eGFR 30–89 mL/min/1.73m²:** No dosage adjustment required.
    - **eGFR 15–29 mL/min/1.73m²:** Oral: No adjustment. IV: Reduce to 200 mg every 12 hours.
    - **ESRD (<15 mL/min/1.73m²):** Not recommended due to insufficient data and accumulation of the IV vehicle (SBECD) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dosage adjustment required for patients with mild, moderate, or severe (Child-Pugh A, B, C) hepatic impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits bacterial DNA gyrase (topoisomerase II) and topoisomerase IV. It exhibits concentration-dependent bactericidal activity.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability of 450 mg oral dose is 58.8%. Peak plasma concentration (Cmax) occurs at ~1 hour (fasted).
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is ~84% (mainly to albumin). Vd is 30–48 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily glucuronidation; oxidative metabolism is minimal (<1%).
    - **Elimination:** Half-life is ~3.7 hours (IV) and 4.2–8.5 hours (Oral). Excretion: 65% in urine (as unchanged drug and glucuronides) and 28% in feces [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ABSSSI:** Two Phase 3 non-inferiority trials (n=1510) compared **delafloxacin** (IV or IV-to-oral) to vancomycin plus aztreonam. **Delafloxacin** achieved similar objective clinical response rates (78.2%–83.7%) at 48–72 hours [US].
- **CABP:** One Phase 3 non-inferiority trial (n=859) compared **delafloxacin** (IV-to-oral) to moxifloxacin. **Delafloxacin** demonstrated comparable early clinical response (88.9% vs 89.0%) at 72–120 hours [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Tablets and Powder:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F). 
- **Reconstituted/Diluted Solution:** May be stored refrigerated (2°C to 8°C) or at room temperature for up to 24 hours. Do not freeze [US].
