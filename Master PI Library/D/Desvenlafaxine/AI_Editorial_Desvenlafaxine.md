# Editorial Product Information: Desvenlafaxine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following official Product Information (PI) document:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: APO-Desvenlafaxine MR (Desvenlafaxine benzoate) PI.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a single reference. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidelines required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the Australian PI was provided for review; therefore, international variations (e.g., US, European) are "Not specified in available sources" unless otherwise noted from the provided text.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Desvenlafaxine (as benzoate).
- **Chemical Class:** Selective Serotonin and Noradrenaline Reuptake Inhibitor (SNRI).
- **Brand Name(s):** APO-Desvenlafaxine MR [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Extended-release (modified-release) tablets: 50 mg and 100 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Major Depressive Disorder (MDD):** Indicated for the treatment of MDD, including the prevention of relapse [Australian].
- **Paediatric Use:** Not indicated for use in the paediatric population [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** The recommended therapeutic dose is 50 mg once daily, administered at approximately the same time each day [Australian].
- **Dosing Range:** Clinical trials demonstrated no additional benefit at doses exceeding 50 mg/day. However, based on clinical judgment, doses may be increased up to a maximum of 200 mg/day [Australian].
- **Titration:** Dose increments should be gradual and occur at intervals of no less than 7 days [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets must be swallowed whole with fluid. They should not be crushed, divided, chewed, or dissolved. May be taken with or without food [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not explicitly specified in the available source; however, the requirement for consistent daily timing is noted [Australian].
- **Discontinuation:** Therapy should be tapered gradually to minimize discontinuation symptoms [Australian].
- **Inert Matrix:** Patients should be advised that the inert tablet matrix may be visible in the stool or via colostomy; the active medication is absorbed prior to this [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to desvenlafaxine benzoate, venlafaxine hydrochloride, or any excipients in the formulation [Australian].
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** 
    - Concomitant use with MAOIs (including reversible MAOIs like moclobemide, linezolid, and intravenous methylene blue) is contraindicated due to the risk of serotonin syndrome [Australian].
    - A minimum of 14 days must elapse after discontinuing an MAOI before starting desvenlafaxine.
    - At least 7 days must elapse after discontinuing desvenlafaxine before starting an MAOI [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Suicide Risk/Clinical Worsening:** Close monitoring is required for clinical worsening and suicidality, particularly at the initiation of therapy and during dose changes. This risk is highest in children, adolescents, and young adults (ages 18–24) [Australian].
- **Serotonin Syndrome/NMS-like Reactions:** Potentially life-threatening conditions may occur. Treatment should be discontinued immediately if symptoms (agitation, hallucinations, tachycardia, hyperreflexia, muscle rigidity) appear [Australian].
- **Blood Pressure:** Dose-related increases in blood pressure have been observed. Pre-existing hypertension must be controlled before starting treatment, and regular BP monitoring is mandatory [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular/Cerebrovascular Disease:** Use with caution in patients with conditions that may be compromised by increases in blood pressure or heart rate. Desvenlafaxine has not been systematically evaluated in patients with a recent history of MI or unstable heart disease [Australian].
- **Narrow-Angle Glaucoma:** Mydriasis has been reported; patients with raised intraocular pressure or those at risk of angle-closure glaucoma should be monitored [Australian].
- **Lipid Metabolism:** Dose-related increases in fasting serum total cholesterol, LDL, and triglycerides have been observed. Periodic lipid testing should be considered [Australian].
- **Seizures:** Prescribe with caution in patients with a history of seizure disorders [Australian].
- **Abnormal Bleeding:** SNRIs increase the risk of bleeding events (e.g., ecchymosis, epistaxis, GI bleeds). Caution is advised when used with NSAIDs, aspirin, or anticoagulants [Australian].
- **Hyponatraemia:** Cases of hyponatraemia and SIADH have been reported, primarily in elderly or volume-depleted patients [Australian].
- **Sexual Dysfunction:** SNRIs may cause persistent sexual dysfunction [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are most frequent during the first week of treatment.
- **Very Common (≥10%):** Nausea, dry mouth, constipation, fatigue, dizziness, headache, somnolence, insomnia, hyperhidrosis [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Palpitations, tachycardia, tinnitus, blurred vision, mydriasis, diarrhoea, vomiting, chills, jitteriness, decreased appetite, musculoskeletal stiffness, tremor, paraesthesia, anxiety, decreased libido, erectile dysfunction, ejaculation disorder, delayed ejaculation, yawning, and rash [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Hypersensitivity, syncope, depersonalisation, hypomania, epistaxis, alopecia, proteinuria, orthostatic hypotension [Australian].
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Convulsion, serotonin syndrome, pancreatitis, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, angioedema, stress cardiomyopathy (Takotsubo) [Australian].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Suicidality, serotonin syndrome, severe hypertension, and post-partum haemorrhage [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **MAOIs:** Contraindicated (see Section 4).
- **Serotonergic Agents:** Increased risk of serotonin syndrome with SSRIs, other SNRIs, triptans, opioids (fentanyl, tramadol), lithium, and St. John's wort. Concomitant use with tryptophan supplements is not recommended [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Potent inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole) may increase desvenlafaxine exposure (ketoconazole 200mg BID increased desvenlafaxine AUC by ~43%) [Australian].
- **CYP2D6 Substrates:** Desvenlafaxine (at 400 mg/day) can increase the AUC of drugs metabolised by CYP2D6 (e.g., desipramine) by approximately 90% [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Substrates:** Desvenlafaxine (at 400 mg/day) may decrease the exposure of drugs metabolised by CYP3A4 (e.g., midazolam AUC decreased by ~31%) [Australian].
- **Ethanol:** While clinical studies do not show increased impairment, patients are advised to avoid alcohol while taking CNS-active medications [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy Category:** **Category B2** [Australian].
- **Pregnancy Risk:** Safety in humans is not established. Use only if benefits outweigh risks. Exposure late in the third trimester may lead to neonatal complications (respiratory distress, feeding difficulties, jitteriness) or persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn (PPHN). There is an increased risk of post-partum haemorrhage in mothers exposed to SNRIs in the month prior to delivery [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Desvenlafaxine is excreted in human milk. Due to the potential for serious reactions in nursing infants, a decision must be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug [Australian].
- **Fertility:** In animal studies (rats), female fertility was reduced at exposures >2 times the human dose (200 mg/day). Male fertility was unaffected at 4 times the human dose [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients under 18 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment is required based on age alone. However, the elderly are at higher risk for systolic orthostatic hypotension and reduced renal clearance [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Mild/Moderate:** No specific adjustment noted for mild; clearance is reduced by ~29–39% [Australian].
    - **Severe (CrCl < 30 mL/min) / ESRD:** Recommended starting dose is **50 mg every other day**. Supplemental doses should not be given after dialysis [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is necessary for patients with mild, moderate, or severe hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A selective serotonin and noradrenaline reuptake inhibitor (SNRI). It lacks significant affinity for muscarinic-cholinergic, H1-histaminergic, or α1-adrenergic receptors [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Bioavailability:** ~80%.
    - **Tmax:** ~7.5 hours.
    - **Protein Binding:** Low (~30%), independent of concentration.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via conjugation (UGT isoforms); oxidative metabolism (CYP3A4) is a minor pathway.
    - **Excretion:** ~45% excreted unchanged in urine; ~19% as glucuronide metabolite [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Does not produce QTc prolongation in healthy individuals [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Acute Efficacy:** Established in four 8-week, randomised, placebo-controlled trials in adults with MDD (DSM-IV). Doses of 50 mg to 400 mg showed superiority over placebo on the HAM-D17 scale [Australian].
- **Maintenance:** A 26-week relapse prevention study showed significantly lower relapse rates in patients continuing desvenlafaxine compared to placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Environment:** Store in a dry place [Australian].
- **Disposal:** Unused medicine should be returned to a pharmacy [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (S4)** – Prescription Only Medicine [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription only [Australian].
