# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following provided Product Information (PI) document:
    - **US (FDA)**: Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation)
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data, regulatory warnings, and dosing guidelines.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Always consult local regulatory guidelines for clinical decision-making.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injection, solution: 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US]; Baxter Pharmaceuticals India Private Ltd [Manufacturer for US supply].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Metabolic Disorders)
- **Standard Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Administration should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Dose Range:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses may be required. The maximum recorded dose is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Adjustment:** Dose adjustments (e.g., downward to 5 mg/kg) should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations and may be implemented as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration and Monitoring
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to parenteral therapy. Monitoring (weekly and monthly) should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Preparation:** Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use. The product is compatible with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** While the source states "None known," clinical warnings exist for serious hypersensitivity reactions (see Section 5) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizures:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a known seizure disorder, an increase in frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Metabolite Accumulation):** While this PI is for IV use, it notes that chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD on dialysis may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites: trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (Clinical Trials in ESRD)
Adverse events reported at a frequency ≥5% in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials [US]:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%), Flu syndrome (25%), Headache (22%), Chest pain (12%), Fever (7%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%), Nausea (8%), Abdominal pain (9%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), Cough increase (9%), Dyspnea (7%).

### Other Reported Effects
- **Neurologic:** Dizziness (13%), Paresthesia (5%), Insomnia (4%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), Hyperkalemia (6%), Hypervolemia (5%).
- **Specific:** Transient nausea/vomiting and body odor have been observed [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not specified in modern labeling, but referenced as follows]: Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. However, there are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant should be weighed against the benefits to the mother [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on body weight (mg/kg) as specified in Section 3 [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically for carnitine deficiency in ESRD patients on dialysis. Note the precaution regarding the accumulation of TMA/TMAO with oral dosing in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Information not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and [3H]-γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Data (ESRD):** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg IV three times per week, mean pre-dialysis (trough) levels increased from ~21 µmol/L at baseline to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Clinical Outcomes:** While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations is established, the effects of supplemental carnitine on specific clinical outcomes in the ESRD population have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% NaCl or Lactated Ringer’s in PVC bags.
- **Preservation:** The formulation does not contain a preservative; discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only. Poison schedule not applicable.
- **Legal Classification:** Human Prescription Drug [US].
