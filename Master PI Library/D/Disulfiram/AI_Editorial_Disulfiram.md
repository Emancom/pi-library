# Editorial Product Information: Disulfiram

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: This editorial synthesis is based on Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)** regulatory authority. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data to support safe prescribing and monitoring.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or SmPC required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Disulfiram (Chemical class: Thiuram derivative/aldehyde dehydrogenase inhibitor).
- **Brand Name(s):** Antabuse [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N07BB01 (Not specified in available source but standard for class).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Effervescent Tablet:** 200 mg (White, flat, scored, engraved "CDC") [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Alcohol Dependence Management:** Indicated as a deterrent to alcohol consumption. It serves as an adjunct in the comprehensive management of selected chronic alcoholic patients who are motivated to remain abstinent and are participating in supportive psychotherapy and rehabilitation programmes [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Initial Dosage:** 100 mg (one-half tablet) daily for 7 to 14 days. If necessary, the dose may be escalated to a maximum of 300 mg daily [Australian].
- **Maintenance Dosage:** The standard maintenance dose is 200 mg (one tablet) daily.
- **Duration:** Recommended for 6 weeks to 6 months. A clinical review of therapy effectiveness is required before considering long-term continuation. High-dose or long-term therapy requires close medical supervision [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Administer as a single daily dose, preferably upon waking.
- If sedation occurs, the dose may be taken at bedtime or the initial dosage may be reduced [Australian].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to disulfiram, any excipients, or other thiuram derivatives (often found in pesticides and rubber vulcanisation) [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Disease:** Severe myocardial disease, ischaemic heart disease (coronary artery disease), uncompensated cardiac failure, or hypertension.
- **Neurological/Psychiatric:** Previous history of cerebrovascular accident (CVA), severe personality disorders, suicidal ideation/risk, psychosis or psychotic states, and serious organic brain damage [Australian].
- **Organ Impairment:** Advanced hepatic or renal disease.
- **Concurrent Medication:** Do not use with metronidazole or paraldehyde [Australian].
- **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated [Australian].
- **Alcohol Status:** Never administer to a patient in a state of alcoholic intoxication or without their full consent and understanding of the disulfiram-alcohol reaction.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Critical Timing Warning
- **DO NOT ADMINISTER** until the patient has abstained from alcohol for at least **24 hours**.

### The Disulfiram-Ethanol Reaction
- Patients must be warned that the reaction can occur up to **14 days** (and in some cases **3 weeks**) after the last dose of disulfiram [Australian].
- **Reaction triggers:** Exposure to alcohol in foods (sauces, vinegar), liquid medicines (cough syrups, tonics), toiletries (aftershave, alcoholic rubs), or "alcohol-free" beverages can elicit a reaction [Australian].
- **Symptoms:** Intense flushing, sensation of heat, sweating, palpitations, tachycardia, dyspnoea, hyperventilation, pounding headache, nausea, and violent vomiting. 
- **Severe Reactions:** May include arrhythmias, convulsions, hypotension, respiratory depression, and death [Australian].

### Hepatic Toxicity
- Severe and potentially fatal drug-induced liver injury has been reported, even in patients without prior hepatic dysfunction. 
- **Monitoring:** Liver function tests (LFTs) must be performed before treatment and periodically thereafter. Full blood counts (FBC) and sequential multiple analysis (SMA-12) are also recommended [Australian].
- **Patient Education:** Patients should immediately report fatigue, malaise, jaundice, or dark urine.

### Other Precautions
- Use with strict medical supervision in patients with diabetes mellitus, hypothyroidism, epilepsy, cerebral damage, or respiratory disease (e.g., asthma) [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects may occur during the first two weeks of therapy and often resolve spontaneously or with dose reduction.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common/Transient** | Drowsiness, fatigue, headache, metallic or garlic-like aftertaste, stomach upset, acneiform eruptions, allergic dermatitis. |
| **Serious/Rare** | **Hepatotoxicity:** Jaundice, hepatitis, hepatic necrosis, cirrhosis (fatal cases reported). <br> **Neurological:** Peripheral neuropathy, polyneuritis, optic neuritis (vision changes/eye pain), encephalopathy. <br> **Psychiatric:** Psychotic reactions (mania, schizophrenia, depression, paranoia), reduced libido. <br> **Other:** Impotence, seizures. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions (Enzyme Inhibition)
Disulfiram inhibits the hepatic metabolism of several drugs, potentially increasing their toxicity:
- **Phenytoin:** Risk of intoxication; monitor serum levels and adjust doses.
- **Anticoagulants (e.g., Warfarin):** May prolong prothrombin time; dosage adjustment may be required.
- **Theophylline:** Enhanced efficacy/toxicity; dose adjustment may be necessary.
- **Benzodiazepines (Chlordiazepoxide/Diazepam):** Enhanced sedative effects; oxazepam is not affected.
- **Isoniazid:** May cause ataxia or changes in mental state.
- **Rifampicin:** Disulfiram inhibits oxidation and renal excretion.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Metronidazole:** Risk of acute psychotic reactions and confusion.
- **Amitriptyline:** May increase the intensity of the disulfiram-alcohol reaction.
- **Chlorpromazine:** May increase the overall intensity of the reaction, though it may decrease certain components.
- **Pimozide:** Rare reports of potentiation of organic brain syndrome.

### Absorption Interactions
- **Antacids (Divalent cations) & Ferrous salts:** May reduce the absorption of disulfiram [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B2 [Australian].** Safety is not established. Disulfiram should not be used in pregnancy; benefits must be weighed against the risks of alcoholism. Rare reports of congenital abnormalities exist.
- **Breastfeeding:** Use is not recommended. It is unknown if disulfiram is excreted in breast milk, and potential interactions with the infant's medications are a concern [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No data available [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No data available [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in advanced renal disease [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in advanced liver disease. Metabolism is not significantly affected by mild-to-moderate impairment, but cirrhosis increases metabolite concentrations [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Irreversibly inhibits aldehyde dehydrogenase (ALDH). This blocks the oxidation of acetaldehyde (a metabolite of ethanol) to acetate. The resulting accumulation of acetaldehyde causes the "aldehyde reaction." It also inhibits dopamine-beta-hydroxylase, reducing noradrenaline synthesis [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid but incomplete from the GI tract; ~20% excreted in faeces.
    - **Distribution:** Highly lipid-soluble; accumulates in fat depots.
    - **Metabolism:** A prodrug rapidly converted to diethyldithiocarbamate (DDC), then further to the active metabolite MeDTC. Peak MeDTC levels occur at 4 hours; however, maximum ALDH inhibition requires 3 daily doses.
    - **Elimination:** Metabolites excreted via urine and expired air (as carbon disulphide). Up to 20% of a dose may persist in the body for one week or longer [Australian].
