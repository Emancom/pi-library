# Editorial Product Information: Dolutegravir and Lamivudine (Dovato)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not intended to substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the local PI for definitive legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** **Dolutegravir** (as sodium), an HIV integrase strand transfer inhibitor (INSTI), and **lamivudine**, a nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NRTI).
- **Brand Name(s):** **DOVATO** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Oval, biconvex, white film-coated tablets debossed with "SV 137". Each tablet contains 50 mg of dolutegravir and 300 mg of lamivudine [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** ViiV Healthcare Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **HIV-1 Infection:** Indicated for the treatment of Human Immunodeficiency Virus type 1 (HIV-1) infection in adults and adolescents (at least 12 years of age and weighing at least 40 kg).
    - **Treatment-Naïve:** For patients with no prior antiretroviral history and no known or suspected resistance to either component.
    - **Virologically Suppressed (Switch):** To replace a stable antiretroviral regimen in patients who are virologically suppressed (HIV-1 RNA <50 copies/mL) with no history of treatment failure and no known or suspected resistance to the integrase inhibitor class or lamivudine [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** One tablet (50 mg/300 mg) taken once daily.
- **Administration:** May be taken with or without food [Australian].
- **Dose Adjustment:** As a fixed-dose combination, this product should not be prescribed for patients requiring individual component dose adjustments (e.g., those with renal impairment requiring lamivudine dose reduction). 
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as the patient remembers, provided the next dose is not due within 4 hours. If the next dose is due within 4 hours, the missed dose should be skipped [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to **dolutegravir**, **lamivudine**, or any excipients (including mannitol and magnesium stearate).
- **Narrow Therapeutic Index Substrates:** Co-administration with medicinal products that are substrates of organic cation transporter 2 (OCT2) with narrow therapeutic windows, specifically **dofetilide**, **pilsicainide**, or **fampridine**, as dolutegravir may increase their plasma concentrations leading to life-threatening toxicity [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Reported with integrase inhibitors. Characterised by rash, constitutional findings, and organ dysfunction (including liver injury). Therapy must be discontinued immediately if hypersensitivity is suspected.
- **Lactic Acidosis and Severe Hepatomegaly with Steatosis:** A known risk for nucleoside analogues, including lamivudine. Most cases occur in women. Treatment should be suspended in any patient developing clinical or laboratory findings suggestive of lactic acidosis or hepatotoxicity.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Hepatic adverse events have been reported, including acute liver failure in patients without pre-existing disease. Monitoring of liver function is recommended.
- **HBV/HCV Co-infection:**
    - **Hepatitis B Resistance:** Lamivudine monotherapy is inadequate for HBV; if used in co-infected patients, an additional antiviral is generally required.
    - **Post-treatment Exacerbations:** Discontinuation of lamivudine in HBV co-infected patients may lead to severe acute exacerbations of hepatitis.
- **Immune Reactivation Syndrome:** Inflammatory responses to indolent or residual opportunistic infections (e.g., MAC, CMV, PCP) may occur during the initial phase of treatment. Autoimmune disorders (e.g., Graves' disease) have also been reported.
- **Lipids and Glucose:** Levels may increase during therapy; monitoring and clinical management are advised [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally consistent with the profiles of the individual components.
- **Very Common (>10%):** Headache, Diarrhoea, Nausea.
- **Common (1–10%):**
    - **Psychiatric:** Insomnia, depression, anxiety, abnormal dreams.
    - **Nervous System:** Dizziness, somnolence.
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Abdominal pain/discomfort, vomiting, flatulence.
    - **General:** Fatigue, pyrexia, malaise.
    - **Other:** Rash, pruritus, weight increased.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Hypersensitivity, Immune Reconstitution Syndrome, Hepatitis, suicidal ideation/attempt (especially in those with pre-existing history), Myalgia, Arthralgia, Neutropenia, Anaemia, Thrombocytopenia.
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Acute hepatic failure, Lactic acidosis, Rhabdomyolysis, Pancreatitis, Amylase elevations.
- **Very Rare (<0.01%):** Pure red cell aplasia, Sideroblastic anaemia, Peripheral neuropathy, Paraesthesia [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **OCT2 Substrates:** Dolutegravir increases concentrations of **dofetilide**, **pilsicainide**, and **fampridine** (Contraindicated).
- **Metformin:** Dolutegravir increases metformin exposure. Clinicians should monitor glycaemic control and consider metformin dose adjustments.
- **Polyvalent Cations:** Antacids containing magnesium/aluminium, calcium supplements, or iron supplements can significantly reduce dolutegravir absorption.
    - **Management:** Administer **DOVATO** 2 hours before or 6 hours after these agents. Alternatively, take with food when administered with calcium or iron supplements.
- **Metabolic Inducers:** Co-administration with potent inducers (e.g., **rifampicin**, **carbamazepine**, **phenytoin**, **phenobarbital**, **St. John’s wort**, **efavirenz**, **nevirapine**, **tipranavir/ritonavir**) is not recommended as they significantly decrease dolutegravir levels.
- **Sorbitol:** Chronic co-administration of sorbitol-containing medicines may decrease lamivudine exposure; avoid when possible [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. 
    - **Neural Tube Defects (NTD):** Surveillance data from Botswana initially suggested an association between dolutegravir at conception and NTDs; however, subsequent larger cohorts in Botswana and Eswatini showed the prevalence (approx. 0.1%) was not significantly different from non-dolutegravir regimens. 
    - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if the benefit justifies the potential risk.
- **Breastfeeding:** Health authorities recommend that HIV-infected women do not breastfeed to avoid virus transmission. Dolutegravir is excreted in human milk in small amounts; lamivudine is excreted at concentrations similar to those in serum.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no adverse effects on male or female fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for adolescents ≥12 years weighing ≥40 kg. Safety and efficacy not established in children <12 years or <40 kg.
- **Elderly:** Limited data in patients ≥65 years. Use caution due to higher frequency of decreased hepatic/renal function and concomitant disease.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Mild to Moderate:** No adjustment for dolutegravir.
    - **CrCl <30 mL/min:** Not recommended because the required lamivudine dose adjustment cannot be achieved with the fixed-dose combination.
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild to Moderate (Child-Pugh A/B):** No dose adjustment required.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Not recommended [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Dolutegravir:** Inhibits HIV integrase by binding to the active site, blocking DNA strand transfer.
    - **Lamivudine:** A NRTI that, following intracellular phosphorylation to lamivudine triphosphate, acts as a chain terminator of viral DNA.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; Tmax 2.5h (dolutegravir) and 1.0h (lamivudine). Food increases dolutegravir AUC but is not clinically significant.
    - **Distribution:** Dolutegravir is 99.3% protein bound; lamivudine <36%. Both penetrate the CSF and genital tract.
    - **Metabolism:** Dolutegravir is primarily metabolised via UGT1A1 (minor CYP3A). Lamivudine metabolism is a minor route (<10%).
    - **Elimination:** Dolutegravir terminal half-life ~14h (primarily faecal excretion of unchanged drug). Lamivudine terminal half-life 18–19h (primarily renal excretion) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **GEMINI-1 & GEMINI-2:** Two identical Phase III trials in treatment-naïve adults. **DOVATO** was shown to be non-inferior to a three-drug regimen (dolutegravir + tenofovir/emtricitabine) through 144 weeks. At 96 weeks, virologic suppression (<50 copies/mL) was 86% for **DOVATO** vs 90% for the triplet regimen (pooled data).
- **TANGO:** A Phase III switch study in virologically suppressed adults. Switching to **DOVATO** was non-inferior to remaining on a tenofovir alafenamide (TAF)-based three- or four-drug regimen through 144 weeks [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C.
- **Handling:** Store in the original package to protect from moisture.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
