# Editorial Product Information: Dactinomycin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: 
    - **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes. It is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing, dispensing, or administering dactinomycin in any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Dactinomycin (also known as Actinomycin D).
- **Chemical Class:** Cytotoxic antibiotic produced by *Streptomyces parvullus*.
- **Brand Name(s):** **COSMEGEN** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01DA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Lyophilised powder for injection: 500 micrograms (0.5 mg) per vial, co-formulated with 20 mg of mannitol [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) injection or regional isolation-perfusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Recordati Rare Diseases Australia, Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Dactinomycin is indicated for the treatment of the following neoplasms [Australian-approved]:
- **Wilms’ Tumour:** Often used in combination with surgery, radiotherapy, and vincristine.
- **Rhabdomyosarcoma:** Particularly as part of the VAC (vincristine, dactinomycin, cyclophosphamide) regimen.
- **Choriocarcinoma:** For both metastatic and non-metastatic gestational trophoblastic disease (often used sequentially with methotrexate).
- **Testicular Carcinoma:** Metastatic, nonseminomatous types.
- **Ewing’s Sarcoma:** Palliative treatment, often in combination with other agents or radiotherapy.
- **Sarcoma Botryoides:** Palliative treatment.
- **Regional Perfusion:** Used palliatively or as an adjunct to surgery in various sarcomas, carcinomas, and adenocarcinomas.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Dosage Calculation:** Must be calculated in micrograms (mcg). For obese or oedematous patients, dosing should be based on body surface area (BSA) to better relate to lean body mass [Australian].
- **Standard IV Regimen:** 500 mcg daily for a maximum of 5 days.
- **Maximum Dose:** Do not exceed 15 mcg/kg/day or 400–600 mcg/m²/day for 5 days.
- **Course Interval:** A second course may be administered after a minimum of three weeks, provided all signs of toxicity have resolved.

### Dosing by Indication
- **Metastatic Nonseminomatous Testicular Carcinoma:** 500 mcg/day for five consecutive days every 6–8 weeks for 4 months or longer [Australian].
- **Ewing’s Sarcoma (Non-metastatic):** 45 mcg/m² given sequentially with cyclophosphamide and radiotherapy [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing
- **Standard IV Regimen:** 15 mcg/kg/day for 5 days. 
- **Alternative Schedule:** A total dose of 2500 mcg/m² administered intravenously over a one-week period [Australian].

### Isolation-Perfusion Dosing
- **Lower Extremity/Pelvis:** 50 mcg/kg.
- **Upper Extremity:** 35 mcg/kg.
- Doses should be adjusted downward for obese patients or those with previous therapy.

### Administration Instructions
- **Reconstitution:** Dissolve in 1.1 mL of Sterile Water for Injection (without preservative). The resulting gold-coloured solution contains ~500 mcg/mL.
- **Dilution:** Can be added to 5% glucose or 0.9% sodium chloride infusions. **Note:** Final concentrations below 10 mcg/mL are unstable and suffer a loss of potency [Australian].
- **Vesicant Precautions:** Use the "two-needle technique" if giving directly into a vein (withdraw with one needle, inject with another). 
- **Filtration:** Cellulose ester membrane filters may partially remove dactinomycin from IV solutions.
- **Incompatibilities:** Avoid preservatives (benzyl alcohol or parabens) as they cause precipitation.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to dactinomycin or mannitol.
- **Infection Risk:** Administration at or near the time of chickenpox or herpes zoster infection is strictly contraindicated due to the risk of severe, potentially fatal generalised disease [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Highly Toxic:** Dactinomycin is extremely corrosive to soft tissue. Extravasation causes severe damage and may lead to contracture.
- **Veno-Occlusive Disease (VOD):** Primarily hepatic; can be fatal. Risk is highest in children under 48 months of age [Australian].
- **Radiation Potentiation/Recall:** Dactinomycin potentiates radiation effects. It can reactivate erythema from previous radiotherapy (radiation recall phenomenon). Severe mucositis may occur if radiation involves the nasopharynx.
- **Myelosuppression:** Frequent (daily) monitoring of platelet and white cell counts is required. Treatment should be withheld if counts drop significantly to allow for marrow recovery (up to 3 weeks).
- **Secondary Neoplasms:** Long-term risk of second primary tumours, including leukaemia, following multi-modal therapy.
- **Vaccinations:** Live virus vaccines must not be administered during therapy.

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Note: Frequencies were not specified in the available source, but the drug is described as having frequent and severe toxicity [Australian].*

- **Serious Adverse Effects:**
    - **Hepatic:** Veno-occlusive disease, hepatomegaly, hepatic failure, ascites, and death.
    - **Haematological:** Anaemia, aplastic anaemia, agranulocytosis, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, and pancytopenia.
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Gastrointestinal ulceration, tumour lysis syndrome, and severe nausea/vomiting (occurring within hours of administration).
    - **Dermatological:** Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN), and severe radiation recall.
    - **Infectious:** Sepsis (including neutropenic sepsis).
- **Other Effects:**
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Anorexia, diarrhoea, proctitis, cheilitis, dysphagia, and oesophagitis.
    - **Dermatological:** Alopecia, acne, and hyperpigmentation of previously irradiated skin.
    - **Neurological:** Peripheral neuropathy, lethargy, and optic neuropathy.
    - **Respiratory:** Pneumonitis and pneumothorax (related to anti-tumour effect).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic:** Potentiation of radiation therapy.
- **Laboratory Interference:** Dactinomycin may interfere with bioassay procedures used to determine antibacterial drug levels [Australian].
- **Management:** No specific drug-drug pharmacokinetic interactions were identified in the source [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy Category:** **Category D** [Australian].
- **Risk Summary:** Animal studies show malformations and embryotoxicity at 3–7 times the human dose. Use only if the benefit justifies the risk to the foetus.
- **Contraception:** 
    - **Females:** Use effective contraception during and for at least **7 months** after the final dose [Australian].
    - **Males:** Use effective contraception during and for at least **4 months** after the final dose [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if dactinomycin is excreted in human milk. Due to potential serious adverse reactions in infants, a decision must be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use with extreme caution in infants. Toxic effects occur with greater frequency; the drug should generally only be given to infants over 6–12 months of age [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical experience suggests a potentially increased risk of myelosuppression in patients ≥65 years old compared to younger patients [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Specific dose adjustments are not provided in the source, but frequent monitoring of renal and hepatic function is advised [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A cytotoxic antibiotic that inhibits RNA synthesis by binding to DNA. It exerts an inhibitory effect on bacteria and fungi, but its toxicity limits its use to antineoplastic therapy.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Concentrated in nucleated cells; does not penetrate the blood-brain barrier.
    - **Metabolism:** Minimally metabolised.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 30% of the dose is recovered in urine and faeces within one week.
    - **Half-life:** The terminal plasma half-life for radioactivity is approximately 36 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **National Wilms’ Tumour Study:** Demonstrated that combination therapy with dactinomycin and vincristine, alongside surgery and radiotherapy, significantly improved the prognosis for patients in groups II and III. The regimen involved seven cycles over approximately 15 months [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store below 25°C in a dry place. Protect from light.
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Does not contain preservatives. Stability is maintained for up to 10 hours at room temperature, but it is recommended to use the product as soon as practicable. If stored, it may be kept at 2–8°C for a maximum of 24 hours [Australian].
- **Disposal:** Unused solution/vials should be destroyed using 5% trisodium phosphate for 30 minutes or via high-temperature incineration (982°C–1204°C).

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
