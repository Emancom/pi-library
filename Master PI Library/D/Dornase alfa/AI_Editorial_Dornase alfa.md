# Editorial Product Information: Dornase alfa

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI. Clinicians must consult the local approved PI for prescribing and legal compliance in their specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Dornase alfa (recombinant human deoxyribonuclease I; rhDNase)
- **Chemical Class:** Mucolytic enzyme
- **Brand Name(s):** **Pulmozyme** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** R05CB13
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Clear, colourless to slightly yellowish nebuliser solution. 1.0 mg/mL in 2.5 mL single-use ampoules (Total 2.5 mg per ampoule) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Inhalation via nebuliser.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Roche Products Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Cystic Fibrosis Management:** Indicated for the chronic management of patients with cystic fibrosis (CF) to improve pulmonary function and manage respiratory complications [Australian].
- **Note on Continued Use:** Ongoing therapy should be contingent upon demonstrating sustained clinical benefit, typically assessed through clinical response and pulmonary function testing [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Standard Adult and Paediatric Dose:** One 2.5 mg (2.5 mL) ampoule inhaled once daily [Australian].
- **Modified Dosing:**
    - Some patients over the age of 21 may derive additional benefit from twice-daily (bd) administration [Australian].
    - Patients with a baseline Forced Vital Capacity (FVC) > 85% may also benefit from twice-daily dosing [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Use the complete contents (2.5 mL) of a single-use ampoule.
    - Administer via a jet nebuliser connected to a compressed air source with a nominal flow rate of 6–8 L/minute.
    - **Mixing:** Do not dilute or mix dornase alfa with other medications or solutions in the nebuliser bowl [Australian].
    - **Nebuliser Type:** Ultrasonic nebulisers should **not** be used as they may inactivate the enzyme or result in inadequate aerosol delivery [Australian].
    - **Physiotherapy:** Patients should maintain their standard chest physiotherapy regimen.
- **Administration Timing:** No specific optimal time of day has been established; however, daily consistency is encouraged [Australian].
- **Dose Limitation:** Recommended doses should not be exceeded; higher doses increase the risk of "irritant" side effects without providing additional clinical efficacy [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to dornase alfa, Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cell products, or any excipients (calcium chloride dihydrate, sodium chloride, water for injections) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
- **Traceability:** To ensure proper monitoring of biological products, the trade name (**Pulmozyme**) should be clearly documented in the patient’s medical record. Substitution with any other biological product requires the explicit consent of the prescriber [Australian].
- **Standard Medical Care:** Patients must continue to receive comprehensive medical care for cystic fibrosis while on therapy.
- **Initial Therapy:** As with any aerosol therapy, a transient decline in pulmonary function and an increase in sputum expectoration may occur upon starting treatment [Australian].
- **Nebuliser Maintenance:** Patients must be trained on the proper care and maintenance of the specific nebuliser and compressor equipment used for delivery [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions attributed specifically to dornase alfa are rare (< 1/1000). Most reported events are sequelae of the underlying cystic fibrosis disease and are typically mild and transient [Australian].

| System Organ Class | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Eye Disorders** | Conjunctivitis |
| **Respiratory/Thoracic** | Dysphonia (voice alteration), dyspnoea, pharyngitis, laryngitis, rhinitis (non-infectious) |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Dyspepsia |
| **Skin/Subcutaneous** | Rash, urticaria |
| **General Disorders** | Pleuritic/non-cardiac chest pain, pyrexia |
| **Investigations** | Decreased pulmonary function tests (transient) |

- **Dose-Dependency:** The incidence of adverse events may increase with higher-than-recommended doses [Australian].
- **Immunogenicity:** Fewer than 5% of patients develop antibodies to dornase alfa; no patients have developed IgE antibodies, and the presence of antibodies does not appear to diminish the clinical efficacy of the drug [Australian].
- **Serious Allergic Reactions:** No reports of anaphylaxis have been associated with administration [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Standard CF Therapies:** Dornase alfa has been safely used in conjunction with standard cystic fibrosis treatments, including antibiotics, bronchodilators, pancreatic enzymes (digestives), vitamins, analgesics, and both inhaled and systemic corticosteroids [Australian].
- **Incompatibilities:** As an unbuffered solution, dornase alfa is physically and chemically incompatible when mixed with other drugs in the nebuliser, which may lead to structural changes in the enzyme [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B1:** Animal studies (rats/rabbits) using high intravenous doses (10 mg/kg/day) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. There are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use only if clearly needed [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** 
    - Systemic absorption is minimal following inhalation, and dornase alfa is not expected to be measurable in human milk. Any ingested enzyme would likely be degraded in the infant's gastrointestinal tract. Exercise caution when administering to breastfeeding women [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No impact on male or female fertility was observed in rat studies at intravenous doses up to 10 mg/kg/day [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:**
    - **Ages 5–17:** Safety and efficacy are well established in this group.
    - **Infants and Children (< 5 years):** Experience is limited. Clinical studies (including infants as young as 3 months) demonstrated adequate lung deposition. In children under 5 who cannot inhale/exhale orally, use of a tight-fitting mask is recommended [Australian].
    - **Safety Nuance:** Younger patients (3 months to < 5 years) reported a higher incidence of cough and rhinitis compared to older children [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Data regarding the use of dornase alfa in patients over 65 years of age are not available [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Dornase alfa is a recombinant version of the human DNase I enzyme. It works by cleaving extracellular DNA found in the purulent secretions of CF patients. High concentrations of DNA (derived from degenerating neutrophils) contribute to the high viscosity of CF sputum; enzymatic cleavage reduces this viscosity, aiding airflow and clearance [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Low systemic exposure. Systemic absorption following inhalation in humans is minimal. Serum DNase levels do not significantly increase above normal endogenous levels [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Mean sputum concentrations of ~3 µg/mL are reached within 15 minutes of inhalation [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Expected to be broken down by proteases in biological fluids.
    - **Elimination:** Following intravenous administration, the serum half-life is 3–4 hours. Following inhalation, the half-life of dornase alfa in the lungs is approximately 11 hours in animal models. Sputum levels decline by half within 2 hours in humans, though rheological effects persist for over 12 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Patients with FVC > 40% Predicted:** A 24-week trial of 968 patients showed that 2.5 mg once or twice daily reduced the relative risk of respiratory tract infections requiring parenteral antibiotics by 27% and 29%, respectively. Significant improvements in FEV1 (mean 5.8%) were also noted [Australian].
- **Patients with FVC < 40% Predicted:** A 12-week trial showed significant improvements in FEV1 (9.4% relative increase) and FVC (12.4% relative increase) compared to placebo [Australian].
- **Early Intervention (Ages 6–10):** A 2-year study in children with FVC > 85% predicted showed a maintenance of FEV1 at baseline levels compared to a decline in the placebo group, and a 34% reduction in the risk of respiratory exacerbations [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store under refrigeration at 2°C to 8°C. Protect from light [Australian].
- **Handling:** Keep ampoules in their protective foil pouch within the outer carton.
- **Temperature Excursion:** A single brief exposure to elevated temperatures (up to 30°C) for no more than 24 hours does not affect stability [Australian].
- **Disposal:** Once an ampoule is opened, the entire contents must be used or discarded; the product contains no preservatives [Australian]. Do not use if the solution is cloudy or discoloured.
