# Editorial Product Information: Dacarbazine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- This document is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing in any particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Dacarbazine (a structural analogue of 5-amino-imidazole-4-carboxamide and an alkylating agent).
- **Brand Names:** DBL Dacarbazine [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01AX04.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Powder for injection; 200 mg per vial.
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metastatic Malignant Melanoma:** For the chemotherapy of metastatic disease.
- **Sarcomas:** For the treatment of various sarcomas. 
- **Note on Efficacy:** Available evidence suggests dacarbazine is less effective or ineffective in other types of cancer compared to established regimens [Australian]. Use is restricted to hospitals with dedicated oncology services.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Two primary regimens are commonly employed:
1. **Body Weight-Based:** 4.5 mg/kg/day administered for 10 consecutive days. This course may be repeated every 4 weeks.
   - *Note:* A lower dose of 2 mg/kg/day for 10 days has also been reported as effective [Australian].
2. **Body Surface Area-Based:** 250 mg/m²/day administered for 5 consecutive days. This course may be repeated every 3 weeks.

### Administration Instructions
- **Reconstitution:** Dissolve 200 mg in 19.7 mL of Water for Injections to achieve a 10 mg/mL concentration (pH 3.0 to 4.0). The resulting solution is hypotonic.
- **Injection:** Administer via intravenous injection over approximately one minute. 
- **Extravasation Risk:** Immediate cessation is required if extravasation occurs; it can result in severe pain and significant tissue damage.
- **Preparation Safety:** Preparation must be performed by trained personnel in a cytotoxic laminar flow cabinet. Use of Luer-Lock syringes and large-bore needles is recommended to prevent aerosolisation.

### Clinical Response
Efficacy is typically observed after the second course of treatment. If a patient relapses, subsequent courses of dacarbazine are unlikely to be effective.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated due to teratogenic and carcinogenic potential [Australian].
- **Lactation:** Breastfeeding is contraindicated during treatment.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to dacarbazine or any excipients (mannitol, citric acid monohydrate).
- **Bone Marrow Status:** Patients with a history of severe myelosuppression.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hematologic Toxicity:** The drug may cause severe, potentially fatal hematopoietic depression. Monitoring of red blood cells, white blood cells, and platelets is mandatory. Leucopenia and thrombocytopenia may be delayed and cumulative.
- **Hepatic Toxicity:** A rare (0.01%) but potentially fatal hepatic vein thrombosis and hepatocellular necrosis (Budd-Chiari Syndrome) has been reported. Monitor liver size and function (including eosinophil counts) frequently.
- **Gastrointestinal Effects:** Severe nausea and vomiting occur in most patients (90%) during the first two days. Restriction of food intake for 4–6 hours pre-treatment and prophylactic antiemetics (5HT3 blockers/dexamethasone) are recommended.
- **Infection and Dental Care:** Bone marrow suppression increases infection risk and delays healing. Dental work should be completed before starting therapy or deferred until blood counts normalise.
- **Vaccinations:** Live virus vaccines are contraindicated due to the risk of serious or fatal infections. Immunisation with oral poliovirus vaccines should be postponed in close contacts of the patient.
- **Handling:** It is recommended that pregnant personnel do not handle this cytotoxic agent.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Hepatic Enzyme Inducers:** Drugs like barbiturates, rifampicin, and phenytoin may theoretically accelerate the activation of dacarbazine via CYP1A1, CYP1A2, and CYP2E1.
- **Phenytoin:** Concomitant use is not recommended due to reduced phenytoin absorption and risk of exacerbated convulsions.
- **Fotemustine:** Concurrent use is contraindicated. Sequential administration (dacarbazine 400–1000 mg/m² and fotemustine 100 mg/m²) has been linked to acute lung toxicity (ARDS). Dacarbazine should only be given one week after fotemustine.
- **Xanthine Oxidase Inhibitors:** Dacarbazine may potentiate mercaptopurine, azathioprine, and allopurinol.
- **Interleukin-2:** May increase dacarbazine clearance (approx. 38%) and volume of distribution.
- **Immunosuppressants:** Ciclosporin and tacrolimus may cause excessive immunosuppression and lymphoproliferation risk.
- **Levodopa:** Dacarbazine may reduce the clinical response to levodopa.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Dacarbazine is teratogenic and carcinogenic in animal studies. It is contraindicated in pregnant women. 
- **Contraception:** Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during and for at least 6 months after the final dose. Male patients should use effective contraception during and for at least 3 months after the final dose [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Distribution into breast milk is unknown; due to potential risks, it is contraindicated.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No specific information available to suggest different dosage requirements or metabolic responses compared to adults.
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available; clinical caution is advised.
- **Renal Impairment:** Approximately 50% of the drug is excreted unchanged in the urine via tubular secretion. Dosage adjustment is likely necessary.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** As the drug undergoes hepatic metabolism, impairment will likely necessitate dosage variation.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** While the exact mechanism is unconfirmed, three pathways are proposed: 
    1. Interference with purine biosynthesis as a structural analogue of 5-amino-imidazole-4-carboxamide.
    2. Alkylation via the metabolite diazomethane.
    3. Functioning as a sulphydryl reagent.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Poor and erratic via the GI tract; IV administration is mandatory.
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution exceeds total body water, suggesting tissue localisation (likely liver). Crosses the blood-brain barrier to a limited extent (14% of plasma levels). 
    - **Metabolism:** N-demethylated by liver microsomal enzymes. 
    - **Excretion:** Biphasic elimination. Initial half-life (t½α) is ~2.9 minutes; terminal half-life (t½β) is 41.4 to 75 minutes. In patients with renal/hepatic dysfunction, terminal half-life can extend to 7.2 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Metastatic Malignant Melanoma:** In a data set of 1,427 patients, a total response rate of 20.3% was observed (5.7% complete remission, 14.6% partial remission). Remission duration ranged from 5 to 100 weeks, with a median of approximately 6 months [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store between 2°C and 8°C. Refrigerate; do not freeze. Protect from light.
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Chemically stable for 8 hours at 25°C or 24 hours at 2°C to 8°C. To minimise microbiological risk, use as soon as possible after preparation.
- **Disposal:** All waste and spillages must be treated as cytotoxic. Incinerate waste at 1100°C.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
