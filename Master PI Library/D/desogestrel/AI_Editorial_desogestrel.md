# Editorial Product Information: Desogestrel and Ethinylestradiol

**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been compiled by synthesizing official regulatory data from the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). Where clinical recommendations or risk assessments vary between jurisdictions, the more comprehensive or conservative guidance has been prioritized, with the source agency noted in brackets.

---

# 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** Desogestrel and Ethinylestradiol Tablets
**Active Ingredients:** 
*   Desogestrel
*   Ethinylestradiol

**Formulations:**
*   **Regimen A [TGA]:** 21 active tablets (150 micrograms desogestrel / 30 micrograms ethinylestradiol) and 7 inert placebo tablets.
*   **Regimen B [FDA]:** 21 active tablets (0.15 mg desogestrel / 0.02 mg ethinylestradiol), 2 inert tablets, and 5 supplementary active tablets (0.01 mg ethinylestradiol).

# 2. Approved Indications
This medicinal product is indicated for oral contraception for the prevention of pregnancy in women who choose this method of birth control.

# 3. Contraindications
Combined oral contraceptives (COCs) must not be used in the presence of the following conditions. If any of these appear during use, the medication should be discontinued immediately:

*   **Circulatory Disorders:** Current or history of venous thromboembolism (VTE), including deep venous thrombosis (DVT) and pulmonary embolism (PE); current or history of arterial thromboembolism (ATE), such as myocardial infarction or stroke; prodromal conditions (e.g., angina pectoris or TIA).
*   **Thromboembolic Risk Factors:** Known hereditary or acquired predispositions for VTE/ATE (e.g., Factor V Leiden, Protein C/S deficiency, antiphospholipid antibodies); major surgery with prolonged immobilization; presence of multiple risk factors for vascular disease.
*   **Neurological:** History of migraine associated with focal neurological symptoms.
*   **Hepatic:** Current or history of severe hepatic disease until liver function markers return to normal; presence or history of liver tumors (benign or malignant); cholestatic jaundice.
*   **Malignancy:** Known or suspected malignancies influenced by sex steroids (e.g., breast or genital organ cancers).
*   **Unexplained Pathology:** Undiagnosed abnormal vaginal bleeding.
*   **Pregnancy:** Known or suspected pregnancy.
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to any active or inactive ingredient.
*   **Concomitant Therapy:** Co-administration with Hepatitis C virus combinations containing ombitasvir/paritaprevir/ritonavir, with or without dasabuvir, or glecaprevir/pibrentasvir [TGA].

# 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Serious Cardiovascular Events and Smoking
**Boxed Warning [FDA]:** Cigarette smoking significantly increases the risk of serious cardiovascular side effects from COC use. This risk is age-dependent and increases with heavy smoking (15 or more cigarettes per day). COCs are strictly contraindicated in women over 35 years of age who smoke.

### Thromboembolic Risks
Use of any COC increases the risk of VTE. Products containing desogestrel are associated with a higher risk of VTE (9–12 cases per 10,000 woman-years) compared to those containing levonorgestrel (5–7 cases per 10,000 woman-years) [TGA]. Practitioners should be vigilant for symptoms of DVT (unilateral leg swelling, pain) and PE (sudden shortness of breath, chest pain).

### Neoplasms
*   **Breast Cancer:** A slightly increased relative risk (RR = 1.24) of breast cancer has been observed in current COC users, which gradually disappears over 10 years following cessation.
*   **Cervical Cancer:** Long-term use (>5 years) may be associated with increased risk, though this may be confounded by sexual behavior and Human Papilloma Virus (HPV).

### Other Precautions
*   **Hepatitis C:** Significant ALT elevations have been observed when COCs are used concurrently with certain HCV antiviral regimens.
*   **Blood Pressure:** Small increases in blood pressure are common; clinically significant hypertension requires discontinuation.
*   **Metabolic Effects:** COCs may impact peripheral insulin resistance and glucose tolerance; diabetic women require careful monitoring.
*   **Chloasma:** Women with a history of chloasma gravidarum should avoid excessive UV exposure while on the pill.

# 5. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
One tablet must be taken daily at approximately the same time for 28 consecutive days. Tablets must be taken in the order prescribed on the packaging.

### Initiation of Therapy
*   **No Preceding Hormonal Contraceptive:** Begin on Day 1 of the natural menstrual cycle. If starting between Days 2–5, a non-hormonal barrier method (e.g., condoms) must be used for the first 7 days of tablet-taking.
*   **Switching from other COCs:** Start the day after the last active tablet of the previous pack, or at the latest, the day following the usual tablet-free/placebo interval.
*   **Post-Partum:** Initiation should occur 21–28 days after delivery in non-lactating women [TGA]. The FDA recommends waiting 4 weeks postpartum due to increased thromboembolic risk.

### Management of Missed Tablets
*   **Delay < 12 Hours:** Contraceptive protection is maintained. Take the missed tablet immediately and continue the schedule as normal.
*   **Delay > 12 Hours:** Contraceptive protection may be reduced. 
    1.  Take the most recent missed tablet immediately (even if it means taking two tablets in one day).
    2.  Use a barrier method for the following 7 days.
    3.  If active tablets remain in the pack for fewer than 7 days, skip the placebo/inert interval and start the next pack immediately.

# 6. Adverse Effects
Commonly reported adverse reactions include:
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, abdominal pain, vomiting, or diarrhea.
*   **Reproductive:** Breast tenderness, pain, or enlargement; irregular bleeding (spotting or breakthrough bleeding); changes in vaginal secretion.
*   **Neurological:** Headache or migraine.
*   **Psychiatric:** Depressed mood or altered libido.
*   **Investigations:** Weight gain or fluid retention.

Serious but rare reactions include VTE, ATE, hypertension, liver tumors, and gallbladder disease.

# 7. Drug Interactions
### Effects of Other Drugs on COCs
Hepatic enzyme inducers (CYP450) can increase the clearance of sex hormones, potentially leading to breakthrough bleeding or contraceptive failure. These include:
*   **Anticonvulsants:** Phenytoin, carbamazepine, phenobarbital, topiramate.
*   **Antivirals:** Ritonavir, efavirenz, nevirapine.
*   **Herbal Remedies:** St. John's Wort.
*   **Antibiotics:** Rifampicin (significant interaction); interactions with other antibiotics like ampicillin or tetracycline are less established but documented [FDA].

### Effects of COCs on Other Drugs
COCs may interfere with the metabolism of other medications, increasing plasma levels (e.g., cyclosporine) or decreasing them (e.g., lamotrigine). Dose adjustments for lamotrigine may be necessary to maintain seizure control.

# 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** COCs are contraindicated during pregnancy. If pregnancy occurs, the medication should be stopped immediately. Epidemiological data do not suggest an increased risk of birth defects in women who inadvertently used COCs in early pregnancy.
*   **Breastfeeding:** COCs may reduce the volume and quality of breast milk. Small amounts of contraceptive steroids are excreted in milk. Use is generally not recommended until the infant is fully weaned.

# 9. Pharmacokinetics
### Desogestrel
*   **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed and converted to the active metabolite, etonogestrel. Bioavailability is 62–81%.
*   **Distribution:** Etonogestrel is 99% bound to serum proteins, primarily albumin and Sex Hormone Binding Globulin (SHBG).
*   **Metabolism:** Hydroxylated in the liver and intestinal mucosa.
*   **Excretion:** Metabolites are excreted in urine and bile (ratio 6:4). Terminal half-life is approximately 30 hours.

### Ethinylestradiol
*   **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed with peak concentrations in 1–2 hours. Bioavailability is approximately 60% due to first-pass metabolism.
*   **Distribution:** Highly (98.5%) but non-specifically bound to albumin.
*   **Metabolism:** Subject to significant presystemic conjugation; primarily metabolized via aromatic hydroxylation.
*   **Excretion:** Excreted as conjugates in urine and bile (ratio 4:6). Half-life is approximately 24 hours.

# 10. Mechanism of Action
COCs provide contraception through the synergy of several factors:
1.  **Inhibition of Ovulation:** Suppression of gonadotropins (LH and FSH) prevents follicular development.
2.  **Cervical Mucus Alteration:** Increases viscosity of cervical mucus, impeding sperm migration.
3.  **Endometrial Changes:** Alters the lining of the uterus to reduce the likelihood of implantation.

# 11. Storage Conditions
Store below 30°C (86°F) [TGA]. The FDA recommends a controlled room temperature of 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Protect from light and moisture. Keep out of reach of children.
