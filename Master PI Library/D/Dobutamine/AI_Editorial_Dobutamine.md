# Editorial Product Information: Dobutamine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)** regulatory authority. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data into a concise, structured format.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource only. It is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing or dispensing in any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Dobutamine hydrochloride (a synthetic sympathomimetic amine).
- **Chemical Class:** Inotropic agent; Beta-1 adrenergic agonist.
- **Brand Name(s):** DBL Dobutamine Hydrochloride Injection [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** C01CA07.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 280.2 mg dobutamine hydrochloride (equivalent to 250 mg dobutamine) in a 20 mL sterile solution for injection [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Short-term treatment of cardiac failure:** Indicated for adults requiring inotropic support due to cardiac failure secondary to acute myocardial infarction or cardiac surgery [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** The standard infusion rate to increase cardiac output is **2.5 to 10 micrograms/kg/min**. Rates up to 40 micrograms/kg/min have been required in rare instances to achieve the desired clinical effect [Australian].
- **Titration:** Dosage must be individualised and titrated based on patient response, including heart rate, blood pressure, presence of ectopic activity, urine flow, and (where possible) central venous or pulmonary wedge pressure and cardiac output [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    *   **Dilution:** Must be diluted to at least 50 mL in 5% Glucose or 0.9% Sodium Chloride injection prior to administration.
    *   **Maximum Concentration:** Concentrations up to 5000 micrograms/mL (250 mg in 50 mL) have been used in humans.
    *   **Incompatibilities:** Do not add to 5% Sodium Bicarbonate or other strongly alkaline solutions. Do not use with agents or diluents containing sodium bisulfite [Australian].
- **Stability:** Once diluted, the solution is chemically stable for 24 hours but should ideally be used immediately [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Idiopathic Hypertrophic Subaortic Stenosis (IHSS):** Contraindicated due to the risk of worsening outflow obstruction [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Previous hypersensitivity to dobutamine or any excipients (including sodium metabisulfite) [Australian].
- **Mechanical Obstruction:** Contraindicated where mechanical factors affect ventricular filling or outflow, such as obstructive cardiomyopathy or constrictive pericarditis [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** Dobutamine may cause significant increases in heart rate (≥30 bpm) or systolic blood pressure (≥50 mmHg). Patients with pre-existing hypertension are at higher risk of an exaggerated pressor response [Australian].
- **Atrioventricular Conduction:** The drug facilitates AV conduction. Patients with atrial fibrillation are at risk of developing a rapid ventricular response; digitalis should be used prior to dobutamine in these patients [Australian].
- **Ectopic Activity:** May precipitate or exacerbate ventricular ectopic activity. Caution is advised in patients with a history of severe ventricular arrhythmia [Australian].
- **Myocardial Ischaemia:** In the context of acute myocardial infarction, there is a risk that increased myocardial oxygen demand may intensify ischaemia and extend the size of the infarction. Dosing must be titrated to avoid excessive increases in heart rate or blood pressure [Australian].
- **Hypovolaemia:** Should be corrected with volume expanders before initiating dobutamine [Australian].
- **Sulfite Sensitivity:** Contains sodium metabisulfite, which may cause anaphylactic symptoms or life-threatening asthmatic episodes in susceptible individuals [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Continuous ECG and blood pressure monitoring is mandatory. Pulmonary wedge pressure and cardiac output should be monitored whenever possible [Australian].
- **Metabolic Effects:** May produce a mild reduction in serum potassium; monitoring is recommended [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally dose-related extensions of the drug's pharmacological activity.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Increase in systolic blood pressure (10–20 mmHg), increase in heart rate (5–15 bpm) [Australian]. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Increased premature ventricular beats (approx. 5%) [Australian]. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Nausea, headache, anginal pain, non-specific chest pain, palpitations, shortness of breath (reported in 1–3% of patients) [Australian]. |
| **Rare (<0.1%) / Isolated** | Thrombocytopenia, cutaneous necrosis (following inadvertent infiltration), phlebitis, eosinophilic myocarditis, myocardial rupture [Australian]. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Ventricular tachycardia, ventricular fibrillation, myocardial ischaemia, and anaphylactic reactions (due to sulfites) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Beta-Adrenergic Antagonists:** May decrease dobutamine potency. Unopposed alpha-agonist effects may lead to peripheral vasoconstriction and hypertension [Australian].
- **Alpha-Adrenergic Blockers:** May enhance beta-mediated effects, potentially resulting in tachycardia and vasodilation [Australian].
- **Anaesthetics:** Myocardium may be sensitised to dobutamine by cyclopropane or halogenated hydrocarbon anaesthetics (e.g., halothane); these combinations should be avoided [Australian].
- **Vasodilators:** Concomitant use with nitroprusside or glyceryl trinitrate (GTN) typically results in higher cardiac output and lower pulmonary wedge pressure compared to dobutamine alone [Australian].
- **Entacapone:** May enhance the effects of dobutamine [Australian].
- **Other:** No evidence of interaction with digitalis, furosemide, spironolactone, lidocaine, morphine, atropine, heparin, protamine, potassium chloride, folic acid, or paracetamol [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B2** [Australian]. There are no adequate or well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use only if potential benefits outweigh risks to the foetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if dobutamine is excreted in human milk. If administration to a nursing mother is necessary, breastfeeding should be suspended for the duration of exposure [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Data not specified in available sources [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available in the provided source [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A direct-acting inotropic agent that primarily stimulates cardiac beta-1 receptors. It produces comparatively mild chronotropic, hypertensive, and vasodilative effects. It increases stroke volume and cardiac output with less increase in heart rate than isoprenaline [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:**
    *   **Onset of Action:** Within 1 to 2 minutes.
    *   **Peak Effect:** May take up to 10 minutes for a specific infusion rate [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Metabolism:** Rapidly metabolised in the liver and other tissues by catechol-O-methyltransferase (COMT) and conjugation [Australian].
    *   **Elimination:** Plasma half-life is approximately 2 minutes. Excreted in urine primarily as conjugates of dobutamine and 3-O-methyl dobutamine (inactive) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Summary:** Clinical experience is primarily short-term (up to several hours). Studies lasting 24, 48, and 72 hours have shown a persistent increase in cardiac output in some patients, while others returned toward baseline. No adverse effects unique to prolonged infusions (up to 72 hours) were identified [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C. Protect from light [Australian].
- **Diluted Solution:** When diluted to 250–500 micrograms/mL in 0.9% NaCl or 5% Glucose, it is stable for 24 hours at room temperature under fluorescent light [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **S4** (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
