# Editorial Product Information: Dorzolamide and Timolol

## Editorial Note
- List of contributing jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of the product information for **dorzolamide** and **timolol** combination therapy, intended for use by healthcare professionals.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis of available regulatory data. It does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Always consult local regulatory guidelines before clinical application.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Dorzolamide hydrochloride (carbonic anhydrase inhibitor) and Timolol maleate (non-selective beta-adrenergic receptor blocker).
- **Brand Names:** COSDOR [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** S01ED51.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile ophthalmic solution containing dorzolamide 20 mg/mL (2.0% w/v) and timolol 5 mg/mL (0.5% w/v) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Topical ophthalmic.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Viatris Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Elevated Intraocular Pressure (IOP):** Indicated for the reduction of elevated IOP in patients with open-angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension when treatment with a combination product is clinically appropriate [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Standard Adult Dose:** One drop in the affected eye(s) twice daily [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - For individual patient use only.
    - If multiple topical ophthalmic agents are being used, they should be administered at least 10 minutes apart [Australian].
    - Patients should be instructed to wash hands before use and avoid contact between the dispensing tip and the eye or surrounding structures to prevent contamination.
- **Transitioning Therapy:** When replacing other antiglaucoma agents, the previous medication(s) should be discontinued after a full day of dosing, and **dorzolamide/timolol** should be initiated on the following day [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
**Dorzolamide/timolol** is contraindicated in patients with:
- **Respiratory Disease:** Reactive airway disease, bronchial asthma (current or historical), or severe chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) [Australian].
- **Cardiac Disorders:** Sinus bradycardia, sick sinus syndrome, sino-atrial block, second or third-degree atrioventricular (AV) block, overt cardiac failure, or cardiogenic shock [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Severe renal impairment (CrCl < 30 mL/min) [Australian].
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Hyperchloremic acidosis [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to any component of the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Systemic Absorption:** Although administered topically, both components are absorbed systemically. The timolol component may cause cardiovascular and respiratory adverse effects similar to systemic beta-blockers [Australian].
- **Cardiac/Vascular Cautions:** 
    - Use with caution in patients with first-degree heart block due to negative effects on conduction time.
    - Monitor patients with cardiovascular disease for signs of deterioration or pulse rate changes.
    - Exercise caution in patients with severe peripheral circulatory disturbances (e.g., Raynaud's phenomenon) [Australian].
- **Respiratory Effects:** Severe respiratory reactions, including death from bronchospasm in asthmatic patients, have been reported following timolol administration [Australian].
- **Masking of Symptoms:** Beta-blockers may mask symptoms of acute hypoglycaemia in diabetic patients and clinical signs of hyperthyroidism (e.g., tachycardia) [Australian].
- **Sulfonamide Sensitivity:** As a sulfonamide, dorzolamide may cause systemic hypersensitivity reactions (e.g., Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis) even with topical use [Australian].
- **Ocular Precautions:** 
    - **Corneal Effects:** Use with caution in patients with low endothelial cell counts or pre-existing corneal defects, as there is an increased risk of corneal oedema.
    - **Choroidal Detachment:** Reported after filtration procedures with aqueous suppressant therapy [Australian].
    - **Preservative Sensitivity:** Formulations containing benzalkonium chloride may cause eye irritation and discolour soft contact lenses. Lenses should be removed prior to application and reinserted no sooner than 15 minutes post-dose [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Approximately 2.4% of patients in clinical trials discontinued therapy due to local ocular adverse reactions [Australian].

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Ocular burning and stinging, taste perversion. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Conjunctival injection, blurred vision, corneal erosion, ocular itching, tearing, blepharitis, headache, sinusitis, nausea, asthenia/fatigue. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Iridocyclitis, urolithiasis (rarely reported). |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Bradycardia, heart block, hypotension, bronchospasm, respiratory failure, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, systemic lupus erythematosus. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Cardiac failure, cardiac arrest, cerebrovascular accident, and anaphylaxis have been associated with the individual components [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Systemic Beta-blockers:** Potential for additive effects on IOP or systemic beta-blockade; concomitant use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Catecholamine-depleting drugs:** Risk of hypotension or marked bradycardia when used with agents like reserpine or guanethidine.
- **CYP2D6 Inhibitors:** Potentiated systemic beta-blockade (e.g., decreased heart rate, depression) may occur when timolol is used with quinidine or SSRIs [Australian].
- **Calcium Channel Blockers/Antiarrhythmics:** Increased risk of AV conduction disturbances, left ventricular failure, and hypotension.
- **Carbonic Anhydrase Inhibitors:** Potential for additive systemic effects; concomitant use with oral CAIs is not recommended [Australian].
- **Acid-Base Balance:** High-dose salicylate therapy may increase the risk of toxicity when used with dorzolamide [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Beta-adrenergic blockers may cause bradycardia in the fetus or newborn. Use only if the potential benefit outweighs the risk to the fetus. Malformations were observed in rabbit studies at maternotoxic doses [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Timolol is excreted in human milk. It is unknown if dorzolamide is excreted in human milk (though it is present in rat milk). A decision should be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the mother's clinical need [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were noted in rat studies for either component at significant multiples of the human dose [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Efficacy not established. Safety not established for children < 2 years. Limited data for children aged 2–6 years suggests an adverse effect profile similar to adults [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy between elderly and younger patients, though greater sensitivity in some individuals cannot be ruled out [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe renal impairment (CrCl < 30 mL/min) [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Has not been studied; use with caution [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Dorzolamide:** Inhibits carbonic anhydrase II in the ciliary processes, reducing bicarbonate ion formation and subsequent sodium/fluid transport, thereby decreasing aqueous humour secretion.
    - **Timolol:** A non-selective beta-blocker that reduces aqueous humour formation [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Combined therapy provides additional IOP reduction compared to either agent alone without the miotic side effects (e.g., night blindness) seen with cholinergic agents [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Dorzolamide:** Reaches systemic circulation; accumulates in RBCs via binding to CA-II. Plasma concentrations remain low. Half-life is approximately four months.
    - **Timolol:** Systemically absorbed; peak plasma concentration following 0.5% BID dosing was 0.46 ng/mL [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- In studies up to 15 months, the IOP-lowering effect of **dorzolamide/timolol** BID was equivalent to concomitant therapy with dorzolamide BID and timolol BID.
- The combination provided significantly greater IOP reduction than monotherapy with either 2% dorzolamide TID or 0.5% timolol BID.
- Clinical equivalence was defined as an absolute difference in mean IOP change of < 1.5 mmHg [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C and protect from light [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Discard 28 days after the initial opening of the container [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
