# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory and clinical data into a single, concise reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule for fatty acid transport (amino acid derivative).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection solution: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose: 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2-to-3-minute bolus or by infusion [US].
    - Severe metabolic crisis: A loading dose may be given, followed by an equivalent dose over 24 hours.
    - Frequency: Administer every 3 or 4 hours (q3h or q4h); never less than every 6 hours (q6h) [US].
    - Maximum reported dose: 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
    - Administration: Slow 2-to-3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Monitoring and Titration
- **Pre-treatment:** Obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to parenteral therapy [US].
- **Frequency:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels [US].
- **Target Levels:** Maintain plasma free carnitine concentrations between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Dose Adjustments:** For ESRD patients, if trough (pre-dialysis) levels are below 40–50 µmol/L, initiation or adjustment is indicated. Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be performed as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use [US].
- Discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate risks before re-administration [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine may cause seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a known seizure history, an increase in frequency or severity has been reported [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Levocarnitine):** While this PI focuses on the injection, it notes that chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequencies are derived from clinical trials in chronic hemodialysis patients.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site reaction (38%), Pain (35%), Pharyngitis (27%), Headache (37% at 20 mg/kg dose), Hypertension (18–21%), Diarrhea (9–35%), Vomiting (9–21%), Nausea (5–12%), Dizziness (10–18%), Dyspnea (3–11%) [US]. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, fever, flu syndrome, infection, chest pain, palpitations, tachycardia, anorexia, constipation, dyspepsia, hypercalcemia, anemia, peripheral edema, weight changes, depression, paresthesia [US]. |
| **Serious/Postmarketing** | Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm, and seizures [US]. |

*Note: Incidence rates are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disorders [US].*

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalised Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Clinical Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dosage adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned in current labeling format. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm.
    - **Recommendation:** Should be used during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. 
    - **Recommendation:** Weigh the risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of supplementation for the mother. Consideration may be given to discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal reproductive studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Refer to the "Metabolic Disorders" dosing section (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - The injection is specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on hemodialysis [US]. 
    - Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours, and the mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

### Pharmacodynamics
In ESRD patients, IV administration of 20 mg/kg three times weekly results in a significant increase in plasma carnitine concentrations, often reaching levels well above the normal physiological range (40–50 µmol/L) within 1 to 8 weeks of therapy [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Study (Healthy Volunteers):** 15 healthy males were used to determine bioequivalence between oral forms and to establish IV distribution/elimination profiles [US].
- **ESRD Studies:**
    - A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg post-dialysis showed trough levels increased from ~20 µmol/L (baseline) to 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks [US].
    - Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, and 40 mg/kg) confirmed a dose-dependent increase in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Clinical Outcomes:** The effects of supplemental carnitine on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, cardiomyopathy) in the ESRD population have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing [US].
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL, stored at room temperature [US].
