# Editorial Product Information: Diclofenac Sodium

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following official Product Information (PI) source:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: APO-Diclofenac Enteric Coated Tablets.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a single clinical reference.
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or clinical guidelines required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Diclofenac sodium (phenylacetic acid derivative).
- **Brand Names**: APO-Diclofenac [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: M01AB05 (not explicitly in source, but standard for generic reference).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**: 
    - Enteric-coated tablets: 25 mg and 50 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Apotex Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Rheumatology**: Inflammatory and degenerative forms of rheumatism, specifically rheumatoid arthritis and osteoarthritis.
- **Pain Management**: Relief of acute or chronic pain states with an underlying inflammatory component.
- **Dysmenorrhoea**: Symptomatic treatment of primary dysmenorrhoea.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing**: Initial daily dosage is 75 mg to 150 mg. For long-term therapy, 75 mg to 100 mg daily is generally sufficient.
- **Dosing Schedule**: The total daily dose should be divided into two or three doses.
- **Indication-Specific Dosing**:
    - **Primary Dysmenorrhoea**: 50 mg to 150 mg daily, individually adapted. Initial dose is 50 mg to 100 mg, which may be increased over several menstrual cycles to a maximum of 200 mg/day. Treatment should commence at the first appearance of symptoms.
- **Administration Instructions**: 
    - Tablets must be swallowed whole with liquid, preferably before meals. 
    - Tablets must not be crushed, divided, or chewed to preserve the enteric coating.
- **Duration of Therapy**: Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration necessary to control symptoms.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Gastrointestinal**: Active gastric or duodenal ulcer, gastrointestinal bleeding, or perforation.
- **Hypersensitivity**: Known hypersensitivity to diclofenac or any excipients (e.g., lactose).
- **Cross-Reactivity**: History of asthma, angioedema, urticaria, or anaphylactic reactions induced by aspirin or other NSAIDs.
- **Organ Failure**: Severe hepatic impairment, renal failure, or severe cardiac failure.
- **Surgical**: Management of perioperative pain in the setting of Coronary Artery Bypass Surgery (CABG).
- **Pregnancy**: Third trimester of pregnancy [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiovascular (CV) Thrombotic Events**: NSAIDs are associated with an increased risk of serious CV events, including myocardial infarction and stroke. Risk may increase with dose and duration.
    - **Note**: Treatment is generally not recommended for patients with established CV disease, peripheral arterial disease, or uncontrolled hypertension. If essential, doses should be ≤100 mg daily if used for >4 weeks [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal (GI) Effects**: Serious GI toxicity (bleeding, ulceration, perforation) can occur at any time without warning. Risk is higher in the elderly, those with a history of ulcer, and patients using concurrent aspirin, corticosteroids, or anticoagulants.
- **Serious Skin Reactions**: Rare reports of exfoliative dermatitis, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN), and Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS). Discontinue at the first sign of rash or mucosal lesions.
- **Hepatotoxicity**: Elevations in liver enzymes (ALT/AST) occur frequently. Severe reactions (jaundice, fulminant hepatitis) are rare. Monitoring is recommended for long-term therapy.
- **Renal Effects**: NSAIDs may cause fluid retention, oedema, and renal papillary necrosis. Caution is required in patients with heart failure or renal impairment. 
- **"Triple Whammy" Risk**: Increased risk of renal impairment when combining an ACE inhibitor (or ARB), a thiazide diuretic, and an NSAID. Monitor serum creatinine closely.
- **Asthma**: Increased risk of bronchospasm in patients with pre-existing asthma or allergic rhinitis.
- **Masking Infection**: Diclofenac may mask the typical signs and symptoms of infection.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (≥1% to <10%)**: Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, dyspepsia, abdominal pain, flatulence, anorexia, headache, dizziness, vertigo, increased transaminases, and rash.
- **Uncommon (≥0.1% to <1%—typically at high doses/long-term)**: Myocardial infarction, cardiac failure, palpitations, chest pain.
- **Rare (≥0.01% to <0.1%)**: Somnolence, gastritis, GI haemorrhage, haematemesis, melaena, gastric/duodenal ulcer, hepatitis, jaundice, urticaria, oedema.
- **Very Rare (<0.01%)**: Thrombocytopenia, leucopoenia, anaemia, agranulocytosis, angioneurotic oedema, psychiatric disturbances (insomnia, depression, nightmares), paraesthesia, visual disturbances (blurred vision/diplopia), tinnitus, hypertension, vasculitis, pneumonitis, colitis, pancreatitis, fulminant hepatitis, bullous eruptions (SJS/TEN), acute kidney injury.
- **Frequency Unknown**: Kounis syndrome (allergic myocardial infarction), DRESS, oligohydramnios, neonatal renal impairment.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions**:
    - **Lithium/Digoxin**: Diclofenac may increase plasma concentrations; monitor levels.
    - **Methotrexate**: Diclofenac may increase methotrexate toxicity if administered within 24 hours of each other.
    - **CYP2C9 Inhibitors (e.g., voriconazole, sulfinpyrazone)**: May significantly increase diclofenac peak plasma levels.
    - **CYP2C9 Inducers (e.g., rifampicin)**: May significantly decrease diclofenac plasma concentrations.
    - **Phenytoin**: Monitor phenytoin levels due to potential increased exposure.
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions**:
    - **Anticoagulants/Anti-platelets**: Increased risk of GI bleeding and haemorrhage.
    - **Diuretics/Antihypertensives**: Reduced antihypertensive effect; increased risk of renal impairment (especially with potassium-sparing diuretics).
    - **SSRIs/Corticosteroids**: Increased risk of gastrointestinal ulceration or bleeding.
    - **Cyclosporin/Tacrolimus**: Increased risk of nephrotoxicity.
    - **Quinolones**: Isolated reports of convulsions.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **Category C [Australian]**: Not recommended in the first two trimesters unless essential.
    - **Second/Third Trimester**: Use from 20 weeks gestation may cause fetal renal dysfunction leading to oligohydramnios. Contraindicated in the third trimester due to risk of premature closure of the ductus arteriosus, uterine inertia, and neonatal renal impairment.
- **Breastfeeding**: Diclofenac passes into breast milk. Use is not recommended in nursing women [Australian].
- **Fertility**: May impair female fertility; not recommended in women attempting to conceive.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and efficacy have not been established; not recommended for use in children [Australian].
- **Elderly Use**: Higher risk of adverse effects. Use the lowest effective dose and monitor for GI bleeding and renal function.
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Close medical surveillance required. Contraindicated in severe impairment.
- **Renal Impairment**: Monitoring of renal function is required. Contraindicated in renal failure.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: A non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) that inhibits prostaglandin synthesis, providing antirheumatic, anti-inflammatory, analgesic, and antipyretic effects.
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: 100% absorbed from enteric-coated tablets. Peak plasma concentration (Cmax) is attained ~2 hours post-dose (fasting). Food delays the rate but not the extent of absorption.
    - **Distribution**: 99.7% protein bound (mainly albumin). Diclofenac enters the synovial fluid, reaching higher concentrations than plasma 4–6 hours after administration.
    - **Metabolism**: ~50% undergoes first-pass metabolism. Metabolism via glucuronidation and hydroxylation.
    - **Elimination**: Plasma half-life is 1–2 hours. ~60% excreted in urine as metabolites; <1% excreted unchanged. Remainder eliminated via bile.
