# Editorial Product Information: Darunavir

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidance required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Darunavir (as ethanolate).
- **Chemical Class:** Human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) protease inhibitor.
- **Brand Names:** **Darunavir Juno** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J05AE10.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 400 mg, 600 mg, and 800 mg [Australian]. 
    - *Note: 400 mg strength may be registered but not actively marketed in some regions [Australian].*
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Juno Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Adult Patients:** Indicated in combination with low-dose ritonavir and other antiretroviral agents for the treatment of HIV-1 infection [Australian].
- **Paediatric Patients:** Indicated in combination with low-dose ritonavir and other antiretroviral agents for the treatment of HIV infection in treatment-experienced paediatric patients aged 6 years and older, weighing at least 20 kg [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- **Boosted Regimen:** **Darunavir** must always be co-administered with low-dose ritonavir (as a pharmacokinetic enhancer) and taken with food. The type of food does not significantly affect exposure.
- **Instruction:** Tablets should be swallowed whole and not halved, as dose equivalence for divided tablets has not been established.

### Adult Dosing
Dosage is determined by the patient's treatment history and the presence of darunavir resistance-associated mutations (DRV-RAMs: V11I, V32I, L33F, I47V, I50V, I54M, I54L, T74P, L76V, I84V, L89V).

- **Once Daily Regimen:** 800 mg **darunavir** with 100 mg ritonavir once daily. Recommended for:
    - Antiretroviral treatment-naive patients.
    - Treatment-experienced patients with no DRV-RAMs and plasma HIV-1 RNA < 100,000 copies/mL.
    - PI-naive patients where genotype testing is unavailable.
- **Twice Daily Regimen:** 600 mg **darunavir** with 100 mg ritonavir twice daily. Recommended for:
    - Treatment-experienced patients with at least one DRV-RAM.
    - PI-experienced patients where genotype testing is unavailable.
    - Treatment-experienced patients with plasma HIV-1 RNA ≥ 100,000 copies/mL.

### Paediatric Dosing (Aged 6 to <18 years, ≥20 kg)
Dosing is weight-based and administered twice daily with ritonavir and food:
- **≥20 kg to <30 kg:** 375 mg **darunavir** / 50 mg ritonavir BID.
- **≥30 kg to <40 kg:** 450 mg **darunavir** / 60 mg ritonavir BID.
- **≥40 kg:** 600 mg **darunavir** / 100 mg ritonavir BID.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to **darunavir** or any excipients.
- **Drug-Drug Interactions:** Co-administration with drugs highly dependent on CYP3A for clearance where elevated plasma concentrations are associated with serious/life-threatening events, including:
    - **Alpha blockers:** Alfuzosin.
    - **Anti-anginals:** Ranolazine, ivabradine.
    - **Antiarrhythmics:** Amiodarone, bepridil, dronedarone, flecainide, lidocaine (systemic), quinidine.
    - **Antihistamines:** Astemizole, terfenadine.
    - **Antipsychotics:** Pimozide, lurasidone.
    - **Ergot derivatives:** Ergotamine, dihydroergotamine, methylergonovine.
    - **GI motility agents:** Cisapride.
    - **HMG-CoA Reductase Inhibitors:** Lovastatin, simvastatin.
    - **PDE-5 inhibitors:** Sildenafil (when used for pulmonary arterial hypertension).
    - **Direct Oral Anticoagulants (DOACs):** Apixaban.
    - **Other:** Naloxegol, dapoxetine, elbasvir/grazoprevir, lomitapide.
- **CYP3A Inducers:** Rifampicin and St. John’s Wort (due to significant reduction in **darunavir** levels).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Drug-induced hepatitis has been reported (0.5% in clinical trials). Monitor liver function tests (AST/ALT) prior to and during therapy, especially in patients with pre-existing liver disease (HBV/HCV co-infection).
- **Severe Skin Reactions:** Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN), DRESS, and AGEP have been reported. Discontinue immediately if signs of severe rash develop.
- **Sulfonamide Allergy:** **Darunavir** contains a sulfonamide moiety; use with caution in patients with known sulfonamide allergy.
- **Metabolic Complications:** New onset or exacerbation of diabetes mellitus and hyperglycaemia have been reported. Monitor blood glucose and lipid profiles.
- **Immune Reconstitution Inflammatory Syndrome (IRIS):** May occur during the initial phase of treatment, leading to inflammatory responses to residual opportunistic infections.
- **Haemophilia:** Increased spontaneous bleeding has been reported in patients with haemophilia A and B treated with PIs.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse drug reactions (ADRs) are generally mild to moderate.
- **Very Common (>10%):** Diarrhoea, nausea [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Headache, abdominal pain, vomiting, rash, dyspepsia, flatulence, fatigue, and anorexia.
- **Serious ADRs:** 
    - Severe skin reactions (SJS <0.1%; TEN/DRESS <0.01%).
    - Acute pancreatitis (0.3–0.4%).
    - Hepatotoxicity and acute hepatitis (0.3–0.4%).
    - Osteonecrosis (0.4% in pooled trials).
- **Laboratory Abnormalities:** Elevations in ALT/AST, triglycerides, total cholesterol, and pancreatic amylase/lipase are common.

## 7. Drug Interactions
**Darunavir** is a substrate and a potent inhibitor of CYP3A. It also inhibits CYP2D6 and P-gp.
- **Pharmacokinetic Enhancers:** Ritonavir significantly increases **darunavir** exposure (approx. 14-fold).
- **Contraindicated Inducers:** Rifampicin, St. John’s Wort, and potent anticonvulsants (phenobarbital, phenytoin, carbamazepine with cobicistat) significantly reduce **darunavir** levels.
- **Management Notes:**
    - **Colchicine:** Contraindicated in patients with renal or hepatic impairment; dose reduction required in others.
    - **Statins:** Avoid lovastatin/simvastatin. Start atorvastatin/rosuvastatin at the lowest possible dose.
    - **PDE-5 Inhibitors:** Use with caution for erectile dysfunction; do not exceed 25 mg sildenafil in 48 hours.
    - **Anticoagulants:** Monitor INR with warfarin; monitor DOACs (dabigatran/edoxaban) for increased bleeding risk.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category B2. Use only if the potential benefit justifies the risk. 
- **Pharmacokinetic Changes:** Exposure to **darunavir** is lower during pregnancy. 
- **Cobicistat Note:** **Darunavir/cobicistat** should **not** be initiated during pregnancy due to substantially low exposure; switch to a ritonavir-boosted regimen if pregnancy occurs [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Mothers should be instructed **not** to breastfeed due to the risk of HIV transmission and potential serious adverse events in the infant.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for children below 3 years of age due to toxicity observed in juvenile animal studies (likely related to immature CYP enzymes and blood-brain barrier) [Australian].
- **Elderly (≥65 years):** Use with caution due to the higher frequency of decreased hepatic function and concomitant disease.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - Mild to Moderate (Child-Pugh A & B): No dose adjustment, but use with caution due to increased unbound levels.
    - Severe (Child-Pugh C): Use is not recommended/not studied.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required as renal clearance is minimal. **Darunavir** is not significantly removed by haemodialysis.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits HIV-1 protease by binding to the enzyme’s catalytic site, preventing the cleavage of Gag-Pol polyproteins and resulting in the formation of immature, non-infectious virions.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ is 2.5–4.0 hours. Bioavailability is 82% when boosted with ritonavir.
    - **Distribution:** ~95% plasma protein bound (primarily to alpha-1-acid glycoprotein).
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised by CYP3A4.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is ~15 hours (with ritonavir). Excretion is 79.5% faecal and 13.9% urinary.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ARTEMIS:** Demonstrated non-inferiority (and later superiority at 96 weeks) of **darunavir/rtv** 800/100 mg once daily compared to lopinavir/rtv in treatment-naive adults.
- **ODIN:** Confirmed that **darunavir/rtv** 800/100 mg once daily is non-inferior to 600/100 mg twice daily in treatment-experienced patients without DRV-RAMs.
- **TITAN:** Showed superiority of **darunavir/rtv** 600/100 mg twice daily over lopinavir/rtv in treatment-experienced, lopinavir-naive adults.
- **POWER 1, 2, & 3:** Established efficacy in highly treatment-experienced patients with significant PI resistance.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Packaging:** Provided in HDPE bottles with child-resistant closures.
