# Editorial Product Information: Tobramycin and Dexamethasone

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction:
    - **United States** (Food and Drug Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of tobramycin and dexamethasone ophthalmic suspension for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for regional legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Tobramycin (aminoglycoside antibiotic) and Dexamethasone (corticosteroid).
- **Brand Names:** Tobramycin and Dexamethasone Ophthalmic Suspension, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** S01CA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Ophthalmic suspension containing tobramycin 3 mg/mL (0.3%) and dexamethasone 1 mg/mL (0.1%).
- **Route of Administration:** Topical ophthalmic use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sportpharm LLC; Relabeled by Enovachem Pharmaceuticals [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Steroid-Responsive Ocular Inflammation:** Indicated for inflammatory conditions of the palpebral and bulbar conjunctiva, cornea, and anterior segment of the globe where the use of a corticosteroid is appropriate.
- **Infection Prophylaxis/Treatment:** Specifically indicated where a risk of superficial bacterial ocular infection exists or where an actual infection is present.
- **Specific Conditions:** 
    - Chronic anterior uveitis.
    - Corneal injury resulting from chemical, radiation, or thermal burns.
    - Penetration of foreign bodies.
    - Infective conjunctivitis where the risk of steroid use is acceptable to reduce edema and inflammation [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Standard Dosing (Adults and Paediatrics ≥ 2 years):** One or two drops instilled into the conjunctival sac(s) every four to six hours [US].
- **Initial Intensive Dosing:** During the first 24 to 48 hours, the frequency may be increased to one or two drops every two hours [US].
- **Titration:** Frequency should be gradually decreased as clinical signs improve. Therapy should not be discontinued prematurely.
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Shake well before use.
    - To prevent contamination, avoid touching the dropper tip to any surface.
    - Not for injection into the eye.
- **Prescription Limitations:** Not more than 20 mL should be prescribed initially [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Viral Diseases:** Epithelial herpes simplex keratitis (dendritic keratitis), vaccinia, varicella, and most other viral diseases of the cornea and conjunctiva.
- **Mycobacterial Infection:** Mycobacterial infection of the eye.
- **Fungal Disease:** Fungal infections of ocular structures.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to any component of the medication.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Topical Use Only:** Strictly for ophthalmic use; not for injection into the eye.
- **Aminoglycoside Sensitivity:** Sensitivity to topically applied aminoglycosides may occur. Reactions range from local itching to generalized anaphylaxis or bullous reactions. Cross-sensitivity to other aminoglycosides may occur [US].
- **Corticosteroid Risks:** 
    - **Glaucoma:** Prolonged use may result in ocular hypertension or glaucoma, potentially damaging the optic nerve and affecting visual fields.
    - **Cataracts:** Posterior subcapsular cataract formation may occur with long-term use.
    - **Secondary Infections:** Prolonged use may suppress host responses and increase the hazard of secondary fungal or bacterial infections.
    - **Delayed Healing:** Steroids are known to delay wound healing.
- **Tissue Thinning:** In diseases causing thinning of the cornea or sclera, topical steroids have been known to cause perforations.
- **Monitoring:** Intraocular pressure (IOP) should be routinely monitored during therapy, particularly in patients treated for longer than 10 days.
- **Contact Lenses:** Patients should be advised not to wear contact lenses during the use of this product [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions may be attributed to either the steroid component, the anti-infective component, or the combination.

### Common Adverse Effects (Frequency < 4%) [US]
- **Tobramycin-related:** Localized ocular toxicity, lid itching, lid swelling, and conjunctival erythema.

### Corticosteroid-related Reactions
- Elevation of intraocular pressure (IOP).
- Development of glaucoma and optic nerve damage (infrequent).
- Posterior subcapsular cataract formation.
- Delayed wound healing.

### Serious Adverse Effects/Others
- **Secondary Infection:** Fungal invasion of the cornea is a risk with long-term steroid application. Secondary bacterial infection may occur following host response suppression.
- **Postmarketing Experience:** Anaphylactic reactions and erythema multiforme have been reported [US].
- **Systemic-like Effects:** Cushing’s syndrome and adrenal suppression in predisposed patients (see Section 7).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Dexamethasone-related Cushing’s syndrome and/or adrenal suppression may occur after use in excess of dosing instructions, particularly in patients treated with CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., ritonavir, cobicistat) [US].
- **Systemic Aminoglycosides:** If topical tobramycin is administered concomitantly with systemic aminoglycoside antibiotics, total serum concentration should be monitored to avoid neurotoxicity, ototoxicity, or nephrotoxicity.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Risk Summary:** No adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Prolonged/repeated use during pregnancy is linked to increased risk of intra-uterine growth retardation [US].
    - **Animal Data:** Dexamethasone is teratogenic in rabbits and rats. Tobramycin reproduction studies showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm.
    - **Clinical Recommendation:** Use only if the potential benefit justifies the risk to the foetus. Infants born to mothers using substantial doses should be monitored for hypoadrenalism.
- **Breastfeeding:** 
    - Systemic corticosteroids appear in human milk. It is unknown if topical ophthalmic administration results in sufficient absorption to produce detectable quantities in milk.
    - **Clinical Recommendation:** Exercise caution when administering to nursing women [US].
- **Fertility:** Subcutaneous tobramycin studies in rats showed no impairment of fertility at doses of 50 and 100 mg/kg/day [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in paediatric patients below 2 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness have been observed between elderly and younger patients [US].
- **Neuromuscular Disorders:** Aminoglycosides may aggravate muscle weakness in patients with known or suspected disorders such as myasthenia gravis or Parkinson’s disease [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources; however, systemic absorption is generally low with topical application.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Dexamethasone:** A potent corticosteroid that suppresses the inflammatory response to various agents and potentially delays healing.
    - **Tobramycin:** An aminoglycoside antibiotic providing bactericidal action against susceptible organisms.
- **Antimicrobial Spectrum:** Active against common ocular pathogens including *Staphylococci* (including *S. aureus*, *S. epidermidis*, and penicillin-resistant strains), *Streptococci* (including *S. pneumoniae*), *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, *Escherichia coli*, *Klebsiella pneumoniae*, and *Haemophilus influenzae*.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Systemic absorption can occur following ocular application, though specific extent data for the combination suspension is not available in source documents [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Not specified in available sources.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store the suspension upright at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Instructions:** Shake well before using.
- **Integrity:** Do not use if the imprinted neckband is not intact.
