# Editorial Product Information: Diphenoxylate Hydrochloride and Atropine Sulfate

**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been compiled by integrating regulatory data from the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). In instances where clinical guidance or safety restrictions differ between these agencies, the most comprehensive or conservative recommendation has been adopted and credited to the respective agency.

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** Diphenoxylate hydrochloride and atropine sulfate (Generic; marketed in Australia as Lofenoxal).

**Active Ingredients:**
*   **Diphenoxylate hydrochloride:** 2.5 mg
*   **Atropine sulfate:** 0.025 mg (25 micrograms)

This combination consists of an antidiarrheal methylpiperidine derivative (diphenoxylate) and a subtherapeutic amount of an anticholinergic (atropine) included specifically to discourage deliberate overdose or misuse [FDA/TGA].

## 2. Approved Indications
This medication is indicated as adjunctive therapy for the management of acute and chronic diarrhea [TGA]. Its use is restricted to patients 13 years of age and older in the United States [FDA], while Australian guidelines emphasize its role as adjunctive therapy without specific age-related indication limits beyond the contraindication for young children [TGA].

## 3. Contraindications
Diphenoxylate and atropine are contraindicated in the following populations and conditions:
*   **Pediatric Patients:** Children under 6 years of age (due to the risk of severe respiratory and CNS depression) [FDA] and specifically not recommended for children under 12 years of age in Australian practice [TGA].
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to diphenoxylate hydrochloride or atropine.
*   **Jaundice:** Patients with obstructive jaundice [FDA] or general jaundice [TGA].
*   **Infectious Diarrhea:** Diarrhea caused by organisms that penetrate the intestinal mucosa (e.g., toxigenic *E. coli*, *Salmonella*, *Shigella*) or pseudomembranous enterocolitis (e.g., *Clostridium difficile*) associated with antibiotic use [FDA/TGA].
*   **Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD):** Patients with acute ulcerative colitis or Crohn’s disease, as the inhibition of motility may exacerbate the condition or induce toxic megacolon [TGA].

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Respiratory and CNS Depression:** Especially in children, accidental ingestion may lead to fatal respiratory depression or coma. Patients must store this medication securely out of reach of children [FDA/TGA].
*   **Fluid and Electrolyte Management:** Antidiarrheal treatment should not replace fluid and electrolyte therapy. In cases of severe dehydration, the drug should be withheld until corrective therapy is initiated to prevent variable drug response [TGA].
*   **Toxic Megacolon:** In patients with acute ulcerative colitis, the use of motility-inhibiting agents can induce toxic megacolon. Therapy should be ceased immediately if abdominal distension occurs [FDA/TGA].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with extreme caution in patients with advanced hepatorenal disease or abnormal liver function, as the drug may precipitate hepatic coma [TGA/FDA].
*   **Atropinism:** Even at subtherapeutic doses, signs of atropine toxicity (hyperthermia, tachycardia, urinary retention, flushing) may occur, particularly in children and patients with Down’s Syndrome [TGA].
*   **Dependency Potential:** While addiction is rare at therapeutic doses, diphenoxylate is chemically related to meperidine/pethidine. High doses may produce morphine-like effects, and physical dependence is theoretically possible [FDA/TGA].

## 5. Dosage and Administration
**Adult Initial Dosage:** The recommended starting dose is 5 mg (two tablets) taken three or four times daily [TGA/FDA]. The maximum daily dose should not exceed 20 mg of diphenoxylate [FDA].

**Maintenance Dosage:** After initial control is achieved, the dose should be reduced to meet the individual's needs. Maintenance can often be achieved with as little as 5 mg (two tablets) daily [TGA].

**Duration of Treatment:** If clinical improvement for acute diarrhea is not observed within 48 hours, or if chronic diarrhea does not improve after 10 days of maximum-dose treatment, the medication should be discontinued as further administration is unlikely to be effective [FDA].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Reported adverse effects are categorized by system:
*   **Nervous System:** Sedation, drowsiness, dizziness, confusion, malaise, lethargy, headache, euphoria, depression, restlessness, and numbness of extremities. Hallucinations have also been reported [FDA].
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, anorexia, abdominal discomfort, paralytic ileus, and toxic megacolon. Pancreatitis has also been observed [FDA].
*   **Allergic:** Anaphylaxis, angioneurotic edema (swelling of gums), urticaria, and pruritus.
*   **Atropine-specific effects:** Tachycardia, hyperthermia, urinary retention, flushing, and dryness of the skin and mucous membranes [TGA/FDA].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **CNS Depressants:** Diphenoxylate may potentiate the effects of alcohol, barbiturates, benzodiazepines, tranquilizers, and other opioids. Concurrent use requires close observation [FDA/TGA].
*   **MAO Inhibitors:** Use with Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs) may precipitate a hypertensive crisis due to the structural similarity between diphenoxylate and meperidine [FDA/TGA].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
**Pregnancy:** Classified as Category C in Australia. Diphenoxylate is related to pethidine and may cause respiratory depression in neonates if used near term [TGA]. Animal studies have shown reduced fertility and litter size at high doses [FDA]. It should only be used in pregnancy if the potential benefit outweighs the risk to the fetus.

**Breastfeeding:** Diphenoxylate's metabolite (diphenoxylic acid) and atropine are excreted in human milk. Nursing infants may exhibit pharmacological effects of the drug; therefore, caution is advised [FDA/TGA].

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Diphenoxylate is rapidly absorbed, with peak plasma levels occurring approximately 2 hours after oral administration [TGA/FDA].
*   **Metabolism:** The drug is extensively metabolized via ester hydrolysis into diphenoxylic acid (also known as difenoxine), which is biologically active and represents the primary metabolite in the blood [FDA].
*   **Half-life:** The parent drug has a relatively short plasma half-life of roughly 2.5 hours [TGA], whereas the elimination half-life of the active metabolite (diphenoxylic acid) is approximately 12 to 14 hours [FDA].
*   **Excretion:** Elimination occurs primarily through the feces (approximately 49%) and urine (14%) over a four-day period [FDA].

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Diphenoxylate is a synthetic opioid related to pethidine (meperidine) that acts directly on the circular smooth muscles of the intestinal wall. This action slows intestinal motility and prolongs transit time, facilitating increased contact between the intestinal mucosa and intraluminal contents to enhance fluid absorption [FDA/TGA].

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **United States:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F), with excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C [FDA].
*   **Australia:** Store below 30°C [TGA].
*   **Unified Recommendation:** For optimal stability, store in a cool, dry place below 25°C and protect from light [Conservative recommendation based on FDA data].
