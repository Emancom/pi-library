# Editorial Product Information: Dihydroergotamine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information provided by the following regulatory authority: **United States (Food and Drug Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data, regulatory requirements, and administration guidelines.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Dihydroergotamine mesylate (an ergotamine derivative).
- **Brand Name(s):** **ATZUMI** [United States].
- **ATC Code:** N02CA01 (Dihydroergotamine).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Nasal powder, 5.2 mg of dihydroergotamine (equivalent to 6.0 mg dihydroergotamine mesylate) provided in a single-dose nasal device [United States].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Nasal administration only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Satsuma Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Migraine Treatment:** Indicated for the acute treatment of migraine headaches with or without aura in adults [United States].
- **Limitations of Use:** 
  - Not indicated for the preventive treatment of migraine [United States].
  - Not indicated for the management of hemiplegic or brainstem aura migraines [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dose:** The recommended dose is 5.2 mg (the contents of one single-dose nasal device) administered into a single nostril [United States].
- **Repeat Dosing:** If required, the dose may be repeated after a minimum of 1 hour. 
- **Maximum Dosing:**
  - 24-hour period: 10.4 mg (two doses) [United States].
  - 7-day period: Safety has not been established for more than 4 doses [United States].
  - 30-day period: Safety has not been established for more than 12 doses [United States].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
  - Priming is not required. 
  - The device nozzle is inserted into one nostril.
  - The dose is delivered via three separate, fast, pulse-like squeezes of the white air pump while the patient inhales. 
  - The pump must be allowed to expand back to its original shape between squeezes [United States].
- **Pre-initiation Assessment:** A cardiovascular evaluation is recommended prior to the first dose [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
Dihydroergotamine is contraindicated in patients with:
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Concomitant use with protease inhibitors, macrolide antibiotics, or azole antifungals [United States].
- **Cardiovascular Disease:** Ischemic heart disease (e.g., angina, history of MI, silent ischemia) or coronary artery vasospasm (including Prinzmetal’s variant angina) [United States].
- **Vascular Conditions:** Peripheral arterial disease, uncontrolled hypertension, or following vascular surgery [United States].
- **Organ Impairment:** Severe hepatic or severe renal impairment [United States].
- **Other:** Sepsis, hypersensitivity to ergot alkaloids, or concomitant use of peripheral/central vasoconstrictors [United States].
- **Drug-Drug Timing:** Use within 24 hours of other 5-HT1 agonists (triptans) or other ergot-type medications [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [United States]:** **Peripheral Ischemia Following Coadministration with Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors.** Serious or life-threatening peripheral ischemia (cerebral or extremity) can occur because CYP3A4 inhibition elevates serum levels of dihydroergotamine, increasing the risk of vasospasm. Concomitant use is strictly contraindicated.
- **Myocardial Ischemia and Cardiac Events:** Serious cardiac events, including fatalities, have occurred. Patients with risk factors for coronary artery disease (CAD) should have their first dose administered in a medically supervised setting with ECG monitoring [United States].
- **Cerebrovascular Events:** Reports of cerebral hemorrhage, subarachnoid hemorrhage, and stroke exist. Discontinue if a cerebrovascular event is suspected [United States].
- **Other Vasospastic Reactions:** May cause non-cardiac ischemia (e.g., colonic or peripheral vascular ischemia). Discontinue if symptoms of vasoconstriction (e.g., muscle pain, coldness, cyanosis of digits) develop [United States].
- **Medication Overuse Headache (MOH):** Use for ≥10 days per month may exacerbate headaches. Detoxification may be necessary [United States].
- **Fibrotic Complications:** Pleural and retroperitoneal fibrosis have been reported with prolonged daily use. The drug is not for chronic daily administration [United States].
- **Local Irritation:** Nasal discomfort, congestion, and epistaxis are common. Suspend use if severe local irritation occurs [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are reported based on trials using both the nasal powder and nasal spray formulations.

| System Organ Class | Common (1–10%) | Very Common (>10%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Respiratory** | Nasopharyngitis (5%), Epistaxis (2%), Pharyngitis (3%) | Rhinitis (26%) |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Vomiting (4%), Diarrhea (2%) | Nausea (10%) |
| **Nervous System** | Dizziness (4%), Somnolence (3%), Altered Taste (8%) | - |
| **General/Site** | Application site reaction (6%), Nasal discomfort (11%) | - |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Myocardial infarction, stroke, life-threatening arrhythmias, and peripheral gangrene [United States].
- **Local Irritation:** Reported in approximately 29% of patients using the nasal powder [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** (e.g., ritonavir, erythromycin, itraconazole) Contraindicated due to risk of life-threatening ischemia [United States].
- **Moderate CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Administer with caution [United States].
- **Triptans (5-HT1 agonists):** Increased risk of coronary vasospasm. Do not use within 24 hours of each other [United States].
- **Beta-Blockers:** May potentiate vasoconstrictive action [United States].
- **Vasoconstrictors:** Synergistic elevation of blood pressure; contraindicated [United States].
- **Nicotine:** May provoke vasoconstriction, leading to increased ischemic response [United States].
- **SSRIs:** Rare reports of weakness, hyperreflexia, and incoordination [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **Preterm Labor:** Dihydroergotamine has oxytocic properties and may cause preterm labor. Avoid use during pregnancy [United States].
  - **Risk Summary:** While data show no increased risk of major birth defects, animal studies indicate developmental toxicity (decreased fetal weight and skeletal ossification) [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** 
  - Ergotamine (a related drug) is known to pass into human milk and may cause serious adverse effects in infants (vomiting, diarrhea, unstable BP).
  - Dihydroergotamine may reduce milk supply by decreasing prolactin levels.
  - **Recommendation:** Do not breastfeed during treatment and for 3 days after the last dose; milk should be pumped and discarded during this period [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include sufficient numbers of subjects ≥65 years. Dosing should be cautious, typically starting at the low end of the range [United States].
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe renal impairment [United States].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe hepatic impairment [United States].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** High affinity for 5-HT1Dα and 5-HT1Dβ receptors. The therapeutic effect in migraine is attributed to agonist activity at the 5-HT1D receptors [United States].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Possesses vasoconstrictor and oxytocic properties [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Mean Tmax is 0.5 hours; Cmax is 2.1 ng/mL [United States].
  - **Distribution:** 93% plasma protein bound; Vd is approximately 800 L [United States].
  - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic via CYP3A4. The major active metabolite is 8’-β-hydroxydihydroergotamine [United States].
  - **Elimination:** Mean half-life is 13 hours. Major excretory route is via bile in the feces; renal clearance is 0.1 L/min [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy Basis:** The efficacy of the nasal powder (ATZUMI) was established based on its relative bioavailability compared to dihydroergotamine nasal spray.
- **Pivotal Trial Data (Nasal Spray):** In randomized, placebo-controlled studies, the 2 mg nasal spray dose showed significantly higher headache response rates at 4 hours (range 47%–70%) compared to placebo (range 22%–35%) [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at controlled room temperature, 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Excursions are permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F and 86°F) [United States].
- **Special Handling:** Store in the original protective foil pouch until immediately before use [United States].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only (Rx Only) [United States].
- **Scheduled Status:** Not a controlled substance [United States].
