# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from the following official Product Information (PI) source: United States (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) when prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP), a naturally occurring carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Generic) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for both acute and chronic management of patients with metabolic defects that result in systemic carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Metabolic Disorders):**
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg [US].
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
    - **Acute Crisis:** Often initiated with a loading dose followed by equivalent doses every 3 or 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours).
    - **Maintenance:** Daily doses typically range from 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Administration:** Slow bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - **Titration:** Adjustments should be based on pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine levels (target range: 35 to 60 µmol/L). Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be performed as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].
- **Monitoring:** Periodic monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations is recommended [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known based on available clinical data [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, sometimes within minutes of administration. These are most frequently reported in ESRD patients on dialysis. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks/benefits before re-administration [US].
- **General Precautions:**
    - Safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been established in patients with renal insufficiency. 
    - Chronic high-dose oral administration in renal impairment may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, such as trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally renally excreted [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine may increase the frequency or severity of seizures in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a prior history [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects observed in clinical trials and post-marketing surveillance include:

| System Organ Class | Adverse Effect | Frequency/Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, diarrhea, gastritis | Transient; common in ESRD trials |
| **Neurological** | Seizures, dizziness, headache, paresthesia | Seizures reported with/without history |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, arrhythmia | Common in dialysis populations |
| **General** | Body odor, fever, pain, injection site reactions | Body odor may result from metabolites |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia | Observed in ESRD trials |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity reactions [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate that levocarnitine may increase the International Normalized Ratio (INR) in patients receiving warfarin. **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specifically letter-graded in current source, but noted as should be used "only if clearly needed" [US].
    - **Data:** Animal reproduction studies in rats and rabbits (up to 3.8 times the human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine levels. The risks of excess carnitine intake to the infant must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing guidelines for metabolic disorders are applicable to pediatric patients. Safety and efficacy in ESRD pediatric populations follow adult principles [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources, though general caution is advised due to potential co-morbidities.
- **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is specifically indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. However, the use of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severe renal impairment or ESRD is cautioned against due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing the substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also aids in the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean terminal half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, IV administration increases plasma carnitine concentrations and may normalize the ratio of acylcarnitine to free carnitine [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** Double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in chronic hemodialysis patients demonstrated that doses of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg significantly increased trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks. While carnitine levels increased, definitive clinical outcomes on signs/symptoms of carnitine deficiency in this population remain under investigation [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Special Precautions:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservative [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
