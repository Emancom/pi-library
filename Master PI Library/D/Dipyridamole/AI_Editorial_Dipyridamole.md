# Editorial Product Information: Dipyridamole (Injection)

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of dipyridamole injection for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial summary intended for clinical reference and is not a substitute for the official, legally approved PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Dipyridamole (a pyrimido-pyrimidine derivative).
- **Chemical Class:** Coronary vasodilator and antithrombotic agent.
- **Brand Names:** **Persantin** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** B01AC07.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 10 mg/2 mL solution for injection in glass ampoules [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Clinect Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Myocardial Imaging:** Indicated as a pharmacological alternative to exercise stress in myocardial perfusion imaging and stress echocardiography [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Myocardial Perfusion Imaging:** 0.14 mg/kg/min (total 0.56 mg/kg) administered via infusion over 4 minutes. A total dose exceeding 60 mg is generally not required [Australian].
- **Stress Echocardiography:** 0.56 mg/kg infused over 4 minutes. If no changes are observed during real-time echo monitoring for an additional 4 minutes, a further 0.28 mg/kg may be given over 2 minutes. The cumulative dose should not exceed 0.84 mg/kg [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Dilution:** Dipyridamole must be diluted in at least a 1:2 ratio using sodium chloride (0.45% or 0.9%) or glucose 5% to a total volume of 20–50 mL. Undiluted infusion may cause local tissue irritation [Australian].
- **Timing:** The imaging agent (e.g., radiopharmaceutical) should be injected within 5 minutes following the completion of the dipyridamole infusion [Australian].
- **Compatibility:** Do not mix with other medications in the same syringe or infusion bag [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to dipyridamole or any excipients.
- **Acute Cardiac Conditions:** Acute myocardial infarction, unstable angina, acute myocarditis, pericarditis, or active endocarditis [Australian].
- **Haemodynamic Instability:** Uncontrolled symptomatic heart failure, uncontrolled cardiac arrhythmias causing haemodynamic compromise, or acute aortic dissection [Australian].
- **Obstructive Conditions:** Symptomatic severe aortic stenosis [Australian].
- **Pulmonary Conditions:** Acute pulmonary embolus or pulmonary infarction [Australian].
- **Current Therapy:** Patients currently receiving regular oral dipyridamole therapy should not receive additional intravenous dipyridamole [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
- **Exclusion Criteria:** Intravenous dipyridamole should not be used in patients with asthma, baseline hypotension (systolic BP <90 mmHg), or a recent history of unexplained syncope or transient ischaemic attacks (TIAs) [Australian].
- **Cardiac Monitoring:** Continuous monitoring is essential. Patients with severe coronary artery disease (CAD) are at higher risk. Caution is required in left main coronary stenosis, valvular heart disease, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, or high-degree atrioventricular (AV) block [Australian].
- **Pre-test Protocol:** Xanthine derivatives (caffeine, theophylline) and oral dipyridamole must be discontinued for at least 24 hours prior to testing to avoid impaired sensitivity or unpredictable adenosine levels [Australian].
- **Reversal Agent:** Parenteral **aminophylline** (50–100 mg via slow IV injection) must be immediately available to reverse bronchospasm or chest pain. If variant angina is induced, nitrates are preferred over aminophylline [Australian].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Non-fatal myocardial infarction (1.8/10,000), death (1/9,000), asystole, pulmonary oedema, ventricular fibrillation, transient ischaemic attack, and grand mal seizures [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Xanthine Derivatives:** Caffeine and theophylline antagonise the vasodilating effects of dipyridamole [Australian].
- **Adenosine:** Dipyridamole significantly increases adenosine plasma levels and enhances its cardiovascular effects; adenosine dosage adjustment may be necessary [Australian].
- **Antihypertensives:** May potentiate the hypotensive effects of blood pressure-lowering agents [Australian].
- **Cholinesterase Inhibitors:** Dipyridamole may counteract the effect of these drugs, potentially aggravating myasthenia gravis [Australian].
- **Antithrombotics:** Addition of dipyridamole to aspirin or warfarin does not appear to increase bleeding frequency or severity beyond that of the single agent [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B1** [Australian]. Limited data in humans. Animal studies show no evidence of foetal damage. Use with caution, especially during the first trimester.
- **Breastfeeding:** Dipyridamole is excreted in breast milk. Caution is advised when administered to nursing mothers [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No human data available for the intravenous form [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available in provided sources [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dipyridamole has low renal excretion (~5%); specific dosing adjustments are not provided in source documents [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Dipyridamole undergoes predominantly hepatic elimination; however, specific dosing adjustments are not provided in source documents [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A coronary vasodilator that increases circulating adenosine by inhibiting its uptake and inhibits cGMP-phosphodiesterase. This leads to a redistribution of coronary blood flow, where normal vessels dilate while flow through stenotic vessels remains restricted ("steal phenomenon") [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Model:** 3-compartment model.
    - **Distribution:** Highly lipophilic; does not cross the blood-brain barrier. Protein binding is 97–99% [Australian].
    - **Half-life:** Alpha phase (distribution) ~3 mins; Beta phase (elimination) ~40 mins; Gamma phase (terminal) ~15 hours [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic via glucuronidation.
    - **Elimination:** Predominantly biliary/faecal; renal excretion is minimal (~5%) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Perfusion Imaging:** Literature review (n=2240) showed a pooled sensitivity of 86% and specificity of 74% compared to coronary arteriography [Australian].
- **Stress Echocardiography:** Literature review (n=2137) showed a sensitivity of 73% and specificity of 91% [Australian].
- **Prognostic Value:** Abnormal dipyridamole stress studies were predictive of future cardiac events, with a post-operative event rate of 20% in intermediate-risk patients compared to 2% for normal studies [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Handling:** Ensure solution is clear, yellow, and free from particles before use.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **S4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
