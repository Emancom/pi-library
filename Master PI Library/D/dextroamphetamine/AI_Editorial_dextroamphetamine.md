# Editorial Product Information: Dextroamphetamine and Amphetamine Mixed Salts (Extended-Release)

## Editorial Note
- **Jurisdictions Contributed:** United States (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of the product information for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is a clinical synthesis for informational purposes and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this editorial, only United States regulatory documentation was provided for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** A mixture of four salts in equal parts by weight: Dextroamphetamine Saccharate, Amphetamine Aspartate Monohydrate, Dextroamphetamine Sulfate, and Amphetamine Sulfate. 
- **Chemical Class:** Non-catecholamine sympathomimetic amines (CNS stimulants).
- **Brand Name(s):** Not specified in source (Generic manufacturer: Elite Laboratories, Inc. [US]).
- **ATC Code:** N06BA01
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Extended-release capsules: 5 mg, 10 mg, 15 mg, 20 mg, 25 mg, and 30 mg [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Elite Laboratories, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD):** Indicated for the treatment of ADHD in adults and pediatric patients aged 6 years and older [US].
- **Limitations of Use:** Use is not recommended in pediatric patients under 6 years of age due to higher plasma exposure and increased risk of adverse reactions, such as weight loss [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Pediatric Patients (Ages 6 to 17):** Initially 10 mg once daily in the morning. Dose may be adjusted in 5 mg or 10 mg increments at weekly intervals. A lower starting dose of 5 mg may be used based on clinical judgment [US].
- **Adults:** 20 mg once daily in the morning [US].

### Indication-Specific Maxima
- **Pediatric (Ages 6 to 12):** Maximum recommended dose is 30 mg daily [US].
- **Pediatric (Ages 13 to 17):** Doses up to 40 mg daily were studied, but 20 mg is the recommended titration target if 10 mg is insufficient [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Administer upon awakening. Avoid afternoon doses to prevent insomnia.
- Capsules may be swallowed whole or opened and the entire contents sprinkled on applesauce. The mixture must be consumed immediately without chewing the beads [US].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity or idiosyncrasy to amphetamine or any component of the formulation. Reactions such as angioedema and anaphylaxis have been reported [US].
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Concurrent use or use within 14 days of discontinuing an MAOI (including linezolid or intravenous methylene blue) due to the risk of hypertensive crisis [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- **Abuse, Misuse, and Addiction [US]:** CNS stimulants have a high potential for abuse and misuse, which can lead to substance use disorders and addiction. Before prescribing, assess the patient's risk. Monitor patients frequently for signs of abuse and misuse during treatment.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Serious Cardiac Disease:** Avoid use in patients with known structural cardiac abnormalities, cardiomyopathy, serious arrhythmias, or coronary artery disease. Sudden death has been reported in these populations at recommended dosages [US].
- **Blood Pressure and Heart Rate:** Can increase blood pressure (2–4 mmHg) and heart rate (3–6 bpm). Monitor pulse and BP at appropriate intervals [US].
- **Psychiatric Adverse Reactions:** May exacerbate pre-existing psychosis or induce manic/mixed episodes in patients with bipolar disorder. New psychotic or manic symptoms (e.g., hallucinations, delusional thinking) can occur in 0.1% of patients [US].
- **Growth Suppression:** Long-term use in children is associated with weight loss and slowing of growth rate. Monitor height and weight closely; treatment interruption may be required [US].
- **Seizures:** May lower the convulsive threshold. Discontinue if seizures occur [US].
- **Peripheral Vasculopathy:** Includes Raynaud’s phenomenon. Digital changes should be monitored [US].
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Risk increases when co-administered with serotonergic agents (SSRIs, SNRIs, triptans) or CYP2D6 inhibitors [US].
- **Motor and Verbal Tics:** May exacerbate tics or Tourette’s syndrome. Evaluate family history before initiation [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5% Incidence)
| Population | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Pediatric (6–12 yrs)** | Loss of appetite, insomnia, abdominal pain, emotional lability, vomiting, nervousness, nausea, fever. |
| **Adolescent (13–17 yrs)** | Loss of appetite, insomnia, abdominal pain, weight loss, nervousness. |
| **Adults** | Dry mouth, loss of appetite, insomnia, headache, weight loss, nausea, anxiety, agitation, dizziness, tachycardia, diarrhea, asthenia, urinary tract infection. |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Sudden death (cardiac)
- Hypersensitivity (angioedema, anaphylaxis)
- Serious skin rashes (Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis)
- Rhabdomyolysis
- Intestinal ischemia

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **MAOIs:** Contraindicated; risk of hypertensive crisis [US].
- **Alkalinizing Agents (e.g., GI antacids, sodium bicarbonate):** Increase blood levels and potentiation of amphetamine [US].
- **Acidifying Agents (e.g., ascorbic acid, fruit juices):** Decrease blood levels and efficacy of amphetamine [US].
- **Serotonergic Drugs (e.g., SSRIs, SNRIs, triptans, lithium):** Increase risk of serotonin syndrome [US].
- **CYP2D6 Inhibitors:** May increase exposure to amphetamine and the risk of serotonin syndrome [US].
- **Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs):** May decrease $T_{max}$ [US].
- **Tricyclic Antidepressants (TCAs):** May enhance activity and cardiovascular effects of amphetamines [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **US Category:** Not assigned under current labeling, but warns of potential fetal harm. 
  - **Clinical Considerations:** Use may cause vasoconstriction, decreasing placental perfusion. Infants born to mothers taking amphetamines have an increased risk of premature delivery and low birth weight. A pregnancy registry is available [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Amphetamine is excreted in human milk. Because of the potential for serious adverse reactions in the infant, breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment [US].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility or early embryonic development were observed in animal studies at clinically relevant doses [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for ADHD in children $\ge$ 6 years. Not recommended for children < 6 years due to risk of higher exposure and weight loss [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Not studied in the geriatric population [US].
- **Renal Impairment:**
  - **Severe (GFR 15 to < 30 mL/min/1.73m²):** Adult dose 15 mg/day; Pediatric (6–17) dose 5 mg/day (max 20 mg for 6–12 years) [US].
  - **ESRD (GFR < 15 mL/min/1.73m²):** Not recommended [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specifically studied; however, hepatic dysfunction has the potential to inhibit elimination and prolong exposure [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Sympathomimetic amines that block the reuptake of norepinephrine and dopamine into the presynaptic neuron and increase their release into the extraneuronal space [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** CNS stimulant; the specific mode of therapeutic action in ADHD is not fully elucidated [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ is approximately 7 hours for extended-release capsules (4 hours longer than immediate-release) [US].
  - **Metabolism:** Partially oxidized to active metabolites (4-hydroxyamphetamine and norephedrine) and deaminated. CYP2D6 is involved in metabolism [US].
  - **Elimination:** Urinary excretion is pH-dependent. $t_{1/2}$ for $d$-amphetamine: 9h (children), 11h (adolescents), 10h (adults). $t_{1/2}$ for $l$-amphetamine: 11h (children), 13–14h (adolescents), 13h (adults) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pediatric (6–12 years):** A 3-week double-blind, placebo-controlled study (N=584) showed significant improvements in teacher-rated attention and hyperactivity for all doses (10 mg, 20 mg, 30 mg) compared to placebo [US].
- **Adolescent (13–17 years):** A 4-week study (N=327) showed significant improvements in ADHD-RS-IV scores at doses up to 40 mg, though doses > 20 mg did not provide additional benefit [US].
- **Adult:** A 4-week study (N=255) demonstrated significant improvements in ADHD-RS scores for doses up to 60 mg, though doses > 20 mg did not provide additional benefit [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store at room temperature: $20^{\circ}$C to $25^{\circ}$C ($68^{\circ}$F to $77^{\circ}$F) [US].
- Dispense in tight, light-resistant containers with child-resistant closures [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Schedule II (CII) federally controlled substance due to high potential for abuse and dependence. Rx-only.
