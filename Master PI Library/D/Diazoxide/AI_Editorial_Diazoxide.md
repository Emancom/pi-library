# Editorial Product Information: Diazoxide

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **Australian (TGA)**.
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals based on available regulatory documentation.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference only and is not a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Diazoxide (a non-diuretic benzothiadiazine derivative).
- **Brand Names:** DBL Diazoxide Injection BP [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** C02DA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for intravenous injection; 300 mg diazoxide in 20 mL ampoules (15 mg/mL) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) injection only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Diazoxide is indicated for the following:
- Emergency reduction of blood pressure in malignant hypertension requiring a prompt decrease in diastolic pressure [Australian].
- Hypertensive crises associated with acute glomerular nephritis, acute hypertensive encephalopathy, cerebral haemorrhage, or congestive heart failure [Australian].
- Refractory hypertension not responsive to other agents [Australian].
- Control of elevated blood pressure prior to initiating oral therapy in chronic hypertension [Australian].
- Hypertensive patients undergoing diagnostic procedures, such as renal biopsy or arteriography, to reduce the risk of haemorrhage [Australian].

**Note:** In Australia, the injection is specifically not indicated for the treatment of hyperinsulinaemic hypoglycaemia [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Standard Bolus:** 300 mg administered undiluted by rapid IV injection (within 30 seconds or less) [Australian]. 
- **Minibolus (Alternative):** 1 to 3 mg/kg administered every 5 to 15 minutes. This method may be as effective as a single 300 mg dose and is associated with a lower incidence of adverse effects [Australian].
- **Small or Large Adults:** 5 mg/kg bodyweight [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing
- 5 mg/kg bodyweight [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Route:** Strict intravenous administration. Due to high alkalinity (pH ~11.5), it must not be given intramuscularly, subcutaneously, or into body cavities [Australian].
- **Technique:** Bolus doses must be given rapidly for maximum effect. Slower administration may reduce the antihypertensive response [Australian].
- **Post-Administration:** The patient must remain recumbent for at least 30 minutes following injection. Monitor blood pressure closely until stable, then hourly thereafter [Australian].
- **Duration:** Treatment for more than 5 days is seldom necessary; transition to oral antihypertensive agents as soon as control is achieved [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to diazoxide or other thiazide derivatives [Australian].
- Hypertension associated with mechanical abnormalities, such as aortic coarctation or arteriovenous shunt [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Extravasation Risk:** Avoid leakage into perivascular tissues due to the highly alkaline nature of the solution [Australian].
- **Hyperglycaemia:** Severe hyperglycaemia may occur, particularly in patients with renal insufficiency or impaired carbohydrate metabolism. Monitor blood glucose daily during IV therapy [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Stress:** Abrupt blood pressure reduction and reflex increases in heart rate/cardiac output may be hazardous in patients with impaired cardiac or cerebral circulation [Australian].
- **Fluid Retention:** Sodium and water retention may occur, potentially leading to oedema, weight gain, or congestive heart failure; this may require concurrent diuretic therapy [Australian].
- **Neonatal Safety:** 
    - **Pulmonary Hypertension:** Post-marketing reports have identified reversible pulmonary hypertension in neonates and infants (mostly during off-label use for hyperinsulinaemia). Monitor for respiratory distress [Australian].
    - **Necrotising Enterocolitis (NEC):** Reports of NEC, including fatal cases, have occurred in neonates and infants. Monitor for gastrointestinal symptoms, particularly in pre-term infants [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Periodic haematological assessment (for leucopenia and thrombocytopenia) is advisable during prolonged therapy. Serum uric acid should be monitored in patients with a history of gout [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are frequent with prolonged use but rare during short-term intravenous administration.

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Frequent** | Hyperglycaemia (may require insulin/oral hypoglycaemics), sodium and water retention (oedema, weight gain), nausea, vomiting, abdominal discomfort [Australian]. |
| **Common/Frequent** | Localised or generalised warmth, burning or itching along the vein, flushing, headache, sweating, lightheadedness [Australian]. |
| **Infrequent** | Severe hypotension (treatable with sympathomimetics), transient myocardial or cerebral ischaemia, rash, leucopenia, fever, thrombocytopenia [Australian]. |
| **Post-marketing/Rare** | Pulmonary hypertension (neonates/infants), necrotising enterocolitis (neonates/infants), pericardial effusion [Australian]. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Diuretics:** Potentiate the hyperglycaemic, hyperuricaemic, and hypotensive effects of diazoxide [Australian].
- **Antihypertensives:** Potentiate the hypotensive effect; requires careful dose adjustment [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Diazoxide is highly protein-bound (90%); displacement by other drugs may occur [Australian].
- **Physical Incompatibilities:** Do **not** dilute. Incompatible with hydralazine hydrochloride, lignocaine hydrochloride, and propranolol hydrochloride [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Diazoxide crosses the placenta. It is a powerful uterine relaxant that may arrest labour (contractions can be restarted with oxytocic agents). New-born hyperglycaemia has been reported. Safety in pregnancy is not established; use only when the potential benefits outweigh risks [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** No data available in the provided source.
- **Fertility:** No data available in the provided source.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Regularly assess bone and psychological maturation and growth in children receiving diazoxide [Australian].
- **Elderly:** No specific data provided in the source [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution. Uremic patients may experience a greater hypotensive effect due to reduced protein binding. The half-life may be prolonged [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources (drug is partly metabolised in the liver) [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Direct vasodilator that reduces peripheral vascular resistance by acting on smooth muscle in peripheral arterioles. It may act as a calcium antagonist. It causes reflex increases in heart rate and cardiac output [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Maximum hypotensive effect usually occurs within 5 minutes of rapid IV injection. Blood pressure typically rises over the following 10–20 minutes, with a total duration of action between 3 and 12 hours [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** 90% protein-bound; crosses blood-brain barrier and placenta [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Partially metabolised in the liver [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily excreted in urine (unchanged and as metabolites); small amounts in faeces. The mean half-life in adults is approximately 28 hours [Australian].

- No clinical trial data was detailed in the source document [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Conditions:** Protect from light; avoid excessive heat and freezing [Australian].
- **Physical State:** Clear colourless solution [Australian].
