# Editorial Product Information: Dimercaprol

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory data into a clinically relevant format.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory guidelines and the current manufacturer's PI before administration.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Dimercaprol (2,3-dimercapto-1-propanol)
- **Chemical Class:** Chelating agent
- **Brand Name:** BAL in Oil [US]
- **ATC Code:** V03AB09 (Not specified in available source; provided for clinical context)
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injection (USP); 100 mg/mL in 3 mL ampules [US]
- **Excipients:** Benzyl benzoate (200 mg/mL) and Peanut oil (700 mg/mL) [US]
- **Route of Administration:** Deep Intramuscular (IM) injection only [US]
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Akorn Operating Company LLC [US]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Arsenic, Gold, and Mercury Poisoning:** Indicated for the treatment of acute poisoning from these heavy metals [US].
- **Acute Lead Poisoning:** Indicated for use in combination with Edetate Calcium Disodium [US].
- **Clinical Limitations:**
    - **Mercury:** Effective for acute poisoning by mercury salts if initiated within one to two hours of ingestion; limited efficacy in chronic mercury poisoning [US].
    - **Other Metals:** Questionable value for antimony or bismuth poisoning [US].
    - **Contraindicated Metals:** Not recommended for iron, cadmium, or selenium poisoning; dimercaprol-metal complexes for these substances may increase toxicity, particularly to the kidneys [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Instructions
- Administration is strictly via **deep intramuscular injection** [US].
- Treatment should begin as early as possible for maximum efficacy [US].
- Visual inspection for particulate matter and discoloration is required before administration [US].

### Dosing Regimens [US]
| Indication | Dosage Schedule |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Mild Arsenic/Gold Poisoning** | 2.5 mg/kg four times daily for 2 days; twice daily on day 3; once daily thereafter for 10 days. |
| **Severe Arsenic/Gold Poisoning** | 3 mg/kg every 4 hours for 2 days; four times daily on day 3; twice daily thereafter for 10 days. |
| **Mercury Poisoning** | 5 mg/kg initially, followed by 2.5 mg/kg once or twice daily for 10 days. |
| **Acute Lead Encephalopathy** | 4 mg/kg (alone) for the first dose. Subsequent doses every 4 hours in combination with Edetate Calcium Disodium (administered at a separate site). |
| **Less Severe Lead Poisoning** | 3 mg/kg after the first dose. Treatment duration typically 2–7 days based on clinical response. |

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hepatic Insufficiency:** Contraindicated in most cases of liver failure, with the specific exception of postarsenical jaundice [US].
- **Renal Insufficiency:** Therapy should be discontinued or used with extreme caution if acute renal failure develops during treatment [US].
- **Specific Metal Poisoning:** Contraindicated in cases of iron, cadmium, or selenium poisoning due to the high toxicity of the resulting complexes [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Local Reaction:** Pain is common at the site of intramuscular injection [US].
- **Paediatric Fever:** A reaction specific to children is the development of fever, which may persist throughout therapy and occurs in approximately 30% of paediatric patients [US].
- **Haematologic Effects:** A transient reduction in polymorphonuclear leukocytes (neutrophils) may occur [US].
- **Renal Protection:** Dimercaprol-metal complexes dissociate more easily in an acidic environment. Maintenance of **alkaline urine** is recommended to protect the kidneys during excretion [US].
- **Peanut Allergy:** Formulated in peanut oil. Use with extreme caution in patients with known peanut allergies. Appropriate emergency equipment (e.g., epinephrine, airway management) must be available [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are often dose-dependent. A consistent response is a **rise in blood pressure accompanied by tachycardia**, roughly proportional to the dose administered [US].

Other effects reported (ordered by approximate frequency at higher doses) [US]:
- **Common (>10%):** Fever (paediatrics: ~30%), Nausea, Vomiting.
- **Systemic Reactions:**
    - Headache.
    - Burning sensations in the lips, mouth, throat, and penis.
    - Feelings of constriction or pain in the chest, throat, or hands.
    - Conjunctivitis, lacrimation, blepharospasm, rhinorrhea, and excessive salivation.
    - Tingling of the hands.
    - Diaphoresis (forehead and hands).
    - Abdominal pain.
    - Anxiety, weakness, and restlessness.
- **Local Reactions:** Painful sterile abscesses at the injection site.
- **Management:** Many systemic symptoms can be relieved by the administration of antihistamines [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Medicinal Iron:** Should not be administered concurrently. Dimercaprol forms toxic complexes with iron that increase renal risk [US].
- **Edetate Calcium Disodium:** Used synergistically for the treatment of lead poisoning but must be administered at a separate injection site [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category C]. Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. It is unknown if dimercaprol causes fetal harm or affects reproductive capacity. Administer to pregnant women only if clearly indicated [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if dimercaprol is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution when administering to nursing women [US].
- **Fertility:** Data on fertility impacts are not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Significant risk of fever (~30% of patients) [US]. Dosing is weight-based (see Section 3).
- **Geriatric Use:** Not specifically addressed in the available source.
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with extreme caution; discontinue if acute renal insufficiency develops [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Generally contraindicated except for postarsenical jaundice [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** The sulfhydryl groups of dimercaprol compete with the sulfhydryl groups of enzymes for binding with heavy metals. This forms a stable chelate complex that is subsequently excreted, preventing or reversing the binding of toxic metals to essential cellular enzymes [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Metabolism/Elimination:** The dimercaprol-metal complex is excreted. The drug promotes the continued excretion of arsenic, gold, and mercury [US].
    - **Absorption/Distribution:** Not detailed in the available source.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The rise in blood pressure and heart rate is dose-related [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Special Handling:** Inspect for discoloration or particles prior to use.
