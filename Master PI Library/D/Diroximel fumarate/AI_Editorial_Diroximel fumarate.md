# Editorial Product Information: Diroximel fumarate

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial product information is based on official documents from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **diroximel fumarate** for healthcare professionals, synthesising available regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved PI for definitive prescribing guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Diroximel fumarate. It is a member of the fumaric acid ester class.
- **Brand Name:** **VUMERITY** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N07XX18.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Enteric-coated capsules containing 231 mg of diroximel fumarate. The capsules contain enteric-coated minitablets [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Biogen Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Relapsing Multiple Sclerosis (RMS):** Indicated for use in patients with relapsing forms of multiple sclerosis to decrease the frequency of clinical relapses and to delay the accrual of physical disability [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):**
    - **Starting Dose:** 231 mg twice daily for the first 7 days.
    - **Maintenance Dose:** 462 mg (two 231 mg capsules) twice daily.
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Capsules must be swallowed whole and intact.
    - Do not crush, chew, or sprinkle the capsule contents on food.
    - May be taken with or without food.
    - To improve gastrointestinal (GI) tolerability, a gradual start is recommended.
    - **Prophylaxis for Flushing:** Administration of 325 mg non-enteric coated aspirin 30 minutes prior to dosing may reduce the incidence and severity of flushing [Australian].
- **Titration and Dose Reduction:** If the maintenance dose is not tolerated, a temporary reduction to 231 mg twice daily may be considered. The recommended dose of 462 mg twice daily should be resumed within 4 weeks. Discontinuation should be considered if the maintenance dose cannot be tolerated after titration [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, it can be taken provided there is at least a 4-hour gap before the next scheduled dose. Otherwise, skip the missed dose. Do not double the dose [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to diroximel fumarate, other fumaric acid derivatives (e.g., dimethyl fumarate), or any excipients in the formulation [Australian].
- Suspected or confirmed Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy (PML) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy (PML):** PML, an opportunistic infection caused by the John Cunningham virus (JCV), has been reported with fumaric acid esters. It can be fatal or result in severe disability. Risks increase with prolonged moderate-to-severe lymphopenia. Treatment must be permanently discontinued if PML is suspected or confirmed [Australian].
- **Lymphopenia:** **Vumerity** can significantly decrease lymphocyte counts. 
    - **Monitoring:** Obtain a Complete Blood Count (CBC) including lymphocytes within 6 months prior to initiation, after 6 months of treatment, and every 6–12 months thereafter. 
    - **Interruption:** Consider interrupting treatment if lymphocyte counts fall below 0.5 x 10⁹/L and persist for more than 6 months [Australian].
- **Infections:** Serious and sometimes fatal infections (including Herpes Zoster) have occurred. Monitor for signs of infection; withhold treatment in patients with serious infections until resolved [Australian].
- **Anaphylactic Reactions:** Reported with dimethyl fumarate; symptoms may occur after the first dose or at any time. Discontinue immediately if anaphylaxis is suspected [Australian].
- **Serious Gastrointestinal Reactions:** Reports of perforation, ulceration, haemorrhage, and obstruction, sometimes fatal, have occurred with fumaric acid esters, typically within the first 6 months of treatment [Australian].
- **Hepatic Function:** Drug-induced liver injury (DILI) has been observed. Monitor ALT, AST, and total bilirubin prior to initiation and during treatment as clinically indicated [Australian].
- **Renal Function:** Proteinuria has been reported. Urinalysis is recommended at baseline and annually [Australian].
- **Fanconi Syndrome:** Reported with products containing other fumarates; clinicians should be alert to symptoms (e.g., bone pain, proximal muscle weakness) even if creatinine is normal [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally similar to those seen with dimethyl fumarate, though **Vumerity** demonstrated significantly better GI tolerability in head-to-head trials (EVOLVE-MS-2) [Australian].

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Flushing (approx. 33%), Diarrhoea (14%), Nausea (13%), Abdominal pain (upper and lower) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Lymphopenia, Leucopenia, Burning sensation, Hot flush, Vomiting, Dyspepsia, Gastritis, Pruritus, Rash, Erythema, Proteinuria, Feeling hot, Increased AST/ALT |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** PML, serious infections (e.g., Herpes Zoster), anaphylaxis, and serious GI events (perforation/haemorrhage) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Other Fumarates:** Do not administer concurrently with dimethyl fumarate or other fumaric acid derivatives [Australian].
- **Nephrotoxic Agents:** Use caution when co-administering with medications associated with renal toxicity (e.g., aminoglycosides, NSAIDs, lithium, diuretics) [Australian].
- **Vaccinations:** Non-live vaccines are permitted. The safety of live attenuated vaccines has not been studied and is not recommended during treatment [Australian].
- **Alcohol:** Co-ingestion with 5% or 40% ethanol did not alter total exposure to the active metabolite (MMF), though peak concentrations (Cmax) were slightly reduced. No evidence of "dose dumping" [Australian].
- **Oral Contraceptives:** No significant interaction expected based on studies with dimethyl fumarate [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. There are no adequate/well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Animal studies showed embryofetal toxicity and skeletal variations at doses associated with maternal toxicity. Not recommended during pregnancy or in women of childbearing potential not using contraception [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if diroximel fumarate or its metabolites are excreted in human milk. A risk to newborns cannot be excluded; clinical decision should weigh the benefit of breastfeeding against the risk to the infant [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal data suggest no significant risk of reduced fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Limited data in patients ≥65 years. It is unknown if they respond differently than younger patients [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for mild, moderate, or severe renal impairment. However, long-term safety has not been studied in moderate/severe impairment [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required. Caution is advised in severe hepatic impairment as it has not been specifically studied [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Diroximel fumarate is a prodrug of monomethyl fumarate (MMF). MMF activates the **Nuclear factor (erythroid-derived 2)-like 2 (Nrf2)** pathway, which is involved in the cellular response to oxidative stress. It also exhibits anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory effects [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly hydrolysed by esterases to MMF. Median Tmax of MMF is 2.5–3 hours. A high-fat, high-calorie meal reduces Cmax by 44% but does not affect the overall AUC [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Apparent Vd for MMF is 72–83 L. Protein binding is <25% [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised by esterases in the GI tract, blood, and tissues. No CYP450 involvement. Major metabolites are MMF (active) and 2-hydroxyethyl succinimide (HES; inactive) [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** MMF is primarily eliminated as CO₂ in expired air (approx. 60%). Only trace amounts are found in urine. Terminal half-life of MMF is approx. 1 hour [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **EVOLVE-MS-1:** A 96-week, open-label safety study (N=1057). Adverse reactions were consistent with dimethyl fumarate experience; however, MMF exposure was comparable, supporting similar efficacy [Australian].
- **EVOLVE-MS-2:** A 5-week, randomised, double-blind trial comparing GI tolerability of **Vumerity** to dimethyl fumarate. **Vumerity** showed a 46% reduction in the number of days with GI symptoms (IGISIS score ≥2) and fewer discontinuations due to GI events (0.8% vs 4.8%) [Australian].
- **Pivotal Efficacy (TECFIDERA Trials):** Efficacy is supported by the **DEFINE** and **CONFIRM** trials of dimethyl fumarate, which demonstrated significant reductions in annualised relapse rates (ARR), disability progression, and MRI lesion activity compared to placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C.
- Keep in the original bottle to protect from moisture [Australian].
- Pack size: 120 capsules in an HDPE bottle with a silica gel desiccant [Australian].
