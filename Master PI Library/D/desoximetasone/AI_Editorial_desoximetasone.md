# Editorial Product Information: Desoximetasone

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of desoximetasone, synthesized from official product information sources to assist healthcare professionals in clinical decision-making.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) mandated for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labeling for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Desoximetasone (a synthetic corticosteroid).
- **Chemical Class:** Topical Corticosteroid (Medium-to-High Potency).
- **Brand Names:** Generic formulations (e.g., Saptalis Pharmaceuticals) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Emollient Cream: 0.05% (0.5 mg desoximetasone per gram) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Topical.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Saptalis Pharmaceuticals, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Corticosteroid-Responsive Dermatoses:** Indicated for the relief of inflammatory and pruritic manifestations associated with these conditions [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** Apply a thin film to the affected skin areas twice daily. The medication should be rubbed in gently [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - For external use only; not for oral, ophthalmic, or intravaginal use [US].
    - Treated areas should not be bandaged or covered with occlusive dressings unless specifically directed by a physician [US].
- **Titration/Discontinuation:** Therapy should be discontinued once clinical control is achieved. If no improvement is observed within 4 weeks of treatment, the diagnosis should be re-evaluated [US].
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to desoximetasone or any other component of the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
- **HPA Axis Suppression:** Systemic absorption of topical corticosteroids can produce reversible hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis suppression, potentially leading to glucocorticosteroid insufficiency. Risks are increased with:
    - Use of high-potency steroids.
    - Application over large surface areas.
    - Prolonged duration of use.
    - Use of occlusive dressings.
    - Application to altered skin barriers or in patients with liver failure [US].
- **Systemic Effects:** Systemic absorption may result in Cushing’s syndrome, hyperglycemia, and the unmasking of latent diabetes mellitus [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Periodic evaluation for HPA axis suppression may be required using the ACTH stimulation test or urinary free cortisol test [US].
- **Local Adverse Reactions:** Prolonged use or occlusion may lead to skin atrophy, striae, telangiectasias, and secondary infections [US].
- **Concomitant Infection:** Appropriate antimicrobial therapy should be initiated if a skin infection is present. If the infection does not resolve promptly, desoximetasone should be discontinued until the infection is controlled [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Frequency (Controlled Studies):** The incidence of adverse reactions was low (0.8%) in clinical trials for the 0.05% cream. Reported effects included pruritus, erythema, vesiculation, and burning sensation [US].
- **Local Reactions (Reported Infrequently):** 
    - Burning, itching, irritation, and dryness.
    - Folliculitis, hypertrichosis, and acneiform eruptions.
    - Hypopigmentation and perioral dermatitis.
    - Allergic contact dermatitis (diagnosed by failure to heal rather than clinical exacerbation).
    - Skin maceration, secondary infection, skin atrophy, striae, and miliaria [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Systemic toxicity (HPA suppression, Cushing's) and irreversible local skin atrophy or striae [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** 
    - **Concomitant Corticosteroids:** The concurrent use of multiple corticosteroid-containing products may increase total systemic exposure and the risk of HPA axis suppression [US].
- **Management Notes:** Patients should be advised not to use other corticosteroids without consulting their physician [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category C:** Animal studies have shown desoximetasone to be teratogenic and embryotoxic in mice, rats, and rabbits at doses 15 to 150 times the human topical dose. 
    - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the risk to the fetus. Avoid extensive use, large amounts, or prolonged periods in pregnant patients [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if topical administration results in sufficient systemic absorption to produce detectable quantities in breast milk. Caution is advised when desoximetasone is administered to a nursing woman [US].
- **Fertility:** Long-term animal studies to evaluate the effect on fertility have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - Pediatric patients are more susceptible to systemic toxicity, HPA axis suppression, Cushing’s syndrome, and intracranial hypertension (e.g., bulging fontanelles, headaches, bilateral papilledema) due to a higher skin surface area to body weight ratio [US].
    - Chronic therapy may interfere with growth and development.
    - **Recommendation:** Limit use to the least amount compatible with an effective regimen [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Patients with liver failure are at increased risk for HPA axis suppression following systemic absorption [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Like other topical corticosteroids, desoximetasone has anti-inflammatory, antipruritic, and vasoconstrictive properties. The exact mechanism of anti-inflammatory activity remains unclear [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Percutaneous absorption is determined by the vehicle, skin integrity, and use of occlusion.
    - **Distribution:** Once absorbed, corticosteroids are bound to plasma proteins in varying degrees.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized in the liver.
    - **Elimination:** Excreted by the kidneys and into the bile. In pharmacokinetic studies using the 0.25% cream with 24-hour occlusion, the half-life was approximately 15 ± 2 hours (urine) and 17 ± 2 hours (feces) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Clinical Efficacy:** While specific pivotal trial names were not provided in the source, clinical trials for desoximetasone 0.05% cream demonstrated a low adverse reaction incidence of 0.8% and established its use for corticosteroid-responsive dermatoses [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Shelf Life:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Handling:** Ensure the cap is replaced securely; follow instructions for puncturing the tube seal using the cap [US].
