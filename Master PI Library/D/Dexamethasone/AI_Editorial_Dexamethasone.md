# Editorial Product Information: Dexamethasone

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of dexamethasone sodium phosphate for healthcare professionals. 
*   **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial reference and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. The information provided is based solely on the source document from the Australian jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Dexamethasone sodium phosphate (synthetic glucocorticoid).
*   **Brand Name(s):** DBL Dexamethasone Sodium Phosphate Injection [Australian].
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    *   Solution for injection: 4 mg/1 mL ampoule [Australian].
    *   Solution for injection: 8 mg/2 mL vial (contains sodium sulfite) [Australian].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV), Intramuscular (IM), Intra-synovial, and soft tissue injection [Australian].
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Dexamethasone is indicated for its potent anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressant effects across various conditions [Australian]:
*   **Replacement Therapy:** For acute or relative adrenocortical insufficiency (e.g., Addison’s disease) when supplemented with mineralocorticoids.
*   **Collagent/Rheumatic Diseases:** Systemic lupus erythematosus, polyarteritis nodosa, dermatomyositis, rheumatoid arthritis, and osteoarthritis (short-term adjunctive therapy during acute episodes).
*   **Pulmonary Disorders:** Status asthmaticus, chronic asthma, and sarcoidosis.
*   **Oedema:** Cerebral oedema associated with brain tumours, neurosurgery, stroke, or acute non-infectious laryngospasm.
*   **Neoplastic States:** Management of hypercalcaemia associated with cancer, and palliative management of leukaemias and lymphomas.
*   **Ocular Disorders:** Allergic conjunctivitis, keratitis, optic neuritis, and anterior ischaemic optic neuropathy.
*   **Dermatological Diseases:** Severe erythema multiforme (Stevens-Johnson syndrome), pemphigus, and exfoliative dermatitis.
*   **Gastrointestinal Disorders:** Ulcerative colitis and regional enteritis (during critical periods).
*   **Shock:** Adjunctive treatment for shock of haemorrhagic, traumatic, or surgical origin.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
*   **Systemic Use:** Typically ranges from 0.5 mg to 24 mg daily, adjusted based on severity and patient response [Australian].
*   **Shock:** 2 to 6 mg/kg bodyweight as a single IV injection, repeatable every 2 to 6 hours if shock persists [Australian]. Alternatively, an initial 20 mg IV bolus followed by a continuous infusion of 3 mg/kg/24 hours may be used [Australian].
*   **Cerebral Oedema:** Initial 10 mg IV followed by 4 mg IM every 6 hours until symptoms subside. Dosage should be tapered over 5 to 7 days [Australian].

### Local Administration (Intra-synovial/Soft Tissue)
*   **Large Joints (e.g., Knee):** 2 mg to 4 mg.
*   **Small Joints:** 800 micrograms to 1 mg.
*   **Bursae:** 2 mg to 3 mg.
*   **Tendon Sheaths:** 400 micrograms to 1 mg.

### Administration Instructions
*   IV and IM routes should be reserved for acute or life-threatening situations; oral therapy should be substituted as soon as practicable [Australian].
*   Infusions must be completed within 24 hours of preparation. If stored, solutions should be kept at 2°C to 8°C [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
*   **Systemic Infections:** Systemic fungal infections or other systemic infections unless specific anti-infective therapy is co-administered [Australian].
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to dexamethasone, other corticosteroids, or any excipients (including sulfites in the 8 mg/2 mL vial) [Australian].
*   **Vaccination:** Administration of live virus vaccines [Australian].
*   **Specific Conditions:** Patients with myasthenia gravis, peptic ulcer, osteoporosis, or psychoses [Australian].
*   **Local Injection Specifics:** Should not be injected into infected sites, unstable joints, or in the presence of bacteraemia [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Abrupt Withdrawal:** Prolonged treatment must not be discontinued suddenly to avoid acute adrenal insufficiency or "withdrawal syndrome" (fever, myalgia, arthralgia). Tapering is essential [Australian].
*   **Tumour Lysis Syndrome (TLS):** Reported in patients with haematological malignancies; monitor high-risk patients closely [Australian].
*   **Infection Masking:** Corticosteroids may mask signs of infection and increase susceptibility. Exposure to chickenpox or measles is of particular concern in non-immune patients [Australian].
*   **Musculoskeletal Effects:** Acute myopathy has been reported, particularly with high doses and concurrent neuromuscular blockers. Prolonged use may lead to osteoporosis or growth retardation in children [Australian].
*   **Ocular Precautions:** Prolonged use may cause subcapsular cataracts, glaucoma, or central serous chorioretinopathy [Australian].
*   **Psychiatric Effects:** Potential for severe reactions (euphoria, depression, suicidal ideation), typically emerging within days or weeks of treatment initiation [Australian].
*   **Pheochromocytoma Crisis:** May be fatal; use only after risk/benefit evaluation in suspected cases [Australian].
*   **Intrathecal Administration:** Formulations containing sulfites are contraindicated for intrathecal use due to neurotoxicity risk [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Frequencies are not specifically quantified in the available source; however, the following system-based effects are associated with prolonged therapy or high doses [Australian]:
*   **Endocrine:** Adrenal suppression, Cushingoid features, decreased carbohydrate tolerance.
*   **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension, thromboembolism, congestive heart failure in susceptible patients, myocardial rupture following recent infarction.
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Peptic ulcer with perforation/haemorrhage, dyspepsia, acute pancreatitis.
*   **Musculoskeletal:** Muscle weakness, steroid myopathy, vertebral compression fractures, avascular necrosis.
*   **Dermatological:** Impaired wound healing, skin atrophy, petechiae, acne.
*   **Neurological:** Convulsions, increased intracranial pressure (papilloedema), dizziness, headache.
*   **Psychiatric:** Affective disorders, irritability, insomnia, cognitive dysfunction.
*   **Fluid/Electrolyte:** Sodium and water retention, potassium loss, hypokalaemic alkalosis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., Phenytoin, Rifampicin, Barbiturates):** May increase dexamethasone metabolism, reducing its efficacy [Australian].
*   **CYP3A4 Inhibitors (e.g., Ketoconazole, Ritonavir):** May decrease clearance, increasing the risk of systemic side effects [Australian].
*   **NSAIDs and Aspirin:** Increased risk of gastrointestinal ulceration and haemorrhage [Australian].
*   **Anticoagulants:** Response may be altered (usually decreased); frequent monitoring of prothrombin time/INR is required [Australian].
*   **Diuretics (Potassium-depleting):** Enhanced risk of severe hypokalaemia [Australian].
*   **Antidiabetic Agents:** Glucocorticoids may increase blood glucose, necessitating dose adjustments of insulin or oral hypoglycaemics [Australian].
*   **Neuromuscular Blockers (e.g., Pancuronium):** Antagonism of blocking effects may occur; increased risk of acute myopathy [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** Classified as **Category C** [Australian]. Corticosteroids cross the placenta. Short-term use for respiratory distress syndrome prevention appears low risk, but long-term use may lead to reduced birth weight or neonatal adrenal suppression.
*   **Breastfeeding:** Corticosteroids appear in breast milk and may suppress growth or interfere with endogenous production in the infant. Breastfeeding is not recommended for women taking dexamethasone [Australian].
*   **Fertility:** May increase or decrease the motility and number of spermatozoa in some patients [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Corticosteroids cause growth retardation; use the minimum dose for the shortest duration. Children are at higher risk of raised intracranial pressure [Australian]. Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy has been reported in prematurely born infants [Australian].
*   **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of hypertension, osteoporosis, diabetes, and infection. Close medical supervision is required [Australian].
*   **Renal Impairment:** Extreme caution is required; specific dosing adjustments by eGFR are not specified in the source [Australian].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Corticosteroids may have an enhanced effect in patients with cirrhosis [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Synthetic glucocorticoid with minimal mineralocorticoid activity. It is 25 to 30 times more potent than hydrocortisone. It inhibits the inflammatory response, capillary dilation, and phagocytosis [Australian].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed post-IM/IV. Peak plasma levels occur at 1 hour post-IM [Australian].
    *   **Distribution:** Small amounts are protein-bound; penetrates tissue and cerebrospinal fluids [Australian].
    *   **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic [Australian].
    *   **Elimination:** Biological half-life is approximately 190 minutes. Up to 65% is excreted in the urine within 24 hours [Australian].
