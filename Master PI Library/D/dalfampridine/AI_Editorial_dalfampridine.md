# Editorial Product Information: Dalfampridine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of dalfampridine based on official product information. It is intended for use by healthcare professionals as a clinically accurate reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial consolidation and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this editorial, only the United States (FDA) documentation was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Dalfampridine (also known as 4-aminopyridine or fampridine).
- **Chemical Class:** Potassium channel blocker.
- **Brand Name(s):** Dalfampridine Extended-Release [US].
- **ATC Code:** N07XX07.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Extended-release film-coated tablets, 10 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Ascend Laboratories, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Multiple Sclerosis (MS):** Indicated to improve walking in adult patients with MS. Efficacy was established based on demonstrated increases in walking speed [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Standard Adult Dose:** 10 mg taken twice daily, approximately 12 hours apart [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** 10 mg twice daily. Doses exceeding this limit provide no additional benefit and are associated with a significantly increased risk of adverse reactions, particularly seizures [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets must be swallowed whole. Do not crush, divide, chew, or dissolve, as this may lead to rapid release of the active ingredient and increase seizure risk. May be taken with or without food [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Patients should not double or take extra doses to make up for a missed dose [US].
- **Renal Monitoring:** Creatinine clearance (CrCl) must be estimated prior to initiation and monitored at least annually thereafter [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Seizure History:** Any prior history of seizures [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Moderate or severe renal impairment, defined as CrCl $\le$ 50 mL/min [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known history of hypersensitivity to dalfampridine or any form of 4-aminopyridine [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Seizure Risk:** Dalfampridine can cause seizures. The risk is dose-dependent and increases with higher plasma concentrations. Treatment must be permanently discontinued if a seizure occurs [US].
- **Renal Impairment Risks:** Because dalfampridine is primarily renally excreted, patients with mild impairment (CrCl 51–80 mL/min) may experience plasma levels similar to those seen with higher, toxic doses. Potential benefits must be carefully weighed against seizure risks in this population [US].
- **Concurrent 4-Aminopyridine Use:** Avoid use with other forms of 4-aminopyridine (fampridine), as the active ingredient is identical and may lead to toxicity [US].
- **Anaphylaxis:** Severe allergic reactions, including respiratory compromise and angioedema, have been reported. Discontinue permanently if anaphylaxis occurs [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are more frequent at doses higher than the recommended 10 mg BID.

| Adverse Reaction | Frequency (Dalfampridine 10 mg BID) | Placebo Rate |

| **Urinary Tract Infection** | 12% | 8% |
| **Insomnia** | 9% | 4% |
| **Dizziness** | 7% | 4% |
| **Headache** | 7% | 4% |
| **Nausea** | 7% | 3% |
| **Asthenia** | 7% | 4% |

- **Common (1–10%):** MS relapse, paresthesia, nasopharyngitis, constipation, dyspepsia, pharyngolaryngeal pain [US].
- **Postmarketing Experience:** Vomiting and vertigo have been reported [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Seizures and anaphylaxis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **OCT2 Inhibitors:** Concurrent use with Organic Cation Transporter 2 (OCT2) inhibitors, such as **cimetidine**, may increase dalfampridine exposure and the subsequent risk of seizures [US].
- **Baclofen:** No clinically significant pharmacokinetic interaction has been identified [US].
- **Interferon:** Pharmacokinetics are not affected by co-administration with interferon beta-1b [US].
- **CYP Enzymes:** Dalfampridine is not a direct or time-dependent inhibitor of major CYP450 enzymes (1A2, 2A6, 2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, or 3A4/5) at therapeutic concentrations [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned under PLLR, but animal data indicates potential fetal harm]. Administration in animals at clinically relevant doses resulted in decreased offspring viability and growth [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** No data exist regarding the presence of dalfampridine in human milk or its effects on the infant or milk production. The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the mother's clinical need [US].
- **Fertility:** In animal studies (rats), no adverse effects on fertility were observed at doses up to 9 mg/kg/day, though reduced offspring viability was noted at this dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clearance decreases modestly with age. Because renal impairment is more common in the elderly, CrCl must be known before treatment initiation [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Mild (CrCl 51–80 mL/min):** Use with caution; plasma levels may reach levels associated with seizure risk [US].
    - **Moderate/Severe (CrCl $\le$ 50 mL/min):** Contraindicated [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No studies have been performed; however, as the drug is primarily renally excreted, hepatic impairment is not expected to significantly impact pharmacokinetics [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Broad-spectrum potassium channel blocker. In demyelinated axons, it is thought to increase action potential conduction by inhibiting potassium channels [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Does not prolong the QTc interval or significantly affect QRS duration [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly and completely absorbed; relative bioavailability of the ER tablet is 96%. $T_{max}$ is 3–4 hours [US].
    - **Distribution:** Largely unbound to plasma proteins (97–99%); volume of distribution is 2.6 L/kg [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Minimal; CYP2E1 is the primary enzyme involved in the 3-hydroxylation pathway [US].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (95.9% recovered in urine, mostly as unchanged drug). Elimination half-life is 5.2 to 6.5 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** Two randomized, placebo-controlled trials (n=540) evaluated walking speed using the Timed 25-Foot Walk (T25FW) [US].
- **Outcomes:** A significantly higher proportion of dalfampridine-treated patients were "responders" (demonstrated consistent improvement in walking speed) compared to placebo (Trial 1: 34.8% vs 8.3%; Trial 2: 42.9% vs 9.3%) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F). Excursions are permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F–86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Keep out of reach of children. Safely discard expired medication [US].
