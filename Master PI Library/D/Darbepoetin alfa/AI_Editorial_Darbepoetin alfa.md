# Editorial Product Information: Darbepoetin alfa

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) documents from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing. Clinicians should consult the local PI for the most current legal and prescribing requirements in their specific region. Information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., US, European, Canadian, Japanese) was not provided in the source material and is therefore noted as "Not specified in available sources" where applicable.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Darbepoetin alfa (rch).
- **Chemical Class:** Erythropoiesis-stimulating agent (ESA); an erythropoietin-like glycoprotein produced by recombinant DNA technology in Chinese Hamster Ovary (CHO) cells. It contains five N-linked oligosaccharide chains (compared to three in native human erythropoietin).
- **Brand Name:** **Aranesp** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** B03XA02.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths [Australian]:** 
    - **Pre-filled Syringe:** 10, 15, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, 80, 100, 150, 200, 300, and 500 µg.
    - **SureClick Pre-filled Pen:** 20, 40, 60, 80, 100, and 150 µg.
    - **Pre-filled Syringe with Automatic Needle Guard:** 15, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, 80, 100, 150, 200, 300, and 500 µg.
- **Routes of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) or Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Amgen Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Renal Failure (CRF):** Treatment of anaemia associated with chronic renal failure in adult patients [Australian].
- **Non-Myeloid Malignancies:** Treatment of anaemia in patients with non-myeloid malignancies where anaemia is a result of concomitantly administered chemotherapy, and where blood transfusion is not considered appropriate [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing Principles
- The goal is to reach and maintain a haemoglobin (Hb) target **not exceeding 120 g/L**.
- Doses should be titrated to ensure the rate of Hb increase does not exceed 10 g/L in any 2-week period.
- Use the lowest effective dose to reduce cardiovascular risk.

### Chronic Renal Failure (Adults)
- **Correction Phase:**
    - **Dialysis patients:** 0.45 µg/kg SC or IV once weekly.
    - **Non-dialysis patients:** 0.45 µg/kg once weekly (SC), 0.75 µg/kg once every 2 weeks (SC), or 1.5 µg/kg once monthly (SC).
- **Maintenance Phase:** 
    - Once weekly or once every 2 weeks. Non-dialysis patients may be dosed once monthly.
- **Conversion from r-HuEPO:** Initial **Aranesp** dose is determined by dividing the total weekly r-HuEPO dose by 200 (for SC) or 240 (for IV).

### Cancer Patients Receiving Chemotherapy
- **Initial Dose:** 500 µg (approx. 6.75 µg/kg) once every 3 weeks (SC), or 2.25 µg/kg once weekly (SC).
- **Commencement:** Only start if Hb falls below 100–110 g/L.
- **Duration:** Continue for approximately 4 weeks after the end of chemotherapy or until Hb approaches 120 g/L.

### Administration Instructions
- **Do not shake;** vigorous shaking may denature the protein.
- Allow the product to reach room temperature before injection.
- Visual inspection for particulate matter or discolouration is required prior to use.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Uncontrolled hypertension [Australian].
- Known sensitivity to products derived from mammalian cells [Australian].
- Known hypersensitivity to darbepoetin alfa or any excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning/Prominent Warning [Australian]:** In cancer patients, ESAs have been associated with increased mortality and are only indicated for chemotherapy-induced anaemia when transfusion is inappropriate. Hb levels must not exceed 120 g/L.
- **Cardiovascular and Thrombotic Events:** Increased risk of death, MI, stroke, and congestive heart failure when ESAs are targeted to Hb > 120 g/L.
- **Hypertension:** Blood pressure must be controlled before and during therapy. Hypertensive encephalopathy and seizures have occurred.
- **Pure Red Cell Aplasia (PRCA):** Reported primarily in CRF patients. If PRCA is suspected (loss of response + reticulocytopenia), discontinue **Aranesp** and test for neutralising antibodies. Do not switch to other ESAs.
- **Severe Cutaneous Reactions:** Discontinue immediately if SJS or TEN is suspected.
- **Tumour Progression:** Potential to act as a growth factor for any tumour type, particularly myeloid malignancies.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### CRF Patients (Common > 5%)
| Body System | Adverse Event (Aranesp %) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Cardiovascular | Hypertension (23%), Hypotension (22%) |
| CNS | Headache (16%) |
| Musculoskeletal | Myalgia (20%), Arthralgia (11%) |
| Respiratory | Upper respiratory infection (14%), Dyspnoea (12%) |
| Gastrointestinal | Diarrhoea (15%), Vomiting (15%), Nausea (14%) |

### Cancer Patients (Common > 5%)
- **General:** Fatigue (32%), Oedema peripheral (15%), Fever (19%), Asthenia (16%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea (38%), Vomiting (27%), Diarrhoea (20%).
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Thromboembolic events (6%), including pulmonary embolism and deep vein thrombosis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Dynamic Interactions:** No clinical evidence of interactions was observed. The theoretical risk is low due to the drug’s clearance mechanism [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Iron status should be monitored; most patients require supplemental iron (target ferritin > 100 µg/L or transferrin saturation > 20% in CRF) [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3 [Australian]**. (Drugs taken by a limited number of pregnant women without evidence of malformation; animal studies show increased foetal damage, the significance of which is uncertain in humans). Use only if the benefit justifies the potential risk.
- **Breastfeeding:** Use with caution. It is unknown if darbepoetin alfa is excreted in human milk. Animal studies showed decreased pup viability and growth, likely due to maternal polycythemia [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility in rats at doses up to 10 µg/kg (4x human exposure) [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established [Australian]. PK data is available for ages 7–16 years.
- **Elderly Use:** No differences in safety, efficacy, or dose requirements observed between geriatric (≥ 65 years) and younger adult patients [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Indicated for use in CRF; dose adjustments are based on Hb response rather than eGFR stage [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients with hepatic dysfunction [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Stimulates erythropoiesis by interacting with the erythropoietin receptor on erythroid progenitor cells in the bone marrow, mirroring the action of endogenous erythropoietin.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Slow and rate-limiting after SC administration. Peak concentration at 34 hours (CRF) or 94.5 hours (Cancer). SC Bioavailability: ~37%.
    - **Distribution:** Predominantly confined to the vascular space (60 mL/kg).
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is ~21 hours (IV) and ~70 hours (SC). This is approximately 3-fold longer than recombinant human erythropoietin.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Stimulates RBC production, resulting in increased Hb and decreased transfusion requirements.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **CRF Studies:** Ten studies involving 1,578 patients confirmed efficacy in correction and maintenance of Hb. **Aranesp** was equivalent to r-HuEPO with less frequent dosing.
- **Cancer Studies:** A trial of 314 lung cancer patients receiving platinum-based chemotherapy showed 26% of **Aranesp** patients required transfusion vs 60% in the placebo group (p < 0.001).
- **TREAT Study:** A large study in CRF patients with Type 2 diabetes (n=4,038) found an increased risk of stroke (HR 1.92) when targeting Hb 130 g/L vs placebo.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze).
- **Handling:** Protect from light. Do not shake.
- **Excursions:** If kept in the pre-filled syringe, it may be stored at -20°C to 30°C for up to 2 days [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging.
