# Editorial Product Information: Doravirine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Doravirine (INN); a pyridinone non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NNRTI).
- **Brand Name(s):** **PIFELTRO** [US].
- **ATC Code:** J05AG05.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 100 mg film-coated tablets. The tablets are white, oval-shaped, and debossed with the corporate logo and "700" on one side [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Merck Sharp & Dohme LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **HIV-1 Infection:** Indicated in combination with other antiretroviral agents for the treatment of HIV-1 infection in adults and paediatric patients weighing at least 35 kg who are:
    - Treatment-naïve (no prior antiretroviral history); **OR**
    - Virologically suppressed (HIV-1 RNA <50 copies/mL) on a stable antiretroviral regimen, to replace the current regimen. Candidates for switching must have no history of treatment failure and no known resistance-associated substitutions to doravirine [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Paediatrics ≥35 kg):** One 100 mg tablet taken orally once daily, with or without food [US].
- **Dosage Adjustment with Rifabutin:** If co-administered with rifabutin, the doravirine dose must be increased to 100 mg twice daily (approximately 12 hours apart) for the duration of co-administration [US].
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** Patients should take a missed dose as soon as remembered. If it is almost time for the next dose, the missed dose should be skipped and the regular schedule resumed. Two doses should not be taken at the same time [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Strong CYP3A Inducers:** Co-administration is contraindicated with drugs that significantly decrease doravirine plasma concentrations, potentially leading to loss of virologic response and resistance. These include, but are not limited to [US]:
    - **Anticonvulsants:** Carbamazepine, oxcarbazepine, phenobarbital, phenytoin.
    - **Androgen receptor inhibitors:** Enzalutamide.
    - **Antimycobacterials:** Rifampin, rifapentine.
    - **Cytotoxic agents:** Mitotane.
    - **Herbal products:** St. John’s wort (*Hypericum perforatum*).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Severe Skin Reactions:** Postmarketing reports include Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), and Drug Rash with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS). Therapy should be discontinued immediately if signs of severe skin reactions or hypersensitivity develop (e.g., painful rash, mucosal involvement, fever, or organ dysfunction) [US].
- **Immune Reconstitution Syndrome:** Reported in patients treated with combination antiretroviral therapy. This may involve inflammatory responses to indolent opportunistic infections (e.g., *Mycobacterium avium*, CMV, PCP, or TB) or the development of autoimmune disorders (e.g., Graves’ disease, Guillain-Barré syndrome) [US].
- **Risk of Resistance due to Drug Interactions:** Subtherapeutic levels of doravirine resulting from drug interactions may lead to loss of efficacy and development of resistance [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The majority of adverse reactions reported in clinical trials were Grade 1 (mild) in severity [US].

### Common Adverse Reactions (≥5%)
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea (7%), diarrhoea (6%), abdominal pain (5%).
- **Neurological/Psychiatric:** Dizziness (7%), headache (6%), abnormal dreams (5%).
- **General:** Fatigue (6%).

### Serious and Other Adverse Effects
- **Neuropsychiatric:** While doravirine showed lower rates of neuropsychiatric events compared to efavirenz-based regimens, events such as insomnia, somnolence, and depression/suicide/self-injury (4%) were reported [US].
- **Hepatobiliary:** Hepatitis and increased hepatic enzymes (postmarketing) [US].
- **Laboratory Abnormalities:** Elevations in ALT/AST (>5x ULN in 1% of switch patients), fasting LDL-cholesterol, and triglycerides [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Profile:** Doravirine is primarily a substrate of CYP3A.
- **Effect of Other Drugs on Doravirine:** 
    - **CYP3A Inducers:** Decrease doravirine concentration (see Contraindications and Dosage Adjustment for rifabutin).
    - **CYP3A Inhibitors:** May increase doravirine concentrations. Co-administration with ketoconazole increased doravirine AUC by 3.06-fold [US].
- **Effect of Doravirine on Other Drugs:** Doravirine is not considered a clinically significant inhibitor or inducer of major CYP enzymes (1A2, 2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 3A4) or transporters (P-gp, OATP1B1/3, BSEP, OAT1/3, OCT2, MATE1/2K) [US].
- **No Clinically Significant Interactions:** No dosage adjustments are required when used with dolutegravir, TDF, lamivudine, elbasvir/grazoprevir, ledipasvir/sofosbuvir, ritonavir, antacids, or oral contraceptives [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified under the old letter system; however, a pregnancy registry is available (1-800-258-4263).
    - **Clinical Data:** No adequate human data are available. Animal studies in rats and rabbits at exposures ≥8 times the human dose showed no adverse developmental outcomes [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Because of the potential for HIV-1 transmission (in HIV-negative infants) and the risk of developing viral resistance (in HIV-positive infants), mothers are instructed not to breastfeed if they are receiving doravirine [US].
- **Fertility:** No effects on fertility or mating performance were observed in rat studies at 7 times the recommended human exposure [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established in patients weighing ≥35 kg. Not recommended for children weighing <35 kg as appropriate dosing has not been determined [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Clinical trials did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65+ to determine different responses. Caution is advised due to higher frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment required for mild, moderate, or severe renal impairment. Not studied in patients with end-stage renal disease (ESRD) or those on dialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dosage adjustment required for mild (Child-Pugh A) or moderate (Child-Pugh B) impairment. Not studied in patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C) [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Doravirine is a non-competitive inhibitor of HIV-1 reverse transcriptase. It does not inhibit human cellular DNA polymerases α, β, or mitochondrial DNA polymerase γ [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is 64%. Peak plasma levels occur at 2 hours (Tmax). 
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is approximately 76% [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily oxidative metabolism via CYP3A4/5.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 15 hours. Primary route of elimination is metabolism; only 6% is excreted unchanged in urine [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **DRIVE-FORWARD:** A Phase 3 trial in treatment-naïve adults. Doravirine (once daily) demonstrated non-inferiority to ritonavir-boosted darunavir at Week 96 (72% vs 65% virologic success with RNA <50 copies/mL) [US].
- **DRIVE-AHEAD:** A Phase 3 trial in treatment-naïve adults. Doravirine/3TC/TDF demonstrated non-inferiority to efavirenz/FTC/TDF at Week 96 (77% vs 74% virologic success) with significantly fewer neuropsychiatric and lipid-related adverse events [US].
- **DRIVE-SHIFT:** A Phase 3 switch study. Virologically suppressed adults switching to doravirine-based therapy maintained viral suppression (91% success at Week 48) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Store in the original bottle. Keep tightly closed to protect from moisture. Do not remove the desiccant [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
