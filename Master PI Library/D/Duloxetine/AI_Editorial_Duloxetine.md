# Editorial Product Information: Duloxetine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of duloxetine for healthcare professionals. 
- **Important Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to local regulatory guidelines for specific legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Duloxetine hydrochloride (a Selective Serotonin and Noradrenaline Reuptake Inhibitor; SNRI).
- **Brand Name(s):** APO-Duloxetine [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Enteric-coated pellets in hard gelatin capsules: 30 mg (white/blue) and 60 mg (green/blue) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Major Depressive Disorder (MDD):** Treatment of MDD [Australian].
- **Generalised Anxiety Disorder (GAD):** Treatment of GAD [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Major Depressive Disorder:** 60 mg once daily. Evidence does not suggest additional benefit from doses exceeding 60 mg/day [Australian].
- **Generalised Anxiety Disorder:** Recommended starting dose is 30 mg once daily for one week, then increased to 60 mg once daily. If required, dose may be increased in 30 mg increments up to a maximum of 120 mg once daily [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- May be taken with or without food.
- Taking the medication with food may improve initial tolerability [Australian].
- Capsules contain enteric-coated pellets and should be swallowed whole; they must not be chewed or crushed [Australian].

### Titration and Discontinuation
- **Initial Tolerability:** For treatment-naïve patients or those with history of sensitivity, a 30 mg starting dose for one week may be used [Australian].
- **Discontinuation:** Therapy should not be stopped abruptly. The dose should be tapered over a minimum of two weeks (e.g., reducing dose by half or alternate-day dosing) to minimise withdrawal symptoms [Australian].

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to duloxetine or any excipients [Australian].
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Use in combination with MAOIs (including reversible MAOIs like moclobemide). At least 14 days must elapse after stopping an MAOI before starting duloxetine; at least 5 days must elapse after stopping duloxetine before starting an MAOI [Australian].
- **Liver Disease:** Contraindicated in patients with liver disease resulting in hepatic impairment [Australian].
- **CYP1A2 Inhibitors:** Use in combination with potent CYP1A2 inhibitors (e.g., fluvoxamine) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- None listed in available Australian source; however, significant warnings regarding suicidality and hepatotoxicity are present.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Suicidality:** Increased risk of suicidal ideation and behaviour in children, adolescents, and young adults (<25 years) with MDD and other psychiatric disorders. Patients should be closely monitored for clinical worsening, especially during the first few months of therapy or at dose changes [Australian].
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Duloxetine increases the risk of elevated serum transaminases. Severe liver injury (including hepatitis, jaundice, and rare fatal liver failure) has been reported. Avoid use in patients with substantial alcohol consumption or pre-existing liver disease [Australian].
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Potentially life-threatening; risk increases with concomitant use of other serotonergic agents (SSRIs, SNRIs, triptans, tramadol, St John’s Wort) [Australian].
- **Blood Pressure:** Associated with increased blood pressure in some patients; monitoring is recommended in patients with known hypertension or cardiovascular disease [Australian].
- **Orthostatic Hypotension/Syncope:** Reported primarily during the first week of therapy or following dose increases [Australian].
- **Abnormal Bleeding:** May increase risk of bleeding (e.g., GI bleeding, bruising). Caution with anticoagulants or NSAIDs [Australian].
- **Angle-Closure Glaucoma:** Mydriasis has been reported; caution in patients with raised intraocular pressure [Australian].
- **Hyponatremia:** Reported rarely, primarily in elderly patients; may be due to SIADH [Australian].

### Monitoring Requirements
- Regular monitoring for suicidal ideation/behaviour.
- Blood pressure monitoring, particularly in hypertensive patients.
- Liver function tests if symptoms of hepatic dysfunction occur.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Categorisation
- **Very Common (>10%):** Nausea, dry mouth, headache (though headache was also frequent in placebo), fatigue, dizziness, somnolence, insomnia [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Decreased appetite, constipation, diarrhoea, vomiting, abdominal pain, dyspepsia, hyperhidrosis, palpitations, tremor, anxiety, agitation, erectile dysfunction, ejaculation delayed/disorder, yawning, pruritus, musculoskeletal pain [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Tachycardia, vertigo, ear pain, tinnitus, mydriasis, visual impairment, gastritis, gastrointestinal haemorrhage, dysphagia, malaise, thirst, falls (especially in elderly), laryngitis, dehydration, muscle twitching, dysgeusia, nocturia, urinary hesitation/retention [Australian].
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Hypothyroidism, gait disturbance, myoclonus, hallucinations, rash, menstrual disorder [Australian].
- **Very Rare (<0.01%):** SIADH, glaucoma, microscopic colitis, anaphylactic reaction, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome, trismus, hypertensive crisis [Australian].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Postpartum Haemorrhage:** Increased risk when exposed close to delivery [Australian].
- **Liver Failure:** Isolated fatal cases reported [Australian].
- **Serotonin Syndrome and Seizures** [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP1A2 Inhibitors (e.g., fluvoxamine):** Markedly increase duloxetine levels; combination is contraindicated [Australian].
- **CYP2D6 Substrates:** Duloxetine is a moderate inhibitor of CYP2D6. It can increase levels of drugs like desipramine, nortriptyline, flecainide, and propafenone. Co-administration with thioridazine is contraindicated [Australian].
- **CYP2D6 Inhibitors (e.g., paroxetine):** May increase duloxetine concentrations by approximately 37% [Australian].
- **Protein Binding:** Duloxetine is >90% protein-bound; caution when used with other highly bound drugs [Australian].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **MAOIs:** Risk of Serotonin Syndrome; use is contraindicated [Australian].
- **Serotonergic Drugs:** (e.g., triptans, tramadol, SSRIs, SNRIs, pethidine, St John’s Wort) increase risk of Serotonin Syndrome [Australian].
- **Anticoagulants/Antiplatelets:** Increased risk of bleeding [Australian].
- **Alcohol:** Potentiates risk of liver injury [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy Category:** **B3** [Australian].
- **Clinical Considerations:** Use only if potential benefit justifies the risk. Exposure late in the third trimester may lead to neonatal complications (respiratory distress, seizures, tremors). Observational data suggests an increased risk of preterm birth and postpartum haemorrhage when used close to delivery [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Duloxetine is excreted into breast milk (approx. 0.1% to 0.3% of maternal dose). Use in nursing mothers is not recommended [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed oestrus cycle disruption at maternally toxic doses, but no effect on male fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not indicated or recommended for patients <18 years of age [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific dose adjustment based on age, but caution is advised due to increased risk of hyponatremia and falls [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - Mild/Moderate: No dose adjustment required.
    - End-Stage Renal Disease (CrCl <30 mL/min): Start at 30 mg once daily [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Potentiates serotonergic and noradrenergic activity in the central nervous system via inhibition of reuptake. It is a weak inhibitor of dopamine reuptake [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Well absorbed; peak plasma concentration (Cmax) at 6 hours. Food delays Cmax to 6–10 hours [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein bound (>90%). Volume of distribution ~1640 L [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Extensive hepatic metabolism via CYP2D6 and CYP1A2 to inactive metabolites [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Elimination half-life is ~12.1 hours. 70% excreted in urine (as metabolites) and 20% in faeces [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **MDD:** Efficacy established in six Phase 3 studies (8–9 weeks). Duloxetine (40–120 mg/day) was superior to placebo in Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HAMD17) scores. Relapse prevention was also demonstrated over 6 months [Australian].
- **GAD:** Efficacy established in five Phase 3 trials. Significant improvement noted on the Hamilton Anxiety Scale (HAM-A). Superiority to placebo was maintained in a 6-month relapse prevention study [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25ºC [Australian].
- Shelf life is specified on the individual product packaging.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **S4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
