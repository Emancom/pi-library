# Editorial Product Information: dextromethorphan + quinidine

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **dextromethorphan + quinidine**
- Chemical class:
  - Dextromethorphan: uncompetitive NMDA receptor antagonist; sigma-1 receptor agonist.
  - Quinidine: class IA antiarrhythmic; potent inhibitor of cytochrome P450 2D6 (CYP2D6); P‑glycoprotein inhibitor.
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration: Oral.

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 2010. [US]
- Most recent full prescribing information revision date shown in available source: December 2022. [US]
- Withdrawals, suspensions, or restrictions: Not specified in available sources.
- Registration/approval dates and last-revision dates for jurisdictions other than the United States: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of pseudobulbar affect (PBA), a disorder of emotional expression characterized by involuntary, sudden, and frequent episodes of laughing and/or crying that are disproportionate or incongruent to underlying emotional state. [US]
- Indications by other jurisdictions: Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General adult dosing (as described in available sources)
- Active dose strength described in sources: dextromethorphan 20 mg plus quinidine 10 mg per oral unit.
- Recommended initiation and maintenance:
  - Start: one oral dose once daily for the first 7 days.
  - Maintenance (from day 8 onward): one oral dose every 12 hours (two doses per day).
- Reassessment: Periodically reassess the need for continued therapy because spontaneous improvement of PBA can occur.

### Administration instructions
- Take by mouth as prescribed.
- When initiating therapy in patients at increased risk of QT prolongation or when co-administering QT-prolonging drugs or moderate/strong CYP3A4 inhibitors, obtain baseline ECG and repeat ECG approximately 3 to 4 hours after the first dose to evaluate QT interval. Re-evaluate ECG if new arrhythmia risk factors emerge during treatment.
- Correct hypokalemia and hypomagnesemia prior to initiation and monitor electrolytes during therapy as clinically indicated.
- If symptoms suggestive of cardiac arrhythmia (e.g., syncope or palpitations) occur, discontinue immediately and evaluate.
- If signs of thrombocytopenia, hepatitis, or other hypersensitivity occur, discontinue immediately unless an alternative cause is clearly identified.

### Titration schedules
- Not applicable beyond the initial start/maintenance schedule specified above.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
List includes all contraindications stated in the available source:
- Concomitant use with other agents containing quinidine, quinine, or mefloquine.
- History of quinidine-, quinine-, or mefloquine-induced thrombocytopenia, hepatitis, bone marrow depression, or other hypersensitivity reactions (including lupus‑like syndrome).
- Known hypersensitivity to dextromethorphan (e.g., rash, urticaria).
- Concomitant use with monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs), or use within 14 days after stopping an MAOI; similarly, allow at least 14 days after stopping dextromethorphan + quinidine before starting an MAOI. [US]
- Patients with prolonged QT interval, congenital long QT syndrome, or history suggestive of torsades de pointes. [US]
- Patients with heart failure. [US]
- Concomitant use with drugs that both prolong the QT interval and are metabolized by CYP2D6 (examples provided: thioridazine, pimozide). [US]
- Complete atrioventricular (AV) block without an implanted pacemaker, or patients at high risk of complete AV block. [US]

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed/Highlighted warnings
- Not separated as a boxed warning in the available source text, but the following serious risks are emphasized and require attention: immune‑mediated thrombocytopenia (which can be severe or fatal), hepatitis, QT prolongation with potential torsades de pointes, and serotonin syndrome when combined with serotonergic agents. [US]

### General warnings and precautions
- Thrombocytopenia and other immune-mediated hypersensitivity reactions:
  - Quinidine may cause immune-mediated thrombocytopenia that can be severe or fatal. Non‑specific prodromal symptoms (lightheadedness, chills, fever, nausea, vomiting) may precede thrombocytopenia. Discontinue immediately if thrombocytopenia is suspected unless clearly unrelated. Do not re‑challenge sensitized patients. Cross-sensitivity between quinidine, quinine, and mefloquine can occur. [US]
- Hepatotoxicity:
  - Hepatitis, including granulomatous hepatitis, has been reported, generally during the first few weeks of therapy. Fever and other hypersensitivity signs may accompany hepatic injury. Discontinue if hepatitis occurs. [US]
- Cardiac effects:
  - Dose-dependent QTc prolongation has been observed. QT prolongation can lead to torsades de pointes‑type ventricular tachycardia. Baseline and post‑dose ECG (3–4 hours after first dose) are recommended when initiating in patients at risk (see Administration). Reassess ECG if risk factors change. Correct electrolyte disturbances (hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia) before starting. Monitor patients with left ventricular hypertrophy or dysfunction. [US]
- CYP2D6 inhibition:
  - Quinidine component inhibits CYP2D6 and can substantially increase exposure to drugs metabolized by CYP2D6, or prevent formation of active metabolites from prodrugs (e.g., codeine, hydrocodone), potentially reducing efficacy of such prodrugs. Dose adjustments or alternative therapies should be considered. [US]
- Dizziness:
  - Dizziness is reported; precautions to reduce fall risk are advised, particularly in patients with impaired gait or prior falls. [US]
- Serotonin syndrome:
  - Concomitant use with SSRIs or tricyclic antidepressants may precipitate serotonin syndrome; monitor for symptoms (altered mental status, autonomic instability, neuromuscular abnormalities). Discontinue if serotonin syndrome is suspected. [US]
- Anticholinergic effects of quinidine:
  - Monitor for worsening of conditions sensitive to anticholinergic effects, including myasthenia gravis. [US]
- CYP2D6 poor metabolizers:
  - Approximately 7–10% of Caucasians and 3–8% of African Americans are CYP2D6 poor metabolizers (PMs). In PMs, quinidine is not expected to increase dextromethorphan effectiveness; however, quinidine adverse effects can still occur. Genotyping may be considered in patients at risk of significant quinidine toxicity prior to therapy. [US]

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and post‑dose ECG (3–4 hours after first dose) in patients with risk factors for QT prolongation or those taking QT‑prolonging drugs or moderate/strong CYP3A4 inhibitors; re-evaluate ECG if risk profile changes.
- Monitor electrolytes (potassium, magnesium) and correct abnormalities prior to and during therapy as clinically indicated.
- Monitor for signs of thrombocytopenia (bleeding, petechiae), hepatitis (jaundice, elevated liver enzymes), and other hypersensitivity signs; discontinue if suspected and evaluate.
- Monitor digoxin plasma concentrations closely if co‑administered; adjust digoxin dose as warranted. [US]

### Specific clinical precautions
- Avoid concomitant strong or moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors when possible; if not avoidable, monitor ECG and consider additional monitoring. Example CYP3A4 inhibitors listed in available source: atazanavir, clarithromycin, indinavir, itraconazole, ketoconazole, nefazodone, nelfinavir, ritonavir, saquinavir, telithromycin, amprenavir, aprepitant, diltiazem, erythromycin, fluconazole, fosamprenavir, grapefruit juice, verapamil. [US]
- Use caution with other centrally acting agents and alcohol. [US]

### Adverse reactions leading to discontinuation (12‑week trial; 20 mg/10 mg twice daily dose)
- Muscle spasticity: 3% (led to discontinuation)
- Respiratory failure: 1%
- Abdominal pain: 2%
- Asthenia: 2%
- Dizziness: 2%
- Fall: 1%
- Muscle spasms: 2%

### Long‑term exposure experience
- Open‑label extension data were consistent with the safety profile observed in controlled trials. Across four Phase 3 trials (controlled + uncontrolled) a total of 946 patients received dextromethorphan/quinidine combinations; 393 patients had exposure ≥180 days and 294 had exposure ≥1 year; median exposure 168 days.

### Adverse reactions reported with the individual components (post‑marketing / voluntary reports)
- Dextromethorphan (reported adverse reactions):
  - Drowsiness, dizziness, nervousness or restlessness, nausea, vomiting, stomach pain. (Frequency not established; post‑marketing.)
- Quinidine (reported adverse reactions; many are post‑marketing or known class effects):
  - Cinchonism: nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, headache, tinnitus, hearing loss, vertigo, blurred vision, diplopia, photophobia, confusion, delirium.
  - Convulsions, apprehension, ataxia (reported but causal relationship unclear).
  - Acute psychotic reactions following first dose (very rare).
  - Depression; mydriasis; disturbed color perception; night blindness; scotomata; optic neuritis; visual field loss.
  - Photosensitivity, keratopathy, abnormalities of skin pigmentation.
  - Hematologic and hypersensitivity events referenced elsewhere in the labeling (thrombocytopenia, agranulocytosis—frequency not established; see Warnings).
  - Hepatotoxicity/hepatitis (frequency not established; see Warnings).

### Serious adverse events highlighted in source
- Immune‑mediated thrombocytopenia (can be severe or fatal).
- Hepatitis, including granulomatous hepatitis.
- QT prolongation and torsades de pointes‑type ventricular tachycardia.
- Serotonin syndrome when co‑administered with serotonergic agents.

## 8. Drug Interactions
Comprehensive interactions and management from the available source:

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- CYP2D6 inhibition (quinidine):
  - Concomitant administration with drugs primarily metabolized by CYP2D6 can result in increased parent drug exposure and/or reduced formation of active metabolites. Consider dose reduction or alternative therapies for CYP2D6 substrates, particularly those with narrow therapeutic indices.
  - Examples with studied interaction magnitude (studies used a higher quinidine/dextromethorphan formulation than the marketed 20/10 mg dose):
    - Desipramine: steady‑state exposure increased approximately 8‑fold when co‑administered with dextromethorphan/quinidine (study with higher-dose combination). If co‑administered, markedly reduce desipramine initial dose and adjust based on clinical response; doses above 40 mg/day not recommended. [US]
    - Paroxetine: when a higher‑dose dextromethorphan/quinidine was added to steady‑state paroxetine, paroxetine AUC increased by ~1.7-fold and Cmax by ~1.5-fold. Consider initiating paroxetine at a lower dose and do not exceed 35 mg/day when combined. [US]
- P‑glycoprotein inhibition (quinidine):
  - Quinidine can raise plasma concentrations of P‑gp substrates. Example: digoxin levels may increase up to ~2‑fold; closely monitor plasma digoxin and reduce digoxin dose as necessary. [US]
- CYP3A4 inhibitors:
  - Concomitant moderate/strong CYP3A4 inhibitors may increase dextromethorphan exposure and QT risk; examples provided: atazanavir, clarithromycin, indinavir, itraconazole, ketoconazole, nefazodone, nelfinavir, ritonavir, saquinavir, telithromycin, amprenavir, aprepitant, diltiazem, erythromycin, fluconazole, fosamprenavir, grapefruit juice, verapamil. Monitor ECG if such combinations cannot be avoided. [US]

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs):
  - Concomitant or recent (within 14 days) MAOI therapy is contraindicated due to risk of serious, possibly fatal interactions including serotonin syndrome. Allow at least 14 days after stopping dextromethorphan + quinidine before initiating an MAOI and vice versa. [US]
- Serotonergic agents:
  - Increased risk of serotonin syndrome when used with SSRIs, SNRIs, tricyclic antidepressants, or other serotonergic drugs. Monitor for signs and discontinue if suspected. [US]
- Drugs that prolong QT interval and are metabolized by CYP2D6:
  - Concomitant use is contraindicated (examples: thioridazine, pimozide) because quinidine‑mediated CYP2D6 inhibition and QT effects can be additive. [US]
- Drugs that prolong QT interval:
  - Concomitant use with other QT‑prolonging agents increases torsades risk; monitor ECG if combination cannot be avoided. [US]
- Alcohol and other CNS depressants:
  - Use with caution; may potentiate central nervous system effects. [US]

### Management recommendations (clinical)
- If initiating or continuing CYP2D6 substrates with narrow therapeutic index, consider dose reduction and monitor clinical response and drug levels where applicable.
- For digoxin co‑administration: closely monitor digoxin concentrations and reduce digoxin dose as necessary.
- For desipramine and paroxetine: apply substantial initial dose reductions and adjust according to clinical response; specific maximum doses recommended in the source when used in combination with the higher‑dose formulation (desipramine ≤40 mg/day; paroxetine ≤35 mg/day). [US]
- Avoid co‑administration with MAOIs, with other quinidine/quinine/mefloquine products, or with drugs that both prolong QT and are CYP2D6 substrates. [US]

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Human data: No adequate data on developmental risk in pregnant women are available.
- Animal data: Administration of dextromethorphan + quinidine to pregnant rats and rabbits produced developmental toxicity in multiple studies, including embryo‑fetal death and fetal malformations at doses tested; reduced pup survival and weight and developmental delays were observed in offspring in rat studies. A no‑effect dose for adverse developmental effects was not identified in animal studies described. Based on animal findings, the combination may cause fetal harm. [US]
- Pregnancy risk categories (jurisdictional systems): Not specified in available sources other than the generalized statement that animal data indicate potential fetal harm. [US]

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Dextromethorphan: acts as an uncompetitive antagonist at NMDA receptors and as a sigma‑1 receptor agonist; pharmacologic activity linked to modulation of glutamatergic and other neurotransmitter systems relevant to the control of emotional expression.
- Quinidine: serves primarily in this combination as a pharmacokinetic enhancer by inhibiting CYP2D6, thereby increasing systemic exposure to dextromethorphan; quinidine also has intrinsic antiarrhythmic and anticholinergic properties.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Quinidine inhibits CYP2D6 leading to higher dextromethorphan exposure than when dextromethorphan is administered alone. The combination produces dose‑dependent QTc prolongation. [US]
- Pharmacogenomics: Approximately 7–10% of Caucasians and 3–8% of African Americans are CYP2D6 poor metabolizers and will have reduced CYP2D6 activity independent of quinidine inhibition; quinidine in the combination is not expected to add efficacy in PMs but can still cause quinidine‑related adverse effects. [US]
- Detailed parameters for absorption, distribution, half‑life, Cmax, AUC, volume of distribution, and clearance: Not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Dose dependent QTc prolongation observed; monitor ECG in at‑risk patients and with interacting drugs. [US]

## 12. Clinical Trials
### Pivotal trials summarized in available source
- Controlled trials:
  - A 12‑week, randomized, placebo‑controlled study compared dextromethorphan 20 mg/quinidine 10 mg twice daily (N=107), a higher dextromethorphan dose combination (30 mg/10 mg, N=110), and placebo (N=109). Approximately 60% of participants had amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) and 40% had multiple sclerosis (MS). Age range 25–80 years; mean age ~51 years.
  - During the 12‑week controlled period, three ALS patients died in each active treatment arm and one ALS patient died in the placebo arm; deaths were consistent with natural disease progression.
  - Adverse reactions leading to discontinuation and the most commonly reported adverse reactions (see Section 7) were identified from these trials.
- Open‑label / uncontrolled studies:
  - Additional uncontrolled studies enrolled patients with PBA secondary to other neurological conditions (e.g., stroke, traumatic brain injury); adverse event experience in these populations was consistent with controlled trials, though different or additional adverse reactions may occur in other neurologic disease populations.
- Patient exposure across trials:
  - Across four Phase 3 trials (controlled and uncontrolled), 946 patients received dextromethorphan/quinidine combinations; 393 patients had exposure ≥180 days and 294 had exposure ≥1 year; median exposure 168 days.
- Primary outcomes and efficacy measures:
  - Specific primary efficacy endpoints, statistical results, and magnitude of clinical benefit are not detailed in the available source text excerpt. Not specified in available sources.
