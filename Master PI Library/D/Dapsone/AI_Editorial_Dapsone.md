# Editorial Product Information: Dapsone

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial was developed based on the Product Information (PI) from the **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. No other regional documents were provided for synthesis.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Dapsone for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference and does not replace the official, legally binding Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Dapsone (4,4'-Sulfonylbisbenzenamine), a sulfone antibacterial and anti-inflammatory agent.
- **Brand Names:** Dapsone [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J04BA02.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Scored tablets: 25 mg and 100 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Link Medical Products Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Dermatitis herpetiformis** [Australian-approved].
- **Leprosy** (all forms) [Australian-approved].
- **Actinomycotic mycetoma** [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Instructions
- Tablets should be taken with or after food to minimise gastrointestinal irritation [Australian]. 
- Scored tablets are provided; however, small doses should be composed of 25 mg tablets rather than splitting tablets [Australian].

### Indication-Specific Dosing (Adults)
- **Dermatitis herpetiformis:** Maintenance dosing typically ranges from 50 to 100 mg daily. Some patients may be controlled on as little as 50 mg weekly. While up to 300 mg daily may be used initially, the dose should be reduced to the minimum effective maintenance level as soon as possible [Australian].
- **Leprosy:** Standard dose is 100 mg daily (equivalent to 1–2 mg/kg body weight). Dapsone must be used as part of a multidrug therapy (MDT) regimen to prevent resistance [Australian]:
    - *Multibacillary leprosy:* Rifampicin (600 mg monthly, supervised), Dapsone (100 mg daily, self-administered), and Clofazimine (300 mg monthly supervised; 50 mg daily self-administered).
    - *Paucibacillary leprosy:* Rifampicin (600 mg monthly, supervised) and Dapsone (100 mg daily, self-administered) for 6 months.
- **Actinomycotic mycetoma:** 100 mg twice daily, continued for several months after clinical symptom resolution. This must be used in combination with other agents, typically Streptomycin [Australian].

### Missed Doses
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to dapsone or its derivatives [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hematologic Toxicity:** Serious and potentially fatal blood dyscrasias, including agranulocytosis and aplastic anaemia, have been reported. Patients must be advised to report sore throat, fever, pallor, purpura, or jaundice immediately [Australian].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Frequent Complete Blood Counts (CBC) are mandatory. The recommended frequency is weekly for the first month, monthly for the next five months, and semi-annually thereafter [Australian]. 
- **G6PD Deficiency:** Haemolysis and Heinz body formation are exaggerated in patients with glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency, methaemoglobin reductase deficiency, or Haemoglobin M. Dose-related haemolysis occurs in nearly all patients; those with G6PD deficiency are at significantly higher risk [Australian].
- **Severe Anemia:** Must be treated prior to initiating dapsone therapy.
- **Dermatologic Reactions:** Rare but severe cutaneous reactions (e.g., Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome) may occur. Dapsone must be discontinued immediately if a new or toxic rash appears [Australian].
- **Porphyria:** Dapsone is considered unsafe in patients with porphyria as it has been associated with acute attacks [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Dose-related haemolysis (loss of 1–2g haemoglobin, increased reticulocytes 2–12%), methaemoglobinaemia [Australian]. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, headache, fever, insomnia [Australian]. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Not specified in available sources. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Agranulocytosis, Dapsone Syndrome (fever, rash, jaundice, eosinophilia), toxic hepatitis, cholestatic jaundice [Australian]. |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Aplastic anaemia [Australian]. |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Dapsone Syndrome:** A hypersensitivity reaction occurring within the first six weeks of therapy. Symptoms include rash, fever, jaundice, and eosinophilia. If not discontinued, it may progress to exfoliative dermatitis, hepatitis, and psychosis [Australian].
- **Peripheral Neuropathy:** Motor loss and muscle weakness may occur; most patients recover after drug discontinuation [Australian].
- **Leprosy Reactional States:** These are not hypersensitivity reactions to dapsone and do not require its discontinuation, though they require management with steroids or analgesics [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Rifampicin:** Lowers dapsone serum levels, potentially compromising efficacy in non-leprosy infections [Australian].
- **Probenecid:** Increases dapsone levels by reducing renal excretion, increasing the risk of toxicity [Australian].
- **Trimethoprim:** Both dapsone and trimethoprim concentrations are increased when co-administered, raising the risk of toxicity [Australian].
- **Cimetidine:** Increases the AUC of dapsone but decreases the AUC of its hydroxylamine metabolite (the metabolite associated with haemotoxicity) [Australian].
- **Didanosine:** Reduces dapsone bioavailability when used in buffered formulations [Australian].
- **Clofazimine:** Dapsone may antagonize the anti-inflammatory effects of clofazimine [Australian].
- **Folic Acid Antagonists (e.g., Pyrimethamine):** Increase the risk of haematologic reactions [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B2** [Australian]. Dapsone should be avoided during pregnancy unless the potential benefit outweighs the risk. Animal studies have shown carcinogenicity at high doses, though animal reproduction studies are limited [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Dapsone is excreted in breast milk in therapeutic amounts. There is a risk of haemolytic anaemia in G6PD-deficient neonates. A decision should be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the mother's clinical need [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Male infertility has been reported as an adverse effect [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Children follow the same dosing schedule as adults, with doses adjusted downward based on body weight. Dapsone is not thought to impact long-term growth or development [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data provided; use with caution [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution due to the drug’s metabolic and excretory pathways [Australian].
- **Cardiopulmonary Disease:** Use with caution, as these patients tolerate haemolysis and methaemoglobinaemia poorly [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bacteriostatic against *Mycobacterium leprae*. It inhibits folic acid synthesis in susceptible organisms, similar to the mechanism of sulfonamides [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Nearly complete absorption via the GI tract; peak plasma levels in 2–8 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Widely distributed; crosses the placenta and enters breast milk. 50–80% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via acetylation (displays genetic polymorphism) and hydroxylation. The hydroxylamine metabolite is associated with haemotoxicity.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 20% excreted unchanged in urine. Half-life ranges from 10 to 80 hours [Australian].

- Protect from light.
