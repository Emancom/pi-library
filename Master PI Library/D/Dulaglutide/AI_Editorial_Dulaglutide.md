## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Dulaglutide (rch)
- **Chemical Class:** Long-acting glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonist. It consists of two identical disulfide-linked chains, each containing a modified human GLP-1 analogue sequence covalently linked to a modified human IgG4 heavy chain fragment (Fc) [Australian].
- **Brand Name:** Trulicity [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** A10BJ05
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Solution for injection in a single-dose pre-filled pen (autoinjector). Strength: 1.5 mg/0.5 mL [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Eli Lilly Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (Glycaemic Control):** Indicated as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycaemic control in adults. It may be used as monotherapy or in combination with other glucose-lowering agents, including insulin, when standard care fails to provide adequate control [Australian].
- **Reduction in Risk of Major Adverse Cardiovascular Events (MACE):** Indicated as an adjunct to standard of care to reduce the risk of MACE (cardiovascular death, non-fatal myocardial infarction, or non-fatal stroke) in adults with type 2 diabetes who have established cardiovascular disease or multiple cardiovascular risk factors [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults ≥18 years):** The recommended dose is 1.5 mg once weekly [Australian].
- **Administration:** Administer via subcutaneous injection in the abdomen, thigh, or upper arm at any time of day, regardless of meals. Do **not** administer intravenously or intramuscularly [Australian].
- **Titration:** [Not specified in available source; Australian source recommends a flat 1.5 mg weekly dose].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, it should be administered as soon as possible if at least 3 days (72 hours) remain before the next scheduled dose. If less than 3 days remain, skip the dose and resume the regular schedule. Do not administer two doses within 72 hours of each other [Australian].
- **Schedule Changes:** The day of weekly administration may be changed provided the last dose was administered 3 or more days prior [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to dulaglutide or any of the excipients (sodium citrate dihydrate, citric acid, mannitol, polysorbate 80) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None listed in provided Australian source.
- **Severe Gastrointestinal Disease:** GLP-1 agonists are associated with GI adverse effects (nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea). Dulaglutide has not been studied in, and is therefore not recommended for, patients with severe GI disease, including severe gastroparesis [Australian].
- **Acute Pancreatitis:** Cases have been reported. Patients should be monitored for persistent severe abdominal pain. If pancreatitis is suspected, discontinue dulaglutide; if confirmed, do not restart [Australian].
- **Hypoglycaemia Risk:** Increased risk when used in combination with sulfonylureas or insulin. Dose reduction of the secretagogue or insulin may be necessary [Australian].
- **Renal Effects:** GI reactions may lead to dehydration, potentially causing acute renal failure. Patients should be advised of the importance of fluid intake [Australian].
- **Psychiatric Disorders:** Suicidal ideation and behaviour have been reported with GLP-1 agonists. Monitor for mood changes or depressive symptoms [Australian].
- **Pulmonary Aspiration:** Due to delayed gastric emptying, there is a risk of pulmonary aspiration during general anaesthesia or deep sedation [Australian].
- **Type 1 Diabetes:** Dulaglutide should not be used in patients with Type 1 DM or for the treatment of diabetic ketoacidosis [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are primarily gastrointestinal and occur most frequently during the initiation of therapy.

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Nausea (21.1%), Vomiting (12.7%), Diarrhoea (12.6%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Abdominal pain, Decreased appetite, Dyspepsia, Fatigue, Constipation, Flatulence, Abdominal distension, GERD |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Cholecystitis (0.1%), Injection site reactions (0.7%), First-degree AV block, Sinus tachycardia |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Anaphylactic reactions, Intestinal obstruction (ileus) [Post-marketing] |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Atrial fibrillation (1.9% vs 1.3% in placebo), acute pancreatitis, and rare reports of Non-arteritic Anterior Ischaemic Optic Neuropathy (NAION) [Australian].
- **Immunogenicity:** Approximately 1.6% of patients developed anti-drug antibodies (ADAs) in clinical trials; however, these did not significantly impact glycaemic efficacy [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Delayed Gastric Emptying:** Dulaglutide slows gastric emptying, which may potentially impact the absorption rate of oral medications. 
- **Clinically Tested Interactions:** No clinically significant impact was observed on the pharmacokinetics of warfarin, metformin, digoxin, lisinopril, metoprolol, paracetamol, oral contraceptives, sitagliptin, or atorvastatin. No dose adjustments are required for concomitant medications [Australian].
- **Metabolism:** Dulaglutide is degraded into amino acids via general protein catabolism; it does not interact with Cytochrome P450 enzymes [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B3.** 
    - Animal studies have shown reproductive toxicity (post-implantation loss, skeletal abnormalities) at high doses. Use in pregnancy is only recommended if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the foetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if dulaglutide is excreted in human milk. Use only if the benefit to the mother outweighs the potential risk to the infant [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in animal models (rats) at doses yielding 30 times the human exposure [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established in patients under 18 years of age [Australian].
- **Elderly Use (≥65 years):** No dose adjustment is required based on age [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for mild, moderate, or severe renal impairment (eGFR down to 15 mL/min/1.73m²). Use is **not recommended** in end-stage renal disease (<15 mL/min) due to very limited experience [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A long-acting GLP-1 receptor agonist that improves glycaemic control by:
    1. Increasing intracellular cAMP in beta cells to stimulate glucose-dependent insulin secretion.
    2. Suppressing glucagon secretion, thereby reducing hepatic glucose output.
    3. Slowing gastric emptying.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Restores first-phase insulin secretion and enhances second-phase secretion in response to glucose [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak plasma concentration reached in 48 hours. Steady state achieved in 2 to 4 weeks. Bioavailability is approximately 47%.
    - **Distribution:** Mean volume of distribution (Vd) is 17.4 L.
    - **Elimination:** Mean apparent clearance is 0.107 L/h with a half-life of **4.7 days**, supporting once-weekly dosing [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Glycaemic Control:** Efficacy was established in eight pivotal Phase 3 studies (n=5,770). Dulaglutide 1.5 mg demonstrated superiority in HbA1c reduction compared to placebo, metformin, sitagliptin, exenatide BID, and insulin glargine [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Outcomes (REWIND Trial):** A study of 9,901 patients followed for a median of 5.4 years. Dulaglutide significantly reduced the risk of MACE (Hazard Ratio 0.88; 95% CI: 0.79, 0.99) compared to placebo. The effect was consistent regardless of prior history of cardiovascular disease [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Refrigeration:** Store at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze [Australian].
- **Room Temperature:** May be stored unrefrigerated for up to 14 days at temperatures not exceeding 30°C [Australian].
- **Light Protection:** Store in original packaging to protect from light [Australian].
