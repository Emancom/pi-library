# Editorial Product Information: diphenoxylate + atropine

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **diphenoxylate + atropine**
- Chemical class:
  - Diphenoxylate: opioid (piperidine derivative chemically related to the narcotic pethidine)
  - Atropine: antimuscarinic (anticholinergic) agent
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral
- Strengths (as presented in sources): diphenoxylate hydrochloride 2.5 mg plus atropine sulfate 25 micrograms per tablet (strength information reported in available sources)

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval (as stated in available sources):
  - 13 February 1985 [Australian]
- Date(s) of last revision/update of source PI (as stated):
  - 12 May 2020 [Australian]
- Withdrawal, suspension or restriction history: Not specified in available sources
- Other jurisdictions' registration/revision dates: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Indicated as an adjunctive therapy for acute and chronic diarrhoea. [Australian]
- Other jurisdictions' indications: Not specified in available sources

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Recommended adult starting dose: 5 mg diphenoxylate (two tablets of diphenoxylate 2.5 mg + atropine 25 µg) three or four times daily.
- After initial control is achieved, dosage should be reduced to individual requirement; maintenance control may often be maintained with as little as 5 mg daily.

### Dosing by indication
- Acute and chronic diarrhoea: use dosing schedule above as the recommended regimen. No additional indication-specific dosing schedules are provided in available sources.

### Administration instructions
- Administer orally.
- Reduce dose once diarrhoea is controlled to the lowest effective dose.
- Patients should be cautioned to adhere strictly to recommended dosing and not to exceed recommended doses due to potential for dependence and toxicity.

### Titration schedules
- Titrate down from starting dose once control of diarrhoea is achieved; no specific stepwise titration schedule beyond "reduce to meet requirements of individual patient" is specified.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
Include all contraindications listed in available sources:
- Known hypersensitivity to diphenoxylate hydrochloride or atropine sulfate.
- Jaundice.
- Diarrhoea associated with pseudomembranous enterocolitis (which may occur during or several weeks following certain antibiotic treatment).
- Diarrhoea associated with inflammatory bowel disease (e.g. ulcerative colitis, Crohn’s disease), bacterial colitis, and amoebic colitis, since diphenoxylate may exacerbate the underlying condition.
- Use in children under 12 years of age: not recommended. (Medication should be kept out of reach of children because accidental overdosage may cause severe, even fatal, respiratory depression.) [Australian]
- Any other contraindications: Not specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed/critical warnings
- **Risk of severe respiratory depression and fatality with overdose — keep out of reach of children; not recommended for children <12 years.** [Australian]

### General warnings and precautions
- CNS depression: Diphenoxylate may potentiate the effects of other central nervous system depressants (e.g. barbiturates, tranquillizers, alcohol). Use caution when co-administered; monitor for additive CNS depression.
- MAO inhibitors: Concurrent use with monoamine oxidase inhibitors may theoretically precipitate hypertensive crisis; close observation is required if combined.
- Antibiotic-associated diarrhoea/pseudomembranous colitis: Consider possibility of serious underlying aetiology before instituting anti-diarrhoeal therapy. Exercise special caution when diarrhoea may be attributable to antibiotics known to cause pseudomembranous colitis.
- Toxic megacolon: Agents that inhibit intestinal motility (including diphenoxylate) have been reported to precipitate toxic megacolon in some patients with acute ulcerative colitis.
- Dehydration and electrolyte imbalance: Correct dehydration and electrolyte disturbances prior to or during use. Withhold therapy until appropriate fluid and electrolyte therapy initiated if severe dehydration or electrolyte imbalance is present, to reduce risk of diphenoxylate toxicity due to variable response.
- Dependence potential: Addiction/dependence is theoretically possible at high dosage due to structural and pharmacologic similarity to addictive narcotics; do not exceed recommended dosage. Use caution in patients with history of substance abuse or those receiving other addicting drugs.
- Atropine-associated effects (atropinism): A subtherapeutic amount of atropine is included to discourage overdose; nevertheless, signs of atropinism may occur — use caution, particularly in individuals with Down’s syndrome where atropinism may be more pronounced.
- Hepatic and hepatorenal disease: Use with extreme caution in patients with advanced hepatorenal disease and in all patients with abnormal liver function, as hepatic coma may be precipitated.
- Effects on driving and machinery: May cause dizziness, drowsiness, confusion, lethargy, sedation — advise patients not to drive or operate machinery until they know how the medicine affects them.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor hydration status and electrolytes in patients with diarrhoea; correct deficits before administration if severe.
- Monitor for signs of CNS depression and respiratory depression, especially when used with other CNS depressants.
- In overdose: continuous monitoring for at least 48 hours recommended due to possibility of delayed or recurrent respiratory depression.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Evaluate for infectious causes of diarrhoea and treat appropriately (antimicrobial therapy for bacterial diarrhoea).
- Avoid use in inflammatory and certain infectious diarrhoeas as above (see Contraindications).
- Exercise caution in elderly patients (no data available regarding specific dosing adjustments).
- Paediatric use: not recommended for children under 12 years — accidental overdose can be life-threatening.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Additive CNS depressant effects:
  - Diphenoxylate may have additive depressant effects when co-administered with barbiturates, tranquillizers (sedatives), and alcohol — increased sedation and respiratory depression possible.
- Antimuscarinic (atropine) effects:
  - Concomitant use of other anticholinergic drugs may increase antimuscarinic adverse effects (not detailed in available sources).

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- No pharmacokinetic interaction data are specified in available sources.

### Other clinically significant interactions and management notes
- MAO inhibitors: Concurrent use may, in theory, precipitate hypertensive crisis; close clinical observation is recommended if combined.
- Antibiotics: Use caution when diarrhoea might be antibiotic-associated; if pseudomembranous colitis is suspected, diphenoxylate should not be used and appropriate antimicrobial therapy should be initiated.
- Management:
  - Avoid or use caution when co-prescribing other CNS depressants; monitor respiratory and CNS status, adjust doses as clinically indicated.
  - If hypertensive symptoms occur with MAO inhibitor co-administration, manage according to standard hypertensive crisis protocols and consider discontinuation.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Pregnancy category (as stated): Category C [Australian].
- Risk summary from available sources:
  - Diphenoxylate is chemically related to narcotic pethidine; narcotic analgesics may cause respiratory depression in the newborn. This drug should not be given at, or near, term.
- Other jurisdictions' pregnancy risk classifications or additional pregnancy data: Not specified in available sources.

### Breastfeeding
- Diphenoxylate and atropine:
  - Diphenoxylate may be excreted in human breast milk; atropine sulfate is also excreted in human milk according to available sources.
  - Infants of nursing mothers taking the combination may exhibit pharmacologic effects of the drugs.
- Recommendations:
  - Consider risk versus benefit when prescribing to breastfeeding mothers; monitor breastfed infants for anticholinergic and opioid effects.

### Fertility
- Effects on fertility: Not specified in available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **Diphenoxylate**: opioid-derivative that slows intestinal motility, thereby reducing diarrhoeal frequency and fluid loss.
- **Atropine**: included in a subtherapeutic amount primarily to discourage deliberate overdosage/self-medication by producing unpleasant antimuscarinic effects at higher-than-recommended doses; it has little therapeutic significance at the included low dose.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: Diphenoxylate is rapidly absorbed; peak blood levels occur at approximately two hours post-oral administration.
- Distribution: Not specified in available sources.
- Metabolism: Rapid biotransformation; relatively short plasma half-life (~2.5 hours) with major metabolic pathway being hydrolysis of the ester to yield diphenoxylic acid, an active metabolite. Metabolites probably undergo enterohepatic circulation.
- Elimination: Excreted principally as metabolites in urine and, to a larger extent, in faeces.
- Atropine pharmacokinetics: Not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Diphenoxylate produces antidiarrhoeal effects through decreased gastrointestinal motility. Atropine contributes antimuscarinic effects at higher doses but is present in a low dose in the combination.

## Overdose
- Symptoms of overdose:
  - Early anticholinergic signs: dryness of skin and mucous membranes, mydriasis, flushing, hyperthermia, tachycardia, restlessness.
  - Progression: lethargy or coma, hypotonic reflexes, nystagmus, pinpoint pupils and respiratory depression.
  - Respiratory depression may present as late as 30 hours after ingestion and may recur after apparent initial response to narcotic antagonists.
- Management of overdose:
  - Treat all suspected overdoses as potentially serious; observe medically for at least 48 hours.
  - Establish and maintain airway; provide artificial ventilation if necessary.
  - If patient is not comatose, gastric lavage and activated charcoal may be considered.
  - Naloxone hydrochloride is the antidote for opioid-induced respiratory depression. Usual initial adult dose: 0.4 mg IV (may be given subcutaneously or intramuscularly if IV not available). Repeat the same IV dose every 2–3 minutes if needed until adequate respiration returns.
  - Because the duration of diphenoxylate action may exceed that of naloxone, repeated or continuous naloxone administration and prolonged monitoring are necessary; respiratory depression can recur.
  - Continuous, preferably hospital-based, observation is recommended for at least 48 hours following overdose.
