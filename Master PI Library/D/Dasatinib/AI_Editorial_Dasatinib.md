# Editorial Product Information: Dasatinib

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information provided by the Australian (TGA) [Australian].
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of dasatinib for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, dosing strategies, and safety profiles.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis for informational purposes and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current approved PI in their specific region before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Dasatinib (as monohydrate)
- **Chemical Class:** Multikinase inhibitor (specifically BCR-ABL and SRC family tyrosine kinase inhibitor).
- **Brand Names:** Dasatinib-Teva [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available source.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 20 mg, 50 mg, 70 mg, and 100 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Teva Pharma Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
### Adult Indications (≥18 years)
- **Chronic Myeloid Leukaemia (CML):**
    - Newly diagnosed Philadelphia chromosome-positive (Ph+) CML in the chronic phase (CP).
    - Ph+ CML (chronic, accelerated, or myeloid/lymphoid blast phase) with resistance or intolerance to prior therapy, including imatinib.
- **Acute Lymphoblastic Leukaemia (ALL):**
    - Newly diagnosed Ph+ ALL, integrated with chemotherapy.
    - Ph+ ALL with resistance or intolerance to prior therapy.

### Paediatric Indications
- Ph+ CML in the chronic phase.
- Newly diagnosed Ph+ ALL in combination with chemotherapy.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing
- **Chronic Phase CML:** 100 mg orally once daily.
- **Accelerated or Blast Phase CML / Ph+ ALL:** 140 mg orally once daily.

### Paediatric Dosing (CML-CP and Ph+ ALL)
Dosing is based on body weight and should be recalculated every three months or more frequently as weight changes.
- **10 kg to <20 kg:** 40 mg once daily.
- **20 kg to <30 kg:** 60 mg once daily.
- **30 kg to <45 kg:** 70 mg once daily.
- **≥45 kg:** 100 mg once daily.
*Note: Tablets are not recommended for children weighing <10 kg. There is no experience in children <1 year of age.*

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets must be swallowed whole; do not crush, cut, or chew.
- May be taken with or without food.
- Administration should be consistent (either morning or evening).
- **Avoid** grapefruit and grapefruit juice.

### Dose Adjustments
- **CYP3A4 Interactions:** If a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor must be co-administered, consider a dose reduction (e.g., 140 mg to 40 mg; 100 mg to 20 mg; 70 mg to 20 mg).
- **Adverse Reactions:** Dose interruption and reduction (e.g., in adults, reducing from 100 mg to 80 mg then to 50 mg) are required for significant myelosuppression or non-haematologic toxicities.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to dasatinib or any excipients (e.g., lactose) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Myelosuppression:** Severe (Grade 3/4) thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, and anaemia occur frequently. Monitoring of complete blood counts (CBC) is required weekly for the first 2 months in advanced phases, and every 2 weeks for 12 weeks in chronic phase patients.
- **Bleeding:** Dasatinib may cause severe CNS and gastrointestinal haemorrhage, often associated with severe thrombocytopenia. It also reversibly affects platelet activation.
- **Fluid Retention:** Very common and potentially severe. Includes pleural effusion (cumulative incidence increases over time), pericardial effusion, and pulmonary oedema. Patients with new respiratory symptoms (dyspnoea, dry cough) require urgent evaluation (e.g., chest X-ray).
- **Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension (PAH):** Reported with dasatinib. If PAH is confirmed, dasatinib must be permanently discontinued.
- **QT Prolongation:** Dasatinib has the potential to prolong the QT interval. Use with caution in patients with hypokalaemia, hypomagnesaemia, or those taking anti-arrhythmic medications.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Monitor transaminases and bilirubin monthly.
- **Hepatitis B Virus (HBV) Reactivation:** Screening for HBV is recommended before treatment initiation.
- **Thrombotic Microangiopathy (TMA):** Rare but reported. Discontinue if TMA is suspected.
- **Paediatric Growth:** Bone growth retardation and epiphyses delayed fusion have been reported in children; long-term monitoring is required.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Table (Single-agent Therapy)
| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Myelosuppression (anaemia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia), headache, diarrhoea, vomiting, nausea, abdominal pain, skin rash, musculoskeletal pain, infection, haemorrhage, pleural effusion, dyspnoea, fatigue, superficial oedema, pyrexia. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Febrile neutropenia, neuropathy, dizziness, dysgeusia, visual disturbances, tinnitus, pulmonary hypertension, lung infiltration, cough, GI bleeding, colitis, gastritis, alopecia, dermatitis, pruritus, arthralgia, myalgia, appetite disturbances. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | CNS bleeding, syncope, arrhythmia, cardiac failure, myocardial infarction, QT prolongation, bronchospasm, pancreatitis, renal failure, rhabdomyolysis, hypothyroidism. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Pure red cell aplasia, convulsion, acute respiratory distress syndrome, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, anaphylactic shock. |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Severe fluid retention (pleural effusion requiring thoracentesis).
- Grade 3/4 haemorrhage (including fatal intracranial/GI bleeding).
- Pulmonary arterial hypertension.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** (e.g., ketoconazole, clarithromycin, grapefruit juice) Increase dasatinib concentrations. Avoid concomitant use.
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** (e.g., rifampicin, phenytoin, St. John’s Wort) Decrease dasatinib concentrations significantly. Use alternatives with less induction potential.
- **Antacids and H2 Antagonists/PPIs:** Dasatinib solubility is pH-dependent. Antacids should be administered 2 hours before or after dasatinib. Long-term suppression by PPIs or H2 blockers (e.g., famotidine, omeprazole) is not recommended.
- **CYP3A4 Substrates:** Dasatinib may increase exposure to narrow-therapeutic-index substrates (e.g., ergot alkaloids, pimozide).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy Category:** Category D [Australian].
- **Recommendations:** Dasatinib may cause fetal harm and malformations. It is not recommended for use during pregnancy or in women contemplating pregnancy. Effective contraception is required for both male and female patients of childbearing potential.
- **Breastfeeding:** Excretion in human milk is unknown. Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies show no effect on male/female fertility, but dasatinib causes testicular atrophy and increased corpora lutea in rodents.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Indicated for Ph+ CML-CP and Ph+ ALL. Monitor for effects on bone growth and development.
- **Elderly Use (≥65 years):** No significant PK differences, but these patients are more likely to experience pleural effusion, dyspnoea, and heart failure [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Caution is recommended in mild, moderate, or severe impairment. No specific dose adjustment is provided, but monitor closely.
- **Renal Impairment:** Renal clearance is <4%. No dose adjustment is expected to be necessary for renal insufficiency.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Potent inhibitor of BCR-ABL kinase and SRC-family kinases. It also inhibits c-KIT, EPHA2, and PDGFβ receptors. It binds to both active and inactive conformations of the BCR-ABL enzyme.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid; peak concentrations (Tmax) in 0.5–6 hours. Bioavailability is not fully determined.
    - **Distribution:** Large apparent volume of distribution (2505 L).
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily by CYP3A4.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily via faeces (85%); terminal half-life is 5–6 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adult CML-CP (Newly Diagnosed):** The Phase III DASISION study showed a significantly higher confirmed complete cytogenetic response (cCCyR) for dasatinib (76.8%) compared to imatinib (66.2%) within 12 months.
- **Imatinib Resistance/Intolerance:** Phase III dose-optimisation studies demonstrated that 100 mg once daily in CP-CML provided comparable efficacy to twice-daily dosing with improved tolerability.
- **Advanced Phases:** In accelerated and blast phase CML, 140 mg once daily was established as the standard based on efficacy and safety profiles.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C.
- Store in the original container.
- **Handling:** Healthcare personnel should wear disposable chemotherapy gloves if tablets are crushed or broken. Pregnant women should avoid exposure to crushed or broken tablets.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
