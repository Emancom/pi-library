# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview intended for healthcare professionals. It synthesizes clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data into a concise reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource only. It is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information (PI) required for prescribing or dispensing in any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for mitochondrial fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Indicated for both acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Standard Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** In cases of severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose may be utilized, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (minimum every 6 hours) over the first 24 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Daily doses typically remain in the 50 mg/kg range, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered if clinically required [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Initial Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine levels. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### General Instructions
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma concentrations prior to treatment. Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended. Target free carnitine levels should be between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Visual Inspection:** Inspect for particulate matter and discoloration before use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** While the US source states "None known" in the specific contraindications section, serious hypersensitivity is addressed in the Warnings section [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These often occur within minutes of injection, particularly in ESRD patients. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks before re-administration [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Periodic monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations is essential [US].
- **Oral Formulation Caution:** The safety of oral levocarnitine has not been established in patients with renal impairment. High-dose oral therapy in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally renally excreted [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions in ESRD patients are often confounded by underlying pathology. The following were reported at a frequency of ≥5% in clinical trials:

| Body System | Adverse Effect (Frequency ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Injection site reaction (33–38%), Pain (21–35%), Flu syndrome (15–29%), Headache (3–37%), Chest pain (6–15%), Asthenia (8–12%), Fever (5–12%), Abdominal pain (5–21%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (18–21%), Hypotension (3–19%), Tachycardia (5–9%), Arrhythmia (3%), Palpitations (3–8%) |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea (9–35%), Vomiting (9–21%), Nausea (5–12%), Anorexia (3–6%) |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia (6–15%), Hyperkalemia (6%), Hypervolemia (3–12%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (15–27%), Cough increase (10–18%), Dyspnea (3–11%) |
| **Neurologic** | Dizziness (10–18%), Paresthesia (3–12%), Insomnia (3–6%) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and seizures [US].
- **Other Effects:** Body odor and gastritis have been reported [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate that levocarnitine may increase the International Normalized Ratio (INR) in patients receiving warfarin. Frequent monitoring of INR is recommended upon initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not specified; however, reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (BSA-based) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm]. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Although bovine studies show increased carnitine in milk, the risks to the infant must be weighed against the benefits to the mother. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug should be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders follows the weight-based 50 mg/kg protocol [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Avoid high-dose **oral** levocarnitine due to metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO). The injection is specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production in all tissues except the brain [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, it alleviates carnitine deficiency caused by dialytic loss, reduced renal synthesis, and dietary restrictions [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 dialysis patients receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly, trough plasma levocarnitine levels increased from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L. 
- **Dose-Ranging:** Trough concentrations remained stable or increased over 12–24 weeks, with higher doses (40 mg/kg) resulting in higher mean trough levels (456 µmol/L) compared to 10 mg/kg (148 µmol/L) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing.
- **Preservation:** The formulation contains no preservatives; discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].
