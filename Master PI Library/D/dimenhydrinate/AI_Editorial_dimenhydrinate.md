# Editorial Product Information: dimenhydrinate

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **dimenhydrinate**
- Chemical class: dimenhydrinate (salt of diphenhydramine and 8‑chlorotheophylline)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral
- Other excipient information noted: contains calcium 35 mg per tablet (as stated in available sources)

## 2. Regulatory History
- Marketing start date shown in available sources: 12/01/1992
- Date of last revision/update in available sources: Revised 6/2026
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources
- If additional jurisdictional registration dates are required: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Prevention and treatment of symptoms associated with motion sickness, including:
  - Nausea
  - Vomiting
  - Dizziness

(Indication statements are taken from available sources; no other indications are specified.)

## 4. Dosage and Administration
General principles
- Do not take more than directed.
- For prevention of motion sickness, take the first dose 1/2 to 1 hour before beginning travel or exposure to the motion stimulus.

Recommended dosing (as stated in available sources)
- Adults and children 12 years and over: 1 to 2 tablets every 4–6 hours. Do not exceed 8 tablets in 24 hours, or as directed by a physician.
- Children 6 to under 12 years: 1/2 to 1 tablet every 6–8 hours. Do not give more than 3 tablets in 24 hours, or as directed by a physician.
- Children 2 to under 6 years: 1/2 tablet every 6–8 hours. Do not give more than 1 1/2 tablets in 24 hours, or as directed by a physician.

Administration instructions
- Take orally. For prevention, administer the first dose 30–60 minutes before exposure to motion.
- Avoid concomitant intake of alcohol and other central nervous system depressants (see Section 8).

## 5. Contraindications

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
Boxed warnings: Not specified in available sources.

General warnings and precautions
- Marked drowsiness may occur with use. Patients should be cautioned about activities requiring mental alertness such as driving or operating machinery.
- Alcohol, sedatives, and tranquilizers may increase drowsiness; concurrent use should be avoided.
- Ask a health professional before use if the patient has:
  - Glaucoma
  - A breathing problem such as emphysema or chronic bronchitis
  - Difficulty in urination due to enlargement of the prostate gland
- Ask a doctor or pharmacist before use if the patient is taking sedatives or tranquilizers.
- If pregnant or breastfeeding, ask a health professional before use.
- Keep out of reach of children.
- Overdose: in case of overdose, obtain medical help or contact a Poison Control Center immediately.

Monitoring requirements
- Specific monitoring recommendations are not specified in available sources. Monitor for excessive sedation, anticholinergic effects, and signs of respiratory compromise in susceptible patients as clinically indicated.

Specific clinical precautions
- Use caution in patients with underlying respiratory disease (e.g., emphysema, chronic bronchitis) as product may cause sedation and could worsen respiratory function.
- Use caution in patients with urinary retention or prostatic hypertrophy because anticholinergic effects may exacerbate obstruction.
- Use caution in patients with glaucoma; increase in intraocular pressure may occur with anticholinergic agents.

## 8. Drug Interactions
Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Alcohol, sedatives, and tranquilizers: concomitant use may increase drowsiness and central nervous system depression. Patients should avoid alcoholic beverages while using the product and use caution with other CNS depressants.
- Ask a doctor or pharmacist before use if the patient is taking sedatives or tranquilizers (stated as an advisory in available sources).

Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Not specified in available sources.

Clinically significant interactions and management
- Avoid coadministration with alcohol, sedatives, or tranquilizers where possible due to additive sedation. Counsel patients regarding increased risk of drowsiness and impaired ability to perform tasks requiring alertness.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
Paediatric use
- Dosing by age group provided in Section 4:
  - Children 2 to under 6 years: 1/2 tablet every 6–8 hours; maximum 1 1/2 tablets in 24 hours.
  - Children 6 to under 12 years: 1/2 to 1 tablet every 6–8 hours; maximum 3 tablets in 24 hours.
  - Children 12 years and over: follow adult dosing.
- Do not use in children under 2 years of age unless directed by a doctor.

Elderly use
- Specific dose adjustments or formal guidance for elderly patients are not specified in available sources. Exercise caution due to potential for increased sensitivity to central nervous system effects (e.g., drowsiness) and anticholinergic effects; counsel regarding driving/operating machinery.
