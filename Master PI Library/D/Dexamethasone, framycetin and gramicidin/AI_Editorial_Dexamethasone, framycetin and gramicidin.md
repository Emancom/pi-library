# Editorial Product Information: Dexamethasone, Framycetin Sulfate, and Gramicidin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of the combination ear drop containing dexamethasone, framycetin, and gramicidin for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** 
    - Framycetin sulfate (5 mg/mL): An aminoglycoside antibiotic.
    - Gramicidin (50 µg/mL): A polypeptide antibiotic.
    - Dexamethasone (as sodium metasulfobenzoate) (500 µg/mL): A potent corticosteroid.
- **Brand Names:** **Otodex** [Australian]. (Note: Referred to as Sofradex in historical clinical data).
- **ATC Code:** S02CA06 (Dexamethasone and antiinfectives).
- **Dosage Form:** Clear, bright, colourless aqueous otic solution.
- **Route of Administration:** Otic (ear drops).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pharmaco (Australia) Ltd under license from CHEPLAPHARM Arzneimittel GmbH [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inflammatory and allergic conditions of the ear:** Specifically indicated for otitis externa.
- **Eczema of the auditory meatus:** Management of associated inflammation, exudation, and pruritus.
- **Secondary infections:** Treatment of infections caused by susceptible organisms, including *Staphylococci*, *E. coli*, *Pseudomonas*, and *Proteus* spp. [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Children)
- **Instillation:** 2 or 3 drops instilled into the affected ear three or four times daily.
- **Alternative Method:** A gauze wick may be inserted into the external auditory meatus and kept saturated with the solution.

### Administration Instructions
- Treatment should not be continued after symptoms have resolved.
- Prolonged use should be avoided to prevent the emergence of resistant organisms or skin sensitisation [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to framycetin sulfate, gramicidin, dexamethasone, or any excipients (including polysorbate 80 and phenethyl alcohol).
- **Perforated Eardrum:** Use is strictly contraindicated due to the risk of ototoxicity if the drug enters the middle ear.
- **Specific Infections:**
    - Viral infections (including varicella and vaccinia).
    - Fungal infections.
    - Tubercular lesions.
    - Acute purulent, untreated infections [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Ototoxicity:** Aminoglycosides (framycetin) may cause irreversible partial or total deafness when applied to open wounds or damaged skin. This risk is increased by renal or hepatic impairment and prolonged treatment duration.
- **Mitochondrial Mutations:** There is an increased risk of aminoglycoside-induced ototoxicity in patients with mitochondrial mutations, particularly the **m.1555A>G mutation**, even if serum levels are within the normal range. 
- **Masking of Infection:** Corticosteroids may mask the clinical signs of an extending infection. If no clinical improvement is observed, the combination should be discontinued.
- **Cross-Sensitivity:** Patients allergic to other *Streptomyces*-derived antibiotics (e.g., neomycin, paromomycin, kanamycin) may exhibit cross-reactivity with framycetin.
- **Visual Disturbances:** Both systemic and topical corticosteroid use are linked to visual disturbances. Patients reporting blurred vision should be evaluated for cataracts, glaucoma, or central serous chorioretinopathy (CSCR).
- **Pheochromocytoma Crisis:** Rare but potentially fatal crises have been reported following corticosteroid administration.
- **Diabetes:** Use may increase the requirement for insulin or oral antidiabetics due to reduced hypoglycaemic reactions [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
| System Organ Class | Adverse Effect | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Immune System** | Local hypersensitivity (irritation, burning, stinging, itching, dermatitis) | Rare / Not Known |
| **Eye Disorders** | Glaucoma, cataracts, corneal perforation, blurred vision, chorioretinopathy | Not Known |
| **Endocrine** | Iatrogenic Cushing’s syndrome, adrenal atrophy | Not Known |
| **Metabolism** | Diabetes mellitus, decreased glucose tolerance | Not Known |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Irreversible ototoxicity (deafness) and adrenal suppression (particularly in infants) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Co-treatment with potent CYP3A inhibitors (e.g., cobicistat-containing products) is expected to increase systemic exposure to dexamethasone and the risk of systemic corticosteroid side effects. This combination should be avoided unless the benefits outweigh the risks [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category D [Australian]:** Aminoglycosides cross the placenta and may cause fetal nephrotoxicity or damage to the eighth cranial nerve.
    - **Recommendation:** Use only if potential benefits to the mother outweigh potential risks to the fetus. Therapeutic maternal blood levels do not guarantee fetal safety.
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data regarding the presence of the drug in human milk. Safety has not been established; use only if the benefit outweighs the risk to the infant [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use in infants carries a risk of adrenal suppression, even without occlusive dressings, particularly with prolonged treatment.
- **Elderly Use:** Specific data are not available for this population [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Increased risk of ototoxicity; duration of treatment should be strictly limited [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Framycetin sulfate:** Bactericidal antibiotic effective against many Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria.
    - **Gramicidin:** Polypeptide antibiotic that enhances the activity of framycetin against *Streptococci*.
    - **Dexamethasone:** High-potency corticosteroid that suppresses the inflammatory and allergic response.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Not specified in available sources for topical otic administration.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Dexamethasone is particularly effective in cases where other corticosteroids have failed [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No specific pivotal trial data are detailed in the available source document [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Refrigeration:** Do not refrigerate.
- **Shelf Life:** Discard 4 weeks after the first opening of the bottle [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only.
