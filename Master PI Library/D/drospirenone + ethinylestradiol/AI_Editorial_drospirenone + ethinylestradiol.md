## 3. Approved Indications
- Use as an oral contraceptive (combined hormonal contraceptive).
- Treatment of moderate acne vulgaris in women who seek an oral contraceptive.
- Treatment of symptoms of premenstrual dysphoric disorder (PMDD) in women who have chosen an oral contraceptive as their method of birth control. (Efficacy for PMDD was not assessed beyond 3 cycles.)
- Note: The product has not been evaluated for treatment of premenstrual syndrome (PMS).

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Standard cycle: 28 consecutive days with 24 days of active tablets followed by 4 days of placebo tablets (continuous daily dosing; one tablet once daily).

[Australian] The stated contraceptive efficacy is based on the 24 active / 4 inactive (28-day) regimen.

### Dosing by indication
- Oral contraception, acne, PMDD: same 24/4 regimen described above. No separate dose strengths or alternative per-indication dosages specified in available sources.

### Administration instructions
- Take one tablet at approximately the same time each day with some liquid as needed.
- Start each new pack the day after the last tablet of the previous pack (i.e. no tablet-free interval other than the 4 placebo days).

How to start
- No hormonal contraceptive use in prior month: start on Day 1 of the natural menstrual cycle (first day of bleeding) by taking an active tablet corresponding to that day; contraceptive protection is immediate if begun on Day 1.
- Starting on Days 2–5 of cycle: allowed, but use an additional barrier method for the first 7 days.
- Switching from another combined hormonal contraceptive (COC) or vaginal ring: start preferably the day after the last active tablet of previous COC or the day after the usual tablet-free/placebo interval; for rings, start preferably on the day of removal or at latest when the next application would have been due.
- Switching from progestogen-only methods: may switch from minipill any day, from implant or IUS on removal day, or from injectable when the next injection is due; when switching from these methods, use an additional barrier method for the first 7 days of tablet-taking.
- After first‑trimester abortion: may start immediately without additional contraception.
- After delivery or second‑trimester abortion: start on Day 21–28 after delivery or second‑trimester abortion; if started later, use additional barrier contraception for first 7 days. If intercourse has occurred before starting, exclude pregnancy or wait for first menstrual period before initiating.

### Missed dose instructions (active tablet)
- Missed white placebo tablets in the final row may be discarded to avoid prolonging the placebo phase.
- If < 24 hours late taking an active tablet: contraceptive protection is not reduced — take the missed tablet as soon as remembered and continue at usual time.
- If > 24 hours late (i.e. missed one or more active tablets): contraceptive protection may be reduced. Management is guided by two principles: (1) the placebo interval is 4 days and active tablet-taking must not be interrupted for longer than 7 consecutive days; (2) 7 days of uninterrupted active tablet-taking are required to restore reliable ovarian suppression.
  - Day 1–7 (first week of pack): take the last missed active tablet as soon as remembered (may take two tablets at once), continue at usual time; use barrier contraception for the next 7 days. If intercourse occurred in prior 7 days, consider pregnancy.
  - Day 8–14 (second week): take the last missed active tablet as soon as remembered (may take two tablets at once), continue at usual time. If the woman had taken tablets correctly in the 7 days before the first missed tablet, no additional precautions are needed. If prior adherence was not correct or more than one active tablet missed, use extra precautions for 7 days.
  - Day 15–24 (third week): because reduced reliability is imminent before placebo phase, adjust intake to maintain protection by either:
    1. Taking the last missed active tablet as soon as remembered (may take two tablets at once), continue active tablets at usual times until all active tablets are finished, discard the 4 placebo tablets and start next pack immediately (withdrawal bleed is unlikely until the end of the active tablets of the following pack; breakthrough bleeding/spotting may occur); or
    2. Stop the current pack, have a tablet-free interval up to 4 days (including days missed), and then resume with the next pack.
  - If no withdrawal bleed occurs during the placebo interval following missed tablets, consider pregnancy.

### Delaying withdrawal bleed
- Continuous use of active tablets without placebo for up to three packs has been associated with similar contraceptive efficacy. There are no data on acne or PMDD efficacy with this extended regimen. Breakthrough bleeding/spotting may occur; this is best managed by taking placebo tablets to induce a withdrawal bleed, then resuming the 28‑day cycle. Long‑term safety data for extended continuous use (in terms of VTE, endometrial safety, or cancer) are not available.

### Gastro-intestinal disturbances
- Severe diarrhoea or vomiting may impair absorption. If vomiting occurs within 3–4 hours of tablet intake, follow the missed active tablet instructions. If the user does not want to change schedule, take an extra tablet from another pack as needed.

## 5. Contraindications
The product must not be used in the presence of any of the conditions below. If any of these conditions appear for the first time during use, stop the product immediately.

- Presence or risk of venous thromboembolism (VTE):
  - Current VTE (on anticoagulants) or history of deep venous thrombosis (DVT) or pulmonary embolism (PE)
  - Known hereditary or acquired predisposition for VTE (e.g. APC-resistance including Factor V Leiden; antithrombin‑III deficiency; protein C deficiency; protein S deficiency)
  - Major surgery with prolonged immobilisation
  - High risk of VTE due to multiple risk factors

- Presence or risk of arterial thromboembolism (ATE):
  - Current ATE or history of ATE (e.g. myocardial infarction or stroke) or prodromal condition (e.g. angina pectoris or transient ischaemic attack)
  - Known hereditary or acquired predisposition for ATE (e.g. hyperhomocysteinaemia; antiphospholipid antibodies such as anticardiolipin antibodies or lupus anticoagulant)
  - History of migraine with focal neurological symptoms
  - High risk of ATE due to multiple risk factors or presence of a serious risk factor such as diabetes mellitus with vascular symptoms, severe hypertension, or severe dyslipoproteinaemia

- Pancreatitis, or history of pancreatitis if associated with severe hypertriglyceridaemia
- Presence or history of severe hepatic disease while liver function values have not returned to normal
- Severe renal insufficiency or acute renal failure
- Concomitant use with the following antiviral agents is contraindicated: glecaprevir, pibrentasvir, sofosbuvir, velpatasvir, voxilaprevir, ombitasvir, paritaprevir, or dasabuvir, and combinations containing these agents
- Presence or history of liver tumours (benign or malignant)
- Known or suspected sex steroid‑influenced malignancy (e.g. of genital organs or breasts)
- Undiagnosed vaginal bleeding
- Known or suspected pregnancy
- Hypersensitivity to any of the ingredients

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Combined hormonal contraceptives increase the risk of both venous and arterial thrombotic and thromboembolic events compared with non-use. These events are rare in average‑risk women but can be life‑threatening or fatal.
- The risk of VTE is highest during the first year of use and may be increased when restarting after a break of ≥4 weeks. Some evidence suggests greatest increased risk during the first 3 months of initiation.
- Epidemiologic evidence regarding comparative VTE risk for drospirenone-containing combined oral contraceptives versus other progestogens is mixed: some studies show comparable risk to levonorgestrel-containing products, while others suggest increased risk (ranging from no increase to up to three‑fold). Interpret comparative risk with caution due to study design differences and small case numbers in some analyses.
- VTE risk related to pregnancy and the post‑partum period is higher than the VTE risk associated with combined oral contraceptive use; this should be considered when counselling patients.
- An additional increase in VTE risk for products containing ≥ 50 μg ethinylestradiol cannot be excluded. When choosing a contraceptive, consider products with the lowest VTE risk and discuss risk/benefit with the woman.
- Discontinue prior to elective surgery with prolonged immobilisation (at least 4 weeks before elective major surgery) and do not resume until at least 2 weeks after complete remobilisation. Consider antithrombotic prophylaxis if product has not been discontinued in advance.
- If hereditary predisposition to VTE or ATE is suspected, refer to a specialist before initiating a combined hormonal contraceptive.
- If any of the conditions or risk factors listed under Contraindications or Warnings appear or worsen during treatment, the product should be stopped.

### Monitoring requirements
- Specific routine monitoring parameters and schedules are not specified in available sources. Monitor for signs/symptoms of thromboembolism and other conditions listed under Warnings and Contraindications.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Patients should be counselled about the signs and symptoms of VTE and ATE and advised to seek urgent medical attention if symptoms occur (see below).
- For women at risk of prolonged immobilisation or multiple thrombotic risk factors, carefully consider alternative contraception.

### Symptoms to advise patients to report urgently
- Symptoms of deep vein thrombosis (DVT): unilateral leg swelling, pain/tenderness in the leg, increased warmth, redness or discoloration.
- Symptoms of pulmonary embolism (PE): sudden unexplained shortness of breath, rapid breathing, sudden cough (possibly with haemoptysis), sharp chest pain, sudden lightheadedness or dizziness, rapid/irregular heartbeat.
- Symptoms of arterial thromboembolism (stroke/MI/TIA): sudden numbness or weakness (face, arm, leg, especially one side), sudden trouble walking/dizziness/loss of coordination, sudden confusion or slurred speech, sudden severe headache of no known cause, chest pain or pressure.
- Other rare thrombosis presentations: severe abdominal pain (mesenteric thrombosis), liver pain/jaundice (hepatic vein thrombosis), sudden visual disturbance (retinal artery/vein occlusion).

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Several epidemiologic and cohort studies concerning thrombotic risk are described in the available source:
  - EURAS (large prospective multinational cohort): reported VTE incidence in oral contraceptive users ranging ~8.0–9.9 per 10,000 women‑years; overall past OC users 4.7 VTE/10,000 WY; noted comparable thrombotic risk for ethinylestradiol/drospirenone 30 μg/3 mg users versus other oral contraceptives including levonorgestrel-containing products.
  - Ingenix (prospective cohort): reported a thromboembolism risk in ethinylestradiol/drospirenone 30 μg/3 mg users comparable to that of other combined oral contraceptives.
  - Additional studies: Heit et al. reported higher incidence rates in pregnant/postpartum women (≈20 VTE/10,000 WY) versus non‑pregnant women (≈4.6 VTE/10,000 WY). Case‑control and retrospective cohort studies (e.g. van Hylckama Vlieg; Lidegaard 2009) suggested a potentially higher VTE risk with ethinylestradiol/drospirenone compared with levonorgestrel-containing COCs in some analyses; but data are inconsistent and may be limited by small numbers or methodological issues.
  - Other retrospective database studies (Parkin; Jick and Hernandez) published later suggested increased VTE risk with drospirenone-containing COCs in some analyses; however, case numbers for drospirenone were small in some reports.
- PMDD trials: The available source states that efficacy for PMDD was demonstrated but was not assessed beyond 3 cycles; no further trial design details or outcomes are provided in the available source.
- Acne clinical trial details are not provided in the available source.

If further pivotal trial summaries, detailed endpoints, or complete safety/efficacy data are required, these are not present in the provided source text.
