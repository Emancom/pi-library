# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States** (Food and Drug Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a concise reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing and dispensing in any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. 
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [United States].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [United States].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [United States-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [United States-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [United States].
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [United States].
- **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [United States].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically remain in the 50 mg/kg range, though therapy may require adjustment based on clinical response. The highest recorded dose administered is 300 mg/kg [United States].

### 3.2 ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [United States].
- **Administration Timing:** Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [United States].
- **Titration:** Therapy initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations <40–50 µmol/L. Dose adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) should be guided by trough plasma levels and may be implemented as early as the third or fourth week of treatment [United States].

### 3.3 Administration Instructions
- Inspect parenteral products visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [United States].
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of IV administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate medical management [United States].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known seizure disorder, an increase in frequency or severity of seizures may occur [United States].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** While not the subject of this IV PI, it is noted that chronic high-dose oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### 6.1 Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported at a frequency of $\ge$5% regardless of causality in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials [United States]:

| Body System | Adverse Event (Incidence $\ge$5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain, headache, injection site reaction, pain, flu syndrome, infection, asthenia, abdominal pain, fever, accidental injury |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, palpitations |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, dyspepsia, anorexia |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight change |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia, depression |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis |
| **Skin** | Pruritus, rash |
| **Urogenital** | Kidney failure, urinary tract infection |

### 6.2 Serious and Other Adverse Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent reports of body odor and gastritis [United States].
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [United States].
- **Immune System:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, laryngeal edema [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [United States: Category C (implied)]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits using doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate, well-controlled studies exist in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased milk concentrations of levocarnitine following exogenous administration. The risks to the infant from excess carnitine intake must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [United States].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models at 3.8 times human doses [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Refer to metabolic disorder dosing (50 mg/kg) [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Safely used in ESRD patients on dialysis (see Section 3.2). The safety and efficacy of the *oral* formulation have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency [United States].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [United States].
    - **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [United States].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis), trough levels increased from a baseline of $21.3 \pm 7.7$ µmol/L to $190 \pm 55$ µmol/L after 8 weeks. While levocarnitine effectively increases carnitine concentrations in this population, the effects on specific clinical outcomes and signs/symptoms of deficiency have not been definitively determined [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [United States].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [United States].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
