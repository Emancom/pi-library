# Editorial Product Information: Droperidol

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australia (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of droperidol product information intended for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial summary and does not replace the official PI. Clinicians must refer to the current approved PI in their specific jurisdiction for prescribing purposes.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Droperidol
- **Chemical Class:** Butyrophenone neuroleptic
- **Brand Names:** Droperidol Lupin [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** N05AD08
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Solution for injection: 2.5 mg/1 mL and 5.0 mg/2 mL in Type I brown glass ampoules [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous (IV), Intramuscular (IM)
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Southern Cross Pharma Pty Ltd / Generic Health Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Anaesthesia [Australian-approved]:** 
    - Induction of tranquillisation and reduction of nausea and vomiting during surgical and diagnostic procedures.
    - Premedication and induction agent.
    - Adjunct for the maintenance of general and regional anaesthesia.
    - Component of neuroleptanalgesia (administered concurrently with a narcotic analgesic) to reduce anxiety and pain while promoting calmness.
- **Psychiatry [Australian-approved]:** 
    - Management of severe agitation, hyperactivity, or aggressiveness in psychotic disorders (e.g., schizophrenia, manic phases of bipolar disorder).
    - Management of acute brain syndrome or nonpsychotic acute excitation states.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Principles
Dosage must be individualised based on age, weight, physical status, underlying pathology, and the nature of the procedure/anaesthesia. Continuous vital sign monitoring and ECG assessment (pre-procedure and for at least 7 hours post-procedure) are required [Australian].

### Adult Dosing
- **Premedication:** 2.5 to 10 mg IV, administered 30 to 60 minutes before surgery.
- **Induction of Anaesthesia:** 2.5 mg per 10 kg body weight, typically administered IV.
- **Maintenance of Anaesthesia:** 1.25 mg to 2.5 mg IV.
- **Regional Anaesthesia Adjunct:** 2.5 to 5 mg IM or slow IV when additional sedation is needed.
- **Psychiatric Management:** 
    - **IM:** 5 to 25 mg (commonly 10 to 25 mg), repeated every 4 to 6 hours as necessary.
    - **IV Drip Infusion:** 50 to 125 mg daily (in normal saline, 5% glucose, or Lactated Ringer's), divided into two 20-minute infusions.

### Paediatric Dosing
- **Children (2 to 12 years):** Induction dose of 1.0 to 1.5 mg per 10 kg body weight [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Product is for single use in one patient only.
- Slow IV injection is recommended to reduce the risk of muscle rigidity when used with narcotics.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to droperidol or its metabolites.
- Severe Central Nervous System (CNS) depression or comatose states.
- Parkinson’s disease.
- Phaeochromocytoma.
- Breastfeeding.
- Cardiac Risk Factors [Australian]:
    - QTc interval >450 msec.
    - Congenital or family history of long QT syndrome.
    - Acquired long QT (e.g., from drugs known to prolong QT).
    - Clinically significant bradycardia, hypokalaemia, or hypomagnesaemia.
- **Relative Contraindication:** Acute alcohol intoxication [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **QT Prolongation and Arrhythmia:** Droperidol is associated with a risk of QT prolongation and torsade de pointes. ECG monitoring and cardiac resuscitation facilities must be available. 
- **Psychiatric Crisis Management (Community):** Outside hospital settings, IM doses for acute mania/agitation should not exceed 5 mg, with immediate transfer to a hospital equipped for resuscitation [Australian].
- **Hypotension:** May occur due to alpha-adrenergic blockade. Fluids should be available. If pressors are needed, avoid adrenaline, as it may paradoxically lower blood pressure further.
- **Venous Thromboembolism (VTE):** Risk should be assessed as VTE has been reported with antipsychotics.
- **Elderly with Dementia:** Use of antipsychotics is associated with an increased risk of death in this population. Droperidol is not indicated for dementia-related behavioural disturbances [Australian].
- **Respiratory Risk:** Caution in patients with COPD, respiratory failure, or sleep apnoea.
- **Seizure Risk:** Use with caution in patients with epilepsy.

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common/Frequent** | Drowsiness, mild to moderate hypotension, tachycardia. |
| **Uncommon/Rare** | QT prolongation, ventricular arrhythmias, sudden death, body temperature dysregulation, hypersensitivity (rash, angio-oedema). |
| **Class Effects / Frequency Unknown** | Extrapyramidal reactions (dystonia, akathisia, oculogyric crisis), Tardive Dyskinesia, Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS), VTE. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Respiratory depression/apnoea (especially when combined with narcotics), laryngospasm, and hepatic impairment (jaundice) have been noted with related compounds [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **QT-Prolonging Drugs:** Contraindicated (e.g., Class IA and III antiarrhythmics, certain antidepressants, phenothiazines).
- **CNS Depressants:** Additive effects with barbiturates, benzodiazepines, and opioids.
- **Antihypertensives:** Potential for increased orthostatic hypotension.
- **Dopamine Agonists:** Droperidol may inhibit the action of levodopa, bromocriptine, and lisuride.
- **CYP1A2 and CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** May decrease droperidol metabolism, prolonging its effect.
- **Enzyme Inducers:** Agents like phenobarbitone or phenytoin, as well as smoking/alcohol, may increase the breakdown of neuroleptics.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category B1. 
    - **Class Effect:** Third-trimester exposure to antipsychotics carries a risk of extrapyramidal symptoms or withdrawal in neonates (e.g., agitation, tremor, respiratory distress). Droperidol should only be used if the benefit outweighs the risk, using the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Butyrophenones are excreted in milk. Breastfeeding should be avoided if droperidol use is essential [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Data not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for children under 2 years of age [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Initial doses should be reduced. Caution is required due to the increased risk of death in dementia-related conditions.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Administer with caution as these organs are vital for metabolism and excretion. Specific dose adjustment tables are not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A butyrophenone neuroleptic that acts as a dopamine antagonist.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Produces tranquillisation, sedation, and a potent antiemetic effect. It induces mild alpha-adrenergic blockade and peripheral vascular dilatation. 
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed following IM administration.
    - **Distribution:** Plasma distribution half-life is approximately 10 minutes.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic; metabolism rate may be affected by CYP inhibitors [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal elimination half-life is ~134 minutes. Approximately 75% is excreted in urine (mostly as metabolites; <1% unchanged) and 22% in faeces.
    - **Onset/Duration:** Onset is 3–10 minutes post-injection. Full sedation may take 30 minutes. Duration of sedation is typically 2–4 hours, though consciousness may be altered for up to 12 hours.
