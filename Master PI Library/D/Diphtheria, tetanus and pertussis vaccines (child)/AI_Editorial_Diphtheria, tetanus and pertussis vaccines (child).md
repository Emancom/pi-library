# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical and regulatory information into a coherent reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for legal and regulatory compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. This may be administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight. It should be administered as a slow bolus (2–3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Parenteral Inspection:** Inspect visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [US].
- **Loading Doses:** In severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Titration:** In ESRD, dose adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations [US].

### Monitoring
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- **ESRD:** Monitor trough (pre-dialysis) concentrations (normal range: 40–50 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** Not specified in available sources, though serious hypersensitivity reactions are listed under warnings [US].
- **General:** None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occurred within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and initiate appropriate medical treatment [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Post-marketing reports indicate seizures may occur in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation):** Safety/efficacy of oral levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal impairment. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) normally excreted in urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trials Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported at a frequency of ≥5% in placebo-controlled trials include:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (21%), accidental injury (12%), asthenia (12%), chest pain (15%), fever (12%), headache (37%), infection (24%), injection site reaction (38%), and pain (35%) [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (21%), hypotension (19%), tachycardia (9%) [US].
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (35%), vomiting (21%), nausea (12%), anorexia (6%) [US].
- **Metabolic/Nutritional:** Hypercalcemia (15%), hypervolemia (12%) [US].
- **Nervous:** Dizziness (18%), paresthesia (12%), insomnia (6%) [US].
- **Respiratory:** Cough increase (18%), pharyngitis (27%), dyspnea (11%) [US].

### Post-marketing Experience
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concomitantly. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned under modern labeling, but previous data remains]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. Risks of excess intake in the infant must be weighed against maternal benefit; consideration may be given to discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, S. cerevisiae, S. pombe) were negative. Long-term animal carcinogenicity studies have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See Dosage and Administration for weight-based dosing (50 mg/kg in metabolic disorders) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically in ESRD patients on dialysis (see Dosage and Administration). Safety of high-dose oral formulations is not established in this population due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria for oxidation and energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV administration, ~76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma carnitine from ~20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks. Clinical outcomes regarding signs and symptoms of carnitine deficiency in this population have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Single-dose vial; discard any unused portion. The formulation contains no preservatives [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
