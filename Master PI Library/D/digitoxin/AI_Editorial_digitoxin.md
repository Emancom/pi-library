# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) documentation from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
*   The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a single clinically relevant reference.
*   **Important:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or SmPC required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
*   **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/amino acid derivative; (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
*   **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
*   **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
*   **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    *   **Administration:** Delivered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
    *   **Acute Management:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the next 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    *   **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are recommended at 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered if clinically required.
*   **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    *   **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    *   **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    *   **Dose Adjustment:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels <40 to 50 µmol/L. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) should be guided by trough levels and may be implemented as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### Administration Instructions
*   Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
*   **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
*   Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels before starting therapy.
*   Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended.
*   Monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine levels: 35 to 60 µmol/L).

## 4. Contraindications
*   None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have occurred, often within minutes of administration. These are reported primarily in ESRD patients. Treatment must be discontinued immediately upon signs of a severe reaction.
*   **Seizures:** Levocarnitine (both oral and IV) has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity of seizures has been reported.

### Specific Clinical Precautions
*   **Oral Formulation Restriction in Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted renally [US].
*   **Monitoring:** Continuous clinical assessment and monitoring of plasma carnitine levels are essential to guide dosing.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions in ESRD patients (occurring at ≥5% frequency) are often confounded by the underlying pathology.

| Body System | Adverse Event (ESRD Population) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Injection site reaction (33-38%), pain (30-35%), headache (3-37%), flu syndrome (25-29%), infection (15-24%), asthenia (8-12%), chest pain (6-15%), abdominal pain (5-21%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (18-21%), hypotension (3-19%), tachycardia (5-9%), arrhythmia (2-3%), palpitations (3-8%) |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Diarrhea (9-35%), vomiting (9-21%), nausea (5-12%), dyspepsia (5-9%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (15-27%), cough increase (10-18%), dyspnea (3-11%), rhinitis (6-11%) |
| **Neurological** | Dizziness (10-18%), paresthesia (3-12%), insomnia (3-6%) |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia (6-15%), hypervolemia (3-12%) |

*   **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and seizures [US].
*   **Less Frequent Effects:** Body odor, gastritis, and taste perversion.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when co-administered with levocarnitine.
*   **Management:** Monitor INR levels in patients on warfarin therapy following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified in available sources]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. However, no adequate, well-controlled studies exist in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
*   **Breastfeeding:** Not specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother. Consider discontinuing nursing or the drug.
*   **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests were negative; however, long-term carcinogenicity studies have not been performed.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders apply to paediatric patients [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the source document, though clinical trials included ESRD patients on dialysis, a population that often includes elderly individuals.
*   **Renal Impairment:** Dosage adjustments for IV levocarnitine in ESRD are based on pre-dialysis trough concentrations. High-dose **oral** levocarnitine is cautioned against in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours.
    *   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    *   **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours.
*   **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, a dose of 20 mg/kg results in a significant increase in plasma carnitine concentrations, with trough levels reaching 189–243 µmol/L after 12–24 weeks of therapy.

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Bioavailability Study:** In healthy volunteers, levocarnitine tablets and oral solution were bioequivalent, with an absolute bioavailability of approximately 15% after correcting for endogenous carnitine.
*   **ESRD Studies:** Double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in dialysis patients demonstrated that doses of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg significantly increased trough levocarnitine concentrations compared to baseline. However, a definitive link between increased carnitine levels and improved clinical outcomes in ESRD has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
*   **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
*   **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of a vial, as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].
