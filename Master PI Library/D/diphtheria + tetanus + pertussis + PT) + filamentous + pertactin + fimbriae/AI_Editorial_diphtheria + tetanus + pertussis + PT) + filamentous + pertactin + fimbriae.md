# Editorial Product Information: diphtheria + tetanus + pertussis + PT) + filamentous + pertactin + fimbriae

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **diphtheria + tetanus + pertussis + PT) + filamentous + pertactin + fimbriae**
- Chemical class: Pertussis antigens combined with diphtheria and tetanus toxoids (purified toxoids/antigens adsorbed to aluminium salt)
- ATC code: J07AJ52
- Route(s) of administration: Intramuscular injection
- DO NOT include brand or trade names, dosage form physical descriptions, or PBS/schedule listings.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Active immunisation against tetanus, diphtheria and pertussis as a booster in persons aged 10 years and older following primary immunisation.
- May be administered during pregnancy for the prevention of pertussis in young infants via transplacental antibody transfer from the pregnant woman to the fetus.
- Indicated for use in elderly persons.
- Paediatric use: indicated for use in children aged 10 years and over.
- Not approved for use as primary immunisation (i.e., not for primary series unless otherwise specified by national recommendations).
- If any jurisdictional differences in indications exist, they are not specified in the available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult and adolescent)
- Recommended dose: 0.5 mL administered intramuscularly.
- Preferred injection site: deltoid muscle.
- Do not inject into the gluteal area.
- Use separate syringes and separate injection sites (preferably separate limbs) when administering concomitant injectable vaccines.

### Dosing by indication / specific situations
- Booster dosing: Give booster doses according to official national immunisation recommendations.
- Unknown or incomplete prior vaccination (adolescents/adults): One dose of this dTpa vaccine should be given as part of a vaccination series against diphtheria, tetanus and pertussis; two additional doses of a diphtheria- and tetanus-containing vaccine should also be administered according to national schedules.
- Repeat vaccination: Can be used for repeat boosting after a previous dTpa or dTpa–IPV dose at intervals of approximately 5 to 10 years, taking into account national recommendations.
- Pregnancy: If administered to pregnant women, follow official national recommendations for timing of pertussis vaccination in pregnancy.

### Administration instructions
- Inspect the product visually for particulate matter and/or discolouration prior to administration; do not use if extraneous particulate matter or discolouration is present.
- The normal appearance is a uniform, cloudy, white suspension which may sediment or form flaky aggregates during storage. Shake the pre-filled syringe well to uniformly distribute the suspension before administering; if aggregates are present, shake until a uniform suspension is obtained.
- Ensure the needle is not in a blood vessel (avoid intravascular injection).
- Product is for single use in one patient on one occasion only. Discard any residue.

## 5. Contraindications
List of contraindications derived from available sources:
- History of a severe allergic reaction (e.g., anaphylaxis) after previous administration of this vaccine or any vaccine containing the same components or constituents.
- Known severe allergic reaction to any component of the vaccine (see composition in Section 11).
- Individuals who experienced an encephalopathy of unknown origin within 7 days of prior immunisation with a pertussis-containing vaccine.
- Individuals who have experienced other neurological complications following prior immunisation with any of the antigens contained in this vaccine.
- Vaccination should generally be postponed in cases of moderate or severe febrile and/or acute disease (low-grade fever is not a contraindication).
- Not specified as contraindications but noted as precautions: history of Guillain–Barré syndrome within 6 weeks of receipt of a prior vaccine containing tetanus toxoid requires careful benefit–risk consideration before administration; progressive or unstable neurological disorders, uncontrolled epilepsy or progressive encephalopathy should defer vaccination until condition stabilises and benefit clearly outweighs risk.
- Latex sensitivity: the soft tip caps of some prefilled syringes contain a natural rubber latex derivative and may cause allergic reactions in latex-sensitive individuals (precaution rather than absolute contraindication).

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Hypersensitivity: Formaldehyde and glutaraldehyde have been used in manufacturing and trace residues may be present; hypersensitivity reactions are possible.
- Anaphylaxis: Appropriate medical treatment and supervision must be readily available in case of rare anaphylactic reactions. Adrenaline (epinephrine) injection (1:1,000) must be immediately available.
- Administration route related: Do not administer intravascularly; ensure correct intramuscular placement.
- Bleeding disorders/thrombocytopenia: Use with caution in individuals with bleeding disorders because bleeding may occur following intramuscular injection.
- Syncope: Fainting can occur before, during, or after injection; have procedures to prevent injury and manage syncopal reactions.
- Latex: Some prefilled syringe components contain natural rubber latex derivatives; assess risk for latex-allergic individuals.
- Neurological disorders: Defer vaccination in progressive or unstable neurological conditions, uncontrolled epilepsy, or progressive encephalopathy until stable and benefit outweighs risk. Consider risks if prior Guillain–Barré syndrome occurred within 6 weeks of prior tetanus-toxoid-containing vaccine.
- Immunocompromised individuals: May have a reduced immune response; consider delaying vaccination until after immunosuppressive treatment if possible. Nonetheless, vaccination is recommended in chronic immunodeficiency (e.g., HIV) though response may be limited.
- Protection: Immunisation may not protect 100% of recipients.
- Use in primary immunisation: This product has not been studied nor is it indicated for primary immunisation; a booster response is only expected in previously primed individuals.
- Use in pregnancy: Administer according to official national recommendations; see Section 9 for pregnancy data and considerations.

### Monitoring requirements
- Observe for immediate hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis following vaccination; facilities for emergency treatment should be available.
- Monitor for syncopal episodes post-vaccination and manage fall/injury risk.
- Monitor injection site for local reactions; large local reactions and extensive limb swelling may occur and usually resolve spontaneously within days.
- For immunocompromised patients, consider follow-up to assess immune response where clinically indicated.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Separate syringes and injection sites must be used when administering concomitant injectable vaccines.
- Concomitant administration with certain vaccines may alter antibody responses to some pertussis antigens (see Section 8).

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Concomitant vaccination:
  - Can be administered concomitantly with influenza vaccine and hepatitis B vaccine; use separate limbs for injection sites.
  - Other live or inactivated parenteral vaccines may be administered simultaneously as appropriate for age and prior vaccination status; separate injection sites and separate syringes must be used.
  - In a clinical trial in adolescents (11–17 years), concomitant administration with a meningococcal conjugate vaccine (Menactra in that trial) produced non-inferior antibody responses to pertussis (except for PRN and FIM), tetanus and diphtheria compared with separate administration. Geometric mean concentrations to PRN and FIM were lower with concomitant administration; clinical significance of these lower pertussis antibody responses is unknown given robust responses overall and absence of accepted serologic correlates for pertussis protection.
- Immunosuppressive treatments:
  - Immunosuppressive therapy may interfere with development of the expected immune response to this vaccine; consider timing of vaccination relative to immunosuppressive therapy when possible.
- Pharmacokinetic interactions:
  - Not specified in available sources.
- Pharmacodynamic interactions:
  - See concomitant vaccination notes above.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Pregnancy category (jurisdictional label in available source):
  - [Australian] Category A (drugs which have been taken by a large number of pregnant women and women of childbearing age without any proven increase in the frequency of malformations or other harmful effects on the fetus having been observed).
- Human data summary:
  - Multiple studies including randomized controlled trials and large observational cohorts have been reported primarily for administration during the second and third trimesters.
  - Safety: No increase in adverse outcomes for pregnant women or their newborns has been demonstrated; adverse events in pregnant women were generally mild or moderate and similar to those seen in non-pregnant women of childbearing age.
  - Some observational studies reported a slight increase in chorioamnionitis after maternal dTpa vaccination; this has not been associated with adverse maternal or neonatal outcomes in the studies cited.
- Immunogenicity and effectiveness:
  - Maternal pertussis antibody responses are robust and antibodies are transferred to newborns via the placenta; antibodies persist in infants for approximately 2–4 months.
  - Maternal vaccination has been reported to be >90% effective in preventing pertussis disease and hospitalisation in infants younger than 3 months in retrospective and observational studies (examples: UK and US observational estimates in infants up to 2–3 months).
  - Maternal antibodies can blunt infants' antibody responses to their own pertussis vaccinations later in infancy; currently there is no evidence that this blunting results in clinically relevant reductions in protection.
- Decision to vaccinate pregnant women should follow official national recommendations.

### Breastfeeding
- It is not known whether active substances are excreted in human breast milk; vaccine antigen-specific antibodies have been shown to transfer to offspring in animal studies.
- The effect on breast-fed infants has not been systematically studied.
- As this is an inactivated vaccine, any risk to mother or infant is considered unlikely; the risks and benefits of vaccination should be assessed before immunising a nursing woman.

### Fertility
- Effects on fertility have not been evaluated in the available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Induces active immunity to diphtheria and tetanus via detoxified, purified toxoids and to Bordetella pertussis via purified acellular pertussis antigens (pertussis toxoid [PT], filamentous haemagglutinin [FHA], pertactin [PRN], and fimbriae types 2 and 3 [FIM]) adsorbed to an aluminium salt adjuvant, leading to antibody production against these antigens.
- The precise immune mechanism conferring protection against B. pertussis disease is not completely understood.

### Composition (antigen content per 0.5 mL dose)
- Diphtheria toxoid: ≥ 2 IU (2 Lf)
- Tetanus toxoid: ≥ 20 IU (5 Lf)
- Pertussis toxoid (PT): 2.5 micrograms
- Filamentous haemagglutinin (FHA): 5 micrograms
- Pertactin (PRN): 3 micrograms
- Fimbriae types 2 and 3 (FIM): 5 micrograms
- Adsorbed on aluminium phosphate: 1.5 mg (0.33 mg aluminium)
- The vaccine is prepared from purified, formaldehyde‑detoxified and adsorbed diphtheria and tetanus toxoids; purified, glutaraldehyde‑detoxified and adsorbed pertussis toxin; purified and adsorbed filamentous haemagglutinin; purified and adsorbed pertactin; and co‑purified and adsorbed fimbriae types 2 and 3.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption, distribution, metabolism, and elimination parameters are not specified in the available sources for this vaccine product.

### Pharmacodynamics / Immunogenicity
- Single-dose administration elicits robust humoral responses to tetanus, diphtheria and pertussis antigens in adolescents and adults; seroprotection rates for tetanus and diphtheria antitoxin (thresholds defined in clinical studies) were high one month post-vaccination.
- Long-term immunogenicity data up to 10 years indicate maintenance of protective tetanus and diphtheria antitoxin levels in most vaccinees; pertussis antibody levels decline toward pre-vaccination levels over time but remain detectable and higher than baseline in many subjects at 10 years.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Overview of pivotal trials and key findings (as reported in available sources):
  - Clinical trial population: A total of 962 individuals (324 adolescents and 638 adults) received a single 0.5 mL dose in three principal clinical trials.
  - Comparative trials:
    - Trials compared a single booster dose of this dTpa product with separate administration of adult Td (tetanus-diphtheria) plus a monovalent acellular pertussis vaccine, and also evaluated concomitant administration with hepatitis B vaccine.
    - Safety and immunogenicity profiles were generally comparable to those observed with Td plus separate acellular pertussis booster.
  - Immunogenicity outcomes:
    - One month after vaccination, near‑universal seroprotection against tetanus and diphtheria antitoxin thresholds was observed in adolescents and adults in the clinical studies (e.g., ≥0.10 IU/mL thresholds reported as approximately 100% for tetanus and high proportions for diphtheria depending on subgroup).
    - Geometric mean concentrations for pertussis antigens (PT, FHA, PRN, FIM) rose substantially post-vaccination; absolute GMCs varied by study and subgroup.
  - Repeat vaccination studies:
    - Repeat administration at approximately 5 years and at 10 years after prior dTpa/dTpa‑containing vaccination was evaluated in open-label studies; solicited injection-site and systemic reactions occurred with frequencies similar to those after primary booster administration (examples: pain reported in ~87% after repeat doses; myalgia, headache, malaise reported commonly).
  - Concomitant vaccine interactions:
    - Concomitant administration with hepatitis B vaccine produced comparable safety and immunogenicity profiles.
    - Concomitant administration with a meningococcal conjugate vaccine produced non-inferior responses overall; lower GMCs for PRN and FIM were observed with concomitant administration but clinical significance is unknown given robust overall responses and lack of established serologic correlates for pertussis protection.
  - Efficacy-related evidence:
    - Although direct efficacy trials of this adult/adolescent booster vaccine against clinical pertussis in target populations were not reported as primary endpoints in available sources, infant efficacy data for an acellular pertussis infant formulation used in other studies demonstrated high protective efficacy; despite containing lower amounts of pertussis toxoid, the adult/adolescent booster induced antibody responses that were at least as robust as those observed in historic efficacy trials.
    - Maternal vaccination studies and observational analyses reported >90% effectiveness in preventing pertussis disease and hospitalisation in infants younger than 3 months when mothers were vaccinated during pregnancy (examples: UK and US observational estimates with VE ~91–93% in infants up to 2–3 months).
- Trial design notes:
  - Trials included randomized controlled and open-label designs; sample sizes and specific subgroup data varied across trials (adolescent and adult cohorts reported separately).
  - Safety endpoints included solicited local and systemic reactions during 0–7 and 0–14 day follow-up windows and longer-term adverse event monitoring; immunogenicity endpoints typically measured antibody concentrations one month post-vaccination and at extended follow-up (including up to 10 years in some cohorts).
