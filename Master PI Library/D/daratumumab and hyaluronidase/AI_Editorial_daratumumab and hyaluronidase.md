# Editorial Product Information: Daratumumab and Hyaluronidase

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial is a harmonised synthesis of Product Information (PI) provided by regulatory authorities. The primary contributing jurisdiction for this document is: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data, dosing schedules, and safety information into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing in any specific region. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority for the most current approved labeling.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Daratumumab (CD38-directed cytolytic antibody) and hyaluronidase-fihj (human recombinant endoglycosidase).
- **Brand Name:** **DARZALEX FASPRO** [US].
- **ATC Code:** L01FC01 (Daratumumab and hyaluronidase).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injection: 1,800 mg daratumumab and 30,000 units hyaluronidase per 15 mL (120 mg and 2,000 units/mL) [US].
- **Physical Characteristics:** Sterile, preservative-free, colorless to yellow, and clear to opalescent solution [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous injection only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Janssen Biotech, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Multiple Myeloma (MM)**
- In combination with bortezomib, lenalidomide, and dexamethasone (VRd) for induction and consolidation in newly diagnosed patients eligible for autologous stem cell transplant (ASCT) [US].
- In combination with bortezomib, lenalidomide, and dexamethasone (VRd) in newly diagnosed patients ineligible for ASCT [US].
- In combination with bortezomib, melphalan, and prednisone (VMP) in newly diagnosed patients ineligible for ASCT [US].
- In combination with lenalidomide and dexamethasone (Rd) in newly diagnosed patients ineligible for ASCT, and in patients with relapsed or refractory MM who have received at least one prior therapy [US].
- In combination with bortezomib, thalidomide, and dexamethasone (VTd) in newly diagnosed patients eligible for ASCT [US].
- In combination with bortezomib and dexamethasone (Vd) in patients who have received at least one prior therapy [US].
- In combination with pomalidomide and dexamethasone (Pd) in patients who have received at least one prior line of therapy including lenalidomide and a proteasome inhibitor (PI) [US].
- In combination with carfilzomib and dexamethasone (Kd) in patients with relapsed or refractory MM who have received one to three prior lines of therapy [US].
- As monotherapy in patients who have received at least three prior lines of therapy including a PI and an immunomodulatory agent, or who are double-refractory to a PI and an immunomodulatory agent [US].

**High-Risk Smoldering Multiple Myeloma**
- As monotherapy for the treatment of adult patients with high-risk smoldering multiple myeloma [US].

**Light Chain (AL) Amyloidosis**
- In combination with bortezomib, cyclophosphamide, and dexamethasone (VCd) in newly diagnosed patients [US].
- *Limitations of Use:* Not recommended for patients with NYHA Class IIIB or Class IV cardiac disease, or Mayo Stage IIIB, outside of controlled clinical trials [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 General Dosing (Adult)
The fixed recommended dose is **1,800 mg daratumumab and 30,000 units hyaluronidase** administered subcutaneously over approximately 3 to 5 minutes [US].

### 3.2 Dosing Schedules
*Refer to the full PI for detailed cycle-based schedules, as duration of weekly, bi-weekly, and monthly dosing varies by indication and combination partner.*
- **Monotherapy & Rd/Pd/Kd combinations:** Weekly for Weeks 1-8; every two weeks for Weeks 9-24; every four weeks from Week 25 onwards [US].
- **VMP combination:** Weekly for Weeks 1-6; every three weeks for Weeks 7-54; every four weeks from Week 55 onwards [US].
- **VTd/VRd (Transplant eligible):** Weekly for Weeks 1-8; every two weeks for Weeks 9-16 (Induction). Post-ASCT, every two weeks for Weeks 1-8 (Consolidation) [US].
- **AL Amyloidosis:** Weekly for Weeks 1-8; every two weeks for Weeks 9-24; every four weeks from Week 25 until disease progression or maximum of 2 years [US].

### 3.3 Administration Instructions
- **Site:** Subcutaneous tissue of the abdomen (approximately 3 inches from the navel). Do not inject into other sites [US].
- **Technique:** Rotate injection sites. Do not inject into areas where skin is red, bruised, tender, hard, or scarred [US].
- **Rate:** 3 to 5 minutes. Pause or slow the rate if the patient experiences pain [US].

### 3.4 Concomitant Medications
- **Pre-medication (1–3 hours prior):** Administer acetaminophen (650–1000 mg), diphenhydramine (25–50 mg), and a corticosteroid (methylprednisolone 100 mg for monotherapy; dexamethasone 20 mg for combinations) [US].
- **Post-medication:** Provide oral methylprednisolone (20 mg) for two days starting the day after injection [US].
- **Antiviral Prophylaxis:** Initiate within 1 week of starting treatment and continue for 3 months post-treatment to prevent Herpes Zoster reactivation [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Patients with a history of severe hypersensitivity to daratumumab, hyaluronidase, or any excipient in the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity and Administration-Related Reactions (ARRs):** Both systemic and local reactions occur. Systemic ARRs occurred in 7% of patients (mostly with the first injection). Severe reactions include hypoxia, dyspnea, hypertension, and ocular adverse reactions (e.g., acute myopia, angle-closure glaucoma). Permanently discontinue for life-threatening reactions [US].
- **Cardiac Toxicity (AL Amyloidosis):** Serious cardiac disorders occurred in 16% and fatal cardiac disorders in 10% of patients with AL amyloidosis. Monitor patients with cardiac involvement closely [US].
- **Infections:** Serious and fatal infections (e.g., pneumonia, sepsis) have been reported. Monitor for signs of infection and treat promptly [US].
- **Neutropenia and Thrombocytopenia:** Monitor complete blood counts periodically. Consider withholding the drug to allow count recovery; no dose reductions are recommended [US].
- **Interference with Serological Testing:** Daratumumab binds to CD38 on red blood cells, resulting in a **positive indirect Antiglobulin Test (Indirect Coombs test)**. This may persist for up to 6 months. Perform type and screen prior to starting therapy and inform blood banks [US].
- **Interference with Response Determination:** Daratumumab may be detected on serum protein electrophoresis (SPE) and immunofixation (IFE) assays, potentially leading to false-positive results in patients with IgG kappa myeloma protein [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
**Very Common (>10%) [US]:**
- **Infections:** Upper respiratory tract infection (URTI), pneumonia, COVID-19, urinary tract infection, bronchitis.
- **General:** Fatigue, pyrexia, peripheral edema, injection site reactions.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea, constipation, nausea, abdominal pain, vomiting.
- **Neurological:** Peripheral neuropathy (sensory and motor), dizziness, headache, insomnia.
- **Musculoskeletal:** Musculoskeletal pain, muscle spasms, back pain.
- **Respiratory:** Cough, dyspnea.
- **Vascular:** Hypertension.

**Hematological Laboratory Abnormalities (≥40%) [US]:**
- Decreased leukocytes, decreased lymphocytes, decreased neutrophils, decreased platelets, and decreased hemoglobin.

**Serious Adverse Effects:**
- Pneumonia, cardiac failure (in AL amyloidosis), sepsis, and fatal administration reactions [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No formal drug-drug interaction studies have been conducted [US].
- **Laboratory Interference:** 
    - **Indirect Coombs Test:** Daratumumab interferes with blood compatibility testing. Mitigation includes treating reagent RBCs with dithiothreitol (DTT) [US].
    - **M-Protein Assessment:** Interferes with SPE and IFE assays used to monitor endogenous M-protein [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: No lettered category; however, PI states "Can cause fetal harm"]. Based on MoA, daratumumab may cause depletion of fetal CD38-positive immune cells and decreased bone density. Defer live vaccines in neonates exposed in utero until hematology evaluation is complete [US].
- **Contraception:** Females of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for **3 months** after the final dose [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on presence in human milk. Maternal IgG is known to be present in milk, but usually does not enter neonatal circulation in substantial amounts. Advise against breastfeeding due to potential serious reactions in the infant [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in effectiveness observed. However, patients ≥65 years reported higher frequencies of certain adverse reactions, including pneumonia, diarrhea, and fatigue [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No clinically meaningful effect on pharmacokinetics in patients with mild to severe renal impairment [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No clinically meaningful effect in patients with mild hepatic impairment. Data are unavailable for moderate to severe impairment [US].
- **Body Weight:** Lower body weight (≤50 kg) is associated with higher daratumumab exposure; higher rates of Grade 3-4 neutropenia were observed in these patients [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Daratumumab is an IgG1κ monoclonal antibody that binds CD38 on hematopoietic cells (including myeloma and AL amyloidosis cells). It induces tumor cell lysis via complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC), antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC), antibody-dependent cellular phagocytosis (ADCP), and apoptosis. Hyaluronidase increases subcutaneous permeability by depolymerizing hyaluronan in the extracellular matrix [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Bioavailability:** 69% [US].
    - **Peak Concentration (Tmax):** ~3 days in MM; ~4 days in AL amyloidosis [US].
    - **Elimination:** Parallel linear and nonlinear saturable target-mediated clearance. Estimated linear half-life is 20 days (MM) to 28 days (AL amyloidosis) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **PERSEUS:** Demonstrated a 60% reduction in risk of progression or death when added to VRd induction/consolidation in transplant-eligible MM [US].
- **CEPHEUS:** Demonstrated improved MRD negativity rates and PFS in transplant-ineligible MM when added to VRd [US].
- **COLUMBA:** Confirmed non-inferiority of subcutaneous daratumumab/hyaluronidase vs. intravenous daratumumab monotherapy regarding overall response rate (ORR) and trough concentrations [US].
- **ANDROMEDA:** In AL amyloidosis, adding the drug to VCd resulted in a significantly higher hematologic complete response rate (42% vs. 13%) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store in a refrigerator at **2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F)** [US].
- **Light:** Store in the original carton to protect from light [US].
- **Handling:** Do not freeze or shake [US].
- **Stability:** Once syringe is prepared, it may be stored at room temperature for up to 12 hours or refrigerated for up to 24 hours [US].
