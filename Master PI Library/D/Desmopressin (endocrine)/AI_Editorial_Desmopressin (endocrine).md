# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine (Intravenous)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory and clinical data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulations and the current approved PI for their specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn metabolic errors [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing hemodialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - Administration: Slow intravenous bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion.
    - Acute Crisis: A loading dose is often administered, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours).
    - Maintenance: Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised based on clinical requirement [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - Administration: Slow intravenous bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - Titration: Dosage adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported. These may occur within minutes of administration, particularly in ESRD patients. Treatment should be discontinued immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine may increase the frequency or severity of seizures in patients with or without a pre-existing seizure disorder [US].
- **Monitoring:** 
    - **Plasma Levels:** Trough (pre-dialysis) concentrations should be monitored weekly or monthly. Target free carnitine levels are generally between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
    - **Biometrics:** Monitoring should include blood chemistries and vital signs [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects listed reflect data from clinical trials in ESRD patients [US].

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, back pain, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, dizziness, pharyngitis. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Gastritis, body odor (transient). |
| **Serious Adverse Effects** | Anaphylaxis, seizures, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm [US]. |

*Note: In ESRD populations, many adverse events are confounded by the underlying pathology.*

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalised Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Frequent monitoring of INR is recommended for patients on anticoagulant therapy after starting levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned under current labeling, but animal studies (rats/rabbits) at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** 
    - Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied.
    - **Clinical Consideration:** Studies in dairy cows suggest increased carnitine concentration in milk following administration. The benefits of maternal therapy should be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no impairment of fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg (see Section 3). Safety and efficacy have been established for the indicated uses [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources; generally follow standard adult dosing with caution for co-morbidities.
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - Intravenous levocarnitine is specifically indicated for ESRD.
    - **Precaution:** Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This provides the necessary substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production in tissues (excluding the brain), particularly skeletal and cardiac muscle [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn metabolic errors, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly cleared [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Studies:** In healthy volunteers, intravenous bolus (20 mg/kg) showed a distribution half-life of 0.585 hours.
- **ESRD Trials:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg post-dialysis three times weekly increased mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified for other jurisdictions in available sources.
