## Close Paraphrases
- **Mechanism of Action:** The editorial describes the "formation of the C3 convertase (C3bBb)" and how background C5 inhibitors maintain control over "terminal pathway-mediated intravascular hemolysis." This mimics the structure of TGA Section 5.1 almost exactly.
- **Distribution/Elimination:** The data points (91.5% to 94.3% protein binding, 7.9-hour half-life, 69% fecal excretion) are presented in a sequence that mirrors the "Pharmacokinetics" sections of both source documents with only slight grammatical variations.

## Originality Strengths
- **Unified Harmonization:** The editorial successfully identifies and labels specific requirements belonging to different agencies (e.g., the TGA-specific 6-day taper vs. the FDA-specific REMS program). 
- **Integrated Storage:** Section 11 merges the Fahrenheit (FDA) and Celsius (TGA) requirements into a single cohesive instruction.
- **Synthesis of Adverse Effects:** Section 6 combines lists from the TGA Clinical Trial tables with qualitative observations from the open-label phases, creating a more comprehensive summary than either source alone.

## Differences from Source PIs
- **Structure:** The editorial uses a simplified, header-based structure rather than the rigorous numbered sections of the regulatory documents.
- **Tone:** The editorial uses a more "encyclopedic" tone, omitting the administrative instructions (e.g., "how to report adverse reactions" links) found in the originals.
- **Simplification:** It omits detailed statistical results from Study ALXN2040-PNH-301 (LS mean differences, p-values), focusing only on clinical relevance.

## Recommendation
**FAIL.** 

The editorial requires revision. While it performs the task of "unifying" the two documents well, it relies too heavily on the exact phrasing of the source material. Sections such as "Approved Indications," "Contraindications," and "Mechanism of Action" are largely comprised of regulatory sentences with only 1-2 words changed. 

**Required Changes:**
1.  **Rewrite Indications:** Express the therapeutic use of the drug without using the exact regulatory string "indicated as an add-on to [drug names] for the treatment of the signs or symptoms of...".
2.  **Paraphrase Warnings:** The Hepatic and Serious Infection warnings use standard regulatory boilerplates. These should be rewritten to convey the same risk in a new voice.
3.  **Synthesize PK/MOA:** Instead of listing the pathways exactly as the PI does, describe the biological process of Factor D inhibition and metabolism using original sentence structures.
4.  **Formatting:** Convert the Tapering and Dosage instructions into a prose summary or an original table format to avoid copying the PI's bullet points verbatim.
