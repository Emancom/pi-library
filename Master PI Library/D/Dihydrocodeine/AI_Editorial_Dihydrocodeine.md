# Editorial Product Information: Dihydrocodeine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information documents contributed to this editorial: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of dihydrocodeine for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises available regulatory data into a single clinical reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource only. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Dihydrocodeine tartrate.
- **Chemical Class:** Semi-synthetic opioid.
- **Brand Name(s):** Rikodeine [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** R05DA08.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Oral Liquid:** 19 mg/10 mL dihydrocodeine tartrate, often formulated with sorbitol (4.4 g/10 mL) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** iNova Pharmaceuticals (Australia) Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Antitussive:** For the symptomatic relief of stubborn, unproductive (dry) cough [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Children ≥12 years)
- **Standard Dose:** 5 mL to 10 mL administered every 4 to 6 hours as required [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing (Children 6–11 years)
- **Standard Dose:** 2.5 mL to 5 mL administered every 4 to 6 hours as required [Australian].
- **Note:** Use in this age group should only occur under the advice of a doctor, pharmacist, or nurse practitioner [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Do not exceed the recommended frequency or dose.
- **Elderly:** Initial dosages should be reduced due to potential pharmacokinetic variability and increased sensitivity to opioid effects [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to dihydrocodeine, other opioids, or any excipients (e.g., sorbitol, hydroxybenzoates) [Australian].
- **Respiratory Status:** Severe or acute respiratory disease, and existing respiratory depression [Australian].
- **Age:** Contraindicated in children under 6 years of age [Australian].
- **General Opioid Contraindications:** Acute alcoholism, convulsive disorders, head injuries, conditions with raised intracranial pressure, paralytic ileus, or comatose states [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [Australian]
- **Hazardous and Harmful Use:** Dihydrocodeine poses risks of misuse, addiction, and overdose which can be fatal. Assess patient risk factors prior to initiation.
- **Life-threatening Respiratory Depression:** Serious or fatal respiratory depression may occur. Monitoring is essential, particularly during treatment initiation or following dose increases.
- **Concomitant Use with CNS Depressants:** Combined use with benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, or alcohol may result in profound sedation, coma, and death. Limit dosages and duration to the minimum required.

### General Precautions
- **Tolerance and Dependence:** Neuroadaptation may occur with repeated use, leading to physical dependence. Abrupt cessation in dependent patients may precipitate withdrawal (e.g., dysphoria, lacrimation, rhinorrhoea, sweating, myalgias). Tapering should be individualised (e.g., 10–25% reduction every 2–4 weeks) [Australian].
- **Asthma and Respiratory Reserve:** Use with caution in patients with asthma, emphysema, or chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) due to potential histamine release and respiratory depressive effects [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Use with caution in inflammatory or obstructive bowel disorders, biliary tract disorders, or pancreatitis. Formulations containing sorbitol may exert a laxative effect [Australian].
- **Endocrine/Other:** Dose reductions are advised in hypothyroidism and adrenocortical insufficiency. Use with caution in prostatic hyperplasia, hypotension, shock, or myasthenia gravis [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Reactions
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, and constipation (though constipation is reported as less common than with codeine) [Australian].
- **Neurological:** Drowsiness, dizziness, headache, and vertigo [Australian].
- **Respiratory:** Respiratory depression [Australian].

### Less Common/Infrequent Reactions
- **CNS:** Hallucinations, visual disturbances, and euphoria [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular:** Atrial fibrillation, syncope, and circulatory failure [Australian].
- **Dermatological:** Pruritus, rash, and urticaria [Australian].
- **Genitourinary:** Urinary retention [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP2D6 Inhibitors (e.g., Quinidine):** Concurrent use may reduce or eliminate the analgesic efficacy of dihydrocodeine by preventing its conversion to the active metabolite, dihydromorphine [Australian].
- **CYP2D6 Phenotype:** Approximately 6–10% of Caucasians are "poor metabolisers" and may experience reduced benefit [Australian].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **CNS Depressants:** Alcohol, benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, tricyclic antidepressants, and antipsychotics increase the risk of respiratory depression and sedation [Australian].
- **MAO Inhibitors:** May lead to anxiety, confusion, and severe respiratory depression [Australian].
- **Anticholinergics:** Increased risk of severe constipation and urinary retention [Australian].
- **Antihypertensives:** Potential for additive hypotensive effects [Australian].
- **Opioid Antagonists (e.g., Naloxone):** Will precipitate withdrawal in dependent patients [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category A [Australian]:** Extensive use in pregnant women has not shown an increase in malformations or direct foetal harm. 
    - **Warning:** Prolonged use late in pregnancy risks neonatal respiratory depression and withdrawal symptoms [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety information is limited. Dihydrocodeine should not be used during lactation unless the clinical benefit to the mother outweighs the potential risks to the infant [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Data not specified in available sources [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Contraindicated under 6 years. Use with caution/professional advice in ages 6–11 [Australian].
- **Elderly:** Increased risk of accumulation and sensitivity. Initial doses should be lower [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Caution is advised in severe impairment as dose accumulation may occur; peak plasma concentrations may be delayed and half-life prolonged [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Dihydrocodeine dosage should be reduced in patients with chronic hepatic disease [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
- Dihydrocodeine is an opioid that acts directly on receptors in the cough centre of the medulla to suppress the cough reflex [Australian].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; peak serum concentrations are reached in 1.6–1.8 hours. Oral bioavailability is approximately 21% due to substantial first-pass metabolism [Australian].
- **Metabolism:** Primarily via the liver. Major pathways include O-demethylation (via CYP2D6) to dihydromorphine and N-demethylation (via CYP3A4) to nordihydrocodeine [Australian].
- **Elimination:** Excreted mainly in the urine as parent drug or conjugates. Half-life is approximately 3.3 to 4.5 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Data regarding pivotal clinical trials for the antitussive indication were not specified in the available source document [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Packaging:** Typically supplied in amber PET bottles (e.g., 100 mL or 200 mL) [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Schedule 3 (Pharmacist Only Medicine) [Australian].
