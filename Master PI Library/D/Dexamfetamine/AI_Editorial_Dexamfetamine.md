# Editorial Product Information: Dexamfetamine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- Information from other major regulatory authorities (e.g., US FDA, European EMA) was not provided in the source materials for this specific summary; therefore, jurisdictional comparisons are limited to the available Australian data.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised clinical overview for healthcare professionals. It is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official, legally approved PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Dexamfetamine sulfate (the dextro isomer of d,l-amfetamine sulfate).
- **Chemical Class:** Non-catecholamine, sympathomimetic amine; CNS stimulant.
- **Brand Names:** Aspen Dexamfetamine [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - 5 mg round, white, scored tablets (marked 'D5') [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aspen Pharmacare Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Narcolepsy:** Treatment of narcolepsy in adults and children [Australian].
- **Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD):** Specifically indicated for hyperkinetic behaviour disorders in children [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Principles
Therapy should commence at the lowest possible dose and be titrated slowly to the minimum effective dose for the individual. Doses should be timed to avoid insomnia; late evening administration is discouraged.

### Narcolepsy
- **Adults:** 5 mg to 60 mg daily in divided doses.
- **Children (6–12 years):** Start with 5 mg daily. Increase in 5 mg weekly increments until optimal response.
- **Patients 12 years and older:** Start with 10 mg daily. Increase in 10 mg weekly increments until optimal response.

### ADHD (Hyperkinetic Activity Behaviour Disorders)
- **Children under 3 years:** Not recommended.
- **Children 3 years and older:** Start with 2.5 mg daily (on awakening). Increase in 2.5 mg weekly increments until optimal response. 
- **Maximum Dose:** 40 mg daily in two divided doses (second dose typically 4–6 hours after the first).
- **Maintenance:** Therapy should be interrupted occasionally to determine if symptoms recur enough to justify continued treatment.
- **Re-initiation:** If therapy is restarted after a hiatus, it must be re-titrated from the starting dose rather than resuming the previous maintenance dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Cardiovascular:** Symptomatic cardiovascular disease, history of myocardial infarction, cardiac arrhythmia, severe angina pectoris, ischaemic heart disease, moderate to severe hypertension, and phaeochromocytoma.
- **Metabolic/Endocrine:** Hyperthyroidism and glaucoma.
- **Psychiatric:** Anxiety, tension, agitation, severe depression, anorexia nervosa, psychotic symptoms, or suicidal tendency.
- **Neurological:** Motor tics and Tourette syndrome.
- **Substance Use:** History of drug dependence or alcohol abuse.
- **Drug Interactions:** Concurrent use of Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs) or use within 14 days of discontinuing an MAOI (risk of hypertensive crisis).
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to dexamfetamine, sympathomimetic amines, or any excipients (including lactose and gluten in specific brands) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings / Major Safety Alerts
- **Drug Dependence [Australian]:** Dexamfetamine has a high potential for abuse, misuse, and addiction. Chronic abuse can lead to severe psychological dependence and abnormal behaviour. Misuse via non-approved routes (snorting/injection) increases the risk of overdose and death.

### Cardiovascular Risks
- **Sudden Death:** Reported in children/adolescents with structural cardiac abnormalities or serious heart problems. Generally, stimulants should not be used in patients with known cardiomyopathy or rhythm abnormalities.
- **QTc Prolongation:** Caution is advised in patients with congenital long QT syndrome or those taking concurrent QTc-prolonging medications.
- **Blood Pressure:** May increase heart rate and blood pressure; monitor regularly.

### Psychiatric Risks
- **Psychosis/Mania:** May exacerbate symptoms in patients with pre-existing psychotic disorders. Emergent psychotic or manic symptoms (hallucinations, delusional thinking) can occur at usual doses in patients without a prior history.
- **Suicidality/Aggression:** Monitor for emergent suicidal ideation or worsening aggressive behaviour.

### Other Clinical Precautions
- **Growth Suppression:** Long-term use in children may be associated with a temporary slowing in growth rate (height and weight). Monitor growth parameters closely.
- **Seizures:** May lower the convulsive threshold. Discontinue if seizures occur.
- **Peripheral Vasculopathy:** Associated with Raynaud’s phenomenon; monitor for digital changes/ulceration.

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Note: Specific frequency percentages were not detailed in available sources, but general categorisations are provided.*

- **Cardiovascular:** Palpitations, tachycardia, hypertension, QTc prolongation, Raynaud’s phenomenon, and cardiomyopathy (with chronic use).
- **Central Nervous System:** Insomnia, restlessness, dizziness, tremor, headache, dyskinesia, and exacerbation of motor/phonic tics.
- **Psychiatric:** Overstimulation, psychotic episodes (uncommon at recommended doses), mania, and aggression.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Anorexia, weight loss, dry mouth, unpleasant taste, diarrhoea, and constipation.
- **Endocrine/Sexual:** Impotence and changes in libido.
- **Dermatologic/Allergic:** Urticaria and severe dermatoses (with chronic intoxication).
- **Serious Effects:** Sudden cardiac death, stroke, myocardial infarction, and posterior reversible encephalopathy syndrome (PRES) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Gastrointestinal Acidifying Agents (e.g., fruit juices, ascorbic acid):** Lower absorption and blood levels of amfetamines.
- **Urinary Acidifying Agents (e.g., ammonium chloride):** Increase urinary excretion, lowering efficacy.
- **Gastrointestinal/Urinary Alkalising Agents (e.g., sodium bicarbonate, acetazolamide):** Increase absorption and decrease excretion, potentially leading to toxic blood levels.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **MAOIs:** Slow amfetamine metabolism and potentiate noradrenaline release; risk of fatal hypertensive crisis and malignant hyperpyrexia.
- **Tricyclic Antidepressants (TCAs):** May enhance activity and cardiovascular effects of amfetamines.
- **Antihypertensives:** Dexamfetamine may antagonise hypotensive effects.
- **Chlorpromazine/Haloperidol:** Inhibit the central stimulant effects of amfetamines by blocking dopamine/noradrenaline uptake.
- **Lithium Carbonate:** May inhibit the stimulatory effects of amfetamines.
- **Pethidine:** Dexamfetamine may potentiate the analgesic effect.
- **Anticonvulsants:** May delay absorption of phenobarbital and phenytoin, though synergistic anticonvulsant actions have been reported.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category B3.** Dexamfetamine has shown embryotoxic and teratogenic effects in animal models. Use in early pregnancy may be linked to congenital malformations. Use should be avoided.
- **Neonatal Risks:** Infants born to dependent mothers face increased risks of premature delivery, low birth weight, and withdrawal symptoms (agitation, lassitude, dysphoria).
- **Breastfeeding:** Amfetamines are excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended.
- **Fertility:** No data available in humans.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for ADHD in children under 3 years. Long-term effects are not fully established. Growth monitoring is essential.
- **Elderly Use:** Information regarding safety and efficacy in the elderly is not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution due to the drug's reliance on renal excretion. Half-life is significantly extended in alkaline urine.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Dexamfetamine is partially metabolised (30–40%) by the liver. Specific dosing adjustments are not provided in source documents.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Dexamfetamine is a sympathomimetic amine that stimulates alpha and beta-adrenergic receptors. It facilitates the release of catecholamines (dopamine and noradrenaline) and blocks their re-uptake from the synapse. It also inhibits Monoamine Oxidase (MAO).

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed from the GI tract; peak levels reached in approximately 2 hours.
- **Distribution:** Concentrates in the brain, lungs, and kidneys. Volume of distribution is 2–3 L/kg.
- **Metabolism:** 30–40% metabolised by the liver.
- **Elimination:** 60–70% excreted unchanged by the kidneys. Elimination is highly pH-dependent:
    - **Acidic urine (pH ≤5.0):** Half-life is 6–8 hours.
    - **Alkaline urine (pH >7.5):** Half-life is 16–31 hours.
    - **Mean half-life:** 10.25 hours.

- **Summary:** Not specified in available sources. The Australian PI notes that clinical trial data for these properties were not available or assessed at the time of registration.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Security:** Due to high abuse potential, it is advised to store the medication in a secure, preferably locked location.
- **Packaging:** HDPE bottles with child-resistant closures and silica gel desiccant [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Schedule 8 (S8) — Controlled Drug. Subject to special restrictions on availability; prescriptions may require State/Territory health department validation.
