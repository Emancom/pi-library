# Editorial Product Information: Danazol

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on Product Information (PI) source documents from the **United States (FDA)** as provided by Teva Pharmaceuticals USA. Note: While the prompt context referred to Australian documentation, the provided source material is specifically the US FDA-approved labeling for Danazol Capsules, USP.
*   **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, regulatory requirements, and safety information into a single reference.
*   **Prescribing Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing in any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Danazol (a synthetic steroid derived from ethisterone).
*   **Chemical Class:** 17α-Pregna-2,4-dien-20-yno [2,3-d]-isoxazol-17-ol.
*   **Brand Names:** Information not specified in available sources (Generic: **Danazol** Capsules, USP).
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    *   [US]: Oral capsules in 50 mg, 100 mg, and 200 mg strengths.
*   **Route of Administration:** Oral.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** 
    *   [US]: Teva Pharmaceuticals USA, Inc.

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Endometriosis:** Indicated for the treatment of endometriosis that is responsive to hormonal management [US].
*   **Hereditary Angioedema:** Indicated for the prevention of all types of angioedema attacks (cutaneous, abdominal, and laryngeal) in both male and female patients [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
*   **Endometriosis:**
    *   **Moderate to Severe/Infertility Cases:** The recommended starting dose is 800 mg daily, administered in two divided doses. This level is intended to achieve rapid symptomatic relief and amenorrhea.
    *   **Mild Cases:** An initial daily dose of 200 mg to 400 mg in two divided doses is recommended.
    *   **Titration:** Doses should be titrated downward to the minimum amount necessary to maintain amenorrhea.
    *   **Timing:** Therapy must commence during menstruation. If not, pregnancy must be ruled out via appropriate testing before initiation.
    *   **Duration:** Typically 3 to 6 months; may be extended to 9 months if necessary. Treatment can be reinstituted if symptoms recur.
*   **Hereditary Angioedema:**
    *   **Initial Dose:** 200 mg two or three times daily.
    *   **Maintenance:** Following a favourable response, the dose should be reduced by 50% or less at intervals of 1 to 3 months (or longer) based on attack frequency.
    *   **Acute Exacerbation:** If an attack occurs, the daily dose may be increased by up to 200 mg.

### Administration Instructions
*   **Monitoring:** Close clinical monitoring is required during dose adjustment, particularly in patients with a history of airway involvement in angioedema.

## 4. Contraindications
**Danazol** must not be administered to patients with:
*   Undiagnosed abnormal genital bleeding.
*   Markedly impaired hepatic, renal, or cardiac function.
*   Pregnancy [US].
*   Breastfeeding [US].
*   Porphyria (as **danazol** can induce ALA synthetase activity).
*   Androgen-dependent tumours.
*   Active thrombosis or thromboembolic disease, including a history of such events.
*   Known hypersensitivity to **danazol**.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
*   **Pregnancy:** **Danazol** is strictly contraindicated. A sensitive pregnancy test (e.g., beta-subunit) is recommended immediately before starting therapy. Non-hormonal contraception must be used throughout treatment. If pregnancy occurs, the drug must be discontinued immediately due to the risk of androgenic effects on the female fetus (clitoral hypertrophy, labial fusion, vaginal atresia).
*   **Thromboembolism:** Reports include thrombotic and thrombophlebitic events, such as sagittal sinus thrombosis and fatal strokes.
*   **Hepatic Effects:** Long-term use is associated with peliosis hepatis and benign hepatic adenomas, which may remain silent until presenting as life-threatening intraabdominal haemorrhage.
*   **Benign Intracranial Hypertension (Pseudotumor Cerebri):** Early symptoms include papilledema, headache, nausea, and visual disturbances. Discontinue immediately if these occur.
*   **Lipid Alterations:** Marked decreases in HDL and possible increases in LDL have been reported, potentially increasing the risk of atherosclerosis.

### General Precautions
*   **Fluid Retention:** Use with caution in conditions sensitive to fluid shifts, including epilepsy, migraine, cardiac or renal dysfunction, polycythemia, and hypertension.
*   **Androgenic Effects:** Patients must be monitored for signs of virilisation (e.g., voice changes, hirsutism), some of which may be irreversible even after discontinuation.
*   **Diabetes:** Use with caution as **danazol** may cause insulin resistance.

### Monitoring Requirements
*   Periodic liver function tests (LFTs) are required due to potential transaminase elevations.
*   Hematologic monitoring should be considered.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Androgen-like Effects
*   **Common:** Weight gain, acne, seborrhoea, mild hirsutism, edema.
*   **Significant:** Voice changes (hoarseness, sore throat, deepening of pitch), which may persist post-therapy.
*   **Rare:** Hypertrophy of the clitoris.

### Endocrine and Genitourinary
*   **Common:** Menstrual irregularities (spotting, cycle changes, amenorrhea).
*   **Estrogen Deficiency Signs:** Flushing, sweating, vaginal dryness/irritation, reduction in breast size.
*   **Male-specific:** Modest reduction in spermatogenesis and abnormalities in semen parameters (volume, viscosity, count) during long-term therapy.

### Hepatic
*   **Dose-related:** Reversible elevation of serum enzymes and/or jaundice, particularly at doses ≥ 400 mg/day.
*   **Serious:** Cholestatic jaundice, peliosis hepatis, hepatic adenoma, and hepatic failure.

### Central Nervous System
*   Headache, nervousness, emotional lability, dizziness, depression, fatigue, sleep disorders, and tremor.
*   **Rare:** Convulsions, Guillain-Barre syndrome.

### Other Reported Effects (Causal relationship not always confirmed)
*   **Musculoskeletal:** Muscle cramps, joint swelling, and carpal tunnel syndrome.
*   **Dermatologic:** Rashes (maculopapular, vesicular, etc.), sun sensitivity, and rarely Stevens-Johnson syndrome.
*   **Hematologic:** Increased red cell and platelet counts; reversible polycythemia.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Prolongation of prothrombin time (PT).
*   **Carbamazepine:** **Danazol** may increase serum levels of carbamazepine.
*   **Antidiabetics:** **Danazol** can cause insulin resistance, requiring adjustment of antidiabetic medications.
*   **Cyclosporin and Tacrolimus:** May increase plasma levels of these agents, leading to increased renal toxicity. Dose adjustments and systemic monitoring are required.
*   **Statins:** Increased risk of myopathy and rhabdomyolysis when used with simvastatin, atorvastatin, or lovastatin.
*   **Vitamin D:** May increase the calcemic response to synthetic Vitamin D analogs in primary hypoparathyroidism.
*   **Laboratory Interference:** May interfere with assays for testosterone, androstenedione, and dehydroepiandrosterone. Affects thyroid-binding globulin and T4 levels without affecting TSH.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   [US]: Contraindicated. Androgenic effects on female fetuses are well-documented (e.g., ambiguous genitalia). Animal studies in rabbits showed inhibition of fetal development at 2 to 4 times the human dose.
*   **Breastfeeding:** 
    *   [US]: Contraindicated.
*   **Fertility:** Current data are insufficient to assess the impact on human fertility or carcinogenicity.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established in paediatric patients [US].
*   **Geriatric Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient subjects aged 65 and over to determine if they respond differently than younger subjects [US].
*   **Renal Impairment:** Markedly impaired renal function is a contraindication. Specific dosing adjustments for mild-to-moderate impairment are not specified in available sources.
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Markedly impaired hepatic function is a contraindication.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
**Danazol** suppresses the pituitary-ovarian axis by depressing the hypothalamic-pituitary response to lowered estrogen. It depresses the output of follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) and luteinizing hormone (LH). It also interacts with sex hormone receptors and has weak androgenic activity. In hereditary angioedema, it increases levels of the deficient C1 esterase inhibitor (C1EI), consequently increasing C4 complement levels.

### Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Peak plasma concentrations occur between 2 and 8 hours (median Tmax: 4 hours).
*   **Food Effect:** A high-fat meal (>30g fat) increases bioavailability and Cmax by 3 to 4-fold compared to fasting.
*   **Distribution:** Lipophilic; likely partitions into deep tissue compartments.
*   **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolized to 2-hydroxymethyl danazol and ethisterone.
*   **Elimination:** Renal and fecal pathways. Mean half-life is approximately 9.7 hours, increasing to 23.7 hours after chronic dosing in endometriosis patients.

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   Key pivotal trial data were not detailed in the provided source document. The PI notes that **danazol** results in the complete resolution of endometrial lesions in the majority of cases and prevents episodic edema attacks in hereditary angioedema.

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F) [US].
*   **Handling:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container with a child-resistant closure. Keep out of reach of children.
