# Editorial Product Information: Dextromethorphan

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for educational and professional reference. it is not a substitute for the official PI or the local regulatory guidance required for prescribing or dispensing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Dextromethorphan hydrobromide monohydrate.
- **Chemical Class:** Centrally acting non-opioid antitussive; methylated dextrorotatory analogue of levorphanol [Australian].
- **Brand Name(s):** Bisolvon Dry [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Oral Liquid:** 10 mg dextromethorphan hydrobromide monohydrate per 5 mL [Australian].
    - **Pastilles:** 10.5 mg dextromethorphan hydrobromide monohydrate (equivalent to 10 mg anhydrous) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sanofi Consumer Healthcare (Australia); Sanofi-Aventis New Zealand Limited (New Zealand) [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- Symptomatic treatment of dry, irritant, and unproductive coughs [Australian].
- Throat soothing (specifically attributed to the pastille formulation) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Self-treatment duration:** Maximum 5 days; medical advice should be sought if the cough persists beyond this period [Australian].
- **Dosing Frequency:** Every 4 to 6 hours when necessary, not to exceed 4 doses in a 24-hour period [Australian].

### Dosing by Age Group
- **Adults and Children 12 years and over:**
    - **Liquid:** 5 – 15 mL (10 – 30 mg). Maximum daily dose: 60 mL (120 mg) [Australian].
    - **Pastilles:** 1 – 3 pastilles (10 – 30 mg). Maximum daily dose: 12 pastilles (120 mg) [Australian].
- **Children 6 – 11 years:**
    - **Liquid:** 2.5 – 7.5 mL (5 – 15 mg). Maximum daily dose: 30 mL (60 mg) [Australian].
    - **Pastilles:** 1 pastille (10 mg). Maximum daily dose: 6 pastilles (60 mg) [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Pastilles should be sucked slowly [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity or idiosyncratic reaction to **dextromethorphan** or any excipients (e.g., maltitol, sorbitol) [Australian].
- Children under 6 years of age [Australian].
- Concurrent use of Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs) or use within 14 days of stopping MAOI therapy [Australian].
- Bronchial asthma [Australian].
- Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) [Australian].
- Pneumonia [Australian].
- Respiratory depression or respiratory insufficiency [Australian].
- Fructose intolerance (due to maltitol and sorbitol content) [Australian].
- Breastfeeding [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
- **Alcohol:** Consumption of alcohol should be avoided as **dextromethorphan** potentiates the CNS inhibitory effects of alcohol [Australian].
- **Cough Character:** Should not be used for chronic persistent coughs or coughs associated with excessive secretions. Use with extreme caution in productive coughs (e.g., cystic fibrosis, bronchiectasis) or where the cough reflex is impaired (e.g., stroke, Parkinson’s disease) [Australian].
- **Asthma History:** Use with caution in patients with a history of asthma; do not use during an acute attack [Australian].
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** There is a risk of potentially life-threatening serotonin syndrome, especially when co-administered with other serotonergic agents (SSRIs, MAOIs) or CYP2D6 inhibitors. Symptoms include mental status changes, autonomic instability, and neuromuscular abnormalities [Australian].
- **Abuse and Dependence:** **Dextromethorphan** has addictive potential. Tolerance and dependence (mental and physical) may develop. Use should be limited to short periods under supervision for patients with a history of substance abuse [Australian].
- **Mastocytosis:** Avoid use in patients with mastocytosis due to potential histamine release [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally uncommon at standard doses.

| System Organ Class | Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nervous System** | Very Common | Dizziness, somnolence [Australian] |
| **Psychiatric** | Common | Confusion [Australian] |
| | Very Rare | Hallucinations, drug dependence [Australian] |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Common | Nausea, vomiting, constipation, GI disorder [Australian] |
| **General** | Common | Fatigue [Australian] |
| **Immune System** | Not Known | Hypersensitivity, anaphylaxis, angioedema, bronchospasm [Australian] |
| **Skin** | Not Known | Rash, pruritus, urticaria, fixed drug eruption, erythema [Australian] |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Respiratory depression and "serotonin syndrome" are associated with high doses or drug interactions [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **MAOIs:** Risk of hypertensive crisis, hyperpyrexia, and convulsions. Contraindicated within 14 days of MAOI use [Australian].
- **CYP2D6 Inhibitors:** Potent inhibitors (e.g., quinidine, amiodarone, fluoxetine, paroxetine, haloperidol, bupropion, ritonavir) can significantly increase **dextromethorphan** serum levels, increasing the risk of toxicity [Australian].
- **Serotonergic Drugs (SSRIs, TCAs):** Increased risk of serotonin syndrome [Australian].
- **CNS Depressants:** Enhanced sedative effects when used with alcohol, narcotic analgesics, or tranquillizers [Australian].
- **Secretolytics:** Risk of serious mucus accumulation if used alongside secretolytics in patients with pre-existing lung disease [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category A:** Used by a large number of pregnant women without proven increases in malformation or harmful foetal effects. 
    - **Clinical Recommendation:** Avoid during the first trimester. Use in later stages only if clearly needed. High doses may cause neonatal respiratory depression [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Excretion into breast milk is unknown, and respiratory depressive effects on infants cannot be ruled out [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Non-clinical data suggests no reported harmful effects on reproduction [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Contraindicated in children < 6 years. For children 6–11 years, use only on the advice of a healthcare professional [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available in provided sources [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Limited data available. Use with caution, particularly in severe impairment. Doses should be lowered or the interval between doses increased [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Centrally acting cough suppressant. It acts on the cough centre in the medulla and nucleus tractus solaris to elevate the cough threshold [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Onset of action is within 1 hour; duration is approximately 3 to 6 hours. It lacks classical analgesic or respiratory depressant effects at therapeutic doses [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Well absorbed from the GI tract.
    - **Metabolism:** Undergoes extensive first-pass metabolism in the liver via CYP2D6. Metabolism is polymorphic.
    - **Elimination:** Excreted in urine as unchanged drug and demethylated metabolites (e.g., dextrorphan). 
    - **Half-life:** 1.2 to 3.9 hours in extensive metabolisers; up to 45 hours in poor metabolisers [Australian].
