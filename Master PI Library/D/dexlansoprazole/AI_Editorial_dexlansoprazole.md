# Editorial Product Information: Dexlansoprazole

## Editorial Note
*   **Jurisdictions contributing to this editorial:** US (Food and Drug Administration).
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of dexlansoprazole product information for use by healthcare professionals.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not intended to replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Dexlansoprazole (the *R*-enantiomer of lansoprazole).
*   **Chemical Class:** Proton pump inhibitor (PPI); substituted benzimidazole.
*   **Brand Name(s):** Dexlansoprazole Delayed-Release Capsules (Generic) [US].
*   **ATC Code:** A02BC06.
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Delayed-release capsules containing 30 mg or 60 mg of dexlansoprazole in a dual delayed-release formulation (two types of enteric-coated granules with different pH-dependent dissolution profiles).
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** TWi Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (Repackaged by Bryant Ranch Prepack) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
Dexlansoprazole is indicated for use in patients 12 years of age and older for the following:
*   **Healing of Erosive Esophagitis (EE):** Treatment of all grades of EE for up to 8 weeks.
*   **Maintenance of Healed EE:** Maintenance of healing and relief of heartburn. Controlled studies have extended to 6 months in adults and 16 weeks in patients aged 12 to 17 years.
*   **Symptomatic Non-Erosive GERD:** Treatment of heartburn associated with symptomatic non-erosive gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) for 4 weeks.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Adult and Adolescent (≥12 years) Dosing
*   **Healing of EE:** 60 mg once daily for up to 8 weeks.
*   **Maintenance of Healed EE:** 30 mg once daily.
*   **Symptomatic Non-Erosive GERD:** 30 mg once daily for 4 weeks.

### Administration Instructions
*   **General:** Capsules should be swallowed whole and may be taken without regard to food.
*   **Alternative Administration:** For patients with swallowing difficulties, capsules may be opened and the intact granules sprinkled on one tablespoon of applesauce. The mixture must be swallowed immediately without chewing the granules.
*   **Nasogastric (NG) Tube Administration:** Granules may be mixed with 20 mL of water and administered via a catheter-tip syringe through an NG tube (size ≥16 French). The tube should be flushed with an additional 10 mL of water twice to ensure complete delivery.

### Missed Doses
If a dose is missed, it should be administered as soon as possible. If the next scheduled dose is nearly due, the missed dose should be skipped. Two doses should not be taken simultaneously.

## 4. Contraindications
*   Known hypersensitivity to dexlansoprazole or any component of the formulation. Hypersensitivity reactions may include anaphylaxis, angioedema, and acute tubulointerstitial nephritis.
*   Concomitant use with rilpivirine-containing products.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Gastric Malignancy:** Symptomatic response does not preclude the presence of gastric malignancy. Consider further diagnostic testing in adults with suboptimal response or early relapse.
*   **Acute Tubulointerstitial Nephritis (TIN):** May occur at any point during therapy. Discontinue treatment if TIN is suspected.
*   **Clostridium difficile-Associated Diarrhea:** PPI therapy may increase the risk of *C. difficile* infection, especially in hospitalised patients. Use the lowest dose and shortest duration appropriate for the condition.
*   **Bone Fracture:** Long-term, high-dose therapy may increase the risk of osteoporosis-related fractures of the hip, wrist, or spine.
*   **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Reports include Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), and DRESS syndrome. Discontinue at the first sign of a severe skin reaction.
*   **Lupus Erythematosus:** Both cutaneous (CLE) and systemic (SLE) lupus have been reported. Discontinue therapy if new symptoms appear and refer to a specialist.
*   **Cyanocobalamin (Vitamin B12) Deficiency:** Long-term use (>3 years) may lead to malabsorption of B12 due to achlorhydria.
*   **Hypomagnesemia:** Reported rarely, usually after one year of therapy. May lead to tetany, arrhythmias, or seizures. Monitor magnesium levels in patients on long-term therapy or concomitant digoxin/diuretics.
*   **Interactions with Diagnostic Investigations:**
    *   **Neuroendocrine Tumors:** Increased Chromogranin A (CgA) levels may cause false-positive results. Stop dexlansoprazole at least 14 days before assessment.
    *   **Secretin Stimulation Test:** May cause a hyper-response in gastrin secretion, falsely suggesting gastrinoma. Stop treatment 30 days prior to testing.
*   **Fundic Gland Polyps:** Risk increases with long-term use (especially >1 year).
*   **Heart Valve Thickening (Pediatric):** Not recommended for patients under 2 years of age due to animal data showing heart valve thickening in juvenile rats.

### Pediatric Adverse Effects (≥5% in patients 12–17 years)
*   Headache, abdominal pain, diarrhea, nasopharyngitis, and oropharyngeal pain.

### Serious Post-Marketing Effects
*   Anaphylactic shock, exfoliative dermatitis, drug-induced hepatitis, acute renal failure, and idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Antiretrovirals:**
    *   **Rilpivirine:** Contraindicated due to significantly reduced exposure.
    *   **Atazanavir/Nelfinavir:** Reduced concentrations may lead to loss of therapeutic effect; avoid or monitor closely.
    *   **Saquinavir:** Increased concentrations may increase toxicity; monitor for saquinavir-related adverse effects.
*   **Warfarin:** Increased INR and prothrombin time may occur. Monitor INR closely when used concomitantly.
*   **Methotrexate:** Concomitant use may elevate and prolong serum levels of methotrexate, potentially leading to toxicity. Consider temporary PPI withdrawal during high-dose methotrexate therapy.
*   **Digoxin:** Reduced gastric acidity may increase digoxin absorption. Monitor serum digoxin levels.
*   **pH-Dependent Drugs:** Absorption of iron salts, erlotinib, dasatinib, nilotinib, mycophenolate mofetil (MMF), and ketoconazole/itraconazole may be reduced. Use MMF with caution in transplant patients.
*   **CYP2C19/CYP3A4 Interactions:**
    *   **Inducers (e.g., St. John's Wort, Rifampin):** Avoid concomitant use as they may reduce dexlansoprazole exposure.
    *   **Inhibitors (e.g., Voriconazole):** May increase dexlansoprazole exposure.
*   **Tacrolimus:** May increase tacrolimus whole blood trough levels, particularly in intermediate or poor metabolizers of CYP2C19.
*   **False Positive THC:** PPIs may cause false-positive urine screening tests for tetrahydrocannabinol (THC).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [US: No category assigned under PLLR]. There are no adequate studies in pregnant women. Animal data (lansoprazole) show potential effects on fetal bone growth and development at high doses. Use during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus.
*   **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if dexlansoprazole is excreted in human milk. Lansoprazole is present in rat milk. Consider the developmental benefits of breastfeeding alongside the mother's clinical need for the drug.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Approved for ages 12–17 years. Not recommended for children <2 years of age. Effectiveness not established for symptomatic GERD in infants <1 year.
*   **Geriatric Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness observed compared to younger patients, though greater sensitivity in some individuals cannot be ruled out.
*   **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required.
*   **Hepatic Impairment:**
    *   **Mild (Child-Pugh A):** No adjustment needed.
    *   **Moderate (Child-Pugh B):** For healing of EE, a maximum dose of 30 mg once daily is recommended for up to 8 weeks.
    *   **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Not recommended.

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** A PPI that suppresses gastric acid secretion by inhibiting the (H+, K+)-ATPase enzyme system at the secretory surface of the gastric parietal cell.
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption:** Dual delayed-release results in two peaks; the first at 1–2 hours and the second at 4–5 hours post-dose. Bioavailability is approximately 70% or more.
    *   **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is 96–99%.
    *   **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised in the liver via CYP2C19 (hydroxylation) and CYP3A4 (oxidation).
    *   **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 1–2 hours. No unchanged drug is excreted in the urine. Approximately 50.7% is excreted in urine and 47.6% in feces.
*   **Pharmacogenomics:** Systemic exposure is higher in CYP2C19 intermediate and poor metabolizers. In poor metabolizers, AUC can be up to 12 times higher than in extensive metabolizers.

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **EE Healing:** In two 8-week studies, dexlansoprazole 60 mg once daily achieved healing rates of 85–87% compared to 79–85% for lansoprazole 30 mg once daily.
*   **Maintenance of EE Healing:** In a 6-month study, 66.4% of patients on dexlansoprazole 30 mg remained healed compared to 14.3% on placebo.
*   **Symptomatic GERD:** In a 4-week study, 30 mg dexlansoprazole provided a significantly higher median percentage of 24-hour heartburn-free days (54.9%) compared to placebo (18.5%).

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F). 
*   Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container.
