# Editorial Product Information: Dantrolene Sodium

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising data from the provided regulatory source. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians must consult local regulatory documents for final prescribing authority.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Dantrolene sodium hemiheptahydrate
- **Chemical Class:** Hydantoin derivative; direct-acting skeletal muscle relaxant.
- **Brand Names:** **Dantrium** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** M03CA01
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - 25 mg capsules (tan body/orange cap) [Australian]
    - 50 mg capsules (tan body/orange cap) [Australian]
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Spasticity Management:** Indicated for the control of clinical spasticity resulting from serious chronic disorders, including spinal cord injury, stroke, cerebral palsy, and multiple sclerosis [Australian].
- **Functional Goal:** Specifically intended for patients where spasticity relief will aid in restoring residual function or where spasticity retards functional rehabilitation [Australian].
- **Non-Indications:** Not indicated for skeletal muscle spasm resulting from rheumatic disorders or electroconvulsive therapy (ECT) [Australian]. 
- **Clinical Note:** There is no evidence of benefit for patients with fixed contractures [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing Principles
Dosage must be individualised and titrated to the lowest dose compatible with optimal response. A clear therapeutic goal (e.g., improved exercise, transfer manoeuvres, or use of braces) should be established before starting therapy [Australian].

### Adult Dosing [Australian]
- **Initial Dose:** 25 mg once daily.
- **Titration:** Increase to 25 mg two, three, or four times daily. If required, further increase by 25 mg increments up to 50 mg two, three, or four times daily.
- **Maximum Dose:** 200 mg/day is the standard maximum. Doses up to 400 mg/day should only be used rarely with intensive monitoring. Doses exceeding 400 mg/day are not recommended.
- **Review Period:** If no observable benefit is seen within 45 days, therapy should be discontinued.

### Paediatric Dosing (Children >5 years) [Australian]
- **Initial Dose:** 0.5 mg/kg body weight twice daily.
- **Titration:** Increase to 0.5 mg/kg three or four times daily.
- **Maximum Dose:** 2 mg/kg three times daily. Doses should not exceed 50 mg four times daily (200 mg/day).

### Administration Instructions
- Each dosage level should be maintained for four to seven days to assess patient response before increasing the dose [Australian].
- Brief withdrawal (2–4 days) may be used to confirm clinical benefit if improvement is subtle [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Active Hepatic Disease:** Including acute hepatitis and active cirrhosis [Australian].
- **Functional Spasticity:** Situations where spasticity is utilised to sustain upright posture, balance in locomotion, or maintain increased function [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Hepatotoxicity
Dantrolene has a significant potential for hepatotoxicity.
- **Dose-Related Risk:** The risk appears higher at doses above 200 mg/day [Australian].
- **High-Risk Groups:** Fatal hepatocellular disease is more likely in females and patients over 35 years of age [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Baseline liver function tests (SGOT/AST, SGPT/ALT, alkaline phosphatase, total bilirubin) are essential. Testing should be repeated at appropriate intervals during therapy [Australian].
- **Management:** Discontinue therapy immediately if symptoms of hepatitis, jaundice, or abnormal LFTs appear. Reinstitution should only occur if the benefit is vital and after laboratory values have normalised, starting with very low doses in a hospital setting [Australian].

### General Warnings
- **Pulmonary/Cardiac Impairment:** Use with caution in patients with obstructive pulmonary disease or severely impaired cardiac function due to myocardial disease. Dantrolene is associated with pleural effusion and eosinophilia [Australian].
- **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** Oral capsules are not indicated for the treatment of NMS; patients may expire despite therapy [Australian].
- **Photosensitivity:** Patients should be cautioned against excessive sunlight exposure [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Approximately 20% of patients experience transient side effects early in treatment, often manageable by gradual titration [Australian].

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Drowsiness, dizziness, weakness, general malaise, fatigue, diarrhoea. |
| **Common/Uncommon** | **GI:** Constipation, anorexia, vomiting, nausea, abdominal cramps. <br>**Neuro:** Seizures, headache, speech disturbance, visual disturbance. <br>**Psych:** Depression, confusion, insomnia. <br>**Urogenital:** Urinary frequency, haematuria, erectile dysfunction. |
| **Serious/Rare** | **Hepatobiliary:** Hepatitis, liver failure. <br>**Haematologic:** Aplastic anaemia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia. <br>**Respiratory:** Respiratory depression, suffocation feeling, respiratory failure. <br>**Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, pericarditis, pleural effusion with eosinophilia. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Oestrogen:** Increased risk of hepatotoxicity in women over 35 years taking concurrent oestrogen therapy [Australian].
- **Calcium Channel Blockers (e.g., Verapamil):** Combination is not recommended due to rare reports of cardiovascular collapse and hyperkalaemia (based on swine data) [Australian].
- **Neuromuscular Blockers:** May potentiate the effects of non-depolarising muscle relaxants and vecuronium [Australian].
- **CNS Depressants:** Alcohol and sedatives may intensify drowsiness and weakness [Australian].
- **Protein Binding:** Binding to plasma proteins is reduced by **warfarin** and **clofibrate**, and increased by **tolbutamide** [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B2** [Australian]. Safety has not been established; use only if potential benefits outweigh risks to the mother and child. Dantrolene crosses the placenta [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Should not be used by nursing mothers; dantrolene is detected in human breast milk [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available in source documents [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety in children under 5 years of age has not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Higher proportion of fatal hepatic events reported. Use the lowest effective dose and monitor closely for hepatic, renal, and cardiac function [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosage may require significant reduction; monitor for drug-related impairment of renal function (transient lowering of GFR has been reported) [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in active disease. Use extreme caution in patients with a history of liver dysfunction [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Dantrolene uncouples the excitation and contraction of skeletal muscle by interfering with the release of calcium ions from the sarcoplasmic reticulum. It acts directly on the muscle itself, beyond the myoneural junction [Australian].

### Pharmacokinetics [Australian]
- **Absorption:** Incomplete and slow but dose-related.
- **Distribution:** Reversibly bound to plasma proteins (mostly albumin); higher association with red blood cells than plasma.
- **Metabolism:** Metabolised by hepatic microsomal enzymes. Major metabolites: 5-hydroxy analogue and acetamido analogue.
- **Elimination:** Mean half-life in adults is 8.7 hours. Approximately 20–25% excreted in urine as metabolites; <1% excreted unchanged in urine. 45–50% excreted in bile.
