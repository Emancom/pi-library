# Editorial Product Information: Desferrioxamine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically accurate editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a reference for clinical information; it is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing in a particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Desferrioxamine mesilate (also referred to as desferrioxamine mesylate).
- **Chemical Class:** Iron chelating agent (hydroxamic acid derivative).
- **Brand Names:** DBL Desferrioxamine Mesylate for Injection BP [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** V03AC01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Powder for injection: 500 mg and 2 g vials [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) infusion, Intramuscular (IM) injection, or Intravenous (IV) infusion. It can also be administered intraperitoneally in patients on peritoneal dialysis [Australian].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Iron Overload:** Indicated for the promotion of iron excretion in patients with iron overload resulting from multiple blood transfusions (e.g., chronic anaemias, thalassaemia) [Australian]. 
    - *Note:* It is not indicated for primary haemochromatosis unless phlebotomy is unsuitable [Australian].
- **Acute Iron Poisoning:** Used as an adjunct to standard supportive measures (e.g., gastric lavage, shock management) for treating acute iron intoxication [Australian].
- **Aluminium Overload (Off-label/Contextual):** Mentioned in the context of treating dialysis patients with aluminium-related encephalopathy or bone disease [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Chronic Iron Overload:** The lowest effective dose should be used to maintain a negative iron balance. 
    - **Average Dose:** 20 to 40 mg/kg body weight daily is generally adequate [Australian].
    - **Maximum Dose:** Should not exceed 80 mg/kg/day [Australian].
    - **Monitoring:** If serum ferritin levels fall below 1000 nanogram/mL, the risk of toxicity increases; dosage reduction should be considered [Australian].
- **Acute Iron Poisoning:** 
    - **Normotensive patients:** Single IM dose of 2 g for adults; 1 g for children [Australian].
    - **Hypotensive patients:** IV infusion at a maximum rate of 15 mg/kg/hour. The rate should be reduced after 4–6 hours so the total IV dose does not exceed 80 mg/kg/24 hours [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Reconstitution:** 
    - **SC/IV:** Reconstitute with Water for Injections (5 mL for 500 mg vial; 20 mL for 2 g vial) to create a 10% solution [Australian].
    - **IM:** Reconstitute with at least 1.5 mL Water for Injections per 500 mg vial (max concentration ~33%) [Australian].
    - **Further Dilution (IV):** May be diluted with 0.9% Sodium Chloride, 5% Glucose, or Ringer’s-Lactate. Do not use 0.9% Sodium Chloride as the primary solvent for the dry powder as it creates a hypertonic solution [Australian].
- **Route Specifics:** 
    - **SC Infusion:** Administered via portable pump over 8–12 hours. Do not insert needles too close to the dermis [Australian].
    - **IV Infusion:** Must be administered slowly. **Rapid IV bolus is contraindicated** due to risks of flushing, hypotension, and circulatory collapse [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to desferrioxamine (except where desensitisation is possible) [Australian].
- Patients with an absence of excess iron stores [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Rapid Intravenous Administration:** Can cause skin flushing, urticaria, hypotension, and shock. Infusions must be slow (max 15 mg/kg/h) [Australian].
- **Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS):** Reported with excessively high IV doses in both acute poisoning and thalassaemic patients. Doses should not exceed recommended limits [Australian].
- **Infection Susceptibility:** Iron overload and desferrioxamine use may increase susceptibility to iron-dependent microorganisms such as *Yersinia enterocolitica* and *Yersinia pseudotuberculosis*. Rare cases of fatal mucormycosis have been reported, particularly in dialysis patients [Australian]. Discontinue treatment if fever with enteritis or abdominal pain occurs.
- **Visual and Auditory Disturbances:** Prolonged use or high doses (especially with low ferritin levels) may cause cataracts, blurred vision, retinal abnormalities, tinnitus, or sensorineural hearing loss. **Baseline and 3-monthly ophthalmological and audiological assessments are required** [Australian].
- **Neurological Impairment:** May exacerbate aluminium-related encephalopathy (seizures) by rapidly increasing circulating aluminium [Australian].
- **Growth Retardation:** Associated with high doses (>40 mg/kg) in young children (<3 years). Monitor height and weight every 3 months in paediatric patients [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic:** Caution in renal failure as the iron complex is excreted renally. Blood creatinine may increase during therapy [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Note: Frequencies are based on Australian data.*
- **Very Common (>10%):** Arthralgia and myalgia [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Growth retardation and bone changes (e.g., metaphyseal dysplasia) in children receiving >60 mg/kg/day [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Asthma, tinnitus, hearing loss, and injection site reactions (blisters, oedema) [Australian].
- **Rare (0.1% to <0.1%):** Anaphylactic/anaphylactoid reactions, hypotension (if IV is too rapid), visual disturbances (blurred vision, night blindness, cataracts), muscle spasms, and bone pain [Australian].
- **Very Rare (<0.01%):** Adult Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS), leucopenia, thrombocytopenia, and aplastic anaemia [Australian].
- **Frequency Not Known:** Discolouration of urine (orange-rose or 'vin rose'), discoloured faeces, nausea, vomiting, dizziness, and seizures [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Prochlorperazine:** Concurrent use may lead to loss of consciousness (48–72 hours). Avoid concomitant use [Australian].
- **Phenothiazines and Methyldopa:** May potentiate neuro-ophthalmic toxicity [Australian].
- **Vitamin C (Ascorbic Acid):** Enhances iron excretion when given orally (150–250 mg daily). However, high doses may increase tissue iron toxicity, potentially leading to cardiac decompensation. **Do not start Vitamin C until after the first week of desferrioxamine treatment.** Monitor cardiac function during combined use [Australian].
- **Gallium-67 Scintigraphy:** Desferrioxamine increases the urinary excretion of gallium-bound radiolabel, distorting results. Discontinue desferrioxamine 48 hours prior to imaging [Australian].
- **Heparin:** Mixing solutions may cause cloudiness or precipitation [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category B3 [Australian]:** Studies in animals have shown evidence of increased fetal damage (delayed ossification and skeletal anomalies). Use in pregnant women (especially during the first trimester) only if the potential benefit outweighs the risk to the fetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Excretion in human milk is unknown. A decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the mother's clinical need [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Desferrioxamine is teratogenic in animal models; specific human fertility data is limited [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for children under 3 years unless significant iron mobilisation (>1 mg/day) is proven. High risk of growth retardation; monitor every 3 months [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies were insufficient to determine if they respond differently. Start at the low end of the dosing range due to the higher frequency of renal, hepatic, or cardiac dysfunction [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Approximately 50% of the ferrioxamine complex is excreted renally. In terminal renal failure, desferrioxamine is dialysable. Weekly doses of 1 to 4 g have been effective in patients on haemodialysis [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A chelating agent that binds ferric ions ($Fe^{3+}$) and, to a lesser extent, aluminium, copper, and zinc. It binds iron from ferritin and haemosiderin but not from transferrin, haemoglobin, or cytochromes. Theoretically, 1 g sequester 85 mg of ferric iron [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Poorly absorbed from the GI tract (requires parenteral administration).
    - **Distribution:** <10% bound to serum proteins.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by plasma enzymes.
    - **Elimination:** Biphasic; initial half-life is 5–10 minutes, followed by a slower phase (6.1 hours). Excreted via urine and faeces (as ferrioxamine and metabolites) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Summary:** Not specified in available sources (The Australian PI notes "No data available") [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C.
- Protect from light.
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Use as soon as practicable. If necessary, store at 2–8°C for no more than 24 hours [Australian].
