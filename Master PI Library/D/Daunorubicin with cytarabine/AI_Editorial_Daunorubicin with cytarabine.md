# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data into a single reference.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine [(R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt].
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
    - **Note:** Oral forms (tablets and solution) are mentioned in pharmacokinetics but were not the primary focus of the provided parenteral PI [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metabolic Disorders:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:**
    - **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
    - **Frequency:** Administer every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported administered dose is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
    - **Dose Adjustment:** Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Visual Inspection:** Inspect for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [US].
- **Monitoring:** Obtain plasma carnitine concentrations before starting therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None Known:** There are no listed contraindications for levocarnitine injection in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including **anaphylaxis**, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, particularly in ESRD patients. Some reactions occur within minutes of IV administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and do not re-administer without a careful risk-benefit analysis [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** Safety and efficacy of the oral form have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Periodic monitoring of plasma carnitine levels and clinical status is essential to guide dosing [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions in clinical trials were often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD.

### Very Common (>10%) [US]
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%), Flu syndrome (25%), Chest pain (12%), Headache (22%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%).

### Common (1%–10%) [US]
- **General:** Abdominal pain (9%), Accidental injury (10%), Asthenia (9%), Fever (7%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Arrhythmia (2%), Tachycardia (6%), Atrial fibrillation (2%), Palpitations (5%).
- **Digestive:** Nausea (8%), Anorexia (5%), Constipation (3%), Dyspepsia (5%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), Hyperkalemia (6%), Hypervolemia (5%), Weight decrease (5%).
- **Nervous:** Dizziness (13%), Paresthesia (5%), Insomnia (4%), Depression (5%).
- **Respiratory:** Cough increase (9%), Dyspnea (7%), Rhinitis (9%).
- **Skin:** Pruritus (5%), Rash (3%).

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Anaphylaxis** and severe respiratory distress [US].
- **Seizures** [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category C - inferred based on standard labeling]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies in nursing mothers have not been conducted. Levocarnitine concentrations in milk increase in dairy cows following administration. The risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefit of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was observed in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders applies to pediatric patients (see Section 3).
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the available source [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - Levocarnitine is the primary treatment for carnitine deficiency in ESRD patients on dialysis. 
    - IV administration is preferred for ESRD to avoid the accumulation of toxic oral metabolites (TMA/TMAO) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance essential for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrates for oxidation and subsequent energy production in all tissues except the brain [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Bioavailability:** Oral bioavailability is low (approximately 15.1% to 15.9%) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Pharmacokinetic Study:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis), pre-dialysis plasma concentrations increased from a baseline of ~20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks of therapy [US].
- **Placebo-Controlled Trials:** Two double-blind trials in chronic hemodialysis patients (n=130) were used to establish the safety profile and dose-response relationship for plasma carnitine levels [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Cautions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing. 
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].
