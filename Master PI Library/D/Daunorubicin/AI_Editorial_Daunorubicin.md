# Editorial Product Information: Daunorubicin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data into a single, coherent reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI. Clinicians must consult the approved PI in their specific region before prescribing or administering this medication.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Daunorubicin (as hydrochloride).
- **Chemical Class:** Cytotoxic anthracycline antibiotic.
- **Brand Names:** Pfizer (Perth) Daunorubicin [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01DB02.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Solution for injection: 20 mg/10 mL (2 mg/mL as base; 2.14 mg/mL as hydrochloride) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Lymphocytic (Lymphoblastic) Leukaemia (ALL):** Generally reserved for cases resistant to other agents, though used in early stages in combination with vincristine and steroids.
- **Acute Myeloblastic Leukaemia (AML):** Used across all stages, either as monotherapy or in combination with other cytotoxic agents (e.g., cytarabine).
- **Disseminated Solid Tumours:** Demonstrated effectiveness in some cases of disseminated neuroblastoma and rhabdomyosarcoma [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Dosage must be individualised based on clinical response and toxicity. The following regimens are common [Australian]:
- 0.5 to 1 mg/kg at intervals of 1 or more days.
- 2 mg/kg at intervals of 4 or more days.
- 2.5 to 3 mg/kg at intervals of 7 to 14 days.

### Indication-Specific Dosing
- **ALL:** 1 mg/kg may be repeated at 1- to 4-day intervals depending on tolerance.
- **AML:** 2 mg/kg repeated at 4- to 7-day intervals. Doses >2 mg/kg require caution and intervals of one week or longer.
- **Combination Therapy (AML):** 1.5 mg/kg IV on Day 1 of a course that includes cytarabine. Usually, 2 to 3 courses are required for remission.

### Administration Instructions
- **IV Only:** Do NOT administer IM or SC; severe tissue necrosis will occur.
- **Infusion Technique:** Add to a free-flowing IV infusion of 0.9% sodium chloride or 5% dextrose. Administer via a large vein over no less than 3–5 minutes.
- **Precautions:** Avoid "direct push" injections due to extravasation risk. Facial flushing or erythematous streaking indicates too rapid administration.
- **Extravasation:** If stinging or burning occurs, stop the infusion immediately and restart in a different vein.

### Maximum Cumulative Dose
- The total lifetime dose should not exceed **20 mg/kg** (or **550 mg/m²**) to minimise the risk of irreversible cardiac failure [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Persistent or marked myelosuppression (induced by prior therapy or radiotherapy).
- Impaired cardiac function (myocardial insufficiency, recent MI, severe arrhythmias).
- Previous attainment of the maximum cumulative dose of **daunorubicin**, doxorubicin, or other anthracyclines/anthracenediones.
- Known hypersensitivity to **daunorubicin**, other anthracyclines, or excipients.
- Severe infections.
- Severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Grade C) or severe renal impairment (GFR <10 mL/min or serum creatinine >7.9 mg/dL) [Australian].
- Pregnancy [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
*Note: While the Australian PI does not use the "Boxed Warning" format common in the US, the following are critical safety alerts:*
- **Cardiac Toxicity:** Risk of irreversible, potentially fatal cardiomyopathy. Risk increases significantly as the cumulative dose approaches 550 mg/m².
- **Bone Marrow Depression:** Severe myelosuppression is expected in all patients.
- **Administration:** Must be administered only by physicians experienced in cancer chemotherapy.

### General Precautions
- **Secondary Leukaemia:** Reported in patients treated with anthracyclines, typically with a 1- to 3-year latency period.
- **Tumour Lysis Syndrome:** Hyperuricaemia may occur due to rapid lysis of leukaemia cells. Monitor uric acid, potassium, and phosphate.
- **Immunosuppression:** Increased susceptibility to infections; avoid live vaccines.
- **Radiotherapy:** Concurrent use may increase skin reactions and mucositis.

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Cardiac:** Baseline ECG and LVEF (MUGA or ECHO) are required. Regular LVEF monitoring is essential, especially at higher cumulative doses.
- **Haematologic:** Full blood counts must be performed before and during each cycle.
- **Hepatic/Renal:** Evaluate bilirubin and creatinine prior to each treatment.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Cardiotoxic Agents (e.g., Trastuzumab, Cyclophosphamide):** Increased risk of cardiac toxicity. Total cumulative dose of **daunorubicin** should not exceed 400 mg/m² if used with cyclophosphamide [Australian].
- **Hepatotoxic Medications:** Concurrent use may increase the risk of liver impairment.
- **Anti-gout Agents (Allopurinol, Probenecid):** **Daunorubicin** increases uric acid; dosage adjustments of anti-gout medications may be required.
- **Live Vaccines:** Risk of serious/fatal infection; vaccination should be avoided.
- **Other Cytotoxics:** Additive myelosuppression and GI toxicity.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category D**. **Daunorubicin** is a potential cause of fetal malformations and is contraindicated during pregnancy.
- **Contraception:** Females of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during and for at least 27 weeks after the final dose. Males must use contraception during and for at least 14 weeks after the final dose [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Excretion in breast milk is unknown. Due to the risk of serious adverse reactions in infants, breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for at least 6 days after the last dose [Australian].
- **Fertility:** May induce chromosomal damage in human spermatozoa and testicular atrophy in animal models. Fertility preservation advice is recommended prior to treatment.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Increased susceptibility to anthracycline-induced cardiac toxicity. 
    - Under 2 years (or <0.5 m² BSA): Calculate dose by body weight (mg/kg); max cumulative dose is 10 mg/kg.
    - Over 2 years: Max cumulative dose is 300 mg/m².
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of cardiotoxicity; use with caution.
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - Serum creatinine >3.0 mg/dL: Reduce dose by 50%.
    - GFR <10 mL/min: Contraindicated [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - Bilirubin 1.2 to 3 mg/dL: Reduce starting dose by 50%.
    - Bilirubin >3 mg/dL: Reduce starting dose by 75%.
    - Child-Pugh Grade C: Contraindicated [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Intercalates between DNA base pairs, inhibiting DNA and DNA-dependent RNA synthesis. It also inhibits polymerase activity and induces free radical damage to DNA.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Rapidly and widely distributed; does not cross the blood-brain barrier but crosses the placenta.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised in the liver by aldo-keto reductases to **daunorubicinol** (active metabolite).
    - **Elimination:** Triphasic plasma decline. Terminal half-life of **daunorubicin** is ~18.5 hours; **daunorubicinol** is ~26.7 hours. Excreted via bile (~40%) and urine (14–23%).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store between 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze).
- **Light:** Protect from light.
- **Handling:** Only trained professionals should handle the drug. Pregnant personnel should not handle cytotoxic agents.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription-only in all documented jurisdictions.
