# Editorial Product Information: Diethylpropion

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the United States PI was available for review; therefore, regional comparisons with other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian) are not included.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Diethylpropion hydrochloride.
- **Chemical Class:** Sympathomimetic amine.
- **Brand Names:** Diethylpropion Hydrochloride Extended-Release (generic), Tenuate Dospan (reference product) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A08AA03.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Extended-release (ER) tablet, 75 mg [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** A-S Medication Solutions (Repackager); Lannett Company, Inc. (ANDA Holder) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Exogenous Obesity:** Indicated as a short-term adjunct (a few weeks) in a weight reduction regimen based on caloric restriction for patients with an initial Body Mass Index (BMI) of 30 kg/m² or higher. It is intended for patients who have not responded to appropriate weight-reducing regimens (diet and/or exercise) alone [US].
- **Usage Limitation:** Indicated for use as monotherapy only [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** One 75 mg extended-release tablet daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** The tablet must be swallowed whole. It is recommended for administration in the midmorning [US].
- **Titration:** Not specifically required; however, if tolerance develops, the drug should be discontinued rather than increasing the dose [US].
- **Treatment Duration:** Restricted to "a few weeks" [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Cardiovascular Disease:** Pulmonary hypertension, advanced arteriosclerosis, severe hypertension [US].
- **Endocrine/Metabolic:** Hyperthyroidism, glaucoma [US].
- **Psychiatric/Behavioral:** Agitated states, history of drug abuse [US].
- **Drug Interactions:** During or within 14 days following the administration of monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs) due to the risk of hypertensive crises [US].
- **Combination Therapy:** Use in combination with other anorectic agents [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity or idiosyncrasy to sympathomimetic amines [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Pulmonary Hypertension:** A case-control study associated anorectic agents, including diethylpropion, with an increased risk of pulmonary hypertension, a rare but often fatal disorder. Use for >3 months increases the risk 23-fold. Discontinue immediately if exertional dyspnea, angina pectoris, syncope, or lower extremity edema occurs [US].
- **Valvular Heart Disease:** While primarily associated with fenfluramine/dexfenfluramine, valvulopathy has been very rarely reported with diethylpropion monotherapy. Baseline cardiac evaluation should be considered [US].
- **Tolerance and Dependence:** Tolerance usually occurs within a few weeks. Diethylpropion is chemically related to amphetamines and carries a risk of psychological and physical dependence. Abrupt cessation after high-dose use can result in extreme fatigue and mental depression [US].
- **CNS Effects:** May impair the ability to operate machinery or drive motor vehicles. Psychosis has been reported, even at recommended doses [US].
- **Seizure Disorders:** May increase convulsions in some patients with epilepsy; careful monitoring and potential dose titration or discontinuation are required [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Weight loss should be assessed within the first 4 weeks of treatment; if weight loss is unsatisfactory (<4 lbs), treatment should be discontinued [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Note: Specific frequencies (e.g., %) are not specified in the source; the following are reported clinical observations.*

- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, palpitation, elevation of blood pressure, precordial pain, arrhythmia (including ventricular), ECG changes, and rare reports of pulmonary hypertension or valvular heart disease [US].
- **Central Nervous System:** Overstimulation, nervousness, restlessness, dizziness, jitteriness, insomnia, anxiety, euphoria, depression, dysphoria, tremor, headache, and rare psychotic episodes. Cerebrovascular accidents have been reported [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Dryness of the mouth, unpleasant taste, nausea, vomiting, abdominal discomfort, diarrhea, constipation [US].
- **Allergic:** Urticaria, rash, ecchymosis, erythema [US].
- **Endocrine/Reproductive:** Impotence, changes in libido, gynecomastia, menstrual upset [US].
- **Hematopoietic:** Bone marrow depression, agranulocytosis, leukopenia [US].
- **Miscellaneous:** Dyspnea, hair loss, muscle pain, increased sweating, polyuria, and dysuria [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Risk of hypertensive crisis. Avoid use within 14 days of MAOI therapy [US].
- **Other Anorectics:** Combined use is contraindicated due to potential for serious cardiac problems [US].
- **Antidiabetic Agents:** Diethylpropion may alter insulin or oral antidiabetic drug requirements [US].
- **General Anesthetics:** Concurrent use may result in arrhythmias [US].
- **Antihypertensive Agents:** Diethylpropion may interfere with the efficacy of drugs like guanethidine or alpha-methyldopa [US].
- **Phenothiazines:** May antagonize the anorectic effect of diethylpropion [US].
- **Alcohol:** Potential for adverse interactions with CNS-active agents [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category B:** Reproduction studies in rats (1.6x human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm.
    - **Clinical Data:** No adequate/well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use only if clearly needed.
    - **Non-teratogenic effects:** Potential for withdrawal symptoms in neonates if abused during pregnancy [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Diethylpropion and its metabolites are excreted in human milk. Caution is advised when administered to a nursing woman [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies revealed no evidence of impaired fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established in patients below 16 years of age. Use is not recommended in this population [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient subjects aged 65+ to determine if they respond differently. Dosing should be cautious, starting at the low end of the range. Renal function monitoring is recommended due to the drug’s primary excretion via the kidneys [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Risk of toxic reactions may be greater. Monitoring of renal function is recommended [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources, though general caution in the elderly mentions hepatic function considerations [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A sympathomimetic amine with pharmacologic activity similar to amphetamines. Its primary action in obesity is appetite suppression, though other CNS or metabolic effects may contribute [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Actions include CNS stimulation and elevation of blood pressure [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed from the GI tract [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolized via N-dealkylation and reduction to several biologically active metabolites. These metabolites cross the blood-brain barrier and the placenta [US].
    - **Elimination:** Excreted mainly by the kidneys (75-106% of the dose recovered in urine within 48 hours). The plasma half-life of aminoketone metabolites is estimated at 4 to 6 hours. Circulating levels are affected by urinary pH [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy:** Short-term clinical trials indicate that adult obese subjects treated with anorectic drugs and dietary management lose more weight on average than those treated with placebo and diet. The magnitude of increased weight loss is typically a fraction of a pound per week, with the greatest loss occurring in the first weeks of therapy [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Keep tightly closed and protect from excessive heat [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as a **Schedule IV (CIV)** controlled substance. Available by Prescription Only (Rx only) [US].
