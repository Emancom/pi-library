# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA). 
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection, synthesised for use by healthcare professionals. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) mandated for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the United States (FDA) PI was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Metabolic Disorders)
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose may be given, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature (25°C) when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Discontinue immediately if severe reactions occur [US].
- **Seizures:** Reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:**
    - Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels before parenteral therapy.
    - Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- **Oral Levocarnitine in Renal Impairment:** Safety of oral formulations has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral use in ESRD may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (ESRD Clinical Trials)
Based on placebo-controlled trials in ESRD patients, the following events occurred at a frequency ≥5% regardless of causality [US]:

| System Organ Class | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain (21%), Asthenia (9-12%), Chest pain (6-15%), Fever (5-12%), Headache (3-37%), Injection site reaction (27-38%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (18-21%), Hypotension (3-19%), Tachycardia (5-9%) |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea (9-35%), Nausea (5-12%), Vomiting (9-21%) |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia (6-15%), Hypervolemia (3-12%) |
| **Nervous System** | Dizziness (10-18%), Paresthesia (3-12%) |
| **Respiratory** | Cough increase (10-18%), Pharyngitis (15-27%), Rhinitis (6-11%) |

### Serious/Other Adverse Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; gastritis [US].
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].
- **Metabolic:** Body odor [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when co-administered with levocarnitine. Monitoring of INR is recommended upon initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned/available sources cite "clearly needed"]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate human studies exist [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine levels after administration. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine should be weighed against maternal benefit. Consideration may be given to discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies indicate levocarnitine is not mutagenic and does not impair fertility in rats/rabbits [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders are applicable; see Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in provided source.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used as an indication for ESRD; however, caution is advised with oral formulations due to metabolite accumulation (see Section 5) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study (n=12), 20 mg/kg doses post-dialysis increased mean trough carnitine levels from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L. Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) showed dose-proportional increases in plasma concentrations [US].
- **Clinical Outcomes:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, its effects on specific clinical outcomes or signs/symptoms of deficiency in this population have not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat. Vials are single-dose; discard unused portions as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources [US].
