# Editorial Product Information: Paracetamol and Diphenhydramine Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information from Australia (Therapeutic Goods Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of the combination of paracetamol and diphenhydramine hydrochloride for healthcare professionals.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the Australian PI was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Paracetamol (p-aminophenol derivative) and Diphenhydramine hydrochloride (ethanolamine-class antihistamine).
- **Brand Name(s):** MersynoNight Night Time Pain Relief [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Film-coated tablets containing Paracetamol 500 mg and Diphenhydramine hydrochloride 25 mg [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sanofi Consumer Healthcare [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Temporary relief of pain associated with sleeping difficulty:** Including headache, migraine, backache, arthritis, rheumatic and muscle pain, neuralgia, toothache, or period pain [Australian].
- **Relief of fever** [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Children >12 years):** 1 to 2 tablets taken with fluid only at bedtime [Australian].
- **Maximum Dose:** No more than 2 tablets in a 24-hour period [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Allow at least four hours between any other paracetamol-containing products and this medication [Australian].
    - Total daily paracetamol intake must not exceed 4,000 mg [Australian].
- **Duration of Use:** Should not be used for more than a few days in adults, or 48 hours in children, unless under medical advice [Australian]. Usage should not exceed 3 days without consulting a doctor [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Age:** Children 12 years of age and younger [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to paracetamol, diphenhydramine hydrochloride, or any excipients [Australian].
- **Hepatic Health:** Severe hepatocellular insufficiency [Australian].
- **Lactation:** Use is contraindicated in breastfeeding women [Australian].
- **Neonates:** Newborns or premature infants [Australian].
- **Drug Interactions:** Concurrent use of monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Overdose Risk:** Contains paracetamol. Do not combine with other paracetamol products as it may lead to fatal liver failure [Australian].
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Life-threatening reactions, including Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), have been reported. Discontinue immediately at the first sign of progressive skin rash or blisters [Australian].
- **High Anion Gap Metabolic Acidosis (HAGMA):** Risk is increased in patients with sepsis, malnutrition, or chronic alcoholism, particularly when paracetamol is used with flucloxacillin [Australian].
- **CNS Effects:** Diphenhydramine may cause significant drowsiness that persists the following day. Patients should avoid driving or operating machinery [Australian].
- **Abuse and Dependence:** Potential for abuse is low but has been reported in patients with psychotic disorders or a history of substance abuse [Australian].
- **Specific Clinical Cautions:** Use with caution in patients with:
    - Narrow-angle glaucoma, stenosing peptic ulcer, or pyloroduodenal obstruction [Australian].
    - Symptomatic prostatic hypertrophy or bladder neck obstruction [Australian].
    - Epilepsy, myasthenia gravis, asthma, bronchitis, or COPD [Australian].
    - Glucose-6-phosphate-dehydrogenase deficiency [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Paracetamol-Related:**
    - **Warfarin:** Prolonged use may increase the risk of bleeding [Australian].
    - **Flucloxacillin:** Risk of HAGMA due to pyroglutamic acidosis [Australian].
    - **Metoclopramide/Domperidone:** Increases paracetamol absorption [Australian].
    - **Cholestyramine/Chelating Resins:** Decreases paracetamol absorption [Australian].
- **Diphenhydramine-Related:**
    - **CNS Depressants:** Alcohol, sedatives, and opioid analgesics potentiate sedative effects [Australian].
    - **Antimuscarinics:** Tricyclic antidepressants and atropine may intensify anticholinergic effects (dry mouth, blurred vision, urinary retention) [Australian].
    - **MAOIs:** May prolong and intensify anticholinergic and CNS depressive effects; contraindicated within 2 weeks of stopping an MAOI [Australian].
    - **CYP2D6 Inhibitor:** Diphenhydramine may increase plasma levels of drugs metabolised by CYP2D6, such as metoprolol and venlafaxine [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category A:** Paracetamol and diphenhydramine have been taken by a large number of pregnant women without a proven increase in malformations. Use at the lowest effective dose for the shortest time [Australian]. 
    - Use in the third trimester may cause reactions in the newborn [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Diphenhydramine is excreted in breast milk and may pose a risk to the infant [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available in source documents.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Contraindicated in children 12 years and under. Children may experience paradoxical excitation with diphenhydramine [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of confusion and paradoxical excitation. Higher susceptibility to CNS depressive effects [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution in moderate to severe renal insufficiency due to increased risk of metabolic acidosis and paracetamol toxicity [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe hepatocellular insufficiency. Use with caution in mild-to-moderate impairment or underlying liver disease [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:**
    - **Paracetamol:** Primarily acts via central inhibition of prostaglandin synthesis. It lacks peripheral anti-inflammatory activity [Australian].
    - **Diphenhydramine:** A lipophilic H1-receptor antagonist that competes with histamine at central and peripheral sites. It also possesses significant anticholinergic activity [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Paracetamol peak 10–60 min; Diphenhydramine peak 1–4 hours [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Both are extensively metabolised in the liver. Diphenhydramine undergoes significant first-pass metabolism (40–60% bioavailability) [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Paracetamol half-life is 1–3 hours. Diphenhydramine half-life is 2.4–9.3 hours; prolonged in liver cirrhosis [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Summary:** Not applicable/No pivotal trials described in the available source document [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Handling:** Protect from moisture [Australian].
