# Editorial Product Information: Doxorubicin

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (TGA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the Product Information (PI) for **doxorubicin hydrochloride**. It is designed as a concise, clinically accurate reference for healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial is a synthesis of available regulatory data and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the Australian PI was provided for synthesis; therefore, jurisdictional variations from other regions (e.g., US, European) are "Not specified in available sources."

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Doxorubicin hydrochloride (an anthracycline cytotoxic antibiotic).
- **Brand Names:** **ADRIAMYCIN** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available source.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - Solution for injection (2 mg/mL).
    - Available in 10 mg/5 mL, 20 mg/10 mL, 50 mg/25 mL, and 200 mg/100 mL vials [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) or Intravesical.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Doxorubicin** is indicated for the production of regression in various neoplastic conditions:
- **Established Activity:** Breast carcinoma, ovarian carcinoma, bronchogenic (lung) carcinoma, soft tissue and bone sarcomas, Wilms’ tumour, neuroblastoma, Hodgkin’s and non-Hodgkin’s lymphomas, acute leukaemia, hepatoma, and thyroid carcinoma.
- **Reported Response:** Gastric, cervical, head and neck, and endometrial carcinomas; testicular tumours; and multiple myeloma.
- **Intravesical Use:** Primary management of non-metastatic bladder carcinoma (Tis, T1, T2).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Standard IV Regimen:** 60–75 mg/m² administered as a single injection every 21 days.
- **Alternative IV Regimen:** 30 mg/m² daily for three consecutive days, repeated every 4 weeks.
- **Lifetime Cumulative Limit:** 550 mg/m². This should be restricted to 450 mg/m² in patients aged 70 or older [Australian].

### Intravesical Administration (Bladder Cancer)
- **Instillation:** 80 mg diluted in 100 mL of normal saline.
- **Procedure:** Catheterise and empty the bladder; instil solution; rotate patient position every 15 minutes for 1 hour. Patients should not void for 1 hour post-instillation. Repeat monthly.

### Administration Instructions
- **IV Infusion:** Administer slowly (not less than 3–5 minutes) into the tubing of a freely running IV of 0.9% sodium chloride or 5% glucose. 
- **Caution:** Direct push injection is not recommended due to the high risk of extravasation.
- **Handling:** **Doxorubicin** is a potent cytotoxin. Protective clothing (gloves, goggles, gowns) is mandatory. Pregnant staff must be excluded from handling.

## 4. Contraindications
### General
- Hypersensitivity to doxorubicin, other anthracyclines, or any excipients.
- Pregnancy and lactation [Australian].

### Intravenous Use
- Persistent myelosuppression or severe stomatitis from prior therapy.
- Generalised infection.
- Severe hepatic impairment.
- Severe myocardial insufficiency, recent myocardial infarction, or severe arrhythmias.
- Previous treatment with maximum cumulative doses of doxorubicin or other anthracyclines (e.g., epirubicin, idarubicin).

### Intravesical Use
- Invasive tumours penetrating the bladder wall.
- Urinary tract infections or bladder inflammation.
- Haematuria or difficulties with catheterisation.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiotoxicity:** Irreversible myocardial toxicity leading to congestive heart failure (CHF) may occur, often as the cumulative dose approaches 550 mg/m². Monitoring of Left Ventricular Ejection Fraction (LVEF) via MUGA or ECHO is required. Risk increases with prior mediastinal irradiation or concurrent cyclophosphamide/trastuzumab therapy.
- **Haematologic Toxicity:** Dose-dependent, reversible leucopenia/neutropenia is the most common dose-limiting toxicity. Nadir typically occurs 10–14 days post-dose.
- **Extravasation:** Severe local tissue necrosis and vesication will occur if the drug leaks into surrounding tissues. Discontinue infusion immediately if stinging or burning occurs.
- **Secondary Malignancy:** Risk of secondary leukaemia (AML/ALL) exists, particularly when used in combination with DNA-damaging agents.
- **Tumour Lysis Syndrome:** Hyperuricaemia may occur; monitor uric acid, potassium, and calcium.
- **Urine Discolouration:** Patients must be advised that urine may turn red for 1–2 days after treatment.

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Leucopenia, neutropenia, anaemia, thrombocytopenia; Mucositis/stomatitis, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea; Alopecia, palmar-plantar erythrodysaesthesia; Pyrexia, asthenia; Infection; Decreased LVEF. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | CHF, cardiomyopathy, sinus tachycardia; Conjunctivitis; Oesophagitis, abdominal pain; Sepsis, cellulitis; Urticaria, rash, skin/nail hyperpigmentation. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Embolism. |
| **Not Known** | Anaphylaxis, dehydration, hyperuricaemia, gastrointestinal haemorrhage, acute leukaemia, somnolence, chromaturia (red urine), recall phenomenon, photosensitivity. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4/2D6 & P-gp:** Doxorubicin is a substrate. Inhibitors (e.g., verapamil) may increase toxicity; inducers (e.g., phenytoin, St John's Wort) may decrease efficacy.
- **Cyclophosphamide:** Enhances cardiotoxicity and may exacerbate haemorrhagic cystitis.
- **Cyclosporin:** Increases AUC of doxorubicin; may lead to profound haematologic toxicity, coma, or seizures.
- **Paclitaxel:** May increase plasma concentrations of doxorubicin if given prior to the anthracycline.
- **Trastuzumab:** High risk of cardiotoxicity. Avoid anthracyclines for up to 7 months after stopping trastuzumab where possible [Australian].
- **Heparin/Fluorouracil:** Incompatible; may cause precipitation. Do not mix in the same IV line.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Doxorubicin is genotoxic and embryotoxic. It should not be used unless the benefit outweighs the fetal risk.
- **Contraception:** Women should use effective contraception during and for 6 months and 10 days after the last dose. Men should use contraception during and for 3 months and 10 days after the last dose [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Doxorubicin is secreted in breast milk. Discontinue breastfeeding for at least 10 days after the last dose.
- **Fertility:** May cause permanent infertility, oligospermia, or premature menopause.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Increased risk of delayed cardiotoxicity. Females may be at higher risk than males.
- **Elderly Use:** Higher risk of marrow depletion. Total cumulative dose should be restricted to 450 mg/m² for patients aged 70+ [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Dose reduction is mandatory based on bilirubin levels:
    - Bilirubin 20–50 $\mu$mol/L: Give 1/2 of normal dose.
    - Bilirubin >50 $\mu$mol/L: Give 1/4 of normal dose.
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available source.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits nucleic acid synthesis by binding to DNA and inhibiting topoisomerase II. It is cell cycle phase non-specific.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Distribution:** Does not cross the blood-brain barrier; significant tissue binding.
    - **Metabolism:** Predominantly hepatic to adriamycinol (active) and aglycone metabolites.
    - **Elimination:** Triphasic plasma clearance (half-lives: 12 min, 3.3 h, and 29.6 h).
    - **Excretion:** Primarily biliary (40–50% in 7 days); minimal renal excretion (4–5% in 5 days).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No specific pivotal trial data described in the available source document.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze).
- **Light:** Protect from light; retain in carton until use.
- **Stability:** Once refrigerated, the solution may gel; it typically returns to a mobile state after 2–4 hours at room temperature (15°C to 25°C).

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only.
