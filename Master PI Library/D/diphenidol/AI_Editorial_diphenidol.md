# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesised from the following jurisdiction’s Product Information: United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is designed to offer a concise, clinically relevant summary of the pharmacological profile, dosing, and safety considerations of **levocarnitine**.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource only. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing or dispensing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; naturally occurring carrier molecule.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Levocarnitine Injection** (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):**
    - **Intravenous Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - **Administration:** Delivered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; frequency should not be less than every 6 hours.
    - **Maximum Dose:** Up to 300 mg/kg has been administered in clinical practice [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Monitoring and Administration Instructions
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels prior to initiating parenteral therapy.
- Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- **Visual Inspection:** Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These typically occur in ESRD patients on dialysis and may manifest within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks/benefits before re-administration [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with and without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **General Precaution (Oral vs. IV):** The safety of oral levocarnitine has not been established in patients with renal insufficiency. High doses of oral formulations in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) include [US]:
- **General:** Asthenia, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain, fever, abdominal pain.
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), chest pain (12%), arrhythmia.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), anorexia.
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%).
- **Neurological:** Dizziness (13%), paresthesia (5%), insomnia (4%), depression (5%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%), hypervolemia (5%).

### Serious and Postmarketing Effects
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis and body odor (rarely reported) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels regularly after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not specified]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (BSA) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. However, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response, it should be used in pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk. The risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant should be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg for metabolic disorders); see Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specific dosing is provided for ESRD patients on hemodialysis (10-20 mg/kg post-dialysis). Caution is advised regarding the use of oral formulations in patients with severe renal compromise [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn metabolic errors, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly cleared [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (found in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (found in faeces).
    - **Elimination:** Following IV administration (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Pharmacokinetics:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg three times per week after dialysis showed that trough levels (normally 40–50 µmol/L) increased from a baseline of 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L by week 8.
- **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses established that carnitine concentrations increase in a dose-dependent manner. While carnitine levels increase effectively, the impact on specific clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial; the formulation contains no preservatives [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) [US].
