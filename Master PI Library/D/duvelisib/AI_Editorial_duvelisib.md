# Editorial Product Information: Duvelisib

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of duvelisib for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory information from the primary source.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference by healthcare professionals and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Duvelisib (hydrate); a kinase inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** **COPIKTRA** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Oral capsules in 15 mg (pink opaque) and 25 mg (white/off-white and Swedish orange opaque) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Secura Bio, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia (CLL) / Small Lymphocytic Lymphoma (SLL):** Indicated for adult patients with relapsed or refractory CLL or SLL following at least two prior lines of systemic therapy [US].
- **Limitations of Use:** Not indicated or recommended for first-line or second-line treatment of CLL or SLL due to an increased risk of treatment-related mortality [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** 25 mg taken orally twice daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Administer in 28-day cycles [US].
    - May be taken with or without food [US].
    - Capsules must be swallowed whole; do not open, break, or chew [US].
- **Recommended Prophylaxis:** 
    - Provide prophylaxis for *Pneumocystis jirovecii* (PJP) during treatment; continue until absolute CD4+ T cell count exceeds 200 cells/µL [US].
    - Consider prophylactic antivirals to prevent cytomegalovirus (CMV) infection/reactivation [US].
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** If a dose is missed by less than 6 hours, it should be taken immediately and the next dose taken at the usual time. If missed by more than 6 hours, the dose should be skipped and the next dose taken at the regular time [US].
- **Dose Modifications for Interactions:**
    - **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Reduce duvelisib dose to 15 mg twice daily [US].
    - **Moderate CYP3A4 Inducers:** If co-administration is unavoidable, increase duvelisib dose to 40 mg twice daily (from 25 mg) or 25 mg twice daily (from 15 mg) starting on Day 12 of the inducer [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None specified in available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [US]:**
    - **Treatment-Related Mortality:** Occurred in 15% of patients in clinical trials, primarily due to infections.
    - **Serious Infections:** Fatal and/or serious infections occurred in 31% of patients. Withhold if infection is suspected.
    - **Diarrhea or Colitis:** Fatal and/or serious events occurred in 18% of patients. 
    - **Cutaneous Reactions:** Fatal and/or serious reactions occurred in 5% of patients.
    - **Pneumonitis:** Fatal and/or serious pneumonitis (without infectious cause) occurred in 5% of patients.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Grade 3 or 4 ALT/AST elevations occurred in 8% and 2% of patients, respectively. Monitor hepatic function regularly [US].
- **Neutropenia:** Grade 3 or 4 neutropenia occurred in 42% of patients. Monitor blood counts at least every 2 weeks for the first 2 months, then weekly if ANC < 1.0 Gi/L [US].
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Can cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status before initiation [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Diarrhea or colitis (50%), neutropenia (34%), rash (31%), fatigue (29%), pyrexia (26%), cough (25%), nausea (24%), upper respiratory tract infection (21%), pneumonia (21%), musculoskeletal pain (20%), anemia (20%), thrombocytopenia (17%), vomiting (16%), transaminase elevation (15%), decreased appetite (14%), edema (14%), mucositis (14%), constipation (13%), headache (12%), dyspnea (12%) [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** 
    - Sepsis (3%), pneumonia (17%), and Grade 3+ diarrhea/colitis (23%) [US].
    - Rare but fatal cases of Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN), and Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors (e.g., Ketoconazole):** Increase duvelisib exposure (AUC) approximately 4-fold. Reduce duvelisib dose [US].
    - **Strong CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., Rifampin):** Decrease duvelisib AUC by 82%; avoid co-administration [US].
    - **Moderate CYP3A4 Inducers:** Decrease duvelisib AUC; avoid if possible or increase duvelisib dose [US].
    - **CYP3A4 Substrates:** Duvelisib increases exposure of sensitive CYP3A4 substrates (e.g., midazolam AUC increased 4.3-fold). Monitor for toxicities [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Risk Summary [US]:** Based on animal studies, duvelisib can cause fetal harm (embryo-fetal mortality and structural abnormalities). No human data available.
    - **Category:** Not specified by letter [US-Modernized Labeling], but animal data indicates significant risk.
- **Breastfeeding:** No data on presence in human milk. Advise against breastfeeding during treatment and for 1 month after the final dose [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies indicate potential for impaired male and female fertility (testicular atrophy, decreased ovary/uterus weight) [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established in patients < 18 years [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No clinically significant differences in safety or efficacy observed between patients $\ge$ 65 years and younger patients [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No clinically significant effect on exposure observed in patients with CrCl 23 to 80 mL/min [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No clinically significant effect on exposure observed in patients with Child-Pugh Class A, B, or C [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Dual inhibitor of PI3K-$\delta$ and PI3K-$\gamma$ isoforms. Inhibits B-cell receptor signaling, chemotaxis, and reduces viability in malignant B-cells [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Reduces levels of phosphorylated AKT (downstream marker). No QTc prolongation > 20 ms observed [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability of 42%; T$_{max}$ 1–2 hours [US].
    - **Distribution:** Protein binding > 98%; V$_{ss}$ 28.5 L [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP3A4 [US].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life 4.7 hours. Excretion: 79% in feces (11% unchanged) and 14% in urine (< 1% unchanged) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **DUO Study:** A phase 3 randomized trial (N=319) compared duvelisib to ofatumumab in relapsed/refractory CLL/SLL.
- **Efficacy Results ($\ge$ 2 prior therapies):** Median Progression-Free Survival (PFS) was 16.4 months for duvelisib vs. 9.1 months for ofatumumab (HR 0.40). Overall Response Rate (ORR) was 78% vs. 39% [US].
- **Long-term Safety:** 5-year follow-up showed 15% mortality in the duvelisib arm vs. 3% for ofatumumab [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F and 86°F) [US].
- Retain in original package/dispense in original container (blister packs) [US].

- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only (Rx-only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources (N/A for US jurisdiction).
