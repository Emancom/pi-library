# Editorial Product Information: Doxycycline

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of doxycycline for healthcare professionals, synthesizing clinical data and regulatory requirements into a concise reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical guide; it is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing within a particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Doxycycline (as hyclate).
- **Chemical Class:** Tetracycline antibiotic (synthetic derivative of oxytetracycline).
- **Brand Names:** APX-DOXYCYCLINE [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - 50 mg film-coated tablets [Australian].
    - 100 mg film-coated tablets with a breakline [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Doxycycline** is indicated for the treatment of infections caused by sensitive strains of the following organisms [Australian]:
- **Respiratory and Atypical Infections:** *Mycoplasma pneumoniae*, *Chlamydia psittaci* (psittacosis), and *Chlamydia trachomatis* (trachoma, inclusion conjunctivitis, and lymphogranuloma venereum).
- **Rickettsial Infections:** Queensland tick typhus, epidemic typhus, Q fever, murine endemic typhus, and scrub typhus.
- **Sexually Transmitted and Treponemal Infections:** Alternative treatment for Syphilis (*Treponema pallidum*) and Yaws (*Treponema perenue*) when penicillin is contraindicated; *Neisseria gonorrhoea*; *Haemophilus ducreyi* (chancroid); and *Calymmatobacterium granulomatis*.
- **Gram-negative Infections:** Cholera (*Vibrio sp.*), Brucellosis (in combination with streptomycin), Plague (*Yersinia pestis*), Tularemia (*Francisella tularensis*), and Bartonellosis (*Bartonella bacilliformis*).
- **Zoonotic and Other Infections:** Relapsing fever (*Borreliae*) and adjunct therapy for acute intestinal amoebiasis.
- **Dermatological:** Adjunctive therapy for severe acne.
- **Malaria Prophylaxis:** Chemoprophylaxis for *Plasmodium falciparum* (adults and children >10 years). Used in combination with other agents for *Plasmodium vivax* suppression [Australian].

**Note:** Doxycycline is generally not the drug of choice for *Staphylococcus*, *Streptococcus*, or enteric bacteria due to high resistance rates unless sensitivity is confirmed [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Children >8 years/50 kg)
- **Standard Dose:** 200 mg on Day 1 (100 mg every 12 hours), followed by 100 mg/day as a single or divided dose.
- **Severe Infections:** 100 mg every 12 hours.

### Dosing by Indication
- **Acute Uncomplicated Gonococcal Infections:** 100 mg twice daily for 5 to 7 days.
- **Primary and Secondary Syphilis:** 300 mg daily in divided doses for at least 10 days.
- **Louse-borne Typhus:** Single oral dose of 100 mg or 200 mg.
- **Scrub Typhus Prevention:** Single dose of 200 mg.
- **Severe Acne:** 50 mg daily for up to 12 weeks.
- **Malaria Prophylaxis:** 100 mg daily, starting 2 days before entering the endemic area and continuing for 2 weeks after departure. (Limit use to 8 weeks due to lack of long-term safety data) [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Fluid Intake:** Administer with at least 100 mL of fluid to prevent esophageal irritation.
- **Posture:** Patients should remain upright for at least 30 minutes post-dose; avoid administration immediately before bed.
- **Food/Milk:** May be taken with food or milk to reduce gastric irritation; absorption is not significantly affected.
- **Duration:** Continue therapy for 24–48 hours after symptoms resolve. Group A streptococcal infections require at least 10 days of treatment.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to doxycycline, other tetracyclines, or any excipients.
- History of benign intracranial hypertension associated with tetracycline use, or concurrent use of vitamin A/retinoids (isotretinoin, etretinate) [Australian].
- Pregnancy (specifically 16 weeks post-conception onwards) and lactation.
- Concurrent use with methoxyflurane (risk of fatal renal toxicity) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Oesophageal Injury:** Risk of adhesion, ulceration, and dysphagia if ingested with insufficient fluid or in a recumbent position.
- **Intracranial Hypertension (IH):** Associated with tetracyclines. Overweight women of childbearing age or those with a history of IH are at higher risk. Symptoms include headache and visual disturbances. Permanent vision loss is possible; prompt ophthalmic evaluation is required for visual changes.
- **Antibiotic-Associated Colitis:** *C. difficile* associated diarrhea (CDAD) may range from mild to fatal. Avoid drugs that delay peristalsis (e.g., opiates).
- **Tooth Development:** Use during the second/third trimester, infancy, or childhood up to age 8 may cause permanent yellow-grey-brown tooth discoloration and enamel hypoplasia.
- **Photosensitivity:** Exaggerated sunburn reactions can occur. Discontinue at the first sign of skin erythema.
- **Severe Skin Reactions:** Cases of exfoliative dermatitis, Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), and DRESS have been reported. Discontinue immediately if these occur.
- **Monitoring:** Periodic laboratory evaluation of hematopoietic, renal, and hepatic organ systems is required during long-term therapy.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### More Common Reactions
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, anorexia, abdominal pain, glossitis, black hairy tongue.
- **Dermatological:** Photosensitive dermatitis, erythematous/maculopapular rash, urticaria, nail discoloration, onycholysis.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Urticaria, exacerbation of systemic lupus erythematosus, Jarisch-Herxheimer reaction (in spirochete infections).
- **Hepatic/Renal:** Cholestatic hepatitis, fatty liver, dose-related increase in serum urea.

### Less Common Reactions
- **Nervous System:** Benign intracranial hypertension (adults), bulging fontanelles (infants), headache, dizziness, taste loss, paresthesia.
- **Hematological:** Leucopenia, thrombocytopenia, hemolytic anemia, eosinophilia.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Enterocolitis, pseudomembranous colitis, pancreatitis (rare: <0.1%).
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, angioedema, serum sickness, pericarditis.

### Rare/Serious Reactions
- **Renal:** Acute renal failure.
- **Dermatological:** SJS, TEN, DRESS, fixed drug eruptions.
- **Ocular:** Blurred vision, diplopia, scotomata [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Antacids and Minerals:** Absorption is impaired by antacids containing aluminium, calcium, or magnesium, and by iron-containing preparations.
- **Metabolism Inducers:** Alcohol, barbiturates, and anticonvulsants (phenytoin, carbamazepine) reduce doxycycline plasma levels.
- **Anticoagulants:** Tetracyclines may depress plasma prothrombin activity; downward adjustment of anticoagulant dosage may be necessary.
- **Penicillin:** Bacteriostatic drugs may interfere with the bactericidal action of penicillin; concurrent use should be avoided.
- **Oral Contraceptives:** Doxycycline may reduce the efficacy of oral contraceptives. Patients should use a barrier method during and for 7 days after completing therapy [Australian].
- **Methoxyflurane:** Concurrent use may result in fatal renal toxicity.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category D. Tetracyclines cross the placenta. They are considered safe for the first 18 weeks of pregnancy (16 weeks post-conception). Use during the second and third trimesters may cause permanent tooth discoloration and impaired bone growth. Acute fatty necrosis of the liver has been reported in pregnant women treated with tetracyclines.
- **Breastfeeding:** Doxycycline is excreted in breast milk. It forms a stable calcium complex in bone-forming tissue and carries a risk of permanent tooth staining in the infant. Use is not recommended for nursing mothers.
- **Fertility:** No data available in source documents.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Contraindicated in children under 8 years of age due to risk of tooth discoloration and enamel hypoplasia. For children >8 years weighing <50 kg, the dose is 2 mg/kg [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available in source documents.
- **Renal Impairment:** Doxycycline does not accumulate in patients with renal impairment; however, the anti-anabolic action of tetracyclines may increase serum urea. The serum half-life (18–22 hours) remains similar in normal and severely impaired renal function.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution; abnormal hepatic function and fatty liver degeneration have been reported rarely.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Primarily bacteriostatic. Inhibits bacterial protein synthesis by binding to the 30S ribosomal subunit.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Active against a wide range of Gram-positive and Gram-negative organisms.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Virtually complete oral absorption; not significantly delayed by food or milk. 
    - **Distribution:** Highly lipid-soluble with low calcium affinity. Protein binding ranges from 23% to 93%. Peak serum levels of ~4.3 µg/mL are reached at 2.7 hours following a 200 mg dose.
    - **Excretion:** Approximately 40% is excreted by the kidneys over 72 hours in patients with normal renal function. The remainder is excreted in the feces.
