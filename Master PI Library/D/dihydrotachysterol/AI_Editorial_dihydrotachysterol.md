# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises available regulatory information into a structured format for clinical reference.
- **Note:** This editorial is a synthesis based on the provided US Product Information. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 
- **Discrepancy Note:** While the introductory prompt mentioned dihydrotachysterol, the provided official documentation pertains exclusively to **levocarnitine**. This editorial focuses on the provided levocarnitine data.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not specified in source, provided for clinical context).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile injection solution, 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
  - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg [US].
  - Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
  - **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
  - **Frequency:** Doses should be given every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
  - **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
  - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
  - **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
  - **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations (normal range: 40 to 50 µmol/L) [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].
- Discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in patients with ESRD on dialysis. Reactions can occur within minutes of injection. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and monitor the patient for recurrence if re-administration is considered [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].

### Precautions
- **Oral Formulation in Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].
- **Monitoring:** For patients with metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (ESRD Clinical Trials)
The following events occurred at a frequency ≥5% in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials of ESRD patients (regardless of causality) [US]:

| Body System | Adverse Event | Levocarnitine (%) | Placebo (%) |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain, Headache, Fever, Injection site reaction, Pain | 9–33% | 5–59% |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, Hypotension, Tachycardia, Chest pain | 6–20% | 5–19% |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, Vomiting, Nausea, Anorexia | 5–16% | 3–19% |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, Cough increase, Dyspnea, Rhinitis | 7–23% | 10–33% |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, Hyperkalemia, Hypervolemia | 5–9% | 3–17% |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, Insomnia, Paresthesia, Depression | 5–13% | 3–11% |

### Other Reported Effects
- **Serious:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, seizures [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent: gastritis and body odor [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
  - **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine therapy or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:**
  - **Category:** Not explicitly lettered, but descriptive of US Category C. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm [US].
  - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration.
  - **Recommendation:** Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of supplementation to the mother. Consider discontinuation of nursing or the drug [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosage is based on body weight (see Section 3). Specific age-range safety profiles are not otherwise detailed in the available source [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. Note the warning regarding the accumulation of metabolites if oral formulations are used (see Section 5) [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance essential for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are quickly eliminated [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine over 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Study (ESRD):** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x weekly), mean pre-dialysis (trough) levocarnitine concentrations increased from ~21 µmol/L at baseline to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** ESRD patients receiving 10, 20, or 40 mg/kg 3x weekly showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels at 12 and 24 weeks. While carnitine levels increased, the effect on clinical outcomes in this specific population was not determined in these trials [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL concentration) for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources for other jurisdictions.
