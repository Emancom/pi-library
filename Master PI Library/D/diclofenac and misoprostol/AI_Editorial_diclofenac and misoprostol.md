# Editorial Product Information: Diclofenac Sodium and Misoprostol

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of the product information for diclofenac sodium and misoprostol, intended for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial reference and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Diclofenac sodium (phenylacetic acid derivative, non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug [NSAID]) and Misoprostol (synthetic prostaglandin E1 [PGE1] analog).
- **Brand Names:** Not specified in available sources (Generic: Mylan/Greenstone) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Delayed-release tablets: 50 mg diclofenac sodium / 200 mcg misoprostol [US].
    - Delayed-release tablets: 75 mg diclofenac sodium / 200 mcg misoprostol [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Mylan Pharmaceuticals Inc. (Labeler); Greenstone LLC (Distributor) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Osteoarthritis (OA):** Treatment of signs and symptoms of OA in adults at high risk of developing NSAID-induced gastric and duodenal ulcers and their complications [US].
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA):** Treatment of signs and symptoms of RA in adults at high risk of developing NSAID-induced gastric and duodenal ulcers and their complications [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration possible consistent with patient goals [US].
- Tablets should be swallowed whole and not crushed, broken, or chewed [US].
- To minimise diarrhoea, take with food and avoid magnesium-containing antacids [US].

### Dosing by Indication [US]
- **Osteoarthritis:** 
    - Recommended: One 50/200 tablet three times daily. 
    - Intolerance adjustments: 50/200 or 75/200 twice daily (note: less effective for ulcer prevention).
    - Maximum diclofenac dose: 150 mg/day.
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis:** 
    - Recommended: One 50/200 tablet three or four times daily.
    - Intolerance adjustments: 50/200 or 75/200 twice daily.
    - Maximum diclofenac dose: 200 mg/day.

### Special Dosing Considerations
- **CYP2C9 Inhibitors:** If concomitant use of a CYP2C9 inhibitor (e.g., voriconazole) is required, the maximum daily dose of diclofenac is 100 mg (e.g., 50/200 twice daily) [US].
- **Misoprostol optimization:** Total misoprostol should not exceed 800 mcg/day; no single dose should exceed 200 mcg [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy:** Absolutely contraindicated due to misoprostol’s abortifacient properties [US].
- **Post-CABG:** In the setting of coronary artery bypass graft (CABG) surgery [US].
- **Active Bleeding:** Active gastrointestinal (GI) bleeding [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to diclofenac, misoprostol, other prostaglandins, or any component of the formulation [US].
- **Aspirin Sensitivity:** History of asthma, urticaria, or allergic-type reactions following aspirin or other NSAIDs [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
- **Uterine Rupture and Abortion:** Misoprostol can cause uterine rupture, abortion, premature birth, and birth defects. It must not be used in pregnant women.
- **Cardiovascular (CV) Risk:** NSAIDs increase the risk of serious thrombotic events, myocardial infarction (MI), and stroke, which can be fatal.
- **Gastrointestinal (GI) Risk:** NSAIDs increase the risk of serious GI adverse events including bleeding, ulceration, and perforation, which can be fatal and occur without warning.

### General Warnings
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Meaningful ALT/AST elevations (>3x ULN) occurred in ~1.6% of patients in clinical trials. Severe hepatic reactions (jaundice, liver failure) have occurred. Monitor transaminases within 4–8 weeks of initiation and periodically thereafter [US].
- **Hypertension:** NSAIDs can cause new-onset or worsening of pre-existing hypertension. Monitor blood pressure closely during therapy [US].
- **Heart Failure and Edema:** NSAIDs cause fluid retention. Avoid in patients with severe heart failure unless benefits outweigh risks [US].
- **Renal Toxicity:** Long-term use may cause renal papillary necrosis. Monitor renal function in patients with heart failure, dehydration, or pre-existing renal/hepatic impairment [US].
- **Serious Skin Reactions:** Discontinue at the first sign of skin rash (e.g., SJS, TEN, DRESS) [US].
- **Anaphylaxis:** Seek emergency help if signs occur [US].
- **Hematologic Toxicity:** Anemia may occur; monitor hemoglobin/hematocrit if symptoms of anemia develop [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency of Adverse Effects [US]
| Adverse Reaction | Diclofenac/Misoprostol | Diclofenac Sodium |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Abdominal Pain** | 21% | 15% |
| **Diarrhea** | 19% | 11% |
| **Dyspepsia** | 14% | 11% |
| **Nausea** | 11% | 6% |
| **Flatulence** | 9% | 4% |

- **Diarrhea:** Usually develops early (2–7 days) and is self-limiting. Minimise by taking with food and avoiding magnesium antacids [US].
- **Serious Events:** Myocardial infarction, stroke, GI perforation/bleeding, liver failure, and acute renal failure [US].
- **Postmarketing Experience:** Sepsis, vasculitis, meningitis, birth defects, pancreatitis, and agranulocytosis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Anticoagulants/Antiplatelets (Warfarin, Aspirin, SSRIs):** Increased risk of serious GI bleeding [US].
- **ACE Inhibitors, ARBs, and Beta-Blockers:** NSAIDs may diminish the antihypertensive effect; increased risk of renal impairment in elderly or volume-depleted patients [US].
- **Diuretics:** Reduced natriuretic effect of loop and thiazide diuretics [US].
- **Lithium and Digoxin:** Increased serum levels/toxicity of these agents [US].
- **Methotrexate/Cyclosporine:** Increased toxicity and nephrotoxicity [US].
- **Pemetrexed:** Avoid diclofenac 2 days before, on the day of, and 2 days after pemetrexed administration (especially in renal impairment) [US].
- **Magnesium-containing Antacids:** Exacerbates misoprostol-associated diarrhea [US].
- **CYP2C9 Inhibitors (e.g., voriconazole):** Significant increase in diclofenac exposure; requires dose reduction [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Contraindicated [US].** Misoprostol induces uterine contractions and is a potent abortifacient. 
    - **Pregnancy Category:** Not specified by letter category in source, but contraindication is absolute [US].
    - **Clinical Note:** Verify pregnancy status in females of reproductive potential within 2 weeks prior to initiation [US].
- **Lactation:** Diclofenac and misoprostol acid are excreted in breast milk. The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the mother's clinical need [US].
- **Fertility:** NSAIDs may delay or prevent follicular rupture, potentially causing reversible infertility. Consider withdrawal in women having difficulty conceiving [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Higher risk for CV, GI, and renal complications. Use the lowest effective dose and monitor renal function [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Avoid in advanced renal disease. Misoprostol acid AUC is doubled in patients with severe renal impairment (hemodialysis-dependent) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Diclofenac concentrations are generally similar to healthy subjects in cirrhosis, but misoprostol acid AUC may double in mild-to-moderate impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Diclofenac:** Inhibition of cyclooxygenase (COX-1 and COX-2) enzymes, reducing prostaglandin synthesis [US].
    - **Misoprostol:** PGE1 analog that increases bicarbonate and mucus production and inhibits gastric acid secretion [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Diclofenac is 100% absorbed but undergoes 50% first-pass metabolism (50% bioavailability). Misoprostol is rapidly absorbed and converted to misoprostol acid (active) [US].
    - **Distribution:** Diclofenac is >99% albumin-bound [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Diclofenac is primarily metabolised via CYP2C9 [US].
    - **Elimination:** Diclofenac half-life is ~2 hours; misoprostol acid half-life is ~30 minutes. Both are primarily excreted by the kidney [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Upper GI Safety:** In a 12-week study (N=1623), misoprostol 200 mcg TID or QID was significantly more effective than placebo in reducing the incidence of gastric ulcers (3% vs 11%) and duodenal ulcers (1-3% vs 6%) [US].
- **OA Studies:** In a study of 572 OA patients with a history of ulcers, the combination product showed a significantly lower gastric ulcer rate (3-4%) compared to diclofenac alone (11%) over 6 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Dispense in tight, child-resistant containers [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
