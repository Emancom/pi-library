# Editorial Product Information: Dasiglucagon

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory guidance.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Always consult the current local PI for the most up-to-date and region-specific requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Dasiglucagon (as dasiglucagon hydrochloride).
- **Chemical Class:** Glucagon analogue (29 amino acid peptide).
- **Brand Name:** **ZEGALOGUE** [US].
- **ATC Code:** H04AA01 (Glucagon and analogues).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Injection: 0.6 mg/0.6 mL solution in a single-dose autoinjector [US].
    - Injection: 0.6 mg/0.6 mL solution in a single-dose prefilled syringe [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Novo Nordisk Inc. [US]; Manufactured by Rechon Life Science AB [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Severe Hypoglycemia:** Indicated for the treatment of severe hypoglycemia in pediatric and adult patients with diabetes aged 6 years and older [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric $\ge$ 6 years):** The recommended dose is 0.6 mg administered once via subcutaneous injection [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Inject into the lower abdomen, buttocks, thighs, or outer upper arm.
    - Visually inspect the solution; it must be clear, colorless, and free of particles.
    - **Emergency Response:** Healthcare providers must instruct patients and caregivers to call for emergency assistance immediately after administration.
    - **Repeat Dosing:** If there is no clinical response after 15 minutes, a second 0.6 mg dose may be administered from a new device while awaiting emergency help [US].
    - **Post-Recovery:** Once the patient responds and can safely swallow, give oral carbohydrates to restore liver glycogen and prevent a recurrence of hypoglycemia.
- **Device Handling:** Single-use only. The autoinjector and prefilled syringe cannot be reused.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pheochromocytoma:** Use is contraindicated as glucagon products may stimulate the release of catecholamines from the tumor, leading to a substantial increase in blood pressure [US].
- **Insulinoma:** Use is contraindicated due to the risk of stimulating exaggerated insulin release, which may cause secondary hypoglycemia [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Substantial Increase in Blood Pressure (Pheochromocytoma):** In cases of suspected undiagnosed pheochromocytoma, 5 to 10 mg of intravenous phentolamine mesylate may be used to lower blood pressure [US].
- **Hypoglycemia in Insulinoma Patients:** If symptoms of hypoglycemia develop after a dose, treat with oral or intravenous glucose [US].
- **Hypersensitivity and Allergic Reactions:** Allergic reactions, including generalized rash and anaphylactic shock (with hypotension and respiratory distress), have been reported with glucagon products. Seek immediate medical attention if these occur [US].
- **Decreased Hepatic Glycogen:** **ZEGALOGUE** is only effective if sufficient hepatic glycogen is present. It may be ineffective in patients in states of starvation, those with adrenal insufficiency, or those with chronic hypoglycemia. These patients should be treated with glucose [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are typically transient and occur within 12 hours of treatment.

### Common Adverse Reactions ($\ge$ 2%)
| Adverse Reaction | Adults (N=116) | Pediatrics (N=20) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nausea** | 57% | 65% |
| **Vomiting** | 25% | 50% |
| **Headache** | 11% | 10% |
| **Diarrhea** | 5% | Not reported ($\ge$ 2%) |
| **Injection site pain** | 2% | 5% |

- **Other Reactions:** Hypertension, hypotension, bradycardia, palpitations, presyncope, and orthostatic intolerance were reported within 12 hours of treatment [US].
- **Immunogenicity:** Anti-drug antibodies (ADAs) were detected in <1% of patients. One patient showed transient neutralizing activity and cross-reactivity with native glucagon [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Beta-blockers:** May cause a transient increase in pulse and blood pressure following administration [US].
- **Indomethacin:** May reduce the ability of dasiglucagon to raise blood glucose or may produce paradoxical hypoglycemia [US].
- **Warfarin:** Dasiglucagon may enhance the anticoagulant effect of warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned; follow Risk Summary]. There are no available data on use in pregnant women. Animal studies in rabbits showed fetal skeletal and visceral malformations at doses $\ge$ 20 times the human dose based on AUC. Daily administration to rats at 709 times the human dose showed no adverse developmental effects [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There is no information regarding the presence of dasiglucagon in human milk. As a peptide, dasiglucagon is expected to be degraded into amino acids in the infant’s digestive tract and is unlikely to cause harm [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy are established in patients aged 6 years and older. Safety has not been established in pediatric patients < 6 years of age [US].
- **Elderly Use ($\ge$ 65 years):** Clinical trials did not include sufficient numbers of patients aged 65 and over to determine if they respond differently than younger adults [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Specific dosing adjustments are not provided in the source documentation. Metabolism is primarily via proteolytic degradation in the blood, liver, and kidneys.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A glucagon receptor agonist that activates hepatic glucagon receptors. This stimulates the breakdown of glycogen (glycogenolysis) and the release of glucose from the liver into the bloodstream [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In adults, the mean glucose increase from baseline at 90 minutes was 168 mg/dL. In pediatric patients (7–17 years), the mean increase at 60 minutes was 162 mg/dL [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Mean peak plasma concentration ($C_{max}$) of 5110 pg/mL reached at approximately 35 minutes ($T_{max}$) in adults. In pediatrics, $C_{max}$ was 3920 pg/mL at 21 minutes [US].
    - **Distribution:** Apparent volume of distribution is 47 L to 57 L.
    - **Elimination:** Half-life is approximately 30 minutes.
    - **Metabolism:** Cleared through proteolytic degradation pathways in the blood, liver, and kidney, similar to native glucagon [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adult Patients (Trials A & B):** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials. The median time to plasma glucose recovery (defined as an increase of $\ge$ 20 mg/dL) was 10 minutes for **ZEGALOGUE** compared to 35–40 minutes for placebo. **ZEGALOGUE** was numerically similar to glucagon for injection (10 vs 12 minutes) [US].
- **Pediatric Patients (Trial C):** Conducted in patients aged 6 to 17 years. The median time to glucose recovery was 10 minutes for **ZEGALOGUE** versus 30 minutes for placebo [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Refrigerated Storage:** Store between 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) in the original carton to protect from light. Do not freeze [US].
- **Room Temperature Storage:** May be stored at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) for a single period of up to 12 months. 
    - Do not return the product to the refrigerator once stored at room temperature.
    - Discard after 12 months at room temperature or after the expiration date, whichever is earlier [US].
- **Special Handling:** The device caps/needle covers contain dry natural rubber (latex derivative), which may cause allergic reactions in sensitive individuals [US].
