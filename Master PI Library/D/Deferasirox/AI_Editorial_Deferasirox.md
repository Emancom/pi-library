# Editorial Product Information: Deferasirox

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for educational and professional reference. It does not replace the official PI used for prescribing in specific jurisdictions. Clinicians should consult the local approved PI before making treatment decisions.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Deferasirox (a tridentate iron chelator).
- **Brand Names:** AKM Deferasirox [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** V03AC03.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Dispersible tablets: 125 mg, 250 mg, and 500 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pharmacor Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Transfusional Iron Overload:** Treatment of chronic iron overload resulting from frequent blood transfusions (transfusional haemosiderosis) in:
    - Adult and paediatric patients 6 years and older [Australian].
    - Paediatric patients aged 2 to 5 years who are unable to take desferrioxamine or in whom desferrioxamine therapy has been ineffective [Australian].
- **Non-Transfusion-Dependent Thalassemia (NTDT):** Treatment of chronic iron overload in patients aged 10 years and older with NTDT syndromes [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
Dosing is calculated in mg/kg and rounded to the nearest whole tablet size. 
- **Switching Formulations:** If switching from deferasirox film-coated tablets to dispersible tablets, the dose of the dispersible tablet must be **40% higher** than the film-coated tablet dose [Australian].

### Transfusional Iron Overload
- **Initiation:** Recommended after transfusion of ~20 units (~100 mL/kg) of packed red blood cells (PRBC) or when serum ferritin exceeds 1,000 mcg/L [Australian].
- **Starting Dose:** Usually 20 mg/kg daily. 
    - Consider 30 mg/kg if the goal is reduction of iron burden and the patient receives >14 mL/kg/month of PRBC.
    - Consider 10 mg/kg if the goal is maintenance and the patient receives <7 mL/kg/month of PRBC [Australian].
- **Titration:** Adjust dose every 3–6 months in steps of 5–10 mg/kg based on serum ferritin trends. 
- **Maximum Dose:** 40 mg/kg daily [Australian].

### Non-Transfusion-Dependent Thalassemia (NTDT)
- **Initiation:** Only when iron overload is evidenced by Liver Iron Concentration (LIC) $\ge$ 5 mg Fe/g dry weight (dw) or serum ferritin consistently >800 mcg/L [Australian].
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg daily [Australian].
- **Maximum Dose:** 20 mg/kg daily (Note: Dosing should not exceed 10 mg/kg if LIC has not been assessed and ferritin is $\le$ 2,000 mcg/L) [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Take once daily on an empty stomach at least 30 minutes before food, preferably at the same time each day [Australian].
- **Preparation:** Disperse tablets by stirring in water, orange juice, or apple juice until a fine suspension is formed.
- **Caution:** Do not chew or swallow tablets whole. Do not disperse in milk or carbonated drinks [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Estimated Creatinine Clearance (CrCl) <40 mL/min or serum creatinine >2 times the age-appropriate upper limit of normal (ULN) [Australian].
- Platelet count <50 x $10^9$/L [Australian].
- High-risk myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS) or other malignancies where chelation therapy is not expected to provide benefit due to rapid disease progression [Australian].
- Hypersensitivity to deferasirox or any excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Renal Toxicity:** Cases of acute renal failure (some fatal) and renal tubulopathy (e.g., Fanconi Syndrome) have been reported. Monitor serum creatinine and/or CrCl in duplicate before initiation and then monthly (weekly in the first month for high-risk patients) [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Post-marketing reports include hepatic failure (predominantly in patients >55 years or those with significant comorbidities). Monitor transaminases, bilirubin, and alkaline phosphatase before initiation, every 2 weeks in the first month, and then monthly [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal (GI) Effects:** Risk of GI ulceration, perforation, and haemorrhage (sometimes fatal). Use caution with concurrent NSAIDs, corticosteroids, oral bisphosphonates, or anticoagulants [Australian].
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** SJS, TEN, and DRESS have been reported. Discontinue permanently if a SCAR is suspected [Australian].
- **Auditory and Ocular Disturbances:** High-frequency hearing loss and lenticular opacities have occurred. Annual auditory and ophthalmic testing (including slit lamp examination) is recommended [Australian].
- **Cytopenias:** Agranulocytosis, neutropenia, and thrombocytopenia have been reported. Monitor blood counts regularly [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Increased blood creatinine [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal pain, dyspepsia, abdominal distension, constipation, transaminases increased, rash, pruritus, and proteinuria [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Dizziness, headache, anxiety, sleep disorder, cataract, maculopathy, deafness, laryngeal pain, GI haemorrhage, gastric/duodenal ulcer, gastritis, pancreatitis, hepatitis, cholelithiasis, pigmentation disorder, pyrexia, oedema, and fatigue [Australian].
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Oesophagitis, erythema multiforme, DRESS, and optic neuritis [Australian].
- **Post-marketing/Frequency Unknown:** Hepatic failure, gastrointestinal perforation, renal tubular necrosis, tubulointerstitial nephritis, alopecia, and anaphylactic reactions [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **UGT Inducers:** Potent inducers (e.g., rifampicin, phenytoin, phenobarbital, ritonavir) can reduce deferasirox exposure by nearly 50%. Dose increases may be necessary based on clinical response [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Substrates:** Deferasirox may decrease the exposure of drugs metabolised by CYP3A4 (e.g., midazolam, ciclosporin, hormonal contraceptives) [Australian].
- **CYP2C8 Substrates:** Deferasirox increases the exposure of CYP2C8 substrates (e.g., repaglinide). Monitor blood glucose closely when co-administered with repaglinide [Australian].
- **CYP1A2 Substrates:** Exposure to CYP1A2 substrates (e.g., theophylline, clozapine, tizanidine) may increase; monitor concentrations and consider dose reductions [Australian].
- **Busulfan:** Concomitant use increases busulfan exposure; monitor plasma busulfan concentrations [Australian].
- **Aluminium Antacids:** Must not be taken concurrently due to potential interaction [Australian].
- **Cholestyramine:** May significantly decrease deferasirox efficacy [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category C. Deferasirox has shown maternotoxicity and foetal developmental impairment in animal studies at doses reaching human exposure levels. Use only if potential benefit justifies the risk [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Deferasirox was found in rat milk at higher concentrations than in plasma. Breastfeeding is not recommended during therapy [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No effect on fertility was observed in rat studies at doses below maximum human values [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is similar to adults. Children aged 2–5 years have approximately 50% lower exposure and may require higher maintenance doses. Monitor growth and sexual development annually [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of toxicity. Closely monitor for adverse effects; data for doses >30 mg/kg are limited [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** For patients with CrCl 40–60 mL/min, reduce the starting dose by 50% [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - Moderate (Child-Pugh B): Reduce starting dose by ~50%.
    - Severe (Child-Pugh C): Use is not recommended [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Orally active tridentate chelator with high affinity for Iron (III) in a 2:1 ratio. It promotes iron excretion primarily via faeces [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Tmax is 1.5 to 4 hours. Bioavailability for dispersible tablets is ~70%. Bioavailability increases with food (approximately doubled with a high-fat meal) [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (98–99%), primarily to albumin. Small volume of distribution (~14 L in adults) [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily glucuronidation by UGT1A1 and UGT1A3. Undergoes enterohepatic recycling [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily faecal (84%); renal excretion is minimal (8%). Elimination half-life (t1/2) is 8 to 16 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study 0107:** A Phase III trial in $\beta$-thalassaemia patients. While deferasirox did not achieve formal non-inferiority to desferrioxamine in the overall population, it demonstrated comparable efficacy (LIC reduction) in patients with high baseline iron burden (LIC $\ge$ 7 mg Fe/g dw) receiving 20–30 mg/kg [Australian].
- **Study 2209:** A randomized study in NTDT patients showing a significant reduction in LIC and serum ferritin compared to placebo over 52 weeks [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C.
- Protect from moisture [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** 2 years [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
