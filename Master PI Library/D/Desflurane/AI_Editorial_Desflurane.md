# Editorial Product Information: Desflurane

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulations and the most current manufacturer-provided documentation before administration.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Desflurane (a halogenated methylethylether).
- **Chemical Name:** (±) 2-difluoromethyl-1,2,2,2-tetrafluoroethyl ether.
- **Brand Name(s):** Piramal Desflurane [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N01AB07 (not explicitly stated in the source but standard for Desflurane).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Liquid for inhalation; 1 mL/mL (100% v/v active ingredient).
- **Route of Administration:** Inhalation via a specifically designed vaporiser.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Piramal Critical Care Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Maintenance of Anaesthesia:** Indicated as an inhalation agent for the maintenance of general anaesthesia in adults and paediatric patients.
- **Induction of Anaesthesia:** Not recommended for mask induction due to a high incidence of moderate to severe upper airway adverse events [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Principles
Desflurane must be administered by personnel trained in general anaesthesia using a vaporiser specifically designed and designated for its use. Due to its high vapour pressure (700 mm Hg at 20°C) and low boiling point (22.8°C), the vaporiser must utilise a heated sump to ensure stable delivery.

### Minimum Alveolar Concentration (MAC)
The MAC is age-specific and decreases as age increases. The following table provides MAC values for different ages:

| Age | 100% Oxygen (end-tidal %) | 60% N2O / 40% O2 (end-tidal %) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 0 – 1 year | 8.95 – 10.65 | 5.75 – 7.75 (for 3-12 months) |
| 1 – 12 years | 7.20 – 9.40 | 5.75 – 7.00 (for 1-5 years) |
| 18 – 30 years | 6.35 – 7.25 | 3.75 – 4.25 |
| 30 – 65 years | 5.75 – 6.25 | 1.75 – 3.25 |
| Over 65 years | 5.17 ± 0.6% | 1.67 ± 0.4% |

### Induction
- **Adults:** After induction with an intravenous agent (e.g., propofol, thiopental), Desflurane can be started at 0.5 to 1 MAC. 
- **Caution:** Mask induction is not recommended due to airway irritability (coughing, breath-holding, laryngospasm).

### Maintenance
- **Adults:** Surgical anaesthesia is generally sustained with 2–6% end-tidal concentrations when used with Nitrous Oxide (N2O) or 2.5–8.5% in O2/enriched air.
- **Paediatrics:** Surgical levels may be maintained at 5.2–10% end-tidal concentrations with or without N2O.

### Administration Instructions
- Rapid increases in concentration should be avoided to prevent transient increases in heart rate and blood pressure. Increments of 1% or less are recommended.
- Ensure moist CO2 absorbents are used to prevent carbon monoxide formation.

## 4. Contraindications
- Patients in whom general anaesthesia is contraindicated.
- Known sensitivity to halogenated anaesthetics.
- Known or genetic susceptibility to **malignant hyperthermia (MH)**.
- History of confirmed MH.
- Patients who have experienced liver dysfunction, jaundice, unexplained fever, or eosinophilia after previous halogenated anaesthetic administration.
- Use as an inhalation induction agent in paediatric patients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Malignant Hyperthermia (MH):** Desflurane can trigger a skeletal muscle hypermetabolic state. Fatal outcomes have been reported. Treatment involves discontinuation of the agent and administration of intravenous dantrolene.
- **Respiratory Irritation:** Highly irritating to the upper airway; not for mask induction.
- **Sympathetic Activation:** Rapid increases in concentration can trigger transient increases in heart rate (HR) and blood pressure (BP) through sympathetic activation. These usually resolve in 4 minutes without treatment but can be blunted by sympatholytic drugs (e.g., fentanyl, esmolol).
- **Carbon Monoxide Formation:** Interaction with dry (desiccated) CO2 absorbents (specifically Baralyme or soda lime) can produce carbon monoxide, leading to elevated carboxyhaemoglobin levels.
- **Perioperative Hyperkalaemia:** Rare cases of serum potassium elevation resulting in arrhythmias and death have occurred postoperatively, particularly in patients with latent or overt muscular dystrophies (e.g., Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy).
- **Intracranial Pressure (ICP):** May produce a dose-dependent increase in CSF/ICP in patients with space-occupying lesions. Administer at 0.8 MAC or less with hyperventilation prior to decompression.
- **QT Prolongation:** Very rare reports of QT prolongation and Torsade de Pointes.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequency is categorised by the stage of anaesthesia.

### Induction (Mask Inhalation)
- **Very Common (>10%):** Coughing (34% adults; 72% paediatrics), breath-holding (27% adults; 68% paediatrics), apnoea (15% adults), laryngospasm (50% paediatrics), oxygen desaturation (26% paediatrics), salivary hypersecretion (21% paediatrics).
- **Common (1–10%):** Laryngospasm (8% adults), pharyngitis (4% adults).

### Maintenance or Recovery
- **Very Common (>10%):** Nausea (27%), vomiting (16%).
- **Common (1–10%):** Tachycardia, hypertension, bradycardia, nodal arrhythmia, breath-holding, apnoea (7%), coughing (4%), laryngospasm (3%), headache (1%), conjunctival hyperaemia (2%).
- **Serious Post-Marketing Reactions:** Cardiac arrest, Torsade de Pointes, hepatic failure/necrosis, malignant hyperthermia, rhabdomyolysis, respiratory arrest, and coagulopathy.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Muscle Relaxants:** Desflurane significantly potentiates both non-depolarising and depolarising muscle relaxants. It reduces the ED95 of succinylcholine by ~30% and atracurium/pancuronium by ~50%. Recovery from blockade is longer than with isoflurane.
- **Opioids and Benzodiazepines:** Concomitant use reduces Desflurane MAC requirements. Fentanyl (3–6 µg/kg) can reduce MAC by 46–64%; Midazolam (25–50 µg/kg) reduces MAC by ~16%.
- **Nitrous Oxide:** Significantly reduces MAC (see Table in Section 3).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy Category:** Category B3 [Australian]. (Studies in rats/rabbits showed no teratogenicity, but increased foetal death was noted in rats at doses causing maternal toxicity).
- **Obstetric Use:** Not recommended for obstetric surgery or termination of pregnancy due to lack of data. Desflurane is a uterine relaxant and reduces uterine-placental blood flow.
- **Breastfeeding:** Not specified in available sources if excreted in human milk; use is not indicated in nursing mothers [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Contraindicated for induction. Not approved for maintenance in non-intubated children <6 years old due to respiratory risks. 
- **Paediatric Neurotoxicity:** Some animal/human studies suggest potential cognitive deficits after prolonged/repeated exposure early in life; clinical significance is not yet established.
- **Elderly Use:** MAC is significantly lower; dose should be individualised based on patient response.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Minimal metabolism (0.02%) suggests dose adjustments are not required, though concentrations of 1-4% have been used safely in these populations.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Induces a dose-related, reversible loss of consciousness and sensation by altering autonomic reflexes and depressing the CNS.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Solubility:** Very low blood/gas partition coefficient (0.42), which is lower than isoflurane (1.4) or nitrous oxide (0.46).
    - **Distribution/Elimination:** Rapid wash-in and wash-out allow for fast induction (following IV induction) and recovery. Elimination occurs primarily via the lungs.
    - **Metabolism:** Minimal; only approximately 0.02% is metabolised.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Pharmacological effect is proportional to inspired concentration. Main adverse effects are extensions of its pharmacological action (e.g., hypotension).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Ambulatory Surgery:** Multicentre studies (n=792) showed patients emerged significantly faster from Desflurane than from isoflurane.
- **Cardiovascular Surgery:** Investigated in patients undergoing CABG and peripheral vascular surgery. While it can cause sympathetic activation if increased rapidly, when used with opioids, the incidence of myocardial ischaemia was comparable to other anaesthetics.
- **Neurosurgery:** Studies in patients with intracranial lesions (n=115) indicated that maintaining MAC at 0.8 or less with hypocapnia helps maintain cerebral perfusion pressure.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Handling:** Store bottle in an upright position with the cap tightly closed. Keep in the original container until use.
- **Safety:** Piramal Desflurane is only compatible with the Piramal Fill Port Vaporiser [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
