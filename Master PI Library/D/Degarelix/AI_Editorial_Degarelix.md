# Editorial Product Information: Degarelix

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of degarelix for healthcare professionals, synthesising available regulatory data into a single clinical reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI or prescribing information required for clinical practice in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Degarelix (as acetate), a synthetic decapeptide and third-generation gonadotrophin-releasing hormone (GnRH) antagonist.
- **Brand Name:** **FIRMAGON** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L02BX02 (Not explicitly stated in source, but standard for this class).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):**
    - Powder for injection (depot): 80 mg and 120 mg vials.
    - Solvent for injection: Clear, colourless water for injections.
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) injection only. **MUST NOT** be administered intravenously.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Ferring Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Prostate Cancer:** Treatment of patients with prostate cancer in whom androgen deprivation therapy is warranted [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult Males)
- **Starting Dose:** 240 mg, administered as two subcutaneous injections of 120 mg each (concentration: 40 mg/mL).
- **Maintenance Dose (Monthly):** 80 mg, administered as one subcutaneous injection (concentration: 20 mg/mL). The first maintenance dose should be given one month after the starting dose.

### Administration Instructions
- **Location:** Administer into the abdominal region. The injection site should vary periodically. 
- **Site Care:** Avoid areas exposed to pressure (e.g., waistband, belt, ribs). The injection site **must not** be rubbed or massaged following administration to prevent altering the depot release profile.
- **Reconstitution:**
    - 120 mg vial: Reconstitute with 3 mL water for injections to achieve a 40 mg/mL concentration.
    - 80 mg vial: Reconstitute with 4.2 mL water for injections to achieve a 20 mg/mL concentration.
    - Swirl gently to dissolve; **do not shake** to avoid foam formation.
    - Administer immediately after reconstitution.

### Monitoring
- Effectiveness should be monitored via clinical parameters and serum Prostate Specific Antigen (PSA) levels.
- Confirm testosterone suppression (medical castration levels $\le$ 0.5 ng/mL) if clinical response appears sub-optimal.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to degarelix, other GnRH antagonists, or any excipients (e.g., mannitol).
- **Contraindicated in women and paediatric patients** [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **QT/QTc Interval Prolongation:** Long-term androgen deprivation therapy may prolong the QT interval. In clinical trials, ~20% of patients had QT/QTc intervals exceeding 450 msec. Use with caution in patients with a history of QT interval >450 msec, risk factors for Torsades de Pointes, or those taking Class IA or Class III antiarrhythmic medications.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Monitor patients for at least one hour post-injection to detect potential histamine-mediated reactions. Rare cases of anaphylaxis, angioedema, and urticaria have been reported post-marketing.
- **Bone Density:** Long-term testosterone suppression is associated with decreased bone density.
- **Antibody Formation:** Approximately 10% of patients develop anti-degarelix antibodies after one year. There is currently no evidence that these antibodies affect efficacy or safety.
- **Hepatic Enzymes:** Transient increases in ALT, AST, and GGT have been observed. Markedly abnormal values (>3x ULN) occurred in 2-6% of patients.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are primarily associated with the physiological effects of testosterone suppression or the subcutaneous injection site.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site reactions (pain, erythema), Hot flushes |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Weight increase, fatigue, chills, dizziness, headache, insomnia, nausea, constipation, night sweats, increased liver transaminases, hypertension, back pain, arthralgia, urinary tract infection |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Haemoglobin decreased, hypersensitivity, loss of libido, erectile dysfunction, testicular atrophy, gynaecomastia, diarrhoea, urticaria, hyperhidrosis, influenza-like illness |

- **Injection Site Reactions:** Most frequent with the starting dose (35%) compared to maintenance therapy (~3% for pain).
- **Serious Effects:** Rare reports of serious injection site reactions, including infection, abscess, or necrosis requiring surgical intervention.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No formal drug-drug interaction studies have been performed.
- **Metabolic Profile:** Degarelix is not a substrate for the human CYP450 system and does not significantly induce or inhibit CYP1A2, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 2E1, or 3A4/5 in vitro.
- **Transporters:** Degarelix is not a substrate for P-glycoprotein or other major efflux/uptake transporters.
- **Management:** Clinically significant pharmacokinetic interactions are considered unlikely.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D [Australian]**. Degarelix must not be used in pregnant women. Animal studies show embryofoetal effects, including increased abortions and premature deliveries.
- **Breastfeeding:** Not indicated for use in women; no data available on excretion into human milk.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies show reversible infertility in both sexes due to pharmacological effects.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established; not indicated for use in children.
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required. Mean age in trials was 74 years.
- **Renal Impairment:** No adjustment required for mild to moderate impairment (clearance reduced by 23% in moderate impairment). Use with caution in severe renal impairment as data are scarce.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment required for mild to moderate impairment. Use with caution in severe hepatic dysfunction as this population has not been studied.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Degarelix is a selective GnRH receptor antagonist. It binds competitively and reversibly to pituitary GnRH receptors, rapidly reducing the release of LH and FSH. This leads to an immediate reduction in testosterone and dihydrotestosterone (DHT). Unlike GnRH agonists, degarelix **does not** induce a testosterone surge/flare upon initiation.

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Forms a subcutaneous depot; released biphasically. $T_{max}$ is approximately 1.4 days.
- **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is 0.65–0.82 L/kg. Plasma protein binding is ~90%.
- **Metabolism:** Undergoes common peptidic degradation; no significant metabolites detected in plasma.
- **Elimination:** Approximately 20–30% is renally excreted (unchanged); 70–80% is excreted via the hepatobiliary system. Terminal half-life is approximately 43 days.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Study:** A 12-month, open-label, randomised Phase 3 trial compared degarelix (240/80 mg and 240/160 mg) to leuprorelin (7.5 mg).
- **Outcomes:** Degarelix 240/80 mg achieved medical castration (testosterone $\le$ 0.5 ng/mL) in 97.2% of patients from Day 28 to Day 364, compared to 96.4% for leuprorelin.
- **Rapidity:** On Day 3, 96% of degarelix patients reached castration levels compared to 0% in the leuprorelin group. Degarelix avoided the testosterone surge seen with the GnRH agonist.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Reconstituted Stability:** Chemical and physical stability demonstrated for 2 hours at 25°C. For microbiological reasons, it should be administered immediately after reconstitution.

- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian]**.
- **Legal Classification:** Not specified for other jurisdictions in available sources.
