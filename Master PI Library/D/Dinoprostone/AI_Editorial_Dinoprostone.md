# Editorial Product Information: Dinoprostone

## Editorial Note
*   This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction:
    *   **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**
*   The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
*   **Important:** This document is an editorial synthesis of the provided source and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing or dispensing in a clinical setting. 

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Dinoprostone (Prostaglandin E2, PGE2).
*   **Chemical Class:** Prostaglandins.
*   **Brand Name:** **Cervidil** [Australian].
*   **ATC Code:** G02AD02.
*   **Dosage Form and Strength:** Vaginal insert (pessary) containing 10 mg of dinoprostone. The delivery system is a thin, flat, semi-opaque, beige rectangular hydrogel matrix (29 mm x 9.5 mm x 0.8 mm) contained within a knitted polyester retrieval system [Australian].
*   **Release Rate:** Releases a mean dose of approximately 0.3 mg per hour over a 24-hour period [Australian].
*   **Route of Administration:** Vaginal.
*   **Sponsor:** Ferring Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Cervical Ripening:** Indicated for the initiation of cervical ripening in patients at or near term (from 37 completed weeks) who have favourable induction features and a medical or obstetrical indication for labour induction [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
*   **General Dosing:** A single pessary (10 mg) is inserted once. A second dose is not recommended [Australian].
*   **Administration Instructions:**
    *   **Preparation:** Remove from the freezer immediately before use; no thawing is required. The foil sachet should be torn along the mark; do not use scissors to avoid damaging the retrieval system.
    *   **Insertion:** The insert should be placed high into the posterior vaginal fornix. A small amount of water-soluble lubricant may be used.
    *   **Positioning:** The insert should be positioned transversely. The withdrawal tape may be cut, ensuring enough remains outside the vagina for retrieval.
    *   **Post-insertion:** The patient must remain recumbent for 30 minutes following insertion.
*   **Removal:** The insert must be removed by gentle traction on the retrieval tape upon:
    *   Onset of labour (regular painful contractions every 3 minutes).
    *   Spontaneous rupture of membranes or amniotomy.
    *   Evidence of uterine hyperstimulation or hypertonic contractions.
    *   Evidence of fetal distress or maternal systemic adverse effects (nausea, vomiting, hypotension, tachycardia).
    *   At least 30 minutes prior to starting other uterotonic agents (e.g., oxytocin).
    *   Completion of 24 hours of treatment if ripening is insufficient.

## 4. Contraindications
**Cervidil** is contraindicated in the following circumstances [Australian]:
*   Known hypersensitivity to dinoprostone or any excipients (e.g., urethane).
*   Multifetal pregnancy or non-vertex presentation.
*   Cases where labour has already started.
*   Concurrent use of uterotonic agents or other labour-inducing agents (or if they are to be administered within 30 minutes of removal).
*   Conditions where strong, prolonged uterine contractions are inappropriate, including:
    *   Previous major uterine surgery (e.g., Caesarean section, myomectomy).
    *   Previous cervical surgery or cervical rupture.
    *   Cephalopelvic disproportion.
    *   Fetal malpresentation.
*   Suspicion or evidence of fetal distress (e.g., abnormal cardiotocography).
*   Active pelvic inflammatory disease (unless adequately treated).
*   Conditions where vaginal delivery is not indicated (e.g., placenta praevia, active herpes genitalis).
*   Unexplained vaginal bleeding during the current pregnancy.
*   Suspected fetal compromise.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Clinical Setting:** Must only be administered by trained obstetrical personnel in a hospital setting with facilities for continuous fetal and uterine monitoring [Australian].
*   **Uterine Rupture:** Uterine rupture has been reported, particularly in patients with contraindicated conditions (e.g., previous uterine surgery). The insert must be removed immediately if uterine hypertonus or prolonged contractions occur [Australian].
*   **Uterotonic Potentiation:** Prostaglandins potentiate uterotonic medicines. Ensure a minimum 30-minute interval between removal of the insert and the start of oxytocin [Australian].
*   **Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC):** Increased risk of post-partum DIC in women aged 35 or older, those with pregnancy complications (gestational diabetes, hypotension, hypothyroidism), or gestational age >40 weeks. Monitor for early signs of fibrinolysis [Australian].
*   **Amniotic Fluid Embolism:** Use may rarely result in abruption of the placenta and subsequent Anaphylactoid Syndrome of Pregnancy (Amniotic Fluid Embolism) [Australian].
*   **Special Clinical Cautions:** Use with caution in patients with:
    *   Cervical (Bishop) scores of 8 or more.
    *   Glaucoma, asthma, or epilepsy.
    *   History of uterine hypertony.
    *   Ruptured membranes (limited experience; release rate may be affected).
    *   Grand multiparity (more than three full-term deliveries).

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common (≥ 1/100 to < 1/10)
*   **Cardiac:** Fetal heart rate disorder (includes bradycardia, tachycardia, decelerations).
*   **Pregnancy/Perinatal:** Abnormal labour affecting the fetus (expression of hyperstimulation syndrome), uterine tachysystole, uterine hypertonus, meconium in amniotic fluid.

### Uncommon (≥ 1/1,000 to < 1/100)
*   **Nervous System:** Headache.
*   **Vascular:** Hypotension.
*   **Respiratory:** Neonatal respiratory distress-related conditions.
*   **Hepatobiliary:** Neonatal hyperbilirubinaemia.
*   **Skin:** Pruritus.
*   **Reproductive:** Vulvovaginal burning sensation, postpartum haemorrhage, premature separation of placenta, uterine atony, chorioamnionitis.
*   **Neonatal:** Low Apgar score, arrested labour.
*   **General:** Febrile disorders.

### Post-Marketing Experience (Frequency Unknown)
*   **Serious Effects:** Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC), Anaphylactic reaction, Anaphylactoid syndrome of pregnancy, Uterine rupture, Fetal death/stillbirth.
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Uterotonic Agents:** Concurrent use is not recommended due to potentiation of effects. A 30-minute waiting period is required after removal of the insert before starting agents like oxytocin [Australian].
*   **NSAIDs:** Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, including aspirin, should be discontinued prior to administration [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   **Australian Category C:** Prostaglandin E2 has shown embryotoxicity and skeletal anomalies in animal studies. Idiosyncratic uterine sensitivity resulting in fetal anoxia is possible.
    *   The product is only indicated for cervical ripening at term when induction is medically necessary; it is not for use in early pregnancy.
*   **Breastfeeding:** Not indicated for use during lactation. While dinoprostone may be excreted in colostrum and breast milk, levels are expected to be very limited and are not considered to hinder breastfeeding [Australian].
*   **Fertility:** No fertility studies have been conducted [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in pregnant women under 18 years of age have not been established. No data available [Australian].
*   **Elderly Use:** No data available [Australian].
*   **Renal/Hepatic/Pulmonary Impairment:** Use has not been specifically studied in these populations. Since these organs are involved in the metabolism/excretion of PGE2, use in these patients is not recommended [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Dinoprostone (PGE2) is a local hormone that triggers cervical ripening. It stimulates the enzyme collagenase, which digests the structural collagen network of the cervix, transforming it from a rigid structure to a softened, dilated state to allow fetal passage [Australian].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption:** Continuously released at ~0.3 mg/hour.
    *   **Distribution:** Approximately 73% bound to human plasma albumin.
    *   **Metabolism:** Rapidly metabolised in the lungs, kidneys, and liver (90% first-pass metabolism). Primary metabolite is 13,14-dihydro-15-keto PGE2.
    *   **Elimination:** Eliminated very rapidly from circulation (half-life <1 minute for parent drug; <10 minutes for primary metabolite). Excreted via the urine [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **12-Hour Insertion Trials:** Three randomised, double-blind trials (including Study 101-801) showed significantly higher treatment success (Bishop score increase or vaginal delivery within 12 hours) and reduced time to delivery compared to placebo for both primiparous and multiparous women [Australian].
*   **24-Hour Insertion Trials (EXPEDITE Study):** Comparison of **Cervidil** 10 mg with misoprostol 200 mcg. Approximately 60% of subjects required ripening beyond 12 hours. **Cervidil** demonstrated consistent efficacy in time to vaginal delivery and cervical ripening success at 24 hours (86.8%) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Storage Temperature:** Store in a freezer below -18°C [Australian].
*   **Handling:** Product should be inserted immediately after removal from the freezer.
*   **Shelf Life Nuance:** Controlled periods of up to one month at 2–8°C are permitted within the overall shelf life. Exposure to high humidity must be avoided as it may alter release characteristics [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
