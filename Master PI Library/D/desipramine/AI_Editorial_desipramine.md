# Editorial Product Information: Desipramine Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of desipramine hydrochloride, intended for use by healthcare professionals as a clinically accurate reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the United States (FDA) PI was provided for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Desipramine hydrochloride, a tricyclic antidepressant (TCA) of the dibenzazepine class. It is a secondary amine.
- **Brand Names:** Available as a generic (Amneal Pharmaceuticals) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Film-coated tablets:** 10 mg, 25 mg, 50 mg, 75 mg, 100 mg, and 150 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Amneal Pharmaceuticals NY LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Depression:** Indicated for the treatment of depression [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):**
    - The standard range is 100 mg to 200 mg daily [US].
    - For severely ill patients, the dose may be increased gradually to a maximum of 300 mg daily [US].
    - Dosages exceeding 300 mg/day are not recommended [US].
- **Adolescent and Geriatric Dosing:**
    - The typical range is 25 mg to 100 mg daily [US].
    - For severely ill patients in these age groups, the dose may be increased to a maximum of 150 mg daily [US].
    - Dosages exceeding 150 mg/day are not recommended in these populations [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Therapy should be initiated at a low level and titrated upward based on clinical response and tolerance [US].
    - Initial therapy may be administered in divided doses or as a single daily dose [US].
    - Maintenance therapy may be given as a once-daily dose to improve compliance [US].
- **Transitioning from/to MAOIs:**
    - At least 14 days must elapse between discontinuing a Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitor (MAOI) and initiating desipramine [US].
    - At least 14 days must elapse after stopping desipramine before initiating an MAOI [US].
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **MAOI Use:** Concomitant use with MAOIs (including linezolid or intravenous methylene blue) is contraindicated due to the risk of serotonin syndrome [US].
- **Post-Myocardial Infarction:** Contraindicated during the acute recovery period following a myocardial infarction [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to desipramine or cross-sensitivity to other dibenzazepines [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Suicidality and Antidepressants [US]**
    - Antidepressants increase the risk of suicidal thinking and behavior (suicidality) in children, adolescents, and young adults (ages 18–24) during short-term studies of Major Depressive Disorder (MDD).
    - Patients of all ages should be monitored closely for clinical worsening, suicidality, or unusual changes in behavior, particularly during the first few months of therapy or during dose adjustments.
    - Desipramine is not approved for pediatric use [US].
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Potentially life-threatening; risk is increased with concomitant use of other serotonergic agents (e.g., SSRIs, SNRIs, triptans, fentanyl, lithium, St. John’s Wort) [US].
- **Cardiovascular Risk:** Extreme caution is required in patients with cardiovascular disease (conduction defects, arrhythmias, tachycardias, stroke, or MI) or a family history of sudden death or cardiac dysrhythmias [US].
- **Bipolar Disorder Screening:** Patients should be screened for bipolar disorder before starting treatment to avoid precipitating a mixed/manic episode [US].
- **Seizure Threshold:** Desipramine may lower the seizure threshold; use with caution in patients with a history of seizure disorders [US].
- **Angle-Closure Glaucoma:** Mydriasis associated with desipramine may trigger an angle-closure attack in patients with anatomically narrow angles [US].
- **Hyponatremia:** May occur, often due to SIADH. Elderly patients and those on diuretics are at higher risk [US].
- **Anticholinergic Effects:** Caution in patients with a history of urinary retention or glaucoma [US].
- **Hematologic Monitoring:** Leukocyte and differential counts should be performed if a patient develops fever and sore throat [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Note: Frequencies (e.g., "Very Common") are not explicitly provided in the source PI. The following are grouped by system.*

- **Cardiovascular:** Hypotension, hypertension, palpitations, heart block, myocardial infarction, stroke, arrhythmias, tachycardia, sudden death [US].
- **Psychiatric:** Confusional states (especially in the elderly), hallucinations, disorientation, anxiety, restlessness, insomnia, nightmares, hypomania, exacerbation of psychosis [US].
- **Neurologic:** Paresthesias, incoordination, ataxia, tremors, peripheral neuropathy, seizures, tinnitus, extrapyramidal symptoms. Symptoms of Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome have been reported [US].
- **Anticholinergic:** Dry mouth, sublingual adenitis, blurred vision, mydriasis, constipation, paralytic ileus, urinary retention [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Anorexia, nausea, vomiting, epigastric distress, diarrhea, jaundice, elevated liver function tests [US].
- **Endocrine:** Gynecomastia (male), breast enlargement and galactorrhea (female), libido changes, impotence, blood sugar fluctuations, SIADH [US].
- **Hematologic:** Agranulocytosis, eosinophilia, purpura, thrombocytopenia [US].
- **Other:** Weight changes, perspiration, flushing, urinary frequency, alopecia, drowsiness, dizziness, proneness to falling [US].
- **Withdrawal Symptoms:** Nausea, headache, and malaise may occur upon abrupt cessation after prolonged therapy [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP2D6 Inhibitors:** Drugs that inhibit CYP2D6 (e.g., quinidine, cimetidine, SSRIs like fluoxetine and sertraline, phenothiazines, Type 1C antiarrhythmics) can significantly increase desipramine plasma levels, leading to toxicity [US].
- **MAOIs:** Risk of severe serotonin syndrome and hyperpyretic crises. 14-day washout required [US].
- **Alcohol:** Response to alcoholic beverages may be exaggerated; potentiation of overdose risk [US].
- **CNS Depressants:** Additive sedative effects with benzodiazepines and other tranquilizers [US].
- **Anticholinergics/Sympathomimetics:** Additive effects require close supervision and dose adjustment [US].
- **Guanethidine:** Desipramine may block the antihypertensive effects of guanethidine and similar compounds [US].
- **Tobacco:** May induce liver enzymes and reduce desipramine plasma levels [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not explicitly assigned a letter category in the provided text [US].
    - Safe use during pregnancy has not been established. Benefits must be weighed against potential hazards to the mother and fetus.
    - There is a pregnancy exposure registry for antidepressants (1-844-405-6185) [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safe use during lactation has not been established; potential risks must be weighed against benefits [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal reproductive studies have been inconclusive [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established. Not recommended for use in children. Reports of sudden death in pediatric patients have occurred [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Lower dosages are recommended. The ratio of 2-hydroxydesipramine to desipramine may be increased due to decreased renal elimination. Higher risk of falling, confusional states, and hyponatremia [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Desipramine is substantially excreted by the kidney. Risk of toxic reactions may be higher; renal function monitoring is advised in patients with impairment [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Desipramine is metabolized in the liver. Although specific dose adjustments by severity are not listed, the source notes the importance of hepatic metabolism [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Tricyclic antidepressant that primarily blocks the re-uptake of norepinephrine in the central nervous system. It has a lesser effect on serotonin re-uptake compared to tertiary amine TCAs [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolized in the liver. Metabolism rate varies significantly among individuals (up to 36-fold difference) due to genetic polymorphisms in CYP2D6 [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 70% is excreted in the urine [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Onset of action may be seen in 2 to 5 days, but full therapeutic benefit typically requires 2 to 3 weeks [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Not specified in available sources beyond references to pooled analyses of short-term placebo-controlled trials for suicidality risk assessment [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container with child-resistant closures. Protect from excessive heat [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only (Prescription only) [US].
