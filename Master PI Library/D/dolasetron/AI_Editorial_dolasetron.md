# Editorial Product Information: Dolasetron

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: US (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of dolasetron for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Dolasetron mesylate (a highly specific and selective serotonin subtype 3 (5-HT₃) receptor antagonist).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Anzemet** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 50 mg [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Validus Pharmaceuticals LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chemotherapy-Induced Nausea and Vomiting (CINV):** Prevention of nausea and vomiting associated with moderately emetogenic cancer chemotherapy. This includes initial and repeat courses in both adult and paediatric patients aged 2 years and older [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** 100 mg administered as a single dose.
- **Dosing by Indication (CINV Prevention):**
    - **Adults:** 100 mg orally within one hour prior to chemotherapy [US].
    - **Paediatric Patients (2 to 16 years):** 1.8 mg/kg orally, up to a maximum dose of 100 mg, administered within one hour prior to chemotherapy [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - For children unable to swallow tablets or for whom 100 mg is inappropriate, the injection solution may be mixed with apple or apple-grape juice for oral administration [US].
    - The diluted product is stable for up to 2 hours at room temperature [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources (typically administered as a single dose prior to treatment).

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to dolasetron mesylate or any component of the formulation [US].
- **Specific Route Contraindication:** Intravenous administration of dolasetron is contraindicated in both adult and paediatric patients for the prevention of CINV due to the risk of dose-dependent QT prolongation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **QT Interval Prolongation:** Dolasetron prolongs the QT interval in a dose-dependent manner. Torsade de Pointes has been reported post-marketing. Avoid use in patients with congenital long QT syndrome, hypokalemia, or hypomagnesemia [US].
- **PR and QRS Interval Prolongation:** Dose-dependent prolongation of PR and QRS intervals may occur, potentially leading to second or third-degree heart block, cardiac arrest, or fatal ventricular arrhythmias. Use with extreme caution in patients with structural heart disease, sick sinus syndrome, or atrial fibrillation with slow ventricular response [US].
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Reported with 5-HT₃ antagonists, particularly when used concomitantly with other serotonergic drugs (e.g., SSRIs, SNRIs, MAOIs, lithium). Symptoms include mental status changes, autonomic instability, and neuromuscular hyperactivity [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:**
    - Correct hypokalemia and hypomagnesemia prior to administration [US].
    - ECG monitoring is recommended for elderly patients, patients with renal impairment, and those with underlying cardiac conduction abnormalities [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequencies from controlled clinical trials in adults receiving chemotherapy:

| Adverse Effect | Frequency (25 mg dose) | Frequency (100 mg dose) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Headache** | 17.9% | 22.9% |
| **Fatigue** | 2.6% | 5.7% |
| **Diarrhea** | 2.1% | 5.3% |
| **Bradycardia** | 5.1% | 4.0% |
| **Dizziness** | 1.3% | 3.1% |
| **Pain** | 0% | 3.1% |
| **Tachycardia** | 3.0% | 2.6% |
| **Dyspepsia** | 3.0% | 2.2% |
| **Chills/Shivering** | 1.3% | 2.2% |

- **Other Serious/Notable Effects (<2%):** Hypotension, Mobitz I AV block, myocardial ischemia, syncope, anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, and transient increases in liver transaminases (AST/ALT) [US].
- **Post-marketing:** Reports of wide complex tachycardia, ventricular fibrillation, and cardiac arrest following IV administration [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **Cimetidine:** Concomitant use increases hydrodolasetron (active metabolite) AUC by 24% [US].
    - **Rifampin:** Concomitant use decreases hydrodolasetron AUC by 28% [US].
    - **Atenolol:** Clearance of hydrodolasetron is decreased by approximately 27% when administered with IV dolasetron [US].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **Serotonergic Drugs:** Increased risk of Serotonin Syndrome when used with SSRIs, SNRIs, or other serotonergic agents [US].
    - **ECG-Prolonging Drugs:** Exercise caution when co-administered with drugs that prolong the QT, PR, or QRS intervals (e.g., flecainide, quinidine, verapamil) [US].
    - **Diuretics:** Potential for electrolyte imbalances that increase arrhythmia risk [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not lettered, but indicates the drug should be used only if clearly needed]. Teratology studies in rats (100 mg/kg/day) and rabbits (100 mg/kg/day) showed no evidence of fetal harm or impaired fertility [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if dolasetron is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution when administering to nursing women [US].
- **Fertility:** No effect on fertility or reproductive performance was observed in animal models at doses up to 8–32 times the recommended human dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established in patients $\ge$ 2 years for CINV prevention. Not established in children < 2 years. Clearances are significantly higher in children than adults [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required for patients > 65 years, but ECG monitoring is recommended due to higher risk of conduction interval prolongation [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required, but ECG monitoring is recommended for patients with severe renal impairment (CrCl $\le$ 10 mL/min) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is recommended for patients with hepatic impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selective serotonin 5-HT₃ receptor antagonist. Nausea and vomiting from chemotherapy are thought to be triggered by serotonin release from enterochromaffin cells, which activates vagal 5-HT₃ receptors. Dolasetron and its metabolite block these receptors peripherally and centrally [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; parent drug is rarely detected due to immediate metabolism to hydrodolasetron. Absolute bioavailability is ~75% [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily reduced by carbonyl reductase to the active metabolite, hydrodolasetron. Further metabolism occurs via CYP2D6 (hydroxylation) and CYP3A/flavin monooxygenase (N-oxidation) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Hydrodolasetron has a half-life of 8.1 hours. Two-thirds is excreted in urine and one-third in feces [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Prolongs PR, QRS, and QTcF intervals in a dose-dependent manner [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **CINV Prevention:** Two double-blind trials in patients receiving moderately emetogenic chemotherapy (e.g., cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin) showed that a 100 mg oral dose was effective. Complete response (no vomiting, no rescue meds) was achieved in 61% to 73% of patients across studies [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Special Handling:** Protect from light [US].
