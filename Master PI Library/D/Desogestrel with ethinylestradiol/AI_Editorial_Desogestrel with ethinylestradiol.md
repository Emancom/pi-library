# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   The jurisdictions contributing to this editorial synthesis include: **US (FDA)**.
*   This document serves as a harmonized editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference. 
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labeling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
*   **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
*   **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
*   **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    *   **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
*   **Carnitine Deficiency in End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
*   **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. This should be administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
    *   *Severe Metabolic Crisis:* A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
    *   *Maintenance:* Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilized [US].
*   **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
    *   *Administration:* Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
    *   *Dose Adjustments:* Adjustments (e.g., reduction to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
*   Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [US].
*   **Compatibility:** Levocarnitine injection is stable when mixed in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL. Mixed solutions are stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags [US].

### Monitoring
*   Prior to therapy, obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations [US].
*   Monitor blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) weekly or monthly [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   **Known Hypersensitivity:** None specifically listed as absolute contraindications in the US PI, but serious reactions require discontinuation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occurred within minutes of IV administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks/benefits before re-administration [US].
*   **Seizures:** Both new-onset seizures and increased seizure frequency/severity have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity receiving levocarnitine [US].
*   **TMA/TMAO Accumulation:** While primarily a concern with oral administration, clinicians should note that in patients with severely compromised renal function, high doses of levocarnitine may lead to the accumulation of metabolites trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

### Serious and Other Adverse Effects
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent: gastritis and body odor [US].
*   **Neurologic:** Seizures (post-marketing) [US].
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Clinical Note:** Monitor INR levels at the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   **Category:** Not specified by letter, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits (up to 3.8x human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm [US].
    *   **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased milk concentrations following exogenous administration. Weigh the benefits to the mother against potential risks to the infant; consider discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
*   **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Dosing guidelines for metabolic disorders apply to pediatric patients [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources.
*   **Renal Impairment:** The drug is indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. However, the safety/efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency, due to potential metabolite (TMA/TMAO) accumulation [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters that are quickly excreted [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
*   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
*   **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; the mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (20 mg/kg post-dialysis), mean pre-dialysis trough concentrations increased from 21.3 µmol/L at baseline to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While levocarnitine concentrations increased significantly, the direct effect on clinical outcomes in this population was not determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C [US].
*   **Precautions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat. Discard unused portions of opened vials as they contain no preservatives [US].
