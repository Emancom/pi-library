# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine (Intravenous)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the **United States (FDA)** regulatory authority. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official PI for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the US (FDA) source was available for analysis; therefore, jurisdictional comparisons are limited to those provided in the source documentation.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (generic name); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
- **Loading Dose:** Often utilised in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Doses should be administered every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest administered dose reported is 300 mg/kg.

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Titration:** Dose adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations.

### Monitoring and Administration Instructions
- **Plasma Monitoring:** Trough plasma carnitine levels should be obtained prior to therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended. The target plasma free carnitine concentration is between 35 and 60 µmol/L.
- **Visual Inspection:** Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration before use.
- **Stability:** Compatible with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL concentration). Stable for 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs.
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been reported.
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulation Note):** While this PI concerns the injection, clinicians should note that high-dose *oral* levocarnitine is not evaluated in renal insufficiency due to the potential accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO).

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common and Clinical Trial Observations
- **General:** Transient nausea and vomiting are frequently observed. Less frequent reactions include body odor and gastritis.
- **ESRD Trial Data (≥5% frequency):**
    *   **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (9%), headache (22%), injection site reaction (33%), chest pain (12%), pain (30%), fever (7%).
    *   **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%).
    *   **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%).
    *   **Neurological:** Dizziness (13%), paresthesia (5%), insomnia (4%).
    *   **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels closely after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin anticoagulation.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not assigned, but descriptive]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits (up to 3.8 times human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been specifically studied in nursing mothers. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. The risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against maternal benefit. Consideration may be given to discontinuing nursing or the drug.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders in children is consistent with adult weight-based dosing (50 mg/kg).
- **Geriatric Use:** Not specifically detailed in the provided source; clinical monitoring is advised as per standard protocols.
- **Renal Impairment:** The injection is specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. Trough levels should guide downward titration to prevent excessive accumulation.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    *   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urinary) and γ-butyrobetaine (faecal).
    *   **Elimination:** Primarily renal; approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, levocarnitine increases plasma carnitine concentrations and may alleviate metabolic abnormalities, though effects on clinical outcomes in this specific population have not been fully determined.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Dose-Ranging Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x/week), mean pre-dialysis trough concentrations increased from 21.3 µmol/L at baseline to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Extended Studies:** 12- and 24-week studies confirmed that trough levels remain stable or increase over time, with higher doses (40 mg/kg) resulting in significantly higher trough concentrations (mean 456 µmol/L at 24 weeks).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C.
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservative.
