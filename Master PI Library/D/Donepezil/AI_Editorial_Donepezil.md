# Editorial Product Information: Donepezil

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- This document is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing in any particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Donepezil hydrochloride monohydrate (a piperidine-based reversible inhibitor of acetylcholinesterase).
- **Brand Name(s)**: APO-Donepezil [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s)**: 
    - 5 mg film-coated tablets (white to off-white, circular, biconvex).
    - 10 mg film-coated tablets (yellow, circular, biconvex).
- **Route(s) of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- Treatment of Alzheimer’s disease at mild, moderate, and severe stages of progression [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing**: Treatment must be initiated and overseen by a clinician experienced in Alzheimer’s dementia management.
- **Titration Schedule**: 
    - **Initial Dose**: 5 mg once daily.
    - **Assessment**: The 5 mg dose should be maintained for at least one month to reach steady-state concentrations and assess early clinical response.
    - **Escalation**: Following a one-month assessment, the dose may be increased to 10 mg once daily.
- **Maximum Dose**: 10 mg/day. Doses exceeding this limit have not been evaluated in clinical trials [Australian].
- **Administration**: Tablets should be taken orally in the evening, immediately prior to retiring. Administration is independent of food intake.
- **Discontinuation**: Beneficial effects abate gradually upon cessation. There is no evidence of a rebound effect following abrupt withdrawal. Discontinuation should be considered when therapeutic benefit is no longer evident.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **donepezil hydrochloride**, piperidine derivatives, or any excipients used in the formulation (including lactose).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Anaesthesia**: As a cholinesterase inhibitor, donepezil is likely to potentiate succinylcholine-type muscle relaxation during anaesthesia.
- **Cardiovascular Conditions**: Due to vagotonic actions, donepezil may induce bradycardia or supraventricular cardiac conduction disorders (e.g., sinoatrial or atrioventricular block). Caution is required in patients with "sick sinus syndrome," pre-existing cardiac disease (uncompensated heart failure, recent MI), or electrolyte disturbances (hypokalaemia, hypomagnesaemia).
- **QTc Prolongation**: Post-marketing reports include QTc interval prolongation and Torsade de Pointes. Clinical monitoring (ECG) may be required in patients with a family history of QTc prolongation or those taking concurrent QTc-prolonging medications.
- **Gastrointestinal Conditions**: Cholinergic activity may increase gastric acid secretion. Patients at risk for ulcers (e.g., history of ulcer disease or concurrent NSAID use) should be monitored for occult gastrointestinal bleeding.
- **Neurological Conditions**:
    - **Seizures**: Cholinomimetics may cause generalised convulsions, though seizures may also be a manifestation of Alzheimer’s disease.
    - **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS)**: Very rare reports of NMS have occurred, characterized by hyperthermia, muscle rigidity, and autonomic instability. Discontinue immediately if NMS is suspected.
- **Pulmonary Conditions**: Prescribe with caution to patients with a history of asthma or obstructive pulmonary disease.
- **Aggressive Behaviour**: Caution is advised in patients with severe Alzheimer's disease who are at risk of aggression.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Most adverse events are mild and transient, often resolving within 1–2 days of continued use. Frequency may be influenced by the rate of dose titration.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Diarrhoea, nausea, headache. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Muscle cramps, fatigue, vomiting, insomnia, dizziness, pain, accident, common cold, anorexia, ecchymosis, weight decrease, hallucinations, agitation, aggressive behaviour, syncope. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Seizures, bradycardia, gastrointestinal haemorrhage, gastric/duodenal ulcers, minor increases in serum creatine kinase. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Heart block, sinoatrial block, NMS, hepatitis, hyponatraemia, pancreatitis. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects**: Cardiac conduction disorders (AV block, QTc prolongation, Torsade de Pointes), NMS, and rhabdomyolysis.
- **Vascular Dementia Note**: In studies of patients with vascular dementia, a numerically higher mortality rate was observed (1.7% vs 1.1% placebo), though this was not statistically significant and largely attributed to vascular causes.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions**: 
    - Avoid concomitant use with other acetylcholinesterase inhibitors, cholinergic agonists, or antagonists.
    - Synergistic effects may occur with succinylcholine, neuromuscular blocking agents, or beta-blockers affecting cardiac conduction.
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions**:
    - **CYP450 Inhibitors**: Potent inhibitors of CYP3A4 (e.g., ketoconazole, erythromycin) and CYP2D6 (e.g., quinidine, fluoxetine) may inhibit donepezil metabolism. Ketoconazole increases donepezil concentrations by ~30% [Australian].
    - **CYP450 Inducers**: Phenytoin, carbamazepine, rifampicin, and phenobarbital may increase the elimination rate of donepezil.
- **Protein Binding**: Donepezil is 96% plasma protein bound. In vitro studies suggest it does not displace highly bound drugs like warfarin, digoxin, or frusemide.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **Category B3 [Australian]**: Animal studies (rats/rabbits) showed no teratogenicity, but some increased stillbirths and reduced pup survival at high doses. Use only if the potential benefit outweighs the fetal risk.
- **Breastfeeding**: Excretion into human milk is unknown. Excretion occurs in rat milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended while taking donepezil.
- **Fertility**: No effect on fertility was observed in rat studies at doses up to 10 mg/kg/day.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Not recommended for use in children [Australian].
- **Elderly Use**: No specific dose adjustments are required beyond standard titration; however, caution is advised regarding cardiovascular and falls risk.
- **Renal Impairment**: Clearance is not significantly affected; a similar dosing schedule may be followed.
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Clearance is not significantly affected; a similar dosing schedule may be followed.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Donepezil is a specific, reversible inhibitor of acetylcholinesterase (AChE), the primary enzyme responsible for acetylcholine hydrolysis in the brain. It is >1000 times more selective for AChE than butyrylcholinesterase.
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Relative bioavailability is 100%. Peak plasma levels ($T_{max}$) are reached in 3–4 hours. Pharmacokinetics are linear.
    - **Distribution**: Steady-state volume of distribution is 12 L/kg. It is 96% bound to human plasma proteins.
    - **Metabolism**: Extensively metabolised by CYP3A4 and CYP2D6, followed by glucuronidation. Two of its four major metabolites are active.
    - **Elimination**: Terminal disposition half-life is approximately 70 hours. Roughly 57% is recovered in urine and 15% in faeces over 10 days.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Mild to Moderate Alzheimer's**: Effectiveness was established in 15- and 30-week placebo-controlled trials. Donepezil showed statistically significant improvements in cognitive function (measured by ADAS-cog) and global clinical assessment (CIBIC plus).
- **Severe Alzheimer's**: Three randomised, double-blind studies (24–26 weeks) demonstrated significant benefits in cognition (Severe Impairment Battery) and activities of daily living (ADCS-ADL-severe).
- **Long-term Efficacy**: 54-week studies indicated that donepezil significantly delayed functional decline compared to placebo. The hazard ratio for clinically evident functional decline was 0.62 [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 30°C.
- Maintain in the original container to protect from light/moisture.
- Shelf life is specified on the external packaging.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule**: Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
