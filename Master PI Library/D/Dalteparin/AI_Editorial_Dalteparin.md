# Editorial Product Information: Dalteparin Sodium

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from Australia (TGA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of dalteparin sodium for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial resource and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in specific jurisdictions. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Dalteparin sodium (a low molecular weight heparin).
- **Brand Name:** **Fragmin** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** B01AB04 (Not specified in available source, but commonly classified as such).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
  - Prefilled syringes: 2,500 IU/0.2 mL, 5,000 IU/0.2 mL, 7,500 IU/0.75 mL, 10,000 IU/1 mL, 12,500 IU/0.5 mL, 15,000 IU/0.6 mL, and 18,000 IU/0.72 mL [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous (s.c.) injection or intravenous (i.v.) infusion. Intramuscular administration is strictly contraindicated.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Surgical Prophylaxis:** Prevention of thromboembolic complications in the peri- or post-operative period.
- **Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT):** Treatment of acute DVT.
- **Venous Thromboembolism (VTE) in Cancer:** Extended treatment of symptomatic VTE (proximal DVT and/or pulmonary embolism) to reduce recurrence in patients with solid tumor malignancies [Australian].
- **Unstable Coronary Artery Disease:** Treatment of unstable angina and non-ST-elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI).
- **Haemodialysis:** Prophylaxis against thrombotic complications in the extracorporeal system during haemodialysis.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Surgical Prophylaxis
- **Standard Risk:** 2,500 IU s.c. 1–2 hours before surgery, followed by 2,500 IU every morning until the patient is mobilised (typically 5–7 days).
- **High Risk (e.g., Malignancy):** 5,000 IU s.c. the evening before surgery, followed by 5,000 IU every evening thereafter. Alternatively, 2,500 IU s.c. 1–2 hours before surgery and 2,500 IU 12 hours later, followed by 5,000 IU daily.
- **Orthopaedic Surgery (e.g., Hip Replacement):** 5,000 IU s.c. the evening before surgery and every evening post-operatively for 5 weeks.

### Acute Deep Vein Thrombosis
- Administered either as twice-daily s.c. injections (100 IU/kg) or as a continuous i.v. infusion (100 IU/kg over 12 hours).

### Extended Treatment of VTE in Cancer
- **Month 1:** 200 IU/kg s.c. once daily (maximum 18,000 IU daily).
- **Months 2–6:** Approximately 150 IU/kg s.c. once daily (maximum 18,000 IU daily).

### Unstable Coronary Artery Disease (UA/NSTEMI)
- 120 IU/kg s.c. every 12 hours (maximum 10,000 IU per 12 hours) for 6 days. Concurrent low-dose aspirin is recommended.

### Haemodialysis
- **Chronic Renal Failure (No bleeding risk):** I.v. bolus of 30–40 IU/kg followed by i.v. infusion of 10–15 IU/kg/hour; OR a single bolus of 5,000 IU for sessions <4 hours.
- **Acute Renal Failure (High bleeding risk):** I.v. bolus of 5–10 IU/kg followed by i.v. infusion of 4–5 IU/kg/hour.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to dalteparin, other low molecular weight heparins (LMWHs), heparins, or pork products.
- **History of HIT:** Confirmed or suspected immunologically mediated heparin-induced thrombocytopenia.
- **Active Bleeding:** Clinically significant bleeding (e.g., gastrointestinal ulcer, cerebral haemorrhage).
- **Coagulation Disorders:** Severe systemic coagulation disorders.
- **Septic Endocarditis:** Acute or sub-acute presentations.
- **Regional Anaesthesia:** Contraindicated when dalteparin is used in high therapeutic doses (e.g., for DVT treatment or UA/NSTEMI) due to risk of neuraxial haematoma.
- **Specific Injuries/Surgery:** Recent injury or surgery involving the brain, spinal cord, eye, or ears.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Neuraxial Haematoma:** Patients undergoing spinal/epidural anaesthesia or spinal puncture are at risk of developing an epidural or spinal haematoma, which can lead to permanent paralysis. Risk increases with indwelling catheters or concomitant NSAIDs/antiplatelet agents.
- **Not Interchangeable:** Dalteparin is not unit-for-unit interchangeable with unfractionated heparin or other LMWHs.
- **Thrombocytopenia:** Platelet counts should be measured before starting treatment and monitored regularly. If platelet counts fall below 100,000/µL, HIT must be excluded.
- **Intracranial Bleeding:** Use with extreme caution in patients with brain tumours due to the risk of fatal haemorrhage.
- **Hyperkalaemia:** Heparin products can suppress adrenal aldosterone secretion. Monitor potassium in patients at risk (e.g., diabetes, chronic renal failure).
- **Latex Allergy:** The needle shield of certain prefilled syringes may contain natural rubber latex.

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common** (≥1/100 to <1/10) | Haemorrhage, reversible non-immunological thrombocytopenia (Type I), transient elevation of liver transaminases (AST, ALT), injection site haematoma. |
| **Uncommon** (≥1/1,000 to <1/100) | Allergic reactions, hyperkalaemia, rash, pruritus, urticaria, osteoporosis (with long-term use), injection site pain. |
| **Rare** (≥1/10,000 to <1/1,000) | Heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (Type II), skin necrosis, alopecia, fever. |
| **Unknown** | Increased serum creatinine, intracranial/retroperitoneal bleeds (post-marketing). |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Potentiating Effects:** Anticoagulant effect is enhanced by thrombolytic agents, aspirin, NSAIDs, vitamin K antagonists, platelet inhibitors (dipyridamole, glycoprotein IIb/IIIa antagonists), dextran, and cytostatics.
- **Antagonising Effects:** Antihistamines, digitalis glycosides, tetracycline, and ascorbic acid may reduce the effect of dalteparin.
- **Andexanet alfa:** Concurrent use may reduce the effectiveness of dalteparin.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **Category C** [Australian]: Not known to cause malformations, but heparin use is associated with maternal risks (osteoporosis, thrombocytopenia) and potential foetal loss/prematurity due to haemorrhage.
  - **Prosthetic Heart Valves:** Dalteparin is **not approved** for thromboprophylaxis in pregnant women with prosthetic heart valves due to high risks of maternal/foetal death.
- **Breastfeeding:** Dalteparin is excreted in breast milk in small amounts (milk/plasma ratio <0.025–0.224). Clinical implications for the infant are likely low due to poor oral absorption of LMWH.
- **Fertility:** No data available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended; safety and efficacy have not been established in children [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of bleeding complications within therapeutic dose ranges; clinical monitoring is advised for patients ≥80 years.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
  - **Severe (CrCl <30 mL/min):** Anti-Xa monitoring is required to determine appropriate dosing, especially in cancer-associated VTE treatment. Target range: 0.5–1.5 IU/mL.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Dose reduction and close monitoring may be required in patients with severely disturbed hepatic function.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Dalteparin potentiates the activity of antithrombin III, which accelerates the neutralisation of activated Factor Xa and, to a lesser extent, Factor IIa (thrombin).
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Has a relatively small effect on platelet function and adhesion compared to heparin.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
  - **Absorption:** ~90% bioavailable after subcutaneous injection.
  - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is approximately 40–120 mL/kg.
  - **Elimination:** Half-life is ~2 hours (i.v.) and 3–4 hours (s.c.). Elimination is primarily renal and not dose-dependent within therapeutic intervals.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **CLOT Study:** Demonstrated that dalteparin was more effective than oral anticoagulants (OAC) in reducing recurrent VTE in cancer patients over 6 months (8.0% vs. 15.7% recurrence rate).
- **UA/NSTEMI Trials:** Dalteparin (120 IU/kg BID) significantly reduced the combined endpoint of death and MI compared to placebo when used for 5–8 days.
- **Orthopaedic Surgery:** Studies in hip replacement showed significant reduction in phlebographically detected thrombosis compared to placebo.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **2,500 IU and 5,000 IU Syringes:** Store below 30°C.
- **7,500 IU, 10,000 IU, 12,500 IU, 15,000 IU, and 18,000 IU Syringes:** Store below 25°C.
- **Reconstituted Solutions:** Prepared i.v. infusion solutions (in saline or glucose) should be used within 12 hours.
