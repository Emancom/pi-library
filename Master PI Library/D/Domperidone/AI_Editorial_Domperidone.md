# Editorial Product Information: Domperidone

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)** regulatory authority. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of domperidone for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises available data into a single reference point to support clinical decision-making.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Domperidone (as maleate), a peripheral dopamine ($D_2$) receptor antagonist.
- **Brand Names:** APO-Domperidone [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A03FA03.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - 10 mg oral tablets [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Domperidone is indicated for the short-term treatment in adults for the following conditions:
- Symptoms associated with idiopathic or diabetic gastroparesis. Treatment should be re-evaluated once diabetic control is established via diet or insulin [Australian].
- Intractable nausea and vomiting arising from any etiology [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults $\ge$ 35 kg)
- **Standard Dose:** 10 mg three times daily [Australian].
- **Maximum Daily Dose:** 30 mg [Australian].
- **Duration:** 
    - Acute nausea and vomiting: Usually limited to a maximum of one week [Australian].
    - Other indications: Initially limited to 4 weeks; a benefit/risk re-analysis is required if extended treatment is contemplated [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets should be taken 15–30 minutes before meals and, if necessary, before retiring. 
- Absorption is delayed if taken after meals [Australian].

### Missed Dose
- If a dose is missed, it should be omitted, and the regular schedule resumed. Do not double the dose to compensate [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
Domperidone is contraindicated in the following clinical scenarios:
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to domperidone or any tablet excipients.
- **Prolactinoma:** Prolactin-releasing pituitary tumours.
- **GI Risk:** Situations where stimulating gastric motility is hazardous (e.g., gastrointestinal haemorrhage, mechanical obstruction, or perforation).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Moderate or severe hepatic impairment.
- **Cardiac Conditions:** Known prolongation of cardiac conduction intervals (particularly QTc), significant electrolyte disturbances (hypokalaemia, hyperkalaemia, hypomagnesaemia), or underlying cardiac diseases such as congestive heart failure.
- **Drug Interactions:** 
    - Co-administration with potent CYP3A4 inhibitors.
    - Co-administration with medicines known to prolong the QTc interval [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiac Effects:** Domperidone is associated with an increased risk of serious ventricular arrhythmias and sudden cardiac death (SCD). The risk is higher in patients over 60 years old, those taking daily doses >30 mg, and those using concomitant QTc-prolonging or CYP3A4-inhibiting medications. Treatment must be discontinued if symptoms of arrhythmia occur [Australian].
- **Prolactin Elevation:** Domperidone increases plasma prolactin levels. While levels return to normal upon discontinuation, long-term effects on breast cancer (given that one-third of human breast cancers are prolactin-dependent in vitro) are not fully established; caution is advised in patients with a history of breast cancer [Australian].
- **Excipients:** Tablets contain lactose; use with caution in patients with galactosemia, glucose/galactose malabsorption, or lactose intolerance [Australian].
- **Extrapyramidal Symptoms:** Although rare due to poor blood-brain barrier penetration, extrapyramidal symptoms may occur, particularly after long-term use [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions observed in clinical trials and post-marketing surveillance are categorised below.

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common** ($\ge$ 1% to < 10%) | Headache (5.6%), Somnolence (2.5%), Depression (2.5%), Diarrhoea (5.2%), Rash (2.8%), Asthenia (1.9%), Dry mouth, Breast enlargement/Gynaecomastia (5.3%), Breast tenderness (4.4%), Galactorrhoea (3.3%) |
| **Uncommon** ($\ge$ 0.1% to < 1%) | Anxiety, Libido loss, Akathisia, Pruritus, Urticaria, Amenorrhoea, Breast pain, Menstruation irregular, Lactation disorder, Increased prolactin, Nervousness, Insomnia, Dizziness, Thirst, Lethargy, Irritability, Heartburn, Nausea, Constipation, Oedema, Palpitations, Muscle spasms, Dysuria |
| **Rare** (< 0.1%) | Extrapyramidal side effects |
| **Very Rare** (< 0.01%) | Anaphylactic reactions/shock, Angioedema, Agitation, Convulsion, Sudden Cardiac Death, Serious Ventricular Arrhythmia, Abnormal liver function tests |

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Domperidone is primarily metabolised by CYP3A4. Concomitant use with **potent** inhibitors (e.g., azole antifungals, macrolides like erythromycin/clarithromycin, HIV protease inhibitors, and certain calcium antagonists like diltiazem/verapamil) is contraindicated as it can increase domperidone plasma levels three-fold [Australian].
- **Moderate CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Use should be avoided [Australian].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **QTc-Prolonging Medicines:** Co-administration with drugs that prolong the QT interval (e.g., Class IA and III anti-arrhythmics, certain antipsychotics, antidepressants, and antibiotics) is contraindicated [Australian].
- **Antacids and Antisecretory Drugs:** These reduce the bioavailability of domperidone. They should not be taken simultaneously; domperidone should be taken before meals and antacids after meals (separated by at least 2 hours) [Australian].
- **Anticholinergics:** May antagonise the prokinetic effects of domperidone [Australian].
- **Dopaminergic Agonists:** Domperidone can be used to suppress the peripheral side effects (nausea/vomiting) of drugs like L-dopa or bromocriptine without affecting their central activity [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B2]. Animal studies show no evidence of dysmorphogenesis, but there are no adequate, well-controlled studies in humans. Use only if the potential benefit justifies the risk [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Domperidone is excreted in breast milk in very small amounts (estimated relative infant dose 0.1%). However, toxicity to the newborn is not fully known; breastfeeding is not recommended for women taking domperidone [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Data not available in provided sources [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Domperidone should not be used in children. Extrapyramidal disorders and CNS effects (convulsions, agitation) have been reported primarily in infants and children [Australian].
- **Elderly:** Increased risk of cardiac complications; use with caution and adhere to the lowest effective dose [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** The elimination half-life is significantly prolonged in severe renal insufficiency (from 7.4 to 20.8 hours). For repeated administration, the dosing frequency should be reduced to once or twice daily, and the dose may need reduction [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in moderate to severe impairment (Child-Pugh B and C). No dose adjustment is required for mild impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A dopamine antagonist that increases lower oesophageal sphincter pressure, improves antroduodenal coordination, and facilitates gastric emptying. Antiemetic effects result from peripheral prokinetic activity and antagonism of dopamine receptors in the chemo-receptor trigger zone (located outside the blood-brain barrier) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; peak plasma levels in 30 minutes (oral). Bioavailability is low (13–17%) due to extensive first-pass metabolism.
    - **Distribution:** 91–93% plasma protein bound. Does not readily cross the blood-brain barrier.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensive hepatic metabolism via hydroxylation and N-dealkylation (primarily CYP3A4).
    - **Elimination:** Excreted in faeces (66%) and urine (31%). Terminal half-life is 7–9 hours in healthy subjects [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Epidemiological Data:** Case-control studies have identified a correlation between domperidone use and sudden cardiac death, with an adjusted odds ratio (OR) of 1.71–1.99. The risk is significantly elevated (OR 11.4) at doses exceeding 30 mg/day [Australian].
- **QT Studies:** A thorough QT study in healthy volunteers using 20 mg four times daily showed a maximal QTc change of 3.4 msec, which was not considered clinically relevant at that dose [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 30°C [Australian].
- Shelf life is specified on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
