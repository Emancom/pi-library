# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Note**: This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official PI when prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Levocarnitine (chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
- **Chemical Class**: Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Names**: Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code**: Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength**: Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration**: Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism**: Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn metabolic errors [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD)**: Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose**: 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose**: Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency**: Administer every 3 to 4 hours; intervals should never be less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maximum Dose**: 300 mg/kg has been administered in clinical practice [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose**: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration**: Administer as a 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustments**: Should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., downward to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions**: Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizures**: Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring**: 
    - Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels [US].
    - Conduct weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations [US].
    - Target plasma free carnitine concentration: 35 to 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation**: Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide). The safety/efficacy of the oral formulation in renal insufficiency has not been established [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5%)
In clinical trials of ESRD patients on hemodialysis, the following events occurred at a frequency of ≥5% [US]:

| System Organ Class | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, paresthesia |
| **Respiratory** | Cough, dyspnea, pharyngitis, rhinitis |

### Serious and Other Notable Effects
- **Serious**: Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm [US].
- **Neurologic**: Seizures [US].
- **Other**: Body odor and gastritis have been reported less frequently [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin**: Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. It is recommended to monitor INR levels following initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: [US: Category not explicitly lettered]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentration in milk following exogenous administration. Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility**: No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Refer to the "Metabolic Disorders" dosing section for weight-based dosing (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use**: Not specifically addressed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment**: Dosing adjustments are required for patients with ESRD on hemodialysis (see Section 3). The safety of oral levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics**: Carnitine deficiency is biochemically defined as plasma free carnitine <20 µmol/L at one week post-term [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Distribution**: Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life: 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism**: Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination**: Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean terminal elimination half-life: 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Study**: A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly showed that trough concentrations reached 54–180 µmol/L after one week and remained stable. 
- **Dose-Ranging Study**: Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg three times weekly demonstrated dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine concentrations at 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Note**: While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the effect on clinical outcomes in this specific population has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature**: 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling**: Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
- **Compatibility**: Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when diluted to concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL in Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's [US].
