# Editorial Product Information: drospirenone + ethinyl

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **drospirenone + ethinyl**
- Chemical class:
  - Drospirenone: synthetic progestin with anti‑mineralocorticoid activity
  - Ethinyl (ethinyl estradiol): synthetic estrogen
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 2001.
- Most recent revision of provided prescribing information: May 2023.
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Prevention of pregnancy in females of reproductive potential (general contraceptive indication).
- Treatment of premenstrual dysphoric disorder (PMDD) in females of reproductive potential who choose to use an oral contraceptive as their method of contraception. Note: effectiveness for PMDD beyond three menstrual cycles has not been evaluated.
- Treatment of moderate acne vulgaris in females at least 14 years of age who have achieved menarche, when an oral contraceptive is desired for birth control.

(If an indication is jurisdiction‑specific it is not distinguished in the available source; where regional differences apply, they are noted elsewhere in this document.)

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Each dosing regimen consists of 28 consecutive daily tablets: 24 active tablets containing 3 mg drospirenone + 0.02 mg ethinyl, followed by 4 inert (inactive) tablets.
- Take one tablet orally at the same time each day.

### Dosing by indication
- Same regimen applies for contraception, PMDD treatment, and acne treatment when used for those indications.

### How to start
- Day 1 start: Begin on Day 1 (first day) of menstrual bleeding. Use backup non‑hormonal contraception for the first 7 days if not started on Day 1.
- Sunday start: Begin on the first Sunday after onset of menstrual bleeding. Use backup non‑hormonal contraception for the first 7 days.
- When switching from another combined oral contraceptive: start on the day a new pack of prior OCP would have been started.
- When switching from transdermal patch or vaginal ring: start on the day the next application or insertion would have been due.
- When switching from injection: start when the next injection would have been due.
- When switching from intrauterine device or implant: start on the day of removal.
- Postpartum non‑breastfeeding women: start no earlier than 4 weeks postpartum due to increased thromboembolism risk.

### Administration instructions
- Take at the same time each day; if preferred, take after evening meal or at bedtime. Can be taken without regard to meals.
- Tablets must be taken in the order directed on the pack.

### Missed dose instructions
- If one active (light pink) tablet missed: take as soon as remembered; take next tablet at regular time (may take two tablets in one day). No backup needed.
- If two consecutive active tablets missed in Week 1 or Week 2: take two tablets as soon as possible and two the next day, then one daily until pack finished. Use additional barrier contraception if sex occurs within 7 days after missed tablets.
- If two consecutive active tablets missed in Week 3 or Week 4:
  - Day 1 start: discard the rest of the pack and start a new pack that same day.
  - Sunday start: continue taking one tablet daily until Sunday, then discard the remainder of the pack and start a new pack that same day.
  - Use additional barrier contraception if sex occurs within 7 days after restarting.
- If three or more consecutive active tablets missed during any week:
  - Day 1 start: discard the remainder of the pack and start a new pack that same day.
  - Sunday start: continue taking one tablet daily until Sunday, then discard remainder and start a new pack that same day.
  - Use backup contraception for 7 days after restarting; consider pregnancy if menses missed.
- If any of the four inactive (white) tablets missed in Week 4: discard missed inactive tablet(s) and continue taking one tablet daily; no backup needed.
- If vomiting occurs within 3–4 hours of dosing, consider the dose missed and follow the missed‑dose recommendations. In severe gastrointestinal disturbance, additional contraceptive measures should be taken.

## 5. Contraindications
Drospirenone + ethinyl is contraindicated in females who currently have or develop any of the following conditions:
- Known or suspected pregnancy: (Note: pregnancy contraindication statement in provided source was revised/removed in May 2023; nonetheless, guidance in the sources states to discontinue if pregnancy is confirmed.)
- Renal impairment
- Adrenal insufficiency
- High risk of arterial or venous thrombotic diseases, including but not limited to:
  - Current or history of deep vein thrombosis or pulmonary embolism
  - Cerebrovascular disease
  - Coronary artery disease
  - Thrombogenic valvular or thrombogenic rhythm diseases of the heart (e.g., subacute bacterial endocarditis with valvular disease, atrial fibrillation)
  - Known hereditary or acquired hypercoagulopathies
  - Women over 35 years who smoke cigarettes [Boxed warning applies; see Section 6]
- Uncontrolled hypertension or hypertension with vascular disease
- Diabetes mellitus with vascular disease
- Headaches with focal neurological symptoms, or migraine headaches with or without aura in women over 35 years
- Undiagnosed abnormal uterine bleeding
- Current diagnosis of, or history of, breast cancer, which may be hormonally sensitive
- Liver tumors (benign or malignant) or liver disease with impaired liver function
- Concomitant use with hepatitis C direct‑acting antiviral combinations containing ombitasvir, paritaprevir/ritonavir, with or without dasabuvir (due to potential for marked ALT elevations)

(These contraindications reflect the conditions listed in the available prescribing information. Specific jurisdictional wording may vary.)

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warning
- **Cigarette smoking and serious cardiovascular events:** Cigarette smoking increases the risk of serious cardiovascular events (including myocardial infarction, stroke, thromboembolism) with combined oral contraceptives. The risk increases with age and number of cigarettes smoked. **Combined oral contraceptives should not be used by women over 35 years who smoke.** [US]

### General warnings and precautions
- Thromboembolic disorders and other vascular problems:
  - Combined oral contraceptives (COCs) containing drospirenone may be associated with a higher risk of venous thromboembolism (VTE) than some COCs containing levonorgestrel or other progestins. Epidemiologic study results varied (risk estimates ranged from no increase to up to about threefold). The absolute risk of VTE in COC users has been estimated at approximately 3 to 9 per 10,000 woman‑years; risk is highest during the first year of use and particularly during the first 6 months.
  - Stop therapy if an arterial or venous thrombotic event occurs.
  - Discontinue at least 4 weeks before and through 2 weeks after major surgery associated with increased risk of thromboembolism.
  - Consider individual VTE risk factors (smoking, obesity, family history, age, immobilization, surgery, hereditary thrombophilias) before initiating.
- Hyperkalemia:
  - Drospirenone has anti‑mineralocorticoid activity and can increase serum potassium. Do not use in patients predisposed to hyperkalemia.
  - Measure serum potassium during the first treatment cycle in women on long‑term medications that may increase serum potassium (e.g., potassium‑sparing diuretics, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, NSAIDs); monitor as clinically indicated.
- Hepatic effects:
  - Discontinue if jaundice or clinically significant liver dysfunction occurs.
  - Use is contraindicated in patients with liver tumors or severe hepatic disease.
  - Concomitant use with certain hepatitis C antiviral regimens (ombitasvir/paritaprevir/ritonavir ± dasabuvir) is contraindicated because of elevated ALT; closely evaluate liver enzymes when relevant.
- Hypertension:
  - Do not prescribe to women with uncontrolled hypertension or hypertension with vascular disease. Monitor blood pressure during therapy.
- Metabolic effects:
  - Monitor carbohydrate and lipid metabolism in women with prediabetes/diabetes or uncontrolled dyslipidemia; consider alternate contraception if uncontrolled.
- Gallbladder disease: may be associated with increased risk; assess clinically.
- Headache and neuropsychiatric effects:
  - Evaluate significant or new onset headaches; consider discontinuation if indicated.
  - Depression: evaluate if significant mood changes occur; discontinue if clinically warranted.
- Bleeding irregularities:
  - Irregular bleeding and amenorrhea may occur; evaluate persistent or prolonged abnormal bleeding.
- Interference with laboratory tests:
  - Hormonal contraceptives can affect results of certain laboratory tests (e.g., coagulation, lipids, glucose tolerance).
- Other conditions:
  - Use caution and consider the balance of risks and benefits when prescribing to women with conditions that may be influenced by hormonal therapy (e.g., systemic lupus erythematosus, history of cholestatic jaundice related to prior COC use, seizure disorders).

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline assessment and periodic monitoring as clinically indicated:
  - Blood pressure
  - Serum potassium if concomitant use of potassium‑elevating medications or other risk factors for hyperkalemia
  - Liver function tests if clinically indicated (especially with symptoms or concomitant hepatotoxic agents)
  - Evaluate for signs/symptoms of VTE; advise patients to seek immediate care for chest pain, dyspnea, unilateral leg pain/swelling, sudden severe headache, vision change, or focal neurologic deficits.
  - Monitor mood/psychiatric symptoms and menstrual bleeding patterns.

## 7. Adverse Effects
- The most frequent adverse reactions reported in contraception and acne clinical trials (frequency ≥ 2%) include:
  - Headache/migraine: 6.7%
  - Menstrual irregularities: 4.7%
  - Nausea/vomiting: 4.2%
  - Breast pain/tenderness: 4.0%
  - Mood changes: 2.2%
- The most frequent adverse reactions reported in PMDD clinical trials (frequency ≥ 2%) include:
  - Menstrual irregularities: 24.9%
  - Nausea: 15.8%
  - Headache: 13.0%
  - Breast tenderness: 10.5%
  - Fatigue: 4.2%
  - Irritability: 2.8%
  - Decreased libido: 2.8%
  - Increased weight: 2.5%
  - Affective lability: 2.1%

### Serious adverse reactions and important risks
- Venous thromboembolism (deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism) and arterial thrombotic events (including myocardial infarction and stroke).
- Hyperkalemia (due to drospirenone’s anti‑mineralocorticoid activity).
- Clinically significant liver enzyme elevations, particularly with concomitant hepatitis C direct‑acting antivirals listed in Contraindications.
- Other serious events: Not fully enumerated in available sources.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Enzyme inducers (e.g., CYP3A4 inducers) may decrease the effectiveness of combined oral contraceptives and increase the risk of breakthrough bleeding. Counsel patients to use a backup or alternative contraceptive method during use of enzyme inducers and for a specified period after discontinuation of the inducer per the inducer’s prescribing information.
- Concomitant use with hepatitis C antiviral combinations containing ombitasvir, paritaprevir/ritonavir, with or without dasabuvir is contraindicated due to significant elevations in ALT.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Concomitant medications that increase serum potassium (e.g., potassium‑sparing diuretics, ACE inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers, potassium supplements, certain NSAIDs) may increase risk of hyperkalemia when used with drospirenone; monitor serum potassium as clinically indicated.

### Clinically significant interactions and management
- If an enzyme‑inducing drug must be used, advise additional non‑hormonal contraception while taking the inducer and for a duration after stopping the inducer per the inducer’s guidance.
- Avoid coadministration with the specific hepatitis C antiviral combinations named in Contraindications.
- Monitor serum potassium when drospirenone is used with drugs that may raise potassium levels; avoid in patients predisposed to hyperkalemia.
- Drospirenone/ethinyl estradiol can affect laboratory tests (coagulation parameters, lipids, glucose tolerance, endocrine tests).

(Where region‑specific interaction details exist, they are not separately distinguished in the available source.)

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Pregnancy classification by jurisdictional categories (e.g., Australian category, US letter categories): Not specified in available sources.
- If pregnancy occurs, discontinue the product.
- For postpartum initiation: start no earlier than 4 weeks after delivery in non‑breastfeeding women because of increased thromboembolism risk.

### Breastfeeding
- Drospirenone + ethinyl can reduce milk production in breastfeeding females. Consider alternative contraception for lactating women, particularly in the early postpartum period. If breastfeeding, discuss potential reduction in milk supply and timing of initiation.

### Fertility
- Fertility after discontinuation: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Approved for acne treatment in females at least 14 years of age who have achieved menarche. Use in younger adolescents for contraception: Not specified beyond acne indication.

### Geriatric use
- Not applicable; product is indicated for females of reproductive potential. Use in elderly populations: Not specified in available sources.

### Renal impairment
- Use is contraindicated in patients with renal impairment. No dosing adjustments or eGFR‑based guidance available in the provided sources.

### Hepatic impairment
- Use is contraindicated in patients with liver tumors or significant hepatic disease. No graded dosing adjustments by severity provided in available sources.

### Other special populations
- Adrenal insufficiency: contraindicated.
- Patients with uncontrolled hypertension, diabetes with vascular disease, current or history of breast cancer, or known thrombophilic conditions: contraindicated or require careful consideration per Contraindications and Warnings.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **Drospirenone + ethinyl** is a combination hormonal contraceptive containing a progestin (drospirenone) and an estrogen (ethinyl). Drospirenone exhibits anti‑mineralocorticoid activity. Detailed mechanistic descriptions (e.g., effects on ovulation, cervical mucus, endometrium) are not specified in the available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Drospirenone’s anti‑mineralocorticoid activity can affect serum potassium. Specific pharmacodynamic parameter values are not provided in the available sources.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption, distribution, metabolism, and elimination parameters (Cmax, Tmax, half‑life, protein binding, metabolic pathways, excretion routes) are not specified in the available sources.

## 12. Clinical Trials
### Contraception and acne clinical trials
- Clinical trial data cited in the prescribing information report the most frequent adverse reactions in contraception and acne trials: headache/migraine (6.7%), menstrual irregularities (4.7%), nausea/vomiting (4.2%), breast pain/tenderness (4.0%), and mood changes (2.2%). Detailed pivotal trial designs, sample sizes, efficacy rates (e.g., Pearl Index), and other primary outcome measures are not specified in the available source text.

### PMDD clinical trials
- PMDD trials reported frequent adverse effects: menstrual irregularities (24.9%), nausea (15.8%), headache (13.0%), breast tenderness (10.5%), fatigue (4.2%), irritability (2.8%), decreased libido (2.8%), increased weight (2.5%), and affect lability (2.1%).
- The effectiveness of the product for PMDD beyond three menstrual cycles has not been evaluated.

### VTE risk epidemiology studies (post‑marketing/regulatory safety assessments)
- Multiple epidemiologic studies and analyses compared VTE risk in users of drospirenone‑containing COCs to users of other low‑dose COCs. Reported hazard ratios varied by study:
  - Ingenix study: HR ~0.9 (0.5–1.6)
  - EURAS study: HR ~0.9 (0.6–1.4)
  - FDA‑funded study (new users): HR ~1.8 (1.3–2.4); versus levonorgestrel/0.03 mg EE comparator HR ~1.6 (1.1–2.2)
  - FDA‑funded study (all users): HR ~1.7 (1.4–2.1); versus levonorgestrel/0.03 mg EE comparator HR ~1.5 (1.2–1.8)
  - Other cohort and case‑control studies reported a range of estimates; overall findings ranged from no increase to an up to approximately three‑fold increased relative risk in some analyses.
- Absolute VTE rates for COC users were estimated at approximately 3–9 events per 10,000 woman‑years; VTE risk is greater during pregnancy and greatest in the postpartum period.

### Notes on trial limitations
- Study designs, inclusion/exclusion criteria, and statistical adjustments varied across the epidemiologic studies; interpretation should consider study design heterogeneity and potential confounding factors. Detailed trial methodology for pivotal efficacy trials is not included in the available source.
