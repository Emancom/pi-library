# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**. 
- **Note:** Documents from other major regulatory authorities (e.g., Australian TGA, European EMA, Health Canada) were not provided for this specific harmonisation.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration Instructions
- **Route:** Administer strictly via intravenous injection.
- **Visual Inspection:** Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration before use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours) over the first 24 hours.
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised based on clinical requirement.
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Monitor weekly and monthly, including blood chemistry and vital signs. Target free carnitine concentrations are between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
- **Administration Timing:** Administer as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels. Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of treatment if levels are adequate [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, often within minutes of administration. These are primarily reported in ESRD patients. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and assess the risks before considering re-administration [US].
- **Neurologic Reactions (Seizures):** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral vs. IV):** The safety of high-dose **oral** levocarnitine has not been established in renal failure. Chronic oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5%)
In clinical trials of ESRD patients on hemodialysis, the following events occurred at a frequency of 5% or greater (regardless of causality):
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (9%), headache (22%), injection site reaction (33%), chest pain (12%), fever (7%), infection (15%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), nausea (8%), vomiting (15%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%).
- **Neurological:** Dizziness (13%), paresthesia (5%).

### Other Reported Reactions
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%), hypervolemia (5%).
- **General:** Body odor, gastritis, and asthenia [US].
- **Post-marketing:** Seizures and hypersensitivity reactions (anaphylaxis) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Clinical Note:** Monitor INR levels at treatment initiation and following any dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Classification:** Not explicitly assigned a letter category in provided sources, but animal reproduction studies in rats and rabbits (up to 3.8 times the human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. 
    - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations after administration. Decisions should weigh the benefits of maternal treatment against the risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant; discontinuation of nursing or treatment may be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (mg/kg) as per the instructions in Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for ESRD; however, see Section 5 regarding the risks of metabolite accumulation with oral formulations.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. 
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (found in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (found in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** Efficacy has been demonstrated in increasing carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients. In a dose-ranging study (10, 20, 40 mg/kg), trough concentrations increased significantly over 12 to 24 weeks compared to baseline. However, the document notes that the specific effects of supplemental carnitine on clinical outcomes and signs/symptoms of deficiency in the ESRD population have not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only [US].
