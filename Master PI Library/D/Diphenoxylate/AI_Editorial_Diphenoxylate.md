# Editorial Product Information: Diphenoxylate and Atropine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. 
- Information from other jurisdictions (e.g., US, European, Canadian, Japanese) was not provided in the source material; therefore, clinical recommendations and regulatory classifications are primarily representative of the Australian context.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a clinical synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Diphenoxylate hydrochloride and atropine sulfate.
- **Chemical Class:** Diphenoxylate is a phenylpiperidine derivative chemically related to the narcotic pethidine; atropine sulfate is an anticholinergic.
- **Brand Names:** LOFENOXAL [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A07DA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Oral tablet containing 2.5 mg diphenoxylate hydrochloride and 25 micrograms atropine sulfate [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** iNova Pharmaceuticals (Australia) Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Adjunctive therapy for acute and chronic diarrhoea** [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:** The recommended initial dose is 5 mg (two tablets) administered three or four times daily.
- **Titration:** Once initial control of symptoms is achieved, the dosage should be reduced to the minimum effective dose required to maintain control.
- **Maintenance:** Control may often be maintained with a dose as low as 5 mg (two tablets) daily [Australian].
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to diphenoxylate hydrochloride or atropine.
- **Jaundice.**
- **Pseudomembranous enterocolitis:** Diarrhoea associated with certain antibiotics, occurring during or up to several weeks post-treatment.
- **Inflammatory/Infective Colitis:** Diarrhoea associated with ulcerative colitis, Crohn’s disease, or bacterial and amoebic colitis, as diphenoxylate may exacerbate these conditions.
- **Paediatric Use:** Contraindicated in children under 12 years of age [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
- **Respiratory Depression:** Accidental overdose in children may result in severe or fatal respiratory depression. Keep out of reach of children.
- **Toxic Megacolon:** In patients with acute ulcerative colitis, agents that inhibit intestinal motility may induce toxic megacolon. Treatment must be discontinued promptly if abdominal distension occurs.
- **Dehydration and Electrolyte Imbalance:** Appropriate fluid and electrolyte replacement is paramount. In cases of severe dehydration, the drug should be withheld until corrective therapy is initiated to prevent variable drug response and potential intoxication.
- **Dependence Potential:** Diphenoxylate is structurally related to drugs with addiction potential. High dosages may lead to dependency. Caution is advised in patients who are addiction-prone or receiving other addictive medications.
- **Atropinism:** Contains a subtherapeutic dose of atropine to discourage deliberate overdose. Signs of atropinism (e.g., tachycardia, urinary retention, flushing) may occur, particularly in patients with Down’s Syndrome [Australian].
- **CNS Effects:** May cause dizziness or drowsiness; patients should be advised against driving or operating machinery until their response is determined.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects reported at therapeutic doses include:

| System Organ Class | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Nervous System** | Malaise, lethargy, confusion, sedation, drowsiness, dizziness, restlessness, depression, euphoria, numbness of extremities, headache. |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Toxic megacolon, paralytic ileus, vomiting, nausea, anorexia, abdominal discomfort. |
| **Allergic/Immune** | Anaphylaxis, angioneurotic oedema, urticaria, swelling of gums, pruritus. |
| **Atropine-related** | Hyperthermia, tachycardia, urinary retention, flushing, dryness of skin and mucous membranes. |

*Note: Frequencies were not specified in available sources.*

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CNS Depressants:** Diphenoxylate may potentiate the effects of barbiturates, tranquillizers, and alcohol [Australian].
- **MAO Inhibitors:** Concurrent use may theoretically precipitate a hypertensive crisis; close monitoring is required [Australian].
- **Antibiotics:** In cases of bacterially induced diarrhoea, appropriate antimicrobial therapy must be used concurrently.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **Australian Category C.** 
  - Diphenoxylate is related to pethidine. Narcotic analgesics may cause respiratory depression in neonates. This medication should not be administered at or near term [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Diphenoxylate hydrochloride may be, and atropine sulfate is, excreted in human breast milk. Effects may occur in nursing infants [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for children under 12 years of age due to the risk of respiratory depression [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available in sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Extreme caution is required in patients with advanced hepatorenal disease [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with extreme caution in patients with abnormal liver function; hepatic coma may be precipitated [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Diphenoxylate acts by slowing intestinal motility. Atropine is included at a subtherapeutic dose to discourage deliberate overdosage [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; peak blood levels are reached in approximately two hours.
  - **Metabolism:** Undergoes rapid biotransformation via hydrolysis to diphenoxylic acid (a pharmacologically active metabolite). It likely undergoes enterohepatic circulation.
  - **Elimination:** Plasma half-life is approximately 2.5 hours. Excreted primarily as metabolites in the faeces and, to a lesser extent, in the urine [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the local regulatory register (e.g., ARTG in Australia).
- **Packaging:** Typically supplied in blister packs (e.g., 20 tablets) [Australian].
