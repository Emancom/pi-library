# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** US (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview of the clinical and regulatory profiles of levocarnitine injection, synthesized for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is intended for informational purposes and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to the current approved PI in their specific region for definitive guidance. Note: Only US-based documentation was available for this synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP); chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. 
- **Chemical Class:** Naturally occurring carrier molecule/amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Clear, colorless sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL).
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Administer via slow intravenous bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use.

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often administered in patients with severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the next 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses range from 50 mg/kg or as clinically required. Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilized [US].
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Monitor weekly/monthly including blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration Timing:** Administer into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
- **Titration:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations (target: 40–50 µmol/L). Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None Known:** There are no listed contraindications for levocarnitine injection in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including **anaphylaxis**, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Formulation Note:** While not specific to the injection, clinicians should be aware that chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5%)
Based on clinical trials in chronic hemodialysis patients (regardless of causality):
- **Body as a Whole:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), flu syndrome (25%), headache (22%), infection (15%), abdominal pain (9%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), chest pain (12%), tachycardia (6%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%).
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), paresthesia (5%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%).

### Other Reported Effects
- **General:** Transient nausea/vomiting, body odor, and gastritis have been observed [US].
- **Post-marketing:** Seizures, anaphylaxis, and laryngeal edema [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels frequently after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned by letter, but noted as follows]: Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine concentrations in milk increase following exogenous administration in dairy cows. The risks of excess carnitine intake to the infant must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother. Consider discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy are supported by the established mg/kg dosing regimens for metabolic disorders and ESRD [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific geriatric trials are not detailed; however, dosing should be cautious and reflect the increased frequency of hepatic, renal, or cardiac dysfunction in this population [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily used in ESRD patients. Note that the safety of the *oral* formulation in renal insufficiency has not been evaluated [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, facilitating oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects, clearing toxic acyl-CoA compounds by forming acylcarnitine [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 17.4 hours. About 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis showed significant increases in plasma levocarnitine. Pre-dialysis (trough) levels rose from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** Studies at 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Integrity:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservative [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
