# Editorial Product Information: Diflorasone Diacetate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (Food and Drug Administration)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonized editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesizing clinical data into a single reference point. 
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidance required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Diflorasone diacetate (a synthetic fluorinated corticosteroid).
- **Chemical Class:** High-potency (Group II) topical corticosteroid.
- **Brand Names:** Diflorasone Diacetate (Generic), [US: Taro, Sun Pharma].
- **ATC Code:** D07AC10 (Corticosteroids, potent (group III)).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Topical Cream: 0.05% (0.5 mg/g) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical (Dermatological) only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sun Pharmaceutical Industries, Inc. (Distributed by); Sun Pharma Canada Inc. (Manufactured by) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Corticosteroid-Responsive Dermatoses:** Indicated for the relief of inflammatory and pruritic manifestations of dermatoses that respond to topical corticosteroid therapy [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** 
    - Apply a thin film to the affected skin areas twice daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - For external use only. Not for ophthalmic use [US].
    - Do not use on the face, groin, or axillae [US].
    - Treated areas should not be bandaged or covered with occlusive dressings unless specifically directed by a physician [US].
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Contraindicated in patients with a known history of hypersensitivity to diflorasone diacetate or any other component of the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
- **HPA Axis Suppression:** Systemic absorption can produce reversible hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis suppression, potentially leading to glucocorticosteroid insufficiency upon treatment withdrawal. This risk is higher with large surface area application, prolonged use, or the use of occlusive dressings [US].
- **Systemic Effects:** Manifestations of Cushing’s syndrome, hyperglycemia, and glucosuria may occur due to systemic absorption [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Patients applying high-potency steroids to large areas or under occlusion should be periodically evaluated for HPA axis suppression via ACTH-stimulation, A.M. plasma cortisol, or urinary free-cortisol tests [US].
- **Skin Infections:** In the presence of dermatological infections, an appropriate antifungal or antibacterial agent should be initiated. If a favorable response does not occur promptly, the corticosteroid should be discontinued until the infection is controlled [US].
- **Specific Clinical Precautions:** 
    - Avoid contact with the eyes [US].
    - Diflorasone diacetate cream (0.05%) should not be used to treat rosacea or perioral dermatitis [US].
    - If local irritation develops, discontinue use and institute appropriate therapy [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following local adverse reactions are reported infrequently with topical corticosteroids but may occur more often with occlusive dressings or high-potency agents. 

**Frequency: Infrequent/Rare (Not quantified in available sources)**
- **Skin and Appendages:** Burning, itching (pruritus), irritation, dryness, folliculitis, hypertrichosis, acneiform eruptions, hypopigmentation, perioral dermatitis, allergic contact dermatitis, skin maceration, secondary infection, skin atrophy, striae, and miliaria [US].

**Serious Adverse Effects:**
- **Systemic Toxicity:** HPA axis suppression and Cushingoid features [US].
- **Pediatric Specific:** Intracranial hypertension, including bulging fontanelles and bilateral papilledema [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions

- **Clinically Significant Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category C [US]:** Corticosteroids are generally teratogenic in laboratory animals when administered systemically or dermally at relatively low doses. 
    - Animal data: Diflorasone diacetate was found to be teratogenic (cleft palate) in rats and rabbits. 
    - Recommendation: Use during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown whether topical administration results in sufficient systemic absorption to produce detectable quantities in breast milk. Caution should be exercised when administered to a nursing woman [US].
- **Fertility:** Topical administration in rats at doses up to 0.5 mg/kg revealed no effects on fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US].
    - Pediatric patients are at a higher risk of HPA axis suppression and systemic toxicity (e.g., Cushing’s syndrome, linear growth retardation, delayed weight gain, and intracranial hypertension) due to a higher skin surface area to body mass ratio [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources; however, general precautions regarding skin atrophy and systemic absorption apply.
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Like other topical corticosteroids, diflorasone diacetate possesses anti-inflammatory, anti-pruritic, and vasoconstrictive properties. It is thought to induce lipocortins (phospholipase A2 inhibitory proteins), which control the biosynthesis of inflammatory mediators like prostaglandins and leukotrienes by inhibiting the release of arachidonic acid [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Studies indicate the cream formulation is in the high range of potency compared to other topical corticosteroids [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Percutaneous absorption is determined by the vehicle, epidermal integrity, and use of occlusive dressings. 
    - **Distribution/Metabolism/Elimination:** Once absorbed through the skin, topical corticosteroids follow pharmacokinetic pathways similar to systemically administered corticosteroids (primarily hepatic metabolism and renal excretion) [US].
