# Editorial Product Information: dolutegravir + lamivudine

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **dolutegravir + lamivudine**
- Chemical class: Not specified in available sources
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of Human Immunodeficiency Virus-1 (HIV-1) infection in adults and adolescents (from 12 years of age weighing at least 40 kg):
  - As first-line therapy in antiretroviral treatment‑naïve patients with no known or suspected resistance to either component.
  - As a replacement regimen for patients who are virologically suppressed (HIV-1 RNA < 50 copies/mL) on a stable antiretroviral regimen with no history of treatment failure and no known or suspected resistance to the integrase inhibitor class or lamivudine.
- Paediatric indications for children < 12 years of age or < 40 kg: Not approved / safety and efficacy not established in available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult/adolescent)
- Recommended dose for adults and adolescents ≥ 12 years and weighing ≥ 40 kg: one tablet containing dolutegravir 50 mg and lamivudine 300 mg once daily.

### Dosing by indication
- No separate dose adjustments for different indications are specified in available sources beyond the general adult/adolescent dosing above.
- For patients requiring lamivudine dose adjustment (e.g., renal impairment) or dolutegravir dose adjustment (e.g., interacting inducers requiring 50 mg twice daily), the fixed-dose combination should be discontinued and individual components used to construct an adjusted regimen.

### Administration instructions
- Can be taken with or without food.
- Avoid concomitant administration with polyvalent cation-containing antacids. If such agents are used, administer dolutegravir-containing product 2 hours before or 6 hours after the antacid.
- For calcium, magnesium or iron supplements, administer 2 hours before or 6 hours after the dolutegravir-containing product, or alternatively administer the dolutegravir-containing product with food.
- If a dolutegravir dose increase to 50 mg twice daily is clinically required because of co‑administered inducing drugs (see Interactions), an additional separate 50 mg dolutegravir tablet should be given approximately 12 hours after the fixed-dose tablet. Use of separate preparations is required in such cases.

### Titration schedules

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to dolutegravir, lamivudine, or any excipient.
- Concurrent administration with medicinal products that are substrates of organic cation transporter 2 (OCT2) with narrow therapeutic windows (examples provided: dofetilide, pilsicainide, fampridine) — concurrent use is contraindicated because dolutegravir inhibits OCT2 and can increase exposure to these agents, potentially causing serious or life‑threatening events.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed / highlighted warnings
- Not specified in available sources as a boxed/major header; the following serious risks are emphasised in the clinical text and should be considered critical: severe hypersensitivity reactions (including systemic organ involvement), lactic acidosis and severe hepatomegaly with steatosis, and serious hepatotoxicity.

### General warnings and precautions
- Hypersensitivity reactions: Integrase inhibitor–class hypersensitivity reactions, including rash and systemic manifestations (fever, malaise, myalgia/arthralgia) and sometimes organ dysfunction such as liver injury, have been reported. Immediate discontinuation of dolutegravir + lamivudine and all suspect agents is recommended if hypersensitivity is suspected.
- Lactic acidosis and severe hepatomegaly with steatosis: Reported with nucleoside analogue antiretrovirals (including lamivudine), sometimes fatal. Exercise caution, especially in patients with risk factors for liver disease. Suspend treatment if clinical or laboratory findings suggest lactic acidosis or hepatosteatosis.
- Hepatotoxicity: Cases including abnormal LFTs, hepatitis and acute liver failure reported, including in patients without pre-existing liver disease. Monitor liver function tests.
- Immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome (IRIS): May occur after initiating antiretroviral therapy; evaluate and treat inflammatory symptoms promptly. Autoimmune disorders have been reported in the setting of immune recovery.
- HBV/HCV co-infection: Lamivudine has activity against hepatitis B virus but monotherapy is not considered adequate for HBV due to resistance risk. When stopping therapy in HBV co-infected patients, severe hepatitis flares or recurrent hepatitis may occur — monitor LFTs and HBV markers periodically. Initiation or maintenance of effective HBV therapy should be considered when starting dolutegravir + lamivudine in HBV co-infected patients.
- Serum lipids and blood glucose: Levels may increase during antiretroviral therapy; monitor and manage clinically as appropriate. In GEMINI trials, pooled groups showed generally improved overall lipid profiles; proportions with favourable total cholesterol/HDL improvements were similar between groups.
- Fat redistribution: Lipoatrophy and lipohypertrophy have been reported with combination antiretroviral therapy.
- Osteonecrosis: Reported in patients with advanced HIV disease and/or long-term ART exposure; consider in patients with joint pain or mobility issues.
- Opportunistic infections: Patients may still develop opportunistic infections; maintain close clinical observation.
- Transmission: Viral suppression reduces but may not eliminate risk of sexual transmission; follow prevention guidelines.

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and periodic monitoring of liver function tests, particularly in patients with pre-existing hepatic disease or HBV/HCV co-infection, and during immune reconstitution.
- Monitor serum creatinine: small increases in serum creatinine observed early and then stable; these reflect inhibition of tubular secretion rather than reduced glomerular filtration rate.
- Monitor serum lipids and blood glucose as clinically indicated.
- Monitor for signs/symptoms of hypersensitivity, lactic acidosis, osteonecrosis and IRIS.
- In patients with moderate renal impairment receiving the fixed-dose tablet, monitor for lamivudine-related adverse events (notably neutropenia and anaemia). If dose adjustment of lamivudine is required, the fixed-dose product must be discontinued.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Use in pregnancy: limited information available; use only if expected benefit justifies potential risk to the fetus.
- Use in elderly: limited data in patients ≥ 65 years; no specific dose adjustment established but consider co-morbidities, hepatic/renal function and concomitant medications.
- Renal impairment: lamivudine exposure increases as renal function declines; fixed-dose product is not recommended for patients with creatinine clearance < 30 mL/min. For CrCl 30–49 mL/min, lamivudine exposure may increase 1.6–3.3 fold; monitor and if lamivudine dose adjustment is indicated, use individual components rather than the fixed-dose combination.
- Hepatic impairment: no dose adjustment required for mild to moderate hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh A or B). Not recommended in severe hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh C) due to lack of data.

## 7. Adverse Effects
- All adverse effects listed in available sources are included below. Frequency information is not provided in the available source text for most events; where frequencies are not specified, this is indicated.

### Key adverse reactions and abnormalities reported in the available sources
- Hypersensitivity reactions: rash, constitutional symptoms, organ dysfunction including liver injury (frequency not specified).
- Lactic acidosis and severe hepatomegaly with steatosis (including fatal cases) associated with nucleoside analogues including lamivudine (frequency not specified).
- Serum lipid and blood glucose increases (frequency not specified); GEMINI trials reported generally improved lipid profiles but monitoring advised.
- Fat loss (lipoatrophy) or fat gain (lipohypertrophy) (frequency not specified).
- Immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome (frequency not specified).
- Hepatotoxicity: liver function abnormalities, hepatitis, acute liver failure (frequency not specified).
- Osteonecrosis (frequency not specified).
- Opportunistic infections may still occur while on therapy (expected clinical phenomenon).
- Renal laboratory changes: small increases in serum creatinine noted (mean change +10.3 µmol/L at 48 weeks; range -36.3 to +55.7 µmol/L). These are considered not to reflect reduced GFR but inhibition of creatinine tubular secretion.
- Small increases in total bilirubin (without clinical jaundice) (frequency not specified).
- Asymptomatic creatine phosphokinase (CPK) elevations, often associated with exercise (frequency not specified).
- Haematologic toxicities (neutropenia, anaemia) have been associated with higher lamivudine exposures in earlier trials; discontinuations for these were <1% in the older registrational trials but increased exposure may raise risk (frequency not specified in current sources).
- Gastro-intestinal and hepatic disorders related to lamivudine at higher exposures (frequency not specified).

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Overview: Dolutegravir and lamivudine have different metabolic and elimination pathways; interactions described reflect effects of dolutegravir on transporters and of lamivudine on renal clearance. Because the fixed-dose tablet does not permit component dose adjustments, concomitant medications that require dose changes to one component may necessitate switching to individual component products.

### Pharmacokinetic interactions — dolutegravir-related
- Metabolism/transport:
  - Dolutegravir is mainly metabolised via UGT1A1 with a minor contribution from CYP3A.
  - In vitro substrate of UGT1A3, UGT1A9, P-gp and BCRP.
  - Dolutegravir inhibits renal transporters OCT2 (IC50 ≈ 1.93 μM), MATE1 (IC50 ≈ 6.34 μM) and MATE2‑K (IC50 ≈ 24.8 μM) in vitro; inhibition of OCT2 and MATE1 has clinical relevance.
  - Dolutegravir exhibited no or weak inhibition/induction of many CYPs and UGT1A1 in vitro and did not affect CYP3A4 probe midazolam in vivo; therefore CYP-mediated interactions are limited.

- Clinically significant interactions:
  - Do not co-administer dolutegravir-containing product with drugs that are OCT2 substrates with narrow therapeutic windows (examples: dofetilide, pilsicainide, fampridine) — contraindicated.
  - Dolutegravir increases plasma concentrations of drugs eliminated via OCT2 or MATE1 (e.g., metformin). Metformin doses may need adjustment when starting or stopping concomitant dolutegravir.
  - Drugs that induce UGT1A1/UGT1A3/UGT1A9/CYP3A or relevant transporters may reduce dolutegravir exposure and require dolutegravir dose adjustment to 50 mg twice daily. Examples include rifampicin, carbamazepine, phenytoin, phenobarbital, St. John’s wort, efavirenz, nevirapine, tipranavir/ritonavir, and etravirine when not given with a boosted protease inhibitor. Because the fixed-dose tablet cannot accommodate twice‑daily dosing, use separate dolutegravir formulation to allow dosing changes.
  - Polyvalent cation-containing antacids reduce dolutegravir absorption; separate dosing by 2 hours before or 6 hours after antacid use. Calcium, magnesium or iron supplements have similar interactions; alternatively take the dolutegravir-containing product with food.

- Specific interaction notes:
  - Etravirine without boosted protease inhibitors significantly decreased dolutegravir exposure and is not recommended with the fixed-dose combination.
  - Certain combinations (e.g., etravirine with boosted protease inhibitors) may not require dose adjustments.

### Pharmacokinetic interactions — lamivudine-related
- Lamivudine is predominantly renally cleared; limited metabolism and low plasma protein binding.
- Lamivudine is an in vitro substrate of P-gp, BCRP, MATE1, MATE2‑K, OCT1 and OCT2; however, clinically significant interactions are uncommon due to high bioavailability and renal clearance predominance.
- Trimethoprim (inhibitor of renal transporters) increases lamivudine concentrations, but the magnitude is not considered to warrant dose adjustment.
- In patients with reduced renal function (CrCl < 50 mL/min) lamivudine exposure increases and dose adjustment of lamivudine is required per lamivudine prescribing information; fixed-dose combination cannot be dose‑adjusted — use individual components when dose adjustment is necessary.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- No specific pharmacodynamic interactions uniquely described beyond additive antiretroviral class effects and risks already noted (e.g., risk of IRIS after immune recovery).

### Clinically significant interaction management summary
- Contraindicated: drugs that are OCT2 substrates with narrow therapeutic indices (dofetilide, pilsicainide, fampridine).
- For agents that reduce dolutegravir exposure (strong inducers): increase dolutegravir dosing to 50 mg twice daily when clinically indicated — this requires using separate dolutegravir tablets rather than the fixed-dose combination.
- Metformin: monitor glycemic control and consider metformin dose adjustment when initiating or discontinuing dolutegravir-containing therapy.
- Polyvalent cations / antacids: separate dosing (2 hours before or 6 hours after) or give dolutegravir with food when co-administering calcium, magnesium or iron supplements.
- In renal impairment (CrCl 30–49 mL/min): monitor for lamivudine-related adverse effects; if lamivudine dose reduction is required, discontinue fixed-dose combination and use individual components.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Adolescents ≥ 12 years and weighing ≥ 40 kg: the fixed-dose tablet (50 mg/300 mg once daily) is indicated.
- Children < 12 years of age or weighing < 40 kg: safety and efficacy of the fixed-dose combination have not been established. Clinical data not available for this combination in younger children; refer to individual component prescribing information for paediatric dosing.

### Elderly use
- Limited data in patients aged ≥ 65 years. No specific dose adjustments are established, but consider age-related declines in hepatic and renal function and concomitant conditions/medications.

### Renal impairment
- Lamivudine clearance is decreased in renal impairment leading to increased exposure. The fixed-dose combination is:
  - Not recommended in patients with creatinine clearance < 30 mL/min.
  - In patients with CrCl 30–49 mL/min, lamivudine exposure may increase 1.6–3.3 fold; monitor for lamivudine-related adverse events (particularly neutropenia and anaemia). If dose reduction of lamivudine is required, discontinue the fixed-dose combination and use individual components to permit dosing adjustment.

### Hepatic impairment
- No dose adjustment required for mild to moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh A or B).
- Not recommended in severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C) due to lack of data.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Not fully specified in available sources for this fixed‑dose combination as a combined statement. (Individual component mechanisms are referenced in source documents but exact text is not provided in the available extracts.)

### Pharmacokinetics (selected information available)
- Dolutegravir:
  - Primary elimination: metabolism mainly via UGT1A1 with minor CYP3A contribution.
  - In vitro: substrate of UGT1A3, UGT1A9, P-gp, BCRP.
  - In vitro inhibition of renal transporters OCT2, MATE1 and MATE2‑K; potential to increase exposure of OCT2/MATE1 substrates (e.g., metformin, dofetilide, pilsicainide, fampridine).
  - In vivo: small increases in serum creatinine reflect inhibition of tubular secretion, not reduced glomerular filtration.
- Lamivudine:
  - Predominantly renally cleared with limited metabolism.
  - In vitro substrate of P-gp, BCRP, OCT1, OCT2, MATE1 and MATE2‑K; clinically significant interactions are uncommon but plasma concentrations increase with reduced renal function and with some transporter inhibitors (e.g., trimethoprim) — not requiring dose adjustment in the trimethoprim interaction described.
- Absorption/distribution/excretion numeric parameters (e.g., Cmax, AUC, half-life) are not specified in available sources.
