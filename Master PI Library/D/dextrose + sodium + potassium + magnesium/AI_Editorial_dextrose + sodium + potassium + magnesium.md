# Editorial Product Information: dextrose + sodium + potassium + magnesium

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): dextrose + sodium + potassium + magnesium
- Chemical class:
  - Dextrose (D‑glucose monohydrate) — carbohydrate (hexose sugar)
  - Sodium — electrolyte (sodium chloride providing Na+ and Cl–)
  - Potassium — electrolyte (provided as potassium acetate)
  - Magnesium — electrolyte (provided as magnesium acetate)
  - Acetate anion (from potassium acetate and magnesium acetate) — metabolizable bicarbonate precursor
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration: Intravenous infusion

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Marketing start date stated as 02/08/2021 [US].
- Date of last revision/update of source PI: March 2020 and Revised: October 2025 are recorded in available source material [US].
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Parenteral maintenance of routine daily fluid and electrolyte requirements with provision of minimal carbohydrate calories from dextrose.
- Magnesium present in the formulation may help prevent iatrogenic magnesium deficiency during prolonged parenteral therapy.
- Jurisdictional specification:
  - Indication language above is provided in the available source [US].

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Dose depends on age, weight, and clinical condition.
- A daily total volume of 1500 mL per m² body surface area is cited as meeting usual adult daily water and principal electrolyte requirements in patients unable to take oral fluids.
- Usual daily maintenance for an average adult (approximately 70 kg, 1.8 m²) is approximately 3 liters.

### Dosing by indication
- Specific disease- or indication-based dosing regimens beyond maintenance fluid guidance are Not specified in available sources.

### Paediatric fluid requirements (guidance)
- Average pediatric daily fluid requirements (weight‑based) presented in source:
  - Up to 10 kg: 100 mL/kg/day
  - 11–20 kg: 1,000 mL + 50 mL/kg for each kg >10 kg/day
  - Above 20 kg: 1,500 mL + 20 mL/kg for each kg >20 kg/day

### Administration instructions
- Administer by intravenous infusion.
- Inspect solution visually prior to use; do not administer unless solution is clear and container is undamaged.
- Single‑dose container; when smaller doses are required, discard unused portion.
- When adding medications:
  - Use aseptic technique.
  - Prepare additive port and inject via resealable port/diaphragm with appropriate needle.
  - Mix contents thoroughly after adding medication.
  - Do not store solutions after additives are introduced.
- Do not connect flexible containers in series.
- Regulate infusion rate according to institutional policy and the patient’s clinical condition.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- None specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Use with great care, if at all, in patients with congestive heart failure, severe renal insufficiency, and clinical states with edema and sodium retention because of the potential for sodium‑related fluid retention.
- Solutions containing potassium should be used with great care, if at all, in patients with hyperkalemia, severe renal failure, and conditions associated with potassium retention.
- In patients with diminished renal function, administration of solutions containing sodium or potassium may lead to retention of these ions.
- Solutions containing acetate should be used with great care in patients with metabolic or respiratory alkalosis and in conditions in which acetate levels are increased or acetate utilization is impaired (e.g., severe hepatic insufficiency). (The product information notes that acetate is converted to bicarbonate by metabolic pathways and states conversion has been shown to proceed even in severe liver disease; nonetheless, caution is advised where acetate utilization may be impaired.)
- Administration can cause fluid and/or solute overloading resulting in:
  - Dilution of serum electrolyte concentrations
  - Overhydration
  - Congested states (peripheral and pulmonary edema)
- Caution when giving parenteral fluids containing sodium to patients receiving corticosteroids or corticotropin.
- Solutions containing dextrose should be used with caution in patients with known subclinical or overt diabetes mellitus.
- In very low birth weight infants, excessive or rapid administration of dextrose may increase serum osmolality and has been associated with possible intracerebral hemorrhage.

### Monitoring requirements
- Clinical evaluation and periodic laboratory determinations (electrolytes, fluid balance, acid–base status, serum glucose in at‑risk patients) are necessary during prolonged parenteral therapy or whenever clinically warranted.
- Frequent monitoring of serum glucose concentrations is required when dextrose is administered to pediatric patients, particularly neonates and low birth weight infants.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Use aseptic technique for additives and administration.
- Do not use unless solution is clear and container is intact.
- Avoid rapid excessive volume or solute administration in neonates and very small infants.
- When adverse reaction occurs, discontinue infusion, evaluate the patient, institute appropriate therapeutic measures, and retain remaining fluid for possible examination.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Additives may be incompatible with this solution. Consult a pharmacist when available before introducing additives.
- To avoid precipitation of calcium salts, this solution does not contain calcium; when introducing other drugs that may interact with calcium, observe caution to prevent precipitation.
- Caution: administration of solutions containing sodium may interact clinically with corticosteroids or corticotropin therapy (increased risk of sodium retention/overload).
- Pharmacodynamic interactions:
  - Concomitant use with agents that impair renal potassium excretion or that raise serum potassium may increase risk of hyperkalemia (exercise caution).
  - Concomitant use with agents affecting fluid balance (e.g., diuretics, IV fluids) requires careful monitoring of volume status and electrolytes.
- Pharmacokinetic interactions: Not specified in available sources.
- Management notes:
  - When adding medications, use aseptic technique, mix thoroughly, and do not store.
  - Inspect parenteral products for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Safety and effectiveness in the pediatric population are based on the similarity of clinical conditions between pediatric and adult patients per available source.
- Neonates and very small infants:
  - Volume of administered fluid may markedly affect fluid and electrolyte balance.
  - Frequent monitoring of serum glucose concentrations is required when dextrose is prescribed to pediatric patients, particularly neonates and low birth weight infants.
  - Excessive or rapid administration of dextrose in very low birth weight infants may increase serum osmolality and has been associated with possible intracerebral hemorrhage.
- Specific pediatric dosing adjustments beyond the provided weight‑based fluid requirement guidance are Not specified in available sources.

### Elderly use
- Dose adjustments or specific cautions for elderly beyond general warnings regarding renal/cardiac function are Not specified in available sources.

### Renal impairment
- Use with great care in patients with severe renal insufficiency; risk of sodium, potassium, and magnesium retention exists.
- Magnesium is excreted primarily and proportionally by the kidney; impaired renal function increases risk of magnesium accumulation.
- Specific dosing adjustments by eGFR or renal stage are Not specified in available sources.

### Hepatic impairment
- Solutions containing acetate should be used with caution in severe hepatic insufficiency because of potential impaired utilization of acetate; however, source material also notes acetate-to-bicarbonate conversion has been observed even in severe liver disease. Clinical judgment required.
- Specific dosing adjustments by severity of hepatic impairment are Not specified in available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Provides water, electrolytes (sodium, chloride, potassium, magnesium) and minimal carbohydrate calories (dextrose) for parenteral maintenance of daily fluid and electrolyte requirements.
- Dextrose supplies readily available carbohydrate energy and can spare protein by providing calories.
- Acetate anions are metabolized (primarily in the liver) to bicarbonate, serving as an alkalinizing/buffer precursor.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: Not applicable (intravenous administration).
- Distribution and metabolism:
  - Dextrose (parenteral) is oxidized to carbon dioxide and water.
  - Sodium chloride dissociates to sodium and chloride ions; distribution and excretion regulated largely by renal mechanisms.
  - Potassium acetate dissociates to potassium and acetate ions; potassium is largely excreted in urine (≈80–90% of intake); distribution between intracellular and extracellular compartments is physiologic.
  - Magnesium acetate dissociates to magnesium and acetate ions; magnesium is excreted by the kidney at a rate proportional to plasma concentration and glomerular filtration.
  - Acetate is metabolically converted to bicarbonate in the liver; source material indicates this conversion proceeds readily, including in severe liver disease, but caution is still advised where acetate utilization may be impaired.
- Elimination: Electrolyte elimination primarily renal; magnesium excreted solely via kidney. No quantitative PK parameters (e.g., clearance, half‑life) are specified.
- Detailed pharmacokinetic parameters: Not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Restores/maintains plasma glucose and provides calories.
- Restores extracellular fluid electrolytes and helps maintain osmotic and acid–base balance via acetate metabolism to bicarbonate.
- Electrolyte effects:
  - Sodium: principal extracellular cation; important in fluid balance.
  - Chloride: important in buffering within red blood cells and acid–base balance.
  - Potassium: primary intracellular cation; important in neuromuscular function and electrolyte balance.
  - Magnesium: intracellular cation; cofactor for enzymatic reactions; important in neuromuscular transmission and excitability.
