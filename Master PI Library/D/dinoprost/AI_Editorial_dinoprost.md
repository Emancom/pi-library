# Product Information: Dinoprostone (Prostaglandin E2)

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Active Ingredient:** Dinoprostone (Prostaglandin E2; PGE2)

**Available Formulations:**
*   **Controlled-Release Vaginal Insert:** A polymeric matrix containing 10 mg of dinoprostone, designed to release approximately 0.3 mg per hour over a 24-hour period.
*   **Endocervical Gel:** A syringe-based gel containing 0.5 mg of dinoprostone per 3 g (2.5 mL) for local application.

## 2. Approved Indications
Dinoprostone is indicated for the initiation and/or continuation of cervical ripening in pregnant patients at or near term (from 37 completed weeks of gestation) who have a medical or obstetrical requirement for the induction of labour.

## 3. Contraindications
Dinoprostone should not be administered to patients in the following circumstances:
*   Known hypersensitivity to dinoprostone, other prostaglandins, or any excipients (e.g., urethane, silica, triacetin).
*   Presence of spontaneous labour.
*   Concurrent administration of oxytocic agents or other labour-inducing drugs.
*   Multiparity: specifically patients with a history of six or more previous term pregnancies [FDA] or more than three full-term deliveries [TGA].
*   Non-vertex fetal presentation or multiple-fetus pregnancy [TGA].
*   History of major uterine surgery, including caesarean section or myomectomy, or history of uterine rupture or cervical surgery [TGA/FDA].
*   Evidence of cephalopelvic disproportion.
*   Suspected or confirmed fetal distress or abnormal cardiotocography (CTG).
*   Conditions where vaginal delivery is inappropriate (e.g., placenta praevia, vasa previa, or active herpes genitalis).
*   Unexplained vaginal bleeding during the current pregnancy.
*   Current pelvic inflammatory disease (unless previously treated effectively).

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
**Hospital Use Only:** Dinoprostone must only be administered in a hospital setting by trained obstetric personnel. Facilities for continuous electronic fetal monitoring and immediate intensive care/surgical intervention must be available.

**Uterine Activity and Monitoring:** The clinician must carefully assess the cervix (Bishop score) before use. Following administration, uterine activity, fetal heart rate, and cervical progression must be monitored regularly. If uterine hyperstimulation, hypertonic contractions, or fetal distress occur, the medication must be removed (insert) or managed conservatively (gel).

**Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC):** There is an increased risk of postpartum DIC in women whose labour is induced pharmacologically. This risk is further elevated in women who are:
*   Aged 30 years or older [FDA] or 35 years or older [TGA].
*   Experiencing pregnancy complications (e.g., gestational diabetes, hypertension, hypothyroidism).
*   At a gestational age exceeding 40 weeks.

**Amniotic Fluid Embolism:** Clinicians should remain alert to the rare risk of Anaphylactoid Syndrome of Pregnancy (Amniotic Fluid Embolism) caused by the inadvertent abruption of the placenta and subsequent embolisation of antigenic tissue.

**Specific Comorbidities:** Use with caution in patients with a history of asthma, glaucoma, raised intraocular pressure, epilepsy, or compromised cardiovascular, hepatic, or renal function.

## 5. Dosage and Administration
Dinoprostone should be brought to the appropriate temperature before use (refer to Storage Conditions). Avoid skin contact during handling to prevent accidental absorption.

### Controlled-Release Vaginal Insert (10 mg)
*   **Insertion:** One insert is placed transversely high into the posterior vaginal fornix. Only a minimal amount of water-soluble lubricant should be used.
*   **Post-Insertion:** The patient should remain recumbent for 30 minutes.
*   **Removal:** The insert should be removed by gentle traction on the retrieval tape:
    1.  At the onset of labour (regular painful contractions every 3 minutes).
    2.  If membranes rupture spontaneously or via amniotomy.
    3.  If uterine hyperstimulation occurs.
    4.  After 24 hours of use.
*   **Sequential Oxytocin Use:** A waiting period of at least 30 minutes is required after removing the insert before starting oxytocin.

### Endocervical Gel (0.5 mg)
*   **Application:** The gel is administered into the cervical canal just below the level of the internal os using the provided sterile catheter.
*   **Post-Administration:** The patient should remain supine for 15 to 30 minutes to prevent leakage.
*   **Repeat Dosing:** If no response is observed, a repeat dose of 0.5 mg may be given after 6 hours. The maximum cumulative dose is 1.5 mg within a 24-hour period.
*   **Sequential Oxytocin Use:** A dosing interval of 6 to 12 hours is recommended after the last gel application before starting oxytocin [FDA].

## 6. Adverse Effects
**Maternal Effects:**
*   Uterine contractile abnormalities (hypertonus, tachysystole, or hyperstimulation).
*   Uterine rupture.
*   Gastrointestinal symptoms (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea).
*   Hypotension and tachycardia.
*   Warmth or irritation in the vagina.
*   Postpartum hemorrhage and DIC (rare).

**Fetal/Neonatal Effects:**
*   Fetal heart rate disorders (bradycardia, decelerations).
*   Fetal distress syndrome and neonatal depression (Apgar score < 7).
*   Meconium in the amniotic fluid.

## 7. Drug Interactions
Prostaglandins significantly potentiate the effects of other uterotonic medicines. Concurrent use of dinoprostone with oxytocin is contraindicated. If sequential use is required, clinicians must adhere to the specific waiting periods (30 minutes for the insert; 6–12 hours for the gel). 

Treatment with Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs (NSAIDs), including aspirin, should be discontinued prior to the administration of dinoprostone, as they may interfere with prostaglandin efficacy.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
**Pregnancy (Category C):** Dinoprostone is intended for use only at term for induction of labour. Animal studies have shown embryotoxicity and skeletal anomalies at high doses, though these are not expected clinically when the drug is used as indicated after organogenesis is complete.

**Breastfeeding:** While dinoprostone may be excreted into colostrum and breast milk, levels are expected to be very low and short-lived. Clinical data suggests the medication does not hinder breastfeeding, and no adverse effects have been noted in breastfed newborns.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Following intravaginal or endocervical administration, dinoprostone is absorbed into the local tissue and systemic circulation.
*   **Distribution:** Approximately 73% of the drug is bound to human plasma albumin.
*   **Metabolism:** Dinoprostone is rapidly metabolized in the lungs, kidneys, and liver. About 90% is cleared during the first pass. The primary metabolite is 13,14-dihydro-15-keto-PGE2.
*   **Excretion:** Elimination occurs primarily through the kidneys. The plasma half-life of dinoprostone is less than one minute.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Dinoprostone (PGE2) is a naturally occurring hormone that plays a critical role in cervical ripening. It triggers biochemical changes in the cervix, including the activation of collagenase, which breaks down the structural collagen network. This process transforms the cervix from a rigid structure into a softened, dilated state suitable for delivery. Additionally, PGE2 can directly stimulate the myometrium to induce uterine contractions.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Vaginal Insert:** Must be stored in a freezer below -18°C. It should be removed from the freezer immediately before use (no thawing required). Controlled storage at 2-8°C is permissible for up to one month [TGA].
*   **Endocervical Gel:** Must be stored under continuous refrigeration between 2°C and 8°C (36°F to 46°F). The gel should be brought to room temperature (15–30°C) naturally before administration; do not use external heat sources.
