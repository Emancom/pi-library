# Unified Product Information: Dydrogesterone

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Active Ingredient:** Dydrogesterone
**Common Co-formulation:** Estradiol (as hemihydrate)

Dydrogesterone is a synthetic, orally active progestogen that is chemically related to naturally occurring progesterone.

## 2. Approved Indications
Dydrogesterone is indicated for:
*   **Hormone Replacement Therapy (HRT):** To treat symptoms of estrogen deficiency caused by natural or surgical menopause in women who have an intact uterus.
*   **Osteoporosis Prevention:** The prevention of postmenopausal bone mineral density loss in women at high risk of future fractures who are unable to use other approved bone-sparing medications [TGA].

The clinical objective of adding dydrogesterone to estrogen therapy is to protect the endometrium from overstimulation.

## 3. Contraindications
Dydrogesterone must not be used in patients with:
*   Confirmed or suspected pregnancy or during lactation.
*   Known or suspected neoplasms that are progestogen-dependent (e.g., meningioma) or estrogen-dependent (e.g., endometrial cancer) [TGA].
*   Current or previous breast cancer.
*   Undiagnosed abnormal genital bleeding.
*   Active or history of venous thromboembolism (VTE), including deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism.
*   Active or recent arterial thromboembolic disease (e.g., myocardial infarction, angina).
*   Acute or chronic liver disease, especially where liver function tests have not returned to normal levels.
*   Untreated endometrial hyperplasia.
*   Porphyria.
*   Known hypersensitivity to dydrogesterone or any of the product’s excipients.

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Cardiovascular and Cerebrovascular Risks
HRT should not be utilized for the prevention of cardiovascular disease or dementia. Evidence from the Women’s Health Initiative (WHI) study indicates an increased risk of stroke and coronary heart disease (CHD) in women using combined HRT. Therapy should be discontinued immediately if a thromboembolic event is suspected.

### Venous Thromboembolism (VTE)
There is a 1.3 to 3-fold higher risk of developing VTE with HRT, particularly during the first year of use. Patients with known thrombophilic states or significant risk factors (obesity, advanced age, major surgery) should be carefully evaluated.

### Malignant Neoplasms
*   **Breast Cancer:** Prolonged use of combined estrogen-progestogen therapy is associated with an increased risk of breast cancer, which typically becomes apparent after 3 years of use [TGA].
*   **Endometrial Cancer:** In women with an intact uterus, the risk of endometrial hyperplasia and carcinoma is significantly increased with unopposed estrogen. Dydrogesterone is added to mitigate this specific risk.
*   **Meningioma:** The use of dydrogesterone has been linked to the occurrence of meningiomas. If a meningioma is diagnosed, treatment must be halted immediately [TGA].

### Medical Examination and Follow-up
Prior to starting therapy, a full medical and family history must be recorded. Physical examinations, including pelvic and breast exams (and mammography), should be conducted regularly. 

### Other Conditions
*   **Liver Function:** Therapy should be stopped if jaundice or a significant decline in liver function occurs.
*   **Fluid Retention:** Progestogens may cause fluid retention; patients with renal or cardiac dysfunction require close monitoring.
*   **Metabolic Effects:** Estrogens and progestogens may influence glucose tolerance and lipid metabolism.

## 5. Dosage and Administration
For postmenopausal symptoms, dydrogesterone is typically administered as part of a continuous combined regimen:
*   **Standard Dose:** One tablet (containing dydrogesterone, often 5 mg, with estradiol) taken orally once daily without interruption.
*   **Missed Dose:** If a dose is forgotten, it should be taken as soon as possible. If more than 12 hours have passed, the missed dose should be skipped, and the next tablet taken at the usual time. Missing doses may increase the risk of breakthrough bleeding [TGA].

Treatment should always utilize the lowest effective dose for the shortest possible duration.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most frequently reported adverse reactions include:
*   **Nervous System:** Migraine, headache (very common), dizziness.
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Abdominal pain (very common), nausea, flatulence.
*   **Reproductive:** Breast pain/tenderness (very common), breakthrough bleeding, spotting, pelvic pain.
*   **General:** Asthenia, weight changes, peripheral oedema.

Rare but serious adverse effects include myocardial infarction, gallbladder disease, and hypersensitivity reactions (urticaria, angioedema) [TGA].

## 7. Drug Interactions
The metabolism of dydrogesterone may be accelerated by drugs that induce hepatic enzymes (specifically CYP450 3A4), including:
*   **Anticonvulsants:** Phenobarbital, phenytoin, carbamazepine.
*   **Anti-infectives:** Rifampicin, rifabutin, nevirapine, efavirenz.
*   **Herbal Preparations:** St. John's Wort.

Conversely, dydrogesterone/estradiol may interfere with the metabolism of other drugs like tacrolimus, cyclosporin, and theophylline, potentially leading to toxic plasma levels. Caution is also advised when co-administering with certain Hepatitis C regimens (e.g., ombitasvir/paritaprevir/ritonavir) due to the risk of ALT elevations [TGA].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** Dydrogesterone is contraindicated during pregnancy. It is not intended for use in women of childbearing potential.
*   **Breastfeeding:** This medication is contraindicated during lactation. Estrogens and progestogens are excreted in human milk and can reduce the quantity and quality of milk produced.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Dydrogesterone is rapidly absorbed following oral administration.
*   **Metabolism:** It is extensively metabolized in the liver. The primary metabolite is 20α-dihydrodydrogesterone (DHD).
*   **Excretion:** Approximately 63% of the dose is excreted via the urine. Elimination is typically complete within 72 hours [TGA].
*   **Special Populations:** In patients with terminal renal insufficiency, the levels of circulating active ingredients may be increased.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Dydrogesterone is an orally active progestogen that produces a complete secretory endometrium in an estrogen-primed uterus. Unlike some synthetic progestogens, dydrogesterone does not have androgenic or glucocorticoid activity at therapeutic doses. In continuous combined HRT, it prevents the estrogen-induced risk of endometrial hyperplasia by maintaining an atrophic endometrial state.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   Store at temperatures below 30°C.
*   Keep the medication in its original packaging to protect it from moisture.
*   Do not use after the expiry date indicated on the blister pack or carton.
