# Unified Product Information: Deoxycholic Acid Injection

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Active Ingredient:** Deoxycholic acid  
**Concentration:** 10 mg/mL  

Deoxycholic acid is a synthetically derived, sterile, clear, colorless, and cytolytic solution intended for subcutaneous injection. The formulation typically includes inactive ingredients such as sodium phosphate dibasic, sodium chloride, and sodium hydroxide in water for injection, adjusted to a pH of 8.3.

## 2. Approved Indications
Deoxycholic acid is indicated for the improvement of the appearance of moderate to severe convexity or fullness associated with submental fat (SMF) in adult patients.

**Limitations of Use:**
*   The safety and efficacy of this drug for the treatment of subcutaneous fat outside the submental region have not been established and are not recommended [FDA].
*   The use of deoxycholic acid is not recommended in patients with mild or extreme submental fat, as efficacy in these populations has not been established [TGA].

## 3. Contraindications
*   **Active Infection:** The drug must not be administered in the presence of an infection at the proposed injection sites [FDA, TGA].
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to deoxycholic acid or any of the inactive components in the formulation is a contraindication [TGA].

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Injection Site Safety
*   **Marginal Mandibular Nerve (MMN) Injury:** Injections placed too close to the MMN may cause temporary motor neuropraxia, resulting in an asymmetrical smile or facial muscle weakness (paresis). To avoid this, injections must be limited to the target submental fat area and must not be placed above or within 1–1.5 cm of the inferior border of the mandible.
*   **Dysphagia:** Difficulty swallowing can occur as a secondary effect of administration site reactions (e.g., pain or swelling). Use is not recommended in patients with a history of or current dysphagia, as the treatment may worsen the condition.
*   **Tissue Damage (Ulceration and Necrosis):** Injection into the dermis (too superficial) increases the risk of skin ulceration and necrosis. If these occur, subsequent treatments should be withheld until the area has fully healed.
*   **Alopecia:** Permanent or temporary hair loss at the injection site has been reported. Withhold treatment until resolution [FDA].

### Anatomical Considerations
*   **Vulnerable Structures:** Care must be taken to avoid injecting into or in close proximity to the thyroid gland, salivary glands, lymph nodes, muscles, and major blood vessels. Inadvertent intravascular injection may result in vascular injury.
*   **Pre-existing Conditions:** Healthcare providers should screen for other causes of submental fullness, such as thyromegaly or cervical lymphadenopathy. 
*   **Structural Concerns:** Use caution in patients with excessive skin laxity, prominent platysmal bands, or significant scar tissue from prior surgeries, as these factors may lead to an aesthetically undesirable outcome or impeded administration.

### Dietary Sodium
Each mL of the solution contains approximately 4.23 mg (184 micromol) of sodium. This should be considered for patients on a strictly controlled sodium diet [TGA].

## 5. Dosage and Administration
### General Requirements
This product should only be administered by a qualified healthcare professional who has a thorough understanding of submental anatomy and the associated neuromuscular structures.

### Dosing Regimen
*   **Dose:** 2 mg/cm².
*   **Injection Volume:** 0.2 mL per injection site.
*   **Spacing:** Injections should be spaced 1 cm apart across the defined treatment area.
*   **Maximum Dose:** No more than 50 injections (10 mL) should be administered in a single session.
*   **Frequency:** Up to 6 single treatments may be performed, with intervals between sessions being no less than one month.

### Technique
The drug must be injected into the pre-platysmal subcutaneous fat tissue. The needle (typically 30 gauge, 0.5-inch) should be inserted perpendicular to the skin. Prior to treatment, the area should be palpated to ensure sufficient fat and to identify anatomical landmarks. Use of a surgical pen and a 1 cm injection grid is recommended.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience
The most frequently reported adverse reactions (>20% of patients) involve the injection site:
*   Edema and swelling (very common, typically resolving within 30 days)
*   Hematoma and bruising (61–72% incidence)
*   Pain and numbness
*   Erythema (redness)
*   Induration (hardness)

Other common effects include headache, nausea, hypertension, and skin tightness. Less common but significant effects include MMN injury (2.0% incidence) and dysphagia (1.1% incidence).

### Post-Marketing Experience
Since approval, the following additional reactions have been reported:
*   **Administration Site:** Scarring, infection (cellulitis/abscess), and injection site mass.
*   **Nervous System:** Oral hypoesthesia and paresthesia.
*   **Vascular:** Injury due to inadvertent intravascular injection.

## 7. Drug Interactions
No formal clinical drug-drug interaction studies have been conducted. In vitro data suggest that deoxycholic acid does not significantly inhibit or induce Cytochrome P450 (CYP) enzymes or major drug transporters at clinically relevant doses, suggesting a low potential for systemic interactions.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
**TGA Pregnancy Category: B1**  
Animal reproduction studies in rats and rabbits did not show evidence of fetal harm at doses significantly higher than the maximum recommended human dose. However, because animal studies are not always predictive of human response, use during pregnancy is not recommended [TGA, FDA].

### Breastfeeding
It is unknown whether synthetic deoxycholic acid is excreted in human milk. Because of the lack of clinical data, the drug is not recommended for use in nursing women.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed following subcutaneous injection. Median Tmax occurs between 18 minutes [FDA] and 30 minutes [TGA].
*   **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (98%). The volume of distribution is estimated at 193 L.
*   **Metabolism and Excretion:** Exogenous deoxycholic acid joins the endogenous bile acid pool and is excreted primarily intact in the feces. Post-treatment plasma levels return to the normal endogenous range within 24 hours.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Deoxycholic acid is a cytolytic agent. When injected into localized adipose tissue, it physically disrupts the cell membranes of adipocytes (fat cells), leading to cell lysis. The resulting cellular debris is cleared by macrophages through natural inflammatory processes. This tissue response is followed by the appearance of fibroblasts and the thickening of fibrous septa (neocollagenesis), which may contribute to a firmer appearance in the treated area.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   Store the product at room temperature.
*   **TGA Guideline:** Store below 30°C.
*   **FDA Guideline:** Store at 20°C to 25°C, with excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C.
*   Vials are for single-use in a single patient only. Any unused portion must be discarded. Do not dilute or mix with other compounds.
