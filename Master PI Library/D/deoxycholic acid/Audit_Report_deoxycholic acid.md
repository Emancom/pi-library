# Originality Audit Report: deoxycholic acid

## Audit Summary
- **Originality Score:** 72/100
- **Verbatim Copied Passages Found:** 4
- **Overall Assessment:** Pass (with minor editorial revisions recommended for specific sections)

## Verbatim Copied Passages

1.  **Section: 2. Approved Indications**
    *   **Editorial Text:** "indicated for the improvement of the appearance of moderate to severe convexity or fullness associated with submental fat (SMF) in adult patients."
    *   **Source Match:** Document 1 (TGA) Page 1 and Document 2 (FDA) Page 1. Both state: "indicated for improvement in the appearance of moderate to severe convexity or fullness associated with submental fat in adults."
    *   **Location:** Section 2, Paragraph 1.

2.  **Section: 4. Warnings and Precautions (MMN Injury)**
    *   **Editorial Text:** "resulting in an asymmetrical smile or facial muscle weakness (paresis)."
    *   **Source Match:** Document 1 (TGA) Page 4. States: "manifests as an asymmetric smile or facial muscle weakness."
    *   **Location:** Section 4, "Marginal Mandibular Nerve (MMN) Injury," Sentence 1.

3.  **Section: 10. Mechanism of Action**
    *   **Editorial Text:** "physically disrupts the cell membranes of adipocytes (fat cells), leading to cell lysis."
    *   **Source Match:** Document 1 (TGA) Page 8. States: "physically disrupts the cell membrane of adipocytes and causes cell lysis."
    *   **Location:** Section 10, Sentence 2.

4.  **Section: 10. Mechanism of Action**
    *   **Editorial Text:** "followed by the appearance of fibroblasts and the thickening of fibrous septa (neocollagenesis)"
    *   **Source Match:** Document 1 (TGA) Page 8. States: "followed by the appearance of fibroblasts and observed thickening of fibrous septa, likely due to an increase in total collagen (i.e., neocollagenesis)."
    *   **Location:** Section 10, Sentence 4.

## Close Paraphrases

1.  **Section: 4. Warnings (Tissue Damage)**
    *   **Editorial Text:** "Injections placed too close to the MMN may cause temporary motor neuropraxia... injections must be limited to the target submental fat area and must not be placed above or within 1–1.5 cm of the inferior border of the mandible."
    *   **Source:** Document 1 (TGA) Page 2. The instruction regarding the 1-1.5 cm line is a technical requirement that is difficult to paraphrase while maintaining safety, but the structure is identical to the source.

2.  **Section: 5. Dosage and Administration (Technique)**
    *   **Editorial Text:** "The needle (typically 30 gauge, 0.5-inch) should be inserted perpendicular to the skin... palpated to ensure sufficient fat and to identify anatomical landmarks."
    *   **Source:** Document 1 (TGA) Page 3. The wording is very similar to "ensure sufficient submental fat... 30 gauge (or smaller) 0.5-inch needle... advancing the needle perpendicular to the skin."

3.  **Section: 9. Pharmacokinetics (Metabolism/Excretion)**
    *   **Editorial Text:** "Exogenous deoxycholic acid joins the endogenous bile acid pool and is excreted primarily intact in the feces."
    *   **Source:** Document 1 (TGA) Page 11. States: "Deoxycholic acid from BELKYRA injection joins the endogenous bile acid pool and is excreted intact in faeces."

## Originality Strengths
*   **Safety Data Synthesis:** The editorial does an excellent job of merging the "Limitations of Use" from both agencies into a single, cohesive bulleted list.
*   **Pharmacokinetic Comparison:** Section 9 successfully highlights the differences between FDA and TGA data regarding median Tmax (18 vs. 30 minutes) rather than just picking one.
*   **Storage Harmonization:** Section 11 is an original synthesis that clearly distinguishes the storage requirements of the two different regulatory bodies in a helpful, comparative format.
*   **Post-Marketing Synthesis:** Section 6 combines the different reporting styles of the TGA and FDA into a unified "Experience" section.

## Differences from Source PIs
*   **Unified Terminology:** The editorial uses the generic "deoxycholic acid" consistently, whereas the sources use brand names (BELKYRA/KYBELLA).
*   **Cross-Referencing:** The editorial adds specific citations (e.g., "[FDA]", "[TGA]") to help the reader understand which agency provides which specific guideline.
*   **Simplified Adverse Effects:** The editorial condenses the massive clinical trial tables into a high-level summary of "most frequently reported" reactions, making it more readable for an editorial product.
*   **Omission of Trial Details:** The editorial omits specific study design details (Trial 1 vs Trial 2 percentages) in favor of a general range (61–72% for hematoma).

## Recommendation
**PASS**

**Reasoning:** While there are instances of near-verbatim copying in the "Indications" and "Mechanism of Action" sections, these are standard medical definitions where clinical accuracy is paramount. The editorial shows high originality in how it synthesizes conflicting data (storage, Tmax, and safety limitations) into a unified format.

**Suggested Revision:** To improve the originality score, Section 10 (Mechanism of Action) should be slightly reworded to avoid the "physically disrupts... cell lysis" and "appearance of fibroblasts" phrasing, as these are direct lifts from the TGA document. 

*   *Suggested Revision for MOA:* "The solution acts as a surfactant that breaks down the integrity of fat cell walls upon contact. This process of adipocytolysis triggers a localized inflammatory response where the body's natural waste-clearing cells (macrophages) remove the resulting debris. As the site heals, new connective tissue forms, which can result in a visible tightening of the treated region."
