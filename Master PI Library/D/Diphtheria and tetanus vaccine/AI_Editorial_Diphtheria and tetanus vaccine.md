# Editorial Product Information: Diphtheria and Tetanus Toxoids and Acellular Pertussis Adsorbed, Hepatitis B (Recombinant) and Inactivated Poliovirus Vaccine (PEDIARIX)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **United States (FDA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational use by healthcare professionals and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians must consult local regulations and the current approved PI for their specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** 
    - Diphtheria toxoid
    - Tetanus toxoid
    - Acellular pertussis antigens: Pertussis toxin (PT), filamentous hemagglutinin (FHA), and pertactin
    - Hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) (Recombinant)
    - Inactivated poliovirus (IPV) (Types 1, 2, and 3)
- **Brand Name:** **PEDIARIX** [US]
- **ATC Code:** J07CA02 (Diphtheria-hemophilus influenzae B-pertussis-tetanus-hepatitis B-poliomyelitis)
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injectable suspension supplied in 0.5 mL single-dose prefilled syringes.
- **Route of Administration:** Intramuscular (IM) injection only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** GlaxoSmithKline Biologicals SA [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Active Immunization:** Indicated for protection against diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis, poliomyelitis, and all known subtypes of hepatitis B virus.
- **Patient Population:** Approved for use in children from 6 weeks of age through 6 years of age (prior to the seventh birthday).
- **Specific Requirement:** Approved as a 3-dose series in infants born to mothers who are hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg)-negative [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** A primary series consists of three doses (0.5 mL each).
- **Standard Schedule:** Administered at 2, 4, and 6 months of age. 
- **Dosing Intervals:** Intervals of 6 to 8 weeks are recommended (preferably 8 weeks). The first dose may be given as early as 6 weeks of age.
- **Interchangeability and Modified Schedules:**
    - **DTaP:** May be used to complete the first three doses of the DTaP series in infants who received 1 or 2 doses of **INFANRIX**; data are unavailable for following other manufacturers' DTaP vaccines [US].
    - **Hepatitis B:** May complete the series in infants born to HBsAg-negative mothers who received 1 or 2 doses of other Hep B vaccines (including other manufacturers).
    - **IPV:** May complete the first three doses of the IPV series in infants who received 1 or 2 doses of IPV from other manufacturers.
- **Booster Doses:** **PEDIARIX** is not indicated for booster doses. Children who complete the 3-dose primary series should receive subsequent DTaP and IPV boosters (e.g., **INFANRIX** or **KINRIX**) according to the recommended schedule [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Shake vigorously to obtain a homogeneous, turbid, white suspension. 
    - Administer via IM injection. **Do not administer in the gluteal area** or near major nerve trunks, as gluteal injection may result in a suboptimal hepatitis B immune response.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known history of severe allergic reaction (e.g., anaphylaxis) to any component of the vaccine, including yeast, neomycin, and polymyxin B, or following a previous dose of any diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis, hepatitis B, or poliovirus-containing vaccine.
- **Encephalopathy:** Any encephalopathy (e.g., coma, decreased level of consciousness, prolonged seizures) occurring within 7 days of a previous pertussis-containing vaccine not attributable to another cause.
- **Neurologic Disorders:** Progressive neurologic disorders, including infantile spasms, uncontrolled epilepsy, or progressive encephalopathy. Vaccination should be deferred until the condition is stabilized.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Fever:** Clinical trials noted higher rates of fever when **PEDIARIX** was administered compared to separately administered vaccines.
- **Guillain-Barré Syndrome (GBS):** If GBS occurred within 6 weeks of a prior tetanus toxoid vaccine, the decision to administer **PEDIARIX** should be based on a careful risk-benefit assessment.
- **Syncope:** Fainting may occur with injectable vaccines; procedures should be in place to prevent injury from falls.
- **Pertussis-Related Precautions:** If any of the following occurred within 48 hours of a previous pertussis-containing vaccine, the decision to give **PEDIARIX** requires careful consideration:
    - Temperature ≥40.5°C (105°F) not due to another cause.
    - Collapse or shock-like state (hypotonic-hyporesponsive episode).
    - Persistent, inconsolable crying lasting ≥3 hours.
    - Seizures (with or without fever) occurring within 3 days.
- **Seizure Risk:** For children at higher risk for seizures, an antipyretic may be administered at the time of vaccination and for the following 24 hours.
- **Apnea in Premature Infants:** Apnea has been observed following IM vaccination in some premature infants. Decision-making should consider the infant's clinical status and the potential benefits/risks of vaccination.
- **Altered Immunocompetence:** Immunosuppressed persons may have a reduced immune response.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience
The following solicited adverse reactions were reported in ≥25% of participants within 4 days of vaccination:
- **Local:** Pain, redness, and swelling at the injection site.
- **General:** Fever (≥100.4°F), drowsiness, irritability/fussiness, and loss of appetite.

| Adverse Reaction | Frequency Range (Dose 1-3) |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Seizures:** Reported in approximately 0.22 per 1,000 doses in specific safety studies [US].
- **Sudden Infant Death Syndrome (SIDS):** Cases were reported in clinical trials at a rate of 0.25/1,000, which is consistent with background rates [US].

### Postmarketing Spontaneous Reports
- **Cardiac:** Cyanosis.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea, vomiting, intussusception.
- **Nervous System:** Bulging fontanelle, hypotonia, encephalitis, lethargy.
- **Immune:** Anaphylactic/anaphylactoid reactions, serum sickness.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Immunosuppressive Therapies:** Irradiation, antimetabolites, alkylating agents, cytotoxic drugs, and corticosteroids (in greater than physiologic doses) may reduce the immune response to the vaccine.
- **Concomitant Vaccines:** Clinical trials showed no evidence of interference when administered with Hib conjugate vaccines and 7-valent pneumococcal conjugate vaccines (PCV7) [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Not applicable. **PEDIARIX** is not indicated for use in adults. 
- **Fertility:** The vaccine has not been evaluated for its potential to impair fertility [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not applicable.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - Safety and effectiveness established for ages 6 weeks through 6 months based on clinical trials.
    - Supported for use up to 6 years of age by evidence from the younger cohort.
    - Not evaluated in infants younger than 6 weeks or children aged 7 to 16 years.
- **Premature Infants:** See *Warnings and Precautions (Section 5)* regarding apnea risk.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Diphtheria/Tetanus:** Induces neutralizing antibodies against toxins produced by *C. diphtheriae* and *C. tetani*.
    - **Pertussis:** Acellular antigens (PT, FHA, pertactin) contribute to immunity, though specific correlates of protection are not established.
    - **Hepatitis B:** Induces antibodies against HBsAg; levels ≥10 mIU/mL are considered protective.
    - **Poliomyelitis:** Induces neutralizing antibodies against poliovirus Types 1, 2, and 3.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy:** Absolute protective efficacy of the DTaP component (measured using **INFANRIX**) was 84% to 89% against WHO-defined typical pertussis.
- **Immunogenicity:** In a U.S. multicenter study, **PEDIARIX** demonstrated non-inferiority to separately administered vaccines for all components (diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis, polio, and hepatitis B) following a 3-dose primary series.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store refrigerated between 2°C and 8°C (36°F and 46°F).
- **Handling:** **Do not freeze.** Discard if the vaccine has been frozen.
- **Light:** Protect prefilled syringes from light.
- **Latex:** The tip cap and rubber plunger stopper of the prefilled syringes are not made with natural rubber latex.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Administration:** For professional use only.
- **Vaccine Information Statements (VIS):** In the United States, VIS must be provided to parents/guardians prior to immunization as required by the National Childhood Vaccine Injury Act of 1986.
