# Editorial Product Information: Dacomitinib

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of dacomitinib for healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical and regulatory information to support evidence-based practice.
*   **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved labelling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Dacomitinib (as monohydrate)
*   **Chemical Class:** Irreversible kinase inhibitor of the human epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) family.
*   **Brand Name:** **Vizimpro** [US]
*   **ATC Code:** L01EB07
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    *   **Film-coated tablets:** 15 mg, 30 mg, and 45 mg [US].
    *   **Appearance:** Blue, film-coated, round biconvex tablets. The 45 mg tablet is debossed with "Pfizer" and "DCB45"; the 30 mg with "Pfizer" and "DCB30"; and the 15 mg with "Pfizer" and "DCB15".
*   **Route of Administration:** Oral
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Laboratories Div Pfizer Inc [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Metastatic Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC):** Indicated for the first-line treatment of patients with metastatic NSCLC who harbour epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) exon 19 deletion or exon 21 L858R substitution mutations, as confirmed by an approved companion diagnostic test [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
*   **General Adult Dosing:** The recommended dose is 45 mg orally once daily. 
*   **Administration Instructions:** Tablets should be taken at the same time each day, with or without food. If a patient vomits or misses a dose, they should not take an additional or make-up dose but should proceed with the next scheduled dose.
*   **Dose Titration/Reduction:** For adverse reactions, the dose should be reduced in the following increments:
    *   **First reduction:** 30 mg once daily.
    *   **Second reduction:** 15 mg once daily.
*   **Concomitant Acid-Reducing Agents:** 
    *   **Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs):** Avoid concomitant use.
    *   **H2-receptor Antagonists:** Administer dacomitinib at least 6 hours before or 10 hours after the H2-receptor antagonist.
    *   **Antacids:** Locally acting antacids may be used without specific timing restrictions [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   None specified in available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD):** Severe and fatal ILD/pneumonitis has occurred (0.5% incidence). Monitor for new or worsening respiratory symptoms (dyspnoea, cough, fever). Withhold dacomitinib during investigation and permanently discontinue if ILD is confirmed.
*   **Diarrhea:** Highly prevalent (86%); severe or fatal cases have occurred. Withhold and reduce dose for Grade 2 or higher diarrhea until resolved to ≤Grade 1. Initiate anti-diarrheal pharmacotherapy (e.g., loperamide) at the first sign of loose stools.
*   **Dermatologic Adverse Reactions:** Rash and exfoliative skin conditions occur frequently (78%). Limit sun exposure and initiate moisturizers at treatment start. Withhold and reduce dose for persistent Grade 2 or any Grade 3–4 reactions. Use topical antibiotics/steroids for Grade 1 and oral antibiotics for ≥Grade 2.
*   **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Dacomitinib can cause fetal harm based on its mechanism and animal data. Verify pregnancy status in females of reproductive potential before initiation.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequencies are derived from the ARCHER 1050 trial.

### Very Common (>10%)
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (87%), stomatitis (45%), nausea (19%), constipation (13%), mouth ulceration (12%).
*   **Dermatologic:** Rash (69%), paronychia (64%), dry skin (30%), alopecia (23%), pruritus (21%), palmar-plantar erythrodysesthesia (15%), dermatitis (11%).
*   **Metabolic:** Decreased appetite (31%), decreased weight (26%).
*   **Respiratory:** Cough (21%), nasal mucosal disorders (19%), dyspnea (13%), upper respiratory tract infection (12%).
*   **Ocular:** Conjunctivitis (19%).
*   **Musculoskeletal:** Pain in extremity (14%), musculoskeletal pain (12%).
*   **General:** Asthenia/fatigue (13%).
*   **Psychiatric:** Insomnia (11%).

### Common (1%–10%)
*   Fatigue (9%), vomiting (9%), dysgeusia (7%), skin fissures (9%), hypertrichosis (1.3%), exfoliative skin reactions (3.5%), keratitis (1.8%), dehydration (1.3%).

### Serious Adverse Effects
*   Serious reactions occurred in 27% of patients. The most frequent serious effects were diarrhea (2.2%) and ILD (1.3%).
*   Fatalities occurred due to diarrhea (0.3%) and ILD (0.3%) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs):** Concomitant rabeprazole decreased dacomitinib $C_{max}$ by 51% and $AUC_{0-96h}$ by 39%. Avoid PPI use.
*   **H2-Receptor Antagonists:** Likely to decrease dacomitinib concentration; follow spacing guidelines (see Section 3).
*   **CYP2D6 Substrates:** Dacomitinib increases the concentration of CYP2D6 substrates (e.g., 9.7-fold increase in dextromethorphan $C_{max}$). Avoid use with substrates where minimal concentration increases may lead to serious toxicity.
*   **Transporters:** Dacomitinib is a substrate for P-gp and BCRP. It inhibits P-gp, BCRP, and OCT1 in vitro [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** No human data available. Animal studies show increased post-implantation loss and reduced fetal weight at clinically relevant exposures.
    *   **Contraception:** Females of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for at least 17 days after the final dose [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Presence in human milk is unknown. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in infants, patients are advised not to breastfeed during treatment and for 17 days following the last dose.
*   **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats showed reversible epithelial atrophy in the cervix/vagina and decreased secretion in the prostate [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** In clinical studies, 40% of patients were ≥65 years. Older patients experienced a higher incidence of Grade 3–4 adverse reactions (67% vs 56%) and more frequent treatment discontinuation (24% vs 10%) compared to younger patients.
*   **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild (CLcr 60–89 mL/min) or moderate (CLcr 30–59 mL/min) impairment. Not studied in severe renal impairment (CLcr <30 mL/min).
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is recommended for patients with mild, moderate, or severe (Child-Pugh A, B, or C) hepatic impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Dacomitinib is an irreversible inhibitor of the kinase activity of the human EGFR family (EGFR/HER1, HER2, and HER4) and specific activating mutations (exon 19 deletion or exon 21 L858R).
*   **Pharmacodynamics:** No large effect on the QTc interval (>20 ms) was observed at recommended doses. Higher exposure correlates with increased probability of Grade ≥3 diarrhea and dermatologic toxicities.
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is 80%. Median $T_{max}$ is 6 hours. High-fat meals do not clinically affect PK.
    *   **Distribution:** Volume of distribution ($V_{ss}$) is 1889 L. Plasma protein binding is 98%.
    *   **Metabolism:** Hepatic metabolism is the primary clearance route. Oxidative metabolism is primarily via CYP2D6, with minor contribution from CYP3A4. The major metabolite is O-desmethyl dacomitinib (active).
    *   **Elimination:** Mean plasma half-life is 70 hours. 79% of a dose is recovered in feces and 3% in urine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ARCHER 1050:** A randomized, multicenter, open-label trial comparing dacomitinib (45 mg/day) to gefitinib (250 mg/day) in 452 patients with EGFR-mutated metastatic NSCLC.
    *   **Outcome:** Dacomitinib demonstrated a statistically significant improvement in Progression-Free Survival (PFS) per independent review: 14.7 months (95% CI: 11.1, 16.6) vs 9.2 months (95% CI: 9.1, 11.0) for gefitinib (HR 0.59; p<0.0001).
    *   **Response:** Overall Response Rate (ORR) was 75% for dacomitinib vs 72% for gefitinib. Duration of response was longer with dacomitinib (14.8 months vs 8.3 months).

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions are permitted between 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
*   **Handling:** Dispense in tight (USP), child-resistant containers.
