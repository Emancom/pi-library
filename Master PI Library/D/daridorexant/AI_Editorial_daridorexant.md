# Editorial Product Information: Daridorexant

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: This editorial is based on the Product Information (PI) provided by the United States (FDA).
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a single clinical reference.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Daridorexant (present as daridorexant hydrochloride).
- **Chemical Class:** Dual orexin receptor antagonist (DORA).
- **Brand Name(s):** **QUVIVIQ** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
  - Film-coated tablets: 25 mg (light purple, arc-triangle shaped) and 50 mg (light orange, arc-triangle shaped) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Idorsia Pharmaceuticals Ltd [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Insomnia:** Indicated for the treatment of adult patients with insomnia characterised by difficulties with sleep onset and/or sleep maintenance [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The recommended dose is 25 mg to 50 mg once per night [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** Take orally within 30 minutes of going to bed, ensuring at least 7 hours remain before the planned time of awakening [US].
- **Food Effect:** While it can be taken with or without food, the time to sleep onset may be delayed if taken with or shortly after a high-fat, high-calorie meal [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources (implied once-nightly use only before sleep).
- **Dose Limitations:** 
  - **Moderate Hepatic Impairment:** Maximum dose 25 mg once per night [US].
  - **Moderate CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Recommended dose 25 mg once per night [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Narcolepsy:** Contraindicated in all patients with narcolepsy [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Contraindicated in patients with a known hypersensitivity to daridorexant or any of the product excipients [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **CNS-Depressant Effects:** Can impair daytime wakefulness and motor coordination even when used as prescribed. The risk of next-day impairment, including impaired driving, increases if taken with less than a full night of sleep or at doses higher than recommended. Use with extreme caution in patients performing activities requiring complete mental alertness [US].
- **Worsening of Depression/Suicidal Ideation:** Like other hypnotics, daridorexant may worsen depression or suicidal thinking. Monitor patients with psychiatric disorders closely [US].
- **Complex Sleep Behaviors:** Events such as sleepwalking, sleep-driving, and engaging in other activities while not fully awake have been reported. **QUVIVIQ** must be discontinued immediately if these occur [US].
- **Sleep Paralysis and Hallucinations:** May cause sleep paralysis (inability to move/speak for several minutes during transitions), hypnagogic/hypnopompic hallucinations, and symptoms similar to mild cataplexy [US].
- **Compromised Respiratory Function:** Effect on respiratory function should be considered in patients with compromised ventilation (e.g., OSA or COPD) [US].
- **Evaluation of Co-morbidities:** If insomnia persists after 7 to 10 days of treatment, the patient should be re-evaluated for primary psychiatric or medical illnesses [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects observed in clinical trials (Study 1) with a frequency ≥2% and higher than placebo include:

| Adverse Reaction | 25 mg (N=310) | 50 mg (N=308) | Placebo (N=309) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Headache** | 6% | 7% | 5% |
| **Somnolence or Fatigue** | 6% | 5% | 4% |
| **Dizziness** | 2% | 3% | 2% |
| **Nausea** | 0% | 3% | 2% |

- **Uncommon (<2% but >placebo):** Sleep paralysis (0.3%–0.5%) and hypnagogic/hypnopompic hallucinations (0.6% at 25 mg) [US].
- **Post-marketing Experience:** Nightmares, abnormal dreams, and hypersensitivity reactions (including angioedema, rash, and urticaria) have been reported [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
  - **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors (e.g., itraconazole):** Avoid concomitant use; increases daridorexant exposure by >400% [US].
  - **Moderate CYP3A4 Inhibitors (e.g., diltiazem):** Maximize dose at 25 mg [US].
  - **Strong or Moderate CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., rifampin, efavirenz):** Avoid concomitant use; significantly reduces efficacy [US].
  - **CYP3A4 Substrates (e.g., midazolam):** Use caution with narrow therapeutic index substrates as daridorexant may increase their exposure [US].
  - **P-gp Substrates (e.g., digoxin):** Use caution with narrow therapeutic index substrates [US].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
  - **Alcohol & CNS Depressants:** Additive psychomotor impairment and CNS depression. Avoid alcohol and use caution with other sedating medications [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **US Category:** No lettered category provided. Data in humans are insufficient to evaluate drug-associated risks. Animal studies showed no malformations at 8–10 times the Maximum Recommended Human Dose (MRHD) [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Daridorexant is present in human milk at low levels (relative infant dose <1%). Monitor breastfed infants for excessive sedation [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal data showed increased pre-implantation loss in female rats at high doses (9 times MRHD), but no effects on male fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established in patients under 18 years of age [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment is required. Somnolence and fatigue may increase with age, and the risk of falls is higher in this population [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required for patients with any degree of renal impairment (not on dialysis) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
  - **Mild:** No adjustment [US].
  - **Moderate (Child-Pugh 7–9):** Max 25 mg once per night [US].
  - **Severe (Child-Pugh ≥10):** Not recommended [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Antagonises OX1R and OX2R receptors, blocking the binding of wake-promoting neuropeptides (Orexin A and B), thereby suppressing the wake drive [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ is 1–2 hours. Absolute bioavailability is 62% [US].
  - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is 31 L; 99.7% protein bound [US].
  - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP3A4 (89%) [US].
  - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 8 hours. Excretion is 57% via feces and 28% via urine [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** Two 3-month multicenter, randomised, double-blind trials (Study 1 and 2) in 1,854 patients.
- **Outcomes:** Doses of 25 mg and 50 mg showed statistically significant improvements compared to placebo in Latency to Persistent Sleep (LPS), Wake After Sleep Onset (WASO), and patient-reported Total Sleep Time (sTST) at Month 1 and Month 3 [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F and 86°F) [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Not specified in available sources.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as a Schedule IV (CIV) controlled substance under the Controlled Substances Act [US].
- **Prescription Status:** Rx-only [US].
