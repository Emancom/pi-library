# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection, synthesised for healthcare professionals. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the United States (FDA) source documentation was provided for analysis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP); Chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Severe Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing intervals should be every 3 to 4 hours (q3h or q4h) and must not be less frequent than every 6 hours (q6h) [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Monitoring and Instructions
- **Target Levels:** Plasma free carnitine concentrations should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Preparation:** Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration before use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate risks before re-administration [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In those with established epilepsy, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulations):** Safety of high-dose oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD on dialysis has not been established; chronic use may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].
- **Monitoring:** Periodic monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations is recommended [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **General/Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting are common. Gastritis and a distinct "fishy" body odor have also been reported [US].
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not specified; strictly "use only if clearly needed"]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers is safe. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after exogenous administration. Weigh the benefits to the mother against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no impairment of fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg). See Section 3 for specific instructions [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources regarding age-related dose adjustments.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosing is specifically defined for ESRD patients on hemodialysis. High-dose oral therapy is cautioned against due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into the mitochondria for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, it alleviates metabolic abnormalities caused by the accumulation of toxic acyl-CoA compounds [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (found in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (found in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), the distribution half-life is 0.585 hours and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x weekly), mean pre-dialysis carnitine levels rose from ~20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** A study (N≈30 per group) compared 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses. Trough concentrations after 24 weeks were 148, 240, and 456 µmol/L respectively. Note: Efficacy regarding clinical outcomes in ESRD remains to be determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Preservation:** Formulation contains no preservative; discard any unused portion of opened vials [US].
