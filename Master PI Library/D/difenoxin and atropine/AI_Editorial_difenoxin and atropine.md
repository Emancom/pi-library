# Editorial Product Information: Difenoxin and Atropine Sulfate

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of the clinical and regulatory profile of difenoxin and atropine sulfate, synthesized for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Difenoxin hydrochloride (an antidiarrheal chemically related to meperidine) and Atropine sulfate (an anticholinergic).
- **Brand Name:** Motofen [US].
- **ATC Code:** A07DA05.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** White, dye-free, five-sided, scored tablets containing 1 mg difenoxin (as 1.09 mg difenoxin hydrochloride) and 0.025 mg atropine sulfate [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Legacy Pharma USA Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Nonspecific Diarrhea:** Indicated as adjunctive therapy in the management of acute nonspecific diarrhea [US].
- **Chronic Functional Diarrhea:** Indicated for the management of acute exacerbations of chronic functional diarrhea [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Adult Dosing:** 
    - **Initial Dose:** 2 tablets (2 mg difenoxin / 0.05 mg atropine).
    - **Maintenance Dose:** 1 tablet (1 mg / 0.025 mg) after each loose stool or 1 tablet every 3 to 4 hours as needed.
- **Maximum Dose:** Total dosage must not exceed 8 tablets (8 mg difenoxin) in any 24-hour period [US].
- **Treatment Duration:** Treatment beyond 48 hours is usually not necessary for acute diarrhea. If clinical improvement is not observed within 48 hours, continued administration is not recommended [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets are scored and can be divided.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Infectious Diarrhea:** Diarrhea associated with organisms that penetrate the intestinal mucosa (e.g., toxigenic *E. coli*, *Salmonella* species, *Shigella*) [US].
- **Pseudomembranous Colitis:** Diarrhea associated with broad-spectrum antibiotic use [US].
- **Pediatric Use:** Children under 2 years of age (due to a narrow margin of safety and risk of respiratory depression) [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to difenoxin, atropine, or any inactive ingredients [US].
- **Jaundice:** Patients presenting with jaundice [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **General Warning:** This is not an innocuous drug; strict adherence to dosage recommendations is required. Overdosage may lead to severe respiratory depression, coma, permanent brain damage, or death [US].
- **Fluid and Electrolyte Balance:** Use of this agent does not preclude the need for appropriate fluid and electrolyte replacement. Dehydration may increase the variability of response and predispose to delayed intoxication [US].
- **Toxic Megacolon:** In patients with acute ulcerative colitis, agents that inhibit intestinal motility can induce toxic megacolon. Discontinue therapy immediately if abdominal distention occurs [US].
- **Hepatorenal Disease:** Use with extreme caution in patients with advanced hepatorenal disease or abnormal liver function tests, as hepatic coma may be precipitated [US].
- **Atropinism:** While the dose of atropine is subtherapeutic, signs of atropinism (dryness of skin/mucosa, tachycardia, hyperthermia) may occur, particularly in children or patients with Down's Syndrome [US].
- **Mental Alertness:** May cause drowsiness or dizziness; patients should be cautioned against operating heavy machinery or driving [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects may be difficult to distinguish from symptoms of the diarrheal syndrome.

| System Organ Class | Adverse Effect | Frequency [US] |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea | 6.7% (1 in 15) |
| | Vomiting | 3.3% (1 in 30) |
| | Dry mouth | 3.3% (1 in 30) |
| | Epigastric distress | 1% (1 in 100) |
| | Constipation | 0.3% (1 in 300) |
| **Central Nervous System** | Dizziness/Lightheadedness | 5% (1 in 20) |
| | Drowsiness | 4% (1 in 25) |
| | Headache | 2.5% (1 in 40) |
| | Tiredness, Nervousness, Insomnia, Confusion | 0.16% – 0.5% |
| **Special Senses** | Burning eyes, Blurred vision | Reported (Rare) |

**Other Serious/Class Effects:** Reported with chemically related drugs: numbness of extremities, euphoria, depression, sedation, anaphylaxis, angioneurotic edema, urticaria, pruritus, toxic megacolon, paralytic ileus, pancreatitis, and anorexia [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **MAO Inhibitors:** Concurrent use may theoretically precipitate a hypertensive crisis due to the structural similarity of difenoxin to meperidine [US].
- **CNS Depressants:** May potentiate the action of alcohol, barbiturates, tranquilizers, and narcotics [US].
- **Microsomal Enzyme Inhibitors:** Difenoxin (derived from diphenoxylate) may inhibit the hepatic microsomal enzyme system, potentially prolonging the half-life of drugs dependent on this system for elimination [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category C:** No well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Animal studies showed increased stillbirths and reduced neonatal survival at 20 times the human dose. Use only if the potential benefit justifies the risk to the fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants. A decision should be made to discontinue nursing or discontinue the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No significant impairment of fertility was found in rat studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - **Contraindicated:** Children under 2 years of age [US].
    - **Safety/Efficacy:** Not established in children under 12 years of age [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed; however, general caution regarding fluid/electrolyte balance and CNS effects applies.
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with extreme caution in advanced renal disease [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with extreme caution; may precipitate hepatic coma [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Difenoxin manifests its antidiarrheal effect by slowing intestinal motility via a local effect on the gastrointestinal wall [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly and extensively absorbed.
    - **Distribution:** Peak plasma levels (~160 ng/mL) occur within 40 to 60 minutes after a 2 mg dose [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolized to an inactive hydroxylated metabolite [US].
    - **Elimination:** Plasma levels decline to <10% of peak within 24 hours. Excreted mainly as conjugates in urine and feces [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Difenoxin is the principal active metabolite of diphenoxylate [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Data:** Specific named clinical trials are not listed in the available source. Clinical investigations established the frequency of adverse effects and confirmed efficacy as an adjunctive antidiarrheal therapy [US].
- **Animal Studies:** 104-week carcinogenicity studies in rats showed no evidence of tumors [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F) [US].
- **Container:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant, child-resistant container [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** 
    - **United States:** Rx-only; Schedule IV (CIV) Controlled Substance [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified for other jurisdictions.
