# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of the official Product Information (PI) for **levocarnitine** injection. It is intended for use by healthcare professionals as a concise, clinically accurate reference.
- **Note:** This editorial synthesis does not replace the official PI approved in a specific jurisdiction. Prescribers should consult the current local regulatory documentation when making clinical decisions. *Note: The user prompt mentioned "dienestrol," but the provided source document and data pertain exclusively to **levocarnitine**; this editorial reflects the provided clinical data.*

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine [(R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt].
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; fatty acid carrier molecule.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (not less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - Daily maintenance doses should be in the range of 50 mg/kg, though higher doses (up to 300 mg/kg) have been utilised [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].

### Monitoring and Titration
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- **ESRD:** Initiation of therapy may be prompted by pre-dialysis (trough) carnitine levels below 40–50 µmol/L. Downward dose adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, therapy must be discontinued immediately [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with reports of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a known seizure disorder, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Metabolite Accumulation:** While this PI focuses on the injection, clinicians should note that chronic high-dose oral administration in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Monitoring:** Periodic monitoring of plasma carnitine levels is essential to guide therapy and prevent excessive accumulation [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disorders.

### Common Adverse Effects (Frequency ≥5% in ESRD Trials)
| Body System | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%), Flu syndrome (25%), Headache (22%), Abdominal pain (21%), Fever (12%), Chest pain (12%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (9%) |

### Post-Marketing Experience
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea, vomiting, gastritis, and body odor [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalised Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned under current labeling, but reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm]. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations of levocarnitine following exogenous administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake to the infant should be weighed against the benefits of maternal therapy [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is applicable to paediatric patients. Safety and efficacy in ESRD paediatric patients have not been specifically detailed outside general clinical use [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is specifically indicated for ESRD on dialysis. However, the safety of **oral** levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency due to metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US]. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, it alleviates metabolic abnormalities resulting from carnitine loss during dialysis and reduced dietary intake.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Elimination:** Mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours [US].
    - **Bioavailability:** Absolute bioavailability of oral formulations is approximately 15–16% [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, IV levocarnitine (20 mg/kg after dialysis) increased mean pre-dialysis plasma concentrations from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. Trough concentrations remained stable or increased over 12 to 24 weeks in dose-ranging studies (10, 20, and 40 mg/kg groups) [US].
- **Clinical Outcome Note:** While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations is proven, the effects on specific clinical outcomes in the ESRD population have not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservative [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only. NDC 36000-368-10 (10 x 5 mL vials) [US].
