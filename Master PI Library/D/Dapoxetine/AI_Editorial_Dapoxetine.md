# Editorial Product Information: Dapoxetine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of dapoxetine for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data into a concise reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information mandated for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory guidelines and the specific approved PI for their region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Dapoxetine (as hydrochloride).
- **Chemical Class:** Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI).
- **Brand Name(s):** **PRILIGY** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** G04BE09.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 30 mg film-coated tablets (light grey, debossed with "30" inside a triangle) [Australian]. 
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** A. Menarini Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Dapoxetine** is indicated for the treatment of premature ejaculation (PE) in adult men aged 18 to 64 years. Usage is restricted to patients meeting all of the following diagnostic criteria [Australian]:
- An intravaginal ejaculatory latency time (IELT) of less than two minutes.
- Persistent or recurrent ejaculation with minimal sexual stimulation before, on, or shortly after penetration and before the patient wishes.
- Marked personal distress or interpersonal difficulty as a consequence of PE.
- Poor control over ejaculation.

**Note:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in men without a diagnosis of PE [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult Men 18–64 years)
- **Recommended Starting Dose:** 30 mg, taken as needed approximately 1 to 3 hours prior to sexual activity [Australian].
- **Maximum Frequency:** Once every 24 hours. **Dapoxetine** is not intended for continuous daily use; it should only be taken when sexual activity is anticipated [Australian].
- **Clinical Review:** A physician should perform a formal benefit-risk appraisal after the first four weeks of treatment (or at least 6 doses) to determine the appropriateness of continued therapy [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets must be swallowed whole to avoid a bitter taste.
- It is recommended to take the tablet with at least one full glass of water.
- May be taken with or without food [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
**Dapoxetine** is contraindicated in the following scenarios [Australian]:
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to dapoxetine hydrochloride or any tablet excipients (including lactose).
- **Cardiac Conditions:** Significant pathological heart disease (NYHA Class II-IV heart failure), conduction abnormalities (AV block or sick sinus syndrome), significant ischemic heart disease, or significant valvular disease.
- **History of Syncope.**
- **Psychiatric History:** History of mania, hypomania, or severe depression.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Moderate and severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class B and C).
- **Concomitant Pharmacotherapy:**
    - **MAOIs:** Concomitant use with Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs), or within 14 days of discontinuing an MAOI. MAOIs should not be started until 7 days after stopping dapoxetine.
    - **Thioridazine:** Concomitant use or within 14 days of discontinuation. Thioridazine should not be started until 7 days after stopping dapoxetine.
    - **Serotonergic Agents:** SSRIs, SNRIs, TCAs, or other products with serotonergic effects (e.g., L-tryptophan, triptans, tramadol, linezolid, lithium, St. John's Wort). Use within 14 days of discontinuation of these products is prohibited; wait 7 days after stopping dapoxetine before starting these agents.
    - **Potent CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Concomitant use with agents such as ketoconazole, itraconazole, ritonavir, saquinavir, telithromycin, nefazodone, nelfinavir, and atazanavir.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning: Syncope [Australian]
Patients may experience syncope (loss of consciousness) at any time, with or without prodromal symptoms (e.g., nausea, dizziness, light-headedness). This effect is dose-dependent. Patients must maintain adequate hydration and be educated to recognize prodromal signs. If symptoms occur, the patient should immediately lie down with their head lower than the body or sit with their head between the knees.

### General Warnings
- **Orthostatic Hypotension:** A careful medical examination, including an orthostatic test (blood pressure/pulse in supine and standing positions), is required before initiation. Treatment should be avoided in patients with a history of documented or suspected orthostatic reactions [Australian].
- **Ethanol and Recreational Drugs:** Patients should be advised to avoid alcohol, as it may increase the risk of syncope and neurocognitive adverse effects. Concomitant use with recreational drugs (serotonergic or sedative) is strictly cautioned due to the risk of serotonin syndrome or profound somnolence [Australian].
- **Seizure Disorders:** Discontinue in patients who develop seizures; avoid in those with unstable epilepsy [Australian].
- **Psychiatric Disorders:** Evaluate for depression prior to treatment. Dapoxetine is not indicated for psychiatric disorders. Patients should report any distressing thoughts or signs of depression; if these develop, treatment should be stopped [Australian].
- **Ocular Effects:** Use with caution in patients with raised intraocular pressure or at risk of angle-closure glaucoma (due to potential mydriasis) [Australian].
- **Hemorrhage:** Caution is advised in patients taking anticoagulants or anti-platelet agents (e.g., NSAIDs, warfarin) or those with known bleeding disorders [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are dose-dependent. The following data represents the 30 mg as-needed dose [Australian]:

| Frequency | System Organ Class / Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Nausea (11.0%), Headache (5.6%), Dizziness (5.8%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Diarrhea, Insomnia, Fatigue, Anxiety, Irritability, Erectile Dysfunction (2.3%), Somnolence, Tremor, Tinnitus, Hyperhidrosis |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Syncope, Orthostatic hypotension, Tachycardia, Depressed mood, Mydriasis, Vision blurred, Flatulence |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Sudden onset of sleep, Defaecation urgency |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Syncope and bradycardia/sinus arrest have been reported, typically within the first 3 hours post-dose or associated with clinical procedures (e.g., blood draws) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Potent inhibitors are contraindicated. Moderate inhibitors (e.g., erythromycin, diltiazem, fluconazole) increase dapoxetine exposure; the maximum dose should be limited to 30 mg [Australian].
- **CYP2D6 Inhibitors:** Potent inhibitors (e.g., fluoxetine) increase the active fraction of dapoxetine. Caution is advised during titration [Australian].
- **Effect of Dapoxetine on other drugs:** Dapoxetine may modestly increase concentrations of drugs metabolised by CYP2D6 (e.g., desipramine) [Australian].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **PDE5 Inhibitors:** Concomitant use with sildenafil or tadalafil may increase the risk of orthostatic hypotension; use with caution [Australian].
- **Warfarin:** Caution is advised with chronic use of warfarin due to increased bleeding risks associated with SSRIs [Australian].
- **CNS Active Medicines:** Systematic evaluation with antiepileptics, antidepressants, or antipsychotics is lacking; caution is required [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Dapoxetine is not indicated for use by women. Rat and rabbit studies showed no teratogenicity, but high doses were associated with maternotoxicity.
- **Breastfeeding:** Not indicated for use by women. It is unknown if dapoxetine or its metabolites are excreted in human milk [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no effects on fertility. The impact on human fertility is unknown [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not for use in individuals under 18 years of age [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients 65 years and over [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild-to-moderate impairment. Not recommended for patients with severe renal impairment [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for mild impairment. Contraindicated in moderate-to-severe (Child-Pugh B/C) impairment [Australian].
- **CYP2D6 Poor Metabolisers:** Caution is advised as exposure levels (AUC and Cmax) are significantly higher in this population [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Dapoxetine is a potent SSRI. Its efficacy in PE is attributed to the inhibition of neuronal reuptake of serotonin and the subsequent potentiation of neurotransmitter action at pre- and post-synaptic receptors. This modulates the ejaculatory expulsion reflex at a supraspinal level [Australian].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; Cmax reached in 1–2 hours. Bioavailability is ~40%.
- **Distribution:** >99% protein bound. Mean steady-state volume of distribution is 162 L.
- **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised in the liver and kidney via CYP2D6, CYP3A4, and FMO1. Major circulating species are dapoxetine and dapoxetine-N-oxide.
- **Elimination:** Metabolites are primarily excreted in the urine. Terminal half-life is approximately 19 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
Efficacy was established in four double-blind, placebo-controlled trials (n=1616 on dapoxetine 30 mg). The mean average IELT at week 12 was significantly higher for the 30 mg group (3.1 minutes) compared to placebo (1.95 minutes). Significant improvements were also noted in patient-reported outcomes, including "control over ejaculation" and "satisfaction with sexual intercourse" [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Disposal:** Unused medicine should be disposed of via local pharmacy programs [Australian].
