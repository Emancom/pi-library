# Editorial Product Information: Dexmedetomidine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of dexmedetomidine clinical data for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises information from the provided regulatory source to ensure clinical accuracy and practical utility.
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local guidelines and the specific approved PI for their region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Dexmedetomidine hydrochloride (a relatively selective alpha2-adrenoreceptor agonist).
- **Brand Names:** Dexmedetomidine-AFT [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available source.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Concentrated injection: 200 microgram/2 mL (as hydrochloride) [Australian]. 
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only, following dilution.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AFT Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Intensive Care Unit (ICU) Sedation:** For the sedation of patients who are initially intubated and undergoing treatment in an intensive care setting. Continuous infusion in this setting should not exceed 24 hours [Australian].
- **Procedural Sedation:** For the sedation of non-intubated patients prior to and/or during surgical or other diagnostic/therapeutic procedures [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Dexmedetomidine must be administered only by healthcare professionals skilled in anaesthesia or intensive care management.
- Continuous monitoring of ECG, blood pressure, and oxygen saturation is mandatory.
- A controlled infusion device must be used.

### Preparation
- **Dilution:** Withdraw 2 mL of the concentrate and add to 48 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride to reach a total volume of 50 mL [Australian].
- **Compatibility:** Compatible with Lactated Ringers, 5% Glucose in Water, 0.9% Sodium Chloride, and 20% Mannitol. Y-site compatibility includes thiopental sodium, vecuronium bromide, pancuronium bromide, glycopyrrolate, and phenylephrine [Australian].

### Adult Dosing (ICU Sedation)
- **Initiation:** Optional loading infusion of 1 mcg/kg over 10 to 20 minutes. Loading doses may not be required for patients converting from other sedatives [Australian].
- **Maintenance:** Generally 0.2 to 1 mcg/kg/hr. The recommended initial maintenance rate is 0.4 mcg/kg/hr, titrated in increments of 0.1 mcg/kg/hr to the desired clinical effect [Australian].

### Adult Dosing (Procedural Sedation)
- **Initiation:** Generally 1 mcg/kg over 10 to 20 minutes. For less invasive procedures (e.g., ophthalmic surgery), 0.5 mcg/kg over 10 minutes may be appropriate [Australian].
- **Maintenance:** Generally initiated at 0.6 mcg/kg/hr and titrated (range 0.2 to 1 mcg/kg/hr).
- **Awake Fibreoptic Intubation:** A fixed maintenance dose of 0.7 mcg/kg/hr is recommended following the loading dose [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to dexmedetomidine or any of the product excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypotension, Bradycardia, and Sinus Arrest:** Significant decreases in blood pressure and heart rate may occur. These effects are more pronounced in patients with high vagal tone or diminished autonomic control (e.g., elderly, diabetic, or chronic hypertensive patients) [Australian].
- **Transient Hypertension:** Observed primarily during loading infusions due to peripheral vasoconstrictive effects. Reduction of infusion rate should be considered if intervention is required [Australian].
- **Arousability:** Patients typically remain rousable and alert when stimulated; this should not be misinterpreted as a lack of efficacy [Australian].
- **Withdrawal:** If administered chronically (beyond 24 hours) and stopped abruptly, symptoms similar to clonidine withdrawal (agitation, headache, rapid blood pressure rise) may occur [Australian].
- **Hyperthermia:** Dexmedetomidine may induce drug-related hyperthermia that may be resistant to conventional cooling. Discontinuation is required if suspected [Australian].
- **Corneal Protection:** Reduced lacrimation may occur; eye lubrication should be considered to prevent corneal dryness [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are primarily related to the pharmacological profile (alpha2-agonist activity).

| Adverse Event | ICU Sedation Frequency [Australian] | Procedural Sedation Frequency [Australian] |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hypotension** | Very Common (28–56%) | Very Common (54%) |
| **Hypertension** | Very Common (11–28%) | Common (13%) |
| **Bradycardia** | Common to Very Common (7–42%) | Common (14%) |
| **Nausea** | Very Common (11%) | Common (3%) |
| **Dry Mouth** | Common (3%) | Common (3%) |
| **Respiratory Depression** | Not specified | Very Common (37%) |
| **Hypoxia** | Common (4%) | Common (2%) |

**Serious Adverse Effects:**
- Sinus arrest, AV block (1st or 2nd degree), myocardial infarction, and atrial fibrillation [Australian].
- Respiratory: Apnoea, bronchospasm, and pulmonary congestion (post-marketing) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Anaesthetics/Sedatives/Opioids:** Co-administration leads to significant enhancement of effects. Dose reductions for concurrent agents (e.g., sevoflurane, isoflurane, propofol, alfentanil, midazolam) are likely required [Australian].
- **Neuromuscular Blockers:** No clinically meaningful interaction observed with rocuronium [Australian].
- **Cytochrome P450:** In vitro studies suggest inhibition of CYP2D6, CYP3A4, and CYP2B6. Caution is advised when co-administering drugs metabolised by these enzymes [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category B1 [Australian].** (Drugs which have been taken by only a limited number of pregnant women and women of childbearing age without an increase in the frequency of malformation or other direct/indirect harmful effects having been observed). 
    - Dexmedetomidine crosses the placenta and is not recommended for use during labour or delivery, including caesarean sections [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Dexmedetomidine is excreted in human milk. A 24-hour "pump and discard" period after dosing may be considered to minimise neonatal exposure [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility observed in animal studies (rats) at doses below the maximum recommended human dose [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients under 18 years of age. Its use is not recommended in this population [Australian].
- **Elderly Use (>65 years):** Increased incidence of bradycardia and hypotension. Dose reductions for loading and maintenance are recommended [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Clearance decreases with the severity of impairment (Child-Pugh A, B, and C). Dose reductions are required [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Pharmacokinetics are not significantly altered by severe renal impairment (CrCl <30 mL/min), but caution is advised with prolonged dosing due to metabolite accumulation [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A relatively selective alpha2-adrenoreceptor agonist. Sedative properties are mediated via the locus coeruleus; analgesic effects are mediated at the spinal cord and brain levels [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Rapid phase (t½ ~6 min); steady-state volume of distribution (Vss) ~118 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Almost complete biotransformation via direct N-glucuronidation and CYP450-mediated metabolism (hydroxylation/methylation).
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life (t½) ~2 hours. Excreted primarily in urine (95%) and faeces (4%) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ICU Sedation:** Two trials (N=754) demonstrated that dexmedetomidine-treated patients required significantly less rescue medication (midazolam or propofol) to maintain target sedation (Ramsay Sedation Scale ≥ 3) compared to placebo [Australian].
- **Procedural Sedation:** Two trials (N=318) showed dexmedetomidine was significantly more effective than placebo in achieving desired sedation levels without the need for rescue midazolam [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Concentrate:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Diluted Solution:** To reduce microbiological hazard, use immediately. If necessary, storage at 2°C–8°C for no more than 24 hours is permitted [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
