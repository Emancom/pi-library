# Editorial Product Information: Diltiazem

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: 
    - **Australian (TGA)**: Cardizem (diltiazem hydrochloride) 60 mg film-coated tablets.
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a structured format for quick reference.
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal and prescribing compliance. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Diltiazem hydrochloride.
- **Chemical Class**: Calcium ion influx inhibitor (benzothiazepine derivative).
- **Brand Names**: Cardizem [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: C08DB01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**: 
    - 60 mg film-coated tablets: Light yellow, round, convex tablets; one side engraved with "Marion," the other scored and engraved with "1772" [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: sanofi-aventis australia pty ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Angina Pectoris**: Treatment of patients with moderate to severe angina due to atherosclerotic coronary artery disease or coronary artery spasm (vasospastic/Prinzmetal's angina) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing**: Dosage must be individualised.
- **Initial Therapy**: Therapy typically commences at 30 mg four times daily, administered before meals and at bedtime.
- **Titration**: Increase dosage gradually in divided doses (three to four times daily) at one- to two-day intervals until the desired clinical response is achieved.
- **Optimum Dosage Range**: Usually 180 mg to 240 mg per day.
- **Maximum Dose**: 360 mg daily [Australian].
- **Administration**: Tablets are for oral use.
- **Missed Dose**: Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Cardiac Conduction Disorders**: Sick sinus syndrome or second-/third-degree AV block, except in patients with a functional ventricular pacemaker.
- **Cardiac Function**: Severe congestive heart failure; left ventricular failure accompanied by pulmonary congestion; acute myocardial infarction with X-ray-documented pulmonary congestion.
- **Haemodynamics**: Severe bradycardia (heart rate below 40 bpm); hypotension (systolic blood pressure <90 mmHg).
- **Concomitant Medications**: Co-administration with dantrolene infusion or ivabradine.
- **Hypersensitivity**: Known idiosyncrasy or hypersensitivity to diltiazem or any tablet excipients.
- **Lactation**: Breastfeeding is contraindicated [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings**: None listed in available sources.
- **Cardiac Conduction**: Diltiazem prolongs AV node refractory periods. Close monitoring is required for patients with first-degree AV block (risk of progression to complete block) or bradycardia. Rarely, patients with Prinzmetal's angina may experience asystole.
- **Congestive Heart Failure**: Use with caution in patients with impaired ventricular function due to potential negative inotropic effects.
- **Acute Renal/Hepatic Failure**: Cases of acute renal failure have occurred, particularly in those with reduced LV function or severe bradycardia. Significant hepatic enzyme elevations (AST, ALT, LDH) may indicate acute hepatic injury; discontinuation is usually required.
- **Dermatological Reactions**: Skin eruptions may be transient, but rare cases of Erythema Multiforme, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN), and exfoliative dermatitis have been reported. Discontinue if reactions persist or progress.
- **Gastrointestinal**: May inhibit intestinal motility; use with caution in patients at risk of obstruction.
- **Diabetes**: Potential for increased blood glucose; monitor patients with latent or manifest diabetes.
- **Respiratory**: May induce or aggravate bronchospasm.
- **Psychiatric**: May be associated with mood changes, including depression.
- **Abrupt Withdrawal**: Sudden cessation has been linked to severe angina.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Distribution (Clinical Trials)
| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Lower limb oedema |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Oedema (2.4%), headache (2.1%), nausea (1.9%), AV block (1.6%), dizziness (1.5%), rash (1.3%), asthenia (1.2%), palpitations, constipation, dyspepsia, flushing, malaise. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Bradycardia, arrhythmia, syncope, tachycardia, hypotension, abnormal dreams, amnesia, depression, hallucinations, insomnia, paraesthesia, tinnitus, tremor, diarrhoea, dry mouth, weight increase, pruritus, photosensitivity, dyspnoea, epistaxis, polyuria, sexual dysfunction. |

### Post-Marketing and Serious Effects
- **Cardiovascular**: Cardiac arrest (asystole), sino-atrial block, congestive heart failure.
- **Dermatological**: SJS, TEN, acute generalised exanthematous pustular dermatitis, leukocytoclastic vasculitis.
- **Other**: Extrapyramidal syndrome, hepatitis, gynaecomastia, haemolytic anaemia, and lupus-like syndrome.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions (CYP3A4 and P-gp)
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors**: May increase diltiazem plasma concentrations.
- **CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., Rifampicin)**: May decrease diltiazem levels.
- **Diltiazem as Inhibitor**: Increases levels of CYP3A4 substrates, including:
    - **Statins**: Significant increases in AUC for simvastatin (nearly 5-fold) and lovastatin; increased risk of rhabdomyolysis.
    - **Cyclosporin**: Increased concentrations and potential nephrotoxicity; dose reduction of cyclosporin is recommended.
    - **Benzodiazepines**: Increased levels of midazolam and triazolam.
    - **Other**: Increased levels of carbamazepine, phenytoin, theophylline, and cilostazol.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Beta-blockers/Amiodarone/Digoxin**: Additive effects on cardiac conduction and contractility. Increased risk of bradycardia, heart block, and heart failure. Digoxin levels may also be increased by diltiazem.
- **Antihypertensives (Alpha-blockers, Nitrates)**: Additive hypotensive effects.
- **Anaesthetic Agents**: Potentiated depression of cardiac contractility and conductivity.
- **Antiplatelets (Aspirin, Ticagrelor)**: Potential additive risk of bleeding.
- **Colchicine**: Increased exposure; combined use is not recommended.
- **Grapefruit Juice**: May increase diltiazem exposure; avoid if interaction is suspected.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: **Category C [Australian]**. Animal studies show embryo/foetal lethality and skeletal abnormalities at high doses. Potential for foetal hypoxia due to maternal hypotension. Not recommended for use during pregnancy or in women of childbearing potential not using effective contraception.
- **Breastfeeding**: Diltiazem is excreted in human milk at concentrations similar to maternal serum. **Contraindicated [Australian]**.
- **Fertility**: No human data available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and efficacy have not been established; use is not recommended.
- **Elderly Use**: Use with caution. Plasma concentrations may be higher and the incidence of adverse reactions is approximately 13% higher in patients ≥65 years. Particular care is required during titration.
- **Renal Impairment**: Use with caution and perform careful titration; diltiazem is excreted by the kidneys.
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Diltiazem is extensively metabolised by the liver. Use with caution; plasma levels may be increased.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Diltiazem inhibits the transmembrane influx of calcium ions during depolarisation in cardiac and vascular smooth muscle. It dilates coronary arteries (epicardial and subendocardial) and reduces myocardial oxygen demand by lowering heart rate and systemic blood pressure.
- **Pharmacodynamics**: It prolongs AV node refractory periods. It has a modest negative inotropic effect in vitro but generally does not reduce cardiac index in humans with normal ventricular function.
- **Pharmacokinetics**: 
    - **Absorption**: Approximately 80% absorbed; absolute bioavailability is ~40% due to significant first-pass metabolism.
    - **Distribution**: 70% to 80% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism**: Extensive hepatic metabolism; one metabolite (desacetyldiltiazem) is active (~25–50% potency of parent).
    - **Elimination**: Plasma half-life is approximately 3.5 hours. Only 2–4% excreted unchanged in urine.
