# Editorial Product Information: Docetaxel

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Docetaxel (as trihydrate).
- **Chemical Class:** Taxane (antineoplastic agent).
- **Brand Name(s):** DBL Docetaxel [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources (Note: L01CD02).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Concentrated injection: 20 mg/2 mL, 80 mg/8 mL, and 160 mg/16 mL (10 mg/mL) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Docetaxel is indicated for the treatment of various malignancies, often in specific combinations or after the failure of previous therapies [Australian]:

- **Breast Cancer:**
  - Metastatic: Monotherapy for locally advanced or metastatic disease after chemotherapy failure.
  - Combination with capecitabine: After failure of anthracycline-containing therapy.
  - Combination with trastuzumab: For HER2-overexpressing metastatic disease in treatment-naive patients.
  - Adjuvant: In combination with doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide (TAC) for node-positive disease.
  - Adjuvant (HER2+): In combination with carboplatin and trastuzumab (TCH) or following doxorubicin/cyclophosphamide with trastuzumab (AC-TH).
  - Adjuvant: In combination with cyclophosphamide for operable tumours (≥1 cm and <7 cm).
- **Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC):** Locally advanced or metastatic disease, including patients who have failed platinum-based chemotherapy.
- **Ovarian Cancer:** Metastatic carcinoma of the ovary after failure of first-line or subsequent therapy.
- **Prostate Cancer:** Metastatic castration-resistant (hormone refractory) prostate cancer, in combination with prednisone or prednisolone.
- **Head and Neck Cancer:** Induction treatment (combination with cisplatin and fluorouracil) for locally advanced squamous cell carcinoma of the head and neck (SCCHN).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Standard Regimen:** 75 to 100 mg/m² administered as a one-hour IV infusion every three weeks [Australian].
- **Premedication:** To reduce fluid retention and hypersensitivity, oral corticosteroids (e.g., dexamethasone 16 mg/day) are required for 3 days, starting 1 day prior to infusion [Australian]. For prostate cancer, dexamethasone 8 mg is given at 12, 3, and 1 hour before infusion.

### Indication-Specific Dosing (Examples) [Australian]
- **Breast Cancer (Adjuvant TAC):** 75 mg/m² 1 hour after doxorubicin 50 mg/m² and cyclophosphamide 500 mg/m² every 3 weeks for 6 cycles.
- **Prostate Cancer:** 75 mg/m² every 3 weeks with continuous oral prednisone/prednisolone (5 mg twice daily).
- **Head and Neck (Induction):** 75 mg/m² followed by cisplatin and fluorouracil; cycles repeated every 3 weeks.

### Administration Instructions
- **Dilution:** Must be diluted in 0.9% sodium chloride or 5% glucose to a final concentration not exceeding 0.74 mg/mL [Australian].
- **Equipment:** Use of non-PVC (polyolefin, polypropylene) bags and polyethylene-lined administration sets is recommended to minimize exposure to the plasticizer DEHP [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- History of severe hypersensitivity to docetaxel or polysorbate 80 [Australian].
- Baseline neutrophil count < 1.5 x 10⁹/L [Australian].
- Severe hepatic impairment [Australian].
- Pregnancy and breastfeeding [Australian].
- Contraindications for concomitant drugs also apply when used in combination [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hematology:** Neutropenia is the dose-limiting toxicity. Neutrophil nadirs typically occur at day 7. Frequent complete blood counts (CBC) are mandatory. Retreatment requires neutrophils ≥ 1.5 x 10⁹/L [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Typically occurs within the first few minutes of the first or second infusion. Severe reactions (hypotension, bronchospasm) require immediate discontinuation and permanent avoidance [Australian].
- **Fluid Retention:** Cumulative peripheral edema, pleural effusion, and ascites may occur. Premedication with corticosteroids reduces incidence/severity [Australian].
- **Cutaneous Reactions:** Localized eruptions on hands/feet and nail toxicity (hyperpigmentation/onycholysis) are common. Severe reactions like Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS) or Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN) are rare but possible [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Increased risk of toxic death, severe infection, and febrile neutropenia in patients with elevated LFTs (ALT/AST > 1.5x ULN and AP > 2.5x ULN). Use 75 mg/m² or avoid entirely if bilirubin is elevated [Australian].
- **Alcohol Content:** Contains ethanol, which may affect the central nervous system and should be considered in high-risk patients (alcoholism, epilepsy, liver disease) [Australian].
- **Cardiac Toxicity:** Heart failure risk increases when used with trastuzumab, especially following anthracyclines [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Summary [Australian]
- **Very Common (>10%):** Neutropenia (often Grade 4), anemia, alopecia, nausea, vomiting, stomatitis, diarrhea, asthenia, myalgia, fluid retention, and nail disorders.
- **Common (1–10%):** Febrile neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, hypersensitivity reactions, infusion site reactions, and non-septic infections.
- **Rare/Very Rare (<0.1%):** Ototoxicity, seizures, gastrointestinal perforation, ischemic colitis, hepatitis, and Stevens-Johnson Syndrome.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Febrile Neutropenia:** Significant risk; G-CSF prophylaxis is recommended for high-risk regimens (e.g., TAC, TPF) [Australian].
- **Enterocolitis:** Can be fatal; requires close monitoring in neutropenic patients.
- **Cystoid Macular Oedema (CMO):** Reported in some patients; requires ophthalmic evaluation if vision is impaired [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic:** Docetaxel is metabolized by **CYP3A4**. Concomitant use with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, ritonavir, clarithromycin) significantly decreases clearance and increases toxicity risk. Avoid these combinations; if unavoidable, close monitoring and dose adjustment are required [Australian].
- **In Vitro Data:** Binding to plasma proteins (>95%) is not affected by common drugs like dexamethasone or phenytoin [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Docetaxel may cause fetal harm. It is embryotoxic and fetotoxic in animal studies. Effective contraception is required during treatment and for at least 2 months (females) or 4 months (males) after the final dose [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. It is unknown if docetaxel is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding should be avoided during treatment and for 1 week after the final dose [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies show testicular atrophy and impaired fertility. Male patients should seek advice on sperm conservation prior to therapy [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific dose adjustments are required based on age alone, but caution is advised as elderly patients may experience a higher incidence of certain toxicities (e.g., anemia, infection, anorexia in prostate cancer trials) [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No specific data or dose adjustments provided in available sources [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Strictly monitor LFTs. Dose reduction to 75 mg/m² is recommended for mild-to-moderate impairment. Avoid use in severe impairment or when bilirubin is elevated [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Disrupts the microtubular network by promoting the assembly of tubulin into stable microtubules and inhibiting their disassembly. This leads to the arrest of vital mitotic and interphase cellular functions [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Distribution:** 95% protein bound; steady-state volume of distribution is 113 L.
  - **Metabolism:** Oxidative metabolism via CYP3A4.
  - **Elimination:** Three-compartment model; terminal half-life of 91–120 hours. Approximately 75% is excreted in feces and 6% in urine [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Breast Cancer:** Phase III trials showed docetaxel 100 mg/m² produced higher response rates than doxorubicin in alkylating agent failures (47.8% vs 33.3%) and was superior to mitomycin C/vinblastine in anthracycline failures [Australian].
- **Adjuvant Breast Cancer (TAC):** 55-month follow-up showed significantly longer disease-free survival and a 30% reduction in mortality risk compared to the FAC regimen [Australian].
- **Prostate Cancer (TAX 327):** Docetaxel every 3 weeks with prednisone significantly improved median survival (18.9 months) compared to mitoxantrone (16.5 months) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Concentrated Vial:** Store below 25°C. Protect from light [Australian].
- **Infusion Solution:** Stable for up to 4 hours at room temperature (25°C). For microbiological safety, use immediately after dilution. If storage is necessary, hold at 2-8°C for no more than 24 hours [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
