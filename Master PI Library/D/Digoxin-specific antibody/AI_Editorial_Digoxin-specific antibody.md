# Editorial Product Information: Digoxin-specific antibody fragment F(Ab) (Ovine)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single, cohesive reference.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Digoxin-specific antibody fragment F(Ab) (Ovine).
- **Brand Name:** **DIGIFAB** [Australian].
- **Chemical Class:** Purified, monovalent immunoglobulin fragments obtained from the serum of healthy sheep immunized with a digoxin derivative.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Lyophilised powder for injection; each vial contains 40 mg of digoxin-specific antibody fragment F(Ab), which binds approximately 0.5 mg of digoxin [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Phebra Pty Ltd (Sponsor); BTG International Inc. (Manufacturer) [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **DIGIFAB** is indicated for the treatment of known or strongly suspected life-threatening digoxin toxicity. 
- Specifically, it is used when toxicity is associated with:
    - Ventricular arrhythmias.
    - Progressive bradycardia.
    - Second or third-degree heart block unresponsive to atropine.
- Use is indicated where measures beyond digoxin withdrawal and electrolyte correction are deemed necessary [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Children >20 kg)
The dosage is determined by the total body load of digoxin to be neutralised. In acute poisoning, the serum concentration may not reflect total body load; in chronic poisoning, full neutralisation may not be necessary as patients require therapeutic digoxin levels.

**1. Based on known amount of digoxin ingested:**
- **Number of vials** = [Amount of digoxin ingested (mg) × bioavailability*] / 0.5 mg/vial.
- *Bioavailability: 0.7 for tablets, 0.8 for elixir.

**2. Based on known serum digoxin concentration:**
- **Number of vials** = [Serum digoxin concentration (ng/mL) × weight (kg)] / 100.

**3. If dose and concentration are unknown:**
- **Acute poisoning:** Administer 5 vials if hemodynamically stable; 10–20 vials if unstable.
- **Chronic poisoning:** Administer 2 vials initially; may repeat with 2 additional vials after 30 minutes if symptoms persist [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Reconstitution:** Reconstitute each vial with 4 mL of Sterile Water for Injection to achieve 10 mg/mL. Mix gently.
- **Infusion:** Further dilute with sterile saline and infuse intravenously over 30 minutes. 
- **Bolus:** May be given as a bolus injection if cardiac arrest is imminent.
- **Paediatric Considerations:** For small doses in infants, a reconstituted vial can be further diluted to 1 mg/mL with isotonic saline to ensure accuracy [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to the active substance or any of the excipients (mannitol, sodium acetate) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity:** Risk of anaphylactic/anaphylactoid reactions exists as the product is a heterologous animal protein. Patients with known allergies to sheep protein, papain, chymopapain, bromelain, or alpha-gal (red meat allergy) are at increased risk.
- **Electrolyte Shifts:** Massive digoxin toxicity causes hyperkalaemia. Treatment with **DIGIFAB** causes a rapid shift of potassium back into the cells, potentially leading to severe hypokalaemia. Frequent monitoring of serum potassium is essential in the first few hours post-administration.
- **Cardiac Function:** Removal of the inotropic effect of digoxin may exacerbate heart failure or low cardiac output states.
- **Laboratory Interference:** **DIGIFAB** interferes with standard digitalis immunoassays. Total serum digoxin levels will rise precipitously but reflect bound (inactive) drug. Clinically relevant (free) digoxin measurements are only reliable once the Fab fragments are eliminated, which may take several days (or a week in renal impairment) [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are ranked by frequency based on limited clinical study data (n=23).

| System Organ Class | Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Metabolism** | Common | Hypokalaemia, hyperkalaemia |
| **Nervous System** | Common | Headache, confusional state |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Common | Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation, abdominal distension |
| **Cardiac** | Common | Worsening cardiac failure, chest pain, hypotension, orthostatic hypotension |
| **Renal** | Common | Renal failure |
| **General** | Common | Fatigue, infusion site phlebitis, influenza-like illness |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Exacerbation of low cardiac output and rapid ventricular response in atrial fibrillation due to withdrawal of digoxin's therapeutic effect [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- No formal drug interaction studies have been conducted.
- **DIGIFAB** specifically binds digoxin; it may also bind other cardiac glycosides (e.g., digitoxin, lanatoside C), though data are limited [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category **B2**. There are no data on use in pregnant women. Use only if the clinical benefit to the mother outweighs the potential risk to the fetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if **DIGIFAB** is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding should be discontinued during treatment [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No animal studies have been performed to evaluate effects on fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No specific studies have been conducted in children, though similar products (Digibind) have been used successfully in infants. Dosing should be based on benefit-risk assessment.
- **Elderly Use:** No specific studies conducted. Monitoring should be more frequent due to the higher likelihood of impaired renal function.
- **Renal Impairment:** The elimination half-life is significantly prolonged (potentially up to 10-fold) in patients with renal failure. These patients must be monitored for a longer period (a week or more) for possible recurrence of toxicity (recrudescent toxicity) due to the delayed excretion of the Fab-digoxin complex [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **DIGIFAB** has a high affinity (10^9 to 10^10 M^-1) for digoxin. It binds molecules of digoxin, reducing free serum levels and shifting the equilibrium away from cardiac receptors, thereby reversing toxic effects.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is approximately 0.3 L/kg.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal.
    - **Half-life:** Approximately 15 hours in patients with normal renal function [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacodynamic Study:** A study of 16 healthy volunteers compared **DIGIFAB** to Digibind. Both products reduced free serum digoxin to undetectable levels within hours.
- **Efficacy Study:** A prospective study of 15 patients with life-threatening toxicity showed that 93% had resolution of toxicity by 20 hours. ECG abnormalities improved in 10 out of 15 patients within 4 hours [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store between **2°C and 8°C** (Refrigerate. Do not freeze).
- Reconstituted product should be used immediately.
- The vial stopper is not made with natural rubber latex [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australian Classification:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
