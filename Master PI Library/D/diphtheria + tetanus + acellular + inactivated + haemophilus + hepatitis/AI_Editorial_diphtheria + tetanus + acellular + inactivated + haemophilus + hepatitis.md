# Editorial Product Information: diphtheria + tetanus + acellular + inactivated + haemophilus + hepatitis

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **diphtheria + tetanus + acellular + inactivated + haemophilus + hepatitis**
- Chemical class: Combination pediatric vaccine comprising diphtheria and tetanus toxoids, acellular pertussis antigens, inactivated (killed) poliovirus, Haemophilus influenzae type b (PRP) conjugate, and recombinant hepatitis B surface antigen; adjuvanted with aluminum salts.
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration: Intramuscular injection.
- DO NOT include brand or trade names, dosage form physical descriptions, or schedule/classification listings.

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 2018. [US]
- Most recent revision/update of the available prescribing information: April 2026. [US]
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources for any jurisdiction.
- Other jurisdictions' first registration/approval dates and PI revision dates: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Indicated for active immunization to prevent:
  - Diphtheria
  - Tetanus
  - Pertussis
  - Poliomyelitis
  - Hepatitis B
  - Invasive disease due to Haemophilus influenzae type b
- Approved patient age range in available sources: children from 6 weeks through 4 years of age (prior to the 5th birthday). [US]
- Additional indication notes:
  - A three-dose series of this combination vaccine constitutes primary immunization against diphtheria, tetanus, Haemophilus influenzae type b invasive disease, and poliomyelitis.
  - A three-dose series may be used to complete the hepatitis B immunization series in infants born to HBsAg‑negative mothers (when a prior dose of any hepatitis B vaccine was given at or before 1 month of age).
  - A three-dose series of this vaccine does NOT by itself constitute completion of the primary pertussis immunization series; an additional dose of a pertussis-containing vaccine is required to complete the primary pertussis series.
  - Use to complete portions of multi-dose schedules (e.g., to complete first three doses of a 5-dose DTaP series) may be acceptable when clinically indicated and when the infant/child is scheduled to receive the other antigens contained in this combination vaccine; data on mixed sequences from different manufacturers are limited or not available.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing
- Recommended dose: single 0.5 mL intramuscular injection per dose.
- Routine schedule (primary series): three doses administered at ages 2 months, 4 months, and 6 months.
- First dose may be given as early as 6 weeks of age.
- The 3-dose primary series is intended for infants from 6 weeks through 4 years of age (prior to 5th birthday).

### Dosing by indication / special schedule notes
- Hepatitis B series completion: a 3-dose series of this vaccine may be used to complete hepatitis B vaccination in infants born to HBsAg‑negative mothers who previously received 1 or 2 doses of another hepatitis B vaccine; however, data on safety and effectiveness in such mixed-series situations are limited.
- Pertussis: three doses of this combination vaccine do not alone complete the primary pertussis series; an additional pertussis-containing vaccine dose is required per national immunization schedules to complete pertussis primary immunization.
- Administration following previous doses of other vaccines:
  - May be used after 1 or 2 prior doses of inactivated poliovirus vaccine (IPV), Haemophilus influenzae type b conjugate vaccine, or certain DTaP‑containing vaccines when the infant/child is also scheduled to receive the remaining antigens contained in this combination product; data on safety and effectiveness of mixed manufacturer sequences are limited or not available.

### Administration instructions
- Shake the prefilled syringe immediately before use until a uniform, white, cloudy suspension is obtained.
- Inspect visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration; do not administer if particulate matter or discoloration is present.
- Administer intramuscularly; for infants younger than 1 year the anterolateral thigh is the preferred site. Do not inject into the gluteal area.
- Do not mix with, reconstitute with, or combine through syringe mixing with any other vaccine. Discard any unused portion remaining in the syringe after administration.

### Titration schedules
- Not applicable.

### Missed dose instructions
- Specific catch-up schedules and intervals for missed doses are not detailed in the available sources; follow local/national immunization schedule guidance for catch-up dosing. Not specified in available sources for explicit intervals.

## 5. Contraindications
List of all contraindications reported in available sources:
- Severe allergic reaction (e.g., anaphylaxis) to a previous dose of this vaccine, to any ingredient of this vaccine (including residuals from manufacturing), or to any other vaccine containing diphtheria toxoid, tetanus toxoid, pertussis antigens, inactivated poliovirus, hepatitis B antigen, or Haemophilus influenzae type b conjugate.
- Encephalopathy (e.g., coma, prolonged seizures, decreased level of consciousness) within 7 days of a previous dose of a pertussis-containing vaccine that is not attributable to another identifiable cause.
- Progressive neurologic disorder (including infantile spasms, uncontrolled epilepsy, progressive encephalopathy) until a treatment regimen has been established and the condition has stabilized.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed/Highlighted warnings
- Not specified as a boxed warning in the available sources; critical warnings are presented below. [US]

### General warnings and precautions
- **Management of acute allergic reactions:** Epinephrine (1:1,000) and other appropriate agents/equipment must be immediately available in case of anaphylactic or acute hypersensitivity reactions following vaccination.
- **Adverse events following prior pertussis vaccine:** If any of the following events occurred after prior administration of a pertussis-containing vaccine, carefully weigh benefits and risks before administering this vaccine:
  - Temperature ≥40.5°C (≥105°F) within 48 hours (not attributable to another cause).
  - Collapse or shock-like state (hypotonic‑hyporesponsive episode, HHE) within 48 hours.
  - Persistent, inconsolable crying lasting ≥3 hours within 48 hours.
  - Seizures with or without fever within 3 days.
- **Guillain‑Barré syndrome and brachial neuritis:** A prior history of Guillain‑Barré syndrome occurring within 6 weeks of receipt of a tetanus‑toxoid–containing vaccine may increase the risk of recurrence after administration of this vaccine.
- **Altered immunocompetence:** Immunocompromised persons, including those receiving immunosuppressive therapy, may not mount an adequate immune response to vaccination.
- **Apnea in premature infants:** Apnea has been observed in some infants born prematurely following intramuscular vaccination. The timing of vaccination in premature infants should consider the individual infant's medical status and the potential benefits and risks.
- **Limitations of vaccine effectiveness:** Vaccination may not protect all vaccine recipients.
- **Interference with diagnostic tests:** Vaccine-derived Haemophilus influenzae type b polyribosylribitol phosphate (PRP) antigen may be detectable in the urine of vaccinees for at least 30 days after vaccination using sensitive assays; urine antigen detection may not be diagnostic for suspected Hib disease for at least 30 days following vaccination.

### Monitoring requirements
- Observe vaccine recipients per local practice for immediate adverse reactions (including anaphylaxis).
- For infants with prior neurologic events after pertussis vaccination, closely assess risks and benefits prior to administration and monitor accordingly.
- For premature infants, consider post-vaccination monitoring for apnea as clinically indicated.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Do not administer to persons meeting contraindications (see Section 5).
- Avoid intramuscular injection into gluteal region.
- Ensure resuscitation equipment and trained personnel are available when vaccinating.

## 7. Adverse Effects
- Presenting every adverse effect listed in the available source material. Frequency categories used: very common (>10%), common (1–10%), uncommon (0.1–1%), rare (<0.1%), very rare (<0.01%), and "Not known" when frequency cannot be estimated from available data.

### Summary of solicited adverse reactions (combined across clinical studies)
- Solicited adverse reactions recorded within 0–5 days following any dose included (percentages from clinical trials):
  - Irritability: ≥55% (very common)
  - Crying: ≥45% (very common)
  - Injection site pain: ≥44% (very common)
  - Somnolence: ≥40% (very common)
  - Injection site erythema: ≥25% (very common)
  - Decreased appetite: ≥23% (very common)
  - Fever ≥38.0°C: ≥19% (very common)
  - Injection site swelling: ≥18% (very common)
  - Vomiting: ≥9% (common)

### Non-fatal serious adverse events (clinical trials)
- Across the two pivotal US studies:
  - Serious adverse events within 30 days following any infant dose occurred in approximately 2.0% of vaccine recipients; vaccine-related serious adverse events were reported in 0.1% (4 participants) consisting primarily of pyrexia and one apparent life‑threatening event (vomiting followed by pallor and lethargy).
  - Deaths occurred in a small number of participants across studies; none were assessed as related to vaccination. Reported causes among participants who received the vaccine included sepsis, sudden infant death syndrome (two cases), asphyxia, unknown cause, and hydrocephalus. Timing of deaths varied and none were attributed to vaccination.

### Postmarketing adverse events (reported voluntarily)
- Immune system disorders: hypersensitivity reactions (rash, urticaria, dyspnea, erythema multiforme), anaphylactic reactions (urticaria, angioedema, generalized edema, facial edema, shock). — Frequency: Not known (cannot estimate from available data).
- General disorders and administration site conditions: extensive swelling of the injected limb (including swelling involving adjacent joints). — Frequency: Not known.
- Nervous system: seizure, febrile seizure, hypotonic‑hyporesponsive episode (HHE). — Frequency: Not known.

### Serious adverse effects highlighted
- Anaphylaxis and other severe hypersensitivity reactions (postmarketing) — monitor and treat promptly.
- Seizures, febrile seizures, hypotonic‑hyporesponsive episodes — cautions for patients with prior events; seizures occurring within 3 days after prior pertussis-containing vaccine warrant careful consideration before subsequent administration.
- Guillain‑Barré syndrome and brachial neuritis — prior occurrence within 6 weeks of tetanus‑toxoid vaccination may increase risk of recurrence.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamic or diagnostic interactions
- Interference with laboratory tests: sensitive urinary latex agglutination and similar assays have detected vaccine-derived PRP antigen in urine for at least 30 days following vaccination; therefore, urine antigen detection tests for Haemophilus influenzae type b may not be diagnostic for at least 30 days after vaccination.

### Clinically significant interactions and management
- No specific concomitant vaccine interactions contraindicating coadministration were identified in the available sources; however:
  - The vaccine has been administered concomitantly at separate injection sites with pneumococcal conjugate vaccine and rotavirus vaccine in clinical studies, and with other routine infant vaccines. Local practice and product-specific guidance for coadministration should be followed.
  - Data are limited regarding mixed sequences with DTaP-containing vaccines from different manufacturers; use of mixed schedules should follow national guidelines and clinical judgement.
- Attribute jurisdiction-specific interactions: Not specified in available sources for jurisdictions other than the provided [US] information.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Approved use: infants and children from 6 weeks through 4 years of age (prior to 5th birthday). [US]

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- The vaccine is indicated for active immunization to prevent disease caused by diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis, poliovirus, hepatitis B, and invasive Haemophilus influenzae type b; it contains toxoids, recombinant antigen (HBsAg), inactivated polioviruses, acellular pertussis antigens, and a PRP‑protein conjugate to elicit an adaptive immune response. Detailed mechanistic pathways (cellular/antibody response kinetics) are not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacokinetics

## 12. Clinical Trials
### Pivotal and supporting studies — summary
- Clinical program overview:
  - Total of six clinical studies evaluated safety (and immunogenicity) in infants; 5,251 infants aged 43 to 99 days at enrollment received at least one dose of the vaccine across studies.
  - Two controlled clinical studies conducted in the United States enrolled a total of 3,380 infants who received at least one dose in the vaccine group (studies referred to as two pivotal trials). Across these two US studies, randomized participants totaled 3,392 in the vaccine group and 889 in control groups.
  - Demographics in the two US studies: approximately 52.6% male and 47.4% female; racial distribution included White (≈71.7%), Black (≈11.0%), American Indian or Alaska Native (≈4.5%), Asian (≈3.5%), and other races (≈9.3%); approximately 81.8% were non-Hispanic/non-Latino.

### Trial design and concomitant vaccines
- Study designs: randomized, controlled clinical trials evaluating safety and immunogenicity when administered as a 3-dose primary series at 2, 4, and 6 months of age.
- Concomitant administration: in clinical studies the investigational vaccine was administered concomitantly (at separate anatomical sites) with routine infant vaccines, including a pneumococcal conjugate vaccine and oral rotavirus vaccine. Control groups received other routine DTaP-containing and hepatitis B vaccines per study protocols.
- At 15 months of age, participants in different study arms received recommended booster doses per protocol (details varied by study).

### Primary outcomes and key findings
- Safety: Solicited injection site and systemic reactions within 0–5 days were recorded by caregivers. The most common solicited reactions and their approximate frequencies are listed in Section 7 (Adverse Effects).
- Serious adverse events: Occurred at low rates (≈2.0% within 30 days post‑vaccination among vaccine recipients in the US studies); four vaccine-related serious adverse events were reported among vaccine recipients (predominantly pyrexia and one apparent life‑threatening event). Deaths occurred in a small number of participants across the clinical program but were not assessed as related to vaccination.
- Immunogenicity / effectiveness: Effectiveness was established in the 6 weeks through 6 months age group; immunogenicity data supported use in the approved pediatric age range. Specific serologic titers and immune-response numeric data are not provided in the available excerpts.

### Limitations of the clinical data
- Data on mixed sequences using DTaP vaccines or other component vaccines from different manufacturers are limited or not available.
- Safety and effectiveness data are not available for infants younger than 6 weeks of age or for children/adolescents 5–17 years of age.
- Long-term outcomes and rare adverse events rely largely on postmarketing surveillance.
