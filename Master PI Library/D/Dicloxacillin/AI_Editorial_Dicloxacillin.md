# Editorial Product Information: Dicloxacillin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) document provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only Australian source data was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Dicloxacillin (as dicloxacillin sodium).
- **Chemical Class:** Penicillinase-resistant isoxazolyl penicillin; semisynthetic antibiotic.
- **Brand Names:** ARX-Dicloxacillin [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J01CF01 (Beta-lactamase resistant penicillins).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
  - Hard gelatin capsules: 250 mg and 500 mg [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Dicloxacillin is indicated for the treatment of confirmed or suspected infections caused by staphylococci and other Gram-positive coccal organisms [Australian]:
- Skin and skin structure infections.
- Wound infections and infected burns.
- Cellulitis.
- Osteomyelitis.
- Pneumonia (Note: Benzylpenicillin remains the preferred agent for streptococcal pneumonia).

**Clinical Usage Note:** Dicloxacillin has narrower intrinsic activity than benzylpenicillin and should not be used for organisms susceptible to benzylpenicillin. If sensitivities reveal a non-resistant staphylococcus, therapy should be switched to a narrower-spectrum penicillin [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Children >12 years)
- **Mild to Moderate Infections:** 250 mg every 6 hours [Australian].
- **Severe Infections:** 500 mg every 6 hours [Australian].

### Dosing by Indication
- **Group A Beta-haemolytic Streptococci:** Minimum 10 days of treatment to prevent acute rheumatic fever or glomerulonephritis [Australian].
- **Severe Staphylococcal Infections:** Minimum 14 days of treatment [Australian].
- **Endocarditis and Osteomyelitis:** Requires long-term therapy [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Fasting State:** Must be administered on an empty stomach, 1 to 2 hours before food [Australian].
- **Oesophageal Safety:** To minimise the risk of oesophageal ulceration, capsules should be taken with at least 120 mL of water. Patients must not take the dose in a supine position or immediately before retiring to bed [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known history of hypersensitivity to any penicillin or any component of the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Anaphylaxis:** Serious and occasionally fatal hypersensitivity (anaphylactoid) reactions have occurred. Cross-allergenicity exists between penicillins, cephalosporins, and other beta-lactams. Immediate emergency treatment with adrenaline is required for severe reactions [Australian].
- **Pseudomembranous Colitis:** *Clostridium difficile*-associated disease may range from mild diarrhoea to life-threatening colitis. Avoid drugs that delay peristalsis (e.g., opiates) if this is suspected [Australian].
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Associated with cholestatic hepatitis and jaundice. Risk is higher in patients over 55 years of age; use in this population should be clearly justified [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Periodic assessment of hepatic, renal, and haematopoietic function is required during prolonged therapy. Baseline white blood cell and differential counts are recommended [Australian].
- **Superinfection:** Overgrowth of non-susceptible organisms may occur, requiring discontinuation of therapy and appropriate intervention [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Probenecid:** Competitively inhibits renal tubular secretion, increasing and prolonging serum concentrations of dicloxacillin [Australian].
- **Warfarin:** Dicloxacillin may reduce the anticoagulant effect. Prothrombin time should be monitored closely [Australian].
- **Phenytoin:** Concurrent use with related oxacillins has resulted in decreased phenytoin levels; monitor for reduced anticonvulsant efficacy [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B2** [Australian]. Safety in human pregnancy has not been established. Use only when clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Dicloxacillin is distributed into breast milk. Exercise caution when administering to nursing women [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Data not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Experience in the neonatal period is limited. Dicloxacillin is not recommended for newborns as renal excretion is incompletely developed, potentially leading to toxic accumulation [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of hepatotoxicity in those >55 years. High doses (2-4g/day) in geriatric patients undergoing surgery have been linked to nephrotoxicity [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** In severe impairment, the half-life increases 2- to 3-fold. It is recommended to increase the dosing interval to **every 8 hours**, though individual doses do not require reduction [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution. Consider dose reduction in patients with significant liver disease [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal; inhibits bacterial cell wall biosynthesis during active multiplication. It is resistant to inactivation by staphylococcal penicillinase [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed (50-94%) in fasting state. Bioavailability is significantly reduced by food.
    - **Distribution:** 95-99% protein bound (mainly albumin). Distributes to bone, bile, and pleural/synovial fluids; minimal penetration into CSF.
    - **Metabolism:** Partially metabolised to active (5-hydroxymethyl) and inactive metabolites.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (glomerular filtration and tubular secretion). Approximately 50% excreted unchanged in urine. Half-life is ~0.7 hours [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Active against *S. aureus* (methicillin-susceptible), *S. pyogenes*, Viridans group streptococci, and *S. pneumoniae*. Not active against MRSA [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store in original container in a dry place below 25°C [Australian].
- Keep out of reach of children.
