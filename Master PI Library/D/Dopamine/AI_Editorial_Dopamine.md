# Editorial Product Information: Dopamine Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) document provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- This document serves as a harmonized editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals to facilitate clinical decision-making. 
- **Important:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, legally binding Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the local approved PI for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Dopamine hydrochloride; a naturally occurring biochemical catecholamine precursor of noradrenaline and adrenaline.
- **Chemical Class:** Adrenergic and dopaminergic agent; catecholamine.
- **Brand Names:** DBL Sterile Dopamine Concentrate [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 200 mg/5 mL sterile solution in glass ampoules [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only (must be diluted).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Dopamine is indicated for the correction of haemodynamic imbalances associated with:
- **Acute hypotension or shock:** Resulting from myocardial infarction, endotoxic septicaemia, trauma, or renal failure [Australian].
- **Cardiac Surgery:** As an adjunct for persistent hypotension following the correction of hypovolaemia [Australian].
- **Chronic Cardiac Decompensation:** Such as in cases of congestive heart failure [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Initial Dose:** 2 to 5 micrograms/kg/min in patients expected to respond to modest increases in cardiac contractility and renal perfusion [Australian].
- **Seriously Ill Patients:** Begin at 5 micrograms/kg/min, increasing gradually in increments of 5 to 10 micrograms/kg/min.
- **Maintenance:** Most patients are stabilized at doses below 20 micrograms/kg/min. Doses between 20 to 50 micrograms/kg/min may be required in severe cases [Australian].

### Dosing by Specific Indication
- **Chronic Refractory Congestive Heart Failure:** Start at 0.5 to 2 micrograms/kg/min, titrating based on urine flow. Usual maintenance is 1 to 3 micrograms/kg/min [Australian].
- **Occlusive Vascular Disease:** Start at low doses (1 to 2 micrograms/kg/min) and titrate with extreme caution due to the risk of peripheral ischaemia [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Dilution:** Must be diluted before use. Common dilutions include 200 mg in 250 mL (800 mcg/mL) or 500 mL (400 mcg/mL) of compatible IV fluid.
- **Compatible Fluids:** Sodium chloride 0.9%, Glucose 5%, Glucose 5% in Sodium Chloride 0.9% or 0.45%, Lactated Ringer's, and Sodium Lactate 1/6 M [Australian].
- **Incompatibility:** Do **NOT** add to alkaline solutions (e.g., Sodium Bicarbonate), as dopamine will be inactivated.
- **Site of Administration:** Infuse into a large vein (preferably the antecubital fossa) to minimize the risk of extravasation and subsequent tissue necrosis [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Phaeochromocytoma:** Risk of acute hypertension due to catecholamine release [Australian].
- **Arrhythmias:** Atrial or ventricular tachyarrhythmias, or ventricular fibrillation [Australian].
- **Anaesthetics:** Concurrent use with cyclopropane or halogenated hydrocarbon anaesthetics [Australian].
- **Hyperthyroidism** [Australian].
- **Ergotamine:** Concurrent use increases the risk of vascular ischaemia and gangrene [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypovolaemia:** Must be corrected with appropriate volume expanders (whole blood, plasma, or fluids) before dopamine administration. Target CVP is 10-15 cm H₂O or pulmonary wedge pressure of 14-18 mm Hg [Australian].
- **Monoamine Oxidase (MAO) Inhibitors:** Patients currently taking or who have taken MAOIs in the last 2-3 weeks require a significant dose reduction (approximately 1/10th of the usual dose) [Australian].
- **Extravasation:** Can cause local tissue necrosis and sloughing. Monitor infusion sites continuously. 
    - **Antidote:** Infiltrate the area immediately with 10–15 mL of saline containing 5–10 mg of **phentolamine** using a fine needle [Australian].
- **Sulfite Sensitivity:** Contains sodium metabisulfite, which may cause anaphylactic or asthmatic reactions in susceptible individuals [Australian].
- **Occlusive Vascular Disease:** High risk of peripheral ischaemia and gangrene. Monitor skin color and temperature in extremities [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Routine monitoring of ECG, blood pressure, cardiac output, renal output (urine flow), and peripheral perfusion is mandatory [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions were observed in 19% of patients during clinical evaluation [Australian].

| Frequency | System Organ Class | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Common** (1-10%) | Cardiovascular | Ectopic beats, tachycardia, anginal pain, palpitation, hypotension, vasoconstriction |
| | Gastrointestinal | Nausea, vomiting |
| | Nervous System | Headache |
| | Respiratory | Dyspnoea |
| **Less Common** (<1%) | Cardiovascular | Atrial fibrillation, aberrant ventricular conduction, bradycardia, widened QRS, hypertension |
| | Nervous System | Piloerection, anxiety |
| | Biochemical | Azotaemia |
| **Serious/Rare** | Tissue/Vascular | Gangrene of feet (at doses >10 mcg/kg/min), tissue necrosis (extravasation), fatal ventricular arrhythmias |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Anaesthetics (Cyclopropane/Halogenated hydrocarbons):** Sensitize the myocardium to dopamine; risk of arrhythmias [Australian].
- **Alpha and Beta Blockers:** Antagonize the respective effects of dopamine. Propranolol and metoprolol antagonize cardiac effects; alpha-blockers antagonize peripheral vasoconstriction [Australian].
- **Tricyclic Antidepressants:** May potentiate cardiovascular effects, leading to arrhythmias or severe hypertension [Australian].
- **Digitalis Glycosides:** Increased risk of cardiac arrhythmias [Australian].
- **Phenytoin (IV):** May cause sudden hypotension and bradycardia [Australian].
- **Oxytocin/Ergot Alkaloids:** Potentiate pressor effects; risk of severe hypertension and cerebral vessel rupture [Australian].
- **Vasodilators (Glyceryl trinitrate, Nitroprusside):** May cause hypotension [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. Animal studies show evidence of increased foetal damage (cataracts and decreased survival in rats). Use only if benefits outweigh risks.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety is not established. It is unknown if dopamine is excreted in human milk. Dopamine is orally inactive, but breastfeeding is not recommended unless essential [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies have not been performed to assess effects on fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended; safety and efficacy in children have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific dose adjustment suggested, but requires intensive monitoring of blood pressure, renal flow, and peripheral perfusion [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Metabolites are excreted renally. Effect of impairment is unknown; close monitoring advised [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Metabolized by MAO and COMT in tissues and liver. Close monitoring advised as the effect of liver dysfunction is unknown [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Dopamine stimulates alpha-adrenergic, beta-adrenergic, and dopaminergic receptors in a dose-dependent manner [Australian]:
- **Low Dose (0.5–2 mcg/kg/min):** Selective dopaminergic activation; renal and mesenteric vasodilation; increased GFR and sodium excretion.
- **Medium Dose (2–10 mcg/kg/min):** Beta-1 receptor activation; increased cardiac output, stroke volume, and heart rate.
- **High Dose (>10 mcg/kg/min):** Alpha-receptor activation; peripheral vasoconstriction; increased systolic and diastolic blood pressure.

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Onset of Action:** 5 minutes.
- **Duration of Action:** <10 minutes (up to 1 hour in patients on MAOIs) [Australian].
- **Half-life:** Approximately 2 minutes (rapidly metabolized and excreted) [Australian].
- **Metabolism:** Metabolized in liver, kidneys, and plasma by MAO and COMT. 75% converted to dopamine metabolites; 25% synthesized into noradrenaline [Australian].
- **Elimination:** 97% excreted in urine as metabolites [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C. Protect from light [Australian].
- **Stability:** Once diluted, the solution is stable for 24 hours (at 23°–25°C). Dilution should be performed immediately prior to administration [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
