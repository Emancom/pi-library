# Editorial Product Information: Death Adder Antivenom

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis designed for use by healthcare professionals. It provides a consolidated clinical overview of the medicine's profile across the listed jurisdictions.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Death Adder Antivenom (equine).
- **Chemical Class:** Equine-derived purified plasma proteins (immunoglobulins) immunised against *Acanthophis antarcticus* venom.
- **Brand Name(s):** Death Adder Antivenom [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J06AA (Antisnake-venom sera) [Not explicitly specified in source, but implied by class].
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Concentrated solution for injection in glass vials. Each vial contains 6,000 units of antivenom (standardised to neutralise the average in vitro venom yield of a death adder).
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only. 
- **Excipients:** 2.2 mg phenol, 8 mg sodium chloride, ≤ 170 mg/mL equine plasma protein, and water for injections [Australian].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Seqirus Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Systemic Envenoming:** For the treatment of patients exhibiting clinical manifestations of systemic envenoming following a bite by a death adder (*Acanthophis antarcticus*) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric):** The recommended initial dose is the contents of one vial (6,000 units) [Australian].
- **Dosing by Severity:** Patients with severe systemic envenoming may require multiple vials to control neurotoxic paralysis. Reports indicate requirements of up to five vials in some cases [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - **Route:** Intravenous infusion only. Do **not** administer via the intramuscular route [Australian].
    - **Dilution:** The antivenom should be diluted 1 in 10 (e.g., 1 part antivenom to 9 parts diluent). In patients at risk of fluid overload (e.g., small children), a 1 in 5 dilution may be more appropriate [Australian].
    - **Diluents:** 0.9% w/v Sodium Chloride or Hartmann’s Solution [Australian].
    - **Rate:** Administer slowly.
    - **Timing:** Use immediately after dilution. Do not store diluted antivenom [Australian].
- **First Aid Interaction:** If a pressure immobilisation bandage and splint have been applied, they must **not** be removed until the antivenom is ready for infusion, as removal may precipitate a sudden surge of systemic envenoming [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Absolute Contraindications:** None recorded when used for confirmed systemic envenoming [Australian].
- **Clinical Threshold:** The product should only be administered if there is clear evidence of systemic envenoming with potential for serious toxic effects [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in provided source.
- **Hypersensitivity and Anaphylaxis:** As a horse-serum-derived product, there is a high risk of severe allergic reactions, including fatal anaphylaxis. 
    - **Pre-treatment Preparation:** Adrenaline must be prepared and ready for immediate use before administration.
    - **Atopy:** Patients with a history of animal allergies (cats, dogs, sheep) or prior exposure to equine-derived products (e.g., equine Tetanus Antitoxin used in Australia prior to 1974) are at increased risk of cross-reactivity [Australian].
    - **Skin Testing:** Skin testing to predict allergy is unreliable and must not be performed [Australian].
- **Serum Sickness:** Delayed serum sickness is common (occurring in at least 5% of first-time recipients) and risk increases with higher volumes of antivenom [Australian].
- **Transmission of Infectious Agents:** As the product is prepared from animal plasma, the risk of transmitting infective agents (including unknown pathogens) cannot be totally excluded [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** 
    - Patients must be monitored for at least 6 hours post-administration [Australian].
    - Suspected snake bite cases should be observed for at least 12 hours after the bite or removal of bandages [Australian].
    - Monitoring should focus on neuromuscular impairment, coagulopathy, myolysis, and renal function [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are categorised by frequency based on post-approval data for equine snake antivenoms.

| Frequency | System Organ Class | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Common** (≥1/100 to <1/10) | Immune System | Allergic reactions, anaphylactic shock, delayed serum sickness |
| | Nervous System | Headache |
| | Skin | Urticaria, rash |
| | General | Pyrexia, chills |
| **Uncommon** (≥1/1000 to <1/100) | Gastrointestinal | Abdominal pain, vomiting, nausea, diarrhoea |
| | Musculoskeletal | Myalgia |
| | General | Local injection site reactions, chest pain |

**Serious Adverse Effects:**
- **Anaphylaxis:** Can occur rapidly during infusion. If it occurs, the infusion must be suspended and treated immediately [Australian].
- **Delayed Serum Sickness:** Typically presents days or weeks after treatment with fever, rash, arthralgia, lymphadenopathy, and albuminuria. Less commonly: arthritis, nephritis, neuropathy, and vasculitis [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** No data available in source documentation [Australian].
- **Management:** No clinically significant interactions with other medicines were identified during registration [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category: Not assigned]. Information is limited and inconclusive. Clinicians must weigh the risks of untreated envenoming against the potential risks of antivenom administration to the fetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** No data available. A risk-benefit assessment is required, balancing the necessity of treatment against potential risks [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** The dose for children is identical to the adult dose. Children may become critically ill more rapidly than adults and may require more antivenom. Caution is advised regarding dilution volumes to prevent fluid overload [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No specific dosage adjustments provided; however, monitoring for renal impairment is required following envenoming [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** The antivenom contains specific antibodies that neutralise the neurotoxins present in death adder venom [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Effective in reversing paralysis resulting from death adder envenoming. Case reports indicate successful reversal even when administered 7 hours post-envenoming [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** No specific information available regarding the absorption, distribution, metabolism, or excretion of this antivenom [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Summary:** Data is limited to case series. One series of 5 patients showed that the antivenom was effective in reversing paralysis. In this series, the maximum dose required was 5 vials, and the longest delay before successful administration was 7 hours [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze) [Australian].
- **Light:** Protect the vial from light [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].
- **Handling:** Contains no antimicrobial preservative; intended for single-use in one patient only. Discard any residue [Australian].
