# Editorial Product Information: Desloratadine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial synthesis: **US (FDA)**. [Note: While the context provided mentioned an Australian document, the source material provided for review was the US Prescribing Information for Lupin Pharmaceuticals, Inc.]
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals to provide a clear, clinically accurate summary of desloratadine.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, approved Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for regional compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Desloratadine.
- **Chemical Class:** A long-acting tricyclic histamine-1 (H1) receptor antagonist; the primary active metabolite of loratadine.
- **Brand Name(s):** Desloratadine (Lupin) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources (R06AX27).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Film-coated tablets:** 5 mg [US].
    - **Oral solution:** (Referenced in pediatric studies; specific strengths for Lupin product not detailed in provided document).
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Lupin Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (US); Lupin Limited (India).

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Seasonal Allergic Rhinitis (SAR):** For the relief of nasal and non-nasal symptoms in patients 12 years of age and older [US-approved].
- **Perennial Allergic Rhinitis (PAR):** For the relief of nasal and non-nasal symptoms in patients 12 years of age and older [US-approved].
- **Pediatric Indications:** The safety and effectiveness of the oral solution have been established for SAR and PAR in pediatric patients 6 months to 11 years of age [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Adolescents $\ge$ 12 years):** 5 mg (one tablet) once daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** May be taken without regard to food [US].
- **Renal and Hepatic Impairment:** In adult patients with liver or kidney dysfunction, a starting dose of 5 mg every other day is recommended [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Titration:** Not applicable.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to **desloratadine**, any of the product excipients, or to **loratadine** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Acute reactions including rash, pruritus, urticaria, edema, dyspnea, and anaphylaxis have been reported. Therapy must be discontinued immediately if such reactions occur, and alternative treatments sought [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Clinical Precautions:** Patients should be advised not to exceed the recommended dose, as higher doses do not increase efficacy and may increase the risk of somnolence [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (Adults and Adolescents $\ge$ 12 years)
Adverse events reported in $\ge$ 2% of patients receiving 5 mg daily and occurring more frequently than with placebo:

| Adverse Event | Desloratadine 5 mg (n=1655) | Placebo (n=1652) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pharyngitis** | 4.1% | 2.0% |
| **Dry Mouth** | 3.0% | 1.9% |
| **Myalgia** | 2.1% | 1.8% |
| **Fatigue** | 2.1% | 1.2% |
| **Somnolence** | 2.1% | 1.8% |
| **Dysmenorrhea** | 2.1% | 1.6% |

### Pediatric Populations
- **6 to 11 months:** Upper respiratory tract infections, diarrhea, fever, and irritability were common [US].
- **12 to 23 months:** Fever, diarrhea, and upper respiratory tract infections were most frequent [US].
- **2 to 5 years:** Fever, urinary tract infection, and varicella were noted [US].

### Post-Marketing Experience (Serious and Spontaneous Reports)
- **Cardiac:** Tachycardia, palpitations.
- **Respiratory:** Dyspnea.
- **Hepatobiliary:** Hepatitis, elevated liver enzymes (including bilirubin).
- **Nervous System:** Psychomotor hyperactivity, movement disorders (dystonia, tics, extrapyramidal symptoms), and seizures.
- **Metabolism:** Increased appetite.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **Cytochrome P450 Inhibitors:** Co-administration with **ketoconazole**, **erythromycin**, or **azithromycin** increases plasma concentrations of desloratadine and 3-hydroxydesloratadine. These increases were not associated with clinically relevant changes in safety profiles or QTc intervals in clinical trials [US].
    - **Fluoxetine and Cimetidine:** Co-administration leads to increased plasma levels but no clinically significant changes in the safety profile [US].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** No specific food or grapefruit juice interactions identified [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly lettered in the revised labeling format, but the data indicates limited human data to inform drug-associated risks. Animal studies (rats/rabbits) at high multiples of the human dose (up to 230–320x) showed no teratogenicity. Reduced fetal weight occurred only at maternally toxic doses [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Desloratadine passes into breast milk. A decision should be made whether to discontinue nursing or the drug, weighing the benefits of breastfeeding against the potential risk to the infant or the mother's clinical need [US].
- **Fertility:** In animal models, male fertility was decreased at doses 65x the human clinical exposure. No effect was seen at 10x the human clinical exposure [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - Approved for use down to 6 months of age (oral solution) [US].
    - Tablets are approved for $\ge$ 12 years of age [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include sufficient numbers of subjects $\ge$ 65 years to determine if they respond differently. Dosing should be cautious, starting at the low end of the range, reflecting the higher frequency of renal, hepatic, or cardiac impairment [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosing adjustment is required (5 mg every other day) due to increased AUC (up to 2.5-fold in severe cases) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Dosing adjustment is required (5 mg every other day) due to an approximate 2.4-fold increase in AUC regardless of severity [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A long-acting, selective H1-receptor antagonist. In vitro data shows inhibition of histamine release from human mast cells. It does not readily cross the blood-brain barrier [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ at approximately 3 hours. Bioavailability is not affected by food or grapefruit juice [US].
    - **Distribution:** 82–87% plasma protein bound [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolized to 3-hydroxydesloratadine (active). Approximately 6% of the population are "poor metabolizers" (mostly Blacks, 17%), who experience 6-fold higher AUC levels [US].
    - **Elimination:** Mean half-life is approximately 27 hours. Elimination is via urine and feces as metabolic products [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Seasonal Allergic Rhinitis:** Four double-blind, placebo-controlled trials (2–4 weeks) in patients $\ge$ 12 years showed 5 mg QD significantly reduced Total Symptom Scores (TSS) compared to placebo ($P < 0.01$). No additional benefit was found at doses $> 5$ mg [US].
- **Perennial Allergic Rhinitis:** Two 4-week trials showed 5 mg QD significantly reduced TSS compared to placebo ($P = 0.01$) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions permitted to 15° to 30°C (59° to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Heat sensitive; avoid exposure at or above 30°C (86°F). Protect from moisture and dispense in a tight, light-resistant container with a child-resistant closure [US].
