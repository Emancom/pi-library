# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **US (FDA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of the official product information for levocarnitine injection. It is intended for use by healthcare professionals as a clinically accurate, consolidated reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current official PI approved in their specific region for definitive prescribing information.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (Generic name); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. 
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule for fatty acid transport).
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for intravenous injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Dosing should be every 3 or 4 hours, and no less frequent than every 6 hours (either by infusion or I.V. injection). 
    - **Daily Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though up to 300 mg/kg has been administered if clinically required.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are adequate.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter and discoloration before administration.
- Use only if the solution is clear and the container is intact.
- Discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservative.

### Monitoring
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Perform weekly and monthly monitoring including blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine range: 35 to 60 µmol/L).
- **ESRD:** Therapy may be prompted by trough concentrations below normal (40 to 50 µmol/L).

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- None specified in available sources [US].

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred—primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions manifest within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and initiate medical treatment. Use caution and monitor closely if re-administering [US].
- **Seizures:** Reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Formulation Safety:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in urine [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- Trough plasma carnitine levels (especially in ESRD).
- Monitoring of INR in patients receiving concomitant warfarin.
- Routine blood chemistries and vital signs.

## 6. Adverse Effects

- **Other Reported Effects:** Body odor, nausea, and gastritis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concurrently with levocarnitine. **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Classification:** Not specified by letter category in provided source.
    - Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm.
    - No adequate/well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Animal data (dairy cows) indicate milk carnitine concentrations increase with exogenous administration. Benefits to the mother should be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility observed in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is based on mg/kg body weight (refer to Section 3).
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosing for ESRD patients is specific to post-dialysis administration (10-20 mg/kg). Caution is advised regarding the accumulation of metabolites if using oral formulations in patients with severely compromised renal function [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters which are rapidly eliminated.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following I.V. administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours.
    - **Bioavailability (Oral):** 15.1% to 15.9% (corrected for endogenous levels).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Studies:** Conducted in healthy volunteers and ESRD patients. Studies in ESRD patients (20 mg/kg three times weekly) showed that trough carnitine levels significantly increased from baseline (approx. 20 µmol/L) to stable concentrations (approx. 190–243 µmol/L) over 8–24 weeks.
- **Clinical Outcomes:** While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations is proven, the source notes that the effects of supplemental carnitine on clinical outcomes in ESRD patients have not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial.
