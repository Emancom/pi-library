# Editorial Product Information: Deferiprone

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of deferiprone, synthesising clinical and regulatory information for use by healthcare professionals.
*   **Prescribing Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Deferiprone (a synthetic bidentate iron (III) chelator).
*   **Brand Name(s):** **Ferriprox** [Australian].
*   **ATC Code:** V03AC02.
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    *   500 mg film-coated tablets [Australian].
    *   1,000 mg film-coated tablets [Australian].
    *   100 mg/mL oral solution [Australian].
*   **Route of Administration:** Oral.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Chiesi Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Iron Overload in Thalassaemia Major:** Indicated for the treatment of iron overload in patients with thalassaemia major for whom desferrioxamine therapy is contraindicated or has proven ineffective [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
*   **Initial Dose:** 75 mg/kg body weight per day, administered in three divided doses [Australian].
*   **Dose Range:** 75 mg/kg/day to 100 mg/kg/day [Australian].
*   **Maximum Dose:** 100 mg/kg/day. Doses exceeding this limit are not recommended due to limited safety experience and increased risk of neurological disorders [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
*   **Calculation:** Doses should be calculated to the nearest half-tablet or nearest 2.5 mL of oral solution [Australian].
*   **Monitoring:** Serum ferritin levels or other indicators of iron load should be monitored every two to three months to assess long-term efficacy.
*   **Therapy Interruption:** Consideration should be given to interrupting therapy if serum ferritin levels fall below 500 µg/L [Australian].

### Missed Dose
*   Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
*   Hypersensitivity to the active substance or any excipients [Australian].
*   History of recurrent episodes of neutropenia [Australian].
*   History of agranulocytosis [Australian].
*   **Pregnancy** [Australian].
*   **Breastfeeding** [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Hematologic Warnings (Agranulocytosis/Neutropenia)
*   **Serious Risk:** Deferiprone can cause fatal agranulocytosis. In clinical trials, the incidence of agranulocytosis (ANC < 0.5 x 10⁹/L) was 1.9%, and the incidence of milder neutropenia (ANC < 1.5 x 10⁹/L) was 6.4% [Australian].
*   **Monitoring:** Absolute Neutrophil Count (ANC) must be monitored **weekly**.
*   **Management:** If an infection develops, therapy should be interrupted and ANC monitored more frequently. Patients must report symptoms like fever or sore throat immediately.
*   **Rechallenge:** Rechallenge is contraindicated following agranulocytosis and not recommended after neutropenia [Australian].

### Neurological Disorders
*   Neurological events (e.g., cerebellar symptoms, diplopia, psychomotor slowdown) have been reported, primarily in children receiving >2.5 times the maximum recommended dose. However, these have also occurred at standard doses in patients with low iron loads [Australian].

### QT Prolongation
*   Caution is advised in patients at risk for QT interval prolongation, including those with congestive heart failure, bradycardia, electrolyte imbalances (hypokalaemia/hypomagnesaemia), or those taking diuretics [Australian].

### Hepatic and Renal Precautions
*   **Hepatotoxicity:** Monitor ALT levels; persistent increases may require therapy interruption. Special care is required for patients with hepatitis C [Australian].
*   **Renal/Hepatic Insufficiency:** Caution is required as data are limited and the drug/metabolites are primarily excreted renally [Australian].

### Other Precautions
*   **Urine Discoloration:** Reddish/brown urine is common due to the excretion of the iron-deferiprone complex [Australian].
*   **Zinc Deficiency:** Plasma Zn²⁺ monitoring and supplementation are recommended if a deficiency occurs [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Nausea, vomiting, arthralgia, chromaturia (red/brown urine) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Neutropenia, agranulocytosis, abdominal pain/discomfort, diarrhoea, dyspepsia, headache, increased ALT/AST, increased appetite, peripheral oedema |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Arrhythmia, Torsade de Pointes, deafness, tinnitus, hypotension, hepatitis, jaundice, rash, alopecia, glucose metabolism disorders |

*   **Gastrointestinal Effects:** More frequent at the start of therapy; usually resolve within weeks.
*   **Arthropathy:** Ranges from mild joint pain to severe arthritis with effusion [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Neutropenia-inducing Agents:** Deferiprone must not be co-administered with drugs known to cause neutropenia or agranulocytosis [Australian].
*   **Trivalent Cations:** As deferiprone binds metallic cations, avoid concomitant ingestion of aluminium-based antacids [Australian].
*   **Vitamin C:** Safety has not been formally studied. Caution is advised based on known interactions between Vitamin C and desferrioxamine [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Teratogenic and embryotoxic in animal studies. Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception. Deferiprone is contraindicated during pregnancy.
*   **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. It is unknown if deferiprone is excreted in human milk [Australian].
*   **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no effect on early embryonic development at clinical doses, though testicular atrophy occurred at high doses (400 mg/kg/day) in dogs [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Evaluated in children aged 1–15 years. Effective in reducing iron load; however, standard doses have been associated with neurological reports in post-marketing settings [Australian].
*   **Elderly Use:** No specific data available [Australian].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Limited data; caution and monitoring of hepatic function are advised [Australian].
*   **Renal Impairment:** No data available; caution is advised due to renal elimination of the drug and its metabolites [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** A synthetic bidentate chelator that forms a 3:1 complex with iron (III). It mobilises iron from cardiomyocytes, hepatocytes, and reticuloendothelial cells [Australian].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed from the upper GI tract. Peak serum concentration in 45–60 minutes (fasted) or up to 2 hours (fed).
    *   **Distribution:** Low protein binding (<10%). Volume of distribution ~1.73 L/kg.
    *   **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised in the liver to an inactive glucuronide conjugate.
    *   **Elimination:** 75–90% recovered in urine within 24 hours (mostly as glucuronide). Half-life is 2–3 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Study LA-01:** A 2-year randomised study comparing deferiprone (75 mg/kg/day) to desferrioxamine (50 mg/kg). No significant difference was found in the reduction of serum ferritin or hepatic iron concentration between the two groups [Australian].
*   **Study LA-16:** A 12-month study in patients previously treated with desferrioxamine for >5 years. Switching to deferiprone (mean dose 92 mg/kg/day) maintained stable iron stores comparable to continuing desferrioxamine [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **500 mg Tablets:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
*   **1,000 mg Tablets:** Store below 30°C. Keep bottle tightly closed to protect from moisture [Australian].
*   **Oral Solution:** Store below 30°C and protect from light. Once opened, store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate) and use within 35 days [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (S4)** – Prescription Only Medicine [Australian].
