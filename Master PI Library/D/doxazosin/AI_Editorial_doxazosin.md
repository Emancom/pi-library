# Editorial Product Information: Doxazosin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- At the time of writing, official PI documents for other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, UK, Canadian, Japanese) were not provided for comparison.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and must not be used as a substitute for the official, current Product Information in the specific jurisdiction where the clinician is practicing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Doxazosin (as mesylate).
- **Chemical Class:** Quinazoline compound; selective alpha$_1$-adrenergic antagonist.
- **Brand Names:** **CARDURA** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Oral Tablets:** 1 mg, 2 mg, 4 mg, and 8 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Viatris Specialty LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (BPH):** Treatment of the signs and symptoms of BPH [US-approved].
- **Hypertension:** Treatment of hypertension to lower blood pressure. Lowering blood pressure reduces the risk of fatal and nonfatal cardiovascular events, primarily strokes and myocardial infarctions [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
Following the initial dose and any subsequent dose increase, patients should be monitored for blood pressure for at least 6 hours due to the risk of postural hypotension. If therapy is interrupted for several days, it should be restarted at the initial 1 mg dose.

### Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia
- **Initial Dose:** 1 mg once daily (morning or evening).
- **Titration:** Dose may be doubled at 1 to 2-week intervals to 2 mg, 4 mg, and up to a maximum of 8 mg once daily based on patient urodynamics and symptoms.
- **Monitoring:** Routine blood pressure monitoring is required.

### Hypertension
- **Initial Dose:** 1 mg once daily.
- **Titration:** Daily dosage may be doubled up to a maximum of 16 mg once daily, as required to achieve target blood pressure reduction.

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets may be taken with or without food.
- Food causes a non-clinically significant reduction in C$_{max}$ (18%) and AUC (12%).
- Evening dosing results in an 11% higher AUC and a delayed T$_{max}$ compared to morning dosing.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to doxazosin, other quinazolines (e.g., prazosin, terazosin), or any component of the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Postural Hypotension/Syncope:** Most common at treatment initiation or during dose escalation. Infrequently, syncope may occur (usually 30–90 minutes post-dose). Patients should avoid driving or hazardous tasks for 24 hours after the first dose or dose increases.
- **Intraoperative Floppy Iris Syndrome (IFIS):** Observed during cataract surgery in some patients currently or previously treated with alpha$_1$ blockers. The patient's surgeon should be prepared to modify surgical techniques.
- **Prostate Cancer Screening:** Carcinoma of the prostate must be ruled out before initiating doxazosin for BPH, as symptoms overlap.
- **Priapism:** Alpha$_1$ antagonists are associated with priapism (persistent, painful erections). If not treated promptly, this can lead to permanent impotence.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Doxazosin is extensively metabolised by the liver. Use is not recommended in patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class C).

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally dose-related. In clinical trials, the discontinuation rate due to adverse events was low (2%).

**Other Significant Reactions:**
- **Common:** Hypotension, palpitation.
- **Laboratory:** Decreases in white blood cell and neutrophil counts (leukopenia/neutropenia) have been observed in clinical trials, though typically asymptomatic.
- **Post-marketing Experience:** Thrombocytopenia, allergic reactions, bradycardia, hepatitis, cholestasis, vomiting, muscle cramps, gynaecomastia, and hematuria.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Strong CYP3A Inhibitors:** Doxazosin is a substrate of CYP3A4. Strong inhibitors (e.g., clarithromycin, itraconazole) may increase doxazosin exposure. Monitor for symptoms of hypotension.
- **PDE-5 Inhibitors:** Concomitant use with phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors (e.g., sildenafil, tadalafil) can result in additive blood pressure lowering and symptomatic hypotension.
- **Cimetidine:** Administration with cimetidine results in a minor (10%) increase in doxazosin AUC.
- **Protein Binding:** In vitro data indicate no effect on the protein binding of digoxin, warfarin, phenytoin, or indomethacin.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned under current labeling]. Limited human data are insufficient to inform drug-associated risks. Animal studies showed reduced fetal survival in rabbits at doses 10 times the human exposure. Untreated hypertension in pregnancy carries significant maternal and fetal risks.
- **Breastfeeding:** A single case study reported doxazosin in human milk at a level resulting in an infant dose <1% of the maternal weight-adjusted dose. Caution is advised as data are insufficient to confirm safety.
- **Fertility:** Studies in rats showed reduced fertility in males at doses roughly 4 times the human AUC; this effect was reversible within two weeks of cessation.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established in children [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** 
    - **BPH:** Safety and efficacy profiles are similar to younger patients.
    - **Hypertension:** No overall differences in response, but cautious dosing is recommended, starting at the low end of the range.
- **Renal Impairment:** Pharmacokinetics are not significantly altered; no specific dose adjustment is required [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild (Child-Pugh A):** Exposure increases by approximately 40%.
    - **Moderate (Child-Pugh B):** Impact unknown; use with caution.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Not recommended.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **BPH:** Selectively blocks alpha$_1$-adrenoceptors in the prostatic stroma, capsule, and bladder neck, reducing smooth muscle tone and improving urinary flow.
    - **Hypertension:** Selectively blocks postjunctional alpha$_1$-adrenoceptors, leading to a decrease in systemic vascular resistance.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Bioavailability ~65%. Peak plasma levels at 2–3 hours.
    - **Distribution:** ~98% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised in the liver via O-demethylation and hydroxylation (primarily CYP3A4).
    - **Elimination:** Biphasic elimination with a terminal half-life of ~22 hours. Excretion is primarily faecal (~63%) with minimal renal excretion (9%).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **BPH:** In placebo-controlled trials of over 900 patients, doxazosin (up to 8 mg) significantly improved maximum urinary flow rates (increase of 2.3–3.3 mL/sec vs 0.1–0.7 mL/sec for placebo) and reduced symptom scores. Efficacy was noted as early as one week.
- **Hypertension:** Doxazosin (1–16 mg) lowered blood pressure at 24 hours by approximately 10/8 mmHg (standing) and 9/5 mmHg (supine) compared to placebo.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F).
- **Excursions:** Permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
