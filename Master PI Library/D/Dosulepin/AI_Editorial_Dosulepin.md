# Editorial Product Information: Dosulepin

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (TGA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of dosulepin (dothiepin) hydrochloride, synthesised from official Product Information (PI) for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this writing, only Australian (TGA) documentation was available for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Dosulepin (dothiepin) hydrochloride.
- **Chemical Class:** Tricyclic antidepressant (TCA); thioanalogue of amitriptyline.
- **ATC Code:** N06AA16.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - 25 mg capsules [Australian].
    - 75 mg tablets [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Viatris (Alphapharm Pty Ltd) [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Major Depression:** Treatment of major depressive disorder in adults [Australian].
- **Maintenance Treatment:** The 75 mg tablet strength is specifically indicated only for the maintenance treatment of major depression [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** Therapy should typically begin at 25 mg three times daily for one to two weeks. If the clinical response is insufficient, the daily dose may be increased by 25 mg to 50 mg at intervals of one to two weeks [Australian].
- **Maximum Dose:** The total daily dose should not exceed 200 mg [Australian].
- **Night-time Dosing:** Once an effective dose is established, up to 150 mg of the total daily dose may be administered as a single dose at bedtime to manage potential drowsiness [Australian].
- **Maintenance:** Dosage should be titrated down to the lowest amount required to maintain symptomatic relief once a satisfactory response is achieved [Australian].
- **Titration Observations:** Plasma steady state is typically reached 10 to 14 days following any dosage adjustment [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to dosulepin hydrochloride [Australian].
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Concomitant use or use within 14 days of discontinuing MAOIs is contraindicated due to the risk of cerebral excitation, coma, and severe hyperthermia [Australian].
- **Myocardial Infarction:** Use during the acute recovery phase following a myocardial infarction [Australian].
- **Cardiac Disorders:** TCAs may produce arrhythmias and conduction defects [Australian].
- **Hepatic Failure:** Contraindicated in patients with severe hepatic impairment [Australian].
- **Epilepsy:** Due to the drug's potential to lower the seizure threshold [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Toxicity in Overdose:** Dosulepin is associated with high mortality in overdose. It has a narrow therapeutic index; the margin between a maximum therapeutic dose and a fatal dose is small. Toxicity onset typically occurs within 4–6 hours [Australian].
- **Suicide Risk:** The risk of suicide is inherent in depression. Patients—particularly children, adolescents, and young adults (18–24 years)—should be closely monitored for clinical worsening or emergent suicidality, especially at the start of therapy or during dose changes [Australian].
- **Prescription Limitations:** To mitigate overdose risk, the smallest quantity of medication consistent with good patient management should be prescribed. For patients at high risk of suicide, a maximum of two weeks' supply (at 75 mg/day) should be considered initially [Australian].
- **Bipolar Disorder:** Patients should be screened for bipolar risk before initiation, as antidepressants may precipitate mixed/manic episodes [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** Caution is required in the elderly and those with suspected cardiovascular disease due to risks of arrhythmias, heart failure, and circulatory collapse [Australian].
- **Anticholinergic Risks:** Dosulepin may aggravate glaucoma, prostatic hypertrophy, and urinary retention [Australian].
- **Ophthalmological Monitoring:** Regular examinations of visual acuity and colour fields are recommended during prolonged therapy as the drug may accumulate in the pigmented area of the eye [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects occur in approximately 30% of patients, with 10% requiring discontinuation [Australian].

| Frequency | System/Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (>10%)** | Drowsiness, dry mouth, sweating, constipation, blurred vision, tachycardia, hypotension/postural hypotension. |
| **Less Common/Rare** | Extrapyramidal symptoms, tremor, confusional states, disorientation, paraesthesia, urinary retention, nausea, vomiting, increased/decreased libido. |
| **Serious Effects** | Bone marrow depression (agranulocytosis, thrombocytopenia), cholestatic jaundice, arrhythmias, conduction defects, seizures, paralytic ileus, myocardial infarction, stroke. |

**Post-marketing Reports:** Hyponatremia, movement disorders, increased intraocular pressure, and blood sugar fluctuations have been reported [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **Alcohol:** Potentiates sedative effects; a fatality has been reported with this combination [Australian].
    - **CNS Depressants/Barbiturates:** Potentiation of sedation [Australian].
    - **Sympathomimetics:** Potentially dangerous potentiation of effects (e.g., in OTC cold remedies) [Australian].
    - **Anticholinergics/Antihistamines:** Effects may be potentiated [Australian].
    - **Antiepileptics:** Dosulepin may antagonize anticonvulsant effects by lowering the seizure threshold [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **Diuretics:** Increased risk of postural hypotension [Australian].
    - **Guanethidine:** Dosulepin may block the antihypertensive effect of adrenergic neurone blocking drugs [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Not associated with an increased incidence of birth defects, but prolonged maternal use has resulted in neonatal withdrawal symptoms. Use only if there are sound clinical indications [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Small amounts are excreted in breast milk. The impact on the infant must be carefully considered [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Data not available in provided sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children and adolescents under 18 years have not been established. It should not be used in this age group for depression [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Use with care; start at the lower end of the dosing range. Increased risk of confusional states and cardiovascular toxicity [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution and reduced dosage to prevent the development of toxic blood levels [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in hepatic failure. Use with caution/reduced dosage in milder impairment as the drug is extensively metabolised by the liver [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits the reuptake of noradrenaline and 5-hydroxytryptamine (serotonin) at central synapses, increasing their availability. It also possesses anticholinergic, antihistamine, and central sedative properties [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Well absorbed from the small intestine. Tmax is 2–3 hours. Significant interindividual variation in plasma levels exists [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Crosses the blood-brain and placental barriers. Protein binding is approximately 84% [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Extensive first-pass metabolism in the liver. Primary active metabolite is desmethyldothiepin (northiaden) [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Biphasic elimination. Mean whole-body elimination half-life is approximately 51 hours. Excreted via urine (56%) and faeces [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **25 mg Capsules:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **75 mg Tablets:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
