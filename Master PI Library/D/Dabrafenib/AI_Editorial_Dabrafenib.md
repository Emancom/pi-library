# Editorial Product Information: Dabrafenib

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **dabrafenib** for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data to facilitate safe and effective prescribing.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Dabrafenib (as mesilate).
*   **Chemical Class:** B-Raf serine-threonine kinase (BRAF) inhibitor.
*   **Brand Name:** **Tafinlar** [Australian].
*   **ATC Code:** L01EC02.
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths [Australian]:**
    *   Hard capsules: 50 mg and 75 mg.
    *   Dispersible tablets: 10 mg.
*   **Route of Administration:** Oral.
*   **Note on Interchangeability:** **Dabrafenib** capsules and dispersible tablets are not bioequivalent and are not interchangeable; frequent switching between formulations is discouraged [Australian].
*   **Sponsor/Manufacturer:** Novartis Pharmaceuticals [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
[Australian-approved indications]
*   **Unresectable or Metastatic Melanoma:** 
    *   As monotherapy or in combination with **trametinib** for the treatment of patients with BRAF V600 mutation-positive unresectable Stage III or metastatic (Stage IV) melanoma.
*   **Adjuvant Treatment of Melanoma:** 
    *   In combination with **trametinib** for patients with BRAF V600 mutation and lymph node involvement following complete resection.
*   **Anaplastic Thyroid Cancer (ATC):** 
    *   In combination with **trametinib** for locally advanced or metastatic BRAF V600 mutation-positive ATC where no satisfactory locoregional treatment options exist.
*   **Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC):** 
    *   In combination with **trametinib** for advanced BRAF V600 mutation-positive NSCLC.
*   **Low-Grade Glioma (LGG):** 
    *   In combination with **trametinib** for paediatric patients (aged 1 year and older) with BRAF V600E mutation-positive LGG requiring systemic therapy.
*   **High-Grade Glioma (HGG):** 
    *   In combination with **trametinib** for paediatric patients (aged 1 year and older) with BRAF V600E mutation-positive HGG who have progressed following prior treatment.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
*   **Recommended Dose:** 150 mg (two 75 mg capsules) taken twice daily.
*   **Total Daily Dose:** 300 mg.

### Paediatric Dosing (Glioma)
Dosing is weight-based. Examples for dispersible tablets [Australian]:
*   8 to 9 kg: 20 mg twice daily.
*   10 to 13 kg: 30 mg twice daily.
*   $\ge$51 kg: 150 mg twice daily.
*   *Note:* For paediatric patients $\ge$26 kg, capsules may be used with specific weight-tiered dosing.

### Administration Instructions
*   **Timing:** Take at least one hour before or two hours after a meal.
*   **Interval:** Doses should be taken approximately 12 hours apart at consistent times each day.
*   **Handling:** Capsules must be swallowed whole with water; do not open, crush, or break. Dispersible tablets must be prepared as a suspension in water (approx. 5–10 mL) and administered within 30 minutes.

### Missed Doses
*   If a dose is missed, it should only be taken if there are more than 6 hours remaining before the next scheduled dose. 
*   If a patient vomits after administration, they should not retake the dose but wait for the next scheduled dose.

### Duration of Treatment
*   **Metastatic disease/ATC/NSCLC:** Until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.
*   **Adjuvant Melanoma:** Maximum of 1 year.

## 4. Contraindications
*   Hypersensitivity to the active substance or any excipients (including microcrystalline cellulose, magnesium stearate, and colloidal anhydrous silica) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **BRAF V600 Mutation Testing:** Status must be confirmed by a validated test before initiating therapy. Efficacy/safety is not established in BRAF wild-type tumours.
*   **Pyrexia and Febrile Events:** Very common, especially in combination with **trametinib**. Serious non-infectious febrile events (rigors, dehydration, hypotension, renal failure) may occur.
    *   *Management:* Interrupt treatment if temperature $\ge$38.0°C. Use antipyretics (e.g., ibuprofen, paracetamol). Consider oral corticosteroids for persistent/severe events.
*   **New Malignancies:**
    *   **Cutaneous Squamous Cell Carcinoma (cuSCC):** Includes keratoacanthoma. Perform skin exams at baseline, monthly during treatment, and for 6 months post-discontinuation.
    *   **New Primary Melanoma:** Monitor as per cuSCC; manage with excision.
    *   **Non-cutaneous Malignancies:** Paradoxical MAP-kinase signalling may trigger RAS-mutation-positive malignancies (e.g., pancreatic cancer, colorectal cancer). Perform head/neck and imaging exams regularly. Permanently discontinue if RAS-positive non-cutaneous malignancies develop.
*   **Cardiac Effects:**
    *   **LVEF Reduction:** Monitor via echocardiogram/MUGA scan (baseline, 1 month, then every 2–3 months). 
    *   **QT Prolongation:** Generally not clinically significant at standard doses, but observe caution in patients with electrolyte imbalances or on pro-arrhythmic medications.
*   **Ocular Toxicities:** Uveitis, iritis, and iridocyclitis have been reported. Monitor for visual changes (e.g., blurred vision, eye pain).
*   **G6PD Deficiency:** Potential risk of haemolytic anaemia due to the sulphonamide moiety in **dabrafenib**.
*   **Renal/Hepatic Effects:** Monitor serum creatinine and liver function (every 4 weeks for the first 6 months).

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Summary (Monotherapy in Melanoma)
| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Hyperkeratosis, headache, pyrexia, arthralgia, fatigue, nausea, skin papilloma, alopecia, rash, vomiting, cough, diarrhoea, decreased appetite. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Constipation, nasopharyngitis, hypophosphataemia, hyperglycaemia, skin tags, seborrhoeic keratosis, actinic keratosis, palmar-plantar erythrodysaesthesia (PPE), LVEF decrease. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | New primary melanoma, uveitis, pancreatitis, renal failure, tubulointerstitial nephritis, panniculitis, QT prolongation. |

### Serious Adverse Effects
*   **Haemorrhage:** Major haemorrhagic events, including fatal intracranial haemorrhages, have occurred (primarily in combination with **trametinib**).
*   **Venous Thromboembolism (VTE):** Deep vein thrombosis (DVT) and pulmonary embolism (PE).
*   **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Including Stevens-Johnson syndrome and DRESS.
*   **Tumour Lysis Syndrome (TLS):** Reported in patients with high tumour burden.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    *   **CYP3A4 and CYP2C8:** **Dabrafenib** is a substrate for these enzymes. Strong inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole) or inducers (e.g., rifampicin) will significantly alter **dabrafenib** concentrations and should be avoided or used with extreme caution.
    *   **Enzyme Induction:** **Dabrafenib** is a moderate-to-strong inducer of CYP3A4 and CYP2C9. It may reduce the efficacy of hormonal contraceptives, warfarin (monitor INR), and midazolam.
    *   **Transporters:** **Dabrafenib** is an in vitro inhibitor of OATP1B1 and OATP1B3; caution is advised with statin co-administration.
*   **Gastric pH:** Solubility is pH-dependent. Drugs that increase gastric pH (PPIs, H2-antagonists, antacids) may reduce bioavailability.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   **Category D [Australian]:** May cause foetal harm. Animal studies showed embryofetal toxicities (ventricular septal defects, reduced body weight).
    *   **Contraception:** Females of reproductive potential must use effective non-hormonal contraception during treatment and for at least 2 weeks (monotherapy) or 16 weeks (combination with **trametinib**) after the last dose.
*   **Breastfeeding:** No data available. Discontinue breastfeeding during treatment due to potential risks to the infant.
*   **Fertility:** Animal data suggests potential for impaired spermatogenesis (possibly irreversible) and reduced corpora lutea.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Approved for LGG and HGG in patients $\ge$1 year. Safety/efficacy in patients <1 year not established.
*   **Elderly Use:** No initial dose adjustment required in patients $\ge$65 years. However, older patients may experience more frequent dose interruptions or reductions due to adverse events.
*   **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild-to-moderate impairment. No data for severe impairment; use with caution.
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild impairment. No data for moderate-to-severe impairment; use with caution.

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** ATP-competitive inhibitor of RAF kinases. It inhibits cell growth in BRAF V600-mutant melanoma, NSCLC, and ATC. Combination with **trametinib** (a MEK inhibitor) provides dual inhibition of the MAPK pathway and delays resistance.
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption:** Median Tmax is 2 hours. High-fat meals reduce Cmax by 51% and AUC by 31%.
    *   **Distribution:** 99.7% plasma protein bound. Volume of distribution (Vss) is 46 L.
    *   **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP2C8 and CYP3A4 to form hydroxy-dabrafenib, which is further oxidised to carboxy-dabrafenib.
    *   **Elimination:** Primarily faecal (71%); 23% in urine. Terminal half-life is approximately 8 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **BREAK-3 (Melanoma):** Phase III trial showed significant improvement in Progression-Free Survival (PFS) for **dabrafenib** (6.9 months) vs. dacarbazine (2.7 months) in untreated BRAF V600E metastatic melanoma.
*   **COMBI-d/COMBI-v (Melanoma):** Phase III combination trials (with **trametinib**) showed superior Overall Survival (OS) and PFS compared to monotherapy (either **dabrafenib** or vemurafenib).
*   **COMBI-AD (Adjuvant):** Phase III trial showed a 53% reduction in risk of relapse or death in the combination arm vs. placebo over 12 months of treatment.
*   **Study G2201 (Paediatric Glioma):** Demonstrated an Overall Response Rate (ORR) of 46.6% in BRAF V600E LGG compared to 10.8% with chemotherapy.

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Hard Capsules:** Store below 30°C. Protect from light and moisture [Australian].
*   **Dispersible Tablets:** Store below 25°C. Do not remove desiccant [Australian].
