# Editorial Product Information: Dapagliflozin

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised clinical overview of **dapagliflozin** for use by healthcare professionals. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis of available regulatory documentation and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this publication, only Australian regulatory data was provided for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Dapagliflozin (as dapagliflozin propanediol monohydrate).
- **Chemical Class**: Sodium-glucose co-transporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitor.
- **Brand Name(s)**: **FORXIGA** [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: A10BK01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s)**: 10 mg film-coated tablets (yellow, biconvex, diamond-shaped) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: AstraZeneca Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Dapagliflozin** is indicated in adults for:
- **Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM)**: 
    - Monotherapy: As an adjunct to diet and exercise when metformin is not tolerated.
    - Combination Therapy: As an adjunct to diet and exercise with other anti-hyperglycaemic agents (including metformin, sulfonylureas, DPP4 inhibitors, GLP-1 receptor agonists, and insulin).
    - Initial Combination: With metformin when glycaemic control is inadequate and prospects for metformin monotherapy response are poor.
- **Prevention of Heart Failure Hospitalisation**: To reduce the risk of hospitalisation for heart failure in adults with T2DM and established cardiovascular disease or multiple cardiovascular risk factors.
- **Heart Failure**: Treatment of symptomatic heart failure regardless of left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), as an adjunct to standard of care.
- **Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)**: To reduce the risk of progressive decline in kidney function in adults with proteinuric CKD (Stages 2, 3, or 4).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing**: The recommended dose for all indications is 10 mg taken orally once daily.
- **Administration**: Tablets may be taken at any time of day, with or without food.
- **Combination Therapy**: When used with insulin or insulin secretagogues (e.g., sulfonylureas), a lower dose of those agents may be required to mitigate the risk of hypoglycaemia.
- **Initial Combination (T2DM)**: 10 mg **dapagliflozin** plus 500 mg metformin once daily. Metformin may be up-titrated based on clinical response [Australian].
- **Missed Dose**: If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as remembered; however, two doses should not be taken on the same day.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **dapagliflozin** or any excipients (e.g., lactose) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA)**: Serious, life-threatening, and fatal cases have occurred. Patients with T2DM may present with "euglycaemic" DKA (blood glucose <14 mmol/L). Treatment must be suspended immediately if DKA is suspected. **Dapagliflozin** is not indicated for Type 1 Diabetes.
- **Necrotising Fasciitis of the Perineum (Fournier’s Gangrene)**: A rare but life-threatening infection. Patients presenting with pain, tenderness, or swelling in the genital/perineal area with fever/malaise should be evaluated urgently.
- **Volume Depletion and Hypotension**: Osmotic diuresis may cause volume depletion. Caution is advised in patients at risk (e.g., elderly, patients on diuretics, or those with a history of hypotension). Therapy should be interrupted during intercurrent conditions leading to volume depletion (e.g., gastrointestinal illness).
- **Urinary Tract Infections (UTIs)**: Serious UTIs, including urosepsis and pyelonephritis, have been reported. Temporary interruption should be considered during treatment of serious UTIs.
- **Surgery**: Treatment should be ceased prior to major surgery or procedures involving prolonged fasting to reduce DKA risk.
- **Lower Limb Amputations**: While not specifically observed as a statistically significant risk for **dapagliflozin** in provided data, foot care and regular examination are recommended for all diabetic patients as a class precaution.
- **Laboratory Interference**: Monitoring glycaemic control with 1,5-anhydroglucitol (1,5-AG) assays is unreliable in patients taking SGLT2 inhibitors.

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (>10%) | Hypoglycaemia (when used with SU or insulin) |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Genital infections (vulvovaginitis, balanitis), Urinary tract infection, Back pain, Polyuria, Increased haematocrit, Dyslipidaemia |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Volume depletion, Thirst, Constipation, Hyperhidrosis, Creatinine increase (initial) |
| **Rare** (<0.1%) | Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) |
| **Very Rare** (<0.01%) | Tubulointerstitial nephritis, Fournier's gangrene |

- **Gender Differences**: Genital infections are more frequent in females (8.4%) than in males (3.4%) [Australian].
- **Renal Function**: Initial therapy is associated with a transient increase in serum creatinine and a decrease in eGFR due to haemodynamic changes.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Lithium**: **Dapagliflozin** may increase urinary lithium excretion, leading to decreased serum lithium levels. Monitoring of serum lithium is required.
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions**: 
    - Primarily metabolised by UGT1A9.
    - **Rifampicin** (inducer) may decrease **dapagliflozin** exposure by ~22%.
    - **Mefenamic acid** (inhibitor) may increase exposure by ~51%. 
    - These changes are not considered clinically significant; no dose adjustment is recommended [Australian].
- **Diuretics**: May enhance the diuretic and dehydration effects of **dapagliflozin**.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: **Category D** [Australian]. There are no data in pregnant women. Animal studies showed renal toxicity in the second and third trimesters. **Dapagliflozin** must not be used during the second and third trimesters; treatment should be discontinued if pregnancy is detected.
- **Breastfeeding**: Not recommended. Animal data show excretion in milk and potential risk to human renal maturation during the first two years of life.
- **Fertility**: No effects on mating or fertility were seen in animal models at 1000 times the human clinical dose [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and efficacy in patients <18 years have not been established.
- **Elderly Use**: No dose adjustment is required based on age. However, older patients are more likely to have renal impairment and volume depletion.
- **Renal Impairment**: 
    - Initiation is not recommended in patients with eGFR <25 mL/min/1.73 m².
    - Glucose-lowering efficacy is reduced when eGFR is <45 mL/min/1.73 m².
    - No dose adjustment is required based on renal function for the prevention/treatment of Heart Failure or CKD progression [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: No dose adjustment for mild or moderate impairment. Use is **not recommended** in patients with severe hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Reversible, competitive inhibitor of SGLT2. By inhibiting glucose reabsorption in the proximal renal tubule, it induces glycosuria and osmotic diuresis. This leads to reduced plasma glucose, weight loss, and reduced blood pressure. Cardio-renal benefits are also attributed to reduced intraglomerular pressure and volume overload.
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Rapidly absorbed; 78% bioavailability. Cmax reached within 2 hours in fasted state.
    - **Distribution**: ~91% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism**: Primarily via UGT1A9 to an inactive 3-O-glucuronide metabolite.
    - **Excretion**: Terminal half-life ~12.9 hours. 75% excreted in urine (mostly as metabolites) and 21% in faeces.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **DECLARE-TIMI 58**: Demonstrated a significant reduction in the composite of HF hospitalisation or CV death in patients with T2DM.
- **DAPA-HF**: Showed a 26% reduction in the risk of the primary composite endpoint (CV death or worsening HF) in patients with HFrEF (LVEF ≤40%), regardless of diabetes status.
- **DELIVER**: Demonstrated benefits in patients with LVEF >40% (HFpEF/HFmrEF).
- **DAPA-CKD**: Showed a 39% risk reduction in the composite of ≥50% eGFR decline, ESKD, or renal/CV death in patients with CKD.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage**: Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Packaging**: Aluminium/aluminium blisters in pack sizes of 7 or 28 [Australian].
