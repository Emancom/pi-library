# Editorial Product Information: dorzolamide + timolol

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **dorzolamide + timolol**
- Chemical class:
  - Dorzolamide: topical carbonic anhydrase inhibitor (sulfonamide derivative)
  - Timolol: non‑selective topical beta‑adrenergic blocking agent (beta‑blocker)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Ophthalmic (topical eye drops)
- Do not include brand or trade names in this document.

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of elevated intraocular pressure (IOP) in patients with ocular hypertension or open‑angle glaucoma when concomitant therapy is appropriate.
- Use in other indications: Not specified in available sources

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- One drop in the affected eye(s) twice daily.

### Dosing by indication
- No additional indication‑specific dosing regimens provided beyond the general dosing above.

### Administration instructions
- For individual patient use only.
- When substituting from another topical ophthalmic antiglaucoma agent(s): discontinue the other agent after proper dosing on one day and initiate dorzolamide + timolol the following day.
- If another topical ophthalmic agent is used concomitantly, administer dorzolamide + timolol and the other agent at least ten minutes apart.
- Contact lenses: remove soft contact lenses prior to administration; do not reinsert earlier than 15 minutes after use (product contains benzalkonium chloride which may be deposited in lenses and cause irritation).

### Titration schedules
- Not specified in available sources

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources

## 5. Contraindications
All contraindications below are from available source material and should be adhered to.

- Hypersensitivity to dorzolamide, timolol, or any component of the product.
- Reactive airway disease, bronchial asthma, history of bronchial asthma, or severe chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD).
- Sinus bradycardia, sick sinus syndrome, sino‑atrial block, second‑ or third‑degree atrioventricular block (unless a pacemaker is present).
- Overt cardiac failure and cardiogenic shock.
- Severe renal impairment (creatinine clearance <30 mL/min) or hyperchloraemic acidosis — **dorzolamide is contraindicated** in such patients because dorzolamide and its metabolite are predominantly renally excreted.
- Any other contraindications specific to components noted above should be applied to the combination product.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed / Highlighted warnings
- **Systemic absorption of the timolol component can produce the same systemic adverse reactions as systemic beta‑blockers; use caution in patients with cardiac or respiratory disease.**
- **Dorzolamide component is a sulfonamide; serious hypersensitivity reactions including Stevens‑Johnson syndrome and toxic epidermal necrolysis have been reported. Discontinue immediately if signs of severe hypersensitivity occur.**

### General warnings and precautions
- Cardio‑respiratory:
  - Timolol may be systemically absorbed and produce beta‑blocker related effects (bradycardia, heart block, worsening heart failure, bronchospasm). Monitor patients with cardiac disease; ensure heart failure is adequately controlled prior to initiation.
  - Use with caution in patients with first‑degree heart block.
  - Use with caution in patients with cerebrovascular insufficiency; monitor for signs of reduced cerebral blood flow.
  - Respiratory reactions including bronchospasm and respiratory failure have been reported; discontinue at first sign of bronchospasm.
- Vascular/peripheral circulation:
  - Use with caution in patients with severe peripheral circulatory disorders (e.g., severe Raynaud’s).
- Diabetes and endocrine:
  - Beta‑blockers can mask the signs and symptoms of hypoglycaemia. Use with caution in patients susceptible to hypoglycaemia or those on insulin/oral hypoglycaemic agents.
  - Beta‑blockers may mask clinical signs of thyrotoxicosis; avoid abrupt withdrawal which may precipitate thyroid storm.
- Surgery/anesthesia:
  - Inform anaesthesiologists that the patient is receiving timolol; beta‑blockade may block systemic beta‑agonist effects (e.g., adrenaline) and may complicate management during anesthesia.
- Myasthenia gravis:
  - Beta‑blockers may worsen symptoms of myasthenia gravis or increase muscle weakness.
- Immunology and hypersensitivity:
  - Dorzolamide (a sulfonamide) may produce systemic sulfonamide‑type reactions, including severe cutaneous reactions. Local ocular allergic‑type reactions (conjunctivitis, lid reactions) have been reported; consider discontinuation if observed.
  - Patients with history of atopy or severe anaphylactic reactions may be more reactive to allergens while on beta‑blockers and may have reduced responsiveness to epinephrine for treatment of anaphylaxis.
- Concomitant therapy:
  - The concomitant use of dorzolamide + timolol with oral carbonic anhydrase inhibitors is not recommended due to potential additive systemic carbonic anhydrase inhibition.
  - Use caution in patients receiving systemic beta‑blockers — monitor for additive effects on heart rate and blood pressure.
  - Use of two topical beta‑adrenergic blocking agents is not recommended.
- Ophthalmic:
  - May cause or aggravate dryness of eyes; use caution in patients with corneal disease.
  - Corneal oedema and irreversible corneal decompensation have been reported in patients with pre‑existing chronic corneal defects or prior intraocular surgery — use with caution in such patients and in those with low endothelial cell counts.
  - Diminished responsiveness to ophthalmic timolol after prolonged therapy has been reported.
  - Risk of bacterial keratitis exists with improper handling or contaminated multi‑dose containers; instruct patients on proper hygiene and avoidance of tip contamination.
- Excipient:
  - Product contains benzalkonium chloride which may cause eye irritation, destabilize tear film and deposit in soft contact lenses; use caution in patients with dry eye or compromised cornea.
- Renal impairment:
  - Dorzolamide is contraindicated in severe renal impairment (CrCl <30 mL/min). Dorzolamide has not been studied in severe renal impairment or in patients with hyperchloraemic acidosis.
- Hepatic impairment:
  - Dorzolamide + timolol has not been studied in hepatic impairment; use with caution.
- Choroidal detachment:
  - Choroidal detachment associated with ocular hypotony has been reported after filtration surgery when aqueous suppressants (including dorzolamide and timolol) were used.
- Pregnancy/lactation (see Section 9): consider fetal/newborn effects (timolol) and animal data (dorzolamide).

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and periodic assessment of pulse and signs/symptoms of cardiac failure, bronchospasm or respiratory compromise.
- Monitor for ocular surface disease or signs of corneal compromise.
- Monitor for signs of systemic sulfonamide hypersensitivity.
- Monitor pediatric patients closely for signs of metabolic acidosis (especially if renal tubular immaturity suspected).

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Additive hypotension/bradycardia: concomitant use with systemic beta‑adrenergic blocking agents, calcium channel blockers, catecholamine‑depleting drugs, antiarrhythmics (including amiodarone), digitalis glycosides, parasympathomimetics, guanethidine, narcotics, and monoamine oxidase inhibitors may increase risk of hypotension, bradycardia, or conduction disturbances.
- Concomitant topical/systemic carbonic anhydrase inhibitors: potential for additive systemic carbonic anhydrase inhibition effects; concomitant use of dorzolamide + timolol with oral carbonic anhydrase inhibitors is not recommended.
- Clonidine: beta‑adrenergic blocking agents may exacerbate rebound hypertension following clonidine withdrawal; exercise caution.
- Adrenaline (epinephrine): concomitant use of topical timolol with adrenaline may rarely produce mydriasis (reportedly).
- Antidiabetic agents: beta‑blockers may increase the hypoglycaemic effect of antidiabetic agents and mask hypoglycaemia symptoms.

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- CYP2D6 inhibitors (e.g., quinidine, some SSRIs): potentiated systemic beta‑blockade (e.g., decreased heart rate, depression) has been reported when CYP2D6 inhibitors are combined with timolol.
- Specific pharmacokinetic interaction studies with the combination product were not provided.

### Clinically significant interactions — management notes
- Observe patients receiving systemic beta‑blockers who are started on dorzolamide + timolol for additive effects on heart rate and blood pressure.
- Avoid concurrent use of dorzolamide + timolol with oral carbonic anhydrase inhibitors.
- Consider monitoring blood glucose closely in diabetic patients starting or stopping beta‑blocker therapy.
- When patients are on CYP2D6 inhibitors plus topical timolol, monitor for signs of increased systemic beta‑blockade (bradycardia, hypotension, depression).

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Pregnancy category: **[Australian: Category C]**
- Effects:
  - Beta‑adrenergic blocking agents (timolol) may cause bradycardia in the fetus and newborn infant.
  - Developmental toxicity studies: dorzolamide produced vertebral malformations in rabbits at oral doses ≥2.5 mg/kg/day associated with maternal metabolic acidosis and reduced fetal weight; no malformations at 1.0 mg/kg/day. No treatment‑related fetal malformations were seen with dorzolamide in rats up to 10 mg/kg/day. Timolol developmental studies in animals demonstrated no clear evidence of fetal malformations at tested doses, but some fetal resorptions and maternotoxicity occurred at high doses in some species.
- Recommendation:
  - Use during pregnancy only if potential benefit justifies potential risk to the fetus.

### Breastfeeding
- Dorzolamide: excreted in milk of lactating rats with effects on offspring weight gain at certain doses; human milk excretion unknown.
- Timolol: has been detected in human milk following oral and ophthalmic administration.
- Recommendation:
  - Because of potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants from dorzolamide + timolol, a decision should be made whether to discontinue nursing or discontinue the drug taking into account the importance of the drug to the mother.

### Fertility
- Dorzolamide: reproduction studies in rats demonstrated no adverse effects on reproductive capacity at specified oral doses.
- Timolol: reproduction and fertility studies in rats demonstrated no adverse effect at tested doses in animals.
- Human fertility effects: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Efficacy and safety:
  - Efficacy in paediatric patients generally: Not established in all age groups; safety in children <2 years not established.
  - A clinical study (3 months duration) in children <6 years old: patients ≥2 and <6 years whose IOP was uncontrolled on monotherapy were switched to preserved dorzolamide + timolol; of 30 patients transferred to preserved combination drops, three experienced drug‑related adverse events (cough; burning/stinging eye; ocular injection).
  - Patients with significant renal tubular immaturity should receive dorzolamide only after careful consideration due to possible risk of metabolic acidosis.
  - From limited data, adverse event profile in children was similar to adults, but children’s eyes may react more strongly to stimuli which may affect adherence.
- Approvals by jurisdiction for pediatric use: Not specified in available sources (note: available clinical data refer to ages ≥2 and <6 in a trial; safety below 2 years not established).

### Elderly
- Approximately 49% of clinical study patients were ≥65 years and 13% were ≥75 years. No overall differences in safety or effectiveness observed; greater sensitivity in some older individuals cannot be ruled out. No specific dose adjustments recommended in the available sources.

### Renal impairment
- Dorzolamide is contraindicated in patients with severe renal impairment (CrCl <30 mL/min) and in patients with hyperchloraemic acidosis. Dorzolamide has not been studied in severe renal impairment.

### Hepatic impairment
- Not studied; use with caution in patients with hepatic impairment.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Dorzolamide: topical carbonic anhydrase inhibitor (sulfonamide derivative).
- Timolol: non‑selective beta‑adrenergic blocker.
- Specific pharmacodynamic details of intraocular pressure reduction (e.g., exact mechanism to lower aqueous humour formation) are not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption:
  - Both components are absorbed systemically following topical ophthalmic administration (timolol systemic absorption and dorzolamide systemic absorption are noted).
- Distribution / Excretion:
  - Dorzolamide and its metabolite are excreted predominantly via the kidney; renal impairment increases risk and is a contraindication for dorzolamide.
  - Timolol has been detected in human milk after oral and ophthalmic administration.
- Metabolism: Not specified in available sources (detailed metabolic pathways, half‑lives, Cmax, Tmax not provided).
- Elimination: Dorzolamide renal excretion emphasized; detailed elimination parameters not specified.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Not specified in available sources beyond class effects and systemic beta‑blockade and carbonic anhydrase inhibition.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Clinical exposure:
  - A total of 1,035 patients were treated with dorzolamide + timolol in clinical development (source provides aggregate patient count).
- Phase III studies:
  - A set of adverse events were observed in phase III studies; specific trial designs and primary endpoints are not fully described in the available sources. The source reports a list of clinical adverse experiences observed in ≥1% of patients in phase III studies (see Section 7).

  - A three‑month study in children <6 years (specifically patients ≥2 and <6 years in whom IOP was not controlled on monotherapy) included patients switched to dorzolamide + timolol; adverse events related to therapy were observed in a small number (3 of 30 transferred patients experienced cough, burning/stinging, ocular injection).
