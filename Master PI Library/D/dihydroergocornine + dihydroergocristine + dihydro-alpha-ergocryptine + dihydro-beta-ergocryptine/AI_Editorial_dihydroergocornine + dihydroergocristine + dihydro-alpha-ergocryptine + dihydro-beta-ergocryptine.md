# Editorial Product Information: dihydroergocornine + dihydroergocristine + dihydro-alpha-ergocryptine + dihydro-beta-ergocryptine

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **dihydroergocornine + dihydroergocristine + dihydro-alpha-ergocryptine + dihydro-beta-ergocryptine**
- Chemical class: hydrogenated ergot alkaloid mixture (ergoloid mesylates; methanesulfonate salts of dihydroergocornine, dihydroergocristine, dihydro‑α‑ergocryptine and dihydro‑β‑ergocryptine)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: oral (oral tablet; sublingual formulation has been studied and shown bioequivalent to the oral tablet)

## 2. Regulatory History
- Marketing start date: 10/31/1991 [US]
- Product information revision / update dates as stated:
  - Rev 02, August 2014 [US]
  - Revised: August 2016 [US]
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources
- Additional regulatory dates (approvals, registrations) not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Indicated for some individuals over 60 years of age who manifest an idiopathic decline in mental capacity (cognitive and interpersonal skills, mood, self‑care, apparent motivation). Clinical response appears to be limited to a subset of patients; the characteristics that predict response are not established.
- Recommended use is for symptomatic relief in selected patients with an ill‑defined process related to aging or underlying dementing conditions (examples cited include primary progressive dementia, Alzheimer's dementia, senile onset, multi‑infarct dementia).
- Prior to initiation, reversible causes of cognitive decline (including delirium, systemic disease, primary neurological disease or mood disturbance) should be excluded.
- Not indicated for the treatment of acute or chronic psychosis, regardless of etiology.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Typical adult dosage reported: 1 mg three times daily (oral).

### Dosing by indication
- No alternative indication‑specific dosing regimens described in the available sources.

### Administration instructions
- Administer orally. A sublingual formulation has been shown to be bioequivalent to the oral tablet in bioequivalence studies.

### Titration schedules
- No titration schedules specified in available sources.

### Onset and duration
- Alleviation of symptoms is usually gradual; clinical effects may not be observed for 3–4 weeks.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to ergoloid mesylates or any component of the product.
- Psychosis — acute or chronic, regardless of etiology (listed as a contraindication).

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- Boxed warnings: Not specified in available sources.
- General warnings and precautions:
  - Because the target symptoms are of uncertain etiology, clinicians should make a careful diagnostic evaluation before initiating therapy.
  - Exclude delirium and dementiform illness secondary to systemic disease, primary neurological disease, or primary mood disturbance prior to treatment.
  - The decision to use therapy should be continually reassessed; the presenting syndrome may evolve to allow a specific diagnosis and alternative treatment.
  - Continued clinical evaluation is required to determine whether any initial benefit persists over time.
  - The preparation has not been found to possess the vasoconstrictor properties attributed to some natural ergot alkaloids.
- Monitoring requirements:
  - Ongoing clinical assessment of cognitive/functional status is advised to evaluate benefit and need for continued therapy.
  - Specific laboratory monitoring parameters are not specified in available sources.
- Specific clinical precautions:
  - Use is not appropriate for treatment of acute or chronic psychosis.
  - Exercise caution in interpreting early clinical improvement; reassess diagnosis periodically.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- The precise mechanism by which ergoloid mesylates exert mental/cognitive effects is not clearly established. There is no conclusive evidence that the drug specifically affects cerebral arteriosclerosis or cerebrovascular insufficiency.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption:
  - Estimated oral absorption calculated from urinary excretion of tritium‑labelled drug: approximately 25%.
  - Following oral administration, peak levels of total radioactivity (ng equivalents) were reported (~0.5 ngEq/mL per mg) within approximately 1.5–3 hours.
  - Bioassay/radioimmunoassay data indicate rapid gastrointestinal absorption with mean peak plasma levels of unchanged ergoloid in the range of 0.05–0.13 ng/mL per mg (range 0.03–0.18 ng/mL/mg) achieved within 0.6–1.3 hours (observed extremes 0.4–2.8 hours).
  - Substantial first‑pass hepatic metabolism is indicated, with less than 50% of the therapeutic moiety reaching systemic circulation.
- Distribution:
  - Distribution specifics (volume of distribution, plasma protein binding) are not specified in available sources.
- Metabolism:
  - Considerable first‑pass metabolism by the liver is reported; metabolites account for a portion of circulating radioactivity.
- Elimination:
  - Elimination of radiolabelled drug plus metabolites demonstrated a biphasic decline with half‑lives of approximately 4 hours and 13 hours for radioactivity.
  - Mean half‑life of unchanged ergoloid in plasma reported as approximately 2.6–5.1 hours.
  - After three half‑lives, amounts of unchanged ergoloid are less than 10% of radioactivity; by 24 hours unchanged ergoloid is not detectable.
- Bioequivalence:
  - Oral tablet and sublingual tablet formulations have been shown to be bioequivalent in studies cited.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Specific pharmacodynamic effects and receptor pharmacology are not definitively characterized in the available sources.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Efficacy assessment:
  - Efficacy was evaluated using the Sandoz Clinical Assessment–Geriatric (SCAG) rating scale.
  - In trials reported, modest but statistically significant improvements at 12 weeks were observed on multiple SCAG items, including: mental alertness, confusion, recent memory, orientation, emotional lability, self‑care, depression, anxiety/fears, cooperation, sociability, appetite, dizziness, fatigue, bothersomeness, and an overall clinical status impression.
- Trial design and outcomes:
  - Specific trial designs (randomization details, control arms, sample size, statistical methods) are not described in the available sources.
  - Length of reported assessment: 12 weeks for the outcomes noted.
  - Details on magnitude of effect, responder rates, or long‑term outcomes beyond the 12‑week assessments are not provided in the available sources.
