# Editorial Product Information: Dimethyl Fumarate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a concise reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information approved in a specific jurisdiction. Prescribers should consult the local approved PI for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Dimethyl fumarate (a methyl ester of fumaric acid).
- **Brand Name(s):** AKM DIMETHYL FUMARATE [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Delayed-release capsules: 120 mg and 240 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pharmacor Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Relapsing Multiple Sclerosis (RMS):** Indicated for use in patients with relapsing forms of multiple sclerosis to decrease the frequency of clinical relapses and to delay the accumulation of physical disability [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):**
    - **Initial Dose:** 120 mg twice daily for the first 7 days.
    - **Maintenance Dose:** 240 mg twice daily thereafter [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Capsules must be swallowed whole. Do not crush, divide, or dissolve, as the enteric coating is required to prevent gastric irritation [Australian].
    - May be taken with or without food. However, administration with food may improve tolerability in patients experiencing flushing or gastrointestinal side effects [Australian].
- **Tolerability Management:**
    - **Dose Reduction:** A temporary reduction to 120 mg twice daily may be considered to manage flushing or gastrointestinal (GI) adverse effects. The target dose of 240 mg twice daily should be resumed within one month [Australian].
    - **Aspirin Co-administration:** Pre-treatment with 325 mg of non-enteric coated aspirin (administered 30 minutes prior to dimethyl fumarate) may reduce the incidence and severity of flushing [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specifically addressed in the available source.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to dimethyl fumarate or any of the excipients (e.g., silicified microcrystalline cellulose, croscarmellose sodium, methacrylic acid copolymers, gelatin) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None listed in the provided source.
- **Lymphopenia:** Dimethyl fumarate may cause a decrease in lymphocyte counts. In clinical trials, mean counts decreased by ~30% in the first year and then stabilised. 
    - **Monitoring:** Obtain a Complete Blood Count (CBC), including lymphocytes, prior to initiation (within 6 months), at 6 months of treatment, and every 6 to 12 months thereafter [Australian]. 
    - **Action:** Consider treatment interruption if lymphocyte counts fall below 0.5 x 10⁹/L and persist for more than 6 months [Australian].
- **Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy (PML):** PML, an opportunistic viral infection caused by the JC virus, has occurred in patients treated with dimethyl fumarate, typically in the setting of prolonged moderate to severe lymphopenia (<0.91 x 10⁹/L). Monitor for new or worsening neurological symptoms (e.g., unilateral weakness, vision changes, cognitive impairment). If PML is suspected, withhold treatment immediately and perform diagnostic evaluation [Australian].
- **Anaphylaxis and Angioedema:** Reported cases have occurred after the first dose or at any time during treatment. Discontinue treatment permanently if anaphylaxis or angioedema occurs [Australian].
- **Infections:** Monitor for signs of serious infection. Consider withholding therapy until resolution. Serious opportunistic infections (viral, fungal, and bacterial) have been reported, including **Herpes Zoster** (including disseminated and ophthalmic cases) [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal Reactions:** Serious events including perforation, ulceration, haemorrhage, and obstruction have been reported post-marketing, often within the first 6 months of therapy [Australian].
- **Hepatic Injury:** Elevations in serum transaminases (≥3x ULN) have occurred. Post-marketing reports include transaminase elevations with concomitant bilirubin increases (>2x ULN). Monitor liver function tests (LFTs) as clinically indicated [Australian].
- **Renal Precautions:** Urinalysis is recommended prior to initiation, annually, and as clinically indicated, due to reports of proteinuria in clinical trials [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions were most frequent during the first month of therapy.
- **Very Common (≥10%):**
    - Flushing (35% vs 4% placebo)
    - Diarrhoea (14% vs 11% placebo)
    - Nausea (12% vs 9% placebo)
    - Abdominal pain (upper and general)
- **Common (1–10%):**
    - Vomiting, Dyspepsia, Gastritis, Gastrointestinal disorder
    - Lymphopenia, Leucopenia
    - Burning sensation, Pruritus, Rash, Erythema
    - Proteinuria, Albuminuria
    - Aspartate aminotransferase (AST) and Alanine aminotransferase (ALT) increased
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** PML, anaphylaxis, serious infections (e.g., Herpes Zoster), and serious gastrointestinal events (perforation/ulceration) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Metabolism:** Dimethyl fumarate is extensively metabolised by esterases; the cytochrome P450 (CYP) system is not involved. No significant interaction risks were identified with CYP inhibitors/inducers or p-glycoprotein [Australian].
- **Specific Medications:**
    - **Interferon beta-1a and Glatiramer Acetate:** No pharmacokinetic interactions observed [Australian].
    - **Oral Contraceptives:** No effect on the PK of norelgestromin and ethinyl estradiol [Australian].
    - **Aspirin:** 325 mg non-enteric aspirin does not alter the PK of dimethyl fumarate [Australian].
- **Vaccinations:** Non-live vaccines are permitted. The safety and efficacy of live attenuated vaccines have not been evaluated and are not recommended during treatment [Australian].
- **Alcohol:** Do not consume alcohol within 1 hour of taking dimethyl fumarate, as it may increase the frequency of GI adverse reactions [Australian].
- **Other Fumarates:** Avoid simultaneous use of other fumaric acid derivatives (topical or systemic) [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category B1 [Australian]:** Drugs which have been taken by only a limited number of pregnant women and women of childbearing age, without an increase in the frequency of malformation or other direct or indirect harmful effects on the human fetus having been observed. 
    - Use only if clearly needed and if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus. Animal studies showed reduced fetal weight and ossification at high doses associated with maternal toxicity [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if dimethyl fumarate/metabolites are excreted in human milk. A risk to the infant cannot be excluded. Decisions should consider the benefits of breastfeeding vs. the mother's need for treatment [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of reduced fertility in animal studies at clinically relevant doses [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Limited data available for patients ≥65 years; it is unknown if they respond differently than younger patients [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No specific studies were conducted. However, as the renal pathway is a secondary route of elimination (<16%), no dose adjustments are recommended [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No specific studies were conducted. As metabolism occurs via esterases (not CYP450), no dose adjustments are recommended [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** The exact mechanism in MS is not fully elucidated. Dimethyl fumarate (DMF) and its primary metabolite, monomethyl fumarate (MMF), activate the **Nrf2 (nuclear factor erythroid-derived 2-like 2) transcriptional pathway**, which upregulates antioxidant response genes. DMF also demonstrates anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory effects via downregulation of pro-inflammatory cytokines (TH1, TH17) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly hydrolysed to MMF by esterases. DMF is not quantifiable in plasma. Tmax of MMF is 2–2.5 hours [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Vd is 60–90 L. Protein binding of MMF is 27–40% [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Extensive metabolism via ubiquitous esterases and the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily eliminated via exhalation of CO₂ (60%). Renal (15.5%) and faecal (0.9%) routes are secondary. MMF half-life is approximately 1 hour [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study 1 (DEFINE):** A 2-year, double-blind, placebo-controlled study (n=1234). Dimethyl fumarate 240 mg BID significantly reduced the proportion of patients relapsing at 2 years (27% vs 46.1% placebo; p<0.0001) and reduced the annualised relapse rate (ARR) by 53% [Australian].
- **Study 2 (CONFIRM):** A 2-year study (n=1417) including an active reference arm (glatiramer acetate). DMF 240 mg BID significantly reduced the ARR (0.224 vs 0.401 placebo; p<0.0001) and the number of new/enlarging T2 lesions [Australian].
- **Pooled Data:** Showed a 32% relative reduction in the time to confirmed disability progression compared to placebo at 2 years [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Conditions:** Store in the original packaging to protect from light [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Not specified in available sources (refer to local packaging) [Australian].
