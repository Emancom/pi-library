# Editorial Product Information: Daprodustat

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of daprodustat, synthesised for use by healthcare professionals. It integrates clinical data, regulatory requirements, and safety information.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Prescribing Information required for clinical practice in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Daprodustat (INN); hypoxia-inducible factor prolyl hydroxylase (HIF PH) inhibitor.
- **Brand Name(s):** **JESDUVROQ** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available source.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 1 mg (gray), 2 mg (yellow), 4 mg (white), 6 mg (pink), and 8 mg (orange). All are biconvex, round, and debossed with specific codes (GS KF, GS V7, GS 13, GS IM, GS 5E, respectively) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** GlaxoSmithKline LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Anemia of Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD):** Indicated for the treatment of anemia due to CKD in adults who have been receiving dialysis for at least four months [US].
- **Limitations of Use:** 
    - Not shown to improve quality of life, fatigue, or patient well-being.
    - Not indicated for use as a substitute for red blood cell (RBC) transfusions in patients requiring immediate correction of anemia.
    - Not indicated for patients not on dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Administration:** Administer once daily, with or without food. Tablets must be swallowed whole; do not cut, crush, or chew. Can be taken without regard to dialysis timing or concomitant phosphate binders/iron [US].
- **Target Hemoglobin (Hb):** Individualize dosing to use the lowest dose sufficient to reduce transfusion needs. Do not target Hb >11 g/dL [US].

### Recommended Starting Dose [US]
Starting dose is determined by whether the patient is currently receiving an Erythropoiesis-Stimulating Agent (ESA).
- **Patients Not Currently Treated with an ESA:**
    - Hb <9 g/dL: 4 mg once daily.
    - Hb 9 to 10 g/dL: 2 mg once daily.
    - Hb >10 g/dL: 1 mg once daily.
- **Patients Switching from an ESA:** The starting dose is based on the previous ESA dose (e.g., Epoetin alfa $\leq$2,000 units/week converts to 4 mg daily; >20,000 units/week converts to 12 mg daily).

### Titration and Monitoring
- **Monitoring:** Monitor Hb every 2 weeks for the first month, then every 4 weeks after initiation or dose adjustment.
- **Adjustments:** Dose may be increased or decreased by one dose level at a time (Levels: 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24 mg). Do not increase dose more frequently than every 4 weeks.
- **Interruption:** If Hb >12 g/dL, interrupt treatment. Restart at one dose level lower when Hb is within the target range.
- **Non-response:** If a clinically meaningful Hb increase is not achieved after 24 weeks, discontinue therapy and investigate alternative causes of anemia.

### Missed Dose
- Take as soon as possible on the same day. If it is the day of the next dose, skip the missed dose and resume the usual schedule. Do not double the dose [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Concomitant use of **strong** CYP2C8 inhibitors (e.g., gemfibrozil) [US].
- Uncontrolled hypertension [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning [US]
- **Increased Risk of Death, Myocardial Infarction, Stroke, Venous Thromboembolism (VTE), and Thrombosis of Vascular Access:** Daprodustat increases the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE). Targeting Hb >11 g/dL increases these risks. Avoid use in patients with MI, cerebrovascular event, or acute coronary syndrome within the 3 months prior to initiation.

### General Warnings
- **Hospitalization for Heart Failure:** Increased risk, particularly in patients with a history of heart failure. Monitor for signs/symptoms (edema, dyspnea).
- **Hypertension:** Worsening of blood pressure or hypertensive crisis may occur. Periodically monitor blood pressure and adjust antihypertensive therapy as needed.
- **Gastrointestinal (GI) Erosion:** Gastric or esophageal erosions and GI bleeding have been reported. Use with caution in patients with a history of peptic ulcers or those using tobacco/alcohol.
- **Malignancy:** HIF-1 levels may be associated with unfavorable effects on cancer growth. Not recommended in patients with active malignancies.
- **Non-Dialysis Patients:** Safety has not been established. In a trial of non-dialysis patients (ASCEND-ND), daprodustat was associated with increased cardiovascular mortality and other serious adverse events compared to rhEPO.

### Monitoring Requirements
- Baseline and periodic assessment of: Hb levels, liver function (ALT, AST, bilirubin), and iron stores (ferritin/TSAT). Ensure iron stores are adequate (ferritin $\geq$100 ng/mL or TSAT $\geq$20%) prior to and during therapy [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Data [US]
Adverse reactions reported in $\geq$5% of patients in clinical trials:
- **Very Common (>10%):** Hypertension (24%), Thrombotic vascular events (see below), Abdominal pain (11%).
- **Common (1–10%):** Dizziness (7%), Hypersensitivity (7%) (including rash, urticaria, dermatitis).

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Thrombotic Vascular Events:** Rate of 9.8 per 100 person-years (PY). Specific events include vascular access thrombosis (5.0 per 100 PY), MI (3.4 per 100 PY), and Stroke (1.2 per 100 PY).
- **Heart Failure Hospitalization:** Occurred in 7.5% of patients (14.5% in those with a pre-existing history).

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Strong CYP2C8 Inhibitors (e.g., gemfibrozil):** Contraindicated. Gemfibrozil increased daprodustat AUC by 18.6-fold.
- **Moderate CYP2C8 Inhibitors (e.g., clopidogrel):** Reduce starting dose of daprodustat by half (except if already 1 mg). Clopidogrel is expected to increase daprodustat AUC by 4-fold.
- **CYP2C8 Inducers (e.g., rifampin):** May decrease daprodustat exposure, potentially leading to loss of efficacy. Monitor Hb and adjust dose accordingly.
- **Transporters:** Daprodustat is a substrate of BCRP, but clinically significant interactions with BCRP inhibitors are considered low. Daprodustat does not significantly inhibit OATP1B1 or OATP1B3.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified, but text states "May cause fetal harm"]. Animal studies in rats and rabbits showed embryo-fetal loss, reduced fetal weight, and skeletal malformations at doses causing maternal toxicity and polycythemia. Use is not recommended unless potential benefits outweigh risks.
- **Breastfeeding:** No human data. Present in rat milk. Because of the potential for serious adverse reactions in the breastfed child (e.g., thrombotic events), breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for one week after the final dose [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats showed decreased fertility and increased post-implantation loss at high doses (41 times the MRHD).

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness observed in patients $\geq$65 years old compared to younger adults [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for patients on dialysis. Exposure is not significantly impacted by hemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild (Child-Pugh A):** No adjustment required.
    - **Moderate (Child-Pugh B):** Reduce starting dose by half (except if already 1 mg).
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Not recommended; not studied [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Daprodustat reversibly inhibits HIF prolyl hydroxylase enzymes (PH1, PH2, and PH3). This stabilizes HIF-1$\alpha$ and HIF-2$\alpha$, leading to increased transcription of HIF-responsive genes, including erythropoietin (EPO), thereby stimulating erythropoiesis.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Increases endogenous EPO in a dose-dependent manner within 6–8 hours. Reticulocyte counts peak between 7–15 days.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Bioavailability 65%. Tmax 1–4 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding >99%.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by CYP2C8 (95%) and minimally by CYP3A4 (5%).
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life 1–4 hours. Excretion: 74% in feces, 21% in urine (as metabolites) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ASCEND-D Trial:** A randomized, active-controlled trial in 2,964 adults on dialysis. Daprodustat was non-inferior to rhEPO (epoetin alfa or darbepoetin alfa) regarding the mean change in Hb from baseline to weeks 28–52 and the time to first MACE event.
    - **Hb Outcome:** Mean Hb change was 0.3 g/dL for daprodustat vs 0.1 g/dL for rhEPO (difference 0.2; 95% CI: 0.1, 0.2).
    - **MACE Outcome:** Hazard ratio 0.93 (95% CI: 0.81, 1.07), meeting the non-inferiority margin of 1.25 [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Handling:** Keep out of reach of children.
