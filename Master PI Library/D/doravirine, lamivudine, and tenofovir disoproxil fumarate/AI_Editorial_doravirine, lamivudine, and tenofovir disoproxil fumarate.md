# Editorial Product Information: Doravirine, Lamivudine, and Tenofovir Disoproxil Fumarate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory guidance and clinical data for the fixed-dose combination of doravirine, lamivudine, and tenofovir disoproxil fumarate.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidance required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Doravirine (NNRTI), Lamivudine (NRTI), and Tenofovir Disoproxil Fumarate (NRTI).
- **Brand Name:** **Delstrigo** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Film-coated tablets containing 100 mg doravirine, 300 mg lamivudine, and 300 mg tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (equivalent to 245 mg tenofovir disoproxil) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Physical Description:** Yellow, oval-shaped tablets, debossed with the corporate logo and "776" on one side, plain on the reverse [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Merck Sharp & Dohme LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Delstrigo** is indicated as a complete regimen for the treatment of HIV-1 infection in adults and paediatric patients weighing at least 35 kg [US]:
- In patients with no prior history of antiretroviral treatment.
- As a replacement for a stable antiretroviral regimen in virologically suppressed patients (HIV-1 RNA <50 copies/mL) who have no history of treatment failure and no known resistance-associated substitutions to the individual components of the drug.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Paediatrics $\ge$ 35 kg):** One tablet taken orally once daily, with or without food [US].
- **Pre-treatment Screening:** Patients must be tested for Hepatitis B Virus (HBV) infection prior to initiating therapy. Renal function (serum creatinine, estimated CrCl, urine glucose, and urine protein) should be assessed before and during treatment [US].
- **Dose Adjustment for Rifabutin Co-administration:** One tablet of **Delstrigo** once daily, followed by an additional 100 mg dose of doravirine (as **Pifeltro**) approximately 12 hours later [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not recommended for patients with an estimated creatinine clearance below 50 mL/min, as the fixed-dose nature prevents the necessary dose adjustments for lamivudine and tenofovir disoproxil fumarate [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Patients should be advised to take a missed dose as soon as remembered, unless it is nearly time for the next scheduled dose. Two doses should not be taken at once [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Strong CYP3A Inducers:** Co-administration with drugs that strongly induce cytochrome P450 3A (CYP3A) is contraindicated, as they significantly decrease doravirine plasma concentrations, potentially leading to loss of virologic response and resistance [US]. Specific examples include:
    - Carbamazepine, oxcarbazepine, phenobarbital, phenytoin.
    - Enzalutamide.
    - Rifampin, rifapentine.
    - Mitotane.
    - St. John’s wort (*Hypericum perforatum*).
- **Hypersensitivity:** Contraindicated in patients with a known previous hypersensitivity reaction to lamivudine [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Post-treatment Acute Exacerbation of Hepatitis B [US]:** Severe acute exacerbations of HBV have been reported in patients co-infected with HIV-1 and HBV who discontinued lamivudine or tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF). Hepatic function should be monitored closely for several months in co-infected patients who stop **Delstrigo**. Initiation of anti-hepatitis B therapy may be required.
- **Skin and Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Severe reactions, including Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), and Drug Rash with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS), have been reported with doravirine-containing regimens. Discontinue therapy immediately if severe skin reactions or constitutional symptoms develop [US].
- **New Onset or Worsening Renal Impairment:** TDF is associated with renal impairment, including acute renal failure and Fanconi syndrome. Avoid use with concurrent or recent nephrotoxic agents (e.g., high-dose NSAIDs) [US].
- **Bone Loss and Mineralization Defects:** Decreases in bone mineral density (BMD) and cases of osteomalacia have been associated with TDF. Consider BMD monitoring in patients with a history of pathologic fractures or risk factors for osteoporosis [US].
- **Immune Reconstitution Syndrome:** Inflammatory responses to indolent opportunistic infections or autoimmune disorders (e.g., Graves’ disease) may occur during the initial phase of treatment [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The majority of adverse reactions reported in clinical trials were Grade 1 (mild) [US].

### Common Adverse Reactions ($\ge$ 5%)
| Adverse Reaction | Frequency [US] |
| :--- | :--- |
| Dizziness | 7% |
| Nausea | 5% |
| Abnormal dreams | 5% |

### Other Notable Reactions (1–5%)
- Headache, insomnia, diarrhoea, somnolence, and rash [US].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Severe skin reactions (SJS/TEN/DRESS).
- Acute exacerbation of Hepatitis B.
- Lactic acidosis and severe hepatomegaly with steatosis (reported with nucleoside analogues).
- Renal failure and proximal renal tubulopathy.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Doravirine is primarily metabolized by CYP3A. Drugs that induce or inhibit CYP3A will alter doravirine concentrations [US].
- **Nephrotoxic Agents:** Co-administration with drugs that reduce renal function or compete for active tubular secretion (e.g., acyclovir, cidofovir, aminoglycosides, high-dose NSAIDs) may increase concentrations of lamivudine or tenofovir [US].
- **Sorbitol:** Co-administration of lamivudine with sorbitol-containing medicines may decrease lamivudine exposure; avoid when possible [US].
- **Hepatitis C Antivirals:** Ledipasvir/sofosbuvir or sofosbuvir/velpatasvir may increase tenofovir concentrations; monitor for TDF-associated adverse reactions [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not assigned a letter category]. Available data from the Antiretroviral Pregnancy Registry (APR) show no difference in the risk of major birth defects for lamivudine and tenofovir compared to background rates. Data for doravirine are insufficient to assess risk. Healthcare providers are encouraged to register patients in the APR (1-800-258-4263) [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Breastfeeding is not recommended due to the potential for HIV-1 transmission, development of viral resistance, and serious adverse reactions in the infant [US]. Lamivudine and tenofovir are known to be present in human milk [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no effects on fertility for doravirine or lamivudine. Tenofovir disoproxil fumarate showed an alteration of the estrous cycle in female rats but no impact on male fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for patients weighing at least 35 kg. Safety and efficacy in children weighing <35 kg have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include sufficient numbers of patients aged 65 and over. Use with caution due to the higher frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not recommended if estimated CrCl is <50 mL/min [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required for mild (Child-Pugh A) or moderate (Child-Pugh B) impairment. Not studied in patients with severe (Child-Pugh C) hepatic impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:**
    - **Doravirine:** Pyridinone NNRTI; non-competitive inhibition of HIV-1 reverse transcriptase.
    - **Lamivudine:** Synthetic nucleoside analogue (NRTI); terminates the DNA chain after phosphorylation to its active metabolite.
    - **Tenofovir Disoproxil Fumarate:** Acyclic nucleoside phosphonate diester (NRTI); competes with natural substrate for HIV-1 reverse transcriptase, causing chain termination [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is 64% (doravirine), 86% (lamivudine), and 25% (tenofovir). Food does not have a clinically significant effect on absorption [US].
    - **Elimination Half-life:** Doravirine (~15 h), Lamivudine (5–7 h), Tenofovir (~17 h) [US].
    - **Excretion:** Lamivudine and Tenofovir are primarily excreted renally; Doravirine is primarily metabolised via CYP3A4 and excreted via the biliary/fecal route [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **DRIVE-AHEAD:** A Phase 3 trial in treatment-naïve adults showed **Delstrigo** was non-inferior to EFV/FTC/TDF at Week 48 and Week 96 (HIV-1 RNA <50 copies/mL in 77% vs 74% at Week 96). **Delstrigo** demonstrated superior lipid profiles and fewer neuropsychiatric events [US].
- **DRIVE-SHIFT:** A Phase 3 trial in virologically suppressed adults showed that switching to **Delstrigo** maintained suppression (91% stayed <50 copies/mL at Week 48) and was non-inferior to continuing a baseline PI-, NNRTI-, or elvitegravir-based regimen [US].
- **IMPAACT 2014:** A trial in paediatric patients ($\ge$ 35 kg) showed safety and efficacy profiles similar to those observed in adults [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- Dispense only in the original bottle and keep tightly closed to protect from moisture.
- Do not remove desiccants [US].
