# Editorial Product Information: Desonide

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis intended for use by healthcare professionals. It provides a consolidated clinical overview of desonide based on available regulatory documentation.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial is a synthesis for informational purposes and does not substitute for the official Product Information approved in a specific jurisdiction for the purpose of prescribing or dispensing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Desonide (a nonfluorinated corticosteroid).
- **Chemical Name:** 11$\beta$, 16$\alpha$, 17, 21-tetrahydroxypregna-1, 4-diene-3, 20-dione-cyclic-16, 17-acetonide.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Desowen** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** D07AB08 (Corticosteroids, moderately potent (group II)).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Cream: 0.05% (0.5 mg/g) [Australian].
    - Ointment: 0.05% (0.5 mg/g) [Australian].
    - Lotion: 0.05% (0.5 mg/g) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Galderma Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- Relief of inflammatory and pruritic manifestations of corticosteroid-responsive dermatoses in adults and children aged 2 years and older [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric $\ge$ 2 years)
- Apply a thin film to the affected skin areas two to three times daily, depending on the severity of the condition.
- **Maximum Duration:** Continuous treatment should not exceed 8 weeks for either adults or children [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Shake the lotion formulation well before use.
    - Occlusive dressings may be used for managing psoriasis or recalcitrant conditions, but must be discontinued if infection develops.
    - An increase in application frequency may exacerbate adverse effects without improving therapeutic outcomes.
    - When treating large surface areas, clinicians should monitor the quantity of product used (e.g., number of tubes).

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to desonide or any excipients in the formulation (e.g., hydroxybenzoates in lotion, sorbates in cream) [Australian].
- Most viral infections of the skin, including vaccinia and herpes simplex [Australian].
- Cutaneous tuberculosis [Australian].
- Acne and rosacea [Australian].
- Application to ulcerated lesions [Australian].
- Application to the eyelids (due to risk of glaucoma) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **HPA Axis Suppression:** Systemic absorption can produce reversible hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis suppression, Cushing’s syndrome, hyperglycaemia, and glucosuria. Risk increases with the use of potent steroids, large surface area application, prolonged use, and occlusive dressings.
- **Face Use:** Prolonged use on the face may lead to corticosteroid-related dermatitis (paradoxically corticosteroid-responsive) followed by rebound upon discontinuation. Gradual tapering is required. Use is not recommended for perioral dermatitis [Australian].
- **Infection:** Should not be applied to areas with bacterial, mycobacterial, viral, fungal, parasitic infections, or ulcerous wounds. If superinfection occurs, appropriate antimicrobial therapy must be initiated; if no prompt response occurs, desonide should be discontinued until the infection is controlled [Australian].
- **Visual Disturbance:** Both systemic and topical corticosteroid use have been associated with visual disturbances. Patients reporting blurred vision or other symptoms should be referred for ophthalmologic evaluation to rule out cataracts, glaucoma, or rare conditions like central serous chorioretinopathy (CSCR) [Australian].
- **Local Intolerance:** Discontinue use if irritation or signs of local intolerance develop.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Concomitant use of systemic corticosteroids (tablets, drops, or injections) may intensify the side effects of topical desonide [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B3:** Corticosteroids are teratogenic in animals after systemic and topical administration. There are no adequate/controlled studies in pregnant women. Use only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the foetus; avoid extensive use, large amounts, or prolonged periods [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if topical application results in sufficient systemic absorption to be excreted in breast milk. Caution should be exercised. **Do not apply to the breasts during lactation** to prevent neonatal ingestion [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Data on the effect of desonide on human or animal fertility are not available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - Approved for children $\ge$ 2 years of age [Australian].
    - Contraindicated in children under 2 years [Australian].
    - Children are more susceptible to HPA axis suppression and Cushing’s syndrome due to a higher skin surface area to body weight ratio. Chronic therapy may interfere with growth and development. 
    - Avoid spontaneous occlusion in skin folds or under diapers.
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available in the provided sources [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources (though metabolism is primarily hepatic and excretion is renal/biliary).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Topical corticosteroids possess anti-inflammatory, anti-pruritic, and vasoconstrictive properties. The exact anti-inflammatory mechanism remains unclear.
- **Potency Classification:** 
    - **US Classification:** Mild (Group VI) using the McKenzie-Stoughton vasoconstrictor assay.
    - **European Classification:** Mid-potent (Rank 3) on a four-point scale.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Can be absorbed through normal intact skin; absorption is increased by inflammation, occlusive dressings, and skin damage.
    - **Distribution:** Bound to plasma proteins in varying degrees once systemically absorbed.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised in the liver.
    - **Excretion:** Excreted by the kidneys and via bile.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Ointment HPA Axis Study:** A study of **Desowen** ointment administered for 4 weeks showed no HPA axis suppression in the study population. Data for longer administration periods are not available [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C.
- Do not freeze.
- Shelf life: Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].
