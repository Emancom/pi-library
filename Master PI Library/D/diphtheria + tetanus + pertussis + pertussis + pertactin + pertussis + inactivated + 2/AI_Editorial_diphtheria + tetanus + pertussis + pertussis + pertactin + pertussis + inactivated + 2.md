# Editorial Product Information: diphtheria + tetanus + pertussis + pertussis + pertactin + pertussis + inactivated + 2

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **diphtheria + tetanus + pertussis + pertussis + pertactin + pertussis + inactivated + 2**
- Chemical class: Combined bacterial and viral vaccine containing diphtheria toxoid, tetanus toxoid, acellular pertussis antigens (pertussis toxoid [PT], filamentous haemagglutinin [FHA], pertactin [PRN], fimbriae types 2 + 3 [FIM 2+3]) and inactivated poliovirus types 1, 2 and 3 (Vero cell-derived)
- ATC code: J07CA02
- Route(s) of administration: Intramuscular injection
- DO NOT include brand or trade names, dosage form descriptors, or regulatory scheduling in this section

- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Primary immunisation of infants against diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis and poliomyelitis beginning at 2 months of age (recommended primary schedule described below).
- Booster (fourth) dose for children previously immunised with three doses of diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis and polio vaccines; indicated for children from 15 months up to six years of age. The vaccine should not be administered after the seventh birthday.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (paediatric)
- Recommended primary series for infants: one 0.5 mL dose administered intramuscularly at 2, 4 and 6 months of age.
- Booster dose (fourth dose): one 0.5 mL intramuscular dose may be given to children aged 15 months to 6 years who have received three prior doses of diphtheria/tetanus/pertussis/polio-containing vaccines.
- Administration beyond the seventh birthday: do not administer after the seventh birthday.

### Dosing by indication
- Primary immunisation (infants): 0.5 mL IM at 2, 4, 6 months.
- Booster dose: 0.5 mL IM at 15 months–6 years in children previously immunised.

### Administration instructions
- Inspect the syringe for particulate matter or discoloration before use; do not use if present.
- Shake well to obtain a uniform suspension immediately prior to administration.
- Route: administer intramuscularly only.
- Preferred injection site: anterolateral thigh for infants and children under 12 months; deltoid for older children when muscle mass is adequate.
- When multiple injectable vaccines are given at the same visit, use separate syringes and separate injection sites (preferably different limbs). Do not mix this vaccine in the same syringe with other vaccines or medicinal products.
- Do not administer intravascularly or subcutaneously.
- Needles should not be recapped; dispose of needles and syringe per local requirements.
- Product is single‑use; discard any residue.

## 5. Contraindications
- History of severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) to any component of the vaccine or to a previous dose of the same vaccine or a vaccine containing the same components.
- Vaccination should be postponed in persons with moderate or severe febrile and/or acute disease. (Low‑grade fever is not a contraindication.)
- Neurological contraindications (pertussis-containing vaccine contraindications):
  - Encephalopathy (e.g., coma, decreased level of consciousness, prolonged seizures) within 7 days of a previous dose of a pertussis‑containing vaccine that is not attributable to another identifiable cause.
  - Progressive neurological disorder (including infantile spasms, uncontrolled epilepsy, progressive encephalopathy); pertussis‑containing vaccine should not be administered until a treatment regimen has been established and the condition has stabilised.
- Do not administer to individuals after their seventh birthday (age limitation).
- Hypersensitivity to residual manufacturing components: caution in individuals with known hypersensitivity to streptomycin sulfate, neomycin, or polymyxin B sulfate (residual trace amounts may be present); severe allergy to these antibiotics is a contraindication to administration of this vaccine.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Prior severe adverse events after pertussis vaccination: if any of the following occurred after administration of a whole‑cell pertussis vaccine or a pertussis‑containing acellular vaccine, careful consideration of risks and benefits is required before administering further doses:
  - Temperature ≥ 40.5°C within 48 hours (not attributed to another cause)
  - Collapse or shock‑like state (hypotonic‑hyporesponsive episode, HHE) within 48 hours
  - Persistent crying lasting ≥ 3 hours within 48 hours
  - Convulsions (with or without fever) within 3 days
- Hypersensitivity: bovine serum albumin, formaldehyde and glutaraldehyde are used in manufacture and residual traces may be present; be cautious in persons with known hypersensitivity to these substances.
- Antibiotic residues: residual neomycin, polymyxin B sulfate and streptomycin sulfate may be present; exercise caution in individuals with hypersensitivity to these antibiotics.
- Anaphylaxis preparedness: as with all injectable vaccines, appropriate medical treatment and supervision should be readily available to manage anaphylactic reactions; adrenaline (epinephrine) should be immediately available when vaccination is given.
- Immunosuppression: immunogenicity may be reduced by immunosuppressive therapy; consider postponing vaccination until the end of immunosuppression where feasible. Vaccination is still recommended for individuals with chronic immunodeficiency (e.g. HIV infection) although antibody responses may be limited.
- Guillain‑Barré syndrome (GBS): evidence exists for a causal relation between tetanus toxoid and both brachial neuritis and GBS. If GBS occurred within 6 weeks of prior tetanus‑toxoid containing vaccine, the decision to administer this vaccine should be based on careful consideration of potential benefits and risks.
- Hypotonic‑hyporesponsive episodes (HHE): occur rarely after whole‑cell pertussis vaccines and less commonly after acellular pertussis vaccines; a history of HHE is not a contraindication but vaccinate with caution.
- Syncope: fainting can occur following vaccination; procedures should be in place to reduce injury and manage syncope.
- Thrombocytopenia/bleeding disorders: administer with caution because bleeding may occur after intramuscular injection.
- Premature infants: for infants born ≤ 28 weeks’ gestation (very premature) there is a potential risk of apnoea following vaccination; consider respiratory monitoring for 48–72 hours after primary immunisation, particularly for those with prior history of respiratory immaturity. Vaccination should not be withheld or delayed solely because of prematurity.
- Large injection‑site reactions and limb swelling: may occur, more commonly after additional (4th or 5th) doses; reactions usually begin 24–72 hours post‑vaccination and resolve within 3–5 days.
- Protection not absolute: vaccination may not protect 100% of susceptible individuals.

### Monitoring requirements
- Be prepared to manage anaphylaxis.
- For very premature infants, consider respiratory monitoring for 48–72 hours after primary immunisation.
- Monitor for fever and neurologic events following vaccination.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Do not mix with other vaccines in the same syringe; when reconstitution of eligible freeze‑dried Hib vaccine is indicated, this vaccine may be used as the diluent as permitted (see Interactions).
- Administer at separate sites when given concomitantly with other vaccines.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Active immunisation: the vaccine contains diphtheria and tetanus toxoids that induce antitoxin production, acellular pertussis antigens (PT, FHA, PRN, FIM 2+3) to generate anti‑pertussis immune responses, and inactivated poliovirus types 1, 2 and 3 (Vero cell‑derived) to induce neutralising antibodies against poliovirus. Together these antigens stimulate humoral immune responses conferring protection against the respective diseases.

### Pharmacokinetics
- No pharmacokinetic studies have been performed or reported in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Immunogenicity data from clinical trials show high seroprotection rates one month after 3‑dose primary series and after a fourth dose:
  - Diphtheria antitoxin ≥ 0.01 IU/mL: 99.1% post‑dose 3; 100% post‑dose 4.
  - Tetanus antitoxin ≥ 0.01 IU/mL: 100% post‑dose 3 and post‑dose 4.
  - Poliovirus neutralising antibody ≥ 1:8: Type 1: 98.1% post‑dose 3, 100% post‑dose 4; Type 2: 100% post‑dose 3 and post‑dose 4; Type 3: 99.1% post‑dose 3, 100% post‑dose 4.
- Geometric mean titres (GMTs) for pertussis antigens increased after the primary series and after the booster (selected examples from a clinical trial):
  - PT GMT: 103 (1 month post‑dose 3); 223 (1 month post‑dose 4)
  - FHA GMT: 165 (1 month post‑dose 3); 252 (1 month post‑dose 4)
  - FIM GMT: 332 (1 month post‑dose 3); 1079 (1 month post‑dose 4)
  - Pertactin GMT: 40.5 (1 month post‑dose 3); 160 (1 month post‑dose 4)

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Efficacy trials referenced were primarily conducted with acellular pertussis combination vaccines related to the antigen composition in this vaccine:
  - A randomized, double‑blind efficacy study in Sweden (Trial 1) with infants receiving an acellular pertussis combination (regular formulation) showed an absolute vaccine efficacy of 85% (95% CI: 81%–89%) against pertussis disease defined by at least 21 days of paroxysmal cough with laboratory or epidemiologic confirmation.
  - A large randomized, double‑blind trial in Sweden (Trial 2) compared three acellular pertussis vaccines and one whole‑cell DTP vaccine in 82,892 infants; a “hybrid” formulation (containing pertussis antigen concentrations similar to this vaccine) demonstrated high efficacy against culture‑confirmed pertussis and lower rates of high fever and seizures compared with whole‑cell DTP.
- Immunogenicity trial (Canada):
  - Infants receiving this vaccine in a 3‑dose primary series at 2, 4 and 6 months achieved high seroprotection rates for diphtheria, tetanus and poliovirus one month after dose 3; antibody GMTs for pertussis antigens were comparable to those elicited by comparator acellular pertussis vaccines used in other efficacy trials.
  - One month after a fourth dose (booster at 18 months), seroprotection rates reached 100% for diphtheria/tetanus/polio thresholds cited above and pertussis antigen GMTs increased substantially versus post‑primary series levels.
- Safety findings from clinical experience:
  - Local and systemic reactions (redness, swelling, tenderness, irritability, fever) were the most frequent adverse events, usually occurring within 24 hours and resolving within 24–72 hours.
  - Comparative trials reported higher rates of fever > 40.5°C and seizures after whole‑cell DTP than after acellular pertussis vaccines.
  - Large injection site reactions and limb swelling were observed in some series, more frequently after additional booster doses; most resolved spontaneously without sequelae.
- Additional multicenter trial observations:
  - In an NIH multicenter evaluation of multiple acellular pertussis formulations administered in 4‑ or 5‑dose schedules, entire limb swelling was reported in a small percentage of children after repeated doses; swelling was transient and resolved without sequelae.
