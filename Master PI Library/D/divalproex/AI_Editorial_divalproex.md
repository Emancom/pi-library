# Editorial Product Information: Divalproex Sodium

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based solely on the Product Information provided by the **United States (Food and Drug Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a clinically relevant format.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians must consult local regulatory guidelines and official PI documents for the most current and region-specific information.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Divalproex sodium (a stable co-ordination compound of sodium valproate and valproic acid in a 1:1 molar relationship).
- **Chemical Class:** Fatty acid derivative / Antiepileptic.
- **Brand Name(s):** Not specifically listed as a proprietary name in the source, but provided as divalproex sodium extended-release tablets (Dr. Reddy’s Laboratories Ltd) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Extended-release (ER) film-coated tablets: 250 mg [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Dr. Reddy’s Laboratories Ltd [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
Divalproex sodium extended-release tablets are indicated for:
- **Acute Mania:** Treatment of manic or mixed episodes associated with bipolar disorder, with or without psychotic features [US].
- **Epilepsy:** 
    - Monotherapy and adjunctive therapy for complex partial seizures in adults and paediatric patients aged 10 years and older [US].
    - Sole and adjunctive therapy for simple and complex absence seizures in adults and paediatric patients aged 10 years and older [US].
    - Adjunctive therapy for multiple seizure types that include absence seizures [US].
- **Migraine Prophylaxis:** Prevention of migraine headaches in adults [US]. Note: There is no evidence of efficacy in the acute treatment of migraine.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing Advice
- **Administration:** Tablets must be swallowed whole; they should not be crushed or chewed [US].
- **Frequency:** Intended for once-a-day administration [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Take as soon as possible unless it is almost time for the next dose. Do not double the dose [US].

### Indication-Specific Dosing
- **Mania:** Initial dose is 25 mg/kg/day. Increase rapidly to the lowest therapeutic dose providing clinical effect or desired plasma concentration (target trough: 85–125 mcg/mL). Maximum recommended dose is 60 mg/kg/day [US].
- **Complex Partial Seizures:** Start at 10 to 15 mg/kg/day. Titrate by 5 to 10 mg/kg/week to achieve optimal response (standard range: 50–100 mcg/mL). Maximum recommended dose is 60 mg/kg/day [US].
- **Absence Seizures:** Start at 15 mg/kg/day. Titrate by 5 to 10 mg/kg/week until control is achieved or side effects limit further increases. Maximum dose is 60 mg/kg/day [US].
- **Migraine Prophylaxis:** Initial dose is 500 mg/day for one week, followed by an increase to 1,000 mg/day [US].

### Conversion from Delayed-Release (DR) to Extended-Release (ER)
In patients with epilepsy previously receiving divalproex sodium DR tablets, the ER daily dose should be 8% to 20% higher than the total daily DR dose [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hepatic Disease:** Known hepatic disease or significant hepatic dysfunction [US].
- **Mitochondrial Disorders:** Known urea cycle disorders (UCD) or mitochondrial disorders caused by mutations in mitochondrial DNA polymerase γ (POLG), such as Alpers-Huttenlocher Syndrome [US].
- **POLG Suspicion:** Children under two years of age suspected of having a POLG-related disorder [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to the drug, sodium valproate, or valproic acid [US].
- **Pregnancy (Migraine Prophylaxis):** Contraindicated in pregnant women and women of childbearing potential not using effective contraception when treated for migraine prophylaxis [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Hepatic failure resulting in fatalities has occurred, typically within the first 6 months of treatment. Children <2 years and those with mitochondrial disorders are at highest risk. Monitor liver function prior to and frequently during therapy.
- **Fetal Risk:** Exposure in utero causes major congenital malformations (e.g., neural tube defects), decreased IQ scores, and neurodevelopmental disorders.
- **Pancreatitis:** Life-threatening, hemorrhagic cases have been reported in children and adults. Ordinarily, divalproex should be discontinued if pancreatitis occurs.

### General Warnings
- **Suicidal Behavior/Ideation:** Antiepileptic drugs increase the risk of suicidal thoughts or behavior. Monitor patients for changes in mood or emergence of depression [US].
- **Hyperammonemia and Encephalopathy:** May occur despite normal liver function. Elevated risk when used concomitantly with topiramate. Evaluate ammonia levels if unexplained lethargy, vomiting, or mental status changes occur [US].
- **Hematologic Effects:** Dose-related thrombocytopenia and inhibition of platelet aggregation may occur. Monitor platelet counts and coagulation parameters before starting therapy and periodically (especially before surgery) [US].
- **Hypothermia:** Reported with and without hyperammonemia, particularly with topiramate use [US].
- **DRESS/Multiorgan Hypersensitivity:** Discontinue if an alternative etiology for severe rash, fever, or lymphadenopathy cannot be established [US].
- **Somnolence in Elderly:** Increased risk of somnolence and decreased nutritional intake; necessitates slower titration and monitoring of fluid intake [US].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Hepatotoxicity** and **Pancreatitis** [US].
- **Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS)** [US].
- **Hyperammonemic Encephalopathy** [US].
- **Significant Thrombocytopenia** (Total valproate ≥110 mcg/mL in females or ≥135 mcg/mL in males) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Hepatic Enzyme Inducers (e.g., Phenytoin, Carbamazepine, Rifampin):** Increase valproate clearance, potentially lowering serum levels [US].
- **Carbapenem Antibiotics:** Can significantly reduce valproate concentrations to subtherapeutic levels; avoid concomitant use if possible [US].
- **Estrogen-Containing Contraceptives:** May increase valproate clearance and decrease serum levels [US].
- **Felbamate:** May increase peak valproate concentrations [US].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Topiramate:** Concomitant use increases the risk of hyperammonemia with or without encephalopathy and hypothermia [US].
- **Cannabidiol:** Increases risk of ALT/AST elevations [US].
- **CNS Depressants/Alcohol:** Potential for additive CNS depression [US].

### Valproate Effects on Other Drugs
- **Lamotrigine:** Valproate inhibits lamotrigine metabolism, doubling its half-life and increasing the risk of serious skin reactions (e.g., SJS/TEN). Reduce lamotrigine dose [US].
- **Phenobarbital:** Valproate inhibits metabolism, increasing phenobarbital levels and risk of severe CNS depression [US].
- **Propofol:** Valproate may increase propofol blood levels; dose reduction of propofol is suggested [US].
- **Warfarin:** Valproate may increase the unbound fraction of warfarin; monitor coagulation tests [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Migraine Prophylaxis:** Contraindicated [US].
    - **Epilepsy/Bipolar Disorder:** Should not be used unless other medications have failed. 
    - **Risks:** Neural tube defects (spina bifida risk 1-2% vs 0.06% in general population), cardiovascular malformations, craniofacial defects, and limb malformations. Risk of decreased IQ (averaging 7-10 points lower than peers) and higher risk of Autism Spectrum Disorder [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Valproate is excreted in human milk (1% to 10% of maternal serum levels). Monitor infants for signs of liver damage (jaundice) or unusual bleeding [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies show adverse reproductive effects (testicular atrophy). Reports of male infertility exist in postmarketing data [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - **Hepatotoxicity:** Significant risk in children <2 years, especially on polytherapy or with metabolic disorders [US].
    - **Dosing:** Children 3 months to 10 years have 50% higher clearance than adults [US]. 
    - **Approvals:** Not indicated for migraine or mania in patients <18 years [US]. Approved for epilepsy in patients ≥10 years [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Reduce the starting dose and titrate more slowly. Monitor for excessive somnolence and dehydration [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Unbound clearance is reduced by approximately 27%. Monitoring of total concentrations may be misleading; clinical response and free valproate levels are more reliable [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in significant dysfunction. Clearance of free valproate is reduced by up to 50% in cirrhosis [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** While the exact mechanism is unconfirmed, its antiepileptic effect is likely related to increased brain concentrations of gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is ~90% relative to IV. Median Tmax is 4 to 17 hours [US].
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound; binding is concentration-dependent and saturable [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Almost entirely metabolized by the liver via glucuronidation and mitochondrial beta-oxidation [US].
    - **Elimination:** Mean terminal half-life for monotherapy is 9 to 16 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Mania:** A 3-week multicenter trial in hospitalized patients with acute mania showed significant reduction in Mania Rating Scale (MRS) scores compared to placebo at doses titrated to 85–125 mcg/mL [US].
- **Epilepsy:** Efficacy established in two controlled trials for complex partial seizures (CPS) as both monotherapy and adjunctive therapy, showing significant reductions in seizure frequency [US].
- **Migraine:** A 12-week trial showed significant reduction in 4-week migraine headache rates (mean reduction 1.2 vs 0.6 for placebo) at 1,000 mg/day [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Packaging:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only (Rx-only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources (US system uses Controlled Substances Act; Divalproex is not a scheduled controlled substance but requires a prescription).
