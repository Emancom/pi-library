# Editorial Product Information: Dutasteride

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the following jurisdictional Product Information (PI) documents: 
    - Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not replace the official PI. Clinicians should consult the current approved PI in their specific jurisdiction before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Dutasteride, a synthetic 4-azasteroid compound and competitive inhibitor of both Type 1 and Type 2 5-alpha-reductase isoenzymes.
- **Brand Names:** AKM Dutasteride [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** G04CB02.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 500 mcg (0.5 mg) soft gelatin capsules [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pharmacor Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (BPH):**
    - Monotherapy for the management of symptomatic BPH in men with an enlarged prostate.
    - Combination therapy with an alpha-blocker (e.g., tamsulosin) for the management of symptomatic BPH [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult Males):** The standard dose is 500 mcg (one capsule) administered orally once daily. 
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Capsules must be swallowed whole. They should not be chewed or opened, as contact with the contents may cause oropharyngeal mucosal irritation.
    - May be taken with or without food.
- **Treatment Duration:** While symptomatic improvement may occur early, a minimum treatment duration of 6 months is generally required to assess therapeutic response.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to **dutasteride**, other 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors, or any excipients in the formulation.
- **Gender and Age:** Use is contraindicated in women and children.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with severe hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Prostate Cancer Risk:** In clinical trials (e.g., REDUCE study), a higher incidence of Gleason 8–10 (high-grade) prostate cancers was observed in men taking **dutasteride** compared to placebo. No causal relationship has been established, but patients should be regularly evaluated for prostate cancer risk.
- **Prostate-Specific Antigen (PSA) Effects:** **Dutasteride** reduces serum PSA levels by approximately 50% after 6 months of treatment. A new PSA baseline should be established at 6 months. Any confirmed increase from the lowest PSA level while on treatment may signal the presence of prostate cancer or non-compliance.
- **Cardiovascular Events:** In some 4-year trials, the incidence of "cardiac failure" (a composite term) was higher in patients taking **dutasteride** in combination with an alpha-blocker compared to monotherapy. No causal relationship is confirmed, and overall cardiovascular event rates did not differ between groups.
- **Male Breast Cancer:** Cases have been reported in clinical trials and post-marketing. Patients should report any breast changes (lumps, pain, or nipple discharge) immediately.
- **Leaking Capsules:** **Dutasteride** is absorbed through the skin. Women who are pregnant or may be pregnant must avoid handling leaking capsules. If contact occurs, the area must be washed immediately with soap and water.
- **Blood Donation:** Men should not donate blood until at least 6 months after their last dose to prevent potential exposure to a pregnant transfusion recipient.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are most common during the first year of treatment and typically diminish thereafter.

| Adverse Event | Year 1 Frequency | Year 2 Frequency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Impotence | 6% | 2% |
| Decreased Libido | 4% | <1% |
| Ejaculation Disorders | 2% | <1% |
| Breast Disorders (incl. tenderness/enlargement) | 1% | 1% |

- **Serious/Post-marketing Effects (Frequency Very Rare <0.01%):**
    - Immune system: Allergic reactions (rash, pruritus, urticaria, angioedema).
    - Psychiatric: Depressed mood.
    - Reproductive: Testicular pain and swelling.
    - Skin: Alopecia (primarily body hair loss) or hypertrichosis [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** **Dutasteride** is metabolised by CYP3A4. Co-administration with potent or moderate inhibitors (e.g., verapamil, diltiazem) may increase serum concentrations. However, no dose adjustment is recommended due to the wide safety margin.
- **Clinically Non-significant Interactions:** No significant interactions were observed with tamsulosin, terazosin, warfarin, digoxin, or cholestyramine.
- **Other Concomitant Meds:** No adverse interactions were identified with ACE inhibitors, beta-blockers, calcium channel blockers, diuretics, NSAIDs, or PDE5 inhibitors [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **[Australian Category X]:** Contraindicated. 
    - **Risk to Fetus:** Exposure to **dutasteride** in a woman carrying a male fetus may inhibit the development of the fetal external genitalia due to the suppression of dihydrotestosterone (DHT). 
    - **Semen Exposure:** Small amounts of **dutasteride** are found in semen. While unlikely to adversely affect a fetus via vaginal absorption, the use of a condom is recommended if the patient's partner is or may be pregnant.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated in women; excretion in milk is unknown.
- **Fertility:** **Dutasteride** 500 mcg daily for 52 weeks reduced total sperm count, semen volume, and sperm motility (approx. 23–26% reduction). These effects may persist after treatment cessation in some individuals.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Contraindicated; safety and efficacy have not been studied.
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment required.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is anticipated, as less than 0.1% of the dose is excreted in urine.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution due to extensive hepatic metabolism and a long half-life (3–5 weeks). Contraindicated in severe impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Competitive and specific inhibitor of both Type 1 and Type 2 5-alpha-reductase isoenzymes. This prevents the conversion of testosterone to 5-alpha-dihydrotestosterone (DHT), the primary androgen responsible for prostatic growth.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Median serum DHT is reduced by 85–90% within 2 weeks of starting 500 mcg daily.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Tmax 1–3 hours; absolute bioavailability ~60%. Food does not affect absorption.
    - **Distribution:** Large volume of distribution (300–500 L); >99.5% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised by CYP3A4 to several metabolites.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is 3 to 5 weeks. Excreted primarily in faeces (~5% as unchanged drug, 40-42% as major metabolites).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Monotherapy (ARIA3001, ARIA3002, ARIB3003):** Three 2-year studies (n=4,325) demonstrated that **dutasteride** significantly reduced the risk of acute urinary retention (AUR) by 57% and the need for BPH-related surgery by 48% compared to placebo.
- **Combination Therapy (CombAT):** A 4-year study (n=4,844) showed that combination therapy (dutasteride + tamsulosin) was superior to monotherapy in improving symptom scores (IPSS) and reducing the risk of BPH progression.
- **Prostate Cancer Risk (REDUCE):** A 4-year study in men with an increased risk of prostate cancer showed an imbalance in high-grade Gleason scores, though the clinical significance is unclear [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].
- **Special Handling:** Do not handle if capsules are leaking, especially if pregnant.
