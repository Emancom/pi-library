# Editorial Product Information: Deucravacitinib

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals (HCPs). 
- **Important:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not intended to replace the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the Australian PI was available for review; information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., US, European, Canadian, Japanese) is not included in this synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Deucravacitinib
- **Chemical Class:** Tyrosine kinase 2 (TYK2) inhibitor (selective, allosteric)
- **Brand Name:** Sotyktu [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** Not available (Removed from official source [Australian])
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 6 mg film-coated tablets. The tablets are pink, round, biconvex, and laser printed with “BMS 895 6 mg” on one side [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Bristol-Myers Squibb [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Plaque Psoriasis:** Treatment of moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis in adult patients who are candidates for systemic therapy or phototherapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Standard Adult Dose:** 6 mg taken orally once daily [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** May be taken with or without food. Tablets must be swallowed whole; do not crush, cut, or chew [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not explicitly detailed in the source, though standard clinical practice for once-daily oral therapies usually involves taking the dose as soon as remembered unless it is close to the next scheduled dose.
- **Titration:** No titration schedule is required [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to deucravacitinib or any of the excipients (including lactose) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity:** Reactions have occurred in trials. If a clinically significant reaction occurs, discontinue the drug and initiate appropriate therapy [Australian].
- **Infections:** Deucravacitinib may increase the risk of infections. Do not initiate in patients with a clinically important active infection. Use with caution in patients with chronic or recurrent infections. Monitor patients for signs of infection during and after treatment; if a serious infection develops, withhold treatment until resolved [Australian].
- **Tuberculosis (TB):** Evaluate patients for TB prior to initiation. Do not administer to patients with active TB. Consider anti-TB therapy for patients with latent TB or a history of inadequate treatment before starting deucravacitinib [Australian].
- **Viral Reactivation:** Cases of herpes virus reactivation (e.g., herpes zoster, herpes simplex) have been reported. Consider screening for viral hepatitis (B and C) before and during therapy; the impact on chronic hepatitis reactivation is currently unknown [Australian].
- **Malignancies:** Lymphomas and non-melanoma skin cancers (NMSC) have been observed. Consider the benefit-risk ratio in patients with known malignancies or those who develop a malignancy during therapy. Periodic skin examinations are recommended [Australian].
- **Immunisations:** Completion of age-appropriate immunisations is recommended prior to therapy. Avoid the use of **live vaccines** during treatment [Australian].
- **Potential Risks of JAK Inhibition:** While deucravacitinib is a selective TYK2 inhibitor, it is unknown if it carries the same risks observed with JAK 1/2/3 inhibitors (e.g., increased all-cause mortality, major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE), and thrombosis). Caution is advised, particularly in patients with cardiovascular risk factors [Australian].
- **Laboratory Abnormalities:**
    - **Creatine Phosphokinase (CPK):** Markedly elevated CPK and rhabdomyolysis have been reported. Discontinue if myopathy is suspected or if CPK levels are significantly elevated [Australian].
    - **Triglycerides:** Elevations in serum triglycerides have been noted; monitor and manage according to hyperlipidaemia guidelines [Australian].
    - **Liver Enzymes:** Evaluate at baseline and monitor in patients with known/suspected liver disease. Interrupt therapy if drug-induced liver injury is suspected [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by frequency based on a 16-week controlled safety pool [Australian].

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (>10%) | Upper respiratory infections (including nasopharyngitis) |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Herpes simplex infections, oral ulcers (aphthous/stomatitis), headache, diarrhoea, acneiform rash, folliculitis, hypertension, increased CPK |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Herpes zoster |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Serious infections (0.6%) and malignancies (0.2% during 52-week period) have been reported [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Effect of Other Drugs on Deucravacitinib:** Deucravacitinib is eliminated via multiple pathways (CYP1A2, CES2, UGT1A9). No clinically meaningful changes in exposure were observed when co-administered with P-gp/BCRP inhibitors, strong CYP1A2 inhibitors/inducers, UGT1A9 inhibitors, or gastric pH modulators (e.g., famotidine, rabeprazole) [Australian].
- **Effect of Deucravacitinib on Other Drugs:** No clinically relevant effects are expected on substrates of CYPs (1A2, 2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 3A4) or various drug transporters (P-gp, OATP, OAT, OCT, MATE). No meaningful effects were found on the exposures of rosuvastatin, methotrexate, mycophenolate mofetil, or oral contraceptives [Australian].
- **Immunosuppressants:** Combination use with other potent immunosuppressants or biologics has not been evaluated and is not recommended [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B1.** Data in pregnant women are insufficient to inform drug-associated risks. Animal studies showed no teratogenicity at exposures significantly higher than the human dose, though lower pup weight gain was noted in rats during pre-weaning [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if deucravacitinib is excreted in human milk. It is present in the milk of lactating rats. A risk to the breastfed infant cannot be excluded [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No effects on mating or fertility were observed in animal models (rats) [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients < 18 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment required for patients ≥ 65 years. Limited data are available for those ≥ 75 years; this group showed higher rates of serious adverse events and discontinuations in trials [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required, including for patients with end-stage renal disease (ESRD) on dialysis [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild to Moderate (Child-Pugh A and B):** No dose adjustment required.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Not recommended [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Deucravacitinib is an allosteric inhibitor of TYK2. It binds to the regulatory domain of the enzyme rather than the ATP-binding site, providing high selectivity against JAK 1, 2, and 3. It inhibits the signalling of cytokines involved in psoriasis, specifically IL-23, IL-12, and Type I interferons [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid absorption; 99% absolute bioavailability. Peak plasma concentration ($T_{max}$) in 2–3 hours [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** $V_{ss}$ of 140 L; 81.6% plasma protein bound [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Mediated by CYP1A2 (to major active metabolite BMT-153261), CES2, and UGT1A9. Parent drug accounts for 49% of circulating components [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 10 hours. 13% excreted unchanged in urine; 26% unchanged in faeces [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** POETYK PSO-1 and POETYK PSO-2.
- **Design:** Multi-centre, randomised, double-blind, placebo- and active-controlled (apremilast) trials in adults with moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis.
- **Key Outcomes:** Deucravacitinib (6 mg QD) demonstrated superiority over both placebo and apremilast at Week 16. In PSO-1, 58.4% of patients achieved PASI 75 compared to 12.7% for placebo. Efficacy was maintained through 52 weeks in continuous responders [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C in the original container to protect from moisture [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
- **Other Jurisdictions:** Not specified in available sources.
