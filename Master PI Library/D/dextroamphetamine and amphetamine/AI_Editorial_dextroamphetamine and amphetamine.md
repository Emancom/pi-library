# Editorial Product Information: Dextroamphetamine and Amphetamine (Mixed Salts)

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: US (FDA).
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of the clinical and regulatory profiles of mixed amphetamine salts, intended for use by healthcare professionals. 
- Disclaimers: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinical decisions should be based on the official PI approved in the practitioner's region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** A single-entity amphetamine product containing four salts in equal parts by weight:
    - Dextroamphetamine Saccharate
    - Amphetamine Aspartate Monohydrate
    - Dextroamphetamine Sulfate
    - Amphetamine Sulfate
- **Chemical Class:** CNS Stimulant; Non-catecholamine sympathomimetic amine.
- **Brand Name(s):** Not specified in provided source (generic identified from Elite Laboratories, Inc.) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Extended-release capsules: 5 mg, 10 mg, 15 mg, 20 mg, 25 mg, and 30 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Elite Laboratories, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD):** Indicated for the treatment of ADHD in adults and paediatric patients aged 6 years and older [US].
- **Limitations of Use:** Not recommended for use in paediatric patients younger than 6 years due to higher plasma exposure and increased incidence of adverse reactions (e.g., weight loss) compared to older patients at equivalent doses [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Administer once daily in the morning upon awakening [US].
- Avoid afternoon doses due to the potential for insomnia [US].
- May be taken with or without food [US].
- Capsules may be swallowed whole or opened and the entire contents sprinkled onto applesauce. Sprinkled applesauce must be consumed immediately without chewing; do not store or divide the contents of a single capsule [US].

### Dosing by Population [US]
- **Paediatric Patients (Ages 6 to 12):** Initial dose of 5 mg or 10 mg once daily. Dose may be adjusted in increments of 5 mg or 10 mg at weekly intervals. Maximum recommended dose is 30 mg/day.
- **Paediatric Patients (Ages 13 to 17):** Initial dose is 10 mg once daily. May increase to 20 mg/day after one week if symptoms are not adequately controlled.
- **Adults:** Recommended dose is 20 mg once daily.
- **Switching from Immediate-Release (IR):** Patients switching from IR formulations (e.g., twice daily) may transition to the extended-release formulation at the same total daily dose taken once daily.

### Titration and Maintenance
- Individualize dosage according to therapeutic needs and patient response. Always use the lowest effective dosage [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity or idiosyncrasy to amphetamine or any component of the formulation. Angioedema and anaphylactic reactions have been reported [US].
- **MAOI Use:** During or within 14 days following the administration of monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs), including linezolid or intravenous methylene blue, due to the risk of hypertensive crisis [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Abuse, Misuse, and Addiction [US]:** Mixed amphetamine salts have a high potential for abuse and misuse, which can lead to substance use disorder and addiction. Misuse may result in overdose or death. Risk is increased with higher doses or unapproved routes (snorting/injection). Assess risk before prescribing and monitor throughout treatment.
- **Serious Cardiovascular Events:** Sudden death has been reported in patients with structural cardiac abnormalities or other serious heart disease. Avoid use in patients with known structural cardiac abnormalities, cardiomyopathy, serious arrhythmias, or coronary artery disease [US].
- **Blood Pressure and Heart Rate:** May cause modest increases in average blood pressure (2–4 mmHg) and heart rate (3–6 bpm). Monitor all patients for hypertension and tachycardia [US].
- **Psychiatric Adverse Reactions:** May exacerbate symptoms in patients with pre-existing psychotic disorders. May induce manic or mixed episodes in patients with bipolar disorder (screen before initiation). New psychotic or manic symptoms (hallucinations, delusional thinking) can occur in 0.1% of patients at recommended doses [US].
- **Growth Suppression:** Long-term use in children is associated with weight loss and slowing of growth. Monitor height and weight closely; treatment interruption may be required if growth is suboptimal [US].
- **Seizures:** May lower the convulsive threshold. Discontinue if seizures occur [US].
- **Peripheral Vasculopathy:** Associated with Raynaud’s phenomenon. Monitor for digital changes (numbness, color change, or unexplained wounds) [US].
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Risk is increased when co-administered with serotonergic agents (SSRIs, SNRIs, triptans) or in overdose. Discontinue immediately if symptoms occur [US].
- **Tics:** May exacerbate or onset motor and verbal tics and Tourette’s syndrome. Evaluate before initiation and monitor regularly [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Reactions (Incidence ≥5% and > Placebo) [US]
| Population | Adverse Reactions |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Paediatric (6–12 years)** | Loss of appetite, insomnia, abdominal pain, emotional lability, vomiting, nervousness, nausea, fever. |
| **Paediatric (13–17 years)** | Loss of appetite, insomnia, abdominal pain, weight loss, nervousness. |
| **Adults** | Dry mouth, loss of appetite, insomnia, headache, weight loss, nausea, anxiety, agitation, dizziness, tachycardia, diarrhea, asthenia, urinary tract infection. |

### Postmarketing/Systemic Reactions
- **Cardiovascular:** Palpitations, cardiomyopathy (with chronic use).
- **CNS:** Psychotic episodes, overstimulation, tremors, dermatillomania, bruxism.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Unpleasant taste, constipation, intestinal ischemia.
- **Allergic:** Urticaria, rash, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis.
- **Musculoskeletal:** Rhabdomyolysis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **MAOIs:** Contraindicated; risk of hypertensive crisis. Wait 14 days after MAOI discontinuation [US].
- **Alkalinizing Agents (GI and Urinary):** e.g., Sodium bicarbonate, antacids. Increase blood levels and potentiate amphetamine action. Avoid co-administration [US].
- **Acidifying Agents (GI and Urinary):** e.g., Ascorbic acid. Decrease blood levels and efficacy. Adjust dose based on clinical response [US].
- **Serotonergic Drugs:** Increased risk of serotonin syndrome. Monitor closely [US].
- **CYP2D6 Inhibitors:** May increase exposure to amphetamine and risk of serotonin syndrome [US].
- **Tricyclic Antidepressants (TCAs):** May enhance activity of TCAs or sympathomimetic agents; cardiovascular effects may be potentiated [US].
- **Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs):** May decrease the time to maximum concentration (Tmax) [US].
- **Laboratory Interference:** May cause significant elevation in plasma corticosteroid levels (greatest in the evening). May interfere with urinary steroid determinations and DAT visualization (ioflupane I-123) [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - [US]: There is a pregnancy exposure registry. Available data have not identified a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, but amphetamines may cause vasoconstriction, leading to premature delivery and low birth weight. Monitor infants for withdrawal symptoms (irritability, agitation, feeding difficulties).
- **Breastfeeding:** Amphetamines are excreted in human milk (relative infant dose 2–13.8% of maternal dose). Due to the risk of serious adverse reactions in the infant, breastfeeding is not recommended [US].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility or early embryonic development were observed in animal studies at doses up to 8 times the maximum recommended human dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established for ADHD in ages 6–17 years. Not recommended for children <6 years [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Not studied in the geriatric population [US].
- **Renal Impairment [US]:**
    - **Severe (GFR 15 to <30 mL/min/1.73m²):** 
        - Adults: 15 mg once daily.
        - Paediatrics (6–17 years): 5 mg once daily (Max for 6–12 years is 20 mg daily).
    - **ESRD (GFR <15 mL/min/1.73m²):** Not recommended.
    - **Dialysis:** d-Amphetamine is not dialyzable.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Non-catecholamine sympathomimetic amines that block the reuptake of norepinephrine and dopamine into the presynaptic neuron and increase their release into the extraneuronal space [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** CNS stimulant activity; the exact mode of therapeutic action in ADHD is unknown [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics [US]:**
    - **Absorption:** Contains two types of beads (immediate and delayed release). Tmax is approximately 7 hours (4 hours longer than IR formulations).
    - **Metabolism:** Oxidized at the 4 position to 4-hydroxyamphetamine or on the side chain to alpha-hydroxy-amphetamine. CYP2D6 is involved in the formation of 4-hydroxy-amphetamine.
    - **Elimination:** Half-life (d-amphetamine) is ~10 hours in adults, 11 hours in adolescents, and 9 hours in children. Urinary recovery is highly pH-dependent; alkaline pH reduces and acidic pH increases renal elimination.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Paediatric (Ages 6–12):** A 3-week double-blind, placebo-controlled study (N=584) showed significant improvements in attention and hyperactivity based on teacher ratings for all doses (10, 20, 30 mg) compared to placebo [US].
- **Adolescent (Ages 13–17):** A 4-week study (N=327) showed significant improvements in ADHD-RS-IV scores for doses 10–40 mg. Doses >20 mg did not provide additional benefit [US].
- **Adult:** A 4-week study (N=255) showed significant improvement in ADHD-RS scores for all doses (20, 40, 60 mg). Doses >20 mg did not provide additional benefit [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at room temperature, 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container with a child-resistant closure. Protect from light [US].
- **Disposal:** Unused drug should be disposed of via medicine take-back programs or mixed with undesirable substances (e.g., coffee grounds) and placed in household trash [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:**
    - [US]: Schedule II (CII) Controlled Substance. Rx-only.
