# Editorial Product Information: Digoxin-specific antibody fragment F(Ab) (Ovine)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory and clinical data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Digoxin-specific antibody fragment F(Ab) (Ovine).
- **Chemical Class:** Monovalent immunoglobulin fragments (ovine-derived).
- **Brand Name(s):** **DIGIFAB** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** V03AB24.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Lyophilized powder for injection. Each vial contains 40 mg of digoxin-specific antibody fragment F(Ab), capable of binding approximately 0.5 mg of digoxin [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Phebra Pty Ltd (Sponsor); BTG International Inc. (Manufacturer) [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Life-threatening Digoxin Toxicity:** Indicated for the treatment of known or strongly suspected life-threatening digoxin toxicity. This includes toxicity associated with:
    - Ventricular arrhythmias.
    - Progressive bradycardia.
    - Second or third-degree heart block unresponsive to atropine.
- **Note:** Use is recommended when additional measures beyond digoxin withdrawal and electrolyte correction are necessary [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Guidelines
- **Route:** Intravenous infusion over 30 minutes. 
- **Bolus Administration:** May be given as a bolus injection if cardiac arrest is imminent [Australian].
- **Preparation:** Reconstitute each vial with 4 mL of Sterile Water for Injection to achieve a 10 mg/mL concentration. For small paediatric doses, further dilution with isotonic saline to 1 mg/mL is recommended [Australian].

### Dosing Calculation (Adults and Children >20 kg)
The dose is determined by the amount of digoxin to be neutralised.
- **Known Ingestion:** Number of vials = [Amount of digoxin ingested (mg) × Bioavailability*] / 0.5 mg/vial.
    - *Bioavailability: 0.7 for tablets, 0.8 for elixir.
- **Known Serum Concentration:** Number of vials = [Serum digoxin concentration (ng/mL) × weight (kg)] / 100.
- **Unknown Dose/Concentration:**
    - **Acute Poisoning:** 5 vials if hemodynamically stable; 10–20 vials if unstable.
    - **Chronic Poisoning:** 2 vials initially; repeat 2 vials after 30 minutes if symptoms persist [Australian].

### Dosing Calculation (Children ≤20 kg)
- **Known Concentration:** Use the serum concentration formula above.
- **Unknown Concentration:** Administer 1 vial; repeat if symptoms persist [Australian].

### Strategy by Toxicity Type
- **Acute Poisoning:** Administer half the estimated dose initially. Monitor for 6–12 hours. If no clinical response within 2 hours, give the remainder.
- **Chronic Poisoning:** Administer half the estimated dose initially. Give the remainder only if toxicity recurs [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to the active substance or any excipients (mannitol, sodium acetate) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Anaphylaxis and Hypersensitivity:** As a heterologous animal protein, there is a risk of anaphylactic/anaphylactoid reactions. Patients with known allergies to sheep protein, papain, chymopapain, bromelain, or the "alpha-gal" sugar molecule (red meat allergy) are at increased risk [Australian].
- **Potassium Shifts:** Digoxin toxicity often causes hyperkalemia. **DIGIFAB** causes a rapid shift of potassium back into the cell, which can lead to severe hypokalemia. Monitor serum potassium closely, especially in the first few hours post-administration [Australian].
- **Withdrawal of Inotropic Effect:** Removal of digoxin’s inotropic effect may cause clinical deterioration in patients with poor cardiac function (e.g., exacerbation of heart failure). Support with other inotropes (e.g., dopamine, dobutamine) may be required [Australian].
- **Redigitalization:** Postpone redigitalization until Fab fragments are eliminated (several days; >1 week in renal impairment) [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions may occur up to 14 days post-infusion.
| System Organ Class | Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Metabolism** | Common | Hypokalaemia, hyperkalaemia |
| **Nervous System** | Common | Headache, confusional state |
| **Gastrointestinal**| Common | Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation, abdominal distension |
| **Cardiac** | Common | Worsening heart failure, chest pain, hypotension, orthostatic hypotension |
| **Renal** | Common | Renal failure |
| **General** | Common | Fatigue, infusion site phlebitis, influenza-like illness |

*Frequency based on a limited study of 23 subjects [Australian].*

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Interactions:** Formal drug interaction studies have not been conducted [Australian].
- **Laboratory Interference:** **DIGIFAB** interferes with standard digitalis immunoassay measurements. Serum digoxin levels will rise precipitously (as they measure bound digoxin) and are clinically misleading until the fragments are cleared [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B2** [Australian]. No data is available regarding use in pregnant women. Use only if the potential benefit outweighs the risk to the fetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if the drug is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding should be discontinued during treatment [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No animal studies have been performed to evaluate effects on fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No specific studies conducted, though successfully used in infants. Use should be based on benefit-risk assessment [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific studies. Increased risk of renal impairment in this population necessitates closer monitoring for recurrent toxicity [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** The elimination half-life is significantly prolonged (up to 10-fold). While the therapeutic effect remains similar, patients must be monitored for a prolonged period (a week or longer) for possible recrudescent toxicity due to delayed clearance of the Fab-digoxin complex [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **DIGIFAB** has a high affinity ($10^9$ to $10^{10} M^{-1}$) for digoxin, which is greater than the affinity of digoxin for its receptor (Na+/K+ ATPase). It binds free digoxin, shifting the equilibrium away from tissue receptors [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is approximately 0.3 L/kg.
    - **Metabolism:** Not specified.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal. Elimination half-life is approximately 15 hours in patients with normal renal function [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacodynamic Study:** A study in 16 healthy volunteers compared **DIGIFAB** to **Digibind**, showing both products reduced free digoxin levels to undetectable levels within hours.
- **Efficacy Study:** A multi-center study of 15 patients with life-threatening toxicity showed that 93% had resolution of toxicity by 20 hours [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze).
- **Stability:** Use immediately after reconstitution. Contains no antimicrobial preservatives [Australian].
- **Container:** Clear glass vial with a non-natural rubber latex stopper [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
