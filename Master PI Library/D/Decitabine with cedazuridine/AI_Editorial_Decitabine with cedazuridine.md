## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Name:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn metabolic defects leading to secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2–3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported clinical dose is 300 mg/kg.
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Aim for a plasma free carnitine concentration between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
- **Administration:** Slow bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL. Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags [US].
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine (both oral and IV) has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, seizure frequency or severity may increase [US].
- **General Monitoring:** Monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels at therapy initiation and following any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not lettered, but indicates usage only if clearly needed]. Reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Controlled human data are unavailable [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies in humans are absent. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration. Clinicians must weigh the benefits of maternal supplementation against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg body weight; see Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and TMAO) which are normally renally excreted [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into the mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also facilitates the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Levocarnitine clears acyl-CoA compounds, preventing autointoxication and subsequent life-threatening acidosis [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis (trough) carnitine levels from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** Trough levels correlate with dose; 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses resulted in mean trough levels of 148, 240, and 456 µmol/L respectively at 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to expiry date on packaging. The formulation contains no preservative; discard unused portions immediately [US].
