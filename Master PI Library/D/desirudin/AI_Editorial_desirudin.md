# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: US (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference only. It is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP)
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL single-dose vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:**
    - **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
    - **Frequency:** Administered every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Maximum Dose:** The highest administered dose reported is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Administration:** Slow 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - **Adjustment:** Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use [US].
- Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks/benefits before re-administration [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients receiving levocarnitine, regardless of whether they have a pre-existing seizure disorder. In patients with known seizure activity, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring:** 
    - **Metabolic patients:** Obtain plasma carnitine levels prior to treatment. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
    - **ESRD patients:** Monitor trough (pre-dialysis) levels. Therapy is typically prompted when levels fall below 40–50 µmol/L [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic errors.

### Frequency Data (ESRD Trials)
| Body System | Adverse Event (Incidence ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, **injection site reaction**, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, arrhythmia |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, dyspepsia, anorexia |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, depression, paresthesia |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis |

### Serious/Specific Adverse Effects
- **Transient GI effects:** Nausea and vomiting [US].
- **Body Odour:** Less frequent but noted in clinical reports [US].
- **Seizures:** Reported in both patient populations [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended that INR levels be monitored after initiating levocarnitine treatment or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly letter-coded in current Baxter PI; however, reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations after exogenous administration. In nursing mothers, the risk to the child of excess carnitine intake must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg for metabolic disorders); see Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the available source.
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency; chronic high doses of oral formulations in ESRD may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide). The **injectable** form is specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours, and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine concentrations from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of the preservative-free formulation once opened [US].
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources (US document only).
