# Editorial Product Information: Paracetamol, Codeine Phosphate, and Doxylamine Succinate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- At the time of this editorial, documents from other jurisdictions (e.g., US, European, Canadian, Japanese) were not provided for comparison.
- **Purpose:** This document serves as a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a synthesis of clinical data and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Paracetamol (500 mg), Codeine phosphate hemihydrate (10 mg), and Doxylamine succinate (5.1 mg).
- **Chemical Class:**
    - Paracetamol: p-aminophenol derivative (analgesic and antipyretic).
    - Codeine phosphate: Opioid analgesic.
    - Doxylamine succinate: Ethanolamine-class antihistamine (H1-receptor antagonist).
- **Brand Name(s):** Cipla Pain Relief Paracetamol + Codeine with Calmative [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources (N02AJ09 commonly associated).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Capsule-shaped tablets containing 500 mg paracetamol, 10 mg codeine phosphate, and 5.1 mg doxylamine succinate [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Cipla Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Moderate Pain:** For the temporary relief of moderate pain [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Children >12 years):** One or two tablets taken with water every 4 to 6 hours as required [Australian].
- **Maximum Dose:** Do not exceed 8 tablets in a 24-hour period [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** Should not be taken concurrently with other paracetamol-containing products unless medically advised [Australian].
- **Titration:** Initiation of opioid treatment should be at the lower end of dosage recommendations with careful titration to achieve effective analgesia [Australian].
- **Ceasing Therapy:** In physically dependent patients, therapy must be tapered slowly (e.g., 10% to 25% reduction every 2 to 4 weeks) to avoid serious withdrawal symptoms [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Age-Specific:** Children under 12 years of age; children 12–18 years of age undergoing tonsillectomy or adenoidectomy for obstructive sleep apnoea [Australian].
- **Metabolism:** Known CYP2D6 ultra-rapid metabolisers [Australian].
- **Respiratory:** Severe or acute respiratory disease (e.g., acute asthma, COPD exacerbation), respiratory depression, or compromised respiratory function in adolescents [Australian].
- **Pharmacotherapy:** Concomitant use of Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs) or use within 14 days of discontinuing MAOIs [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal/Hepatobiliary:** Severe hepatocellular insufficiency, chronic constipation, diarrhoea caused by pseudomembranous colitis or poisoning (until toxin elimination), or biliary tract obstruction [Australian].
- **Other:** Known hypersensitivity to any ingredient or ethanolamine-class antihistamines; narrow-angle glaucoma; stenosing peptic ulcer; symptomatic prostatic hypertrophy; bladder neck or pyloroduodenal obstruction; G6PD deficiency; active alcoholism [Australian].
- **Obstetric:** During labour when premature birth is anticipated (risk of neonatal codeine withdrawal) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings [Australian]:**
    - **Limitations of Use:** Use only when other treatment options are inadequate.
    - **Hazardous Use:** Potential for abuse, misuse, and addiction, which can lead to fatal overdose.
    - **Respiratory Depression:** Fatal respiratory depression may occur; monitor closely during initiation and dose increases.
    - **Concomitant CNS Depressant Use:** Use with benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, or alcohol may result in profound sedation, coma, and death.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Paracetamol may cause liver failure even at therapeutic doses. In adults, 10–15g is hepatotoxic; 25g+ is potentially fatal [Australian].
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Life-threatening reactions like Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS) and Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN) have been reported with paracetamol [Australian].
- **QT Interval Prolongation:** Doxylamine may prolong the QT interval; caution is advised in patients with hypokalaemia, existing QT prolongation, or those using other QT-prolonging drugs [Australian].
- **Hyperalgesia:** Opioid-induced hyperalgesia (OIH) may occur, where increasing doses paradoxically increase pain sensitivity [Australian].
- **Adrenal Insufficiency:** Reported with long-term opioid use [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Frequency Not Specified (Infrequent generally):** Anorexia, drowsiness, depression, dizziness, sweating, anaphylaxis, angioedema, dyspnoea, hypotension, nausea, diarrhoea, dry mouth, erythema, and rash [Australian].
- **Codeine-specific:** 
    - *Common:* Nausea, vomiting, drowsiness, dizziness, constipation.
    - *Other:* Euphoria, dysphoria, miosis, tinnitus, urinary retention, cough suppression, and Central Sleep Apnoea Syndrome [Australian].
- **Doxylamine-specific:**
    - *CNS Depressive:* Sedation, impaired coordination, blurred vision, thickened secretions.
    - *Anticholinergic:* Dry eyes/mouth/nose, urinary hesitancy, tachycardia [Australian].
    - *Paradoxical:* Excitation or stimulation (more common in children and elderly) [Australian].
- **Serious Effects:** Fatal hepatic necrosis, agranulocytosis, SJS/TEN, rhabdomyolysis (in doxylamine overdose) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CNS Depressants:** Additive effects with benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids (not recommended), alcohol, tricyclic antidepressants, and antipsychotics [Australian].
- **CYP2D6 Inhibitors:** (e.g., fluoxetine, paroxetine, quinidine) May reduce the analgesic efficacy of codeine by preventing conversion to morphine [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** (e.g., rifampicin) May reduce codeine's analgesic effect [Australian].
- **Anticoagulants:** Long-term paracetamol use may increase the risk of bleeding with warfarin [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal Agents:** Metoclopramide/domperidone increase paracetamol absorption; propantheline and anticholinergics decrease it [Australian].
- **Flucloxacillin:** Increased risk of high anion gap metabolic acidosis (HAGMA) [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category A**. However, use should be avoided during the third trimester and labour due to risks of neonatal respiratory depression and withdrawal syndrome [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** **Contraindicated**. Codeine and its metabolites are excreted in breast milk. Mothers who are CYP2D6 ultra-rapid metabolisers can produce dangerously high levels of morphine in milk, leading to fatal neonatal respiratory depression [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Contraindicated under 12 years. Contraindicated in adolescents 12-18 years following tonsillectomy/adenoidectomy or with compromised respiratory function [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of paradoxical excitation (doxylamine), CNS depression, confusion, and urinary retention [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution due to increased risk of toxicity and respiratory depression [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution in mild-to-moderate insufficiency; contraindicated in severe insufficiency [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:**
    - *Paracetamol:* Central inhibition of prostaglandin synthesis.
    - *Codeine:* Acts centrally on opioid receptors; efficacy largely depends on conversion to morphine.
    - *Doxylamine:* H1-receptor antagonist with lipophilic properties allowing BBB penetration; also blocks cholinergic receptors [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - *Absorption:* Paracetamol (peak 10–60 min), Codeine (peak ~1 hr), Doxylamine (peak 2–3 hr).
    - *Metabolism:* Primarily hepatic. Paracetamol produces a hepatotoxic intermediate (detoxified by glutathione). Codeine is O-demethylated to morphine via CYP2D6.
    - *Elimination:* Half-lives: Paracetamol (1–3 hr), Codeine (3–4 hr), Doxylamine (~10 hr). Primarily renal excretion [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy:** Systematic reviews indicate that the addition of codeine to paracetamol provides a small but statistically significant increase in analgesia compared to paracetamol alone, though with a higher incidence of adverse effects [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Disposal:** Unused medicine should be returned to a pharmacy for disposal [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** **Schedule 4 (S4)** - Prescription Only Medicine [Australian].
- **Other Jurisdictions:** Not specified in available sources.
