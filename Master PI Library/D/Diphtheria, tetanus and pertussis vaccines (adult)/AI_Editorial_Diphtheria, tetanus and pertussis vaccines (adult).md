# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided for the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview designed for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises available regulatory data into a single, clinically focused reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Always consult local regulatory guidelines and the current manufacturer-supplied PI for definitive prescribing instructions.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with metabolic defects resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) on Dialysis:** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Administer by intravenous injection only.
- Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration before use [US].
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial, as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often utilised in patients presenting with severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 to 4 hours; intervals must never be less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are recommended in the range of 50 mg/kg, though higher doses may be required based on clinical response.
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest dose administered in clinical settings is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration Timing:** Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and provide supportive care [US].
- **Neurologic Reactions (Seizures):** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** The safety of oral levocarnitine in patients with renal impairment or ESRD has not been established. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these populations may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are typically excreted renally [US].
- **Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of the patient population (ESRD or metabolic disorders).

### Frequency >10% (Very Common) [US]
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33–59%), pain (21–49%), flu syndrome (15–40%), headache (3–37%), asthenia (8–12%), abdominal pain (5–21%), fever (5–12%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (14–21%), hypotension (3–19%), chest pain (6–15%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–33%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–19%), rhinitis (6–11%).
- **Neurological/Psychiatric:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypervolemia (3–17%), hypercalcemia (6–15%).

### Common (1–10%) and Other Effects [US]
- **Metabolic:** Body odor (reported with carnitine therapy), weight increase/decrease, hyperkalemia.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis, anorexia, constipation, dyspepsia, melena.
- **Musculoskeletal:** Leg cramps, myalgia.
- **Nervous System:** Anxiety, depression, insomnia, vertigo, hypertonia.
- **Postmarketing Reports:** Seizures, anaphylaxis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels at the start of therapy and following any dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specifically lettered]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. However, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response, use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after exogenous administration. Benefits of supplementation to the mother should be weighed against potential risks of excess intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (mg/kg) for metabolic disorders and ESRD. See Section 3 for specific instructions [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Information not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for use in ESRD patients undergoing dialysis. Dose adjustments in ESRD are based on pre-dialysis trough levels. Safety of oral forms in renal failure is not established due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Information not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with metabolic disorders, levocarnitine facilitates the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly cleared [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Efficacy:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis showed significant increases in plasma levocarnitine. Trough levels rose from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg in ESRD patients demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough levocarnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Note:** While levocarnitine effectively increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the impact on specific clinical outcomes and signs/symptoms of deficiency in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Special Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
