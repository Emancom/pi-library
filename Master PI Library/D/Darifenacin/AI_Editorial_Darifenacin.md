# Editorial Product Information: Darifenacin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. 
- **Note:** At the time of this editorial’s composition, only the Australian PI was provided for review. Information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., US, Europe, Canada, Japan) is marked as "Not specified in available sources" where specific regional differences could not be verified from the provided text.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official, current PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Darifenacin (as hydrobromide).
- **Chemical Class:** Selective muscarinic M3 receptor antagonist.
- **Brand Name(s):** Enablex [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** G04BD10.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Modified-release tablets containing 7.5 mg or 15 mg of darifenacin.
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aspen Pharmacare Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Overactive Bladder (OAB):** For the symptomatic treatment of urgency, urge urinary incontinence, or increased micturition frequency.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** 
    - Initial dose: 7.5 mg once daily.
    - Maintenance/Titration: If symptom relief is insufficient after two weeks of therapy, the dose may be increased to 15 mg once daily.
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Tablets should be swallowed whole with liquid and must not be chewed, divided, or crushed.
    - May be administered with or without food.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to darifenacin hydrobromide or any of the product’s excipients.
- Urinary retention.
- Gastric retention.
- Uncontrolled narrow-angle glaucoma.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None reported in available sources.
- **General Warnings and Precautions:**
    - **Angioedema:** Discontinue immediately if edema of the tongue, hypopharynx, or larynx occurs, or if the patient experiences difficulty breathing.
    - **Narrow-angle Glaucoma:** Use with caution in patients receiving treatment for this condition.
    - **Gastrointestinal Disorders:** Use with caution in patients with hiatus hernia, gastro-oesophageal reflux (GERD), or those taking medications (e.g., oral bisphosphonates) that can cause oesophagitis. Caution is also advised in patients with a risk of decreased GI motility or obstructive disorders (e.g., pyloric stenosis).
    - **Bladder Outflow Obstruction:** Use with caution due to the risk of urinary retention.
    - **Autonomic Neuropathy:** Caution is advised in this population.
    - **Cardiac Disease:** Caution is recommended when prescribing antimuscarinics to patients with pre-existing cardiac conditions.
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Standard monitoring for antimuscarinic effects (e.g., constipation, dry mouth). Monitor prothrombin time if co-administered with warfarin and digoxin levels if co-administered with digoxin.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events are typically mild to moderate and most frequently occur during the first two weeks of treatment.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Dry mouth, constipation. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Headache, abdominal pain, dyspepsia, nausea, urinary tract infection (UTI), pharyngitis, nasal dryness. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Asthenia, accidental injury, diarrhoea, vomiting, dizziness, insomnia, somnolence, dysgeusia, abnormal vision/blurred vision, dry eyes, dry skin, pruritus, hypertension, peripheral oedema, dyspnoea, cough, flatulence, mouth ulceration, urinary retention, bladder pain, erectile dysfunction, weight gain, increased AST/ALT. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Not specified in available sources. |
| **Not Known/Post-market** | Angioedema, generalised hypersensitivity reactions, palpitations. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Angioedema and urinary retention.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Potent inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, itraconazole, miconazole, nefazodone, ritonavir) significantly increase darifenacin exposure. **Daily dose must not exceed 7.5 mg** when co-administered. Moderate inhibitors (e.g., erythromycin, fluconazole) do not require dose adjustment.
    - **CYP2D6 Inhibitors:** Inhibitors (e.g., paroxetine, cimetidine, fluoxetine) may increase exposure and the risk of adverse events; use with caution.
    - **CYP2D6 Substrates:** Caution is required when darifenacin is used with drugs having a narrow therapeutic window (e.g., flecainide, thioridazine, tricyclic antidepressants).
    - **P-glycoprotein:** Darifenacin is a substrate for P-gp.
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **Warfarin:** Monitor prothrombin time; however, no alteration in effect was observed in trials.
    - **Digoxin:** Small increase in digoxin exposure; therapeutic drug monitoring is recommended when starting, stopping, or changing darifenacin doses.
    - **Antimuscarinic Agents:** Concomitant use with other anticholinergics (including anti-Parkinson agents and tricyclic antidepressants) may increase the frequency/severity of side effects like dry mouth and constipation.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B3.** 
    - Animal studies show darifenacin crosses the placenta and causes developmental delays (delayed ossification, post-implantation loss) at maternotoxic doses. 
    - Clinical safety in human pregnancy is not established; use only if maternal benefit outweighs fetal risk.
- **Breastfeeding:** Excreted in the milk of rats; human excretion is unknown. Exercise caution when administering to nursing women.
- **Fertility:** No significant effects on fertility were observed in animal (rat) studies.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established. Not recommended for use in children.
- **Elderly Use:** No special dosage requirements; however, a trend for decreased clearance (19% per decade) has been noted.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment required.
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild (Child-Pugh A):** No adjustment required.
    - **Moderate (Child-Pugh B):** Daily dose must not exceed 7.5 mg.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Not recommended for use [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Competitive, selective antagonist of the muscarinic M3 receptor. The M3 receptor is the primary subtype controlling bladder detrusor muscle contraction.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Increases bladder capacity and volume threshold for unstable contractions; reduces the frequency of urgency and incontinence episodes. Shows 9- to 59-fold selectivity for M3 over M1, M2, M4, and M5 receptors.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid and complete (>98%) following oral administration. Significant first-pass metabolism results in bioavailability of approximately 15% (7.5 mg) to 19% (15 mg).
    - **Distribution:** Lipophilic base, 98% protein-bound (alpha-1-acid-glycoprotein). Negligible blood-brain barrier penetration.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively hepatic via CYP2D6 and CYP3A4. Three primary routes: monohydroxylation, ring opening, and N-dealkylation.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 60% excreted in urine (only 3% as unchanged drug) and 40% in faeces. Estimated half-life is 12.8 to 18.7 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** Evaluated in four 12-week, randomised, placebo-controlled, multicentre trials.
- **Outcomes:** Significant reductions in weekly incontinence episodes, micturition frequency, and urgency episodes compared to placebo. 
- **Onset:** Significant improvement in incontinence episodes was observed within the first 2 weeks of therapy.
- **Long-term Safety:** Sustained efficacy and similar safety profile observed in studies up to 12 months in duration.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Conditions:** Protect from light.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** 
    - **Australian:** Schedule 4 (S4) – Prescription Only Medicine.
    - **Other Jurisdictions:** Not specified in available sources.
