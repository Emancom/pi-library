# Editorial Product Information: Denosumab

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information provided by the Australian (TGA) regulatory authority.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **denosumab** (specifically the **CORORA** brand) for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, safety warnings, and administration guidelines into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource only. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Denosumab (rch)
- **Chemical Class:** Fully human IgG2 monoclonal antibody
- **Brand Name(s):** **CORORA** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** M05BX04
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 60 mg/1 mL solution for injection in a single-use pre-filled syringe. [Australian]
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Amgen Australia Pty Ltd (Distributed by Arrotex Pharmaceuticals) [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Postmenopausal Osteoporosis:** Treatment of osteoporosis in postmenopausal women to reduce the risk of hip, vertebral, and non-vertebral fractures. [Australian]
- **Bone Mass Increase (Men with Osteoporosis):** Treatment to increase bone mass in men with osteoporosis who are at an increased risk of fracture. [Australian]
- **Androgen Deprivation Therapy (ADT) Induced Bone Loss:** Treatment to increase bone mass in men with osteopenia receiving ADT for non-metastatic prostate cancer. [Australian]
- **Glucocorticoid-Induced Osteoporosis (GIOP):** Treatment to increase bone mass in men and women at increased risk of fracture due to long-term systemic glucocorticoid therapy. [Australian]

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** A single 60 mg subcutaneous injection administered once every 6 months. [Australian]
- **Supplementation:** All patients should receive adequate daily supplementation of calcium (1,000 mg) and vitamin D (at least 400 IU; 800 IU recommended for men in clinical trials). [Australian]
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Must be administered by an individual trained in injection techniques.
    - Inject into the thigh, abdomen, or upper arm.
    - Before administration, allow the pre-filled syringe to reach room temperature (up to 25°C) to reduce discomfort.
    - Inspect for particulate matter; the solution should be clear and colourless to slightly yellow.
    - Do not shake the syringe excessively.
- **Discontinuation:** If treatment is discontinued, clinicians should consider transitioning the patient to an alternative antiresorptive therapy to prevent rapid loss of bone mineral density (BMD). [Australian]
- **Missed Doses:** If a dose is missed, administration should occur as soon as convenient. Subsequent doses should then be scheduled every 6 months from the date of the last injection. [Australian]

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypocalcaemia:** Pre-existing hypocalcaemia must be corrected before initiating therapy. [Australian]
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known clinically significant hypersensitivity to denosumab, CHO-derived proteins, or any excipients (sorbitol, acetate, polysorbate 20). [Australian]
- **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated in women who are pregnant or attempting to become pregnant. [Australian]

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypocalcaemia:** Significant risk of severe symptomatic hypocalcaemia, especially in patients with severe renal impairment (CrCl < 30 mL/min) or those on dialysis. Monitoring of calcium levels is recommended prior to each dose and within the first two weeks of initiation in predisposed patients. [Australian]
- **Osteonecrosis of the Jaw (ONJ):** Reported rarely in osteoporosis patients. Risk factors include poor oral hygiene, invasive dental procedures, cancer, or concomitant therapies (e.g., chemotherapy, corticosteroids). A dental examination is recommended prior to treatment in patients with risk factors. [Australian]
- **Multiple Vertebral Fractures (MVF) Post-Discontinuation:** Risk of new vertebral fractures increases following treatment cessation, particularly in those with a history of prior vertebral fractures. [Australian]
- **Atypical Femoral Fractures:** Low-trauma subtrochanteric or diaphyseal fractures have been reported. Evaluate patients presenting with new thigh, hip, or groin pain. [Australian]
- **Skin Infections:** Increased incidence of cellulitis leading to hospitalisation has been observed. Patients should seek prompt medical attention for signs of skin infection. [Australian]
- **Concomitant Medications:** Do not use **CORORA** concurrently with other denosumab-containing products (e.g., **Xgeva**). [Australian]

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Back pain, arthralgia, hypertension, nasopharyngitis, pain in extremity, and osteoarthritis. [Australian]
- **Common (1–10%):** Hypercholesterolaemia, eczema (including dermatitis), bronchitis, dizziness, and alopecia. [Australian]
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Injection site reactions (including pain), lichenoid drug eruptions. [Australian]
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Pancreatitis, drug-related hypersensitivity (rash, urticaria, facial swelling, anaphylaxis), and **DRESS** syndrome. [Australian]
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Severe symptomatic hypocalcaemia (potentially fatal), osteonecrosis of the jaw, atypical femoral fractures, and hypersensitivity vasculitis (very rare). [Australian]

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Denosumab does not affect the pharmacokinetics of drugs metabolised by CYP3A4 (tested with midazolam). [Australian]
- **Calcimimetics:** Concomitant use (e.g., cinacalcet) may increase the risk and severity of hypocalcaemia. [Australian]
- **Laboratory Tests:** No known interference with laboratory or diagnostic tests. [Australian]

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Contraindicated. Denosumab can cross the placenta and may cause foetal harm (e.g., lymph node absence, abnormal bone growth).
- **Contraception:** Women of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for at least 5 months after the last dose. [Australian]
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if denosumab is excreted in human milk. A decision must be made to discontinue breastfeeding or the drug, considering the benefit to the mother and the infant. [Australian]
- **Fertility:** No human data available. Animal studies showed no effect on female fertility or male reproductive organs. [Australian]

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not indicated for paediatric patients. Safety and efficacy in those $\le$ 18 years have not been established. Use in children with open growth plates may impair bone growth and inhibit tooth eruption. Hypercalcaemia has occurred in paediatric patients with osteogenesis imperfecta. [Australian]
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy were observed in patients $\ge$ 65 years. No dosage adjustment required. [Australian]
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment necessary. However, patients with severe impairment (CrCl < 30 mL/min) or on dialysis are at significantly higher risk for hypocalcaemia. [Australian]
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Safety and efficacy have not been studied. [Australian]

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Denosumab is a RANK ligand (RANKL) inhibitor. By binding to RANKL, it prevents the activation of the RANK receptor on osteoclast surfaces, thereby inhibiting osteoclast formation, function, and survival. This leads to reduced bone resorption and increased BMD. [Australian]
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Bioavailability is 61% following SC administration. $C_{max}$ occurs at approximately 10 days (range 2–28 days). [Australian]
    - **Distribution:** No evidence of accumulation with 6-monthly dosing.
    - **Metabolism/Elimination:** Expected to follow immunoglobulin clearance pathways, degrading into small peptides and amino acids. Mean half-life is 26 days. [Australian]
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Reduces the bone resorption marker CTX by ~70% within 6 hours, reaching 85% reduction by day 3. Effects are reversible upon treatment cessation. [Australian]

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **FREEDOM Study:** A 3-year trial in 7,808 postmenopausal women. Denosumab 60 mg Q6M reduced the risk of new vertebral fractures by 68%, hip fractures by 40%, and non-vertebral fractures by 20%. [Australian]
- **ADAMO Study:** A 1-year trial in men with osteoporosis. Showed a 4.8% increase in lumbar spine BMD compared to placebo. [Australian]
- **GIOP Study:** A 12-month trial comparing denosumab to risedronate. Denosumab demonstrated non-inferiority (and superiority in some subgroups) regarding BMD increases at the lumbar spine. [Australian]

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store in a refrigerator at 2°C to 8°C. [Australian]
- **Stability:** Once removed from the refrigerator, the product may be stored at room temperature (up to 25°C) in the original carton but must be used within 30 days. [Australian]
- **Handling:** Do not freeze. Protect from direct light. Do not shake. [Australian]

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Medicine)** [Australian]
- **Genotoxicity/Carcinogenicity:** Not evaluated in long-term animal studies; however, as a recombinant protein, it is unlikely to have genotoxic potential. [Australian]
