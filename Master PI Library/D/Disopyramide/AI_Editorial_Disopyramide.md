# Editorial Product Information: Disopyramide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory guidance for legal compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Disopyramide (as base)
- **Chemical Class:** Class Ia antiarrhythmic; sodium channel blocker
- **Brand Name(s):** Rythmodan [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** C01BA03
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Hard capsules: 100 mg (Available) [Australian]
    - Hard capsules: 150 mg (Not currently marketed) [Australian]
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pharmaco (Australia) Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Ventricular Arrhythmias:** Indicated for the management of documented, life-threatening ventricular arrhythmias, such as sustained ventricular tachycardia [Australian].
- **Important Usage Limitations:** 
    - Not recommended for "lesser" (non-life-threatening) arrhythmias due to proarrhythmic potential [Australian].
    - Treatment of asymptomatic ventricular premature contractions (VPCs) should be avoided [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Normal Renal/Hepatic Function:** The recommended total daily dosage is 300 mg to 800 mg, administered in no fewer than three equal divided doses [Australian].
### Dosing by Indication
- **Initiation:** Start at 400 mg to 600 mg per day. The dose should be progressively reduced (by no more than 100 mg/day) until a final maintenance dose of 300 mg to 400 mg per day is reached [Australian].
- **Electroconversion:** It is advisable to commence treatment 1–2 days prior to the procedure to potentially achieve sinus rhythm beforehand [Australian].
### Administration Instructions
- Doses must be divided equally throughout the day [Australian].
### Missed Dose Instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Cardiogenic shock [Australian].
- Second- or third-degree atrioventricular (AV) block (in the absence of a functional pacemaker) [Australian].
- Bundle branch block associated with first-degree AV block [Australian].
- Double block (e.g., left posterior/anterior hemiblock with right bundle branch block) [Australian].
- Pre-existing long QT interval [Australian].
- Severe sinus node dysfunction [Australian].
- Cardiac insufficiency, unless the insufficiency is secondary to a cardiac arrhythmia [Australian].
- Concomitant use with other antiarrhythmics or medications known to provoke ventricular arrhythmias or *torsades de pointes* [Australian].
- Known hypersensitivity to disopyramide [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Negative Inotropic Effects:** Disopyramide may cause or worsen congestive heart failure (CHF) or produce severe hypotension, particularly in patients with structural heart disease or uncompensated CHF [Australian].
- **Proarrhythmic Risk:** Like other Class I antiarrhythmics, disopyramide may aggravate existing arrhythmias or induce new ones (proarrhythmia). This is a particular risk in structural heart disease [Australian].
- **Anticholinergic Activity:** Due to its atropine-like effects, use with extreme caution (or avoid) in patients with:
    - Urinary retention (unless catheterised/treated) [Australian].
    - Glaucoma (measure intraocular pressure prior to start in those with family history) [Australian].
    - Myasthenia gravis (may precipitate crisis) [Australian].
    - Risk of paralytic ileus (especially in the elderly) [Australian].
- **Myocardial Infarction:** Prophylactic use following myocardial infarction is not recommended and may be hazardous based on Class I antiarrhythmic trial data (e.g., CAST) [Australian].
- **Blood Glucose:** Significant hypoglycemia has been reported; monitor glucose levels closely in the elderly, malnourished, diabetics, or those with renal impairment [Australian].
- **Potassium Imbalance:** Hypokalemia may render the drug ineffective, while hyperkalemia may enhance toxic effects. Monitor and correct potassium levels [Australian].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** 
    - ECG monitoring for QRS widening (>20%) or QT prolongation (>20%); treatment must be discontinued if these thresholds are exceeded [Australian].
    - Monitor for new AV block or double block [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Very Common (>10%)
- **Anticholinergic:** Dry mouth, acute urinary retention (especially in prostatism), constipation [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, indigestion, vomiting, diarrhoea, flatulence, anorexia, bad taste [Australian].

### Common (1–10%)
- **Anticholinergic:** Blurred vision, dry eyes/nose/throat, urinary hesitation/frequency [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypotension (with/without CHF), increased CHF, conduction disturbances, proarrhythmic effects, oedema, dyspnoea, chest pain [Australian].
- **Dermatologic:** Rash, pruritus, urticaria, photosensitisation [Australian].
- **General:** Dizziness, vertigo, drowsiness, profuse sweating [Australian].
- **Laboratory:** Raised SGOT (AST) levels [Australian].

### Uncommon/Rare (<1%)
- Dysuria, headache, peripheral paraesthesia, fatigue, insomnia, transitory psychosis, hypoglycaemia (can be severe), neutropenia, agranulocytosis, and cholestatic jaundice [Australian].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Cardiac arrest, *torsades de pointes*, ventricular fibrillation, and anaphylactic-type reactions culminating in shock [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **QT Prolonging Agents:** Contraindicated with Class I, II (beta-blockers), III (amiodarone, sotalol), and IV (verapamil, diltiazem) antiarrhythmics. Also contraindicated with TCAs, IV erythromycin, vincamine, and sultopride [Australian].
- **Torsadogenic Drugs:** Co-administration with astemizole, cisapride, pentamidine, pimozide, and terfenadine is not recommended [Australian].
- **Anticholinergics:** Potentiation of atropine-like effects when used with phenothiazines or other anticholinergics [Australian].

### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Substrate:** Disopyramide is metabolised by CYP3A4. Inhibitors (e.g., macrolides, azole antifungals) may increase serum levels; inducers (e.g., rifampicin, phenytoin) may decrease levels [Australian].
- **Competitive Inhibition:** May increase levels of other CYP3A4 substrates such as theophylline, HIV protease inhibitors, and warfarin [Australian].
- **Protein Binding:** Roxithromycin may displace disopyramide from protein binding sites [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B2.** Animal studies show no teratogenicity but lower weaning weights. 
    - Limited human data. Disopyramide stimulates uterine contractions and crosses the placenta. Use only if the benefit outweighs the hazard [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Disopyramide is excreted into breast milk. If treatment is essential, alternative infant feeding methods should be used [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies show minimal evidence of impaired fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not approved for paediatric use [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of cognitive disorders and paralytic ileus. Monitor closely for anticholinergic toxicity [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Reduce dosage in patients with CrCl < 40 mL/min. 
    - Recommended regimen: 200 mg loading dose followed by 100 mg maintenance doses [Australian].
    - Interval (based on CrCl): 15-40 mL/min (q10h); 5-15 mL/min (q20h); 1-5 mL/min (q30h) [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Reduce dosage by 25%. Monitor ECG and plasma concentrations closely [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Class Ia sodium channel blocker with membrane-stabilising effects. Depresses phase 4 diastolic depolarisation, slows conduction velocity (atria, AV node, Purkinje fibres, ventricles), and prolongs action potential duration/refractory period [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Exhibits negative inotropic effects and significant anticholinergic activity (approx. 10% as potent as atropine) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly and almost completely absorbed (Peak: ~1 hour) [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** 30–40% protein bound to alpha-1-acid glycoprotein (concentration-dependent); Vd is ~80 L [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolised via N-dealkylation to mono-N-dealkylated disopyramide (retains some activity) [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** 50–70% excreted unchanged in urine. Elimination half-life is 4.5–8.2 hours (prolonged in renal/cardiac impairment) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Trials:** No specific registration trial data provided in the source PI.
- **Reference Studies:** Mention of the Cardiac Arrhythmia Suppression Trial (CAST) regarding the hazards of Class I agents in post-MI patients [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
