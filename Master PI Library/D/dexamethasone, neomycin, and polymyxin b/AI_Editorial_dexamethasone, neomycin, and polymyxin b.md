# Editorial Product Information: Dexamethasone, Neomycin Sulfate, and Polymyxin B Sulfate (Ophthalmic)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data into a concise, structured format.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing or dispensing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory guidance for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Dexamethasone (corticosteroid), Neomycin Sulfate (aminoglycoside antibiotic), and Polymyxin B Sulfate (cyclic lipopeptide antibiotic).
- **Brand Names:** Maxitrol (original), various generics [US].
- **ATC Code:** S01CA01
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Ophthalmic Ointment containing:
    - Dexamethasone 0.1% (1 mg/g)
    - Neomycin Sulfate equivalent to 3.5 mg neomycin base/g
    - Polymyxin B Sulfate 10,000 units/g [US]
- **Route of Administration:** Topical Ophthalmic.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** NuCare Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (relabeler); Bausch + Lomb (original manufacturer) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Steroid-responsive inflammatory ocular conditions** where bacterial infection or a risk of such infection exists [US].
- **Inflammatory conditions** of the palpebral and bulbar conjunctiva, cornea, and anterior segment of the globe (e.g., allergic conjunctivitis, acne rosacea, superficial punctate keratitis, herpes zoster keratitis, iritis, cyclitis) where the risk of steroid use is accepted to reduce edema and inflammation [US].
- **Chronic anterior uveitis**.
- **Corneal injury** resulting from chemical, radiation, or thermal burns, or penetration of foreign bodies [US].

**Antibacterial Spectrum:**
This product is active against: *Staphylococcus aureus*, *Escherichia coli*, *Haemophilus influenzae*, *Klebsiella/Enterobacter* species, *Neisseria* species, and *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*. It does **not** provide adequate coverage against *Serratia marcescens* or *Streptococci* (including *Streptococcus pneumoniae*) [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Adult Dosing:** Apply a small amount (approximately 1/2 inch or 1.5 cm ribbon) into the conjunctival sac(s) up to three or four times daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    1. Tilt the head back.
    2. Pull down the lower lid to create a "V" pocket.
    3. Place the ointment in the pocket without touching the tube tip to the eye, eyelids, or any other surface to maintain sterility.
    4. Look downward before closing the eye [US].
- **Prescription Limits:** The initial prescription should not exceed 8 g. Refills should only be authorized after clinical evaluation (magnification/slit lamp) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Viral Diseases:** Epithelial herpes simplex keratitis (dendritic keratitis), vaccinia, varicella, and most other viral infections of the cornea and conjunctiva [US].
- **Mycobacterial Infection:** Tuberculosis of the eye [US].
- **Fungal Diseases:** Fungal infections of ocular structures [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known or suspected hypersensitivity to any component of the medication. (Note: Hypersensitivity to the antibiotic components occurs at a higher rate than other ingredients) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Route Warning:** **NOT FOR INJECTION.** For topical ophthalmic use only [US].
- **Ocular Pressure/Glaucoma:** Prolonged use (10 days or longer) may result in increased intraocular pressure (IOP), potentially leading to glaucoma, optic nerve damage, and visual field defects. IOP must be monitored routinely during extended therapy [US].
- **Cataracts:** Posterior subcapsular cataract formation may occur with prolonged corticosteroid use [US].
- **Secondary Infections:** Corticosteroids may suppress the host immune response, increasing the hazard of secondary ocular infections (fungal or bacterial). In acute purulent conditions, steroids may mask or enhance existing infection [US].
- **Corneal Thinning:** In diseases causing thinning of the cornea or sclera, topical steroids have been known to cause perforations [US].
- **Delayed Healing:** Use following cataract surgery may delay healing and increase the incidence of bleb formation [US].
- **Neomycin Sensitization:** Topical aminoglycosides may cause cutaneous sensitization. If a sensitivity reaction (erythema, itching, urticaria, anaphylaxis) occurs, discontinue use immediately [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are categorized by the component to which they are typically attributed:

| Category | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Antibiotic Component** | Allergic sensitizations (local or generalized) [US]. |
| **Steroid Component** | Elevation of IOP, glaucoma (infrequent), optic nerve damage, posterior subcapsular cataract formation, delayed wound healing [US]. |
| **Secondary Infection** | Fungal invasion of the cornea (high risk with long-term use), secondary bacterial ocular infection [US]. |
| **Other/Post-marketing** | Keratitis, conjunctivitis, corneal ulcers, conjunctival hyperemia, headache, and Stevens-Johnson syndrome [US]. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Specific Interactions:** Not specified in available sources [US].
- **General Note:** Clinicians should consider the potential for additive effects if used concurrently with other systemic aminoglycosides or topical corticosteroids.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not explicitly assigned in provided source [US]. 
    - **Data:** Dexamethasone is teratogenic in mice and rabbits. Prolonged/repeated use in human pregnancy is linked to increased risk of intra-uterine growth retardation. 
    - **Recommendation:** Use only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus. Infants born to mothers receiving substantial doses should be monitored for hypoadrenalism [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Systemically administered corticosteroids appear in human milk. It is unknown if topical ophthalmic administration results in sufficient systemic absorption to produce detectable quantities in milk. Exercise caution when prescribing to nursing women [US].
- **Fertility:** Long-term animal studies are lacking. Polymyxin B has been reported to impair equine sperm motility, but effects on human fertility are unknown [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in paediatric patients have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall clinical differences in safety or effectiveness have been observed between the elderly and younger adult patients [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources. Systemic absorption is expected to be low, but caution is advised in severe impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Dexamethasone:** Suppresses the inflammatory response (edema, fibrin deposition, capillary dilation, leucocyte migration, and scar formation) [US].
    - **Neomycin Sulfate:** Provides bactericidal action by inhibiting protein synthesis in susceptible bacteria [US].
    - **Polymyxin B Sulfate:** Increases the permeability of the bacterial cell membrane, leading to cell death [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** The relative potency of the steroid depends on molecular structure and release from the vehicle. Specific ADME (Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Elimination) parameters for this topical combination are not specified [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Summary:** Formal clinical trial data for this fixed-dose combination were not provided in the source documentation [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store between 15°C and 25°C (59°F–77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Keep the tube tightly closed when not in use. Avoid contamination of the tip. Keep out of reach of children [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only (Prescription only) [US].
- **Packaging:** Typically supplied in 3.5 g sterile aluminum tubes [US].
