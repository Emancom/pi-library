# Editorial Product Information: Doxepin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following jurisdiction's official Product Information:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: Deptran (Doxepin hydrochloride) Product Information.
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of doxepin for healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Doxepin (as hydrochloride).
- **Chemical Class**: Dibenzoxepin psychotherapeutic agent; Tricyclic Antidepressant (TCA).
- **Brand Names**: Deptran [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: N06AA12.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**: 
    - **Capsules**: 10 mg and 25 mg [Australian].
    - **Tablets**: 50 mg and 75 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Viatris (Alphapharm Pty Ltd) [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Major Depression**: Treatment of major depressive disorder.
    - *Note*: In the Australian jurisdiction, the 50 mg and 75 mg tablet strengths are indicated specifically for **maintenance treatment** only and are not intended for treatment initiation [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Dose Range**: 30 mg to 300 mg daily.
- **Initial Titration**: Usually administered in a three-times-daily (TID) regimen. 
- **Maintenance**: Once an optimum dose is established, it may be administered as a single daily dose (maximum 150 mg for a single dose) [Australian].

### Dosing by Severity
- **Mild Symptoms**: Initiate at 30 mg daily; many patients respond well to 30–50 mg daily [Australian].
- **Moderate to Severe Symptoms**: Recommended initiation at ≤75 mg daily in divided doses. 
- **Severely Ill Patients**: May require up to 300 mg daily in divided doses to achieve clinical response. Treatment should not be initiated at this high dosage [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- If insomnia is a prominent symptom, a larger portion of the daily dose (up to 150 mg) may be administered in the evening [Australian].
- If daytime drowsiness occurs, the dose should be reduced or shifted toward evening administration.

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity**: Known allergy to doxepin, other tricyclic antidepressants (TCAs), or any excipients [Australian].
- **Glaucoma**: Known or suspected glaucoma [Australian].
- **Urinary Retention**: Tendency towards urinary retention, particularly in elderly patients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Suicidality and Clinical Worsening**: All patients, especially those under 25 years of age, should be monitored for suicidal ideation and behaviour. This risk is highest during the first few months of therapy or following dose adjustments. Prescriptions should be for the smallest feasible quantity to reduce overdose risk [Australian].
- **Cardiac Effects**: TCAs may cause dose-related arrhythmias and conduction disturbances (e.g., QRS widening, PR interval prolongation). Use with extreme caution in patients with pre-existing cardiac disease [Australian].
- **Seizure Disorder**: Use with caution in patients with a history of seizures or increased propensity for convulsions [Australian].
- **Bipolar Disorder**: Screening for bipolar risk is essential; antidepressants may precipitate a manic or mixed episode [Australian].
- **Serotonin Syndrome**: Risk is increased when used concomitantly with buprenorphine or other serotonergic agents. Monitor for mental status changes, autonomic instability, and neuromuscular abnormalities [Australian].
- **Angle-Closure Glaucoma**: Pupillary dilation (mydriasis) may trigger an acute attack in patients with anatomically narrow angles [Australian].
- **Abrupt Cessation**: Withdrawal symptoms (nausea, headache, malaise) may occur upon sudden discontinuation after prolonged use [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Note: Frequencies are not explicitly categorised in the source document but are presented by system.*

- **Anticholinergic**: Dry mouth, blurred vision, constipation, urinary retention, and rarely, elevated intraocular pressure [Australian].
- **Central Nervous System**: **Very Common**: Drowsiness (often diminishes with continued therapy). **Infrequent**: Confusion, disorientation, agitation, hallucinations, tremors, ataxia, seizures, and extrapyramidal symptoms [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular**: Hypotension, hypertension, tachycardia. Very rarely, ECG changes (QRS/PR widening) [Australian].
- **Allergic**: Skin rash, facial oedema, photosensitisation, pruritus [Australian].
- **Haematologic**: Eosinophilia; rare reports of bone marrow depression (agranulocytosis, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia) [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal**: Nausea, vomiting, indigestion, taste disturbances, anorexia, aphthous stomatitis [Australian].
- **Endocrine**: Changes in libido, testicular swelling, gynaecomastia, galactorrhoea, and fluctuations in blood sugar levels [Australian].
- **Other**: Weight gain, sweating, dizziness, alopecia, and jaundice [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **MAO Inhibitors**: Risk of severe reactions or death. Discontinue MAOIs at least 14 days prior to starting doxepin [Australian].
- **CYP2D6 Inhibitors**: Drugs such as quinidine, cimetidine, and certain SSRIs (fluoxetine, sertraline, paroxetine) can significantly increase TCA plasma levels. Monitor TCA levels if co-administered [Australian].
- **Fluoxetine**: Due to its long half-life, a 5-week washout may be required before starting a TCA [Australian].
- **Hepatic Enzyme Inducers**: Barbiturates, phenytoin, and carbamazepine may decrease doxepin levels while doxepin may increase levels of phenytoin or carbamazepine [Australian].
- **Sympathomimetics**: Potentiation of cardiovascular effects of adrenaline, noradrenaline, and amphetamines [Australian].
- **Anticholinergics**: Additive effects leading to paralytic ileus, hyperpyrexia, or acute glaucoma [Australian].
- **Guanethidine**: Doxepin doses >150 mg/day may block the antihypertensive effects of guanethidine [Australian].
- **Alcohol**: Potentiation of CNS depressant effects and increased risk in overdose [Australian].
- **Tolazamide**: Report of severe hypoglycaemia when used concurrently [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: **Category C [Australian]**. Not associated with birth defects, but may interfere with central neurotransmission in animals. Withdrawal symptoms have been reported in neonates following prolonged maternal use. Use only if clinical benefit outweighs potential risk.
- **Breastfeeding**: Doxepin and its active metabolite (desmethyldoxepin) are excreted in breast milk (milk:plasma ratio 0.4:1.5). Reports of apnoea and drowsiness in nursing infants exist; breastfeeding is not recommended [Australian].
- **Fertility**: No data available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and efficacy in children <18 years have not been established. Use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Elderly Use**: Require careful dose adjustment and monitoring due to increased risk of anticholinergic effects and urinary retention [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment**: Use with caution [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Dose reduction may be required; use with caution [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Not fully elucidated. Primarily believed to inhibit the reuptake of noradrenaline (norepinephrine) at the synaptic cleft, thereby increasing adrenergic activity [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics**: Exhibits anticholinergic, antiserotonergic, and antihistamine properties on smooth muscle [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Readily absorbed from the GI tract; undergoes extensive first-pass metabolism to desmethyldoxepin [Australian].
    - **Distribution**: Widely distributed; highly protein-bound. Crosses the blood-brain and placental barriers [Australian].
    - **Metabolism**: Demethylation, hydroxylation, N-oxidation, and glucuronide conjugation in the liver [Australian].
    - **Elimination**: Half-life is 8–24 hours for doxepin; longer for the active metabolite. Primary excretion is via urine as metabolites [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Capsules (10 mg, 25 mg)**: Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Tablets (50 mg, 75 mg)**: Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life**: Refer to packaging or regional registers [Australian].
