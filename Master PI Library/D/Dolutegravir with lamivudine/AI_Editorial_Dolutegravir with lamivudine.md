# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions’ Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine Injection for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (generic name); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. 
- **Chemical Class:** A naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Administration:** Administer via the intravenous route only. Parenteral products should be visually inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use [US].
- **Metabolic Disorders:**
    - **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg, administered as a slow 2-to-3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
    - **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose is often given, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours (q3h or q4h), and never less than every 6 hours (q6h) [US].
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been used [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow 2-to-3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].
- **Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target 35 to 60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. These may occur within minutes of IV administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate medical management [US].
- **Oral Formulation Caution:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of the oral formulation in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) which are normally excreted in urine [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Periodic monitoring of plasma carnitine levels is recommended to guide dosing and ensure levels remain within the therapeutic range [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **General:** Transient nausea and vomiting are common. Body odor and gastritis are reported less frequently [US].
- **Neurologic:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Trial Data (ESRD Patients):** In clinical trials of ESRD patients on dialysis, the following adverse events occurred at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) [US]:
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%), chest pain (6–15%).
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%), abdominal pain (5–21%).
    - **Systemic/Other:** Injection site reaction (27–38%), pain (21–35%), headache (3–37%), infection (10–24%), fever (5–12%), pharyngitis (15–27%).
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and severe respiratory distress (bronchospasm/laryngeal edema) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended that INR levels be monitored closely after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned a letter grade in the source, but noted that reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm [US].
    - **Recommendation:** Should be used during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased milk concentrations of carnitine following exogenous administration. The benefits to the mother should be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders applies to infants and children as indicated in the general dosing section [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the available source; clinical trial data focused on adult ESRD and metabolic disorder populations [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Specific dosing is provided for ESRD patients on dialysis (see Section 3). Caution is advised regarding the use of oral formulations in this population due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Dosing adjustments for hepatic impairment are not specified in available sources [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg IV three times per week showed that trough levocarnitine concentrations increased from a baseline of ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. 
- **Dose-Ranging Trials:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses in ESRD patients demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks. While increases in carnitine concentration were confirmed, the effects on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population have not been fully determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial, as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription only) [US].
