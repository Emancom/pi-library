# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a concise, structured format.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing in any particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine [(R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt].
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Generic) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic management of patients with metabolic defects resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** In cases of severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose administered over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours, and never less frequently than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported administered dose is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustments:** Clinical judgment and trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels should guide adjustments. Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration and Monitoring
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Visual Inspection:** Parenteral products should be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration before use [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known:** There are currently no known contraindications for levocarnitine injection [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These can occur within minutes of administration, particularly in ESRD patients. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation):** While this PI concerns the injectable form, clinicians should note that chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with and without pre-existing seizure disorders. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (≥5% frequency in ESRD trials):**
    - **General:** Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (30–35%), headache (22%), chest pain (12%), fever (7–12%), abdominal pain (5–21%) [US].
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%) [US].
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%) [US].
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%) [US].
    - **Neurological:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%) [US].
- **Less Frequent/Post-marketing:**
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis and transient body odor [US].
    - **Neurological:** Seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels regularly following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not specified by letter, but reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm]. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after exogenous administration. In nursing mothers, the risk of excess carnitine intake by the infant must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg). Safety and efficacy in children for other indications are as per adult recommendations where applicable [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific dose adjustments for the elderly are not detailed in the source, though general caution in dose selection is standard clinical practice [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** In ESRD patients on dialysis, the IV route is preferred to avoid accumulation of toxic metabolites associated with high-dose oral therapy [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This process allows for fatty acid oxidation and subsequent energy production in tissues, particularly skeletal and cardiac muscle [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** Clinical trials in patients with ESRD on maintenance hemodialysis demonstrated that IV administration of levocarnitine (10, 20, and 40 mg/kg) significantly increased plasma carnitine concentrations. Pre-dialysis (trough) levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to between 116 and 371 µmol/L after 12 weeks of therapy [US].
- **Note:** While IV levocarnitine effectively increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the definitive effect on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, cardiomyopathy) in this specific population has not been fully determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
- **Preservatives:** The formulation contains no preservatives; discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only [US].
