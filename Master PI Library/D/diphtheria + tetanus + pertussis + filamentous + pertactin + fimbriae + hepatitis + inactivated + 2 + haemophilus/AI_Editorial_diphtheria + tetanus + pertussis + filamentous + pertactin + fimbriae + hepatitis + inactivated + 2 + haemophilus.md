# Editorial Product Information: diphtheria + tetanus + pertussis + filamentous + pertactin + fimbriae + hepatitis + inactivated + 2 + haemophilus

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **diphtheria + tetanus + pertussis + filamentous + pertactin + fimbriae + hepatitis + inactivated + 2 + haemophilus**
- Chemical class: Combined bacterial and viral vaccine (diptheria and tetanus toxoids; acellular pertussis antigens including pertussis toxoid, filamentous haemagglutinin, pertactin, fimbriae types 2 & 3; recombinant hepatitis B surface antigen; inactivated poliovirus types 1–3; Haemophilus influenzae type b capsular polysaccharide conjugated to protein)
- ATC code: J07CA09 (vaccines, bacterial and viral combined) — as stated in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intramuscular injection only
- Do not include brand or trade names in clinical use

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Primary and booster immunisation of infants and toddlers from the age of 6 weeks against:
  - Corynebacterium diphtheriae (diphtheria)
  - Clostridium tetani (tetanus)
  - Bordetella pertussis (whooping cough; acellular components)
  - Hepatitis B virus
  - Poliovirus (inactivated) — types 1, 2 and 3
  - Invasive disease caused by Haemophilus influenzae type b (Hib)
- Use should follow official national immunisation recommendations.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing
- Primary vaccination:
  - A primary series may consist of either 2 or 3 doses.
  - Doses may be given from age 6 weeks.
  - Interval between doses: at least 1 month.
  - Can be used whether or not a monovalent hepatitis B dose was given at birth; if a HepB dose is required before 6 weeks, use monovalent HepB for that earlier dose.
  - May be used in mixed hexavalent/pentavalent/hexavalent schedules as per official recommendations.
- Booster vaccination:
  - After a 3-dose primary series: a booster may be given provided it is at least 6 months after the last priming dose.
  - After a 2-dose primary series: a booster should be given at least 6 months after the last priming dose.
  - May be used as a booster in children primed with another hexavalent vaccine; follow official recommendations for timing.

### Administration instructions
- Route: Intramuscular injection only. Do not administer intravascularly, intradermally or subcutaneously.
- Recommended injection sites:
  - Anterolateral thigh (preferred for infants under 1 year)
  - Deltoid muscle of the upper arm (older infants/toddlers as per local practice)
- Preparation and handling (clinical-use instructions provided):
  - Shake pre-filled syringe gently to obtain a homogeneous, whitish cloudy suspension.
  - Inspect visually prior to administration for particulate matter or variation in appearance; discard if observed.
  - Fit the needle firmly to the pre-filled syringe (rotate by one quarter turn).
  - Single use only; discard any unused contents.
- Coadministration:
  - May be administered concomitantly with pneumococcal conjugate vaccine, rotavirus vaccines, MMR and varicella-containing vaccines, meningococcal B and C conjugate vaccines.
  - When coadministered with other injectable vaccines, give at separate injection sites and preferably in separate limbs.
  - Do not mix with other vaccines or parenteral products in the same syringe.

### Titration / missed dose
- Not applicable (vaccine schedule guidance only). If a scheduled dose is missed, follow official immunisation catch-up guidance. Not specified further in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity (anaphylaxis or severe immediate hypersensitivity) to any component of the vaccine, or to any residues from production including traces of glutaraldehyde, formaldehyde, neomycin, streptomycin, polymyxin B, or bovine serum albumin.
- History of an anaphylactic reaction after a previous administration of this vaccine or a vaccine containing any of the same components, constituents or residues.
- Encephalopathy of unknown aetiology occurring within 7 days after prior vaccination with a pertussis-containing vaccine — in such cases, discontinue further pertussis vaccination and continue the remainder of the immunisation course with diphtheria, tetanus, hepatitis B, poliomyelitis and Hib vaccines.
- Uncontrolled neurologic disorder or uncontrolled epilepsy: do not administer pertussis-containing vaccine until the condition is treated and stabilised and benefits clearly outweigh risks.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- **Do not administer by intravascular, intradermal or subcutaneous routes.**
- Appropriate medical treatment and supervision must be readily available in case of rare anaphylactic reactions following vaccination.
- Acute or febrile illness: postpone vaccination in children with moderate to severe acute disease, with or without fever. Minor illness or low-grade fever is not a contraindication.
- Prior severe events after pertussis vaccination:
  - Carefully consider further pertussis-containing vaccination if any of the following occurred after a prior pertussis-containing vaccine:
    - Temperature ≥ 40°C within 48 hours (not attributable to another cause)
    - Collapse or shock-like state (hypotonic-hyporesponsive episode [HHE]) within 48 hours
    - Persistent inconsolable crying ≥ 3 hours within 48 hours
    - Convulsions with or without fever within 3 days
  - There may be situations (e.g., high pertussis incidence) where benefits may outweigh risks.
- Neurological adverse events:
  - If Guillain–Barré syndrome occurred within 6 weeks of receipt of a prior tetanus-toxoid-containing vaccine, weigh benefits and risks before administering any vaccine containing tetanus toxoid.
  - History of febrile convulsions, family history of convulsions, or SIDS does not contraindicate vaccination, but individuals with prior febrile convulsions should be closely followed as febrile convulsions may occur within 2–3 days post-vaccination.
- Premature infants:
  - Limited data (n=111 preterm infants in clinical trials) indicate vaccine can be given to preterm infants; immune responses were generally similar but may be lower.
  - For very premature infants (born ≤28 weeks gestation), consider the risk of apnoea and the need for respiratory monitoring for 48–72 hours after primary immunisation, particularly in those with prior respiratory immaturity. Do not withhold or delay vaccination because benefits are high.
- Immunocompromised individuals:
  - Immunogenicity may be reduced by immunosuppressive therapy or immunodeficiency. Postpone vaccination until after treatment/disease resolution where possible; however, vaccination is recommended in chronic immunodeficiency (e.g., HIV) even if antibody responses may be limited. Specific data in immunocompromised children are limited.
- Blood disorders:
  - Use caution in individuals with thrombocytopenia or bleeding disorders because IM administration may cause bleeding.
- Laboratory tests:
  - Hib capsular polysaccharide antigen may be excreted in urine and can cause false-positive urine tests for at least 30 days post-vaccination; confirmatory tests should be used to diagnose Hib infection during this time.
- Coadministration considerations:
  - When given concomitantly with pneumococcal conjugate vaccine (PCV13), an increased rate of fever was observed following the booster dose in the second year of life compared with the primary series; most fevers were mild/moderate (<39.5°C) and transient (≤2 days).
- Immunosuppressive therapy: may interfere with expected immune response.
- Use in women of child-bearing age: Not intended for administration to women of child-bearing age in available sources (see pregnancy/lactation).

### Solicited adverse reaction incidence from pivotal phase 3 trials (percentages within 5 days post-vaccination)
- Injection site (pooled study ranges):
  - Pain: ~70–74%
  - Erythema: ~44.6–69.0% (varied by study)
  - Swelling: ~34.5–56.9% (varied by study)
- Systemic (pooled study ranges):
  - Irritability: ~80.7–91.6%
  - Crying: ~72.3–89.3%
  - Somnolence: ~71.6–86.1%
  - Pyrexia (≥38.0°C): ~33.2–73.8% (varied by study and schedule)
  - Decreased appetite: ~43.3–65.8%
  - Vomiting: ~21.5–32.8%
- In a study where given with PCV13 as booster, fever ≥38.0°C occurred in 52.5% of children vs. 33.1–40.7% during the primary series; fever ≥39.5°C occurred in 3.7% post-booster vs. 0.2–0.8% post-primary.

### Description of selected adverse reactions
- Extensive swelling of the vaccinated limb: typically begins 24–72 hours after vaccination, may be accompanied by erythema, warmth, tenderness or pain, resolves spontaneously within 3–5 days; risk appears to increase with the number of prior acellular pertussis-containing vaccine doses (greater after 4th and 5th doses).
- Very premature infants (≤28 weeks gestation): apnoea has been reported; monitor respiratory status for 48–72 hours after primary immunisation.
- Reporting: healthcare professionals should report suspected adverse reactions to appropriate national reporting systems.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Concomitant vaccines:
  - May be given at the same time as pneumococcal conjugate vaccine, rotavirus vaccines, MMR, varicella-containing vaccines, meningococcal B and C conjugate vaccines.
  - When coadministered with PCV13, higher fever rates were observed following the toddler booster dose.
  - When coadministered with other injectable vaccines, administer at separate injection sites and preferably separate limbs.
  - Do not mix with other vaccines in the same syringe.
- Immunosuppressive therapy:
  - May reduce immune response to vaccine; consider postponing vaccination until immunosuppression has ended where possible.
- Pharmacokinetic interactions: Not specified in available sources.
- Pharmacodynamic interactions: Possible interference with immune response when given during immunosuppressive therapy (see above).

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Immunisation with the combined vaccine induces production of antibodies directed against diphtheria and tetanus toxoids, acellular pertussis antigens (including pertussis toxoid, filamentous haemagglutinin, pertactin, fimbriae types 2 and 3), hepatitis B surface antigen, poliovirus types 1–3 (inactivated), and Haemophilus influenzae type b capsular polysaccharide (PRP) conjugated to protein. This antibody response mediates protection against the respective diseases.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Seroprotection/seroresponse rates after primary series and after booster were demonstrated in pivotal clinical trials (see Section 12). A protective immune response may not be elicited in all vaccinees.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Not specified in available sources (standard kinetic parameters for vaccines such as absorption/distribution/metabolism/excretion are not provided in the available PI text).

## 12. Clinical Trials
### Pivotal studies — design summary
- Immunogenicity and safety were evaluated in four pivotal phase 3 clinical studies with infants enrolled between 43–99 days of age. Study comparators included pentavalent and hexavalent combination vaccines and monovalent hepatitis B as required by study design.
- Primary vaccination schedules studied included:
  - 2, 4 months (without HepB at birth)
  - 2, 3, 4 months (without HepB at birth)
  - 2, 4, 6 months (with and without HepB at birth)
- Booster doses studied:
  - Booster at 11–12 months after a 2-dose primary series (2,4 months)
  - Booster at 12 months after a 3-dose primary series (2,3,4 months)
  - In some studies a non-study booster at 15 months after a 3-dose primary series (2,4,6 months)

### Key immunogenicity outcomes (one month after primary series)
- High seroprotection/response rates were observed across antigens after primary vaccination:
  - Anti-diphtheria (≥0.01 IU/mL): ~98–99.8%
  - Anti-tetanus (≥0.01 IU/mL): 100%
  - Pertussis components (vaccine response): PT ~98.1–99.4%; FHA ~88.1–89.0%; PRN ~80.3–86.7%; FIM ~90.0–97.2%
  - Polio neutralising titres (≥1:8 dilution): Type 1 ~93.8–100%; Type 2 ~98.0–100%; Type 3 ~92.9–100%
  - Anti-HBs (≥10 mIU/mL): with HepB at birth ~99.8%; without HepB at birth ~97.8–98.1% (data from the available sources)
  - Anti-PRP (Hib) (≥0.15 µg/mL): ~96.6–98.4%

### Booster responses (pre-booster and one month post-booster)
- Substantial increases in antibody levels and high seroprotection/response rates were observed one month after booster doses across studies for diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis antigens, polio, hepatitis B and Hib.
- Pre-booster waning and robust post-booster responses were documented (specific study-by-study percentages reported in pivotal trials; see Section 7 for safety data and the described immunogenicity ranges).

### Safety findings in clinical trials
- Safety profile in pivotal trials: most common reactions included irritability, crying, somnolence, injection site reactions (pain, erythema, swelling), fever (≥38°C), decreased appetite and vomiting.
- Solicited local and systemic reactions occurred commonly within 5 days of vaccination; rates varied by study and schedule (see Section 7 for detailed percentages).

### Special population study notes
- Preterm infants (n=111 in clinical program): immune responses generally similar to overall population though may be lower; safety data limited; observed risk of apnoea in very premature infants.
