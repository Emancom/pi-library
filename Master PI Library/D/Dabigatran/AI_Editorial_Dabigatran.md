# Editorial Product Information: Dabigatran

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based solely on the official Product Information (PI) from the **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of dabigatran etexilate for healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, dosing, and safety information into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI. Prescribers should consult the current official PI approved in their specific region for definitive prescribing information. Information for other major jurisdictions (e.g., US, European, Canadian, Japanese) was not provided in the source context and is therefore not included in this synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Dabigatran etexilate (as mesilate), a small-molecule prodrug of dabigatran.
- **Chemical Class:** Direct Thrombin Inhibitor (DTI).
- **Brand Name(s):** **AKM Dabigatran** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** B01AE07.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Hard capsules: 75 mg, 110 mg, and 150 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pharmacor Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Prevention of Venous Thromboembolism (VTE):** Indicated for adult patients following elective total hip or knee replacement surgery [Australian-approved].
- **Prevention of Stroke and Systemic Embolism:** Indicated for patients with non-valvular atrial fibrillation (NVAF) who possess at least one additional stroke risk factor [Australian-approved].
- **Treatment and Secondary Prevention of VTE:** Indicated for the treatment of acute deep vein thrombosis (DVT) and pulmonary embolism (PE), and the prevention of recurrent DVT and PE in adults [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Instructions
- Capsules must be swallowed whole with a full glass of water.
- **Warning:** Do not chew, break, or open capsules; doing so may significantly increase the risk of bleeding by increasing bioavailability (up to 75% increase in a single dose) [Australian].
- May be taken with or without food.

### Indication-Specific Dosing (Adults)
- **Post-Orthopaedic Surgery VTE Prophylaxis:** 
    - 220 mg once daily (taken as two 110 mg capsules). 
    - *Initiation:* Single 110 mg capsule within 1–4 hours of surgery completion, followed by 220 mg daily once hemostasis is secured.
    - *Duration:* 10 days (knee replacement); 28–35 days (hip replacement).
- **Prevention of Stroke and Systemic Embolism (NVAF):** 
    - 300 mg daily (taken as 150 mg twice daily). Treatment is typically lifelong.
- **Treatment and Recurrence Prevention of DVT/PE:** 
    - 300 mg daily (taken as 150 mg twice daily) following at least 5 days of parenteral anticoagulant treatment.

### Missed Dose
- **VTE Prophylaxis (Ortho):** Continue the daily dose at the usual time the next day; do not double the dose.
- **NVAF/DVT/PE:** A missed dose may be taken up to 6 hours before the next scheduled dose. If less than 6 hours remain, the dose should be omitted. Do not double the dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to dabigatran, dabigatran etexilate, or any excipients.
- Severe renal impairment (CrCL <30 mL/min).
- Active, clinically significant bleeding or a known bleeding diathesis.
- High-risk lesions or conditions (e.g., active GI ulceration, malignant neoplasms, recent brain/spinal injury, recent ophthalmic/brain surgery, known esophageal varices, vascular aneurysms).
- Concomitant use of other anticoagulants (e.g., UFH, LMWH, warfarin, apixaban), except during therapy switching or when UFH is used to maintain catheter patency or during AF ablation.
- Presence of a prosthetic heart valve requiring anticoagulation.
- Hepatic impairment or liver disease expected to impact survival.
- Concomitant treatment with systemic ketoconazole, cyclosporin, itraconazole, dronedarone, or the fixed-dose combination glecaprevir/pibrentasvir [Australian].
- Simultaneous initiation of oral verapamil in postoperative orthopaedic patients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Haemorrhagic Risk:** Dabigatran significantly increases the risk of bleeding, which may be fatal. Close monitoring for signs of anemia or occult bleeding is required, especially in high-risk patients (e.g., age $\geq$ 75, moderate renal impairment). 
- **Reversal Agent:** **Idarucizumab** is a specific reversal agent available for life-threatening or uncontrolled bleeding [Australian].
- **Anticoagulant-Related Nephropathy (ARN):** Post-marketing reports link anticoagulant use to acute kidney injury, particularly in patients with altered glomerular integrity or excessive anticoagulation.
- **Surgery and Interventions:** Temporary discontinuation may be necessary. For elective procedures, stop 24 hours prior if CrCL is normal, or 2–4 days prior if renal function is impaired or bleeding risk is high.
- **Spinal/Epidural Anesthesia:** Risk of spinal/epidural hematoma leading to permanent paralysis. Do not administer dabigatran within 2 hours of catheter removal.
- **Myocardial Infarction:** A slight, non-statistically significant increase in MI risk was observed in some trials compared to warfarin.
- **Active Cancer:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in DVT/PE patients with active cancer.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Bleeding is the most clinically significant adverse effect.
- **Very Common (>10%):** 
    - Bleeding (general): ~14% in short-term ortho surgery; ~16.6% yearly in NVAF.
    - Dyspepsia, nausea, and upper abdominal pain (especially in the RE-LY trial) [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** 
    - Anemia, epistaxis, gastrointestinal hemorrhage, diarrhea, and vomiting.
    - Abnormal hepatic function tests.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** 
    - Thrombocytopenia, hypersensitivity reactions (rash, pruritus), hematuria.
- **Rare (<0.1%):** 
    - Intracranial hemorrhage, angioedema, anaphylactic reaction, esophageal ulcer, alopecia.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **P-gp Inhibitors (Increase exposure):** 
    - *Contraindicated:* Ketoconazole, itraconazole, cyclosporin, dronedarone, glecaprevir/pibrentasvir.
    - *Caution/Dose Reduction:* Amiodarone, quinidine, verapamil, clarithromycin, ticagrelor. 
    - *Staggered Dosing:* If starting verapamil, dabigatran should be administered at least 2 hours before verapamil [Australian].
- **P-gp Inducers (Decrease exposure):** Concomitant use with rifampicin, St John’s Wort, or carbamazepine generally should be avoided as they reduce dabigatran plasma levels.
- **Anticoagulants and Antiplatelets:** Concomitant use with ASA, clopidogrel, or NSAIDs (especially those with half-lives >12 hours) increases bleeding risk.
- **Gastric pH-Elevating Agents:** Pantoprazole may reduce dabigatran AUC by ~30%, but this is not considered clinically significant in clinical trials.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Anticoagulants may cause placental hemorrhage. Dabigatran should not be used during pregnancy unless clearly necessary.
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. While no human data exist, animal studies show the presence of dabigatran in milk.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed decreased implantations at high doses, but the effect on human fertility is unknown.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - *Moderate (CrCL 30–50 mL/min):* Reduced dosing may be required (e.g., 150 mg daily for VTE ortho prophylaxis; 220 mg daily for NVAF/DVT treatment) [Australian].
    - *Severe (CrCL <30 mL/min):* Contraindicated.
- **Elderly (Age $\geq$ 75):** Increased risk of bleeding due to age-related renal decline. Dosing should be reduced to 220 mg daily (110 mg BID) for NVAF and DVT/PE indications [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not recommended in patients with liver disease expected to impact survival or with liver enzymes >2x ULN.
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in patients <18 years of age.
- **Body Weight:** Limited data for weight <50 kg. Trough levels may be 20% higher in low-weight patients and 20% lower in patients >100 kg.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Dabigatran is a potent, competitive, reversible, direct inhibitor of thrombin. It inhibits free thrombin, fibrin-bound thrombin, and thrombin-induced platelet aggregation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - *Absorption:* Rapid; Tmax 0.5–2.0 hours. Bioavailability is ~6.5%.
    - *Distribution:* Low protein binding (34–35%).
    - *Metabolism:* Prodrug dabigatran etexilate is hydrolyzed by esterases to active dabigatran. Not metabolized by CYP450.
    - *Elimination:* Predominantly renal (85%). Terminal half-life is 12–14 hours in healthy subjects, extending to 27 hours in severe renal impairment.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **RE-MODEL/RE-NOVATE:** Demonstrated non-inferiority to enoxaparin in VTE prevention following knee and hip replacement.
- **RE-LY:** Demonstrated that dabigatran 150 mg BID was superior to warfarin in reducing stroke/systemic embolism in NVAF, while 110 mg BID was non-inferior. Both doses reduced intracranial hemorrhage compared to warfarin.
- **RE-COVER/RE-COVER II:** Confirmed non-inferiority to warfarin in the treatment of acute DVT/PE.
- **RE-MEDY/RE-SONATE:** Demonstrated efficacy in the long-term prevention of recurrent DVT/PE.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C.
- Keep in the original package (blister or bottle with desiccant) to protect from moisture.
- Shelf life: Refer to expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
