# Editorial Product Information: Daratumumab

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview of daratumumab, synthesised for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Daratumumab
- **Chemical Class:** Immunoglobulin G1 kappa (IgG1κ) human monoclonal antibody directed against the CD38 antigen.
- **Brand Name:** **Darzalex** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** L01FC01
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Concentrated solution for intravenous (IV) infusion (preservative-free).
    - 100 mg/5 mL single-use vial [Australian].
    - 400 mg/20 mL single-use vial [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous infusion (after dilution).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Janssen-Cilag Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Newly Diagnosed Multiple Myeloma:**
    - In combination with bortezomib, thalidomide, and dexamethasone (VTd) for patients eligible for autologous stem cell transplant (ASCT) [Australian].
    - In combination with bortezomib, melphalan, and prednisone (VMP) for patients ineligible for ASCT [Australian].
    - In combination with lenalidomide and dexamethasone (Rd) for patients ineligible for ASCT [Australian].
- **Relapsed or Refractory Multiple Myeloma:**
    - In combination with bortezomib and dexamethasone (Vd), lenalidomide and dexamethasone (Rd), or carfilzomib and dexamethasone (Kd) for patients who have received at least one prior therapy [Australian].
    - As monotherapy for patients who have received at least three prior lines of therapy (including a proteasome inhibitor [PI] and an immunomodulatory agent) or who are double-refractory to a PI and an immunomodulatory agent [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults ≥ 18 years)
The standard dose is **16 mg/kg** actual body weight.

### Dosing by Regimen and Phase
Dosing schedules vary significantly by combination partner and treatment phase (Induction, Consolidation, or Maintenance). Detailed schedules are provided in the source documents:
- **With VTd (4-week cycles):** Weekly for Weeks 1–8; Every 2 weeks for Weeks 9–16. Consolidation: Every 2 weeks for 4 doses [Australian].
- **With VMP (6-week cycles):** Weekly for Weeks 1–6; Every 3 weeks for Weeks 7–54; Every 4 weeks from Week 55 onwards [Australian].
- **With Rd or Monotherapy (4-week cycles):** Weekly for Weeks 1–8; Every 2 weeks for Weeks 9–24; Every 4 weeks from Week 25 onwards [Australian].
- **With Vd (3-week cycles):** Weekly for Weeks 1–9; Every 3 weeks for Weeks 10–24; Every 4 weeks from Week 25 onwards [Australian].
- **With Kd (4-week cycles):** For the 20/56 mg/m² carfilzomib regimen, the first dose (16 mg/kg) is split: 8 mg/kg on Day 1 and Day 2 of Week 1. Subsequently, 16 mg/kg weekly (Weeks 2–8), every 2 weeks (Weeks 9–24), then every 4 weeks [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Dilution:** Must be diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride.
- **Initial Rate:** 50 mL/hour (except for Week 1 split dose or subsequent infusions where no reactions occurred).
- **Escalation:** The rate may be increased by 50 mL/hour every hour, to a maximum of 200 mL/hour, provided no infusion-related reactions (IRRs) occur.
- **Concomitant Medications:**
    - **Pre-infusion:** Administer 1–3 hours prior: Corticosteroids (e.g., dexamethasone 20 mg or methylprednisolone 100 mg), Antipyretics (e.g., paracetamol 500–1000 mg), and Antihistamines (e.g., diphenhydramine 25–50 mg).
    - **Post-infusion:** Administer oral corticosteroids for 2 days following infusion. For patients with COPD, consider short- and long-acting bronchodilators and inhaled corticosteroids.
    - **Prophylaxis:** Consider anti-viral prophylaxis for herpes zoster virus reactivation.

### Missed Dose Instructions
If a dose is missed, administer as soon as possible and adjust the schedule to maintain the prescribed treatment interval.

## 4. Contraindications
- Severe hypersensitivity (e.g., anaphylaxis) to daratumumab or any excipients listed in the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Infusion-Related Reactions (IRRs):** Occur in approximately half of all patients, most frequently during the first infusion. Symptoms include bronchospasm, hypoxia, dyspnoea, and hypertension. Severe reactions, including myocardial infarction and ocular adverse reactions (e.g., acute angle-closure glaucoma), have been reported. **Management:** Interrupt infusion for any grade; permanently discontinue for Grade 4 or recurrent Grade 3 reactions.
- **Neutropenia and Thrombocytopenia:** Daratumumab increases the incidence of haematological toxicities. Monitor CBC periodically. Dose delays may be required, but no dose reductions are recommended.
- **Hepatitis B Virus (HBV) Reactivation:** Fatal cases have occurred. Screen all patients before initiation. Monitor infected patients during and for 6 months after treatment.
- **Laboratory Interference:**
    - **Indirect Coombs Test:** Daratumumab binds to CD38 on RBCs, causing false-positive results that may persist for 6 months. Perform type and screen prior to initiation.
    - **Disease Monitoring:** May interfere with SPE and IFE assays, potentially causing false-positive results in patients with IgG kappa myeloma protein.
- **Hereditary Fructose Intolerance:** Certain formulations contain sorbitol and should be avoided in patients with HFI unless strictly necessary [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Category
- **Very Common (>10%):** Infusion-related reactions, fatigue, nausea, diarrhoea, constipation, pyrexia, cough, dyspnoea, upper respiratory tract infection, peripheral oedema, back pain, arthralgia, anaemia, neutropenia, and thrombocytopenia.
- **Common (1–10%):** Pneumonia, bronchitis, influenza, sepsis, hypertension, hypokalaemia, hyperglycaemia, insomnia, dizziness, and muscle spasms.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Cytomegalovirus infection, COVID-19.
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Anaphylactic reaction, HBV reactivation.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Pneumonia, sepsis, and severe IRRs (including anaphylaxis) are the most significant clinical risks.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic:** No formal drug-drug interaction studies were performed. Clinical assessments in combination with lenalidomide, pomalidomide, thalidomide, bortezomib, and dexamethasone showed no clinically relevant interactions [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamic:** Interference with blood compatibility testing (Indirect Coombs test) is a major pharmacodynamic interaction requiring clinical management.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category C:** Daratumumab is an IgG1 monoclonal antibody known to cross the placenta after the first trimester. Use only if the potential benefit outweighs the risk to the foetus. 
    - **Contraception:** Women of reproductive potential must use effective contraception during and for 3 months after treatment.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if daratumumab is excreted in human milk. A decision should be made to discontinue breastfeeding or the drug, considering the importance of the drug to the mother.
- **Fertility:** No data available regarding effects on male or female fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in patients ≤ 17 years [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustments required for patients ≥ 65 years. However, a higher incidence of serious adverse events (e.g., sepsis, pneumonia) was observed in older populations [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment necessary for patients with mild to severe renal impairment based on population PK analyses [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment necessary for patients with mild hepatic impairment. Data are limited for moderate to severe impairment, but IgG1 molecules are not metabolised via hepatic pathways [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Daratumumab is a CD38-directed monoclonal antibody. It induces cell death via complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC), antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC), antibody-dependent cellular phagocytosis (ADCP), and apoptosis. It also has immunomodulatory effects by decreasing CD38+ immunosuppressive cells (Tregs, Bregs, MDSCs).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Consistent with initial distribution into the plasma compartment; central volume of distribution ~57 mL/kg.
    - **Metabolism/Elimination:** Clearance decreases with increasing dose and repeated dosing. Steady state is reached at approximately 5 months. Estimated terminal half-life is ~18 days following saturation of target-mediated clearance.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Treatment leads to a significant decrease in absolute counts and percentages of NK cells and a subset of T cells in peripheral blood and bone marrow.

## 11. Clinical Trials
Key pivotal trials included:
- **Study MMY3006 (CASSIOPEIA):** DVTd in ASCT-eligible newly diagnosed MM. Showed improved sCR at Day 100 and reduced risk of progression by 53% [Australian].
- **Study MMY3007 (ALCYONE):** DVMP in ASCT-ineligible newly diagnosed MM. Showed a 50% reduction in risk of progression or death [Australian].
- **Study MMY3008 (MAIA):** DRd in ASCT-ineligible newly diagnosed MM. Demonstrated significant improvement in PFS (Median PFS 61.9 vs 34.4 months) [Australian].
- **Study MMY3004 (CASTOR) & MMY3003 (POLLUX):** Demonstrated efficacy in relapsed/refractory MM when combined with Vd or Rd, respectively [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Vials:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (refrigerate). Do not freeze. Protect from light.
- **Diluted Solution:** Contains no preservative. Use immediately or store for up to 24 hours at 2°C to 8°C, followed by up to 15 hours at room temperature (15°C to 25°C).
