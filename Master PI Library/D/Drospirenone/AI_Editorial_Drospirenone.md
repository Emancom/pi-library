# Editorial Product Information: Estradiol and Drospirenone

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Estradiol (as hemihydrate) and Drospirenone.
- **Chemical Class:** Estradiol is a naturally occurring estrogen; Drospirenone is a synthetic progestogen (spironolactone analogue).
- **Brand Names:** ANGELIQ 1/2 [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** G03FA17 (Estradiol and progestogen, fixed combinations).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Film-coated tablets:** 1.0 mg estradiol and 2.0 mg drospirenone [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Bayer Australia Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hormone Replacement Therapy (HRT):** Short-term treatment of climacteric syndrome (symptoms of estrogen deficiency) in postmenopausal women with an intact uterus [Australian].
- **Underlying Conditions:** Indicated for menopause caused by natural cessation, hypogonadism, castration, or primary ovarian failure [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** One tablet taken orally once daily [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Tablets should be swallowed whole with liquid, with or without food [Australian].
    - Treatment is continuous; each 28-day blister pack should be followed immediately by the next without a break [Australian].
    - Tablets should ideally be taken at the same time each day [Australian].
- **Initiation of Therapy:**
    - Women not currently on HRT or those switching from continuous combined products may start at any time [Australian].
    - Women switching from sequential combined HRT should start the day after completing their previous scheduled bleeding [Australian].
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** 
    - Take the missed tablet as soon as possible. 
    - If more than 24 hours have elapsed, do not take an extra tablet. 
    - If multiple tablets are missed, vaginal bleeding may occur [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
Treatment must not be initiated or continued if any of the following are present:
- **Undiagnosed vaginal bleeding.**
- **Known or suspected breast cancer.**
- **Known or suspected sex steroid-influenced malignancies** (or premalignant conditions).
- **Liver tumors** (history or presence; benign or malignant).
- **Severe hepatic disease** (until liver function markers return to normal).
- **Severe renal disease** (history or presence; until renal function markers return to normal).
- **Acute arterial thromboembolism** (e.g., myocardial infarction, stroke).
- **Active deep venous thrombosis (VVT)**, thromboembolic disorders, or a documented history of these conditions.
- **High risk of venous or arterial thrombosis.**
- **Severe hypertriglyceridemia.**
- **Pregnancy or lactation.**
- **Hypersensitivity** to active substances or any excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning (Cardiovascular and Dementia Risk):**
    - Estrogens/progestogens should not be used for the prevention of cardiovascular disease or dementia [Australian].
    - The WHI study indicated increased risks of stroke, DVT, myocardial infarction, and invasive breast cancer in postmenopausal women (50–79 years) [Australian].
    - The WHIMS study reported an increased risk of probable dementia in women aged 65 or older [Australian].
- **Venous Thromboembolism (VTE):** Risk is highest in the first year of treatment. Risk factors include obesity, personal/family history, and increasing age. Discontinue treatment if VTE is suspected [Australian].
- **Breast Cancer:** HRT is associated with an increased risk of breast cancer diagnosis, which increases with duration of use and returns to baseline within approximately 5 years of discontinuation [Australian].
- **Endometrial Cancer:** In women with an intact uterus, the addition of drospirenone effectively opposes the risk of endometrial hyperplasia/carcinoma induced by unopposed estrogens [Australian].
- **Hepatic Effects:** If jaundice or deterioration of liver function occurs, HRT should be discontinued [Australian].
- **Hyperkalemia:** Drospirenone has antimineralocorticoid properties. While studies in mild-to-moderate renal impairment showed no significant potassium increase, a theoretical risk exists for patients with baseline potassium in the high-normal range who use potassium-sparing medications [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Periodic medical examinations (including pelvic organs, breasts, and blood pressure) are required [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequencies are based on Phase III clinical studies (n=1532) [Australian].

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Breast pain (reported by approx. 1 in 5 women). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Abdominal pain/bloating, asthenia, pain in extremity, nausea, headache, mood swings, hot flushes, nervousness, benign breast neoplasms, breast enlargement, leucorrhoea, uterine fibroid enlargement. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Migraine, hypertension, venous thrombosis, superficial thrombophlebitis, weight gain, hyperlipidaemia, muscle cramps, insomnia, dizziness, libido decreased, dyspnoea, alopecia, vulvovaginitis, ovarian cysts. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Arterial and venous thromboembolic events, breast cancer, and (rarely) benign or malignant liver tumors [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions (CYP450):**
    - **Inducers:** Drugs like phenytoin, barbiturates, carbamazepine, rifampicin, and St. John’s wort may increase the clearance of sex hormones, leading to reduced efficacy and/or breakthrough bleeding [Australian].
    - **Inhibitors:** Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, erythromycin) may increase serum concentrations of estradiol and drospirenone [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **Antihypertensives:** Drospirenone may have an additive effect on blood pressure reduction when used with antihypertensive medications [Australian].
    - **Potassium-Sparing Agents:** Concomitant use with NSAIDs or other potassium-sparing drugs may slightly increase serum potassium, particularly in diabetic women [Australian].
    - **Anticoagulants:** HRT may antagonise the effectiveness of anticoagulants [Australian].
- **Alcohol:** Acute ingestion may increase circulating estradiol levels [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B3.** 
    - Contraindicated during pregnancy. If pregnancy occurs, treatment must be discontinued immediately. Animal data show dose-dependent feminisation of male fetuses and virilisation of female fetuses at high exposures [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Small amounts of hormones are excreted in breast milk, and estrogens can decrease the quantity and quality of milk [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Not specified in available sources as the product is indicated for postmenopausal women.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not indicated for use in children or adolescents [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Data from the WHIMS study suggest increased dementia risk if HRT is initiated in women aged 65 or older [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Mild to moderate (CLcr 30–80 mL/min):** Drospirenone exposure increases by approximately 37% in moderate impairment, but this is not considered clinically relevant [Australian].
    - **Severe:** Contraindicated [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild to moderate:** Drospirenone is generally well tolerated, though terminal half-life increases by approximately 1.8-fold in moderate impairment [Australian].
    - **Severe:** Contraindicated [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Estradiol:** Replaces the loss of endogenous estrogen production in menopausal women, alleviating vasomotor and organic symptoms.
    - **Drospirenone:** A progestogen that inhibits the proliferative effects of estrogen on the endometrium. It possesses anti-androgenic and antimineralocorticoid properties [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Drospirenone is 76–85% bioavailable. Estradiol has low oral bioavailability (~5%) due to extensive first-pass metabolism. Food does not significantly affect the absorption of either component [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Drospirenone binds to albumin but not SHBG; Estradiol binds specifically to SHBG and non-specifically to albumin [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Drospirenone is metabolised by CYP3A4. Estradiol is metabolised into estrone and estriol primarily in the liver [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Drospirenone metabolites are excreted in urine and faeces (t½ ~40 hours). Estradiol metabolites are excreted primarily via urine [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Vasomotor Symptoms (Report AR98):** ANGELIQ 1/2 demonstrated a significant reduction in the frequency and severity of hot flushes compared to placebo over 16 weeks [Australian].
- **Bone Mineral Density (Report AR99):** Treatment showed a significant increase in hip bone mineral density compared to placebo over 104 weeks in both osteopenic and non-osteopenic patients [Australian].
- **Endometrial Safety (Report A02827):** After 1 year, atrophic/inactive endometrium was achieved in 71–77% of women across various drospirenone doses [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Expiry date as marked on the pack.
- **Handling:** Keep out of reach of children [Australian].
