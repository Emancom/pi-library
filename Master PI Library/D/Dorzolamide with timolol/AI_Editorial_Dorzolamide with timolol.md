# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: **United States (FDA)**.
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data from the provided Product Information.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (L-carnitine); a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Dosing Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) and monitor blood chemistries and vital signs weekly or monthly [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow bolus into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Titration:** Dose adjustments may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma carnitine concentrations (normal range: 40 to 50 µmol/L) [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and monitor if re-administration is attempted [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been reported [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral vs. IV):** The safety of high-dose **oral** levocarnitine has not been established in patients with severe renal impairment or ESRD, as it may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide). This is less relevant for the IV route which bypasses gastrointestinal metabolism [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disorders.

### Common Adverse Effects (≥5%) [US]
| Body System | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain, fever, flu syndrome |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, abdominal pain, anorexia |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia |
| **Neurologic** | Dizziness, paresthesia |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough, dyspnea, rhinitis |

### Other Reported Effects
- **Rare/Less Frequent:** Body odor, gastritis, and seizures [US].
- **Serious:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. 
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** US Category C (not explicitly lettered in PI, but noted as "use only if clearly needed"). Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations following exogenous administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See Section 3 for weight-based dosing; safety and efficacy are supported by use in inborn errors of metabolism [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources; generally, dose selection should be cautious [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. High-dose oral therapy is cautioned in this group due to metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces). 
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg post-dialysis), trough carnitine levels increased from ~20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks. 
- **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks.
- **Clinical Outcomes Note:** While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations is proven, the effect on specific clinical outcomes in ESRD patients has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only. Scheduled under NDC 36000-368-10 [US].
