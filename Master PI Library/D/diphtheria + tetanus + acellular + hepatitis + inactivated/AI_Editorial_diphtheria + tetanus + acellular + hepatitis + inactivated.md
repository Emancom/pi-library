# Editorial Product Information: diphtheria + tetanus + acellular + hepatitis + inactivated

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **diphtheria + tetanus + acellular + hepatitis + inactivated**
- Chemical class: Combination vaccine containing diphtheria and tetanus toxoids, acellular pertussis antigens, recombinant hepatitis B surface antigen, and inactivated poliovirus antigens.
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration: Intramuscular injection.
- DO NOT include brand or trade names, dosage form physical descriptions, or PBS/schedule listings.

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources.
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI if stated: A revision date of 5/2026 is reported in one source document. Other source PI revision dates: Not specified in available sources.
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Active immunization against:
  - Diphtheria
  - Tetanus
  - Pertussis (whooping cough)
  - Infection caused by known subtypes of hepatitis B virus
  - Poliomyelitis
- Population and schedule-related indication:
  - Approved as a 3-dose primary series in infants born to hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg)-negative mothers.
  - May be administered as early as 6 weeks of age through 6 years of age (prior to the seventh birthday).
- Jurisdictional variations: Not specified beyond the above in available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing
- Primary series (infants): Three intramuscular injections of 0.5 mL each given at 2, 4, and 6 months of age. Intervals of 6 to 8 weeks between doses are acceptable, with 8 weeks preferred.
- Earliest administration: First dose may be given as early as 6 weeks of age.
- Upper age limit for use: May be given through 6 years of age (prior to the seventh birthday).

### Dosing by prior vaccination status
- Children who have received 1 or 2 prior doses of a diphtheria–tetanus–acellular pertussis (DTaP) vaccine manufactured identically to the DTaP component contained in this combination vaccine may receive this vaccine to complete the first three doses of the DTaP series when they are also scheduled to receive the other vaccine components.
- Data are not available on safety and effectiveness if prior DTaP doses were from a different manufacturer.
- This vaccine may be used to complete a hepatitis B vaccination series following 1 or 2 prior doses of another hepatitis B vaccine (monovalent or combination), including products from other manufacturers, in infants born of HBsAg‑negative mothers. Limited safety data exist when a birth (dose at or shortly after birth) hepatitis B dose was previously administered; caution is advised.
- This vaccine may be used to complete the first three doses of inactivated poliovirus vaccine (IPV) in children who received 1 or 2 prior IPV doses from a different manufacturer and who are scheduled to receive the other vaccine components.

### Booster immunization guidance
- Children who complete a 3-dose primary series with this vaccine should continue and complete the routine DTaP and IPV booster series according to local schedules.
- Because pertussis antigens in certain licensed subsequent DTaP or combined DTaP-IPV vaccines are the same as those in this vaccine, children should receive an appropriate licensed DTaP vaccine for their fourth dose and an appropriate DTaP or combined DTaP‑IPV vaccine for their fifth dose as indicated by national schedules and the prescribing information of those vaccines.

### Administration instructions
- Shake vial vigorously to obtain a homogeneous turbid white suspension. Do not use if the vaccine cannot be resuspended with vigorous shaking.
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration; do not administer if such conditions are present.
- Administer intramuscularly. Do not inject into gluteal area or areas where major nerve trunks may be present — gluteal administration can produce suboptimal hepatitis B immune responses.
- Administer concomitant vaccines at separate injection sites and using separate syringes when given concomitantly.

### Titration and missed dose instructions
- No titration schedule applicable.
- Guidance for missed doses or alternate schedules: Not specified in available sources beyond statements on acceptable intervals between doses and provisions for completing series after prior doses of component vaccines.

## 5. Contraindications
- Severe allergic reaction (e.g., anaphylaxis) after a previous dose of any diphtheria toxoid-, tetanus toxoid-, pertussis antigen-, hepatitis B-, or poliovirus-containing vaccine, or to any component of this vaccine, including yeast, neomycin, and polymyxin B.
- Encephalopathy (e.g., coma, decreased level of consciousness, prolonged seizures) within 7 days of administration of a previous dose of a pertussis-containing vaccine that is not attributable to another identifiable cause.
- Progressive neurologic disorders (including infantile spasms, uncontrolled epilepsy, or progressive encephalopathy) are contraindications to administration of any pertussis-containing vaccine until the neurologic status is clarified and stabilized.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- No boxed (black box) warning text is specified in the available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Fever: Clinical trials reported higher rates of fever following administration of this combination vaccine compared with separately administered component vaccines; the highest prevalence of fever was on the day of vaccination and the day after vaccination. Most fevers resolved within 4 days (day of vaccination plus 3 days).
- Guillain-Barré syndrome: If Guillain-Barré syndrome has occurred within 6 weeks after receipt of a prior tetanus‑toxoid-containing vaccine, the decision to administer this vaccine (which contains tetanus toxoid) should weigh potential benefits and risks.
- Syncope: Fainting can occur in association with injectable vaccines. Procedures to avoid injury from syncope should be in place when administering this vaccine.
- Adverse reactions following prior pertussis vaccination: If the following events have occurred in temporal relation to receipt of a previous pertussis-containing vaccine, the decision to give any pertussis-containing vaccine (including this vaccine) should be based on careful consideration of benefits and risks:
  - Temperature ≥40.5°C (≥105°F) within 48 hours not due to another identifiable cause;
  - Collapse or shock-like state (hypotonic‑hyporesponsive episode) within 48 hours;
  - Persistent, inconsolable crying lasting ≥3 hours occurring within 48 hours;
  - Seizures (with or without fever) occurring within 3 days.
- Children at increased risk for seizures: For children who are at higher risk for seizures than the general population, an appropriate antipyretic may be administered at the time of vaccination and for the ensuing 24 hours to reduce the possibility of post‑vaccination fever.
- Apnea in premature infants: Apnea has been observed in some infants born prematurely following intramuscular vaccination. Decisions regarding timing of intramuscular vaccination in premature infants should consider the individual infant’s medical status and the balance of benefits and risks.
- Preventing and managing allergic reactions: Appropriate medical treatment must be immediately available to manage potential anaphylactic reactions following administration.
- Altered immunocompetence: Immune responses may be reduced in immunosuppressed persons (including those receiving immunosuppressive therapy such as irradiation, antimetabolites, alkylating agents, cytotoxic drugs, and corticosteroids at greater than physiologic doses). As with any vaccine, expected immune response may not be obtained in these individuals.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor for immediate signs of hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis after vaccination.
- Monitor infants with prior history of seizures, hypotonic-hyporesponsive episodes, or other neurologic conditions closely; consider antipyretic prophylaxis in children at increased risk of seizures.
- For premature infants, consider post‑vaccination monitoring for apnea according to clinical judgment.

### Specific clinical precautions
- If Guillain-Barré syndrome, encephalopathy, or progressive neurologic disorder history is present, consult specialist and weigh risks/benefits prior to administration.
- Medical attention is recommended if fever or other severe reactions develop post‑vaccination as clinically indicated.

## 7. Adverse Effects
- The following adverse reactions were observed in clinical trials and reported in safety surveillance. Frequencies are presented according to available trial data and standard frequency categories where source data allow. If a specific frequency category for an event is not provided in the available sources, it is reported as "Not specified in available sources."

- Overall clinical-trial exposure: 23,849 doses administered to 8,088 infants in 14 clinical studies (as reported in source material).

- Solicited local and systemic adverse reactions (solicited diary-card data; day of vaccination and 3 subsequent days):
  - Local injection-site reactions (pain, redness, swelling) — very common (≥10%); pain, redness, and swelling were reported in ≥25% of participants across doses in clinical studies.
  - Fever (measured ≥100.4°F / ≥38.0°C) — very common; rates reported per dose in one U.S. study: approximately 28% (Dose 1), 39% (Dose 2), 34% (Dose 3). Fever >101.3°F and higher thresholds were observed at lower frequencies. Most fevers resolved within 4 days.
  - Drowsiness (somnolence) — very common (≥10%); rates reported ~41–57% across doses in one U.S. study.
  - Irritability/fussiness — very common (≥10%); rates reported ~61–65% across doses.
  - Loss of appetite — very common (≥10%); rates reported ~26–31% across doses.

- Serious adverse events reported within 30 days in the U.S. safety study (concomitant Hib and pneumococcal vaccination):
  - Reported serious events among recipients included: pyrexia, gastroenteritis, culture‑negative clinical sepsis, and bronchiolitis. Frequency: 7 events in 673 recipients (1%) in the vaccine group and 5 events in 335 recipients (1%) in the comparator group in that study.

- Deaths reported in clinical trials:
  - Across 14 clinical trials, 5 deaths among 8,088 recipients (0.06%) of this vaccine and 1 death among 2,287 recipients (0.04%) of comparator vaccines were reported. Causes among vaccine recipients included two cases reported as sudden infant death syndrome (SIDS), one convulsive disorder, one congenital immunodeficiency with sepsis, and one neuroblastoma. The observed SIDS rate among vaccine recipients across trials was 0.25 per 1,000; in one large safety study in Germany it was 0.2 per 1,000. Background SIDS rates in population differed by country and time; by chance some cases are expected to follow vaccination.

- Seizures and neurologic events:
  - In one German safety study (4,666 infants receiving this vaccine), 6 participants reported seizures during the study period: 2 febrile seizures (one of which later had afebrile seizures) and 4 afebrile seizures (including 2 cases of infantile spasms). Two seizures occurred within 7 days post‑vaccination (overall seizure rate reported as 0.22 per 1,000 doses; febrile seizures 0.07 per 1,000 doses; afebrile seizures 0.14 per 1,000 doses).
  - In the U.S. safety study (673 infants receiving this vaccine), 4 participants reported seizures over the entire follow‑up: 3 febrile seizures and 1 afebrile seizure. No seizures were reported within 7 days of vaccination in that U.S. study.
  - No cases of hypotonic‑hyporesponsive episode (HHE) or encephalopathy were reported in the cited German or U.S. safety studies.

- Other events and post‑vaccination reporting:
  - New onset chronic illnesses during follow‑up (1–6 months after last dose) were reported in both vaccine and comparator groups; examples included asthma, diabetes mellitus, and chronic neutropenia in small numbers.
  - Postmarketing spontaneous reports and broader surveillance data: Not specified in available sources beyond headings; detailed postmarketing frequencies are Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Pharmacokinetic interactions: Not specified in available sources.
- Pharmacodynamic / concomitant administration:
  - This vaccine has been administered concomitantly (at separate injection sites) with Haemophilus influenzae type b conjugate vaccines, pneumococcal conjugate vaccines, and other routine infant vaccines in clinical studies. Concomitant administration at separate sites with other routine pediatric vaccines is an established practice in the clinical trials described.
  - Clinical data show that administration of this combination vaccine was associated with higher rates of fever compared with separately administered component vaccines in comparative trials — clinicians should be aware of increased reactogenicity when giving combination vaccine plus other routine vaccines.
- Clinically significant interactions and management:
  - No specific drug–drug pharmacokinetic interactions are described. If multiple vaccines are administered concomitantly, give them at separate anatomic sites and use separate syringes.
  - For children at higher risk for seizures, consider antipyretic prophylaxis when administering this vaccine to reduce postvaccination fever risk.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Approved for use from 6 weeks of age up to 6 years (prior to seventh birthday).
- This vaccine is indicated as a 3-dose primary series in infants born to HBsAg‑negative mothers.
- Limited safety data exist for use when an infant received a birth (neonatal) dose of hepatitis B vaccine prior to starting this 3‑dose series; available data are limited and caution is advised.

### Premature infants

Decisions about timing of intramuscular vaccination in premature infants should be individualized based on the infant’s medical status and potential benefits and risks; consider monitoring for apnea following vaccination.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- The vaccine induces active immunity by presenting diphtheria and tetanus toxoids, acellular pertussis antigens, recombinant hepatitis B surface antigen, and inactivated poliovirus antigens to the immune system, stimulating antibody production and immunologic memory against these pathogens.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Overview of key safety and comparative studies as described in available sources:
  - German safety study:
    - Design: Large comparative safety study of this vaccine administered concomitantly (at separate sites) with one of several Hib conjugate vaccines versus separately administered comparator vaccines (DTaP, Hib, OPV) in infants.
    - Population: 4,666 infants received this vaccine concomitantly with Hib and PCV in the study group; 768 infants received comparator vaccines.
    - Safety data collection: Adverse events within 30 days after vaccination were collected.
    - Findings: Higher rates of fever were reported with the combination vaccine compared with separately administered vaccines; seizures were reported in 6 participants in the group that received this vaccine (overall seizure rate 0.22 per 1,000 doses); no hypotonic‑hyporesponsive episode or encephalopathy reported.
  - U.S. safety study:
    - Design: Comparative study of this combination vaccine versus separately administered component vaccines (DTaP, hepatitis B, and IPV) with concomitant Hib and pneumococcal conjugate vaccines at separate sites.
    - Population: 673 infants received this vaccine; 335 infants received separately administered vaccines in the comparator group.
    - Safety data collection: Solicited reactions recorded by parents via standardized diary cards for 4 days after each dose; telephone follow‑up at 1 month and 6 months to inquire about serious adverse events and new chronic illnesses.
    - Findings: Fever ≥100.4°F was significantly more frequent in the combination vaccine group for each dose. Solicited local and systemic reactions (pain, redness, swelling, fever, drowsiness, irritability, loss of appetite) were common. Serious adverse events occurred in ~1% of participants in each group in the 30‑day period; a small number of seizures were reported over the entire study period (4 in the vaccine group; 2 in the comparator group), none occurring within 7 days of vaccination.
  - Additional studies:
    - Aggregate clinical‑trial exposure: 23,849 doses administered to 8,088 infants across 14 studies.
    - Deaths and chronic illness onset events were reported at low absolute rates across studies; reported SIDS rates among vaccine recipients were lower than or comparable to background rates in some populations but varied by region and study.
- Immunogenicity and efficacy:
  - The provided source includes sections referencing immunological evaluations, efficacy summaries of component vaccines, and concomitant administration immunologic data; however, comprehensive immunogenicity endpoints, seroprotection rates, and efficacy outcome data are Not specified in the available source excerpts.
