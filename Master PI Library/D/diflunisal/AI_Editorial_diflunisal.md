# Editorial Product Information: Diflunisal

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of diflunisal for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, regulatory warnings, and pharmacological profiles into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI. Clinicians should consult the local approved labelling when prescribing in their specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Diflunisal (a difluorophenyl derivative of salicylic acid).
- **Chemical Class:** Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID); peripherally acting non-narcotic analgesic.
- **Brand Names:** **Dolobid** [US].
- **ATC Code:** M01BA03.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Tablets (Film-coated):** 250 mg and 375 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** INA Pharmaceutics Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Mild to Moderate Pain:** Symptomatic treatment for acute or long-term use [US].
- **Osteoarthritis:** Symptomatic treatment for acute or long-term use [US].
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis:** Symptomatic treatment for acute or long-term use [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Prescribers should use the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration necessary to achieve patient treatment goals.
- **Maximum Dose:** Maintenance doses exceeding 1,500 mg per day are not recommended [US].

### Indication-Specific Dosing
- **Mild to Moderate Pain:**
    - **Initial Loading Dose:** 1,000 mg.
    - **Maintenance:** 500 mg every 12 hours. Some patients may require 500 mg every 8 hours.
    - **Alternative Lower Dose:** Depending on pain severity or patient factors (e.g., advanced age, low weight), an initial 500 mg dose followed by 250 mg every 8 to 12 hours may be appropriate [US].
- **Osteoarthritis and Rheumatoid Arthritis:**
    - **Suggested Range:** 500 mg to 1,000 mg daily administered in two divided doses. Dosage should be adjusted based on clinical response [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets must be swallowed whole; they should not be crushed or chewed [US].
- A loading dose is recommended to reach steady-state plasma levels more rapidly, as the drug exhibits a long half-life and non-linear pharmacokinetics [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to diflunisal or any inactive components [US].
- **NSAID-induced Allergy:** History of asthma, urticaria, or allergic-type reactions following the use of aspirin or other NSAIDs. Severe, rarely fatal, anaphylactic reactions have occurred in these patients [US].
- **Peri-operative Pain:** Use in the setting of coronary artery bypass graft (CABG) surgery [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
- **Cardiovascular (CV) Thrombotic Events:** NSAIDs cause an increased risk of serious CV thrombotic events, including myocardial infarction (MI) and stroke, which can be fatal. This risk may occur early in treatment and increases with duration of use.
- **Gastrointestinal (GI) Risk:** NSAIDs increase the risk of serious GI adverse events, including bleeding, ulceration, and perforation of the stomach or intestines, which can be fatal. Elderly patients are at greater risk.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Borderline elevations of liver tests occur in up to 15% of patients. Severe reactions, including jaundice and fatal fulminant hepatitis, are rare. Discontinue if liver disease clinical signs develop [US].
- **Hypertension:** NSAIDs can lead to the onset of new hypertension or worsening of pre-existing hypertension, contributing to CV events [US].
- **Heart Failure and Edema:** NSAIDs increase the risk of heart failure hospitalisation and may cause fluid retention. Avoid in severe heart failure [US].
- **Renal Effects:** Long-term use may cause renal papillary necrosis. Patients at highest risk include those with pre-existing renal impairment, heart failure, liver dysfunction, or those taking diuretics/ACE inhibitors [US].
- **Advanced Renal Disease:** Treatment is not recommended in patients with advanced renal disease unless benefits outweigh risks; close monitoring of renal function is required [US].
- **Serious Skin Reactions:** Can cause exfoliative dermatitis, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), and Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS). Discontinue at the first appearance of a skin rash [US].
- **Reye’s Syndrome:** Due to its salicylic acid derivative structure, the possibility of an association with Reye’s syndrome cannot be excluded [US].
- **Masking of Inflammation:** The pharmacological activity of diflunisal may diminish the utility of fever/inflammation as diagnostic signs for infection [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- Periodic CBC and chemistry profiles for patients on long-term therapy [US].
- Monitoring for signs of GI bleeding and CV symptoms [US].
- Blood pressure monitoring during initiation and throughout therapy [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **ACE Inhibitors and ARBs:** NSAIDs may diminish the antihypertensive effect. Co-administration in the elderly or volume-depleted may result in renal deterioration, including acute renal failure [US].
- **Acetaminophen:** Concomitant use increases acetaminophen plasma levels by ~50%. Use with caution due to potential hepatotoxicity [US].
- **Antacids:** Continuous use of antacids may reduce diflunisal plasma levels [US].
- **Aspirin:** Not recommended due to increased risk of GI toxicity. Diflunisal reduces aspirin protein binding without altering its clearance [US].
- **Cyclosporine:** Increased risk of cyclosporine-induced toxicity; monitor renal function [US].
- **Diuretics:** Diflunisal may reduce the natriuretic effect of furosemide and thiazides [US].
- **Lithium:** NSAIDs increase plasma lithium levels (approx. 15% increase) and decrease renal clearance. Monitor for lithium toxicity [US].
- **Methotrexate:** NSAIDs may increase methotrexate toxicity by inhibiting renal accumulation [US].
- **NSAIDs:** Concomitant use with other NSAIDs (e.g., indomethacin, sulindac) is not recommended due to increased GI toxicity with little added efficacy. Specifically, diflunisal decreases the renal clearance of indomethacin [US].
- **Oral Anticoagulants (Warfarin):** Prolongs prothrombin time by displacing coumarins from protein binding. Use results in a synergistic risk of serious GI bleeding [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:**
    - **First/Second Trimester:** Observational studies are inconclusive regarding embryofetal risks. Animal data show increased pre- and post-implantation loss [US].
    - **Week 20 Gestation onwards:** Risk of fetal renal dysfunction leading to oligohydramnios and neonatal renal impairment. If necessary between weeks 20–30, limit dose/duration and consider ultrasound monitoring if treatment exceeds 48 hours [US].
    - **Week 30 Gestation onwards:** **Avoid use.** Risk of premature closure of the fetal ductus arteriosus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Diflunisal is excreted in human milk (2% to 7% of plasma levels). A decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the mother's need for the medication [US].
- **Fertility:** In rat studies, no evidence of impaired fertility was found at doses up to 50 mg/kg/day [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in children below age 12 have not been established. Use is not recommended in this age group [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Caution is advised as the elderly are at higher risk for GI bleeding and renal toxicity. Diflunisal is substantially excreted by the kidney; dosage selection should be conservative [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosing adjustments are not specifically quantified, but the drug is not recommended in advanced renal disease. Monitor renal function closely [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources, though caution is warranted given potential hepatotoxicity [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Diflunisal is a prostaglandin synthetase inhibitor. Its analgesic and anti-inflammatory effects are likely due to the peripheral decrease of prostaglandins that sensitize afferent nerves and potentiate bradykinin [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Rapid and complete after oral administration. Peak plasma levels occur at 2 to 3 hours [US].
- **Distribution:** >99% protein bound. Crosses the blood-brain barrier and placental barrier to a minor degree [US].
- **Metabolism/Elimination:** Excreted in urine (~90%) as two soluble glucuronide conjugates. It is not metabolized to salicylic acid [US].
- **Non-linear PK:** A doubling of dosage produces a greater than doubling of drug accumulation.
- **Half-life:** 8 to 12 hours. Several days are required to reach steady-state without a loading dose [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Osteoarthritis/Rheumatoid Arthritis:** Double-blind studies showed diflunisal 500–750 mg daily was comparable in efficacy to aspirin (2,000–3,900 mg daily), ibuprofen (1,600–2,400 mg daily), and naproxen (750 mg daily), generally with a lower incidence of tinnitus than aspirin [US].
- **Pain:** Single-dose studies established that 500 mg was comparable to aspirin 650 mg or acetaminophen 650 mg, but with a longer duration of action (8–12 hours). A 1,000 mg dose was comparable to acetaminophen 600 mg with codeine 60 mg [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15° to 30°C (59° to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Keep container tightly closed and dispense in a well-closed, child-resistant container [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription-only (Rx only). Poison schedule not specified beyond federal Rx status [US].
