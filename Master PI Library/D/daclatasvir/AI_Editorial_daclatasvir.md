# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating data on the clinical use of levocarnitine.
- This document is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [United States].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [United States].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Metabolic Disorders:** For the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [United States].
- **Carnitine Deficiency in End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion. In acute metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) [United States].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). It should be administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [United States].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration before administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [United States].

### Monitoring and Titration
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Daily doses subsequent to the initial dose should be adjusted based on clinical response and range typically around 50 mg/kg/day, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been used. Trough plasma free carnitine levels should be monitored (target: 35–60 µmol/L) [United States].
- **ESRD:** Dose adjustments may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Normal levels are considered 40–50 µmol/L [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If severe reactions occur, discontinue immediately and consider risks/benefits before re-administration [United States].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine (oral or IV) has been associated with reports of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [United States].
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation Precaution):** While the IV form is indicated for ESRD, the safety of chronic high-dose *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD has not been established and may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not assigned in modern labeling, but reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8x human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm]. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Studies in dairy cows indicate milk concentrations increase with exogenous administration. The risks to the infant from excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [United States].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use in paediatric patients is supported by the metabolic disorder and ESRD indications; refer to mg/kg dosing schedules in Section 3 [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific clinical studies in the elderly were not detailed in the available source.
- **Renal Impairment:** Indicated for use in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. Dose adjustments should be guided by plasma trough levels (see Section 3) [United States].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This process provides substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production in all tissues except the brain. It also facilitates the excretion of toxic organic acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [United States].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [United States].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [United States].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Administration to ESRD patients on hemodialysis results in increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis carnitine levels from 21.3 µmol/L (baseline) to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** A study comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg three times weekly showed a dose-proportional increase in trough carnitine concentrations at 12 and 24 weeks [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C [United States].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat [United States].
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [United States].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** Rx-only [United States].
