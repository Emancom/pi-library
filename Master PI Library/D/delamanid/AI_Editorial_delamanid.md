# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals (HCPs). 
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official, legally approved Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP); Chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Administration should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance Dose:** Subsequent daily doses typically remain in the 50 mg/kg range, though individual requirements vary. The maximum recorded dose is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Notes
- Inspect parenterals visually for particulate matter or discolouration before use.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal oedema, and bronchospasm have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, treatment must be discontinued and appropriate medical management initiated [US].
- **Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, it is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Regular monitoring (weekly/monthly) should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine (oral or IV) has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been reported [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic errors.

### Very Common (>10%)
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (21–35%), headache (up to 37%), flu syndrome (15–29%) [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (up to 19%) [US].
- **Digestive:** Diarrhoea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%) [US].
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), rhinitis (6–11%) [US].
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (10–18%) [US].

### Common (1–10%)
- **General:** Abdominal pain, fever, chest pain, asthenia, accidental injury.
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, palpitations, arrhythmia, atrial fibrillation.
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcaemia, hyperkalaemia, peripheral oedema.
- **Nervous:** Paresthesia, insomnia, depression.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Anaphylaxis/Hypersensitivity:** [US].
- **Seizures:** Reported in postmarketing experience [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. Frequent monitoring of INR is recommended after initiating treatment or following dose adjustments [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not assigned]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (BSA-based) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies in nursing mothers are unavailable. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration. HCPs should weigh the benefits to the mother against the potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant; discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing guidelines for metabolic disorders apply to paediatric patients (see Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed; general caution regarding dosing in the elderly is standard, though not explicitly restricted in provided sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of the oral form in ESRD may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO), which are normally excreted in urine [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Distribution half-life is approximately 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) and γ-butyrobetaine [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly, mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine concentrations increased from ~20 µmol/L (baseline) to 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks. While IV levocarnitine effectively increases carnitine concentrations, the direct impact on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Vials are single-dose only; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
