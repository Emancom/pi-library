# Editorial Product Information: Deflazacort

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of deflazacort for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesizes regulatory data to provide clinical guidance on the drug’s use.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Deflazacort (a glucocorticoid/corticosteroid).
- **Brand Names:** Emflaza [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Oral Tablets:** 6 mg, 18 mg, 30 mg, and 36 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Upsher-Smith Laboratories, LLC (Distributor) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy (DMD):** Indicated for the treatment of DMD in patients 5 years of age and older [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (DMD):** The recommended dose is approximately 0.9 mg/kg/day, administered orally once daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - May be taken with or without food [US].
    - Tablets may be swallowed whole or crushed and mixed with applesauce for immediate consumption [US].
    - **Do not** administer with grapefruit juice, as it may increase exposure to the active metabolite [US].
- **Titration and Discontinuation:** Dosage must be decreased gradually if the drug has been administered for more than a few days to avoid acute adrenal insufficiency [US].
- **Dose Rounding:** When using tablets, the dose should be rounded up to the nearest possible dose using a combination of available strengths [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to deflazacort or any inactive ingredients (e.g., lactose monohydrate, magnesium stearate, colloidal silicon dioxide, corn starch) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Alterations in Endocrine Function:** Chronic use may cause hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis suppression, Cushing’s syndrome, and hyperglycemia. Patients with thyroid dysfunction or pheochromocytoma (risk of fatal crisis) require specific caution [US].
- **Immunosuppression and Infection Risk:** Increased susceptibility to new infections and risk of exacerbating, disseminating, or reactivating latent infections (viral, bacterial, fungal, protozoan, or helminthic). Signs of infection may be masked. Specific risks include:
    - Potential fatal course of Varicella (chickenpox) or Measles in non-immune patients [US].
    - Reactivation of latent Tuberculosis or Hepatitis B [US].
- **Cardiovascular and Renal Function:** May cause elevation in blood pressure, sodium/water retention, and increased excretion of potassium and calcium. Use with caution in patients with heart failure or hypertension [US].
- **Gastrointestinal Perforation:** Increased risk in patients with certain GI disorders (e.g., peptic ulcer, diverticulitis, ulcerative colitis). Symptoms like peritoneal irritation may be masked [US].
- **Behavioral and Mood Disturbances:** Potential for euphoria, insomnia, mood swings, personality changes, severe depression, or psychosis. Symptoms typically emerge within days or weeks of starting therapy [US].
- **Ophthalmic Effects:** Increased risk of cataracts, glaucoma, and secondary ocular infections. Intraocular pressure should be monitored if used for >6 weeks [US].
- **Bone Density:** Long-term use decreases bone formation and increases resorption, potentially leading to osteoporosis and fractures [US].
- **Serious Skin Rashes:** Toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN) has been reported, typically within 8 weeks of treatment initiation. Discontinue at the first sign of a rash unless clearly unrelated to the drug [US].
- **Vaccination:** Do not administer live or live-attenuated vaccines to patients on immunosuppressive doses. Administer such vaccines at least 4 to 6 weeks *before* starting deflazacort [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Reactions (Frequency ≥10% in clinical trials [US]):
- **Endocrine/Metabolic:** Cushingoid appearance (33%), weight increased (20%), increased appetite (14%), central obesity (10%).
- **Infectious:** Upper respiratory tract infection (12%), nasopharyngitis (10%).
- **Renal/Urinary:** Pollakiuria (12%).
- **Respiratory:** Cough (12%).
- **Skin:** Hirsutism (10%).

### Other Reported Reactions (Frequency 1–10% [US]):
- Erythema, irritability, rhinorrhea, abdominal discomfort, constipation, abnormal behavior, pyrexia, back pain, and rash.

### Serious Adverse Effects (Post-marketing or Class Effects [US]):
- Anaphylaxis, acute pancreatitis (especially in children), thromboembolic events, avascular necrosis, tendon rupture (when co-administered with quinolones), and Kaposi’s sarcoma.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Moderate or strong inhibitors (e.g., clarithromycin, fluconazole, diltiazem, grapefruit juice) increase exposure to the active metabolite (21-desDFZ). 
    - **Management:** Reduce deflazacort dosage to one-third of the recommended dose [US].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Moderate or strong inducers (e.g., rifampin, efavirenz, carbamazepine, phenytoin) significantly decrease exposure and efficacy.
    - **Management:** Avoid concomitant use [US].
- **Neuromuscular Blockers:** Increased risk of acute myopathy when co-administered [US].
- **Antidiabetic Agents:** Corticosteroids may increase blood glucose, necessitating dose adjustments of anti-diabetic medications [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not assigned a letter category in provided sources, but listed as "Use only if potential benefit justifies potential risk to the fetus" [US].
    - **Clinical Considerations:** Readily crosses the placenta. Associated with increased risk of orofacial clefts and intrauterine growth restriction in human epidemiological studies. Infants born to mothers on substantial doses should be monitored for hypoadrenalism [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Systemically administered corticosteroids appear in human milk and could potentially suppress growth or interfere with endogenous production in the infant. No specific data is available for deflazacort's effect on milk production [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in monkeys and rats showed no effects on the male reproductive system [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established in patients 5 years and older. Safety/efficacy in children under 2 years has not been established. Long-term use may inhibit growth and development [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include patients over 65; however, DMD is primarily a disease of children and young adults [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild, moderate, or severe renal impairment, including end-stage renal disease [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild or moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class B). No data available for severe hepatic impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A corticosteroid prodrug. Its active metabolite, 21-desacetyl-deflazacort (21-desDFZ), acts via glucocorticoid receptors to exert anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive effects [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; median Tmax is 1 hour (fasted). High-fat meals delay Tmax but do not affect total exposure (AUC) [US].
    - **Distribution:** Protein binding of the active metabolite is approximately 40% [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly converted by esterases to the active metabolite 21-desDFZ, which is further metabolized by CYP3A4 [US].
    - **Elimination:** Predominantly renal (68%); elimination is mostly complete within 24 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study 1:** A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 52-week study in 196 male pediatric patients (ages 5–15). 
    - **Outcome:** Deflazacort 0.9 mg/kg/day demonstrated a significant increase in average muscle strength compared to placebo at week 12 (p=0.017).
    - **High Dose Note:** A 1.2 mg/kg/day dose was also tested but showed no additional benefit and a higher incidence of adverse effects [US].
- **Study 2:** A 104-week trial in 29 male children (ages 6–12). While the primary endpoint at 2 years was not statistically significant (likely due to small sample size), secondary measures of functional capacity numerically favored deflazacort over placebo [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Excursions:** Permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription only (Rx-only). Controlled substance: No.
