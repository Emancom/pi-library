# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information contained in this editorial was synthesized from official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP); chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism leading to secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours (never less frequently than every 6 hours) during acute phases.
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg or as clinically required; the maximum recorded dose is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Titration:** Dose adjustments may be made based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations, starting as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** 
    - **Plasma Levels:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to starting therapy. Regular weekly and monthly monitoring is advised. Target free carnitine levels are typically between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
    - **General Monitoring:** Includes blood chemistries, vital signs, and overall clinical status.
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** Note that the safety of oral levocarnitine in patients with renal impairment has not been established; chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common and Serious Effects
- **General:** Transient nausea and vomiting are common. Less frequent effects include body odor and gastritis.
- **Neurologic:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been noted [US].

### Clinical Trial Data (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported at a frequency ≥5% in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials (regardless of causality) [US]:

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specifically assigned a letter category in provided text, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine in milk following administration. The risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefit of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impairment in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is based on mg/kg (see Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. Monitoring of trough levels is essential for dose adjustment [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Clears toxic acylCoA compounds by forming acylcarnitine, which is rapidly excreted.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** Clinical trials demonstrated that 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis significantly increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Trough levels remained stable or increased over 8 to 24 weeks of therapy. However, the effect of this increase on specific clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Discard any unused portion as the formulation does not contain preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** US: Rx-only.
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified for other jurisdictions in available sources.
