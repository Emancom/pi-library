# Editorial Product Information: diphtheria + tetanus + acellular + inactivated

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **diphtheria + tetanus + acellular + inactivated**
- Chemical class: Combination toxoid and inactivated viral vaccine (diphtheria toxoid; tetanus toxoid; acellular pertussis antigens; inactivated poliovirus)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intramuscular injection

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 2015 [US]
- Date of last revision/update in available source: Revised 4/2026 [US]
- Withdrawals, suspensions, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources
- Registration/approval dates for other jurisdictions: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Active immunization against diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis and poliomyelitis. [US-approved]
- Use as a single 0.5 mL dose:
  - As a fifth dose in the diphtheria-tetanus-acellular pertussis (DTaP) immunization series in children 4 through 6 years of age whose prior DTaP doses were with DTaP-containing vaccines specified in product labeling. [US-approved]
  - As a fourth or fifth dose in the inactivated poliovirus (IPV) vaccination series in children 4 through 6 years of age. [US-approved]

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing
- Pediatric (approved): single 0.5 mL intramuscular dose for children 4 through 6 years of age as noted in Indications. [US-approved]
- Adult dosing: Not specified in available sources / not approved for adults in the available source.
### Dosing by indication
- Fifth dose in DTaP series (see Indications). [US-approved]
- Fourth or fifth dose in IPV series (see Indications). [US-approved]
### Administration instructions
- Administer intramuscularly into the deltoid muscle of the upper arm.
- Just before use, shake the vial or syringe well until a uniform, white, cloudy suspension results.
- Inspect visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration; do not administer if either is present.
- Do not combine, reconstitute with, or mix this vaccine with any other vaccine.
- When given concomitantly with other injectable vaccines, use separate syringes and administer at different injection sites.
- Discard any unused portion remaining in a vial after withdrawal.
### Titration schedules
- Not applicable / Not specified in available sources.
### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Severe allergic reaction (e.g., anaphylaxis) to any ingredient of **diphtheria + tetanus + acellular + inactivated**. [US]
- Severe allergic reaction to a prior dose of any diphtheria toxoid-, tetanus toxoid-, pertussis-containing vaccine, or inactivated poliovirus vaccine. [US]
- Encephalopathy (e.g., coma, decreased level of consciousness, prolonged seizures) within 7 days of a previous dose of a pertussis-containing vaccine that is not attributable to another identifiable cause — contraindication to administration of any pertussis-containing vaccine, including this product. [US]
- Progressive neurologic disorder (including infantile spasms, uncontrolled epilepsy, or progressive encephalopathy) — contraindication to administration of any pertussis-containing vaccine, including this product, until a treatment regimen has been established and the condition has stabilized. [US]

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources as a boxed warning. [If present in other jurisdictions: Not specified in available sources]

### General warnings and precautions
- Management of acute allergic reactions: Epinephrine (1:1,000 solution) and other appropriate agents/equipment must be available for immediate use in case of anaphylaxis or acute hypersensitivity reactions. [US]
- Adverse events following prior pertussis vaccination: If any of the following occurred after a prior pertussis vaccine, carefully weigh risks and benefits before administering this vaccine:
  - Fever ≥40.5°C (≥105°F) within 48 hours (not attributable to another cause). [US]
  - Hypotonic-hyporesponsive episode (HHE; collapse or shock-like state) within 48 hours. [US]
  - Persistent, inconsolable crying lasting ≥3 hours within 48 hours. [US]
  - Seizures with or without fever within 3 days. [US]
- Guillain‑Barré syndrome (GBS): If GBS occurred within 6 weeks of receipt of a prior vaccine containing tetanus toxoid, the decision to administer any tetanus toxoid‑containing vaccine, including this product, should be based on careful consideration of potential benefits and risks. [US]
- Limitations of vaccine effectiveness: Vaccination may not protect all individuals. [US]
- Altered immunocompetence: Immunocompromised persons, including those receiving immunosuppressive therapy (see Drug Interactions), may have a reduced immune response. Consider potential for reduced effectiveness. [US]
- Syncope: Fainting may occur in association with administration of injectable vaccines; procedures should be in place to avoid injury from fainting. [US]

### Monitoring requirements
- Observe for immediate allergic reactions following administration; ensure availability of resuscitation equipment and epinephrine. [US]
- Routine post-vaccination monitoring as clinically indicated; monitor for serious adverse events for up to months after vaccination as per local guidance and pharmacovigilance requirements. [US]

### Specific clinical precautions
- Do not administer to individuals with contraindications listed in Section 5.
- If progressive neurologic disorders are present, defer pertussis-containing vaccination until condition stabilized and treatment established. [US]
- Consider withholding tetanus-containing vaccines if prior GBS within 6 weeks of tetanus-containing vaccination. [US]

## 7. Adverse Effects
- Adverse event data come from a randomized, controlled, multicenter clinical study in children 4–6 years of age and from postmarketing spontaneous reports. Postmarketing events are from voluntary reports and frequencies cannot be reliably estimated.

- Definitions for frequency categories used below:
  - Very common: >10%
  - Common: 1–10%
  - Uncommon: 0.1–1%
  - Rare: <0.1%
  - Very rare: <0.01%
  - Frequency not possible to estimate: Postmarketing spontaneous reporting / not determinable from available data

### Summary from clinical trial (children 4–6 years)
- Most common solicited injection site reactions (within 7 days): pain, increase in arm circumference, erythema, swelling.
- Most common solicited systemic reactions (within 7 days): myalgia, malaise, headache.
- Fever (any) occurred in approximately 6.0% of participants in the clinical trial.
- Serious adverse events (SAEs) within 28 days: 0.1% in vaccine group; SAEs within 6 months: 0.8% (none assessed as related to vaccination in the study).

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Concomitant vaccine administration
- In the pivotal US clinical trial, this vaccine was administered concomitantly (same visit) with measles‑mumps‑rubella (MMR) vaccine and varicella vaccine; when given concomitantly with other injectable vaccines, they should be administered with different syringes and at different injection sites. [US]
### Immunosuppressive therapies
- Immunosuppressive therapies (including irradiation, antimetabolites, alkylating agents, cytotoxic drugs, and corticosteroids when used in doses greater than physiologic replacement) may reduce the immune response to this vaccine. Consider timing of vaccination relative to immunosuppressive therapy and counsel that an adequate immune response may not be achieved. [US]
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Not specified in available sources.
### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Not specified beyond the reduction in immune response with immunosuppressive therapies and the requirement to administer separate-site injections when given concomitantly with other injectable vaccines.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Diphtheria: Induces neutralizing antibodies to diphtheria toxin; serum antitoxin levels correlate with protection (0.01 IU/mL gives some protection; ≥0.1 IU/mL generally regarded as protective; ~1.0 IU/mL associated with long-term protection). [US]
- Tetanus: Induces neutralizing antibodies to tetanus toxin; a serum tetanus antitoxin level of ≥0.01 IU/mL (neutralization assay) is considered minimally protective; ≥0.1 IU/mL (ELISA used in studies) is considered protective. [US]
- Pertussis: Vaccination induces antibodies to included acellular pertussis antigens (detoxified pertussis toxin [PT], filamentous hemagglutinin [FHA], pertactin [PRN], fimbriae [FIM]). There is no well‑established serologic correlate of protection for pertussis. Vaccine effectiveness for the pertussis component was inferred by comparison of immune responses to established acellular pertussis vaccines. [US]
- Poliomyelitis: Inactivated poliovirus antigens induce type‑specific neutralizing antibodies that correlate with protection against poliomyelitis. [US]

### Pharmacokinetics
- Not applicable in the traditional sense for this inactivated toxoid/viral vaccine; immunogenicity and antibody responses are the primary pharmacodynamic measures reported. Specific ADME parameters are not provided in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Immunogenicity endpoints in clinical study: booster response rates and antibody geometric mean concentrations/titers (GMCs/GMTs) to diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis antigens and poliovirus serotypes measured pre-vaccination and 28 days post-vaccination. The vaccine demonstrated non-inferiority to comparator vaccinations in eliciting antibody responses in the studied population. [US]

- Formulation antigen content (per 0.5 mL dose) as described in available source:
  - Diphtheria toxoid: 15 Lf
  - Tetanus toxoid: 5 Lf
  - Acellular pertussis antigens: detoxified PT 20 mcg, FHA 20 mcg, PRN 3 mcg, FIM (types 2 and 3) 5 mcg
  - Inactivated polioviruses (D-antigen units): Type 1 29 DU, Type 2 7 DU, Type 3 26 DU
  - Contains aluminum phosphate adjuvant (amount specified in source) and other residuals; product does not contain a preservative. [US]

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Key pivotal trial (Study M5I02; ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT01346293) — randomized, controlled, multicenter study conducted in the United States and Puerto Rico:
  - Population: 3,372 children aged 4 to 6 years who had received 4 prior doses of DTaP-containing vaccine(s).
  - Interventions: single dose of this vaccine vs. comparator regimen of separate DTaP + inactivated poliovirus vaccines; concomitant administration of MMR and varicella vaccines at separate sites in both groups.
  - Safety population: 2,733 subjects received this vaccine; 621 subjects received the comparator.
  - Immunogenicity subset: 263 subjects (vaccine) and 253 subjects (comparator) had serology performed pre‑ and 28 days post‑vaccination.
  - Primary immunogenicity endpoints: booster response rates and post‑vaccination antibody GMCs/GMTs to diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis antigens and poliovirus serotypes.
  - Results: This vaccine was non‑inferior to the comparator (concomitant but separate DTaP + IPV vaccines) with respect to booster response rates and antibody GMCs/GMTs for diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis antigens, and poliovirus types 1–3 in the measured population.
  - Safety outcomes: solicited injection site and systemic adverse reactions collected for 7 days post‑vaccination; unsolicited adverse events for 28 days; serious adverse events for 6 months. Solicited local reactions (e.g., pain, limb circumference change, erythema, swelling) occurred commonly; solicited systemic reactions (myalgia, malaise, headache) occurred commonly. SAEs within 28 days occurred in 0.1% of vaccine recipients; SAEs within 6 months occurred in 0.8% (none assessed as related to vaccination in the study).
- Additional clinical data: The formulation in the pivotal immunogenicity trial used poliovirus component grown in MRC‑5 cells; an analytically comparable formulation using poliovirus grown in Vero cells is described in product description; immunogenicity bridging was used to support comparability. [US]
