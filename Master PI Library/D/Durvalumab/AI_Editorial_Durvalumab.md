# Editorial Product Information: Durvalumab

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **durvalumab** for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Durvalumab (a fully human, high-affinity, immunoglobulin G1 kappa [IgG1κ] monoclonal antibody).
- **Brand Name:** **IMFINZI** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01FF03.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Concentrated solution for infusion; 120 mg/2.4 mL and 500 mg/10 mL single-dose vials [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AstraZeneca Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
[All indications listed below are Australian-approved]:

- **Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC):** 
    - Neoadjuvant treatment (in combination with platinum-based chemotherapy) followed by adjuvant monotherapy for resectable tumours (≥4 cm and/or node-positive) lacking EGFR or ALK mutations.
    - Monotherapy for locally advanced, unresectable disease that has not progressed after platinum-based chemoradiation therapy (CRT).
- **Small Cell Lung Cancer (SCLC):**
    - Monotherapy for limited-stage SCLC (LS-SCLC) following platinum-based CRT.
    - First-line treatment of extensive-stage SCLC (ES-SCLC) in combination with etoposide and either carboplatin or cisplatin.
- **Biliary Tract Cancer (BTC):** In combination with gemcitabine and cisplatin for locally advanced or metastatic BTC.
- **Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC):** In combination with tremelimumab for unresectable HCC (uHCC) in patients not previously treated with PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors.
- **Endometrial Cancer:** First-line treatment of primary advanced or recurrent disease in combination with carboplatin and paclitaxel, followed by monotherapy.
- **Bladder Cancer:** Neoadjuvant treatment of muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC) in combination with gemcitabine and cisplatin, followed by adjuvant monotherapy after radical cystectomy.
- **Gastric/Gastroesophageal Junction (GOJ) Cancer:** Neoadjuvant and adjuvant treatment of resectable adenocarcinoma in combination with FLOT chemotherapy, followed by adjuvant monotherapy.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults >30 kg)
- **Standard Infusion:** All doses are administered as an IV infusion over 1 hour.
- **Monotherapy (Unresectable NSCLC):** 10 mg/kg every 2 weeks OR 1500 mg every 4 weeks for up to 1 year.
- **LS-SCLC Monotherapy:** 1500 mg every 4 weeks for up to 24 months.
- **Combination Therapy (NSCLC, BTC, MIBC, Gastric/GOJ):** 1500 mg in combination with specified chemotherapy (see table below for intervals), typically followed by 1500 mg monotherapy maintenance.
- **uHCC (STRIDE Regimen):** A single priming dose of tremelimumab 300 mg combined with **durvalumab** 1500 mg at Cycle 1/Day 1, followed by **durvalumab** 1500 mg monotherapy every 4 weeks.

### Weight-Based Dosing (≤30 kg)
Patients weighing 30 kg or less must receive weight-based dosing (typically 10 mg/kg every 2 weeks or 20 mg/kg every 3–4 weeks depending on the indication) until weight increases to >30 kg.

### Administration Instructions
- **Preparation:** Dilute in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or 5% Dextrose to a final concentration between 1 mg/mL and 15 mg/mL. Mix by gentle inversion; do not shake.
- **Filters:** Use a sterile, low-protein binding 0.2 or 0.22 micron in-line filter.
- **Sequence:** If given with chemotherapy, administer **durvalumab** first. For uHCC, administer tremelimumab before **durvalumab**.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to the active substance (durvalumab) or any excipient (histidine, trehalose dihydrate, polysorbate 80) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Immune-Mediated Adverse Drug Reactions (imADRs)
Durvalumab can cause severe or fatal imADRs. Early identification and management with corticosteroids (typically 1–4 mg/kg/day prednisone or equivalent) and dose tapering over at least 1 month are essential.
- **Pneumonitis:** Monitor for new or worsening respiratory symptoms. Incidence is higher in patients who have received prior thoracic radiation (e.g., 33.9% in the PACIFIC study).
- **Hepatitis:** Monitor liver function tests (ALT, AST, bilirubin) prior to each infusion.
- **Colitis:** Monitor for diarrhoea or abdominal pain. Consult surgery immediately if perforation is suspected.
- **Endocrinopathies:** Monitor for changes in thyroid function, adrenal insufficiency, hypophysitis, and Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus.
- **Nephritis:** Monitor for changes in renal function.
- **Dermatological:** Monitor for rash and rare but serious reactions like SJS/TEN.

### Specific Precautions
- **VTE Risk:** A higher incidence of venous thromboembolic events (including pulmonary embolism) was noted when used with chemotherapy in MIBC (15.7%) and Gastric/GOJ cancer (4.2%) [Australian].
- **Autoimmune Disease:** Use with caution in patients with pre-existing autoimmune disease due to the risk of flares.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequencies (Monotherapy Pool, n=3006)
- **Very Common (>10%):** Cough/productive cough (21.5%), diarrhoea (16.3%), rash (16.0%), pyrexia (13.8%), upper respiratory tract infections (13.5%), abdominal pain (12.7%), pruritus (10.8%), hypothyroidism (10.1%).
- **Common (1–10%):** Pneumonia (8.9%), AST/ALT increased (8.1%), peripheral oedema (9.7%), myalgia (5.9%), hyperthyroidism (4.6%), dyspnoea (25% in PACIFIC trial), infusion-related reactions (1.6%).
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Colitis, hepatitis, nephritis, pancreatitis, thyroiditis.
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Myocarditis, immune thrombocytopenia, pemphigoid, uveitis, coeliac disease.

### Serious Adverse Effects
Grade 3–4 reactions are reported in approximately 13–15% of monotherapy patients. Serious events commonly include pneumonia, pneumonitis, and immune-mediated hepatitis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetics:** As an immunoglobulin, durvalumab is eliminated via protein catabolism; it is not metabolised by cytochrome P450 enzymes. Formal drug-drug interaction studies with P450 inhibitors/inducers have not been conducted.
- **Chemotherapy:** No clinically meaningful PK interactions were identified between durvalumab and etoposide, carboplatin, or cisplatin [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D [Australian]**. Durvalumab can cross the placental barrier and cause fetal harm. Animal studies showed increased fetal loss and neonatal deaths. Use is not recommended during pregnancy.
- **Contraception:** Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during treatment and for at least 3 months after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** IgG is excreted in human milk. Due to potential serious adverse reactions in infants, breastfeeding is advised against during treatment and for 3 months post-therapy.
- **Fertility:** No human data. Animal studies showed no effects on reproductive organs.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients <18 years. Limited PK data in children ≥35 kg show exposure similar to adults.
- **Elderly Use (≥65 years):** No overall differences in safety or efficacy. Data are limited for patients ≥75 years.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild or moderate impairment. Not studied in severe renal impairment (CrCl <30 mL/min).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild or moderate impairment. Not studied in severe hepatic impairment (bilirubin >3x ULN).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Durvalumab blocks the interaction of programmed cell death ligand-1 (PD-L1) with PD-1 and CD80. This reverses PD-L1-mediated T-cell suppression and enhances anti-tumour immune responses.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** PD-L1 blockade increases T-cell activation and cytokine production. Durvalumab does not induce antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Steady-state volume of distribution (Vss) is 5.64 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Protein catabolism via the reticuloendothelial system.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 18 days. Clearance decreases over time by approximately 22% until reaching a steady state.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **PACIFIC:** Demonstrated significant PFS and OS benefit in unresectable Stage III NSCLC following CRT. 5-year OS rate was 42.9% for durvalumab vs 33.4% for placebo.
- **CASPIAN:** Showed OS benefit in first-line ES-SCLC when combined with platinum-etoposide (HR 0.73).
- **TOPAZ-1:** Demonstrated significant OS improvement in advanced BTC when combined with gemcitabine/cisplatin.
- **HIMALAYA:** Confirmed the STRIDE regimen (single 300 mg tremelimumab + durvalumab) improved OS in uHCC compared to sorafenib.
- **MATTERHORN:** Showed significant improvements in pathological complete response (pCR) for resectable Gastric/GOJ cancer when added to FLOT chemotherapy.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Vials:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate). Do not freeze. Protect from light. Do not shake.
- **Diluted Solution:** If not used immediately, store for up to 30 days at 2°C to 8°C or up to 12 hours at room temperature (up to 25°C) [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4) [Australian]**.
