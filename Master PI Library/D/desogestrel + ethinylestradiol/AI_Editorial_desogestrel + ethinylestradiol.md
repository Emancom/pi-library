# Editorial Product Information: desogestrel + ethinylestradiol

## 4. Dosage and Administration

General dosing (adult)
- Active tablet composition per available source: 150 micrograms desogestrel + 30 micrograms ethinylestradiol.
- Pack/Regimen: 21 consecutive active tablets followed by 7 inert (placebo) tablets; one tablet is taken daily.

Administration instructions
- Take one tablet at approximately the same time each day, with liquid as needed.
- Follow the order indicated on the pack and begin each pack on the tablet corresponding to the current day of the week.
- Start each subsequent pack immediately after the last placebo tablet; do not extend the tablet-free interval beyond 7 days.

How to start
- Preferably start on Day 1 (first day of menstrual bleeding). If started on Days 2–5 of the cycle, use an additional non-hormonal barrier method (e.g., condoms) for the first 7 days of uninterrupted tablet-taking.
- When changing from another combined hormonal contraceptive (COC, vaginal ring, transdermal patch): preferably start the day after the last active tablet of the previous method, or at the latest the day after the customary tablet-free interval or placebo tablets. If switching from a ring/patch, start preferably on the day of removal or at the latest when the next application would have been due.
- When switching from a progestogen-only method:
  - Minipill: may switch any day; use barrier method for first 7 days of active tablet-taking.
  - Implant or intrauterine system (IUS): start on day of removal; use barrier for first 7 days.
  - Injectable: start when the next injection would be due; use barrier for first 7 days.
- Following first trimester abortion: may start immediately without additional contraceptive measures.
- After childbirth or second/third trimester abortion: advise to start 21–28 days after delivery or second-trimester abortion. If starting later than day 28, use additional barrier contraception for the first 7 days of active tablet-taking; if intercourse has already occurred, exclude pregnancy before starting or wait for the first menstrual period.
  - Consider the increased risk of venous thromboembolism (VTE) in the postpartum period when restarting.

Missed tablet instructions
- If less than 12 hours late in taking an active tablet: contraceptive protection is maintained. Take the missed tablet as soon as remembered and continue at the usual time.
- If more than 12 hours late in taking any active tablet: contraceptive protection may be reduced. Clinical rules to guide management:
  1. Active tablet-taking must never be discontinued for longer than 7 days.
  2. Seven consecutive days of uninterrupted active tablet-taking are required to re‑establish adequate suppression of the hypothalamic–pituitary–ovarian axis.
- Practical advice:
  - Take the last missed active tablet as soon as remembered (this may require taking two tablets in one day), then continue at the usual time.
  - Use additional contraceptive precautions (barrier method or abstain) for the next 7 days.
  - If these 7 days would include the scheduled placebo tablets, start the active tablets from the next pack immediately after finishing the current pack to avoid an extended hormone-free interval. Withdrawal bleeding will be delayed until the end of the second pack but this is not harmful.
  - If active tablets are missed during the first 7 days of a pack and unprotected intercourse occurred, consider the possibility of pregnancy.

Advice in case of gastrointestinal disturbance
- Severe gastrointestinal disturbance may reduce absorption; additional contraceptive measures should be used.
- If vomiting occurs within 3–4 hours after tablet intake, manage as for a missed tablet. If the woman does not wish to change her schedule, she may take the extra tablet(s) needed from another pack.

Additional contraceptive precautions
- When additional precautions are required, advise abstinence or a barrier method (e.g., condom, diaphragm plus spermicide). Rhythm methods are not reliable because the pill alters natural cycle markers (temperature, cervical mucus).

How to delay or shift periods
- To delay a withdrawal bleed: start the next active pack immediately after finishing the current pack (omit the placebo interval). Continue until the desired delay; breakthrough bleeding or spotting may occur.
- To shift the period earlier: shorten the forthcoming placebo tablet interval by the desired number of days. Shortening the placebo interval increases the risk of absent withdrawal bleeding and breakthrough bleeding/spotting in the subsequent cycle.

## 5. Contraindications
Do not prescribe **desogestrel + ethinylestradiol** to women with any of the following conditions. If any of these conditions appear for the first time during use, the product should be stopped immediately.

- Presence or risk of venous thromboembolism (VTE):
  - Current VTE (on anticoagulant treatment) or history of deep vein thrombosis (DVT) or pulmonary embolism (PE)
  - Known hereditary or acquired predisposition to VTE (e.g., APC resistance including Factor V Leiden, antithrombin III deficiency, protein C deficiency, protein S deficiency, hyperhomocysteinaemia, antiphospholipid antibodies)
  - Major surgery with prolonged immobilisation
  - High risk of VTE due to multiple risk factors

- Presence or risk of arterial thromboembolism (ATE):
  - Current ATE or history of ATE (e.g., myocardial infarction, stroke) or prodromal conditions (e.g., angina pectoris, transient ischaemic attack)
  - Known hereditary or acquired predisposition to ATE (e.g., hyperhomocysteinaemia, antiphospholipid antibodies, APC resistance, antithrombin III deficiency, protein C/S deficiency)
  - History of migraine with focal neurological symptoms
  - High risk of ATE due to multiple or serious risk factors (e.g., diabetes mellitus with vascular symptoms, severe hypertension, severe dyslipoproteinaemia)

- Pancreatitis, or history of pancreatitis if associated with severe hypertriglyceridaemia
- Presence or history of severe hepatic disease until liver function tests return to normal
- Presence or history of liver tumours (benign or malignant)
- Known or suspected sex steroid‑dependent malignancies (e.g., of the breasts or genital organs)
- Undiagnosed vaginal bleeding
- Known or suspected pregnancy
- Hypersensitivity to any ingredient in the product
- Concomitant use with certain hepatitis C virus combination regimens (specifically regimens including ombitasvir/paritaprevir/ritonavir with or without dasabuvir) and with medicinal products including glecaprevir and pibrentasvir (coadministration is contraindicated)

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources.

General warnings and precautions
- **Risk of venous thromboembolism (VTE):**
  - Use of combined hormonal contraceptives containing ethinylestradiol is associated with an increased VTE risk compared with no use. The excess risk is highest during the first year of initial use and when restarting after a break of ≥4 weeks.
  - VTE risk for combined hormonal contraceptives is lower than the VTE risk associated with pregnancy.
  - Products containing desogestrel are associated with a higher VTE risk than products containing levonorgestrel, norgestimate, or norethisterone.
  - If a woman has multiple VTE risk factors, the overall risk may be greater than the sum of individual risks; evaluate benefit–risk and avoid prescribing if balance is unfavorable.

- Quantified annual risk estimates (per 10,000 women per year) presented in the available source:
  - Women not using a CHC and not pregnant: about 1–5
  - CHC containing levonorgestrel, norethisterone or norgestimate: about 5–7
  - CHC containing etonogestrel or norelgestromin: about 6–12
  - CHC containing drospirenone, gestodene, desogestrel or cyproterone acetate: about 9–12

- Risk factors for VTE (non‑exhaustive):
  - Obesity (BMI >30 kg/m2; risk rises with BMI)
  - Prolonged immobilisation, major surgery (including pelvic/leg surgery, neurosurgery), major trauma
  - Temporary immobilisation including air travel >4 hours, especially with other risk factors
  - Positive family history of VTE (first‑degree relative with VTE at a young age)
  - Biochemical thrombophilic states (APC resistance/Factor V Leiden, antithrombin III deficiency, protein C/S deficiency, antiphospholipid antibodies)
  - Medical conditions associated with VTE: cancer, systemic lupus erythematosus, haemolytic uraemic syndrome, chronic inflammatory bowel disease, sickle cell disease
  - Increasing age (>35 years) and smoking

- Management around surgery/immobilisation:
  - In women at risk of prolonged immobilisation, consider discontinuing the product (elective surgery: at least 4 weeks in advance) and not resuming until two weeks after complete remobilisation. Use alternative contraception to avoid unintended pregnancy. Consider antithrombotic treatment if product has not been discontinued in advance.

- Symptoms of VTE: unilateral leg swelling, leg pain/tenderness (often on standing/walking), increased warmth, red/discoloured skin; pulmonary embolism symptoms include sudden unexplained shortness of breath, rapid breathing, sudden cough (possibly with haemoptysis), sharp chest pain, severe light‑headedness/dizziness, rapid/irregular heartbeat.

- **Risk of arterial thromboembolism (ATE):**
  - Epidemiological association between CHC use and increased risk of arterial thrombotic events (myocardial infarction, stroke, TIA). Risk increases with age and presence of specific risk factors.
  - Contraindicated in women with one serious or multiple risk factors for ATE that confer high risk.

- Risk factors for ATE (non‑exhaustive):
  - Increasing age (particularly >35 years), smoking, hypertension, obesity (BMI >30), positive family history of arterial thromboembolism at an early age, biochemical factors (hyperhomocysteinaemia, antiphospholipid antibodies, APC resistance), migraine, diabetes mellitus, valvular heart disease, atrial fibrillation, dyslipoproteinaemia, systemic lupus erythematosus.

- Symptoms of ATE/stroke/MI:
  - Stroke/TIA: sudden numbness or weakness (face/arm/leg), especially unilateral; sudden trouble walking, dizziness, loss of balance/coordination; sudden confusion, speech difficulty; sudden visual disturbance; sudden severe or prolonged headache; loss of consciousness or fainting.
  - Myocardial infarction: chest pain/pressure/heaviness; discomfort radiating to back/jaw/arm/epigastrium; nausea, vomiting, dizziness, sweating, extreme weakness, shortness of breath; rapid/irregular heartbeats.

- Neoplasms:
  - Some epidemiological studies report an association between long‑term (≥5 years) use of combined oral contraceptives and increased risk of cervical intraepithelial neoplasia or invasive cervical cancer; confounding by sexual behaviour and HPV is likely.
  - Meta-analysis data: small increased relative risk for breast cancer diagnosis in current COC users (relative risk ~1.24); excess risk decreases over 10 years after stopping.
  - Large cohort data: small absolute increase in breast cancer incidence with longer duration of use; cancers detected in users tended to be less advanced clinically.
  - Rare reports of benign and very rarely malignant liver tumours in users of combined oral contraceptives; consider hepatic tumour in the differential when severe upper abdominal pain, hepatomegaly or signs of intra‑abdominal haemorrhage occur.

- Other clinical precautions:
  - If a hereditary thrombophilia is suspected, refer to specialist for evaluation before initiating CHC.
  - An increase in frequency or severity of migraine during CHC use, especially with focal neurological symptoms, may warrant immediate discontinuation.
  - Advise women of the signs and symptoms of VTE and ATE and to seek urgent medical attention if they occur; inform treating clinicians that the patient is taking a combined hormonal contraceptive.

Monitoring requirements
- Baseline medical history and assessment of personal and family history of thromboembolism, cardiovascular disease, migraines, and hepatic disease before initiation.
- Clinical monitoring for symptoms/signs of thromboembolic events, hepatic dysfunction, and new or worsening migraine or hypertension during use.
