# Editorial Product Information: Deutetrabenazine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial has been developed based on the primary Product Information (PI) document from the **United States (FDA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised clinical overview of deutetrabenazine for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing within any specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, specific documents from other authorities (e.g., Australian TGA, European EMA) were not available for comparison; therefore, information regarding other regions is marked as "Not specified in available sources."

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Deutetrabenazine, a deuterated hexahydro-dimethoxybenzoquinolizine derivative.
- **Chemical Class:** Vesicular monoamine transporter 2 (VMAT2) inhibitor.
- **Brand Names:** **AUSTEDO**, **AUSTEDO XR** [US].
- **ATC Code:** N07XX10.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
  - **Tablets (Immediate-release):** 6 mg, 9 mg, and 12 mg [US].
  - **Extended-release (XR) Tablets:** 6 mg, 12 mg, 18 mg, 24 mg, 30 mg, 36 mg, 42 mg, and 48 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Teva Neuroscience, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chorea associated with Huntington’s Disease:** Indicated for the management of involuntary movements (chorea) in adult patients [US].
- **Tardive Dyskinesia:** Indicated for the treatment of tardive dyskinesia in adults [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
The dose must be individualised based on the reduction of symptoms and patient tolerability. 
- **Starting Dosage:** 12 mg per day (administered as 6 mg twice daily for immediate-release or 12 mg once daily for XR) [US].
- **Titration:** Dosage may be increased at weekly intervals in increments of 6 mg per day.
- **Maximum Dose:** 48 mg per day (total daily dose) [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **AUSTEDO (Immediate-release):** Must be administered with food [US].
- **AUSTEDO XR:** May be administered with or without food [US].
- **Handling:** Tablets must be swallowed whole. Do not crush, chew, or break [US].
- **Missed Doses:** If treatment is interrupted for more than one week, the dose should be re-titrated upon resumption. If interrupted for less than one week, resume at the previous maintenance dose [US].

### Switching from Tetrabenazine
When transitioning patients from tetrabenazine to deutetrabenazine, tetrabenazine should be discontinued, and deutetrabenazine initiated the following day. The starting dose depends on the previous tetrabenazine daily requirement (e.g., tetrabenazine 25 mg/day converts to deutetrabenazine 12 mg/day) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Huntington's Disease:** Patients who are suicidal or have untreated/inadequately treated depression [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in all degrees of hepatic impairment [US].
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Do not use concomitantly or within 14 days of discontinuing MAOI therapy [US].
- **Reserpine:** Do not use with reserpine. At least 20 days should elapse after stopping reserpine before starting deutetrabenazine [US].
- **VMAT2 Inhibitors:** Concomitant use with tetrabenazine or valbenazine [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning: Depression and Suicidality [US]
Patients with Huntington’s disease are at increased risk for depression and suicidal thoughts/behaviour. Deutetrabenazine may further increase this risk. 
- Clinicians must balance the risks of suicidality with the clinical need for chorea treatment.
- Monitor for new or worsening depression or unusual changes in behaviour.
- Inform caregivers and families of these risks.

### General Warnings
- **QT Prolongation:** Deutetrabenazine may prolong the QT interval. Avoid in patients with congenital long QT syndrome or history of cardiac arrhythmias.
- **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** A potentially fatal condition reported with dopaminergic transmission-reducing drugs. Discontinue immediately if NMS is suspected.
- **Akathisia, Agitation, and Restlessness:** Monitor for signs of akathisia; dose reduction or discontinuation may be required.
- **Parkinsonism:** May cause or worsen rigidity and bradykinesia. Symptoms typically resolve upon discontinuation.
- **Sedation and Somnolence:** Common dose-limiting adverse effect. Patients should avoid operating heavy machinery until a stable maintenance dose is established.
- **Hyperprolactinemia:** VMAT2 inhibitors can elevate serum prolactin levels. 

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect profiles vary slightly by indication. Frequencies are derived from 12-week placebo-controlled trials [US].

| Adverse Reaction | Huntington’s Chorea (%) | Tardive Dyskinesia (%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Somnolence** | 11% | Not specified |
| **Nasopharyngitis** | Not specified | 4% |
| **Dry Mouth** | 9% | Not specified |
| **Fatigue** | 9% | Not specified |
| **Insomnia** | 7% | 4% |
| **Diarrhea** | 9% | Not specified |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Suicidality, NMS, severe parkinsonism, and clinically significant QTc prolongation [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Strong CYP2D6 Inhibitors (e.g., paroxetine, fluoxetine, quinidine):** Significantly increase exposure to active metabolites ($\alpha$-HTBZ and $\beta$-HTBZ). Maximum dose should not exceed 36 mg/day [US].
- **Reserpine:** Irreversible VMAT2 binding may lead to major monoamine depletion. Wait 20 days after reserpine discontinuation before starting treatment [US].
- **MAOIs:** Risk of psychiatric/neurological crisis. Contraindicated [US].
- **Alcohol/CNS Depressants:** May result in additive sedation and somnolence [US].
- **Neuroleptic Drugs:** Increased risk of NMS, parkinsonism, and akathisia [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** There are no adequate data on developmental risk in humans. Animal studies showed no clear embryofetal toxicity at 6 times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD), but tetrabenazine (related) caused increased stillbirths in rats [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** No data available regarding presence in human milk or effects on the infant. The benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the mother’s clinical need [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal data for tetrabenazine showed estrous cycle disruption; direct data for deutetrabenazine is limited [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established. Trials in Tourette syndrome failed to show efficacy [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65+ to determine if they respond differently. Cautious dosing is recommended due to higher potential for hepatic/renal/cardiac dysfunction [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specifically studied; however, active metabolites are primarily renally eliminated [US].
- **Poor CYP2D6 Metabolizers:** Exposure is increased approximately 3-fold. Maximum recommended dose is 36 mg/day [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Reversible depletion of monoamines (dopamine, serotonin, norepinephrine, and histamine) from nerve terminals via inhibition of VMAT2.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** At least 80% absorbed. Peak plasma levels of active metabolites reached in 3–4 hours [US].
  - **Distribution:** Active metabolites $\alpha$-HTBZ and $\beta$-HTBZ have high volumes of distribution (500 L and 730 L, respectively) [US].
  - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised in the liver, primarily via carbonyl reductase and subsequently by CYP2D6 [US].
  - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (75–86%). Half-life of active metabolites is approximately 9–11 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Huntington’s Disease (Study 1):** A 12-week trial (n=90) showed a statistically significant improvement in the Total Maximal Chorea Score (-4.4 units for treatment vs. -1.9 for placebo; p<0.0001) [US].
- **Tardive Dyskinesia:** Two 12-week trials (n=335) demonstrated significant improvement in Abnormal Involuntary Movement Scale (AIMS) scores compared to placebo [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from light and moisture. Dispense in the original, tight, light-resistant container with the desiccant canister [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription only (Rx-only). Contains a Boxed Warning regarding depression and suicidality in Huntington’s disease patients.
- **Australian (TGA):** Not specified in available sources.
- **European (EMA):** Not specified in available sources.
