# Editorial Product Information: drospirenone + estetrol

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **drospirenone + estetrol**
- Chemical class:
  - Drospirenone: synthetic progestin with anti-mineralocorticoid activity
  - Estetrol (E4): natural estrogen (native estrogenic steroidal compound)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 2021 [US]
- Date of last PI revision reported: February 2023 [US]
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources
- Registration/approval dates for jurisdictions other than the United States: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Hormonal contraception for prevention of pregnancy in females of reproductive potential.
- Limitation of effectiveness: May be less effective in females with body mass index (BMI) ≥ 30 kg/m2; decreasing effectiveness may be associated with increasing BMI.

(Regional differences in approved indications beyond the above: Not specified in available sources.)

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- One tablet once daily containing drospirenone 3 mg and estetrol 14.2 mg, taken at approximately the same time each day.
- Regimen: 28-day cycle composed of 24 active hormone tablets followed by 4 inert (non-hormonal) tablets.

### Administration instructions
- Start: Day 1 start (take first active tablet on the first day of menses).
- If not started on Day 1 of menses: use a non-hormonal back-up method (e.g., condoms and/or spermicide) until one active tablet has been taken daily for 7 consecutive days.
- Switching from another contraceptive:
  - From combined oral contraceptive: start the new pack when the next pack of previous product would have started.
  - From transdermal system or vaginal ring: start when the next application/insertion would have been scheduled.
  - From injectable: start when the next injection would have been scheduled.
  - From intrauterine system or implant: start after removal.
  - From progestin-only pill: start after the last tablet was taken.

### Postpartum and pregnancy-related guidance
- Do not start earlier than 4 weeks after delivery (in non-breastfeeding and breastfeeding females) because of increased risk of thromboembolism postpartum.
- After abortion or miscarriage:
  - ≤ 14 weeks gestation (first trimester): may start within first 7 days; if started within first 7 days, use back-up non-hormonal contraception for the next 7 days.
  - >14 weeks but ≤20 weeks gestation: start no earlier than 4 weeks after the event; if cycles have not resumed, consider pregnancy and use back-up for first 7 days if restarting before return of menses.

### Missed dose instructions
- If 1 active (pink) tablet missed: take as soon as possible and then take next tablet at scheduled time (may take two in one day). Continue daily.
- If ≥2 active tablets missed in Week 1 or Week 2: take one missed tablet as soon as possible (may take two that day), discard other missed tablets, continue daily; use additional non-hormonal contraception until active tablets have been taken for 7 consecutive days.
- If 2 active tablets missed in Week 3: take one missed tablet as soon as possible (may take two that day), discard remaining missed tablets, finish active tablets from pack, discard inert tablets, start a new pack the next day; use additional non-hormonal contraception until active tablets have been taken for 7 consecutive days.
- If one or more inert tablets missed: skip missed inert days and continue one tablet daily until pack is finished.
- After vomiting or acute diarrhea within 3–4 hours of dosing: take a new active tablet as soon as possible, within 12 hours of the usual dosing time if possible. If more than two tablets are missed or vomiting persists, follow missed-tablet guidance above and use back-up contraception as indicated.

### Titration schedules
- Not applicable / Not specified in available sources

## 5. Contraindications
(Include all conditions listed in available sources. Where specific subgroups are described, they are listed individually.)

- Cigarette smoking and age ≥ 35 years (contraindicated in females who smoke and are 35 years of age or older) [US].
- Current diagnosis or history of breast cancer, which may be hormonally sensitive.
- Known hepatic adenoma or malignant liver tumors.
- Acute hepatitis or severe (decompensated) cirrhosis.
- A history of, increased risk for, or current arterial or venous thrombotic/thromboembolic disease, including but not limited to:
  - Current or history of deep vein thrombosis (DVT) or pulmonary embolism (PE)
  - Cerebrovascular disease
  - Coronary artery disease or history of myocardial infarction
  - Thrombogenic valvular or rhythm diseases of the heart (e.g., subacute bacterial endocarditis with valvular disease, atrial fibrillation)
  - Inherited or acquired hypercoagulopathies (e.g., antiphospholipid syndrome)
- Uncontrolled hypertension or hypertension with vascular disease.
- Diabetes mellitus with hypertension or end-organ damage, or diabetes mellitus of > 20 years' duration.
- Migraines with aura.
- Abnormal uterine bleeding of undiagnosed etiology.
- Renal impairment.
- Adrenal insufficiency.
- Co-administration with hepatitis C drug combinations containing ombitasvir/paritaprevir/ritonavir, with or without dasabuvir (due to risk of significant ALT elevations).

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed / highlighted warning
- **Cigarette smoking increases the risk of serious cardiovascular events in combined hormonal contraceptive users; women aged 35 years or older who smoke should not use this product.** [US]

### General warnings and precautions
- Thromboembolic disorders and vascular events:
  - Combined hormonal contraceptives increase the risk of arterial (myocardial infarction, stroke) and venous thromboembolic events (DVT, PE). Evaluate individual and family history of thrombotic disorders; consider risk factors (age, smoking, obesity, hypertension, dyslipidemia, diabetes).
  - Risk of VTE is highest during the first year of use and when restarting after a break of ≥ 4 weeks; risk returns toward baseline after approximately 3 months following discontinuation.
  - Discontinue if a thrombotic or thromboembolic event occurs. Discontinue during prolonged immobilization.
  - Do not start earlier than 4 weeks postpartum due to elevated postpartum VTE risk.
- Hyperkalemia:
  - Drospirenone has anti-mineralocorticoid activity with potential to increase serum potassium. Check serum potassium during the first treatment cycle in women receiving long-term concomitant medications that may increase serum potassium.
  - Monitor serum potassium in women at increased risk for hyperkalemia (including those taking long-term strong CYP3A4 inhibitors concomitantly).
  - In pooled Phase 3 trials (N = 3,632), seven subjects developed hyperkalemia; one discontinued due to elevated potassium.
- Hypertension:
  - Monitor blood pressure periodically in all users; discontinue if blood pressure rises significantly. Contraindicated in uncontrolled hypertension or hypertension with vascular disease.
- Migraine:
  - Contraindicated with migraine with aura. Discontinue if new, recurrent, persistent, or severe migraines develop; increased frequency or severity may presage cerebrovascular events.
- Hormone-sensitive malignancies:
  - Contraindicated in current or past breast cancer. Discontinue if hormonally-sensitive malignancy is diagnosed.
- Liver disease:
  - Contraindicated in hepatic adenoma, hepatocellular carcinoma, acute hepatitis, or severe (decompensated) cirrhosis.
  - Withhold or permanently discontinue for persistent or significant elevations of liver enzymes.
  - CHCs increase the risk of hepatic tumors, including hepatic adenoma; rupture can be fatal.
- Risk with certain hepatitis C combination therapy:
  - Contraindicated with ombitasvir/paritaprevir/ritonavir-containing regimens (with or without dasabuvir) due to risk of marked ALT elevations; discontinue contraceptive prior to starting such regimens and resume approximately 2 weeks after completion of therapy.
- Glucose tolerance and hypertriglyceridemia:
  - Monitor glucose in women with prediabetes or diabetes. Consider alternative contraception in women with hypertriglyceridemia due to increased risk of pancreatitis.
- Gallbladder disease and cholestasis:
  - Consider discontinuation in women with symptomatic gallbladder disease or cholestasis; CHCs may worsen or increase risk.
- Bleeding irregularities and amenorrhea:
  - Irregular bleeding (including spotting) and amenorrhea may occur. Evaluate persistent abnormal bleeding for other causes.
- Depression:
  - Monitor for new or worsening depression during use.
- Hereditary angioedema:
  - Estrogens may exacerbate symptoms of hereditary angioedema.
- Chloasma (melasma):
  - May occur or worsen; avoid excessive sunlight or ultraviolet exposure.

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and periodic:
  - Blood pressure
  - Serum potassium when clinically indicated (see Hyperkalemia)
  - Liver function tests for persistent or clinically indicated abnormalities
  - Glucose monitoring in prediabetes/diabetes
  - Lipid profile if hypertriglyceridemia is suspected or present
- Evaluate abnormal uterine bleeding to exclude pregnancy or pathology

### Specific clinical precautions (summary)
- Consider overall cardiovascular risk profile before initiating.
- Use back-up contraception or alternative methods when interacting medications (e.g., CYP3A inducers) are required.
- Advise women to discontinue use and seek prompt evaluation for signs/symptoms of thromboembolism, severe abdominal pain (possible hepatic adenoma), jaundice, severe hypertension, or markedly elevated blood glucose/triglycerides.

## 7. Adverse Effects
- Most common adverse reactions reported (≥ 2%): bleeding irregularities, mood disturbance, headache, breast symptoms, dysmenorrhea, acne, weight increased, libido decreased.
- Serious adverse reactions described in the available sources include: venous thromboembolism (deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism), arterial thrombotic events (myocardial infarction, stroke), hyperkalemia, significant elevations of liver enzymes or hepatic tumors, gallbladder disease, pancreatitis (in the setting of hypertriglyceridemia), and severe depression. Frequency for many serious events is not specified in available sources.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- CYP3A inducers:
  - Concomitant use may reduce contraceptive efficacy and/or increase breakthrough bleeding. Avoid concurrent use when possible. If unavoidable, advise use of an alternative or additional non-hormonal contraceptive method during co-administration and for up to 28 days after discontinuation of the inducer.
- Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors:
  - Long-term concomitant use may increase systemic drospirenone exposure and may increase risk of hyperkalemia; monitor serum potassium in women at risk.
- Hepatitis C treatment regimens:
  - Co-administration with ombitasvir/paritaprevir/ritonavir containing regimens (with or without dasabuvir) is contraindicated because of increased risk of clinically significant elevations in ALT. Discontinue the contraceptive before starting such regimens and it may be resumed about 2 weeks after completion of hepatitis C therapy.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Concomitant use of other agents that increase serum potassium (e.g., potassium-sparing diuretics, potassium supplements, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, aldosterone antagonists) may increase risk of hyperkalemia; monitor serum potassium when such combinations are used.
- Estrogens may interact with medications or conditions that affect coagulation or hepatic metabolism, thereby altering thrombotic risk or hepatic enzyme levels.

### Clinically significant interactions and management
- If strong CYP3A inducers are required: provide back-up contraception during co-administration and for 28 days following discontinuation.
- For women taking long-term medications that can raise serum potassium: obtain baseline potassium and recheck during the first treatment cycle.
- Avoid concurrent use with contraindicated hepatitis C regimens; ensure contraceptive is discontinued before initiation and restarted after recommended interval.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Two prospective Phase 3 trials were conducted for prevention of pregnancy in females aged 16–50 years: one in Europe/Russia (C301) and one in North America (C302). Combined N = 3,632.
- Safety findings:
  - One venous thromboembolic event reported in the Europe/Russia study.
  - Seven subjects across Phase 3 studies developed hyperkalemia; one subject discontinued due to elevated potassium.
