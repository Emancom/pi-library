# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The PI documents contributing to this editorial include: **US (FDA)** (Baxter Healthcare Corporation).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine Injection for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data and regulatory requirements.
- **Note:** This synthesis is an editorial resource and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Doses should be administered every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Monitoring
- **Target Concentration:** For metabolic disorders, plasma free carnitine levels should be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Frequency:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations is recommended [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks/benefits before re-administration [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine (oral or IV) has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been reported [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** Chronic administration of high oral doses in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in urine [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Periodic monitoring of INR is recommended for patients concurrently receiving warfarin [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
In double-blind, placebo-controlled trials, the following adverse events were reported at a frequency of ≥5% (regardless of causality):
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), headache (22%), infection (15%), accidental injury (10%), chest pain (12%), flu syndrome (25%) [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), palpitations (5%) [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (16%), abdominal pain (9%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), anorexia (5%) [US].
- **Neurological:** Dizziness (13%), paresthesia (5%), insomnia (4%), depression (5%) [US].
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%) [US].
- **Musculoskeletal:** Leg cramps (4%) [US].
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%), hypervolemia (5%) [US].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Anaphylaxis and respiratory distress** (bronchospasm, laryngeal edema) [US].
- **Seizures** [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. However, there are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after exogenous administration. Risks to the child must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg body weight (refer to Section 3) [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. IV levocarnitine is used in ESRD patients; however, dose reductions may be required based on trough concentrations [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific dose adjustments for the elderly are not specified in available sources, though general caution regarding organ function is standard.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following a single IV dose, ~76% is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; the mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Efficacy:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly, mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine concentrations increased from ~20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** A study comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses in ESRD patients showed dose-proportional increases in trough concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of an opened vial as the formulation is preservative-free [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources for other jurisdictions.
