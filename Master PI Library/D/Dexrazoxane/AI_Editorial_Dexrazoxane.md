# Editorial Product Information: Dexrazoxane

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official, legally binding Product Information used for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Dexrazoxane (INN).
- **Chemical Class:** Detoxifying agent for antineoplastic treatment; topoisomerase II inhibitor.
- **Brand Names:** Dexrazoxane-Reach [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** V03AF02.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Lyophilised powder for injection (vial): 250 mg and 500 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Reach Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Cardioprotection in Metastatic Breast Cancer:** Indicated for the reduction of incidence and severity of cardiomyopathy associated with doxorubicin administration in women with metastatic breast cancer who have already received a cumulative doxorubicin dose of 300 mg/m² and require continued doxorubicin therapy for tumour control [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** The recommended dosage ratio of **dexrazoxane** to doxorubicin is 10:1 (e.g., 500 mg/m² of **dexrazoxane** for every 50 mg/m² of doxorubicin).
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Administer via IV infusion over 15 minutes.
    - **Crucial Timing:** Do not administer doxorubicin prior to **dexrazoxane**. Doxorubicin must be administered within 30 minutes following the completion of the **dexrazoxane** infusion.
    - **Safety Note:** Do not administer via intravenous push.
- **Reconstitution and Dilution:**
    - Reconstitute with Sterile Water for Injection to a concentration of 10 mg/mL or 20 mg/mL.
    - Reconstituted solution has a low pH (~1.6) and must be further diluted in Lactated Ringer’s Injection or 0.16 M sodium lactate to reduce the risk of thrombophlebitis.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources (typically managed within a clinical oncology setting).

## 4. Contraindications
- **Age:** Contraindicated in children and adolescents aged 0 to 18 years [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to **dexrazoxane**.
- **Lactation:** Breastfeeding is contraindicated during treatment.
- **Vaccination:** Concomitant administration with the yellow fever vaccine [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Myelosuppression:** Effects may be additive to those of concurrent chemotherapy. Nadir cell counts may be lower than with chemotherapy alone. Frequent haematological monitoring is required.
- **Second Primary Malignancies:** As a cytotoxic agent with topoisomerase II inhibition activity, **dexrazoxane** in combination with chemotherapy may increase the risk of secondary malignancies, particularly Acute Myeloid Leukaemia (AML) and Myelodysplastic Syndrome (MDS) [Australian].
- **Cardiac Monitoring:** Standard cardiac monitoring associated with anthracycline treatment (e.g., LVEF assessment) must continue. There is no data to support use in patients with pre-existing heart failure, myocardial infarction, or symptomatic valvular disease.
- **Thromboembolism:** Increased risk when used in combination with chemotherapy.
- **Anaphylaxis:** Rare but serious reactions, including angioedema and hypotension, have occurred.
- **Handling:** As a cytotoxic agent, **dexrazoxane** must be handled by trained staff using protective clothing. Reconstitution should not be performed by pregnant staff.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by concurrent anthracycline-based chemotherapy.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Anaemia, leukopenia, nausea, vomiting, stomatitis, alopecia, asthenia. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, anorexia, paraesthesia, dizziness, headache, peripheral neuropathy, phlebitis, dyspnoea, diarrhoea, abdominal pain, increased transaminases, nail disorders, pyrexia, injection site reactions. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Sepsis, infection, AML, eosinophilia, syncope, vertigo, venous thrombosis, gingivitis, cellulitis. |
| **Not Known** | Anaphylactic reaction, embolism, pulmonary embolism. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Myelosuppression, secondary malignancies (AML/MDS), and anaphylactic shock.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Vaccines:** Contraindicated with yellow fever vaccine (risk of fatal generalised vaccine disease). Not recommended with other live attenuated vaccines.
- **Phenytoin:** Cytotoxic agents may reduce phenytoin absorption, potentially exacerbating convulsions.
- **Ciclosporin/Tacrolimus:** Risk of excessive immunosuppression and lymphoproliferative disease.
- **Chemotherapy Interference:** While some studies suggested **dexrazoxane** might interfere with anthracycline anti-tumour efficacy, most adult studies show no significant difference in overall survival or time to progression.
- **Epirubicin:** Limited evidence suggests **dexrazoxane** may increase the clearance of epirubicin at high doses (120–135 mg/m²).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category D:** **Dexrazoxane** is embryotoxic and teratogenic in animal models. It should not be used in pregnant women unless clearly necessary.
    - **Contraception:** Sexually active men and women must use effective contraception during and for at least 6 months after treatment cessation.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in infants.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies show testicular atrophy; **dexrazoxane** may impair male fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Contraindicated in patients aged 0 to 18 years. Safety and efficacy have not been established in this population [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Caution is advised due to higher rates of concomitant disease and potential for decreased organ function (hepatic, renal, or cardiac).
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Moderate to Severe (CrCl < 40 mL/min):** The dose of **dexrazoxane** should be reduced by 50%.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No specific dose adjustment provided, but routine liver function tests (LFTs) are recommended before and during treatment in patients with known liver disorders.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Anthracycline-induced cardiotoxicity is believed to be caused by iron-dependent free radical oxidative stress. **Dexrazoxane** is a pro-drug that is hydrolysed in cardiac cells to ICRF-198, which chelates metal ions, preventing the formation of reactive radicals. It also inhibits topoisomerase IIb.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Low protein binding (2%); distributes mainly into total body water. Does not significantly penetrate the CSF.
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolised by dihydropyrimidine amidohydrolase and dihydroorotase to ring-opened metabolites.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 40% excreted unchanged in urine. Terminal half-life is approximately 2.2 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adult Studies:** Clinical trials in advanced breast cancer demonstrated that the 10:1 dosing ratio provides cardioprotection without significantly impacting survival or tumour response compared to higher ratios (20:1), which were previously associated with higher mortality rates in some trials.
- **Paediatric Data (P9404/P9754):** While contraindicated in children in Australia, trials (e.g., Children's Oncology Group) have evaluated use in T-ALL and osteosarcoma. In Study P9404, children receiving **dexrazoxane** had better-preserved left ventricular wall thickness and structure 3 years post-diagnosis compared to those receiving doxorubicin alone.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Vials:** Store below 25°C. Protect from light by keeping in the original package.
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Stable for 30 minutes at room temperature or up to 4 hours under refrigeration (2°C–8°C).
- **Diluted Infusion:** Stable for 6 hours at room temperature or up to 24 hours under refrigeration (2°C–8°C).

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
