# Editorial Product Information: Demeclocycline

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: US (FDA).
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of demeclocycline hydrochloride, synthesised from official product information for use by healthcare professionals. 
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the United States (FDA) source documentation was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Demeclocycline hydrochloride; a tetracycline antibiotic isolated from a mutant strain of *Streptomyces aureofaciens*.
- **Brand Names:** Not specified in available sources (Generic: Amneal Pharmaceuticals) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Oral Tablets: 150 mg and 300 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Amneal Pharmaceuticals of New York LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
Demeclocycline is indicated for the treatment of infections caused by susceptible strains of designated microorganisms, including [US]:
- **Rickettsial Infections:** Rocky Mountain spotted fever, typhus group fevers, Q fever, rickettsialpox, and tick fevers.
- **Respiratory Tract Infections:** Caused by *Mycoplasma pneumoniae*.
- **Chlamydial Infections:** Lymphogranuloma venereum, psittacosis (ornithosis), and inclusion conjunctivitis.
- **Sexually Transmitted/Urogenital Infections:** Nongonococcal urethritis (adults) caused by *Ureaplasma urealyticum* or *Chlamydia trachomatis*.
- **Specific Bacterial Infections:** Relapsing fever (*Borrelia recurrentis*), chancroid (*Haemophilus ducreyi*), plague (*Yersinia pestis*), tularemia (*Francisella tularensis*), cholera (*Vibrio cholerae*), and brucellosis (in conjunction with streptomycin).
- **Alternative Therapy:** Used when penicillin is contraindicated for the treatment of syphilis (*Treponema pallidum*), yaws (*Treponema pallidum pertenue*), listeriosis (*Listeria monocytogenes*), anthrax (*Bacillus anthracis*), and actinomycosis (*Actinomyces israelii*).
- **Adjunctive Therapy:** Severe acne and acute intestinal amebiasis.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Usual Daily Dose:** 600 mg daily, administered as either 150 mg four times daily or 300 mg twice daily [US].

### Dosing by Indication
- **Gonorrhea (Penicillin-sensitive patients):** Initial dose of 600 mg, followed by 300 mg every 12 hours for four days (total 3 grams) [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Timing:** Administer at least 1 hour before or 2 hours after meals [US].
- **Absorption:** Do not administer with dairy products, antacids (containing aluminium, calcium, or magnesium), or iron preparations, as these reduce absorption by more than 50% [US].
- **Oesophageal Safety:** Administer with adequate fluids to reduce the risk of oesophageal irritation and ulceration. Do not take immediately before lying down [US].

### Duration of Therapy
- Continue treatment for at least 24 to 48 hours after symptoms and fever have subsided [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to demeclocycline, any other tetracycline-class antibiotic, or any excipient in the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Fetal Harm:** **Demeclocycline can cause fetal harm when administered to pregnant women.** It crosses the placenta and can have toxic effects on skeletal development [US].
- **Tooth Discoloration:** Use during tooth development (last half of pregnancy through age 8 years) may cause permanent yellow-gray-brown discoloration of the teeth and enamel hypoplasia [US].
- **Photosensitivity:** Exaggerated sunburn reactions can occur. Patients should avoid direct sunlight or UV light; discontinue at the first sign of skin erythema [US].
- **Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus:** Long-term therapy may induce a dose-dependent and reversible diabetes insipidus syndrome (polyuria, polydipsia, weakness) [US].
- **Clostridium difficile-Associated Diarrhoea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all antibacterial agents; severity ranges from mild diarrhoea to fatal colitis [US].
- **Pseudotumor Cerebri:** Benign intracranial hypertension has been reported in adults. Symptoms include headache and blurred vision [US].
- **Antianabolic Action:** Tetracyclines may increase BUN. In patients with renal impairment, this may lead to azotaemia, hyperphosphataemia, and acidosis [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Note: Frequencies are not specified in the available source [US].*

- **Gastrointestinal:** Anorexia, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, glossitis, dysphagia, enterocolitis, pancreatitis, and oesophageal ulcerations.
- **Skin:** Maculopapular/erythematous rashes, photosensitivity, exfoliative dermatitis (uncommon), Stevens-Johnson syndrome (rare), and pigmentation of skin/mucous membranes.
- **Renal:** Acute renal failure, rise in BUN (dose-related), and nephrogenic diabetes insipidus.
- **CNS:** Headache, dizziness, tinnitus, visual disturbances, and pseudotumor cerebri.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Urticaria, angioneurotic oedema, anaphylaxis, and exacerbation of systemic lupus erythematosus.
- **Hematologic:** Hemolytic anaemia, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, and eosinophilia.
- **Other:** Microscopic brown-black discoloration of thyroid glands (prolonged use).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Antacids and Iron:** Absorption is impaired by preparations containing aluminium, calcium, magnesium, or iron [US].
- **Anticoagulants:** Tetracyclines depress plasma prothrombin activity; may require downward adjustment of anticoagulant doses [US].
- **Penicillin:** Bacteriostatic drugs may interfere with the bactericidal action of penicillin; avoid co-administration [US].
- **Oral Contraceptives:** Concurrent use may render oral contraceptives less effective [US].
- **Methoxyflurane:** Concurrent use has been reported to result in fatal renal toxicity [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [US]. Tetracyclines are known to cross the placenta and may cause fetal harm and permanent tooth discoloration.
- **Breastfeeding:** Tetracyclines are excreted in human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants, a decision should be made to either discontinue nursing or discontinue the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No effect on fertility was demonstrated in rat studies at doses 45 times the human dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Contraindicated in children under 8 years of age due to risk of permanent tooth discoloration and effects on bone growth, unless other drugs are ineffective or contraindicated [US].
    - *Pediatric Dose (>8 years):* 7 mg/kg to 13 mg/kg per day, divided into 2 to 4 doses (max 600 mg/day) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed; generally follows adult dosing with caution for renal/hepatic function [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution. High serum levels may lead to liver toxicity and azotaemia. Total dosage should be decreased by reducing individual doses or extending intervals [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution; decrease total dosage as per renal impairment guidelines [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Primarily bacteriostatic. Inhibits bacterial protein synthesis by binding to the 30S ribosomal subunit [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Slower than tetracycline; peak concentrations reached at approximately 4 hours [US].
    - **Distribution:** Penetrates well into various body fluids and tissues. 40% to 90% protein bound [US].
    - **Metabolism/Excretion:** Concentrated in the liver and excreted in bile. Approximately 44% is excreted in urine and 13-46% in faeces as active drug within 96 hours [US].
    - **Half-life:** Ranges between 10 and 16 hours [US].
