# Editorial Product Information: Didanosine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the Product Information for didanosine. It is designed as a concise, clinically accurate reference for healthcare professionals.
- **Note:** This synthesis is an editorial overview and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Didanosine (also known as ddI), a synthetic purine nucleoside analogue.
- **Brand Names:** Not specified in available sources (Generic formulation by Teva/Physicians Total Care, Inc. provided).
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Delayed-Release Capsules (enteric-coated pellets):** 200 mg, 250 mg, and 400 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Teva Pharmaceuticals USA; Distributed by Physicians Total Care, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **HIV-1 Infection:** Indicated for use in combination with other antiretroviral agents for the treatment of human immunodeficiency virus (HIV)-1 infection [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Pediatrics)
Didanosine must be administered on an **empty stomach**. Capsules should be swallowed intact. Dosing is strictly based on body weight.

**Table 1: Once-Daily Dosing Schedule (Adults and Pediatrics ≥20 kg)**
| Body Weight | Dose (Once Daily) |
| :--- | :--- |
| 20 kg to <25 kg | 200 mg |
| 25 kg to <60 kg | 250 mg |
| ≥60 kg | 400 mg |

### Pediatric Specifics
- Approved for pediatric patients aged 6 to 18 years who can swallow capsules and weigh at least 20 kg [US].
- For patients <20 kg or those unable to swallow capsules, a pediatric powder for oral solution should be used (refer to specific PI for that formulation) [US].

### Dose Adjustments
- **Tenofovir Disoproxil Fumarate Coadministration:** A dose reduction of didanosine is required.
    - Adults ≥60 kg (CrCl ≥60 mL/min): Reduce to 250 mg once daily.
    - Adults <60 kg (CrCl ≥60 mL/min): Reduce to 200 mg once daily.
    - Administer with tenofovir and either a light meal (≤400 kcal, ≤20% fat) or in a fasted state [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required [US].

### Missed Dose Instructions
Patients should take a missed dose as soon as possible. If it is nearly time for the next scheduled dose, the missed dose should be skipped. Doses should not be doubled [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Allopurinol:** Coadministration is contraindicated due to significantly increased didanosine systemic exposure and associated toxicity [US].
- **Ribavirin:** Coadministration is contraindicated. Increased exposure to the active metabolite of didanosine may lead to fatal hepatic failure, peripheral neuropathy, pancreatitis, and symptomatic hyperlactatemia/lactic acidosis [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
- **Pancreatitis:** Fatal and nonfatal cases have occurred. Suspend therapy if pancreatitis is suspected; discontinue if confirmed. Use with extreme caution in patients with risk factors or those on stavudine.
- **Lactic Acidosis and Severe Hepatomegaly with Steatosis:** Fatal cases reported, particularly with nucleoside analogues. A specific high risk exists for pregnant women receiving the combination of didanosine and stavudine.

### General Warnings
- **Hepatic Toxicity:** Safety not established in patients with significant underlying liver disease. Monitor for worsening liver function. Avoid combination with hydroxyurea [US].
- **Non-cirrhotic Portal Hypertension:** Postmarketing cases (some fatal) reported. Monitor for early signs (e.g., thrombocytopenia, splenomegaly). Discontinue if evidence of portal hypertension develops [US].
- **Peripheral Neuropathy:** Occurs more frequently in advanced HIV or with neurotoxic drugs (e.g., stavudine). Consider discontinuation if symptoms develop [US].
- **Retinal Changes and Optic Neuritis:** Reported in both adult and pediatric patients. Periodic retinal examinations are recommended [US].
- **Immune Reconstitution Syndrome:** Inflammatory responses to indolent opportunistic infections or autoimmune disorders (e.g., Graves’ disease) may occur during the initial treatment phase [US].
- **Fat Redistribution:** Observed changes include central obesity, "buffalo hump," and peripheral wasting [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency in Adults (Study AI454-152)
| Adverse Reaction | Frequency (All Grades) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Diarrhea | 57% |
| Peripheral Neuropathy | 25% |
| Nausea | 24% |
| Headache | 22% |
| Rash | 14% |
| Vomiting | 14% |
| Pancreatitis | <1% |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Fatal pancreatitis.
- Fatal lactic acidosis/steatosis.
- Hepatotoxicity and liver failure.
- Non-cirrhotic portal hypertension.
- Retinal depigmentation and optic neuritis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **Tenofovir:** Increases didanosine AUC by 48–60%. Requires dose reduction [US].
    - **Ganciclovir:** Increases didanosine concentration. Monitor for toxicity [US].
    - **Methadone:** Decreases didanosine concentration. Monitor viral load. Avoid using pediatric powder with methadone [US].
    - **Nelfinavir:** No interaction if nelfinavir is administered 1 hour after didanosine [US].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **Stavudine/Hydroxyurea:** Increased risk of pancreatitis, neuropathy, and hepatotoxicity. Combination with hydroxyurea should be avoided [US].
    - **Neurotoxic/Pancreatotoxic Drugs:** Use with extreme caution [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B** [US]. Use only if the potential benefit justifies the risk. 
    - **Warning:** Fatal lactic acidosis has occurred in pregnant women using didanosine with stavudine. This combination should be avoided in pregnancy unless the benefit clearly outweighs the risk [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** HIV-infected mothers should not breastfeed to avoid postnatal transmission of HIV. It is unknown if didanosine is excreted in human milk, though animal studies show excretion in rat milk [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established in children ≥6 years weighing ≥20 kg for delayed-release capsules [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Higher frequency of pancreatitis (10% vs 5% in younger patients). Monitor renal function closely and adjust dose accordingly [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dose reduction is required based on Creatinine Clearance (CrCl) [US]:

**Table 2: Dosing in Renal Impairment (Adults)**
| CrCl (mL/min) | Dose if ≥60 kg | Dose if <60 kg |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ≥60 | 400 mg QD | 250 mg QD |
| 30–59 | 200 mg QD | 125 mg QD |
| 10–29 | 125 mg QD | 125 mg QD |
| <10 | 125 mg QD | 125 mg QD |

- **Dialysis:** For patients on Hemodialysis or CAPD, follow the <10 mL/min dosing. Supplemental doses after hemodialysis are not required [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Didanosine is an NRTI. It is converted intracellularly to the active metabolite dideoxyadenosine 5’-triphosphate, which inhibits HIV-1 reverse transcriptase by competing with natural substrates and causing DNA chain termination [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid; peak plasma levels in 0.25 to 1.5 hours. Bioavailability is ~42%.
    - **Food Effect:** Food reduces $C_{max}$ by 46% and AUC by 19%. Must be taken fasted [US].
    - **Distribution:** Low protein binding (<5%). CSF-to-plasma ratio is ~21%.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (18% as unchanged drug). Elimination half-life is ~1.5 hours in adults [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study AI454-152:** A 48-week open-label study in 511 treatment-naive adults. Didanosine + stavudine + nelfinavir showed a virologic response (HIV RNA <400 copies/mL) in 55% of patients, comparable to the zidovudine/lamivudine + nelfinavir arm (56%) [US].
- **Pediatric Studies (ACTG 152):** Demonstrated that didanosine monotherapy or combination with zidovudine resulted in lower rates of disease progression or death compared to zidovudine alone [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Keep container tightly closed and out of reach of children [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** **Rx-only** (Prescription only) [US].
