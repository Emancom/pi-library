# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided for the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview designed for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data to provide a concise, expert-level reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine, USP (INN: Levocarnitine).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Not specified in provided source (Baxter Healthcare Corporation).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **US:** Sterile injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing chronic hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    - **Initial Crisis Management:** A loading dose may be given in severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
    - **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; should not be administered less frequently than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been used [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Recommended Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
    - **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Monitoring and Instructions
- **Target Plasma Levels:** For metabolic disorders, maintain free carnitine concentrations between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Pre-treatment Assessment:** Obtain plasma carnitine concentrations, blood chemistries, and vital signs prior to parenteral therapy [US].
- **Inspection:** Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** While the source does not list specific contraindications, it warns against use in patients who experience severe hypersensitivity to the product [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with reports of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulations):** The safety of oral levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported at a frequency of ≥5% in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials for ESRD:

| Body System | Adverse Event | Levocarnitine (10-40 mg) | Placebo |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain, Fever, Headache, Infection, Injection site reaction, Pain | 7% - 33% | 5% - 59% |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, Hypotension, Tachycardia | 6% - 20% | 5% - 19% |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, Nausea, Vomiting | 8% - 16% | 10% - 19% |
| **Respiratory** | Cough, Pharyngitis, Rhinitis | 9% - 23% | 10% - 33% |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, Paresthesia | 5% - 13% | 3% - 11% |

### Other Adverse Effects
- **Common/Frequent:** Transient nausea and vomiting [US].
- **Less Frequent:** Body odor, gastritis [US].
- **Post-marketing:** Seizures, anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels in patients on warfarin therapy after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Classification:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate/well-controlled human studies exist. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration. Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of therapy for the mother [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is weight-based (mg/kg), covering paediatric ranges as specified in Dosage and Administration [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** See Warnings and Precautions regarding the accumulation of metabolites with oral formulations. For IV use, the dose is specifically titrated for ESRD patients post-dialysis [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Naturally occurring carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, facilitating oxidation and energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Promotes excretion of excess organic/fatty acids in patients with defects in fatty acid metabolism or organic acidopathies [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of a single IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Efficacy:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly showed mean pre-dialysis (trough) levels increased from ~20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks. Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) confirmed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C [US].
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
