# Editorial Product Information: fluorouracil + salicylic

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **fluorouracil + salicylic**
- Strength: 1 g of topical solution contains 5 mg fluorouracil and 100 mg salicylic acid. (As stated in source material.)
- Chemical class:
  - Fluorouracil: cytostatic antimetabolite (fluoropyrimidine)
  - Salicylic acid: phenolic aromatic keratolytic/keratolytic agent
- Excipients with known effect (as stated): contains ethanol (approximately 19% v/v) and dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) which may be an irritant.
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration: topical only.
- Important: Do not include or apply to mucous membranes or ocular surfaces.

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources.
- Date of last revision/update of available product information: Not specified in available sources.
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.
- Additional regulatory note: This product is subject to additional monitoring in Australia. [Australian]

## 3. Approved Indications
- Topical treatment of solar (actinic) keratosis.
- Use limited to actinic keratosis of mild to moderate severity (Grade I–II according to Olsen 1991).
- Anatomical sites specified for use: face, forehead and bald scalp only (use on eyelids, lips and mucosa is excluded).
- Use on other indications (e.g., basal cell carcinoma, Bowen’s disease) has not been established and is not recommended.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Apply once daily to affected lesion(s) or as field treatment on an area up to 25 cm2 (5 cm × 5 cm) once daily until lesions have completely cleared or for a maximum of 12 weeks.
- If field treatment of an area larger than 25 cm2 is required, treat sequential sections not exceeding 25 cm2 at a time; treat the clinically most relevant area first.

### Dosing by indication / application
- Focal application: apply to the lesion and a rim of up to 0.5 cm of surrounding healthy skin. Experience exists for treating up to ten lesions concurrently.
- Field-directed application: multiple lesions and surrounding skin may be treated simultaneously but total area at any one time must not exceed 25 cm2.

### Administration instructions
- For topical use only. Not for oral, ophthalmic, intranasal, intravaginal, intra-aural canal or intra-anal use.
- Apply using the supplied applicator (brush) and avoid overloading the brush; wipe excess on the neck of the bottle prior to application.
- Do not cover the treated area after application. The solution forms a white film after solvent evaporation; remove any existing film (peel off, warm water may assist) before each reapplication.
- Protect treated areas from direct sunlight and UV exposure where possible (physical barriers such as clothing/hats). If the treated skin is unbroken, apply a broad-spectrum sunscreen (SPF 30 or higher) daily before outdoor exposure and reapply regularly.
- Do not allow contact with textiles or acrylics (may cause staining).
- Do not apply to hairy skin without prior hair removal (hair may become matted).
- Keep away from fire or open flame.

### Dose modification/titration
- If severe local side effects occur, reduce application frequency to three times per week until side effects improve, then resume once-daily as tolerated.
- In case of bleeding from the treated site, stop treatment until the adverse reaction improves.

### Missed dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to fluorouracil, salicylic acid, acetylsalicylic acid (aspirin), or any excipient.
- Pregnancy, existing or suspected — must not be used during pregnancy or by women for whom pregnancy cannot be excluded. [Australian: Pregnancy Category D]
- Lactation — must not be used during breastfeeding.
- Renal insufficiency / renal impairment — must not be used.
- Known dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase (DPD) deficiency.
- Concomitant use with potent inhibitors of DPD (e.g., certain nucleoside analogues) — must not be used in conjunction.
- Product must not come into contact with eyes or mucous membranes.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- **Critical warning:** Use only on a total area no greater than 25 cm2 at any one time. Excessive treated surface increases frequency and severity of local adverse reactions.
- Local skin reactions (erythema, inflammation, irritation including burning, pain, pruritus, bleeding, erosion) are common and usually reflect pharmacological effect; severe local reactions can occur and may require dose reduction or treatment interruption.
- Application to areas with thin epidermis (e.g., around the eyes, temples) can increase risk of systemic absorption and local adverse events; apply less frequently and monitor more closely if treating such areas.
- Do not apply to bleeding lesions.
- Avoid concomitant use with other topical dermatologic agents that have drying, peeling, or abrasive effects (e.g., abrasive/medicated cleansers, benzoyl peroxide, resorcinol, sulfur, tretinoin, topical alcohol-containing preparations, isotretinoin, or cosmetics with strong drying effects) because of risk of cumulative irritation.
- No data available on use outside the approved anatomical sites (face, forehead, bald scalp) or on skin affected by another disease; outcomes may differ.
- Contains dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), which may be an irritant.
- Fluorouracil is a cytostatic agent; systemic toxicity is a theoretical concern if significant systemic absorption occurs.
- DPD enzyme considerations:
  - The enzyme dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase (DPD) is important in fluorouracil catabolism. Inhibition, deficiency, or decreased activity of DPD can lead to accumulation of fluorouracil and increased toxicity.
  - If applicable, determination of DPD enzyme activity should be considered before starting therapy with fluorouracil-containing preparations.
- Phenytoin: patients taking phenytoin concomitantly should have plasma phenytoin levels monitored due to potential for elevated phenytoin concentrations (reported with systemic fluorouracil).
- Patients with sensory disturbances (e.g., diabetic neuropathy) require close monitoring of the treatment area.
- Safety and efficacy for treatment of recurrent lesions have not been evaluated in clinical trials.
- Use in the elderly: usual adult dose may be used.
- Paediatric use: safety and efficacy not established; not indicated for paediatric populations.
- Effects on laboratory tests: Not applicable / Not specified in available sources.

## 7. Adverse Effects
- General comment: Local application-site reactions are the most frequently reported adverse effects and are expected due to the pharmacological action of the active ingredients. Most are mild to moderate, but severe local reactions have been reported. No serious drug-related adverse events were reported in the pivotal trial population described.

- Frequency convention used (as in available sources):

## 8. Drug Interactions
- DPD inhibitors:
  - Concomitant administration of potent DPD inhibitors (for example certain nucleoside analogues) may markedly increase plasma fluorouracil concentrations and toxicity.
  - An interval of at least 4 weeks between fluorouracil use and nucleoside analogues is recommended where applicable.
  - If nucleoside analogues are accidentally administered to a patient receiving fluorouracil, consider measures to reduce fluorouracil toxicity and hospital admission if indicated; provide supportive care including protection from systemic infection and correction of dehydration.
- Phenytoin:
  - Elevated plasma phenytoin levels and symptoms of phenytoin intoxication have been reported with concomitant administration of systemic fluorouracil; patients receiving phenytoin should have levels monitored regularly when fluorouracil is used.
- Salicylates:
  - There is no strong evidence for clinically relevant systemic absorption of salicylic acid after topical application, but absorbed salicylate may interact with methotrexate and sulphonylureas.
- Topical dermatologic agents:
  - Concurrent use with drying, peeling, desquamating or abrasive topical agents may lead to cumulative irritant effects; avoid combining such products.
- Other interactions:
  - Known DPD deficiency is a contraindication due to risk of fluorouracil accumulation.
- Jurisdictional differences in interaction warnings: Not specified beyond the above in available sources.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- Pregnancy:
  - **Contraindicated in pregnancy.** [Australian: Pregnancy Category D]
  - There are no data from topical fluorouracil use in pregnant women. Systemic fluorouracil has shown teratogenic/embryotoxic effects in animals; salicylic acid has adversely influenced pregnancy outcomes in rodents. The potential human risk is unknown.
- Breastfeeding:
  - Use during lactation is contraindicated because it is unknown whether fluorouracil or metabolites are excreted in human milk after topical application.
- Fertility:
  - Systemic fluorouracil studies in animals showed transient male infertility and reduced pregnancy rates in female rodents; topical use is expected to have limited systemic exposure, so clinical relevance in humans is uncertain.
  - No studies of fertility with the combined fluorouracil/salicylic acid topical product were reported.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
- Paediatric use:
  - Safety and efficacy have not been established in paediatric populations. Solar keratosis is not typically a paediatric disease. Not recommended.
- Elderly:
  - No special dose adjustment indicated; usual adult dosing may be used.
- Renal impairment:
  - Use is contraindicated in renal insufficiency / renal impairment.
- Hepatic impairment:
  - Not specified in available sources (no dosing adjustments or data on use in hepatic impairment were provided).

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Fluorouracil (5‑FU): a cytostatic antimetabolite that interferes with thymine nucleotide formation and incorporation into RNA/DNA, inhibiting DNA and RNA synthesis in rapidly proliferating cells (targeting dysplastic keratinocytes in actinic keratoses).
- Salicylic acid: keratolytic and corneolytic agent that disrupts corneocyte adhesion and intercellular cement, aiding removal of hyperkeratotic material and enhancing penetration of fluorouracil. Also has anti‑inflammatory and anti‑exudative properties possibly via prostaglandin synthesis inhibition and vasoconstriction.
- Dimethyl sulfoxide (excipient) acts as a solvent/penetration enhancer for fluorouracil.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Pharmacodynamic studies of the combination were not reported in the available sources.
- Systemic absorption:
  - No strong evidence of clinically relevant systemic absorption of salicylic acid; however increased absorption may occur when applied to skin with a thin epidermis.
  - Increased treated surface area and impaired skin barrier may increase systemic exposure risk.
- Detailed ADME (absorption, distribution, metabolism, elimination) parameters for topical fluorouracil + salicylic acid were not specified in the available sources.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Pivotal randomized trial (Study No. H1005 6002-0702 — Phase III):
  - Design: Randomized, placebo‑controlled, double‑blind, three‑armed, parallel‑group, multicenter trial with 470 patients having Olsen Grade I–II actinic keratoses (4–10 lesions) on face/forehead/bald scalp; treatment area up to 25 cm2; treatment up to 12 weeks or until lesion clearance/ulceration.
  - Comparators: fluorouracil + salicylic vs 3% diclofenac gel vs placebo.
  - Primary endpoint: histological clearance of a pre‑defined representative target lesion assessed 8 weeks after end of treatment.
  - Key efficacy results:
    - Histological clearance at post‑treatment assessment: 70.1% (fluorouracil + salicylic) vs 54.1% (diclofenac) vs 42.7% (placebo) — full‑analysis set.
    - Lesion clearance rate at post‑treatment: 74.5% (fluorouracil + salicylic) vs 54.6% (diclofenac) vs 36.5% (placebo).
    - Complete response (all lesions cleared): 55.4% (fluorouracil + salicylic) vs 32.0% (diclofenac) vs 15.1% (placebo); statistical superiority reported for fluorouracil + salicylic vs both comparator arms.
  - Safety: Local skin reactions common; no serious drug‑related adverse events reported in this trial population.
- Field-directed Phase III study (Study No. 98605101-1401):
  - Design details: field-directed application studies reported; adverse event rates were high, with general disorders/administration site conditions reported in ~99.1% of treated subjects and higher rates of premature discontinuation compared with focal application.
  - Safety: adverse events consistent with focal application but increased rates and severity observed with field application; discontinuation more frequent.
- Phase II comparator-controlled trial (Study No. H1005 6002-1007):
  - Reported findings: most common local skin reactions included erythema, scab/crusting and burning, mainly mild to moderate.
- Onset and duration of effect:
  - A clinical response may be observed as early as 4 weeks into treatment; response increases over time up to the allowed treatment duration (12 weeks).
  - Complete healing or optimal therapeutic effect may not be apparent for up to 8 weeks post‑treatment cessation.
- Other trial observations:
  - Patients were generally not tested for DPD deficiency in the pivotal trial; patients with known DPD deficiency were excluded.
  - When larger contiguous areas are treated, local adverse reaction rates and severity increase.
