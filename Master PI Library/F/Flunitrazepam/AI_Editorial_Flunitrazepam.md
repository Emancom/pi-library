# Editorial Product Information: Flunitrazepam

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised clinical overview for healthcare professionals. It synthesises available regulatory data into a single, coherent reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official, legally binding Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Flunitrazepam (a benzodiazepine derivative).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Hypnodorm** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N05CD03.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - 1 mg film-coated tablets (oval, convex, green, debossed "FM/1" and alpha symbol) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Viatris (Alphapharm Pty Ltd) [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Severe Insomnia:** Short-term management of severe cases of insomnia [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Standard Dose:** 1 mg to 2 mg administered immediately before retiring to bed [Australian].
- **Duration:** Treatment should generally be limited to a few nights or a short period (e.g., 2 to 4 weeks). Continuous long-term use is not recommended due to the risk of tolerance and dependence [Australian].

### Special Populations
- **Elderly:** 0.5 mg to 1 mg at bedtime [Australian].
- **Renal or Hepatic Impairment:** Initiate treatment with low doses and titrate slowly based on clinical response [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Administer immediately before going to bed. 
- **Tapering:** Discontinue treatment gradually to mitigate the risk of withdrawal symptoms and rebound insomnia [Australian].
- Due to plasma accumulation, a 2 mg dose should not be used daily by patients requiring high levels of concentration in the early part of the following day [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to flunitrazepam, other benzodiazepines, or any excipients (e.g., lactose, sulfites) [Australian].
- Myasthenia gravis [Australian].
- Sleep apnoea [Australian].
- Chronic obstructive airways disease with incipient respiratory failure [Australian].
- Severe hepatic insufficiency [Australian].
- Use in children [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Angioedema:** Rare cases involving the tongue, glottis, or larynx have been reported and can be fatal. Discontinue immediately if angioedema occurs [Australian].
- **Complex Sleep Behaviours:** Activities such as "sleep-driving," preparing food, or making phone calls while not fully awake (with subsequent amnesia) have been reported. The risk increases with concurrent alcohol or CNS depressant use [Australian].
- **Dependence and Abuse:** Prolonged use may lead to physical and psychological dependence. Caution is advised in patients with a history of alcohol or drug abuse [Australian].
- **Memory Impairment:** Transient amnesia or impaired memory may occur. This is more frequent when the drug is combined with alcohol [Australian].
- **Psychiatric Effects:** Not recommended as primary therapy for depression, psychosis, or schizophrenia. Benzodiazepines may unmask suicidal tendencies or pre-existing depression [Australian].
- **Paradoxical Reactions:** Acute rage, excitement, or stimulation may occur, particularly in elderly patients. Discontinue if these reactions emerge [Australian].
- **Respiratory Function:** Use with caution in patients with respiratory depression or COPD, as benzodiazepines may increase $CO_2$ tension and decrease $O_2$ tension [Australian].
- **Hypotension:** Use with caution in patients where a drop in blood pressure could lead to cardiac or cerebral complications (e.g., the elderly) [Australian].
- **Blood Dyscrasias:** Rare instances of blood dyscrasias have been reported; periodic blood counts are recommended during long-term therapy [Australian].
- **Glaucoma:** Use with caution in acute narrow-angle glaucoma due to potential atropine-like side effects [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CNS Depressants:** Additive effects occur with alcohol, barbiturates, antipsychotics, sedatives, antidepressants, nonselective MAO inhibitors, antiepileptics, and anaesthetics.
- **Alcohol:** Mutual potentiation can lead to unpredictable reactions; avoid alcoholic drinks during treatment [Australian].
- **Hepatic Enzyme Inhibitors:** Disulfiram and cimetidine may decrease flunitrazepam metabolism, increasing plasma levels and sedation [Australian].
- **Anticholinergics:** Effects of atropine-like drugs, antihistamines, and certain antidepressants may be potentiated [Australian].
- **Cisapride:** May lead to faster absorption and temporary increases in sedation [Australian].
- **Narcotic Analgesics:** May enhance euphoria, potentially increasing psychological dependence [Australian].
- **Anticonvulsants:** Serum concentrations of either drug may be altered; frequent monitoring is recommended [Australian].
- **Anticoagulants/Antidiabetics:** No significant interactions with coumarin or oral hypoglycaemics have been identified [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Benzodiazepines cross the placenta. High doses during labor may cause neonatal hypotonia, respiratory depression, and hypothermia. Withdrawal symptoms may occur in the newborn after prolonged maternal use. Avoid use during pregnancy, especially near term [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Flunitrazepam is excreted in human breast milk. Use is not recommended for breastfeeding mothers [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Studies assessing human fertility impairment are not available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Contraindicated in children [Australian].
- **Elderly/Debilitated Use:** Increased susceptibility to sedative effects, confusion, and ataxia, significantly increasing the risk of falls and domestic accidents. Doses should be restricted to 0.5 mg to 1 mg [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution; dose reduction may be necessary as metabolite elimination is impaired [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe hepatic insufficiency. Use with caution in mild-to-moderate impairment; periodic liver function tests are recommended [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Flunitrazepam is a potent benzodiazepine with marked sedative, hypnotic, anxiolytic, anticonvulsant, and muscle-relaxant properties. It induces, maintains, and deepens sleep by acting on GABA receptors in the central nervous system [Australian].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Almost completely absorbed; peak plasma levels occur ~45 minutes post-ingestion. Bioavailability is 64–77% due to a 10–15% first-pass effect [Australian].
- **Distribution:** Rapid and extensive; 77–80% plasma protein bound [Australian].
- **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised in the liver to 7-amino-flunitrazepam and N-desmethyl-flunitrazepam (both pharmacologically active, though less so than the parent) [Australian].
- **Elimination:** Half-life of parent drug is 20–30 hours. Metabolite half-lives: 7-amino (10–16 hours), N-desmethyl (23–33 hours). Excreted primarily via the kidneys as glucuronides [Australian].
