# Editorial Product Information: Felodipine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information from the following jurisdiction: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration).
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Felodipine (a dihydropyridine calcium antagonist).
- **Brand Names:** FELODUR ER [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** C08CA02 (inferred from class; not explicitly stated in source).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Extended-release (ER) tablets: 2.5 mg, 5 mg, and 10 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Clinect Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypertension:** Treatment of high blood pressure in adults [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** Dosing must be individualised. The standard starting dose is 5 mg once daily.
- **Maintenance Dose:** Usually ranges from 5 mg to 10 mg daily. 
- **Maximum Dose:** Doses exceeding 20 mg daily are not recommended [Australian].
- **Titration:** If required, the dose may be increased in increments of 2.5 mg or 5 mg per day.
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets must be swallowed whole with water. They must not be crushed, divided, or chewed.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated throughout all stages of pregnancy. Treatment should not be initiated in women who may become pregnant [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to felodipine or any inactive components of the formulation.
- **Cardiovascular Conditions:** 
    - Uncompensated heart failure.
    - Acute myocardial infarction.
    - Unstable angina pectoris.
    - Haemodynamically significant cardiac valvular obstruction.
    - Dynamic cardiac outflow obstruction [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Excessive Hypotension:** Felodipine may cause significant hypotension, potentially leading to myocardial ischaemia in susceptible patients. Monitoring is required during initiation and titration, especially in patients on concurrent antihypertensives.
- **Exacerbation of Angina:** Reflex tachycardia following blood pressure reduction may precipitate or worsen angina, particularly in patients with severe obstructive coronary artery disease.
- **Fixed Outflow Obstruction:** Use with caution in patients with fixed left ventricular outflow obstruction.
- **Peripheral Oedema:** Dose-related mild to moderate peripheral oedema (from precapillary vasodilation) occurs in approximately 20% of patients.
- **Gingival Enlargement:** Reported in patients with existing gingivitis or periodontitis; can be mitigated by strict dental hygiene.
- **Lactose Intolerance:** Tablets contain lactose; avoid in patients with hereditary galactose intolerance or glucose-galactose malabsorption [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are often dose-dependent, appearing at treatment initiation or after dose increases. They are typically transient.

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (≥10%)** | Peripheral oedema |
| **Common (>1%)** | Headache, dizziness/vertigo, flushing, nausea, vomiting, gum hyperplasia |
| **Less Common (≤1%)** | Hypotension, palpitations, tachycardia, syncope, chest pain, dyspnoea, respiratory infection, dyspepsia, flatulence, abdominal pain, constipation, gingivitis, paraesthesia, depression, increased liver enzymes (ALP, ASAT, ALAT) |

- **General/Rare Reactions:** Hypersensitivity (rashes, pruritus, urticaria, angio-oedema), photosensitivity, fever, arthralgia, myalgia, fatigue, impotence/sexual dysfunction, urinary frequency, and leucocytoclastic vasculitis [Australian].
- **Serious Adverse Events:** Rare reports of myocardial infarction (non-fatal), second-degree AV block, stroke, and chest pain; however, a definitive causal relationship has not been established [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Concurrent use with substances that inhibit the cytochrome P450 3A4 system (e.g., cimetidine, erythromycin, itraconazole, ketoconazole) increases felodipine plasma levels.
- **Grapefruit Juice:** Contains bioflavonoids that inhibit CYP3A4, significantly increasing felodipine bioavailability.
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Substances that induce CYP3A4 (e.g., phenytoin, carbamazepine, rifampicin, barbiturates, St John’s wort) will decrease felodipine plasma levels.
- **Tacrolimus:** Felodipine may increase tacrolimus concentrations; serum level monitoring and dose adjustment of tacrolimus may be necessary.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Beta-blockers:** While felodipine appears safe in uncompensated heart failure, the combination with beta-blockers is not recommended in this specific population [Australian].
- **Digoxin:** No significant effect on digoxin levels has been observed [Australian].
- **Food:** No significant effect on absorption when taken with food.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Calcium channel blockers may cause foetal hypoxia due to maternal hypotension. Animal studies have shown morphological abnormalities of the phalanges (rabbits) and prolonged labour (rats). Felodipine is contraindicated in pregnancy.
- **Breastfeeding:** Felodipine is excreted in breast milk. At therapeutic doses, it is considered unlikely to affect the nursing infant [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Human data regarding effects on male or female fertility are unavailable.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use is not recommended due to limited clinical trial experience [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Consider a lower starting dose (2.5 mg once daily). Plasma levels are generally higher in the elderly due to reduced clearance; however, age itself has a relatively minor impact on pharmacokinetics.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Patients with severely impaired liver function require a dose reduction, as they may have elevated plasma concentrations and increased sensitivity.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosage adjustment is not required, as impaired renal function does not significantly influence peak plasma concentrations or AUC [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A highly selective calcium antagonist that acts primarily on vascular smooth muscle in the arterioles to decrease peripheral vascular resistance and lower arterial blood pressure.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Exhibits vascular vs. myocardial selectivity (6 times more potent in vascular inhibition than myocardial in vitro). It does not cause orthostatic hypotension as it does not affect venous smooth muscle. It also possesses a mild natriuretic/diuretic effect.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Completely absorbed; 20% bioavailability due to extensive first-pass metabolism.
    - **Distribution:** 99% protein bound (primarily albumin); volume of distribution is approximately 10 L/kg.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised in the liver by CYP3A4; all metabolites are inactive.
    - **Excretion:** 70% excreted in urine as metabolites; <0.5% recovered unchanged. Elimination is biphasic: α-phase half-life is ~4 hours; β-phase half-life is ~24 hours [Australian].
