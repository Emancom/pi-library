# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The jurisdiction contributing to this editorial synthesis is: **United States (FDA)**.
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of Levocarnitine injection for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory guidance from the provided source.
- This is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine [(R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt].
- **Chemical Class:** Naturally occurring carrier molecule/amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion. 
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is typically followed by equivalent doses every 3 or 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - Maintenance daily doses are generally 50 mg/kg, though higher doses (up to 300 mg/kg) have been used [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). 
    - Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Monitor plasma carnitine concentrations prior to and during therapy. The target free carnitine range is generally 35 to 60 µmol/L [US].
- For ESRD patients, dose adjustments (downward to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) levels [US].
- Inspect parenteral products for particulate matter or discoloration before use [US].

### Compatibility
- Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL in PVC bags [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate the risks before considering re-administration [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine may increase the frequency or severity of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders [US].
- **Monitoring:** Periodic monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations is recommended [US].
- **Oral Formulation Precaution:** While this editorial focuses on the IV form, it is noted that oral levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency and may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO) in patients with severely compromised renal function [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disorders.
- **Common (≥5% in ESRD trials):**
    - **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain, headache, injection site reaction, fever, flu syndrome, asthenia, chest pain.
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia.
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, anorexia.
    - **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia.
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis.
    - **Nervous:** Dizziness, paresthesia, depression [US].
- **Less Frequent/Specific:** Body odor and gastritis.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and increased seizure activity [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels in patients on warfarin therapy following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not lettered in provided source]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine in milk following administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant should be weighed against the benefit of therapy for the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg). Safety and efficacy in children follow the same general guidelines as adults [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources; however, monitoring of renal function is advisable given the drug's primary renal excretion.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily used in ESRD patients on dialysis; dose reductions (to 5 mg/kg) may be necessary based on trough carnitine levels.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following IV administration (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; the mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Bioavailability:** In healthy volunteers, oral tablets and solutions were found to be bioequivalent, with an absolute bioavailability of approximately 15% after correcting for endogenous carnitine.
- **ESRD Studies:** Clinical studies demonstrated that IV administration of 20 mg/kg after dialysis significantly increased plasma carnitine levels. Trough concentrations increased from baseline (approx. 20 µmol/L) to steady-state levels (approx. 190–243 µmol/L) over 8 to 24 weeks of therapy [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription Only Medicine).
