# Editorial Product Information: Finerenone

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **finerenone** for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, dosing protocols, and safety information into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official, legally approved Product Information in any specific jurisdiction. Prescribers should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labelling for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Finerenone (a nonsteroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist).
- **Brand Name:** **Kerendia** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** C03DA05.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 10 mg (pink), 20 mg (pale-yellow), and 40 mg (grey-orange) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Bayer Australia Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD):** Indicated to delay the progressive decline of renal function and reduce cardiovascular mortality and morbidity in adults with CKD (accompanied by albuminuria) associated with Type 2 Diabetes (T2D), as an adjunct to standard care [Australian-approved].
- **Heart Failure (HF):** Indicated for the treatment of symptomatic heart failure in adults with a left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) $\ge$ 40% [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
The starting dose is determined by the patient’s estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR). Serum potassium must be measured before initiation.

### Initiation Criteria (Serum Potassium)
- **CKD (T2D):** Recommended initiation if serum potassium is $\le$ 4.8 mmol/L. If potassium is > 4.8 to 5.0 mmol/L, initiation may be considered with increased monitoring in the first 4 weeks. Do not initiate if potassium > 5.0 mmol/L [Australian].
- **Heart Failure:** Recommended initiation if serum potassium is $\le$ 5.0 mmol/L [Australian].

### Starting Dose (All Indications)
- **eGFR $\ge$ 60 mL/min/1.73m²:** 20 mg once daily.
- **eGFR $\ge$ 25 to < 60 mL/min/1.73m²:** 10 mg once daily.
- **eGFR < 25 mL/min/1.73m²:** Initiation is not recommended due to limited clinical experience [Australian].

### Target Dose and Titration
Four weeks after initiation, restart, or dose adjustment, serum potassium and eGFR should be re-measured to determine the maintenance dose.

- **CKD (T2D) Target:** 20 mg once daily.
- **Heart Failure Target:**
    - 40 mg once daily (if starting eGFR was $\ge$ 60 mL/min/1.73m²).
    - 20 mg once daily (if starting eGFR was 25 to < 60 mL/min/1.73m²).

*Dose increases (e.g., 10 mg to 20 mg, or 20 mg to 40 mg) are contingent on serum potassium $\le$ 4.8 mmol/L (CKD) or < 5.0 mmol/L (HF) and a stable eGFR (decline $\le$ 30%).*

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets may be taken with or without food, but should be taken with a glass of water.
- **Grapefruit:** Avoid intake of grapefruit or grapefruit juice during treatment.
- **Swallowing Difficulties:** Tablets may be crushed and mixed with water or soft foods (e.g., apple sauce) immediately prior to oral administration.

### Missed Dose
Take the missed dose as soon as noticed on the same day. If not possible, skip the dose and take the next scheduled dose. Do not double the dose.

## 4. Contraindications
**Finerenone** is contraindicated in patients:
- Receiving concomitant strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., itraconazole, ketoconazole, ritonavir, clarithromycin, nefazodone) [Australian].
- With adrenal insufficiency [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hyperkalaemia:** Very common. Risk factors include low eGFR, high baseline potassium, and history of hyperkalaemia. Periodic monitoring of serum potassium is mandatory.
- **Worsening Renal Function (Heart Failure):** An initial, reversible decrease in eGFR may occur due to haemodynamic changes. Monitor eGFR regularly; consider dose interruption or delay in up-titration if clinically significant worsening occurs [Australian].
- **Concomitant Medications:** Avoid use with potassium-sparing diuretics (e.g., amiloride) and other mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (MRAs) like spironolactone or eplerenone.
- **Severe Hepatic Impairment:** Avoid use in patients with Child-Pugh C due to lack of study data and expected significant increase in exposure [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Hyperkalaemia |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Hypotension, Hyponatraemia, Hyperuricaemia, Glomerular filtration rate decreased, Blood creatinine increased |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Not specified in available sources |

- **Hyperkalaemia Details:** In CKD/T2D trials, hyperkalaemia events occurred in up to 18.2% of patients vs. 9% in placebo. In Heart Failure (FINEARTS-HF), it was 9.7% vs. 4.2% [Australian].
- **Hypotension:** Mean systolic blood pressure typically decreases by 3 mmHg and diastolic by 1-2 mmHg, usually stabilising after one month.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** 
    - *Strong:* Contraindicated (e.g., Itraconazole increases AUC by 531%). 
    - *Moderate/Weak:* Increases **finerenone** exposure; monitor serum potassium (e.g., Erythromycin increases AUC by 248%; Verapamil by 170%).
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Avoid concomitant use with strong (e.g., rifampicin, carbamazepine) or moderate (e.g., efavirenz) inducers, as they markedly decrease **finerenone** plasma concentrations.
- **Transporters:** At 40 mg daily, **finerenone** is a weak inhibitor of CYP3A4 and CYP2C8 *in vivo*. Use caution with sensitive substrates with narrow therapeutic windows (e.g., repaglinide).

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Potassium-elevating agents:** Concomitant use with potassium supplements, trimethoprim, or trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole requires caution and frequent potassium monitoring.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category D]. Animal studies show reproductive toxicity (reduced fetal weight, malformations). **Finerenone** should not be used during pregnancy unless the benefits clearly outweigh the risks. Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety is unknown in humans. In animal studies, **finerenone** and its metabolites were excreted in milk and caused adverse effects in pups. Breastfeeding should be discontinued if the drug is essential for the mother.
- **Fertility:** Animal data suggest no expected impairment of male or female fertility at clinical exposures.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended; safety and efficacy in patients < 18 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required. However, elderly patients ($\ge$ 65 years) may have higher plasma concentrations (AUC up to 34% higher) than younger patients [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **eGFR < 25 mL/min/1.73m²:** Initiation not recommended.
    - **End-stage Renal Disease (eGFR < 15 mL/min/1.73m²):** Continue treatment with caution; clinical experience is limited.
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Child-Pugh A or B:** No initial dose adjustment. Monitor potassium closely in Child-Pugh B.
    - **Child-Pugh C:** Avoid use.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A nonsteroidal, selective antagonist of the mineralocorticoid receptor (MR). It blocks MR overactivation, which mediates inflammation and fibrosis in the heart and kidneys. Unlike steroidal MRAs, it has no relevant affinity for androgen or progesterone receptors.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability 43.5%. $C_{max}$ reached in 0.5–1.25 hours (fasted). 
    - **Distribution:** 91.7% plasma protein bound (mainly albumin). $V_{ss}$ is 52.6 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily CYP3A4 (90%) and CYP2C8 (10%). All metabolites are inactive.
    - **Elimination:** Half-life of 2–3 hours. 80% excreted in urine (mostly as metabolites), 20% in faeces.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **FIDELIO-DKD & FIGARO-DKD:** Pivotal Phase III trials in CKD associated with T2D. Finerenone significantly reduced the risk of composite kidney outcomes (kidney failure, $\ge$ 40% eGFR decline, renal death) and cardiovascular outcomes (CV death, non-fatal MI, non-fatal stroke, or HF hospitalisation) [Australian].
- **FINEARTS-HF:** Phase III trial in symptomatic Heart Failure (LVEF $\ge$ 40%). Finerenone demonstrated superiority over placebo in reducing the composite of CV death and total heart failure events (Rate Ratio 0.84) and significantly improved symptoms via KCCQ-TSS scores [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Container:** PVC/PVDC/Al blister packs.
- **Disposal:** Any unused medicine should be disposed of via local pharmacy programs [Australian].
