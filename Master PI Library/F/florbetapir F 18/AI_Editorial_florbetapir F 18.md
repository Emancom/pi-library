# Editorial Product Information: Florbetapir F 18

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, incorporating regulatory requirements and clinical data for the use of florbetapir F 18.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Florbetapir F 18 (a radioactive diagnostic agent).
- **Brand Name:** **AMYVID** [United States].
- **ATC Code:** V09AX05 (Note: Not explicitly in source, but standard for florbetapir).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injection solution. Each mL contains 500 MBq to 1,900 MBq (13.5 mCi to 51 mCi) of florbetapir F 18 at the End of Synthesis (EOS) in a multiple-dose vial [United States].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Eli Lilly and Company / Avid Radiopharmaceuticals [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
**AMYVID** is a radioactive diagnostic drug indicated for Positron Emission Tomography (PET) imaging of the brain to estimate amyloid beta neuritic plaque density in adults with cognitive impairment who are being evaluated for:
- Alzheimer’s disease (AD) and other causes of cognitive decline [United States].
- Selection of patients for whom amyloid beta-directed therapy is indicated [United States].

**Clinical Note:** A negative scan reduces the likelihood that cognitive impairment is due to AD but does not preclude future amyloid accumulation. A positive scan indicates moderate-to-frequent plaque density but does not independently establish a diagnosis of AD or other cognitive disorder.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 Recommended Dosing
- **Adult Dose:** 370 MBq (10 mCi) administered as a single intravenous bolus in a total volume of up to 10 mL [United States].
- **Maximum Mass Dose:** 50 mcg [United States].
- **Flush:** Follow the injection with a bolus of approximately 10 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride to ensure complete delivery.

### 3.2 Patient Preparation
- Instruct patients to hydrate before and after administration.
- Patients should void frequently after the procedure to reduce radiation exposure [United States].

### 3.3 Image Acquisition and Interpretation
- **Acquisition:** 10-minute PET images should be obtained starting 30 to 50 minutes after the intravenous injection. The patient should be supine with the head centered in the scanner field of view [United States].
- **Interpretation:** Images should be interpreted only by readers who have completed the manufacturer’s specific training program. Interpretation is based on a visual assessment of the cortical grey matter signal intensity compared to adjacent white matter [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- None identified in available sources [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Image Misinterpretation:** Errors in estimating plaque density can occur. Interpretation should be performed independently of clinical features. Extensive brain atrophy or motion artifacts may limit the ability to distinguish grey and white matter [United States].
- **Radiation Risk:** **AMYVID** contributes to long-term cumulative radiation exposure, which is associated with an increased risk of cancer. Ensure safe handling to protect healthcare providers and patients [United States].
- **Administration Precautions:** Do not use if the solution contains particulate matter or is discolored. Use a short intravenous catheter (≤1.5 inches) to minimize drug adsorption to the tubing [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The safety of **AMYVID** was evaluated in 555 subjects in clinical trials. Adverse reactions were generally mild.

| Adverse Reaction | Frequency (%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Headache** | 1.8% |
| **Musculoskeletal pain** | 0.7% |
| **Increased blood pressure** | 0.7% |
| **Nausea** | 0.7% |
| **Fatigue** | 0.5% |
| **Injection site reaction** | 0.5% |

Other reactions (incidence <0.4%) include anxiety, back pain, claustrophobia, dizziness, insomnia, and neck pain [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Cholinesterase Inhibitors:** Donepezil, galantamine, and memantine do not appear to alter florbetapir F 18 brain uptake or binding [United States].
- **Amyloid Beta-Directed Therapy:** In vitro data suggest that donanemab and lecanemab do not alter florbetapir F 18 binding to its target [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** There are no available data on use in pregnant women. All radiopharmaceuticals have the potential to cause fetal harm. Benefits and risks should be carefully weighed [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if florbetapir F 18 is excreted in human milk. To minimize radiation exposure to the infant, a lactating woman should pump and discard breast milk for **24 hours** after administration (>10 radioactive half-lives) [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established in patients under 18 years of age [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** In clinical studies (n=496), no overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed between subjects ≥65 years and younger adults [United States].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources. However, PK data indicate primarily biliary/GI elimination for metabolites [United States].

## 10. Pharmacology
### 10.1 Mechanism of Action
Florbetapir F 18 binds specifically to amyloid beta plaques in the brain. The F 18 isotope produces a positron signal that is detected by PET imaging. In vitro studies show a dissociation constant ($K_d$) of $3.7 \pm 0.3 \text{ nM}$ [United States].

### 10.2 Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Following IV injection, the drug diffuses across the blood-brain barrier.
- **Metabolism:** F 18 in circulation is principally in the form of polar metabolites.
- **Elimination:** Less than 5% of radioactivity remains in the blood by 20 minutes post-injection. Radioactivity accumulates in the liver within 4 minutes and is eliminated primarily through the biliary/gastrointestinal tract [United States].
- **Half-life:** The physical half-life of Fluorine-18 is 109.8 minutes [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study 1 (Autopsy Study):** Evaluated 59 terminally ill subjects. PET scans were compared to post-mortem neuritic plaque density. Sensitivity was 92% (95% CI: 78–98%) and specificity was 100% (95% CI: 80–100%) [United States].
- **Study 2 (Reproducibility):** Evaluated inter-reader and intra-reader reproducibility using images from 151 subjects. The overall Fleiss’ kappa statistic for inter-reader agreement was 0.83, indicating high reproducibility [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [United States].
- **Shelf Life:** The product must be stored within the original container or equivalent radiation shielding and expires **10 hours** after the End of Synthesis (EOS) [United States].
- **Preservatives:** The solution contains no preservatives.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only. Must be used by or under the control of healthcare providers qualified by specific training in the safe use and handling of radionuclides [United States].
