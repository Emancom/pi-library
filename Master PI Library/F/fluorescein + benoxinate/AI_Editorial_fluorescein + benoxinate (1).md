# Editorial Product Information: fluorescein + benoxinate

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **fluorescein + benoxinate**
- Chemical class:
  - Fluorescein (fluorescein sodium): fluorescent disclosing agent/dye
  - Benoxinate (benoxinate hydrochloride): short‑acting ester local ophthalmic anesthetic
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Ophthalmic (topical)
- Note: preservative reported in source material — chlorobutanol (reported concentration 1.3%); inactive/supporting excipients reported include povidone, boric acid, hydrochloric acid (pH adjuster) and water.

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 2017
- Marketing start date reported (U.S.): 03/20/2020
- Document revision history reported in source:
  - Revised: 8/2022
  - Revised: 5/2025
- Withdrawals, suspensions, or restrictions: Not specified in available sources
- Other jurisdictions (registration/approval dates): Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Indicated for ophthalmic procedures requiring a combination of a disclosing agent and a topical anesthetic in adult and pediatric patients (approved clinical uses reported include enabling visualization and providing corneal anesthesia sufficient for applanation tonometry, tear fluid dynamics evaluation, and short conjunctival and corneal procedures).
- Jurisdictional differences in approved indications: Not specified in available sources beyond the indication described above.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult and pediatric)
- Instill 1 to 2 drops topically into the eye as needed to achieve adequate anesthesia.

### Dosing by indication
- No separate, indication‑specific dosing schedules provided beyond the general instruction above.

### Administration instructions
- Apply 1–2 drops to the affected eye(s) to provide corneal anesthesia and fluorescein staining.
- Onset of corneal anesthesia: typically within about 5–45 seconds after administration.
- Duration of effect: typically about 20 minutes after a single administration.
- Additional dosing: anesthetic effect may be extended by administering additional drops 10–20 minutes after the prior dose (repeat dosing as clinically required).
- Patient precaution: advise patients not to touch or rub the eye for approximately 20 minutes after application because local anesthesia can cause insensitivity and increase the risk of accidental ocular injury.

### Titration schedules

### Missed dose instructions

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to any component of the product (includes active ingredients fluorescein sodium and benoxinate hydrochloride and any excipient/preservative).
- No additional contraindications reported in the available source materials.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- **Corneal toxicity:** Prolonged use or abuse of topical ophthalmic anesthetics may cause corneal epithelial toxicity, which can present as epithelial defects and may progress to permanent corneal damage with associated visual loss.
- **Corneal injury due to insensitivity:** Local anesthetic-induced corneal or ocular insensitivity can lead to accidental injury; patients should be instructed not to touch the eye for approximately 20 minutes after administration.
- Monitoring requirements: Specific formal monitoring schedules are not provided in the available sources. Clinicians should be vigilant for signs of corneal epithelial injury (e.g., persistent pain, decreased vision, epithelial defects) following use, particularly with repeated or prolonged administration.
- Other general precautions:
  - Avoid prolonged or repeated administration beyond that necessary for the procedure because of the risk of corneal epithelial toxicity.
  - If signs of corneal injury (e.g., epithelial defect) occur, discontinue use and manage appropriately.
- Regional boxed warnings: Not specified in available sources.

## 7. Adverse Effects
- Serious ocular adverse reactions described in labeling: **Corneal toxicity** (epithelial defects potentially progressing to permanent corneal damage and visual loss) and **corneal injury due to insensitivity**.
- Other adverse reactions identified following use: ocular hyperemia/conjunctival redness, burning, stinging, eye irritation, blurred vision, punctate keratitis.

- Frequency information: frequencies are not provided in the available sources; several reactions are reported voluntarily and frequency cannot be reliably estimated from the data provided.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Risk summary: There are no available clinical data on use of this ophthalmic combination in pregnant women to inform drug‑associated risk. Adequate animal reproduction studies with fluorescein sodium and/or benoxinate hydrochloride have not been conducted.
- Recommendation: Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- Pregnancy category systems: Not specified in available sources (no jurisdictional pregnancy categories provided in the source material).

### Breastfeeding
- Risk summary: No data are available regarding the presence of fluorescein sodium or benoxinate hydrochloride in human milk after ocular administration, nor on effects on the breastfed infant or milk production.
- Recommendation: Consider the developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding, the mother's clinical need for the product, and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed infant when deciding whether to use the product while breastfeeding.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Fluorescein: acts as a disclosing/staining agent facilitating visualization of corneal and conjunctival surface defects and tear film dynamics.
- Benoxinate: a short‑acting ester local anesthetic that produces corneal anesthesia to facilitate ophthalmic examination and short procedures.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Onset of corneal anesthesia typically occurs within approximately 5–45 seconds after topical administration.
- Duration of corneal anesthesia from a single administration is about 20 minutes.
- Additional administration 10–20 minutes after a prior dose may extend anesthetic effect.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption (systemic), distribution, metabolism and elimination data for the combination after ophthalmic administration are not specified in the available sources.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Controlled clinical studies in adults and pediatric patients demonstrated that topical administration of the fluorescein + benoxinate ophthalmic solution enables visualization (fluorescein staining) and provides corneal anesthesia sufficient to permit applanation tonometry, tear fluid dynamics evaluation, and short conjunctival and corneal procedures.
