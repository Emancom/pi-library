# Editorial Product Information: fluorescein + benoxinate

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **fluorescein + benoxinate**
- Chemical class:
  - Fluorescein: ophthalmic disclosing agent (dye)
  - Benoxinate: short‑acting topical ester local anesthetic
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: topical ophthalmic
- Strength (as stated in available sources): fluorescein sodium 2.6 mg/mL (0.3%); benoxinate hydrochloride 4.4 mg/mL (0.4%)

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 2017
- Marketing start date listed: 03/20/2020 (as reported in product listing)
- Last revision(s)/updates in source PI:
  - Revised: 8/2022
  - Revised: 5/2025
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Indicated for procedures in adult and pediatric patients requiring a disclosing agent in combination with a topical ophthalmic anesthetic to allow visualization and corneal anesthesia sufficient for procedures such as applanation tonometry, tear fluid dynamics evaluation, and short conjunctival and corneal procedures.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult and pediatric)
- Instill 1 to 2 drops topically in the eye as needed to achieve adequate anesthesia.

### Dosing by indication
- Not further specified by indication in available sources beyond use for ophthalmic procedures requiring a disclosing agent plus topical anesthetic.

### Administration instructions
- Instill 1–2 drops into the eye(s) to be examined or treated.
- Maximal corneal anesthesia typically occurs within about 5–45 seconds after single administration and generally lasts about 20 minutes.
- The anesthetic effect may be extended by subsequent administration 10–20 minutes after the prior dose.
- Advise patients not to touch or rub the treated eye for approximately 20 minutes after administration because corneal insensitivity increases the risk of accidental injury.

### Titration schedules
- Not specified in available sources.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to any component of the product (all jurisdictions in available sources).

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- **Corneal toxicity:** Prolonged use or abuse may lead to corneal epithelial toxicity manifesting as epithelial defects that can progress to permanent corneal damage and accompanying visual loss.
- **Corneal injury due to insensitivity:** Because topical anesthesia produces corneal insensitivity, patients should be counseled not to touch the eye for approximately 20 minutes after application to avoid accidental injury.
- Use with caution in settings where prolonged or repeated topical anesthetic use may occur; avoid unnecessary repeated or prolonged administration.
- Monitoring requirements: Specific monitoring schedules or tests are not specified in available sources; monitor the ocular surface for evidence of epithelial damage if repeated use is necessary.

## 7. Adverse Effects
- The most commonly reported ocular adverse events in the available labeling are: stinging, burning, and conjunctival redness.
- Additional adverse reactions reported include: ocular hyperemia, eye irritation, blurred vision, and punctate keratitis.
- Serious ocular adverse reactions described in labeling: corneal toxicity (epithelial defects, potential permanent corneal damage) and corneal injury due to insensitivity.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Risk summary: No available human data on use in pregnant women to inform drug‑associated risk. Adequate animal reproduction studies have not been conducted with fluorescein sodium and/or benoxinate hydrochloride. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.

- Pregnancy category/system: Not specified in available sources.

### Breastfeeding
- Risk summary: No data are available on the presence of fluorescein sodium or benoxinate hydrochloride in human milk after ocular administration, nor on effects on the breastfed infant or on milk production. Consider the developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding, the mother’s clinical need for therapy, and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed infant.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Fluorescein: acts as a disclosing agent (fluorescent dye) to visualize tear dynamics, corneal defects, and facilitate certain ophthalmic assessments.
- Benoxinate: a short‑acting topical ester local anesthetic that produces corneal anesthesia when applied topically.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Onset of corneal anesthesia: typically about 5–45 seconds after instillation.
- Duration of effect after a single administration: about 20 minutes.
- The anesthetic effect may be extended by re‑dosing 10–20 minutes after the previous administration.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption, distribution, metabolism, and elimination after ocular administration: Not specified in available sources.

### Nonclinical toxicology
- Studies to evaluate carcinogenicity and mutagenicity have not been conducted. Studies to evaluate impairment of fertility have not been conducted.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Controlled clinical studies in adults and pediatric patients demonstrated that topical administration of this combination enables visualization and corneal anesthesia sufficient to perform applanation tonometry, evaluate tear fluid dynamics, and carry out short conjunctival and corneal procedures.
- Trial design details, patient numbers, comparator arms, and quantitative efficacy outcomes are not specified in available sources.
