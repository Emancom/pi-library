# Editorial Product Information: Faricimab

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following jurisdictional Product Information (PI): 
    - **Australian (TGA)**: Vabysmo (faricimab)
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of faricimab for healthcare professionals. It synthesises available regulatory data into a single clinically focused reference.
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved PI for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Faricimab (a humanised bispecific immunoglobulin G1 (IgG1) antibody).
- **Chemical Class**: Angiopoietin-2 (Ang-2) and vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) inhibitor.
- **Brand Name**: Vabysmo [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: S01LA09.
- **Dosage Form and Strength**: 
    - **Vial**: 28.8 mg/0.24 mL (concentration 120 mg/mL).
    - **Pre-filled Syringe**: 21 mg/0.175 mL (concentration 120 mg/mL).
    - Both presentations deliver a single 0.05 mL dose containing 6 mg of faricimab.
- **Route of Administration**: Intravitreal injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Roche Products Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Faricimab is indicated for the treatment of:
- Neovascular (wet) age-related macular degeneration (nAMD).
- Diabetic macular oedema (DMO).
- Macular oedema secondary to retinal vein occlusion (RVO), including branch (BRVO) and central/hemiretinal (C/HRVO) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
The recommended dose is 6 mg (0.05 mL) via intravitreal injection.

### Dosing by Indication
- **nAMD**: Initial phase of 4 doses every 4 weeks. Disease activity is assessed at weeks 20–24. Treatment is then individualised via a "treat-and-extend" approach:
    - No disease activity: Consider extending to every 16 weeks.
    - Active disease: Consider every 8 or 12 weeks.
- **DMO**: Initial phase of 4 doses every 4 weeks. Thereafter, the interval may be extended in 4-week increments up to every 16 weeks based on anatomic and visual outcomes.
- **RVO**: Initial phase of consecutive 4-weekly injections (minimum of 3). Thereafter, intervals may be extended based on a treat-and-extend approach up to 16 weeks [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Must be administered by a qualified ophthalmologist.
- For single-eye use only (one vial/syringe per eye).
- Vials and syringes must reach room temperature (20°C to 25°C) before use.
- Use a 30-gauge x ½ inch injection needle for administration [Australian].

### Missed Dose
If a dose is missed, the patient must be assessed at the next available visit and dosing resumed at the ophthalmologist’s discretion.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to faricimab or any excipients (e.g., histidine, methionine, polysorbate 20).
- Ocular or periocular infections (active or suspected).
- Active intraocular inflammation.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Injection-related Reactions**: Risk of endophthalmitis, intraocular inflammation, rhegmatogenous retinal detachment, and iatrogenic cataract. Aseptic technique is mandatory.
- **Retinal Vasculitis/Occlusive Vasculitis**: Reported in post-marketing settings. Treatment must be discontinued if these occur [Australian].
- **Intraocular Pressure (IOP)**: Transient increases in IOP may occur within 60 minutes of injection. Do not inject if IOP is ≥ 30 mmHg. Perfusion of the optic nerve head must be monitored.
- **Arterial Thromboembolic Events (ATEs)**: There is a theoretical risk of ATEs (stroke, MI) related to systemic VEGF inhibition.
- **Bilateral Treatment**: Safety and efficacy of concurrent bilateral treatment have not been established [Australian].
- **Withholding Treatment**: Treatment should be withheld in cases of retinal detachment, macular holes (stage 3/4), or a decrease in BCVA of ≥ 30 letters compared to the last assessment.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The safety profile is based on pooled data from over 2,500 patients in Phase III trials.

### Common Adverse Effects (1–10%)
| Adverse Reaction | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- |
| Cataract | 9.7% |
| Conjunctival haemorrhage | 6.7% |
| Vitreous detachment | 4.2% |
| Intraocular pressure increased | 3.5% |
| Vitreous floaters | 3.5% |
| Retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) tear | 2.9% (nAMD only) |
| Eye pain | 2.5% |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Uveitis**: 0.5%
- **Endophthalmitis**: 0.4%
- **Retinal tear**: 0.2%
- **Rhegmatogenous retinal detachment**: 0.1%
- **Retinal Vasculitis/Occlusive Vasculitis**: Rare (post-marketing).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- No formal drug-drug interaction studies have been conducted.
- There are no data on the concomitant use of faricimab with other anti-VEGF products in the same eye [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **Category D** [Australian]: Faricimab should not be used during pregnancy unless the potential benefit outweighs the potential risk to the foetus. 
    - **Contraception**: Women of childbearing potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for at least 3 months following the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding**: It is unknown if faricimab is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution and consider the benefits of breastfeeding versus the clinical need for the mother.
- **Fertility**: No human studies available. Animal data (cynomolgus monkeys) showed no effects on reproductive organs.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients < 18 years.
- **Elderly Use**: No dose adjustment required for patients ≥ 65 years.
- **Renal Impairment**: No dose adjustment required. Minimal impact on systemic pharmacokinetics observed in patients with mild to severe impairment [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: No dose adjustment required. Metabolism occurs via proteolysis and is independent of hepatic function.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Faricimab is a bispecific antibody that neutralises both Ang-2 and VEGF-A. Inhibition of Ang-2 promotes vascular stability and desensitises blood vessels to VEGF-A, while VEGF-A inhibition reduces vascular permeability and pathological angiogenesis.
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Peak plasma concentrations (Cmax) occur ~2 days post-dose.
    - **Distribution**: Systemic concentrations are ~600 to 6000-fold lower than in ocular humours.
    - **Elimination**: The estimated mean ocular and systemic half-life is approximately 7.5 days. Elimination occurs via catabolism [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **nAMD (TENAYA, LUCERNE)**: Vabysmo Q16W showed non-inferior vision gains compared to aflibercept Q8W through 112 weeks.
- **DMO (YOSEMITE, RHINE)**: Vabysmo (Q8W or up to Q16W) demonstrated superior or non-inferior CST (central subfield thickness) reductions compared to aflibercept through 100 weeks.
- **RVO (BALATON, COMINO)**: Vabysmo Q4W demonstrated non-inferior mean change in BCVA compared to aflibercept Q4W at week 24, with vision gains maintained through week 72 [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze).
- **Light**: Keep in the original carton to protect from light.
- **Pre-injection**: Unopened vials/syringes may be kept at room temperature (20°C to 25°C) for up to 24 hours.
- **Shelf Life**: 
    - Vial: 30 months.
    - Pre-filled syringe: 24 months [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule**: **S4** (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
- **Monitoring**: Subject to additional monitoring [Australian].
