This editorial Product Information (PI) document provides a unified overview of ferric derisomaltose. It integrates clinical data, safety warnings, and administration guidelines from the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA).

**Editorial Note:** Information in this document is synthesized from the TGA (Monofer®) and FDA (Monoferric®) approved labeling. Where recommendations differ between regions, the more comprehensive or conservative guidance has been provided, with the source agency noted in brackets.

# Unified Product Information: Ferric Derisomaltose

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Active Ingredient:** Ferric derisomaltose (also known as iron (III) isomaltoside 1000).

Ferric derisomaltose is an iron-carbohydrate complex consisting of a core of iron (III) hydroxide atoms within a matrix of derisomaltose (hydrogenated isomaltooligosaccharides). The preparation is a sterile, dark brown, non-transparent solution for intravenous injection or infusion, provided at a concentration of 100 mg of elemental iron per 1 mL.

## 2. Approved Indications
Ferric derisomaltose is indicated for the treatment of iron deficiency or iron deficiency anemia (IDA) in adult patients under the following circumstances:
*   When oral iron preparations are ineffective, not tolerated, or have produced an unsatisfactory clinical response [TGA, FDA].
*   When there is a clinical requirement for the rapid delivery of iron [TGA].
*   In patients with non-hemodialysis dependent chronic kidney disease (NDD-CKD) [FDA].

The diagnosis must be confirmed by appropriate laboratory investigations.

## 3. Contraindications
Administration is contraindicated in patients with:
*   Known serious hypersensitivity to ferric derisomaltose or any of the inactive components in the formulation (e.g., water for injections, sodium hydroxide, or hydrochloric acid).
*   Evidence of iron overload (e.g., hemochromatosis, hemosiderosis) [TGA, FDA].
*   Anemias not caused by iron deficiency (e.g., hemolytic anemia) [TGA].

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Hypersensitivity Reactions
Intravenous iron complexes can cause serious, potentially fatal anaphylactic or anaphylactoid reactions. These may occur even if previous doses were well-tolerated.
*   **Monitoring:** Patients must be monitored for signs of hypersensitivity during the infusion and for at least 30 minutes following completion [TGA, FDA].
*   **Risk Factors:** Risk is increased in patients with known drug allergies, severe asthma, eczema, or immune/inflammatory conditions (e.g., systemic lupus erythematosus, rheumatoid arthritis) [TGA].
*   **Kounis Syndrome:** There have been reports of hypersensitivity reactions progressing to Kounis syndrome (acute allergic coronary arteriospasm leading to myocardial infarction) [TGA].

### Iron Overload
Excessive parenteral iron therapy can lead to iatrogenic hemosiderosis. Iron parameters (serum ferritin and transferrin saturation) should be monitored to avoid toxicity [FDA].

### Hepatic and Infectious Considerations
*   **Liver Dysfunction:** In patients with compensated liver dysfunction, use only after a careful benefit-risk assessment. Avoid use in patients with severe hepatic dysfunction (ALT/AST > 3x the upper limit of normal) where iron overload is a precipitating factor [TGA].
*   **Infection:** Use with caution in patients with acute or chronic infections. Ferric derisomaltose should not be administered to patients with ongoing bacteremia [TGA].

### Extravasation
Caution is required to avoid paravenous leakage. Extravasation may cause skin irritation and potentially long-lasting or permanent brown discoloration at the site of injection. If leakage occurs, stop the administration at that site immediately [TGA, FDA].

## 5. Dosage and Administration
The dose must be individualized based on the patient’s weight and iron deficit. The cumulative iron requirement can be determined using the Ganzoni formula or a simplified weight-based table [TGA].

### Recommended Dosing [FDA/TGA Combined]
*   **Patients weighing $\ge$ 50 kg:** Administer 1,000 mg as a single intravenous infusion.
*   **Patients weighing < 50 kg:** Administer 20 mg/kg actual body weight as a single intravenous infusion.
*   **Maximum Doses [TGA]:** The cumulative iron need may be given in a single infusion up to 20 mg iron/kg body weight. If the total deficit exceeds 20 mg/kg, the dose should be split into two administrations separated by at least one week. Single doses exceeding 1,500 mg are not recommended.

### Method of Administration
*   **Infusion:** For doses up to 1,000 mg, the infusion should last at least 20 minutes. For doses exceeding 1,000 mg, the duration should be 30 minutes or more [TGA].
*   **Bolus Injection:** May be administered as a bolus up to 500 mg, up to three times a week, at a rate of 250 mg/minute [TGA].
*   **Dilution:** Must only be mixed with sterile 0.9% sodium chloride. The final concentration should remain above 1 mg iron/mL.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most common adverse reactions (incidence $\ge$ 1%) are **nausea** and **rash** [FDA].

*   **Common:** Nausea, injection site reactions [TGA].
*   **Uncommon:** Hypophosphatemia (serum phosphate < 2.0 mg/dL), headache, dizziness, hypotension, hypertension, tachycardia, dyspnea, and abdominal pain [TGA, FDA].
*   **Fishbane Reaction:** Characterized by flushing, chest/back pain, and tightness. These symptoms typically resolve quickly after stopping the infusion and may not recur if restarted at a slower rate [TGA].
*   **Delayed Reactions:** Arthralgia, myalgia, or fever may occur several hours to days after administration, usually resolving within 2–4 days [TGA].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Oral Iron:** Concomitant use reduces the absorption of oral iron. Oral iron therapy should not be started for at least 5 days after the last injection [TGA].
*   **Laboratory Tests:** High doses (5 mL or more) may cause a brown tint to serum samples drawn within four hours of administration. It may also lead to falsely elevated bilirubin and falsely decreased calcium values [TGA].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
**TGA Category B3:** There are no adequate, well-controlled trials in pregnant women.
*   **First Trimester:** Use is generally not recommended; oral iron should be used if possible.
*   **Second/Third Trimester:** Use only if the potential benefit justifies the risk to the mother and fetus. Fetal bradycardia may occur secondary to maternal hypersensitivity reactions [TGA, FDA].
*   **Animal Data:** Studies in rats and rabbits showed skeletal developmental delays and malformations at doses well below the human clinical dose [FDA].

### Breastfeeding
Ferric derisomaltose is excreted in human milk. While studies show levels return to baseline within one week [TGA], clinicians should monitor breastfed infants for gastrointestinal toxicity, such as constipation or diarrhea [FDA].

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** Following injection, the complex is rapidly cleared from the plasma and taken up by the reticuloendothelial system (RES), primarily in the liver and spleen.
*   **Metabolism:** Iron is slowly released from the complex and binds to transferrin for use in hemoglobin synthesis or storage as ferritin/hemosiderin.
*   **Elimination:** The half-life is approximately 1 to 4 days [TGA], with a mean half-life of 27 hours reported for a 1,000 mg dose [FDA]. Renal elimination is negligible; the large size of the complex prevents excretion via the kidneys.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Ferric derisomaltose acts as a source of iron to replenish depleted stores. The complex is designed to provide a controlled, slow release of bioavailable iron (III) to iron-binding proteins (transferrin), minimizing the risk of free iron toxicity. This allows for the delivery of high doses in a single session.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   Store below 30°C [TGA]. The FDA recommends a controlled room temperature of 20°C to 25°C, with excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C.
*   Do not freeze.
*   **Post-Dilution:** Once diluted with 0.9% sodium chloride, the solution should ideally be used immediately, though it may be stored for up to 8 hours at room temperature [FDA].
