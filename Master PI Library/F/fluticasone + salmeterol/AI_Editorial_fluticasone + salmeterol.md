# Editorial Product Information: fluticasone + salmeterol

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **fluticasone + salmeterol**
- Chemical class:
  - Fluticasone propionate: inhaled corticosteroid (glucocorticoid)
  - Salmeterol xinafoate: long-acting β2-adrenoceptor agonist (LABA)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration: Inhalation only

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources.
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources.
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Regular maintenance treatment of asthma in patients for whom a combination inhaled corticosteroid plus long-acting β2-agonist is appropriate, including:
  - Patients already maintained on effective doses of an inhaled corticosteroid plus a long-acting β2-agonist.
  - Patients symptomatic on inhaled corticosteroid therapy alone.
  - Initiation of maintenance therapy in patients with moderate persistent asthma not controlled on as‑needed reliever medication, with moderate/severe airway limitation and daily symptoms requiring daily reliever use.
- Symptomatic treatment of patients with severe chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) with FEV1 < 50% predicted and a history of repeated exacerbations who remain symptomatic despite regular bronchodilator therapy. This product is not indicated for initiation of bronchodilator monotherapy in COPD.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Recommended total fluticasone/salmeterol dose for regular asthma management: 100/50 to 500/50 micrograms twice daily (total daily fluticasone 200–1000 µg; total daily salmeterol 100 µg).
  - Typical delivery described as two inhalations twice daily, with per‑inhalation amounts in clinical use described in the sources as ranges between 50/25 µg and 250/25 µg (see product-specific labelling for device-specific actuation strength).
- COPD (adults): Recommended total fluticasone/salmeterol dose of 250/50 or 500/50 micrograms twice daily.
  - Typical delivery described as two inhalations twice daily (e.g. two inhalations of 125/25 µg twice daily or two inhalations of 250/25 µg twice daily, giving total doses of 250/50 or 500/50 µg twice daily). Use of the lower fluticasone dose may be considered for patients at greater risk of inhaled corticosteroid adverse effects.

### Dosing by indication
- Asthma — initiation of maintenance therapy (moderate persistent asthma): 100/50 micrograms twice daily (commonly given as two inhalations of 50/25 µg twice daily). Patients started on initial maintenance therapy should be reviewed after 6–12 weeks.
- Children ≥4 years: Recommended dose for regular asthma management 100/50 micrograms twice daily (commonly given as two inhalations of 50/25 µg twice daily). Safety and efficacy for initiation of maintenance therapy in this paediatric subgroup have not been established for initial therapy. No data available for children <4 years.
- Elderly, renal impairment, hepatic impairment: No dose adjustment is generally required.

### Administration instructions
- For inhalation only. Must be used regularly for optimum benefit, including when asymptomatic.
- Not for relief of acute bronchospasm. Patients must have a short-acting bronchodilator available for rescue use.
- Use of a spacer with a metered-dose inhaler is recommended for many patients, particularly those with poor inhaler technique; instruct patient on correct spacer use and cleaning (wash in warm water and detergent and allow to air dry without rinsing or towel-drying; perform before initial use and at least monthly).
- Patients should be instructed to inhale slowly after actuation, hold breath as long as comfortable, then breathe out slowly; repeat for each actuation and avoid long delays between actuation and inhalation.

### Titration and discontinuation
- Titrate the fluticasone component to the lowest effective dose that maintains asthma control.
- Do not stop corticosteroid therapy abruptly in asthma patients because of risk of exacerbation; taper under physician supervision.
- Cessation in COPD may cause symptomatic decompensation and should be supervised.

### Missed dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to fluticasone, salmeterol, or any excipients of the product.
- No other absolute contraindications explicitly listed in the available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed / highlighted warnings
- Not specified as a boxed warning in the available sources. However: **Do not initiate in patients with unstable or acutely deteriorating asthma**; initiation of salmeterol in this setting has been associated with serious acute respiratory events including fatalities.

### General warnings and precautions
- Not for relief of acute symptoms; provide and instruct on rescue short‑acting bronchodilator use.
- Increasing use of short-acting bronchodilators indicates deterioration of control — review patient.
- Sudden/progressive deterioration of asthma control is potentially life‑threatening; review and consider increasing corticosteroid therapy and additional treatments.
- There is an increased reporting of pneumonia in COPD patients receiving fluticasone/salmeterol; remain vigilant as pneumonia and COPD exacerbation can have overlapping clinical features.
- Use with caution in patients with active or quiescent pulmonary tuberculosis.
- Use with caution in patients with thyrotoxicosis.
- Use with caution in patients with pre-existing cardiovascular disease; sympathomimetic effects (increased systolic blood pressure, heart rate) may occur, particularly at higher-than-therapeutic doses.
- Use with caution in patients predisposed to low serum potassium; β2-agonists may cause transient hypokalaemia.
- Rare reports of glaucoma and increased intraocular pressure with inhaled corticosteroids; refer for ophthalmologic evaluation if visual changes occur.
- Exercise caution when switching patients from systemic corticosteroids due to potential adrenal suppression; patients who have required high-dose inhaled or intermittent oral steroids may have impaired adrenal reserve for a considerable time; consider specialist advice for perioperative stress dosing.
- Rare cases of eosinophilic conditions (e.g., Churg–Strauss syndrome) have been reported, often associated with reduction/withdrawal of systemic corticosteroids.
- Paradoxical bronchospasm may occur; treat immediately with a short-acting bronchodilator, discontinue this product and institute alternative therapy if necessary.
- Behavioural and sleep disturbances, including hyperactivity and irritability, have been reported, predominantly in children.
- Possible systemic corticosteroid effects at high doses or with drug interactions (see Interactions), including Cushing’s syndrome, adrenal suppression, decreased bone mineral density, cataracts, glaucoma, growth retardation in children/adolescents.
- One‑puff twice-daily regimens have not been investigated in clinical trials [device-specific instructions vary]; follow approved dosing regimen.

### Monitoring requirements
- Regular review of asthma control and lung function; reassess dose regularly and titrate to lowest effective steroid dose.
- Monitor for signs/symptoms of pneumonia in COPD patients.
- Monitor growth in paediatric patients receiving inhaled corticosteroids.
- Consider assessment of adrenal function if signs of adrenal suppression or if high cumulative corticosteroid exposure or interacting medications are present.
- Periodic monitoring for effects on bone mineral density should be considered in patients at risk for osteoporosis with long-term therapy.
- Monitor for visual changes; consider ophthalmologic evaluation for suspected cataract or glaucoma.
- Monitor serum potassium in patients predisposed to hypokalemia or on concomitant medications that may lower potassium.
- Monitor for cardiovascular signs (palpitations, tachyarrhythmias) in susceptible patients.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Avoid coadministration with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors unless benefits outweigh risks (see Interactions).
- Avoid non-selective and selective β-blockers unless compelling reasons exist; these may blunt or antagonise bronchodilator effect and provoke bronchospasm.
- Not recommended to transfer patients from oral corticosteroids to this combination inhaled therapy without specialist supervision.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Fluticasone:
  - Fluticasone is extensively metabolised by cytochrome P450 3A4 (CYP3A4) in the gut and liver leading to low systemic exposure after inhalation under normal circumstances.
  - Potent CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., ritonavir) can markedly increase fluticasone plasma concentrations and have been shown to cause clinically significant cortisol suppression and systemic corticosteroid effects (including Cushing’s syndrome and adrenal suppression). Concomitant use of fluticasone and ritonavir should be avoided unless benefit outweighs risk.
  - Other CYP3A4 inhibitors such as ketoconazole may increase systemic exposure to fluticasone to a lesser extent; exercise caution with potent CYP3A4 inhibitors.
- Salmeterol:
  - Concomitant administration of ketoconazole significantly increased salmeterol plasma exposure (reported ~1.4‑fold increase in Cmax and ~15‑fold increase in AUC in a study); this may increase the risk of prolongation of the QTc interval and other cardiovascular adverse effects.
  - Coadministration of salmeterol with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (examples: ketoconazole, atazanavir, ritonavir, clarithromycin, indinavir, itraconazole, nefazodone, nelfinavir, saquinavir) is not recommended due to potential increased risk of cardiovascular adverse effects.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Beta‑blockers (non-selective and selective) may antagonise the bronchodilatory effect of salmeterol and may provoke bronchospasm; avoid unless compelling reasons for use.
- Concomitant drugs that prolong QT interval or increase risk of hypokalemia may have additive cardiovascular risks when combined with β2-agonists.

### Clinically significant interactions and management
- Ritonavir (and other potent CYP3A4 inhibitors): avoid concomitant use with fluticasone unless necessary; if coadministration cannot be avoided, monitor closely for signs of systemic corticosteroid excess and adrenal suppression and consider alternative asthma/COPD therapy.
- Ketoconazole and other strong CYP3A4 inhibitors: avoid or use with caution due to increased exposure to fluticasone and salmeterol; increased salmeterol exposure may prolong QTc — use is not recommended with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors.
- Beta‑blockers: avoid or use with caution; if required, consider cardioselective agents and monitor respiratory status.
- Monitor for drug interactions that may increase risk of hypokalaemia (e.g., diuretics) or affect cardiac conduction when used with salmeterol.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Pregnancy category: **[Australian] Category B3**.
- Clinical data: Limited data from pregnant women. An observational retrospective epidemiological cohort study reported no increased risk of major congenital malformations (MCMs) diagnosed by 1 year following first‑trimester exposure to inhaled fluticasone propionate alone or combination salmeterol–fluticasone when compared with non‑fluticasone inhaled corticosteroids in an asthma cohort. Reported adjusted odds ratios did not show increased risk (example: adjusted OR 1.1; 95% CI: 0.5–2.3 in one comparison).
- Animal studies: Reproductive toxicity studies demonstrated foetal effects at excessive systemic exposures of potent β2‑agonists and glucocorticosteroids; relevance to humans at therapeutic inhaled doses is uncertain.
- Recommendation: Use during pregnancy only if expected benefit to the mother outweighs potential risk to the fetus.

### Breastfeeding
- Fluticasone and salmeterol plasma concentrations after inhaled doses are low; therefore breastmilk concentrations are likely to be low.
- Animal data: Salmeterol detected in milk of lactating animals; radiolabelled fluticasone in lactating rats produced milk levels higher than plasma.
- No human breastmilk data available.
- Recommendation: Consider benefits of breastfeeding and clinical need for the drug; use only if expected benefit to the mother outweighs risk to the infant.

### Fertility
- Neither fluticasone nor salmeterol alone showed significant effects on fertility in available data; combination studies were not reported.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Children ≥4 years: Recommended regimen for regular asthma management described as 100/50 micrograms twice daily (commonly two inhalations of 50/25 µg twice daily).
- Not recommended as initiation of maintenance therapy in children without prior trial of alternative options for those <6 years; for children <6 years consider alternative options such as an inhaled corticosteroid and/or leukotriene receptor antagonist first.
- No data in children <4 years.
- Growth: Monitor growth in children and adolescents receiving inhaled corticosteroids and weigh potential growth effects against clinical benefit. Growth retardation has been reported rarely (postmarketing).

### Elderly
- No special dose adjustment required; use caution as with younger adults. Monitor for systemic corticosteroid effects and cardiovascular events.

### Renal impairment
- No dose adjustment required in renal impairment as stated in sources.

### Hepatic impairment
- No dose adjustment required in hepatic impairment as stated in sources; however caution may be warranted because metabolism is predominantly via CYP3A4 — monitor for systemic effects in severe hepatic impairment.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **Fluticasone**: inhaled corticosteroid with local anti‑inflammatory effects in the airways.
- **Salmeterol**: long‑acting β2‑adrenoceptor agonist producing bronchodilation via relaxation of bronchial smooth muscle.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption/distribution:
  - After inhalation, fluticasone systemic exposure is generally low due to extensive first‑pass metabolism and high systemic clearance mediated by CYP3A4 in gut and liver.
  - Specific bioavailability, Cmax, half‑life, volume of distribution, and protein binding details: Not specified in available sources.
- Metabolism:
  - Fluticasone is extensively metabolised by CYP3A4.
  - Salmeterol metabolism details beyond CYP interactions: Not specified in available sources.
- Elimination:
  - Specific routes and clearance parameters: Not specified in available sources.
- Notable interaction pharmacokinetics:
  - Ritonavir (strong CYP3A4 inhibitor) greatly increases fluticasone plasma concentrations and results in marked reductions in serum cortisol.
  - Ketoconazole coadministration significantly increases salmeterol exposure (reported ~1.4‑fold Cmax, ~15‑fold AUC) and may prolong QTc.

### Pharmacodynamics
- The combination provides both anti‑inflammatory action (fluticasone) and sustained bronchodilation (salmeterol), indicated for maintenance therapy in asthma and symptomatic treatment in selected COPD patients.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Clinical trial evidence described in the available sources supports:
  - Use of the combination for maintenance therapy in asthma, including initiation in patients with moderate persistent asthma not controlled on reliever therapy; patients initiated on combination therapy should be reviewed after 6–12 weeks.
  - Increased reporting of pneumonia in COPD trials in patients receiving fluticasone/salmeterol — clinicians should be alert to this risk as clinical features of pneumonia and COPD exacerbation overlap.
- Pregnancy observational study:
  - A retrospective epidemiological cohort study of first‑trimester inhaled corticosteroid exposure in an asthma cohort (n≈5,362) reported 131 major congenital malformations identified overall; among 1,612 pregnancies exposed to fluticasone or salmeterol–fluticasone there were 42 diagnosed MCMs. Adjusted OR for MCMs by 1 year was 1.1 (95% CI: 0.5–2.3) for fluticasone‑exposed vs non‑fluticasone ICS exposed women with moderate asthma, and 1.2 (95% CI: 0.7–2.0) for women with considerable to severe asthma. Absolute risks across severity strata were comparable to unexposed populations in the epidemiological comparison presented in the source.
- Limitations/other trial notes:
  - One inhalation (one puff) twice daily regimens have not been investigated in clinical trials per available sources.
  - Specific pivotal trial names, designs, endpoints, and full results are not detailed in the available sources.
