# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single, cohesive reference. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Name:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [United States].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [United States].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [United States].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - **Administration:** Delivered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
    - **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) over the first 24 hours.
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically range from 50 mg/kg/day, though doses up to 300 mg/kg/day have been administered if clinically required.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** 
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Dose Adjustments:** Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations.

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration before use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [United States].

### Monitoring
- Plasma carnitine concentrations should be obtained prior to initiating parenteral therapy.
- Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Reactions may occur within minutes of injection. Therapy should be discontinued immediately if a severe reaction occurs [United States].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with and without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [United States].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** Chronic high doses of **oral** levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology (ESRD or metabolic errors).

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site reaction (33–38%), Pharyngitis (15–27%), Hypertension (18–21%), Diarrhea (9–35%), Headache (3–37%), Nausea/Vomiting (8–21%), Cough (10–18%), Dizziness (10–18%), Fever (5–12%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Abdominal pain, Chest pain, Asthenia, Infection, Tachycardia, Hypercalcemia, Peripheral edema, Dyspnea, Pruritus |
| **Postmarketing/Other** | Body odor, Gastritis, Seizures, Anaphylaxis |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, and increased seizure activity [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concomitantly with levocarnitine.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels at therapy initiation and after any dose adjustments [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [United States: Category not assigned]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant should be weighed against the benefits of maternal treatment [United States].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy for metabolic disorders are established; dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg) [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed; however, general caution is advised in dose selection for elderly patients due to the higher frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. Safety and efficacy of high-dose **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency [United States].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, it alleviates metabolic abnormalities caused by carnitine loss during dialysis and reduced renal synthesis.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on dialysis receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly showed a significant increase in pre-dialysis (trough) plasma carnitine from 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L by week 8.
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservative [United States].
