# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory information into a clinically relevant format.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI when prescribing. At the time of preparation, only the United States PI was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/amino acid derivative; (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not explicitly stated in source, but standard classification).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injection (solution), 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Titration:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours, and no less frequently than every 6 hours.
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported administered dose is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustments:** Adjustments should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### General Instructions
- **Monitoring:** Obtain plasma carnitine concentrations before starting parenteral therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine (target free carnitine levels: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- **Inspection:** Visually inspect parenteral products for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** No contraindications are currently listed in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. Treatment must be discontinued immediately upon a severe reaction [US].
- **Seizures:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulation Caution):** While this PI concerns the injection, it is noted that chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

### Serious/Specific Reactions
- **Metabolic:** Transient nausea and vomiting.
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (post-marketing).
- **Other:** Body odor and gastritis have been reported less frequently [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Clinical Note:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned/Current labeling]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine in milk. Weigh the benefits to the mother against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use is indicated for inborn errors of metabolism; see general dosing and administration instructions.
- **Geriatric Use:** Not specified in the available source [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** This product is specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. Monitor for the accumulation of metabolites if oral forms are used in this population [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for energy production (via beta-oxidation). It also facilitates the excretion of toxic acyl-CoA esters by forming acylcarnitines [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Administration to ESRD patients results in increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Normal plasma free carnitine levels are 40 to 50 µmol/L; deficiency is defined as <20 µmol/L [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis trough concentrations from 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** Studies at 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg demonstrated dose-dependent increases in trough concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial. The formulation contains no preservatives.
