# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical data into a structured format.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP); a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Metabolic Disorders:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. This may be administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours) over the first 24 hours.
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though individual requirements may vary. The maximum recorded dose is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
    - **Administration:** Administer as a 2 to 3 minute bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine levels, starting as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### Monitoring
- **Plasma Concentrations:** It is recommended to establish a baseline plasma carnitine concentration before starting parenteral therapy.
- **Target Range:** For metabolic disorders, maintain plasma free carnitine between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Frequency:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma concentrations is advised.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None Known:** There are no known contraindications listed for levocarnitine injection in the provided source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in dialysis patients. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. Treatment should be discontinued immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients both with and without pre-existing seizure disorders. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Metabolite Accumulation):** While the injection bypasses the gut, healthcare providers should note that chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function can lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **General Monitoring:** Monitor INR in patients concurrently taking warfarin.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects ($\ge$ 5% in ESRD Trials)
The following events were reported in clinical trials of ESRD patients regardless of causality:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), headache (22%), abdominal pain (9%), fever (7%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), chest pain (12%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%).
- **Other:** Asthenia (9%), dizziness (13%), anemia (6%).

### Serious and Postmarketing Effects
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Large doses may cause diarrhea [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used with levocarnitine. **Management:** Monitor INR levels frequently after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not explicitly assigned in updated format, but animal studies at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm]. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess intake must be weighed against maternal benefit [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in rat and rabbit studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg), which applies to pediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically in ESRD patients on dialysis (see Section 3). Caution is advised regarding the accumulation of metabolites if switching to oral formulations in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with specific metabolic defects [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, IV administration increases plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Normal plasma concentrations are approximately 40 to 50 µmol/L; dialysis patients often have baseline levels near 20 µmol/L [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses administered three times weekly post-dialysis increased trough concentrations from a baseline of 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L by week 8.
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine levels in ESRD patients, the impact on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, malaise) in this specific population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].
