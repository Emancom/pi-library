# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial review is based on official Product Information (PI) documents from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a single, clinically focused reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule for fatty acid transport).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection, solution: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Doses should be administered every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to treatment. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels <40 to 50 µmol/L. Adjustments should be guided by trough levels; downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL. Store in PVC bags at 25°C for up to 24 hours [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occurred within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine (oral or IV) has been reported to cause seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with pre-existing conditions, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **General Precautions:** The safety/efficacy of oral levocarnitine in renal insufficiency has not been evaluated. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD or severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide), which are normally excreted in urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Anaphylaxis/Hypersensitivity:** [US].
- **Seizures:** [US].

### Common Adverse Effects (≥5% in ESRD clinical trials)
In dialysis patients, the following events occurred at a rate ≥5% (regardless of causality):
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), Hypotension (3–19%), Tachycardia (5–9%), Chest pain (6–15%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (9–35%), Vomiting (9–21%), Nausea (5–12%), Abdominal pain (5–21%).
- **Neurological/Nervous:** Headache (3–37%), Dizziness (10–18%), Paresthesia (3–12%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), Cough increase (10–18%), Dyspnea (3–11%).
- **Body as a Whole:** Injection site reaction (27–38%), Pain (21–35%), Infection (10–24%), Fever (5–12%), Asthenia (8–12%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), Hypervolemia (3–12%).

### Other Reported Effects
- **Body Odor:** Transient nausea and vomiting have been observed; less frequent reactions include body odor and gastritis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. Monitoring of INR is recommended after initiation or dose adjustment [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned [US]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after exogenous administration. Weigh the risks to the child against the benefits to the mother; discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is based on mg/kg, applicable to paediatric populations (see Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically in ESRD patients (see Section 3). Caution is advised regarding the accumulation of toxic metabolites with high-dose *oral* therapy in patients with compromised renal function [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for β-oxidation and subsequent energy production. In patients with inborn errors of metabolism, it promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and [3H]-γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Administration to ESRD patients on hemodialysis results in increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** Efficacy to increase carnitine concentrations was demonstrated in two double-blind, placebo-controlled trials. Doses of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg three times per week were evaluated. While plasma concentrations increased significantly, the effects on clinical outcomes (e.g., signs/symptoms of carnitine deficiency) have not been determined in this population [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C (59°F–86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of an opened vial as the formulation is preservative-free [US].

- **United States:** Rx-only.
