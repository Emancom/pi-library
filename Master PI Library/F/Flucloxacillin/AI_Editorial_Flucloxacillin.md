## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Flucloxacillin sodium monohydrate.
- **Chemical Class:** Beta-lactamase-stable isoxazolylpenicillin.
- **Brand Names:** DBL Flucloxacillin Sodium [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J01CF05.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Powder for injection, 1 g (as flucloxacillin sodium monohydrate) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intramuscular (IM), Intravenous (IV), Intrapleural, Intra-articular [Australian].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Flucloxacillin is indicated for the treatment of infections caused by confirmed or suspected staphylococci and other Gram-positive cocci. Specific indications include [Australian]:
- Pneumonia.
- Osteomyelitis.
- Skin and skin structure infections (e.g., cellulitis, infected burns, wound infections).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Adult Dosing [Australian]
- **Intramuscular:** 250 mg every 6 hours.
- **Intravenous:** 250 mg to 1 g every 6 hours.
- **Intrapleural:** 250 mg once daily.
- **Intra-articular:** 250 mg to 500 mg once daily.
- *Note:* Systemic doses may be doubled for severe infections.

### Paediatric Dosing [Australian]
- **Children 2 to 10 years:** 1/2 the adult dose.
- **Children under 2 years:** 1/4 the adult dose.
- *Note:* Dosage may be increased in severe infections.

### Administration Instructions [Australian]
- **IM Injection:** Dissolve 1 g in 2.5 mL Water for Injections.
- **IV Injection:** Dissolve 1 g in 15 to 20 mL Water for Injections. Administer by slow IV injection over 3 to 4 minutes.
- **IV Infusion:** May be added to compatible infusion fluids or injected into the drip tube over 3 to 4 minutes.
- **Stability:** Reconstituted solutions are stable for 24 hours when stored at 2°C to 8°C.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known history of hypersensitivity to beta-lactam antibiotics (e.g., penicillins, cephalosporins) [Australian].
- **Hepatic History:** Previous history of flucloxacillin-associated jaundice or hepatic dysfunction [Australian].
- **Ocular Use:** Must not be used in the eye, either conjunctivally or locally [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning: Liver Toxicity [Australian]
> **Flucloxacillin sodium monohydrate can cause severe hepatitis and cholestatic jaundice, which may be protracted. This reaction is more frequent in older patients and those who take the drug for prolonged periods.**

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Anaphylaxis:** Serious and occasionally fatal hypersensitivity reactions have been reported. Immediate emergency treatment with adrenaline, oxygen, and steroids is required for serious reactions [Australian].
- **Clostridioides difficile-Associated Disease:** Pseudomembranous colitis ranging from mild to life-threatening has been reported. Discontinue therapy if suspected and avoid anti-peristaltic agents [Australian].
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCAR):** SJS, TEN, DRESS, and AGEP have been reported. Discontinue immediately if SCAR is suspected [Australian].
- **Metabolic Acidosis:** Caution is required when co-administering with paracetamol due to the risk of high anion gap metabolic acidosis (HAGMA), particularly in patients with sepsis, malnutrition, or renal impairment [Australian].

### Monitoring Requirements
- Periodically check hepatic function during prolonged treatment [Australian].
- In high-dose treatments (>5 days), monitor electrolyte balance, blood counts, and renal function [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Hepatic
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Hepatitis and cholestatic jaundice (estimated frequency 1 in 15,000 exposures). Risks increase with age (>55 years) and duration of therapy (>14 days) [Australian].

### Hypersensitivity
- **Common (1–10%):** Rashes, urticaria, purpura [Australian].
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Anaphylaxis, erythema multiforme, Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN) [Australian].

### Gastrointestinal
- **Common (1–10%):** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, dyspepsia [Australian].

### Other Systems
- **Haemic/Lymphatic:** Haemolytic anaemia, thrombocytopenia, leucopoenia, and agranulocytosis (usually reversible) [Australian].
- **Renal:** Isolated cases of interstitial nephritis and haematuria [Australian].
- **Metabolic:** Very rare cases of HAGMA when used with paracetamol [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Probenecid:** Decreases renal tubular secretion of flucloxacillin, leading to increased and prolonged serum levels [Australian].
- **Paracetamol:** Increased risk of high anion gap metabolic acidosis (HAGMA) [Australian].
- **Aminoglycosides:** Potential for precipitation and inactivation if mixed in the same solution for injection [Australian].
- **Blood Products:** Should not be mixed with blood products or proteinaceous fluids [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category B1. Limited human data, but no evidence of increased foetal damage or teratogenicity in animal studies. Use should be reserved for essential cases [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Excreted in breast milk in trace amounts. Alternative feeding is recommended to avoid newborn sensitisation [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available in source documents [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use with extreme caution in jaundiced neonates or premature infants as flucloxacillin may reduce albumin-bound bilirubin, increasing the risk of bilirubin encephalopathy [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of liver toxicity; monitoring is advised [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dose or interval modification may be required due to increased half-life. Flucloxacillin is not significantly removed by haemodialysis [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Modification may not be necessary as hepatic metabolism is minimal; however, periodic monitoring of liver function is recommended [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal narrow-spectrum antibiotic; inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis. Stable against degradation by staphylococcal beta-lactamases [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Well absorbed following IM administration [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Highly bound to serum proteins [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily excreted via the kidneys through glomerular filtration and tubular secretion [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Active against *Staphylococcus aureus* (including beta-lactamase producing strains), *Streptococcus pyogenes*, and *Streptococcus pneumoniae*. Not active against MRSA or Gram-negative bacilli [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C in a dry place. Protect from light and moisture [Australian].
- **Reconstituted Solution:** If storage is necessary, hold at 2°C to 8°C for no more than 24 hours [Australian].
