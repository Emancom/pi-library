# Editorial Product Information: Fluvoxamine

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial was developed using official Product Information from the following regulatory authorities:
    *   Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of fluvoxamine for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority for the most current and legally binding prescribing information.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Fluvoxamine maleate.
*   **Chemical Class:** Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitor (SSRI).
*   **Brand Names:** APO-Fluvoxamine [Australian].
*   **ATC Code:** N06AB08.
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    *   Film-coated tablets: 50 mg (golden, round, scored) and 100 mg (reddish-brown, pillow-shaped, scored) [Australian].
*   **Route of Administration:** Oral.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Major Depression:** Treatment of major depression in adults [Australian].
*   **Obsessive Compulsive Disorder (OCD):** Treatment of OCD in adults and children aged 8 years and older [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
*   **Major Depression:** Initial dose is 50 mg daily, administered in the evening for one week. Titrate by 50 mg weekly until effective. Maximum dose: 300 mg/day. The typical effective dose is 100 mg/day [Australian].
*   **Obsessive Compulsive Disorder (OCD):** Initial dose is 50 mg daily for 3 to 4 days. Increase gradually (50 mg every 4 to 6 days) to an effective dose. Maximum dose: 300 mg/day. Usual effective range is 100–300 mg/day [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
*   Tablets should be swallowed with water, without chewing.
*   Doses up to 150 mg may be given as a single daily dose (preferably in the evening).
*   Total daily doses exceeding 150 mg should be divided into 2 or 3 doses [Australian].

### Missed Dose Instructions
*   Not specified in available sources.

### Withdrawal and Discontinuation
*   **Abrupt discontinuation must be avoided.** The dose should be tapered over at least one to two weeks to reduce the risk of withdrawal reactions. If intolerable symptoms occur, resume the previous dose and taper more gradually [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
*   **Tizanidine:** Concurrent use is contraindicated due to increased plasma concentrations of tizanidine.
*   **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Contraindicated during treatment and within 14 days of discontinuing an irreversible MAOI or linezolid. A 1-week washout period is required after stopping fluvoxamine before starting an MAOI [Australian].
*   **Pimozide and Ramelteon:** Concurrent use is contraindicated [Australian].
*   **Cisapride:** Concurrent use is contraindicated due to risk of QT prolongation.
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to any component of the formulation.
*   **Lactation:** Fluvoxamine is contraindicated in nursing mothers [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Clinical Worsening and Suicide Risk
*   **Suicidality:** Increased risk of suicidal thinking and behavior in children, adolescents, and young adults (18–24 years) during initial treatment. Patients of all ages should be monitored for clinical worsening, especially at the start of therapy or during dose changes.
*   **Bipolar Disorder:** Screen for bipolar disorder prior to initiating antidepressants to avoid precipitating a manic episode [Australian].

### General Warnings
*   **Serotonin Syndrome (SS) / Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** Risk is increased when combined with other serotonergic agents (e.g., triptans, tramadol, St John’s Wort) or neuroleptics. Discontinue immediately if symptoms (hyperthermia, rigidity, autonomic instability, mental status changes) occur [Australian].
*   **Akathisia:** Development of psychomotor restlessness may occur, typically within the first few weeks; dose increases may be detrimental in these patients [Australian].
*   **Seizures:** Caution in patients with a history of convulsive disorders. Avoid in unstable epilepsy and discontinue if seizures occur [Australian].
*   **Hyponatremia:** Reported rarely, typically in elderly patients; appears reversible upon discontinuation.
*   **Glycemic Control:** May disturb glucose tolerance; diabetic patients may require dose adjustments of anti-diabetic medication [Australian].
*   **Haematological Effects:** Increased risk of cutaneous bleeding (ecchymosis, purpura) and haemorrhagic manifestations (GI or gynaecological bleeding). Caution with concomitant use of NSAIDs, aspirin, or anticoagulants.
*   **Dermatological Effects:** Serious skin reactions, including Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), have been reported [Australian].
*   **Mydriasis:** Use with caution in patients with raised intraocular pressure or acute narrow-angle glaucoma.

## 6. Adverse Effects

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (>10%) | Nausea (often with vomiting; typically diminishes within 2 weeks). |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Anorexia, agitation, nervousness, anxiety, insomnia, somnolence, tremor, headache, dizziness, palpitations/tachycardia, abdominal pain, constipation, diarrhoea, dry mouth, dyspepsia, hyperhydrosis, asthenia, malaise. |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Extrapyramidal disorder, ataxia, (orthostatic) hypotension, cutaneous hypersensitivity reactions (rash, pruritus, angioedema), arthralgia, myalgia, abnormal (delayed) ejaculation, hallucinations, confusional state, aggression. |
| **Rare** (<0.1%) | Convulsions, hepatic function abnormal, photosensitivity, galactorrhoea, mania. |
| **Frequency Not Known** | Hyperprolactinaemia, SIADH, hyponatremia, weight changes, serotonin syndrome, SJS/TEN, akathisia, paraesthesia, glaucoma, haemorrhage, bone fractures, micturition disorders, anorgasmia, menstrual disorders, postpartum haemorrhage. |

*   **Paediatric-specific events (OCD):** Abnormal thinking, cough increase, emotional lability, epistaxis, and hyperkinesia were reported more frequently than in adults [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
Fluvoxamine is a potent inhibitor of **CYP1A2** and **CYP2C19**. It inhibits CYP2C9, CYP2D6, and CYP3A4 to a lesser extent.
*   **Strong CYP1A2 Inhibition:** Increases plasma levels of tricyclic antidepressants (clomipramine, amitriptyline), neuroleptics (clozapine, olanzapine), caffeine, theophylline, tacrine, and methadone. Combination with these agents is generally not recommended or requires significant dose reduction [Australian].
*   **Ramelteon:** AUC increased 190-fold; concurrent use is contraindicated [Australian].
*   **Warfarin:** Increases plasma concentrations and prolongs prothrombin time; monitor closely.
*   **Benzodiazepines:** Increases levels of oxidatively metabolised benzodiazepines (e.g., alprazolam, diazepam, midazolam).

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
*   **Serotonergic Drugs:** Potential for Serotonin Syndrome with MAOIs, SSRIs, SNRIs, triptans, lithium, and St John’s Wort.
*   **Alcohol:** Patients should avoid alcohol consumption.
*   **QT-Prolonging Agents:** Increased risk with terfenadine, astemizole, or cisapride (contraindicated) [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy Category:** **Category C** [Australian].
*   **Risk:** Periodic pulmonary hypertension of the newborn (PPHN) risk is increased. Neonates exposed late in the third trimester may develop withdrawal/discontinuation symptoms (e.g., respiratory distress, seizures, temperature instability).
*   **Postpartum Haemorrhage:** Increased risk when SSRIs are used within the month prior to birth.
*   **Breastfeeding:** Fluvoxamine is excreted in human milk. Due to potential risks (and observed postnatal mortality in animal studies), it is **contraindicated** for use by nursing mothers [Australian].
*   **Fertility:** Animal studies showed impaired fertility at high doses; human risk is unknown. Use is not recommended for those attempting to conceive unless clinically necessary [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Approved for OCD (ages 8–17). Initial dose: 25 mg at bedtime. Titrate by 25 mg every 4–7 days. Maximum dose: 200 mg/day. **Not** recommended for Major Depression in patients <18 years [Australian].
*   **Elderly Use:** Pharmacokinetics are similar to younger adults, but upward titration should be slower and performed with caution [Australian].
*   **Renal Impairment:** Start with a low dose and titrate slowly under careful monitoring [Australian].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Clearance is slower (e.g., cirrhosis). Start with a low dose and titrate slowly; monitor for increases in hepatic enzymes [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
*   Selective inhibition of presynaptic reuptake of serotonin (5-HT). It has minimal interference with noradrenergic or dopaminergic processes and limited affinity for alpha/beta-adrenergic, histaminergic, or muscarinic receptors [Australian].
*   Fluvoxamine exists as two isomers; only the **E-isomer** is pharmacologically active [Australian].

### Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Almost completely absorbed; absolute bioavailability is ~53%. Unaffected by food.
*   **Distribution:** 80% plasma protein bound. $V_d$ is ~20 L/kg. Steady state reached within one week.
*   **Metabolism:** Extensively transformed in the liver, primarily via oxidative demethylation.
*   **Elimination:** Plasma half-life is 12–13 hours after a single dose and ~22 hours at steady state. Excreted almost entirely via urine (94% as metabolites) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Depression:** Efficacy established in 15 trials (6-week duration) showing superiority over placebo and comparable efficacy to tricyclic antidepressants (imipramine, desipramine) [Australian].
*   **Relapse Prevention:** A 1-year study demonstrated a significantly lower relapse rate for fluvoxamine (13%) compared to placebo (35%) [Australian].
*   **OCD:** Adult efficacy shown in two 10-week placebo-controlled trials. Paediatric efficacy (ages 8–17) demonstrated in a 10-week multicentre trial with sustained benefit shown in open-label extensions up to 2 years [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   Store below 30°C [Australian].
*   Shelf life is specific to the batch/packaging (refer to expiry on pack) [Australian].
