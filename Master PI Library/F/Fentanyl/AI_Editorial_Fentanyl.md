# Editorial Product Information: Fentanyl (Sublingual)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonized, clinically accurate editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or the specific legal requirements for prescribing in any particular jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory guidelines for specific prescribing mandates.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Fentanyl (as citrate)
- **Chemical Class:** Phenylpiperidine derivative; opioid agonist
- **Brand Names:** **ABSTRAL** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** N02AB03
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Sublingual tablets: 100 mcg, 200 mcg, 300 mcg, 400 mcg, 600 mcg, and 800 mcg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Sublingual
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** A. Menarini Australia Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Breakthrough Cancer Pain:** Indicated for the management of breakthrough pain in adult patients with cancer who are already receiving maintenance opioid therapy for chronic background pain [Australian].
- **Requirement for Opioid Tolerance:** Patients must be considered opioid-tolerant, defined as taking at least 60 mg of oral morphine daily, or an equianalgesic dose of another opioid, for one week or longer [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Initial Dose:** All patients must begin therapy with a single 100 mcg sublingual tablet, regardless of their current background opioid dose [Australian].
- **Titration:** Upward titration is required until an optimal maintenance dose is reached that provides adequate analgesia with acceptable adverse effects.
- **Dosing Frequency:**
    - No more than two (2) doses may be used to treat a single episode of breakthrough pain.
    - If the first dose does not provide adequate relief, a second (supplemental) dose may be administered 30 minutes later [Australian].
    - Patients must wait at least 2 hours before treating another episode of breakthrough pain [Australian].
    - Maximum of four (4) doses per day [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Place the tablet directly under the tongue at the deepest part.
- Allow the tablet to dissolve completely; do not swallow, chew, or suck the tablet.
- Avoid eating or drinking until the tablet has fully dissolved.
- In patients with dry mouth, the buccal mucosa may be moistened with water before administration [Australian].

### Maintenance and Re-adjustment
- If a patient experiences more than four breakthrough pain episodes per day for more than four consecutive days, the maintenance dose for background chronic pain should be re-evaluated [Australian].
- **Switching Products:** Fentanyl products are not interchangeable on a microgram-per-microgram basis. Patients switching from other immediate-release fentanyl products must be re-titrated from the 100 mcg starting dose [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to fentanyl or any excipients [Australian].
- **Opioid Non-Tolerant Patients:** Due to the risk of life-threatening respiratory depression [Australian].
- **Severe Respiratory Disease:** Including severe obstructive lung conditions or acute respiratory depression [Australian].
- **Specific Exclusions:** Not for use in non-cancer pain, acute pain, or chronic non-cancer pain [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [Australian]
- **Hazardous and Harmful Use:** Potential for abuse, misuse, and addiction, which can lead to overdose and death.
- **Life-Threatening Respiratory Depression:** Serious or fatal respiratory depression may occur. Monitoring is critical during initiation and titration.
- **Concomitant CNS Depressants:** Use with benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, or alcohol may result in profound sedation, coma, and death.

### General Warnings
- **Accidental Ingestion:** Can be fatal, especially to children; must be stored out of reach [Australian].
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Risk increases when co-administered with SSRIs, SNRIs, or MAOIs [Australian].
- **Sleep-Related Breathing Disorders:** Can cause central sleep apnoea (CSA) and hypoxemia in a dose-dependent manner [Australian].
- **Head Injury/Intracranial Pressure:** Use with extreme caution in patients susceptible to the effects of CO2 retention [Australian].
- **Hyperalgesia:** Opioid-induced hyperalgesia may occur, requiring dose reduction or opioid rotation [Australian].

### Monitoring Requirements
- Assess risk for substance use disorder prior to and during treatment.
- Monitor for signs of respiratory depression and sedation, particularly during the first 24–72 hours of therapy and following dose increases.

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Note: Frequencies based on Australian clinical trial data.*

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Nausea |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Dizziness, headache, somnolence, dyspnoea, vomiting, constipation, dry mouth, fatigue, hyperhidrosis |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Anorexia, depression, anxiety, insomnia, tremor, hypotension, tachycardia, bradycardia, malaise, asthenia |
| **Not Known** | Respiratory depression, central sleep apnoea, convulsion, drug dependence, delirium, anaphylaxis, neonatal abstinence syndrome |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Respiratory depression, hypotension, shock, and toxic leukoencephalopathy (observed with overdose) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** (e.g., ritonavir, ketoconazole, erythromycin, grapefruit juice) May increase fentanyl plasma concentrations, leading to prolonged opioid effects and fatal respiratory depression [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** (e.g., rifampicin, carbamazepine) May decrease efficacy [Australian].
- **CNS Depressants:** (e.g., benzodiazepines, alcohol, gabapentinoids) Increase risk of respiratory depression and profound sedation [Australian].
- **MAOIs:** Not recommended within 14 days of MAOI use due to potential for severe potentiation [Australian].
- **Serotonergic Drugs:** (e.g., SSRIs, SNRIs) Risk of serotonin syndrome [Australian].
- **Partial Agonists/Antagonists:** (e.g., buprenorphine) May precipitate withdrawal symptoms in dependent patients [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category C** [Australian]: Fentanyl crosses the placenta. Use only if benefits justify risks to the fetus.
    - **Neonatal Abstinence Syndrome:** Prolonged maternal use may result in life-threatening withdrawal in the neonate [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Fentanyl is excreted in breast milk and may cause sedation/respiratory depression in the infant. Breastfeeding should not be restarted until at least 5 days after the last dose [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Prolonged use may lead to sexual dysfunction and impaired fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for patients under 18 years due to lack of safety/efficacy data [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** May have reduced clearance and increased sensitivity; dose titration should be approached with extreme caution [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Bioavailability may increase and clearance decrease. Careful monitoring is required during titration [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Fentanyl is a pure $\mu$-opioid receptor agonist. It is approximately 100 times more potent than morphine as an analgesic [Australian].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed via oral mucosa (~30 mins); absolute bioavailability is approximately 54%.
- **Distribution:** Highly lipophilic; 80–85% protein-bound (primarily $\alpha$1-acid glycoprotein). Vd is 3–6 L/kg.
- **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP3A4 to inactive norfentanyl. Undergoes first-pass metabolism if swallowed.
- **Elimination:** ~75% excreted in urine (mostly metabolites); elimination half-life is ~7 hours, with a terminal half-life of ~20 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study EN3267-005:** A Phase III, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 131 opioid-tolerant cancer patients.
- **Primary Outcome:** Sum of Pain Intensity Difference (SPID) at 30 minutes.
- **Results:** Abstral was statistically superior to placebo (SPID 49.3 vs 35.23, p=0.0004). Significant pain relief was observed as early as 10 minutes post-dose [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C.
- Keep in the original blister package to protect from moisture [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 8 (Controlled Drug)** [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription only; requires specific storage and recording for controlled substances.
