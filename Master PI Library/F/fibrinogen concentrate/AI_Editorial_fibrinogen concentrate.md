# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for healthcare professionals. 
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis of available regulatory information and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved labelling for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
*   **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
*   **Brand Name(s):** **Levocarnitine Injection** (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    *   **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Metabolic Disorders)
*   **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg [US].
*   **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US]. 
*   **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
*   **Frequency:** Administered every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours (either by infusion or IV injection) [US].
*   **Maximum Dose:** Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
*   **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
*   **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
*   **Titration:** Dose adjustments may begin as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
*   **Parenteral Inspection:** Visually inspect for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [US].
*   **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].
*   **Discard Unused Portion:** The formulation contains no preservative; discard any unused portion of an opened vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
*   None [US].

### General Warnings and Precautions
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate risks versus benefits before re-administration [US].
*   **Seizures:** Reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency and/or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
*   **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulation Precaution):** While the injection is indicated for ESRD, clinicians should note that chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may result in the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
*   **Carnitine Levels:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to parenteral therapy. 
*   **Targets:** In metabolic disorders, monitoring should be weekly or monthly, targeting plasma free carnitine concentrations between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
*   **ESRD Monitoring:** Initiation of therapy may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) levels below 40 µmol/L to 50 µmol/L [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concurrently with levocarnitine.
*   **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
*   **Jurisdictional Category:** Not explicitly assigned a letter category in source, but reproductive data is provided [US].
*   **Risk Summary:** Animal studies (rats/rabbits) at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. There are no adequate/well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].

### Breastfeeding
*   **Safety Information:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration [US].
*   **Recommendation:** Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of maternal supplementation. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].

### Fertility
*   No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Use is described in the context of inborn errors of metabolism; see Section 3 for weight-based dosing (50 mg/kg) [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Renal Impairment:** Indicated for use in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. The safety of *oral* levocarnitine is not established in renal insufficiency [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted [US].

### Pharmacodynamics
In ESRD patients, carnitine deficiency (defined as free carnitine <20 µmol/L) may be caused by reduced intake, reduced renal synthesis, and dialytic losses. Levocarnitine injection increases plasma carnitine concentrations [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours (IV bolus) [US].
*   **Elimination:** Primarily renal; approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
*   **Bioavailability:** Absolute bioavailability of oral formulations is approximately 15–16% [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (n=12), 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma concentrations from ~20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
*   **Dose-Ranging Study:** A study comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses in ESRD patients showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
*   **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing [US].
*   **Shelf Life:** Not specified; refer to the expiry date on the packaging.
