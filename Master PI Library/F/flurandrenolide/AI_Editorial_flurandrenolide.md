# Editorial Product Information: Flurandrenolide

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of flurandrenolide for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis intended for clinical reference and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Flurandrenolide (USP), a potent synthetic corticosteroid.
- **Chemical Class**: Fluorinated corticosteroid.
- **Brand Names**: Cordran [US].
- **ATC Code**: D07AC07 (Corticosteroids, moderately potent; Group III).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**: 
    - Topical Cream: 0.05% (0.5 mg per gram) [US].
- **Routes of Administration**: Topical.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: INA Pharmaceutics, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Corticosteroid-Responsive Dermatoses**: Indicated for the relief of inflammatory and pruritic manifestations associated with dermatoses that respond to topical corticosteroids [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult)**: Apply a small quantity of the cream to the affected area(s) two or three times daily.
- **Administration Instructions**: 
    - The cream should be rubbed gently into the skin.
    - Specifically indicated for moist lesions [US].
    - Do not use on the face, underarms, or groin unless directed by a physician [US].
    - External use only; avoid contact with the eyes.
- **Therapy Duration**: Discontinue therapy once control is achieved. If no clinical improvement is observed within 2 weeks, a reassessment of the diagnosis is required [US].
- **Occlusive Dressings**: Should not be used unless specifically directed by a clinician. In pediatric patients, tight-fitting diapers or plastic pants may function as occlusive dressings and should be avoided if the diaper area is being treated [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity**: Known history of hypersensitivity to flurandrenolide or any excipient in the preparation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **HPA Axis Suppression**: Systemic absorption of topical corticosteroids can produce reversible hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis suppression. Risk factors include the use of potent steroids, application over large surface areas, prolonged use, and the addition of occlusive dressings.
- **Systemic Effects**: Manifestations of Cushing’s syndrome, hyperglycemia, and glucosuria may occur following systemic absorption.
- **Monitoring**: Patients receiving large doses or using occlusive dressings should be periodically evaluated for HPA axis suppression via urinary-free cortisol and ACTH stimulation tests.
- **Local Irritation**: If skin irritation develops, the medication should be discontinued and appropriate therapy initiated.
- **Concomitant Infections**: In the presence of dermatologic infections, an appropriate antifungal or antibacterial agent must be used. If the infection does not respond promptly, flurandrenolide should be discontinued until the infection is controlled [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following local adverse reactions are reported infrequently but may occur more often with the use of occlusive dressings.
- **Common/Infrequent Local Reactions**:
    - Burning and itching
    - Irritation and dryness
    - Folliculitis
    - Hypertrichosis
    - Acneform eruptions
    - Hypopigmentation
    - Perioral dermatitis
    - Allergic contact dermatitis
- **Reactions Associated with Occlusive Dressings**:
    - Maceration of the skin
    - Secondary infection
    - Skin atrophy
    - Striae
    - Miliaria
- **Postmarketing Reports**: Skin striae, hypersensitivity, skin atrophy, contact dermatitis, and skin discoloration [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions**: Not specified in available sources. 
- **Clinical Note**: Patients should be advised not to use other corticosteroid-containing products concurrently without consulting a physician [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **US Category C**: Corticosteroids are generally teratogenic in laboratory animals at low dosages systemically and following dermal application of potent versions. 
    - There are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Flurandrenolide should be used during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus. It should not be used extensively, in large amounts, or for prolonged periods in pregnant patients [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: It is unknown if topical administration results in sufficient systemic absorption to produce detectable quantities in breast milk. Caution should be exercised when administered to a nursing woman [US].
- **Fertility**: Long-term animal studies to evaluate the effect of topical corticosteroids on fertility have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**:
    - Pediatric patients are at a higher risk of HPA axis suppression and Cushing’s syndrome due to a larger skin surface area-to-body weight ratio.
    - Reported effects include linear growth retardation, delayed weight gain, and intracranial hypertension (manifesting as bulging fontanelles, headaches, and bilateral papilledema).
    - Chronic therapy may interfere with growth and development. Use should be limited to the least amount compatible with an effective therapeutic regimen [US].
- **Elderly Use**: Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment**: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Primarily effective through anti-inflammatory, antipruritic, and vasoconstrictive actions. Corticosteroids may stabilize cellular and lysosomal membranes, preventing the release of proteolytic enzymes.
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Percutaneous absorption is determined by the vehicle, epidermal integrity, and use of occlusive dressings. Inflammation increases absorption.
    - **Metabolism**: Primarily metabolized in the liver once absorbed systemically.
    - **Excretion**: Excreted via the kidneys and bile [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Not specified in available sources.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling**: Keep the container tightly closed and protect from light [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States**: Rx-only (Prescription only).
