# Editorial Product Information: Famotidine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- **Note:** At the time of this editorial preparation, only the Australian PI was available for review. Consequently, jurisdictional comparisons with US, European, UK, Canadian, or Japanese sources are limited to references made within the provided text.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Famotidine (a competitive histamine H2-receptor antagonist).
- **Brand Names:** Ausfam [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A02BA03 (not explicitly in source, but standard clinical classification).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Tablets: 20 mg (tan, square, marked “FO over 20”) and 40 mg (brown, square, marked “FO over 40”) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Duodenal Ulcer:** Treatment of active ulcer and prevention of relapses.
- **Benign Gastric Ulcer:** Treatment of active benign gastric ulceration.
- **Zollinger-Ellison Syndrome:** Management of hypersecretory conditions.
- **Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD):** 
    - Short-term symptomatic relief (up to 12 weeks).
    - Healing of oesophageal erosion or ulceration.
    - Maintenance therapy for the prevention of symptom recurrence and erosions/ulcerations.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Adult Dosing
- **Duodenal Ulcer:** 40 mg once daily at night for 4–8 weeks. 
    - *Maintenance:* 20 mg once daily at night.
- **Benign Gastric Ulcer:** 40 mg once daily at night for 4–8 weeks.
- **Zollinger-Ellison Syndrome:** Initial dose of 20 mg every six hours. Dosage should be titrated to individual patient requirements. Doses up to 800 mg/day have been utilised [Australian].
- **Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD):**
    - *Symptomatic relief:* 20 mg twice daily.
    - *Healing of erosion/ulceration:* 20 mg twice daily.
    - *Maintenance:* 20 mg twice daily (efficacy data available up to 6 months).

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets should be taken orally. 
- For once-daily regimens, administration at night is recommended.

### Renal Adjustment
- **Moderate (CrCl < 50 mL/min) or Severe (CrCl < 10 mL/min) Impairment:** Due to increased elimination half-life (exceeding 20 hours in anuric patients) and associated CNS adverse effects, the dose should be reduced to 50% of the standard dose or the dosing interval prolonged to 36–48 hours [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to famotidine or any excipients.
- Cross-sensitivity within the H2-receptor antagonist class has been observed; famotidine should not be administered to patients with a history of hypersensitivity to other H2-receptor antagonists [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Gastric Neoplasm:** Symptomatic relief does not preclude the presence of gastric malignancy. Malignancy should be excluded prior to treating gastric ulcers.
- **Community-Acquired Pneumonia:** Use of H2-receptor antagonists may be associated with an increased risk of developing community-acquired pneumonia in certain populations (elderly, immunocompromised, or those with chronic lung disease/diabetes) [Australian].
- **Nosocomial Pneumonia:** Agents that increase gastric pH may increase the risk of nosocomial pneumonia in intubated patients in intensive care units.
- **H. pylori Status:** Status should be determined in ulcer patients, and eradication therapy should be initiated where appropriate.
- **Withdrawal:** Abrupt withdrawal after long-term treatment of ulcer disease should be avoided.
- **Monitoring:** Long-term, high-dose therapy warrants monitoring of blood counts and liver function.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Famotidine is generally well tolerated. Adverse effects reported at >1% in clinical trials include headache, dizziness, constipation, and diarrhoea.

| Frequency | System Organ Class | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Common** (>1–10%) | Nervous System; GI | Headache, dizziness; Constipation, diarrhoea |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Nervous System; GI; Skin; General | Taste disorder; Nausea, vomiting, abdominal distension, flatulence, dry mouth; Rash, pruritus, fatigue; Anorexia |
| **Rare** (0.01–0.1%) | Psychiatric; Hepato-biliary; Immune; Musculoskeletal | Hallucinations, confusion, anxiety, agitation, depression; Cholestatic jaundice, hepatitis; Anaphylaxis; Muscle cramps, arthralgia |
| **Very Rare** (<0.01%) | Blood; Psychiatric; Nervous; Cardiac; Respiratory; Skin | Thrombocytopenia, leukopenia, agranulocytosis, pancytopenia; Disorientation, insomnia, reduced libido; Seizures/convulsions (esp. in renal impairment), somnolence, paraesthesia; AV block, palpitations, arrhythmias, **QT prolongation** (esp. in renal impairment); Interstitial pneumonia; Alopecia, **SJS/TEN** |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **pH-Dependent Absorption:** Absorption of ketoconazole, itraconazole, and atazanavir may be reduced. Ketoconazole should be administered 2 hours before famotidine.
- **Probenecid:** Inhibits renal tubular secretion of famotidine, leading to increased plasma levels; concomitant use should be avoided.
- **Antacids/Sucralfate:** May reduce famotidine absorption. Administer famotidine 1–2 hours before antacids.
- **Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitors (TKIs):** May decrease plasma concentrations of dasatinib, erlotinib, gefitinib, and pazopanib. Co-administration is generally not recommended [Australian].
- **Cytochrome P450:** Famotidine does not interact with the CYP450 enzyme system; no significant interactions with warfarin, theophylline, phenytoin, or diazepam were noted.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B1** [Australian]. Not recommended for use unless clearly needed; the potential benefits must outweigh the risks. Animal studies (rats/rabbits) showed no teratogenicity at high doses.
- **Breastfeeding:** Famotidine is detectable in human milk. A decision should be made to either discontinue breastfeeding or discontinue the drug [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in rat models at oral doses up to 2,000 mg/kg.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment is required based on age alone; however, since the elderly are more likely to have decreased renal function, renal monitoring and dose selection care are advised.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dose reduction or interval extension is required for patients with CrCl < 50 mL/min to prevent drug accumulation and CNS toxicity.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Pharmacokinetics do not appear significantly altered in patients with alcohol-related cirrhosis.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Competitive inhibition of histamine at the H2-receptors of gastric parietal cells. It reduces both the acid concentration and the volume of gastric secretion.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Onset of effect occurs within one hour; maximum effect is reached within 1–3 hours. Duration of inhibition is 10–12 hours for doses of 20–40 mg.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - *Absorption:* Incomplete absorption; bioavailability is 40–45%.
    - *Distribution:* 15–20% protein bound.
    - *Metabolism:* Minimal first-pass metabolism; S-oxide is the only identified metabolite in humans.
    - *Elimination:* Renal (65–70%) and metabolic (30–35%) pathways. Elimination half-life is 2.5–5 hours in patients with normal renal function.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **GERD:** In patients with endoscopically verified erosive or ulcerative oesophagitis, famotidine (20 mg and 40 mg twice daily) was superior to placebo. Some studies suggest 40 mg twice daily is more effective for healing than 20 mg twice daily, while others found similar efficacy between the two strengths [Australian].
- **Maintenance:** Treatment with 20 mg twice daily for six months significantly reduced the relapse rate of oesophageal erosions compared to placebo.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store in a dry place below 30°C.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **S4 (Prescription only medicine)** [Australian].
