# Editorial Product Information: Filgrastim

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of filgrastim for healthcare professionals. It is designed to facilitate quick clinical reference by synthesising regulatory data into a coherent structure.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory guidelines for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Filgrastim (recombinant methionyl human granulocyte colony-stimulating factor; r-metHuG-CSF).
- **Chemical Class:** Hematopoietic growth factor; produced by recombinant DNA technology in *Escherichia coli*.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Nivestim** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L03AA02.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
  - Sterile, clear, colourless, preservative-free liquid for parenteral administration [Australian].
  - Pre-filled syringes: 120 mcg in 0.2 mL; 300 mcg in 0.5 mL; 480 mcg in 0.5 mL [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) injection or intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Filgrastim is indicated for the following:
- **Reduction of Febrile Neutropenia:** To decrease the incidence of infection (febrile neutropenia) in patients with non-myeloid malignancies receiving myelosuppressive anti-cancer drugs [Australian].
- **Acute Myeloid Leukaemia (AML):** To reduce the duration of neutropenia and clinical sequelae in patients undergoing induction or consolidation chemotherapy [Australian].
- **Autologous PBPC Mobilisation:** For the mobilisation of autologous peripheral blood progenitor cells (PBPCs) to accelerate neutrophil and platelet recovery after myeloablative or myelosuppressive therapy [Australian].
- **Allogeneic PBPC Mobilisation:** For the mobilisation of PBPCs in normal volunteers for use in allogeneic transplantation [Australian].
- **Bone Marrow Transplantation (BMT):** To reduce the duration of neutropenia and related sequelae following autologous or allogeneic BMT in patients receiving myeloablative chemotherapy [Australian].
- **Severe Chronic Neutropenia (SCN):** Long-term administration to increase neutrophil counts and reduce the incidence/duration of infections in patients with congenital, cyclic, or idiopathic neutropenia [Australian].
- **HIV-Related Neutropenia:** For the reversal of clinically significant neutropenia and subsequent maintenance in patients with HIV infection [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Standard Cytotoxic Chemotherapy/AML:** 5 mcg/kg/day as a single daily SC injection or short IV infusion (15–30 minutes) [Australian].
- **Timing:** Do not administer in the period 24 hours before to 24 hours after cytotoxic chemotherapy [Australian].

### Indication-Specific Dosing
- **BMT or High-Dose Chemotherapy:** 10 mcg/kg/day via continuous SC/IV infusion or 4–24 hour IV infusion. Titrate dose based on absolute neutrophil count (ANC) response [Australian].
- **Autologous PBPC Mobilisation (alone):** 10 mcg/kg/day (SC or continuous infusion) for 4–7 days [Australian].
- **Allogeneic PBPC Mobilisation (normal donors):** 10 mcg/kg/day SC for 4–5 consecutive days [Australian].
- **Severe Chronic Neutropenia (SCN):**
  - **Congenital:** Starting dose 12 mcg/kg/day SC (single or divided doses).
  - **Idiopathic/Cyclic:** Starting dose 5 mcg/kg/day SC [Australian].
- **HIV Infection:** Starting dose 1 mcg/kg/day SC, titrated up to 4 mcg/kg/day until normal ANC is reached; maintenance typically involves 300 mcg/day 1–7 days per week [Australian].

### Administration and Dilution
- **Dilution:** If required, dilute only in **5% glucose**. Filgrastim is **incompatible with saline** (sodium chloride) solutions, which may cause precipitation [Australian].
- **Concentration:** For concentrations <15 mcg/mL, add Human Albumin to a final concentration of 2 mg/mL to prevent adsorption to plastic [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to *E. coli*-derived products, filgrastim, or any component of the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Splenic Rupture:** Rare but potentially fatal cases of splenic rupture have been reported. Monitor for left upper abdominal or shoulder tip pain [Australian].
- **Sickle Cell Crisis:** Severe (sometimes fatal) sickle cell crises have occurred in patients with sickle cell trait or disease [Australian].
- **Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS):** Onset of pulmonary signs (cough, fever, dyspnoea) may be a precursor to ARDS; discontinue immediately if ARDS is suspected [Australian].
- **Glomerulonephritis:** Reported in patients receiving filgrastim; typically resolves upon dose reduction or withdrawal [Australian].
- **Capillary Leak Syndrome:** Characterised by hypotension, hypoalbuminaemia, oedema, and haemoconcentration. Requires urgent symptomatic treatment [Australian].
- **Aortitis:** Reported in healthy donors and cancer patients; symptoms include fever, abdominal pain, and malaise [Australian].
- **MDS/AML Risk:** Monitor breast and lung cancer patients receiving filgrastim with chemo/radiotherapy for signs of Myelodysplastic Syndrome or AML [Australian].
- **Laboratory Monitoring:** Monitor CBC/platelets twice weekly during chemotherapy and regularly during chronic administration [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Summary
| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Musculoskeletal/bone pain (24% in chemo patients; 33% in SCN), pyrexia, anaemia, nausea, vomiting, headache, fatigue [Australian]. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Splenomegaly (30% in SCN), thrombocytopenia, hypertension, cough, oropharyngeal pain, alopecia, constipation, diarrhoea [Australian]. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Splenic rupture, hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis, cutaneous vasculitis, maculopapular rash [Australian]. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Glomerulonephritis, Sweet’s syndrome, aortitis, extramedullary haematopoiesis [Australian]. |

- **Medullary Bone Pain:** The most frequent side effect; usually manageable with non-narcotic analgesics [Australian].
- **Leukocytosis:** WBC counts ≥100 x $10^9$/L observed in 2% of cancer patients; usually transient [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Lithium:** May potentiate the release of neutrophils; use with caution though no formal harmful interaction is identified [Australian].
- **Cytotoxic Chemotherapy:** Do not use 24 hours before or after chemotherapy due to the sensitivity of rapidly dividing myeloid cells [Australian].
- **Bone Imaging:** Increased bone marrow activity may cause transient positive changes in bone scans [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. No adequate studies in pregnant women; animal studies show increased embryonic loss and fusions of sternebrae at high doses. Use only if potential benefit outweighs risk to the foetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety not established; unknown if filgrastim is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No observed effect on fertility in rat studies [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established in SCN (congenital, cyclic, idiopathic). Long-term data show no adverse effect on growth or endocrine function. Use in paediatric AML is limited [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific studies performed; no specific dosage recommendations available [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No specific dose adjustments provided; however, exercise caution in patients with severe organ impairment as thrombocytopenia risk may be increased [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A lineage-specific colony-stimulating factor that binds to specific cell surface receptors on hematopoietic cells, stimulating the proliferation, differentiation, and functional activation of neutrophils [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Peak serum concentrations occur 4–6 hours after SC injection; bioavailability ~50% SC [Australian].
  - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution ~150 mL/kg.
  - **Elimination:** Monoexponential decline; half-life approximately 3 hours (normal volunteers) [Australian].
  - **Clearance:** Clearance is higher in normal volunteers than in cancer patients and may increase as neutrophil counts rise [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Small Cell Lung Cancer (SCLC):** A phase 3 trial showed significant reductions in the incidence, severity, and duration of severe neutropenia (ANC <0.5 x $10^9$/L), as well as reduced hospitalisation and antibiotic use [Australian].
- **AML:** Reduced the median duration of neutropenia from 19 days (placebo) to 14 days (filgrastim) [Australian].
- **SCN:** 88% of patients achieved a complete response (median ANC ≥1.5 x $10^9$/L) within 1–2 weeks [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze) [Australian].
- **Ambulatory Use:** Syringes may be removed from the refrigerator and stored at room temperature (up to 25°C) for a single period of up to 15 days [Australian].
- **Handling:** Keep in the outer carton to protect from light. Avoid vigorous shaking [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only.
