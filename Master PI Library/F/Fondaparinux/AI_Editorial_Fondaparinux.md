# Editorial Product Information: Fondaparinux Sodium

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for educational and professional reference. It does not replace the official PI required for prescribing and dispensing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Fondaparinux sodium, a synthetic and specific inhibitor of activated Factor X (Xa).
- **Brand Name(s):** **ARIXTRA** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Solution for injection in pre-filled automatic safety syringes [Australian]:
    - 2.5 mg in 0.5 mL
    - 5.0 mg in 0.4 mL
    - 7.5 mg in 0.6 mL
    - 10.0 mg in 0.8 mL
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) injection; Intravenous (IV) injection (limited to the first dose in specific Acute Coronary Syndrome indications) [Australian].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aspen Pharmacare Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Venous Thromboembolism (VTE) Prophylaxis:** 
    - Patients undergoing major orthopaedic surgery of the lower limbs (e.g., hip fracture, major knee or hip replacement) [Australian].
    - Patients undergoing abdominal surgery who are at risk of thromboembolic complications [Australian].
- **Treatment of Thromboembolic Events:**
    - Acute deep venous thrombosis (DVT) [Australian].
    - Acute pulmonary embolism (PE) [Australian].
- **Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS):**
    - Treatment of unstable angina (UA) or non-ST segment elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI) in patients where urgent (<120 min) invasive management (PCI) is not indicated [Australian].
    - Treatment of ST segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) in patients managed without initial reperfusion therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **VTE Prophylaxis:** 2.5 mg once daily via SC injection. For surgical patients, the initial dose must be administered no earlier than 6 hours post-operatively, provided haemostasis is established [Australian].
- **Treatment of DVT and PE:** Administered once daily via SC injection based on body weight:
    - < 50 kg: 5 mg
    - 50–100 kg: 7.5 mg
    - > 100 kg: 10 mg
    - Treatment should continue for at least 5 days and until INR is therapeutic (2.0–3.0) with oral anticoagulants [Australian].
- **UA/NSTEMI and STEMI:** 2.5 mg once daily. In STEMI, the first dose is administered **intravenously**; subsequent doses are SC [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Subcutaneous:** Administer in the anterolateral or posterolateral abdominal wall, alternating sides. Do not expel the air bubble from the syringe prior to injection to avoid loss of product. The needle should be inserted fully and perpendicularly into a skin fold held throughout the injection [Australian].
- **Intravenous (STEMI only):** Administer through an existing IV line directly or via a mini-bag (0.9% saline) over 1 to 2 minutes [Australian].

### Missed Dose and Transitions
- **To Heparin/LMWH:** Give the first injection of the new agent 24 hours after the last fondaparinux dose [Australian].
- **To Direct Oral Anticoagulants (DOACs):** Start the DOAC ≤ 2 hours before the next scheduled fondaparinux dose [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Severe renal impairment (Creatinine Clearance [CrCl] < 30 mL/min) [Australian].
- Active clinically significant bleeding [Australian].
- Known hypersensitivity to fondaparinux sodium [Australian].
- Acute bacterial endocarditis [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Haemorrhage:** Use with extreme caution in patients with increased bleeding risk (e.g., congenital bleeding disorders, active GI ulceration, recent intracranial haemorrhage, or shortly after brain/spinal/ophthalmic surgery) [Australian].
- **PCI and Catheter Thrombus:** In patients undergoing PCI, fondaparinux is **not recommended** as the sole anticoagulant during the procedure due to an increased risk of guiding catheter thrombosis. Adjunctive unfractionated heparin (UFH) should be used [Australian].
- **Spinal/Epidural Anaesthesia:** Risk of spinal or epidural haematomas which may result in permanent paralysis. Risk increases with indwelling catheters, concomitant NSAIDs, or traumatic/repeated punctures [Australian].
- **Latex Allergy:** The needle shield may contain dry natural latex rubber [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Platelet counts should be monitored at baseline and at the conclusion of therapy [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by frequency:

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common** ($\ge$1% to <10%) | Anaemia, post-operative haemorrhage, nausea, vomiting, oedema, fever, rash, constipation, insomnia, urinary tract infection, headache [Australian]. |
| **Uncommon** ($\ge$0.1% to <1%) | Thrombocytopenia, thrombocythaemia, abnormal platelets, coagulation disorder, dizziness, hypotension, dyspnoea, pruritus, hepatic enzyme increase [Australian]. |
| **Rare** (<0.1%) | Allergic reactions (including angioedema/anaphylaxis), somnolence, vertigo, fatigue, flushing, syncope, gastritis, bilirubinemia [Australian]. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Major bleeding (including intracranial and retroperitoneal), spinal haematoma, and catheter-related thrombosis in the context of PCI [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic:** Concomitant use with agents that enhance bleeding risk (e.g., antiplatelet agents, NSAIDs, other anticoagulants) should be avoided unless essential; if used, close clinical monitoring is required [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetic:** Fondaparinux does not inhibit CYP450 enzymes (1A2, 2A6, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 2E1, or 3A4) in vitro and does not significantly bind to plasma proteins other than ATIII. No significant interactions were observed with warfarin, aspirin, piroxicam, or digoxin [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Anticoagulants may cause placental haemorrhage and foetal loss. Fondaparinux should only be used if the potential benefit justifies the risk, as clinical experience is limited.
- **Breastfeeding:** Excreted in rat milk; human excretion is unknown. Breastfeeding is **not recommended** during treatment [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No human data available. Animal studies (rats) showed no effect on fertility at doses resulting in twice the human exposure [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients under 17 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use ($\ge$ 75 years):** Increased risk of bleeding due to age-related decline in renal function. Clearance is 1.2 to 1.4 times lower than in younger patients [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Mild (CrCl 50–80 mL/min):** No dose adjustment usually required, but use with caution [Australian].
    - **Moderate (CrCl 30–50 mL/min):** Use with caution [Australian].
    - **Severe (CrCl < 30 mL/min):** **Contraindicated** [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment required in mild to moderate impairment. Use with caution in severe impairment due to the potential deficiency of coagulation factors [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Fondaparinux is a synthetic pentasaccharide that binds selectively to Antithrombin III (ATIII). This binding potentiates (approx. 300 times) the innate neutralisation of Factor Xa by ATIII, interrupting the coagulation cascade and inhibiting thrombin formation [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** It does not inactivate thrombin (Factor IIa) and has no effect on platelet function. It does not typically affect routine tests like aPTT, PT/INR, or bleeding time at therapeutic doses [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** 100% bioavailable after SC injection; $T_{max}$ is 2 hours [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Limited to 7–11 L (consistent with blood volume); $\ge$97% bound to ATIII [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** No evidence of metabolism [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Excreted unchanged in urine. $T_{1/2}$ is approximately 17 hours in healthy young subjects and 20 hours in the elderly [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **VTE Prophylaxis:** Superiority over enoxaparin was demonstrated in major orthopaedic trials (PENTHIFRA, PENTAMAKS, EPHESUS) with significant reductions in VTE rates. Non-inferiority to dalteparin was shown in abdominal surgery (PEGASUS) [Australian].
- **DVT/PE Treatment:** Demonstrated comparable efficacy and safety to enoxaparin (DVT) and UFH (PE) [Australian].
- **ACS:** The OASIS 5 (UA/NSTEMI) and OASIS 6 (STEMI) trials demonstrated efficacy in reducing death and MI, particularly in patients not undergoing primary PCI. A notable reduction in major bleeding was observed compared to enoxaparin in UA/NSTEMI [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C [Australian].
- If added to a 0.9% saline mini-bag, it is chemically stable for up to 6 hours at 25°C [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (S4)** (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
