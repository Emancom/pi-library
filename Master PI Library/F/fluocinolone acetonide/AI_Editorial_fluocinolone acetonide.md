## 3. Approved Indications

- No other indications or jurisdictional variations are specified in the available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General dosing (adult)
- Single intravitreal administration of an implant containing 0.18 mg fluocinolone acetonide. The implant is designed for sustained release at an initial rate of approximately 0.25 mcg/day and to provide drug release for up to 36 months.

### Dosing by indication
- Chronic non‑infectious posterior segment uveitis: single intravitreal implantation as described above. No alternative dosing schedules, repeat dosing recommendations, or titration regimens are specified in the available sources.

### Administration instructions (procedure and precautions)
- The intravitreal injection procedure must be performed under aseptic conditions and by clinicians trained in intravitreal implantation techniques. Recommended aseptic measures include sterile gloves, sterile drape, sterile calipers, and a sterile eyelid speculum (or equivalent).
- Provide adequate local anesthesia (topical and/or subconjunctival) at the injection site; the inferotemporal quadrant is recommended as the injection site.
- Administer a broad‑spectrum topical microbicide (2–3 drops into the lower fornix) before the procedure; eyelid margins may be scrubbed with microbicide‑soaked applicators. Allow 30–60 seconds for the antiseptic to dry prior to injection.
- Measure ~4 mm inferotemporally from the limbus for the scleral entry point (optimal implant placement is inferior to the optic disc and posterior to the equator).
- Handle the preloaded sterile applicator by the barrel only; do not grasp the plunger. Remove the plunger stop and protective needle cap just prior to use. Keep the applicator tip above the horizontal plane prior to insertion to prevent premature loss of the implant from the applicator.
- Displace the conjunctiva gently so the conjunctival and scleral entry sites do not line up after needle withdrawal.
- Insert the needle through conjunctiva and sclera up to the positive stop of the applicator, then fully depress the plunger to deliver the implant into the vitreous cavity.
- Withdraw and dispose of the applicator in a sharps container. Remove the lid speculum and perform indirect ophthalmoscopy to confirm central retinal artery perfusion, absence of acute complications, and placement of the implant. Scleral depression may assist visualization.
- Immediate measurement of intraocular pressure (IOP) may be performed at the clinician’s discretion.

### Post‑procedure monitoring and patient instructions
- Monitor patients for elevation in IOP and for signs of endophthalmitis following the injection.
- Suggested monitoring: check optic nerve head perfusion immediately after injection, consider tonometry within 30 minutes post‑injection, and perform slit lamp/biomicroscopic examination between 2 and 7 days after the procedure.
- Instruct patients to report immediately any eye redness, photophobia, eye pain, or changes in vision.
- Advise patients that temporary visual blurring may occur after intravitreal injection and to avoid driving or operating machinery until vision stabilizes.

### Titration / missed dose
- No titration schedules or missed dose instructions are specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Active ocular or periocular infections, including but not limited to:
  - Active epithelial herpes simplex keratitis (dendritic keratitis)
  - Vaccinia
  - Varicella
  - Mycobacterial infections
  - Fungal ocular infections
- Known hypersensitivity to fluocinolone acetonide or any component of the product.
- No additional jurisdiction‑specific contraindications are provided in the available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed warnings
- No boxed (black box) warnings are specified in the available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Intravitreal injection‑related effects: Intravitreal administration has been associated with endophthalmitis, intraocular inflammation, increases or decreases in intraocular pressure, choroidal or retinal detachments, and globe perforation where there is thinning of the cornea or sclera. Hypotony has been observed within 24 hours post‑injection and typically resolved within 2 weeks.
- Steroid‑related effects: Use of corticosteroids such as fluocinolone acetonide may cause or accelerate posterior subcapsular cataract formation, increase IOP and glaucoma risk, and may predispose to secondary ocular infections from bacteria, fungi, or viruses. Corticosteroids are generally not recommended in patients with a history of ocular herpes simplex due to potential reactivation.
- Implant migration: Patients with an absent or ruptured posterior lens capsule (e.g., aphakia, posterior capsular tear) are at risk for anterior chamber migration of the implant; migration may cause corneal edema or injury.
- Surgical interventions: Some patients may require medical or surgical management of steroid‑induced IOP elevation.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor intraocular pressure periodically following administration; in trials, tonometry within 30 minutes after injection and biomicroscopy between 2 and 7 days were suggested monitoring points.
- Monitor for signs and symptoms of endophthalmitis and instruct patients to seek urgent ophthalmic evaluation for redness, pain, photophobia, or vision changes.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Use with caution (or avoid) in patients with ocular infections or known hypersensitivity to product components.
- Consider risk of cataract development and counsel phakic patients accordingly regarding potential for cataract surgery.
- Be aware of possible need for IOP‑lowering medications and, rarely, surgical intervention for IOP control.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

### Pregnancy
- Adequate and well‑controlled studies of intravitreal fluocinolone acetonide implant in pregnant women have not been conducted.
- Animal reproduction studies specific to the implant were not conducted per the available source; systemic corticosteroids have shown teratogenic effects in animal studies when administered systemically at relatively low doses.
- Recommendation: Use in pregnancy only if the potential maternal benefit justifies potential risk to the fetus.
- Pregnancy risk category / classification: Not specified in available sources (no jurisdictional category provided).

### Breastfeeding
- Systemically administered corticosteroids are present in human milk and can suppress infant growth and interfere with endogenous corticosteroid production.
- There are no clinical or nonclinical lactation studies for the implant; it is unknown whether intravitreal administration results in sufficient systemic exposure to produce measurable levels in human milk or to affect a breastfed infant.
- Clinical decision should weigh benefits of breastfeeding, maternal need for the implant, and potential risk to the infant.

### Fertility
- Long‑term animal studies to evaluate effects of the implant on fertility have not been conducted; the systemic fertility impact is not specified in the available source.

## 11. Pharmacology

### Mechanism of action
- Corticosteroids inhibit multiple components of the inflammatory cascade including inflammatory cytokine production, edema, fibrin deposition, capillary dilation, leukocyte migration, capillary proliferation, fibroblast proliferation, collagen deposition, and scar formation. The anti‑inflammatory effect is postulated to arise from inhibition of phospholipase A2 through induction of inhibitory proteins (lipocortins), reducing arachidonic acid release and downstream prostaglandin and leukotriene synthesis.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Formulation design: The intravitreal implant contains 0.18 mg fluocinolone acetonide and is designed for sustained ocular release at an initial rate of approximately 0.25 mcg/day with drug release lasting up to 36 months.
- Physicochemical properties: fluocinolone acetonide is a microcrystalline powder, practically insoluble in water, soluble in methanol, ethanol, chloroform and acetone; molecular weight ~452.50 g/mol (molecular formula details are provided in source).
- Systemic absorption, distribution, metabolism, and elimination following intravitreal implantation: Not fully described in the available sources (systemic PK parameters not specified).

### Pharmacodynamics
- Clinical pharmacodynamic effects include reduction of ocular inflammation associated with non‑infectious posterior segment uveitis and potential effects on intraocular pressure and cataract formation as steroid class effects.

## 12. Clinical Trials

### Pivotal trials (summary)
- Two randomized, double‑masked, multicenter, parallel‑group trials evaluated the efficacy of a single intravitreal fluocinolone acetonide implant compared with sham injection in patients with non‑infectious posterior segment uveitis (study identifiers reported in source: NCT #01694186 and NCT #02746991).
- Study design: Randomized 2:1 (implant:sham), masked trials; patients received a single implant or sham injection and were followed with standard care. Primary endpoint was proportion of study eyes with recurrence of uveitis within 6 months; recurrence within 12 months was also assessed. Recurrence was defined as deterioration in visual acuity, vitreous haze attributable to non‑infectious uveitis, or need for rescue medications.

### Key efficacy results (recurrence rates)
- Study 1 (YUTIQ‑type implant vs sham): 
  - 6‑month recurrence: 16/87 YUTIQ eyes (18%) vs 33/42 sham eyes (79%); absolute difference ~60% (95% CI 41%, 73%); p < 0.01.
  - 12‑month recurrence: 24/87 YUTIQ eyes (28%) vs 36/42 sham eyes (86%).
- Study 2:
  - 6‑month recurrence: 22/101 YUTIQ eyes (22%) vs 28/52 sham eyes (54%); absolute difference ~32% (95% CI 15%, 48%); p < 0.01.
  - 12‑month recurrence: 33/101 YUTIQ eyes (33%) vs 31/52 sham eyes (60%).
- Additional study: a multicenter randomized masked trial compared two applicators for the implant; all patients received the implant and then standard care for the study duration (Study 3).
- Clinical interpretation: Single intravitreal implantation of fluocinolone acetonide significantly reduced the proportion of eyes experiencing uveitis recurrence at 6 months and 12 months compared with sham implants in the randomized trials cited.

### Other clinical observations
- In controlled studies, commonly observed adverse outcomes included cataract progression and IOP elevation requiring medical or occasionally surgical intervention.
