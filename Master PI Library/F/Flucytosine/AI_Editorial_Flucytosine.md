# Editorial Product Information: Flucytosine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial product information was developed using official Product Information from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of flucytosine prescribing information for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial summary and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for legal and regulatory compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Flucytosine (5-fluorocytosine; 5-FC), a fluorinated pyrimidine antifungal.
- **Brand Names:** Ancotil [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J02AX01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Intravenous Infusion:** 2.5 g/250 mL (1% w/v) isotonic solution [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** iNova Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Flucytosine is indicated for the treatment of the following systemic fungal infections [Australian]:
- Generalised candidiasis
- Cryptococcosis
- Chromoblastomycosis

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Standard Dose:** 37.5 mg/kg to 50 mg/kg administered via short-term intravenous infusion (20 to 40 minutes) [Australian].
- **Frequency:** Every six hours in patients with normal renal function [Australian].

### Renal Impairment Adjustments
Dosing intervals must be extended based on creatinine clearance (CrCl) to avoid toxicity [Australian]:

| Creatinine Clearance | Dosing Interval (for 50 mg/kg dose) |
| :--- | :--- |
| >40 mL/min | 6 hours |
| 40 – 20 mL/min | 12 hours |
| 20 – 10 mL/min | 24 hours |
| <10 mL/min | Maintain serum concentrations between 25 and 50 µg/mL; determine level 12 hours after the initial dose. |

### Administration Instructions
- Administer separately from amphotericin B infusions [Australian].
- Continuous monitoring of serum concentrations is recommended, especially in renal impairment.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to flucytosine or any excipients [Australian].
- Known complete deficiency of dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase (DPD) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **DPD Deficiency:** 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) is a metabolite of flucytosine. Patients with DPD deficiency are at a significantly increased risk of severe drug toxicity. Consideration should be given to DPD activity testing if toxicity is suspected [Australian].
- **Renal Function:** Renal status must be monitored before and during treatment (preferably via creatinine clearance). Dose adjustment is mandatory in impairment. Do not use flucytosine in renal impairment if blood level monitoring is unavailable [Australian].
- **Haematological/Hepatic Monitoring:** Extreme caution is required in patients with bone marrow depression or blood dyscrasias. The blood picture (CBC), renal, and liver function should be monitored daily at treatment initiation and twice weekly thereafter [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
At recommended dosages, flucytosine is generally well-tolerated, though serious toxicities can occur [Australian].

### Common/Reported Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea. Gastrointestinal bleeding has been observed, particularly when combined with amphotericin B or corticosteroids.
- **Dermatological:** Skin rashes.
- **Neurological:** Headache, sedation, vertigo, convulsions.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Haematological:** Leucopenia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, agranulocytosis, and aplastic anaemia. Bone marrow toxicity may be irreversible and fatal in immunosuppressed patients.
- **Hepatic:** Elevated serum hepatic enzymes, hepatitis, and hepatic necrosis. Fatal acute liver injury may occur in debilitated patients.
- **Cardiac:** Myocardial toxicity and ventricular dysfunction.
- **Other:** Lyell’s syndrome (Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Cytosine Arabinoside (Cytarabine):** The antimycotic activity of flucytosine is inhibited by cytarabine. Evidence is contradictory; strict blood level monitoring is required during concurrent use [Australian].
- **Phenytoin:** Concomitant administration with flucytosine (via its 5-FU metabolite) may increase phenytoin plasma levels, potentially leading to phenytoin toxicity [Australian].
- **Drugs Inhibiting Glomerular Filtration:** Since flucytosine is excreted renally, drugs that reduce GFR will prolong its half-life, necessitating frequent CrCl monitoring and dose adjustment [Australian].
- **Cytostatics:** Concurrent use increases the risk of leucopenia (specifically neutropenia); daily blood monitoring is required [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B3.** Teratogenic effects have been observed in rats. Safety in human pregnancy is not established. Use is not recommended unless the potential benefit justifies the risk to the fetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if flucytosine is excreted in human milk. Use in nursing mothers is not recommended unless the benefit outweighs potential risk [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available in source documents [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No data provided in source [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data provided in source; however, renal monitoring is critical given age-related GFR decline [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Requires mandatory dose interval extension (see Section 3) and frequent monitoring of serum levels to prevent accumulation [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution; monitor liver function regularly [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Flucytosine is taken up by fungal cells and deaminated by cytosine deaminase into 5-fluorouracil (5-FU). This metabolite is incorporated into fungal RNA, inhibiting protein synthesis, and also interferes with DNA synthesis. It is fungistatic and can be fungicidal upon prolonged contact [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is approximately 0.78 ± 0.13 L/kg. Protein binding is negligible. It achieves high concentrations in CSF and peritoneal fluid (approx. 75% of serum levels) [Australian].
- **Elimination:** Excreted almost exclusively via the kidneys as unchanged drug. Half-life is 3 to 6 hours in adults with normal renal function and 6 to 7 hours in premature infants [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Optimal serum concentrations should be 20–25 µg/mL (minimum) and should not exceed 100–120 µg/mL to minimize toxicity [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Precipitation:** If stored below 15°C, a precipitate may form. This can be redissolved by heating the solution to no more than 80°C for a maximum of 30 minutes [Australian].
- **Degradation:** Storage above 25°C may lead to the formation of 5-fluorouracil (cytostatic degradation product) which is not visible to the naked eye [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** 18 months [Australian].
