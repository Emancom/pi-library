# Editorial Product Information: Fruquintinib (FRUZAQLA)

**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been compiled by integrating clinical data and regulatory guidance from the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). Where recommendations or safety specifications differ between agencies, the most comprehensive or conservative information has been selected and annotated accordingly.

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** FRUZAQLA  
**Active Ingredient:** Fruquintinib  
**Formulation:** Oral capsules (1 mg and 5 mg)

---

## 2. Approved Indications
FRUZAQLA is indicated for the treatment of adult patients with metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC). Eligible patients must have received prior treatment with:
*   Fluoropyrimidine-, oxaliplatin-, and irinotecan-based chemotherapy.
*   An anti-VEGF biological therapy.
*   An anti-EGFR biological therapy (specifically if the tumor is RAS wild-type and such therapy is considered medically appropriate).

---

## 3. Contraindications
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to the active substance or any of the excipients (e.g., tartrazine) listed in the formulation [TGA].
*   The FDA label currently lists no specific contraindications [FDA].

---

## 4. Warnings and Precautions

### Cardiovascular and Vascular Events
*   **Hypertension:** Blood pressure elevation is very common (approx. 49%). Hypertension should be controlled prior to initiation. Monitoring should occur weekly during the first month and at least monthly thereafter. 
*   **Arterial Thromboembolism:** Events such as myocardial infarction or stroke have been reported. Use caution in patients with a recent history of thromboembolic events; discontinue therapy if an arterial thromboembolism occurs.
*   **Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES):** This rare neurological condition has been observed. If a patient presents with seizures, headache, or visual disturbances, confirm via MRI and discontinue treatment immediately.

### Hemorrhagic and Gastrointestinal Risks
*   **Hemorrhage:** Serious and sometimes fatal bleeding events can occur. Patients on anticoagulants require more frequent monitoring of INR/coagulation profiles.
*   **Gastrointestinal (GI) Perforation:** Treatment may lead to GI perforation or fistula. Patients should be monitored for inflammatory symptoms, and therapy must be permanently discontinued if perforation occurs.

### Organ Toxicity
*   **Hepatotoxicity:** Liver function test (LFT) abnormalities are frequent. Monitor ALT, AST, and bilirubin before and during treatment. Significant elevations require dose interruption, reduction, or permanent cessation.
*   **Proteinuria:** Frequent monitoring for urine protein is required. Discontinue therapy if the patient develops nephrotic syndrome.

### General Safety
*   **Infections:** There is a heightened risk of various infections (most commonly urinary and respiratory tracts), including fatal cases. Withhold therapy during active, severe infections.
*   **Wound Healing:** As a VEGF inhibitor, FRUZAQLA can impair wound healing. Withhold the drug for at least 2 weeks before major surgery and for at least 2 weeks afterward, resuming only when the wound is fully healed.
*   **Allergic Reactions (Excipients):** The 1 mg capsules contain FD&C Yellow No. 5 (tartrazine) and No. 6 (sunset yellow FCF), which may trigger allergic-type reactions, particularly in patients with aspirin sensitivity [FDA].

## 5. Dosage and Administration

### Recommended Posology
The standard dose is **5 mg taken orally once daily**.  
*   **Schedule:** 21 consecutive days of treatment followed by a 7-day rest period (a 28-day cycle).
*   **Administration:** Capsules should be swallowed whole with or without food, ideally at the same time each day.

### Missed Doses and Vomiting
*   **Missed Dose:** If the delay is less than 12 hours, the dose should be taken immediately. If more than 12 hours have passed, skip the dose.
*   **Vomiting:** If a patient vomits after administration, do not take an additional dose that day; resume the schedule the following day.

### Dose Modifications
If adverse reactions occur, the following reduction tiers are recommended:
1.  **First reduction:** 4 mg daily (three weeks on/one week off).
2.  **Second reduction:** 3 mg daily (three weeks on/one week off).
3.  **Third level:** Permanently discontinue treatment.

Specific modification criteria (withholding vs. discontinuation) are based on the Grade of toxicity for hypertension, hemorrhage, LFT abnormalities, and proteinuria.

---

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most common adverse reactions (incidence ≥20%) identified in clinical trials include:
*   Hypertension
*   Palmar-plantar erythrodysesthesia (PPE/Hand-foot syndrome)
*   Proteinuria
*   Dysphonia (voice changes)
*   Abdominal pain and diarrhea
*   Asthenia or fatigue

Serious adverse reactions occurred in approximately 38% of patients, with hemorrhage and GI perforation being the most clinically significant. Laboratory abnormalities often include increased triglycerides, increased LFTs, and decreased magnesium or sodium levels.

---

## 7. Drug Interactions

### Effects of Other Drugs on FRUZAQLA
*   **Strong CYP3A Inducers:** Avoid concomitant use (e.g., rifampicin), as these significantly reduce fruquintinib exposure (AUC decreased by ~65%), potentially reducing efficacy.
*   **Moderate CYP3A Inducers:** Avoid if possible. If necessary, continue the standard 5 mg dose, but be aware of a predicted 32% decrease in AUC.
*   **CYP3A Inhibitors / Gastric Acid Agents:** No clinically significant changes were observed with strong inhibitors (e.g., itraconazole) or proton pump inhibitors (e.g., rabeprazole).

### Effects of FRUZAQLA on Other Drugs
*   Fruquintinib may inhibit P-gp and BCRP in the gut. However, clinical studies with substrates like dabigatran or rosuvastatin showed no meaningful changes requiring dose adjustments.

---

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

### Pregnancy
*   **Category D [TGA]:** Based on animal data and its mechanism of action, FRUZAQLA can cause fetal harm and malformations (teratogenicity).
*   **Contraception:** Females of reproductive potential, and males with partners of reproductive potential, must use highly effective contraception during treatment and for at least **2 weeks** after the final dose.

### Breastfeeding
It is unknown if the drug passes into human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in the infant, breastfeeding should be discontinued during treatment and for **2 weeks** after the final dose.

---

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Reaches peak plasma concentration (Tmax) in approximately 2 hours. Steady-state is achieved after 14 days with a 4-fold accumulation.
*   **Distribution:** High plasma protein binding (~95%) with an apparent volume of distribution of 46 L.
*   **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized via CYP3A4, with minor contributions from CYP2C8, 2C9, and 2C19.
*   **Excretion:** Mean elimination half-life is roughly 42 hours. Approximately 60% of the dose is recovered in urine and 30% in feces.

---

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Fruquintinib is a highly selective, small-molecule tyrosine kinase inhibitor. It targets Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor Receptors (VEGFR) -1, -2, and -3. By inhibiting these receptors, the drug blocks VEGF-induced endothelial cell proliferation and tubule formation, thereby suppressing tumor angiogenesis and growth.

---

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store between 20°C and 25°C [FDA]. (TGA guidance allows for storage below 30°C). 
*   **Moisture Protection:** Keep the bottle tightly closed and do not remove the desiccant cartridge. 
*   **Packaging:** High-density polyethylene (HDPE) bottles with child-resistant closures.
