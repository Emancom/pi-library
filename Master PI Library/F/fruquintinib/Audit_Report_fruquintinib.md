# Originality Audit Report: fruquintinib

## Audit Summary
- **Originality Score:** 62/100
- **Verbatim Copied Passages Found:** 5
- **Overall Assessment:** Concern

## Verbatim Copied Passages
1.  **Approved Indications:** 
    - **Editorial:** "indicated for the treatment of adult patients with metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC). Eligible patients must have received prior treatment with: Fluoropyrimidine-, oxaliplatin-, and irinotecan-based chemotherapy."
    - **Source:** Document 1 (TGA), Section 4.1: "FRUZAQLA is indicated for the treatment of adult patients with metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC) who have been previously treated with fluoropyrimidine-, oxaliplatin-, and irinotecan-based chemotherapy..."
    - **Location:** Section 2.

2.  **Adverse Reactions List:** 
    - **Editorial:** "Hypertension, Palmar-plantar erythrodysesthesia... Proteinuria, Dysphonia (voice changes), Abdominal pain and diarrhea, Asthenia or fatigue."
    - **Source:** Document 2 (FDA), Highlights - Adverse Reactions: "Most common adverse reactions (incidence ≥20%) are hypertension, palmar-plantar erythrodysesthesia, proteinuria, dysphonia, abdominal pain, diarrhea, and asthenia."
    - **Location:** Section 6.

3.  **Vomiting Instructions:** 
    - **Editorial:** "If a patient vomits after administration, do not take an additional dose that day; resume the schedule the following day."
    - **Source:** Document 1 (TGA), Section 4.2: "If a patient vomits after taking a dose, the patient should not repeat the dose on the same day but resume the usual dosing as scheduled on the following day."
    - **Location:** Section 5.

4.  **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Editorial:** "blocks VEGF-induced endothelial cell proliferation and tubule formation, thereby suppressing tumor angiogenesis and growth."
    - **Source:** Document 1 (TGA), Section 5.1: "VEGF-mediated endothelial cell proliferation, and tubular formation were inhibited by fruquintinib in vitro... antitumor effects resulting from suppression of tumour angiogenesis."
    - **Location:** Section 10.

5.  **Storage/Desiccant:** 
    - **Editorial:** "Keep the bottle tightly closed and do not remove the desiccant cartridge."
    - **Source:** Document 1 (TGA), Section 6.4: "Keep bottle tightly closed. Do not remove desiccant cartridge from the bottle."
    - **Location:** Section 11.

## Close Paraphrases
- **Hypertension Monitoring:** The editorial states "Monitoring should occur weekly during the first month and at least monthly thereafter." This is a minor simplification of the TGA/FDA text: "Monitor blood pressure weekly the first month, at least monthly thereafter and as clinically indicated."
- **Wound Healing:** The instructions regarding the 2-week withholding period before and after surgery are identical in substance and nearly identical in structure to Section 5.9 of the FDA label.
- **Drug Interactions (CYP3A4):** The specific percentages (65% reduction for strong inducers, 32% for moderate) and the recommendation to "avoid" are taken directly from the "Effect of other medicinal products" sections in both PIs.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** The numerical values for Volume of Distribution (46 L), Half-life (42 hours), and Excretion (60% urine / 30% feces) are copied directly from the TGA Section 5.2.

## Originality Strengths
- **Introduction:** The "Editorial Note" successfully frames the document as a unified integration of two agencies, which is original framing.
- **Section 3 (Contraindications):** The editorial effectively synthesizes the fact that the TGA lists excipient hypersensitivity while the FDA lists "None," creating a unified safety view.
- **Section 11 (Storage Conditions):** The writer correctly identifies and annotates the difference in temperature guidance (20-25°C vs 30°C) between the two agencies, which is an original comparative synthesis.

## Differences from Source PIs
- **Harmonization:** The editorial converts the TGA's "haemorrhage" and "faeces" to US English "hemorrhage" and "feces" to maintain internal consistency, except where specifically quoting TGA annotations.
- **Formatting:** Uses a streamlined bulleted structure for warnings and dose modifications, whereas the sources use dense paragraphs or complex tables.
- **Excipient Synthesis:** Combines the TGA’s mention of tartrazine in the contraindications with the FDA’s specific clinical warning about FD&C Yellow No. 5/6 and aspirin sensitivity.

## Recommendation
**REVISION NEEDED**

While the editorial performs an excellent job of *regulatory harmonization* (identifying and merging differences between TGA and FDA), it relies too heavily on the technical prose of the source documents. 

**Required Changes:**
1.  **Reword Section 6 (Adverse Effects):** The list is currently a verbatim copy of the FDA Highlights section. Rephrase the descriptions (e.g., "Voice changes" instead of just "Dysphonia").
2.  **Reword Section 5 (Vomiting/Missed Doses):** These sentences are nearly identical to the TGA PI. Use more distinct instructional language.
3.  **Synthesize Section 10 (Mechanism of Action):** Move away from the "endothelial cell proliferation and tubule formation" phrasing which is used identically in both source PIs. Describe the process of anti-angiogenesis in more original prose.
4.  **Pharmacokinetics:** Instead of listing the numbers in the exact order found in the TGA "Excretion" and "Distribution" sections, integrate them into a more narrative summary.
