# Editorial Product Information: Fludarabine Phosphate

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of fludarabine phosphate (oral formulation) for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved PI for definitive prescribing guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Fludarabine phosphate.
- **Chemical Class:** Fluorinated nucleotide analogue of the antiviral agent vidarabine (9-β-D-arabinofuranosyladenine).
- **Brand Names:** Fludara Oral [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01B B05.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 10 mg film-coated tablets (salmon-coloured, oval-shaped, indented with "LN" in a hexagon) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** sanofi-aventis australia pty ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **B-cell Chronic Lymphocytic Leukaemia (CLL):** Indicated for the treatment of patients with B-cell CLL [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Prescribing Requirement:** Must be prescribed by a physician experienced in antineoplastic therapy.
- **General Adult Dose:** 40 mg/m² body surface area administered once daily for 5 consecutive days every 28 days.
- **Duration of Therapy:** Treatment should continue until the best response is achieved (complete or partial remission, typically 6 cycles) and then discontinued.
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Tablets must be swallowed whole with water.
    - Do not chew, break, or crush tablets.
    - May be taken on an empty stomach or with food [Australian].
- **Dose Modifications:** Dosage should be delayed or decreased in the event of haematological or non-haematological toxicity.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to fludarabine phosphate or any excipients (e.g., lactose).
- Renal impairment with creatinine clearance (CrCl) < 30 mL/min.
- Decompensated haemolytic anaemia.
- Pregnancy and lactation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Neurotoxicity:**
    - **High-Dose Risks:** Severe neurologic effects, including blindness, coma, and death, were observed in dose-ranging studies at ~4 times the recommended CLL dose (96 mg/m²/day).
    - **Standard Dose Risks:** Confusion is uncommon; seizures, agitation, and coma are rare at recommended doses. Latency for neurotoxicity ranges from 7 to 225 days.
    - **Leukoencephalopathy (LE/ATL/RPLS):** Fludarabine is associated with leukoencephalopathy, acute toxic leukoencephalopathy, and reversible posterior leukoencephalopathy syndrome. Symptoms include headache, seizures, visual loss, and altered sensorium. If suspected, discontinue permanently and perform MRI [Australian].
- **Myelosuppression:** Severe anaemia, thrombocytopenia, and neutropenia are common. Cumulative myelosuppression can occur. Periodic blood counts are mandatory.
- **Autoimmune Phenomena:** Life-threatening autoimmune disorders (e.g., autoimmune haemolytic anaemia, ITP, Evans’ syndrome, pemphigus) may occur regardless of prior history or Coombs status.
- **Tumour Lysis Syndrome (TLS):** Reported in patients with high tumour burdens; response can begin as early as the first week of treatment.
- **Infections:** Increased risk of opportunistic infections (e.g., *Pneumocystis*, fungi, herpes zoster, EBV, PML).
- **Vaccination:** Avoid live vaccines during and after treatment.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pentostatin (Deoxycoformycin):** Use in combination is **not recommended** due to an unacceptably high incidence of fatal pulmonary toxicity [Australian].
- **Dipyridamole:** May reduce therapeutic efficacy by inhibiting adenosine uptake.
- **Cytarabine (Ara-C):** Pharmacokinetic interaction observed; fludarabine increases intracellular Ara-CTP levels.
- **Food Interaction:** No significant effect on pharmacokinetic parameters after oral administration.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category D [Australian]:** Evidence of human foetal risk. Fludarabine is genotoxic and teratogenic in animal studies. 
    - **Recommendations:** Must not be used during pregnancy. Females of childbearing potential and fertile males must use effective contraception during and for at least 6 months after treatment.
- **Breastfeeding:** Discontinue breastfeeding during therapy. It is unknown if fludarabine is excreted in human milk, but animal data suggests transfer into milk.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies show testicular degeneration and necrosis. Potential for adverse effects on human fertility has not been adequately evaluated.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for children < 18 years due to lack of safety/efficacy data [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Limited data for patients > 75 years; exercise caution.
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **CrCl 30–70 mL/min:** Reduce dose (e.g., by 50%) and monitor haematological toxicity closely.
    - **CrCl < 30 mL/min:** Contraindicated [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No data available. Use with caution if the potential benefit outweighs the risk.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A prodrug that is rapidly dephosphorylated to 2F-ara-A, then phosphorylated intracellularly to the active triphosphate 2F-ara-ATP. It inhibits ribonucleotide reductase, DNA polymerase, primase, and ligase, thereby inhibiting DNA synthesis. It also partially inhibits RNA polymerase II and induces apoptosis.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Oral bioavailability is 50–65%. Tmax occurs 1–2 hours post-dose.
    - **Distribution:** No pronounced protein binding.
    - **Metabolism:** Intracellular activation; 2F-ara-A is the primary systemic metabolite.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (40–60% of IV dose excreted in urine). Terminal half-life is approximately 20 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Oral Formulation Study:** An uncontrolled trial in 81 treatment-naive B-cell CLL patients (40 mg/m² for 5 days) showed an overall response rate of 80% and a complete response rate of 12%.
- **IV Comparisons:** Three randomised trials (using 25 mg/m² IV) demonstrated superior progression-free survival compared to chlorambucil and CAP (cyclophosphamide, adriamycin, prednisone) regimens in first-line CLL [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Packaging:** Al blister packs of 20 tablets.
- **Handling:** As a potent antineoplastic, unused medicine or waste must be disposed of according to local hazardous material protocols [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
