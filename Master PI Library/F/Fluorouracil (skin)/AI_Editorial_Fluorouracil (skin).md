# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdictions: 
    - **United States (FDA)**: Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation)
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. The clinician should refer to the local approved PI for definitive legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine [(R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt].
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Intravenous injection: 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL).
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Metabolic Errors:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency.
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing maintenance dialysis.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion. 
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). 
    - Administration should occur as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - Therapy may be initiated if trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations are below normal (40 µmol/L to 50 µmol/L).
    - **Dose Adjustments:** Should be guided by trough plasma concentrations; downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be performed as early as the third or fourth week of treatment.

### Administration and Monitoring
- **Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended.
- **Preparation:** Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported, particularly in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and monitor for recurrence if re-administration is attempted.
- **Seizure Risk:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity may occur.
- **General (Oral vs IV):** The safety of high-dose *oral* levocarnitine in renal impairment is noted as a concern due to metabolite accumulation (TMA and TMAO), but this is less applicable to the intravenous route [US].
- **Monitoring:** Periodic monitoring of plasma carnitine levels is essential to guide dosing.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (≥5% in ESRD trials):**
    - **General:** Pain, injection site reaction, chest pain, flu syndrome, headache, infection, abdominal pain, fever, accidental injury.
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia.
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, anorexia.
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis.
    - **Neurological:** Dizziness, paresthesia, depression.
- **Less Frequent/Post-marketing:**
    - **Neurological:** Seizures.
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Body odor, gastritis.
    - **Cardiovascular:** Arrhythmia, atrial fibrillation.
    - **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
    - **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy.
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Not further specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified by letter category, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine after administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits to the mother. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug should be considered.
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is based on mg/kg (see Section 3).
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. High-dose *oral* therapy is cautioned in this population due to metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO); however, the IV form is used to manage dialytic losses.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also facilitates the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) and γ-butyrobetaine.
- **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; the mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. In ESRD, carnitine is readily removed by hemodialysis.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x weekly post-dialysis), mean pre-dialysis carnitine levels rose from ~20 µmol/L at baseline to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks. 
- **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg demonstrated dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks. While carnitine levels increased, the effect on specific clinical outcomes in this population has not been definitively determined in the provided source.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservative.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL).
