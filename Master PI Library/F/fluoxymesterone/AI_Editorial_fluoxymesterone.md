# Editorial Product Information: Fluoxymesterone

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of fluoxymesterone for healthcare professionals, synthesizing clinical data from the provided regulatory source.
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Fluoxymesterone (an androgenic hormone; 17-$\alpha$-alkylated androgen).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Halotestin** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available source.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Oral tablets: 2 mg, 5 mg, and 10 mg [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pharmacia and Upjohn Company (Division of Pfizer Inc) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Male Hypogonadism:** Replacement therapy for conditions associated with a deficiency or absence of endogenous testosterone, including primary hypogonadism (testicular failure) and hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (congenital or acquired) [US].
- **Delayed Puberty (Males):** Treatment of definitively established delayed puberty that is not a familial trait [US].
- **Breast Cancer (Females):** Palliative treatment for androgen-responsive recurrent mammary cancer in women who are one to five years postmenopausal, or those with proven hormone-dependent tumors [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- The total daily dose may be administered as a single dose or in three to four divided doses [US].
- Dosage must be individualized based on the condition and patient response [US].

### Indication-Specific Dosing
- **Male Hypogonadism:** Typically 5 mg to 20 mg daily for complete replacement in most patients [US].
- **Delayed Puberty:** Carefully titrated using low doses. Therapy duration is generally limited to four to six months [US].
- **Inoperable Breast Cancer:** 10 mg to 40 mg daily. Due to a short half-life, divided doses are preferred to maintain stable blood levels. Subjective response may take one month; objective response may require two to three months of therapy [US].

### Administration and Titration
- In males, priapism is a sign of excessive dosage and requires temporary discontinuation of the drug [US].
- Skeletal monitoring is required for pediatric/adolescent patients [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to fluoxymesterone or any tablet components [US].
- Males with known or suspected carcinoma of the prostate or breast [US].
- Women who are pregnant or may become pregnant (Category X) [US].
- Patients with serious cardiac, hepatic, or renal disease [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Prolonged use of high-dose 17-$\alpha$-alkylated androgens is associated with life-threatening hepatic complications, including peliosis hepatis, hepatocellular carcinoma, and hepatic adenomas. Cholestatic hepatitis and jaundice may also occur [US].
- **Hypercalcemia:** May occur in immobilized patients or those with breast cancer; discontinuation is required if this develops [US].
- **Edema:** Androgens may cause sodium and water retention, posing a risk of congestive heart failure in patients with pre-existing cardiac, renal, or hepatic disease [US].
- **Prostatic Effects:** Geriatric patients may face an increased risk of prostatic hypertrophy or carcinoma [US].
- **Bone Maturation:** In children, androgens can accelerate bone maturation without a compensatory gain in linear growth, potentially resulting in premature epiphyseal closure [US].
- **Athletic Performance:** This drug is not safe or effective for enhancing athletic performance and should not be used for this purpose due to the risk of serious adverse effects [US].
- **Tartrazine Sensitivity:** Some formulations contain FD&C Yellow No. 5 (tartrazine), which may cause allergic reactions, including bronchial asthma, particularly in patients with aspirin hypersensitivity [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Frequencies are not explicitly quantified in the source document.
- **Endocrine/Urogenital:** 
    *   *Females:* Virilization (deepening of the voice, clitoral enlargement, hirsutism), amenorrhea, and menstrual irregularities. Note: Clitoral enlargement and voice changes are often irreversible [US].
    *   *Males:* Excessive frequency/duration of penile erections, oligospermia (at high doses), and gynecomastia [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, cholestatic jaundice, and rare hepatic neoplasms [US].
- **Hematologic:** Suppression of clotting factors (II, V, VII, X) and polycythemia [US].
- **Skin:** Acne, seborrhea, male pattern baldness, and hirsutism [US].
- **Fluid/Electrolytes:** Retention of sodium, chloride, water, potassium, calcium, and inorganic phosphates [US].
- **Nervous System:** Changes in libido, headache, anxiety, depression, and generalized paresthesia [US].
- **Allergic:** Hypersensitivity and anaphylactoid reactions [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Oral Anticoagulants:** Androgens may increase sensitivity to anticoagulants; the dose of the anticoagulant may need reduction to prevent excessive hypoprothrombinemia [US].
- **Oxyphenbutazone:** Concurrent use may lead to elevated serum levels of oxyphenbutazone [US].
- **Insulin/Antidiabetics:** In diabetic patients, androgens may decrease blood glucose levels, potentially reducing insulin requirements [US].
- **Laboratory Interferences:** Androgens may decrease thyroxine-binding globulin, leading to lower total T4 levels and increased resin uptake of T3 and T4. Free thyroid hormone levels typically remain unchanged with no clinical evidence of dysfunction [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category X [US]**. Fluoxymesterone is contraindicated during pregnancy as it can cause virilization of the external genitalia of a female fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended for use in nursing mothers; safety has not been established [US].
- **Fertility:** Impairment of fertility is a possible outcome of treatment, as evidenced by reports of oligospermia in males and amenorrhea in females [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use with extreme caution under specialist supervision. Bone age must be monitored via X-ray every six months to assess the rate of bone maturation and effects on epiphyseal centers [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of developing prostatic hypertrophy and carcinoma [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with serious renal or hepatic disease [US]. Periodic liver function tests are required due to potential hepatotoxicity [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Fluoxymesterone is an exogenous androgen that provides replacement for endogenous testosterone. It promotes the development and maintenance of male secondary sex characteristics, increases protein anabolism, and decreases catabolism. It also stimulates red blood cell production by enhancing erythropoietic stimulation factor [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    *   *Metabolism:* Primarily inactivated in the liver [US].
    *   *Elimination:* Half-life is approximately 9.2 hours following oral administration [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Large doses inhibit endogenous testosterone release through feedback inhibition of pituitary luteinizing hormone (LH) and may suppress spermatogenesis by inhibiting follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) [US].
