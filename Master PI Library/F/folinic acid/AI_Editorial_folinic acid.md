# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the **United States (FDA)**. As only one jurisdictional document was available for this review, comparisons between regions are not present; however, all information is attributed to the source jurisdiction.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not substitute for the official PI or prescribing information required for clinical practice in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Levocarnitine).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
    - Subsequent daily doses should be in the range of 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
    - Administration: Slow bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - Dose adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations and may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].

### Monitoring
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels prior to parenteral therapy.
- Conduct weekly and monthly monitoring, including blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of injection. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizures:** Reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

### Serious and Other Adverse Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent: gastritis and body odor [US].
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (post-marketing report) [US].
- **Allergic:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when co-administered with levocarnitine. Monitor INR levels after initiating treatment or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned by letter, but classified as:] Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies have not been conducted in nursing mothers. In dairy cows, levocarnitine concentrations in milk increase following exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See Section 3 (Dosage and Administration). Clinical documents indicate use in premature infants and newborns for secondary deficiency [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety and efficacy of the *oral* formulation have not been evaluated in renal impairment. The *injectable* formulation is indicated for use in ESRD patients on hemodialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US]. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean trough carnitine levels from 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** A 24-week study comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations (148, 240, and 456 µmol/L respectively) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing [US].
- Single-dose vials: Discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservative [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in US source.
