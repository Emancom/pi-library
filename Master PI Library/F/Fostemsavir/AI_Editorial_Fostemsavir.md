# Editorial Product Information: Fostemsavir

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of fostemsavir for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory guidance into a single, structured reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Fostemsavir (as trometamol).
- **Chemical Class:** Human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) attachment inhibitor; prodrug of temsavir.
- **Brand Name:** **RUKOBIA** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J05AX29.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 600 mg extended-release film-coated tablets.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** ViiV Healthcare [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **HIV-1 Infection:** Indicated in combination with other antiretroviral agents for the treatment of heavily treatment-experienced (HTE) adults with multidrug-resistant HIV-1 infection. It is reserved for patients for whom it is otherwise impossible to construct a suppressive antiviral regimen due to resistance, intolerance, or safety considerations [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The recommended dose is 600 mg taken orally twice daily [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Tablets must be swallowed whole. Do **not** chew, crush, or split, as this may alter the extended-release profile [Australian].
    - May be taken with or without food [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specifically detailed in the primary source beyond standard antiretroviral practice (refer to clinical judgment).
- **Initiation:** Therapy should be initiated by a physician experienced in the management of HIV infection [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to fostemsavir or any excipient in the formulation [Australian].
- **Strong CYP3A Inducers:** Co-administration with strong CYP3A inducers is contraindicated as significant decreases in temsavir plasma concentrations may lead to loss of virologic response. Examples include:
    - **Anticonvulsants:** Carbamazepine, phenytoin.
    - **Antineoplastics:** Mitotane.
    - **Androgen receptor inhibitors:** Enzalutamide.
    - **Antimycobacterials:** Rifampicin.
    - **Herbal products:** St John’s wort (*Hypericum perforatum*) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Immune Reconstitution Syndrome (IRIS):** Reported in patients treated with combination antiretroviral therapy (cART). Inflammatory reactions to asymptomatic or residual opportunistic infections (e.g., CMV retinitis, mycobacterial infections, *Pneumocystis jiroveci* pneumonia) may occur, typically within the first few weeks or months of initiation. Autoimmune disorders (e.g., Graves’ disease) have also been reported [Australian].
- **QTc Prolongation:** Supratherapeutic doses (2400 mg twice daily) have been shown to significantly prolong the QTc interval. Use with caution in patients with a history of QT prolongation, those taking drugs with a known risk of Torsade de Pointes, or those with pre-existing cardiac disease. Elderly patients may be at higher risk [Australian].
- **Hepatitis B or C Co-infection:** Monitoring of liver chemistries is recommended. Diligence is required when initiating or maintaining Hep B therapy in co-infected patients [Australian].
- **Opportunistic Infections:** Patients remain at risk of developing opportunistic infections and should be under close clinical observation by physicians experienced in HIV medicine [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The safety profile is based on the Phase III BRIGHTE study (n=370) [Australian].

| System Organ Class | Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nervous System** | Very Common | Headache |
| | Common | Dizziness, peripheral neuropathy, somnolence, dysgeusia |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Very Common | Diarrhoea, nausea, abdominal pain, vomiting |
| | Common | Dyspepsia |
| **Skin** | Very Common | Rash |
| | Common | Pruritus |
| **Immune System** | Common | IRIS |
| **Psychiatric** | Common | Insomnia |
| **Cardiac** | Common | QTc prolongation |
| **Hepatobiliary** | Common | Increased transaminases |
| **Musculoskeletal** | Common | Myalgia |
| **Investigations** | Common | Increased blood creatinine, increased CPK |

- **Laboratory Changes:** Increases in creatine phosphokinase (CPK) were usually mild/moderate and rarely associated with musculoskeletal complaints. Creatinine increases primarily occurred in patients with pre-existing renal risk factors. Transient increases in direct bilirubin (without jaundice) were observed [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Profile:** Temsavir is a substrate of CYP3A4, esterases, P-gp, and BCRP. Temsavir inhibits OATP1B1, OATP1B3, and BCRP [Australian].
- **Effect of Fostemsavir on Other Drugs:**
    - **Statins:** Temsavir increases plasma concentrations of rosuvastatin, atorvastatin, pitavastatin, simvastatin, and fluvastatin. Use the lowest possible starting dose of statins and monitor for adverse effects [Australian].
    - **Oral Contraceptives:** Increases ethinyl estradiol (EE) concentrations. EE dose should not exceed 30 μg daily. Caution is advised in patients with thromboembolic risk factors [Australian].
    - **Hepatitis C Antivirals:** Co-administration with elbasvir/grazoprevir is **not recommended** due to increased grazoprevir levels and risk of ALT elevations [Australian].
- **Effect of Other Drugs on Temsavir:**
    - **Strong CYP3A Inducers:** Contraindicated (see Section 4).
    - **CYP3A4/BCRP/P-gp Inhibitors:** Temsavir levels may increase, but no dose adjustment is necessary when co-administered with inhibitors such as cobicistat or ritonavir [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. There are insufficient data in humans. Animal studies at high doses (maternal toxicity) showed fetal abnormalities (cleft palate, etc.) in rats and abortions in rabbits. Use only if the benefit justifies the potential risk [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** HIV-infected women are advised not to breastfeed to avoid transmission of HIV. It is expected that temsavir will be secreted in human milk based on animal data [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No human data. Animal studies showed no effects on fertility at clinically relevant doses [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended in children under 18 years of age due to lack of safety and efficacy data [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Limited data in patients ≥65 years. No evidence of different dosing requirements, though caution is advised regarding QTc prolongation [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required, including for patients on haemodialysis (approximately 12.3% of the dose is removed during a 4-hour session) [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required for mild, moderate, or severe (Child-Pugh C) impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Fostemsavir is a prodrug of **temsavir**. Temsavir binds directly to the gp120 subunit of the HIV-1 envelope glycoprotein gp160. This prevents the interaction between the virus and cellular CD4 receptors, thereby inhibiting viral attachment and entry into host cells [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Bioavailability is 26.9%. Tmax is approximately 2 hours (fasted).
    - **Distribution:** 88% protein bound (primarily to albumin).
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via esterase hydrolysis (36.1%) and secondarily by CYP3A4 (21.2%).
    - **Excretion:** Terminal half-life is approximately 11 hours. 51% excreted in urine and 33% in faeces [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **BRIGHTE Study:** A Phase III, partially-randomised, international trial in 371 HTE adults with multi-class resistance.
- **Randomised Cohort:** Subjects received 8 days of functional monotherapy (fostemsavir vs. placebo). At Day 8, the fostemsavir group showed a significantly greater decline in HIV-1 RNA (0.79 vs. 0.17 log10 copies/mL; p<0.0001).
- **Long-term Results:** At Week 96, 60% of subjects in the randomised cohort achieved HIV-1 RNA <40 copies/mL with fostemsavir plus optimised background therapy (OBT) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Packaging:** Supplied in high-density polyethylene (HDPE) bottles containing 60 tablets [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
