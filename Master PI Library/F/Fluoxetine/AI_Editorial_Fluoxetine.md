# Editorial Product Information: Fluoxetine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of fluoxetine product information, synthesised for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this publication, only the Australian PI was available for synthesis; therefore, information regarding US, European, Canadian, or Japanese regulatory positions is not specified in available sources.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Fluoxetine (as hydrochloride).
- **Chemical Class:** Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitor (SSRI).
- **Brand Names:** APO-Fluoxetine [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N06AB03.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Capsules:** 20 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Major Depression:** Treatment of major depressive disorder in adults [Australian].
- **Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD):** Treatment of symptoms associated with OCD [Australian].
- **Premenstrual Dysphoric Disorder (PMDD):** Treatment of PMDD as defined by DSM-IV criteria [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Major Depression:** The standard initial dose is 20 mg/day, typically administered in the morning. If no clinical improvement is noted after several weeks, dose escalation may be considered. Doses exceeding 20 mg/day should be divided into a twice-daily (b.i.d.) schedule (morning and noon) [Australian].
- **Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder:** Recommended starting dose is 20 mg/day. While the standard range is 20–60 mg/day, doses up to 80 mg/day have been utilised. If required, doses above 20 mg/day can be given once daily or b.i.d. [Australian].
- **Premenstrual Dysphoric Disorder:** 
    - **Continuous:** 20 mg daily throughout the menstrual cycle.
    - **Intermittent:** 20 mg daily starting 14 days before the anticipated onset of menses through the first full day of menstruation [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Capsules should be swallowed whole. 
- Fluoxetine may be administered with or without food [Australian].

### Special Considerations
- **Maximum Dose:** 80 mg/day [Australian].
- **Titration:** Full therapeutic effects for depression may take 4 weeks or longer; for OCD, effect may be delayed until 5 weeks or longer [Australian].
- **Tapering:** Due to the long half-life of fluoxetine and its metabolite norfluoxetine, the risk of withdrawal symptoms is reduced, and tapering is often unnecessary in most patients [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to fluoxetine or any excipients [Australian].
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** 
    - Concurrent use with MAOIs is contraindicated due to the risk of serotonin syndrome.
    - A minimum of 14 days must elapse after stopping an MAOI before starting fluoxetine.
    - At least 5 weeks must elapse after stopping fluoxetine before starting an MAOI [Australian].
- **Pimozide:** Concomitant use is contraindicated due to risks of QTc prolongation [Australian].
- **Metoprolol:** Contraindicated when metoprolol is used specifically for cardiac failure [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Suicidality:** Increased risk of suicidal ideation and behaviour in children, adolescents, and young adults (<25 years). Clinical worsening and emergence of suicidality must be monitored, especially during the first few months of therapy or during dose adjustments [Australian].
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Potentially life-threatening; risk increases with concomitant serotonergic agents. Monitor for mental status changes, autonomic instability, and neuromuscular hyperactivity [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** Use with caution in patients with conditions predisposing to QT prolongation (e.g., congenital long QT, hypokalaemia, or hepatic impairment) [Australian].
- **Glycaemic Control:** Diabetic patients may experience hypoglycaemia during therapy and hyperglycaemia upon discontinuation; insulin or oral hypoglycaemic doses may require adjustment [Australian].
- **Abnormal Bleeding:** SSRIs increase the risk of bleeding events, particularly gastrointestinal haemorrhage and postpartum haemorrhage. Caution is advised with concurrent NSAIDs, aspirin, or anticoagulants [Australian].
- **Rash and Allergic Events:** Approximately 4% of patients develop rash or urticaria. Serious systemic illnesses (e.g., vasculitis, erythema multiforme) have been reported; discontinue fluoxetine if a rash appears [Australian].
- **Mydriasis:** Caution in patients with raised intraocular pressure or at risk of narrow-angle glaucoma [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are dose-dependent and most common at doses exceeding 20 mg/day.

| Frequency | Adverse Events [Australian] |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Fatigue (asthenia), nausea, diarrhoea, insomnia, anxiety, nervousness, dizziness, headache, somnolence, tremor. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Palpitations, vasodilatation, anorexia, dyspepsia, dry mouth, vomiting, weight loss, abnormal dreams, libido decreased, sleep disorder, rash, pruritus, sweating, urticaria, abnormal vision, abnormal ejaculation, impotence. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Hypotension, dysphagia, ecchymosis, akathisia, ataxia, bruxism, depersonalisation, myoclonus, seizures, alopecia, anorgasmia. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Oesophageal pain, priapism, serum sickness, photosensitivity. |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Serotonin syndrome, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), hepatic failure, toxic epidermal necrolysis, thrombocytopenic purpura. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic (CYP2D6):** Fluoxetine is a potent inhibitor of CYP2D6. Drugs with a narrow therapeutic index metabolised by this enzyme (e.g., flecainide, carbamazepine, TCAs) should be initiated at the lower end of the dose range [Australian].
- **Tamoxifen:** Fluoxetine may reduce the efficacy of tamoxifen by inhibiting the formation of its active metabolite, endoxifen [Australian].
- **Warfarin:** Altered anticoagulant effects and increased bleeding have been reported; monitor coagulation indices closely [Australian].
- **Serotonergic Drugs:** Increased risk of serotonin syndrome when combined with triptans, tramadol, lithium, or St John's Wort [Australian].
- **Protein Binding:** Potential for displacement interactions with other highly protein-bound drugs [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Classification:** Category C [Australian].
    - **Risks:** Potential risk of cardiovascular defects if exposed in the first trimester. Use in late pregnancy increases the risk of Persistent Pulmonary Hypertension of the Newborn (PPHN) and neonatal withdrawal/discontinuation symptoms [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Fluoxetine is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended as significant plasma levels can be found in infants, and adverse effects (e.g., sleep disturbance, vomiting) have been reported [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no effect on fertility at standard doses, though juvenile rat studies showed some reproductive impairment at high exposures [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended in patients under 18 years. Clinical trials have shown decreased weight gain and height gain in children treated with fluoxetine [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment is required based on age alone, but caution is warranted in patients with concurrent illnesses or multiple medications. Monitor for hyponatraemia [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Clearance is decreased and half-life is prolonged (mean 7.6 days). A lower or less frequent dose must be used [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** In patients on dialysis, steady-state levels are comparable to those with normal function. Lower doses are generally not required but should be used with caution in severe renal dysfunction [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Primarily inhibits the CNS neuronal reuptake of serotonin (5-HT). It has minimal affinity for muscarinic, histaminergic, or $\alpha_1$-adrenergic receptors [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** 80–95% absorbed; peak plasma levels in 6–8 hours [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (94.5%); large volume of distribution (30–40 L/kg) [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Hepatically metabolised (CYP2D6) to the active metabolite norfluoxetine [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Fluoxetine half-life is 1–3 days (acute) to 4–6 days (chronic). Norfluoxetine half-life is 4–16 days. This leads to persistence of the drug for weeks after discontinuation [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Depression:** Meta-analyses show efficacy equal to TCAs and superior to placebo, including in patients with associated anxiety and in elderly populations [Australian].
- **PMDD:** Trials showed significant improvement in mood and physical symptoms with both 20 mg/day continuous and intermittent dosing compared to placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Conditions:** Protect from light [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Not specified in available sources for other jurisdictions.
