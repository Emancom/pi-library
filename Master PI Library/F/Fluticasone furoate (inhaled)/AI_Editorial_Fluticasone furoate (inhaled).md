# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine Injection for healthcare professionals. It synthesises the provided regulatory information into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinical practice should always be guided by the current approved PI in the clinician's respective region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources (Note: Traditionally A16AA01).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Clear, colourless sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **Carnitine Deficiency in ESRD:** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease (ESRD) undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Frequency:** Usually administered every 3 or 4 hours (q3h/q4h); intervals should never be less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in patients with severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow (2 to 3 minute) bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Titration:** Dose adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Monitoring and General Instructions
- **Plasma Concentrations:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is advised [US].
- **Visual Inspection:** Parenteral products should be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** Not specified in available sources as a contraindication, though warnings regarding hypersensitivity exist.
- **General:** None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If severe reactions occur, discontinue immediately and consider the risks/benefits before re-administration [US].
- **Neurologic Reactions (Seizures):** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** While not evaluated for the injection, chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trials Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported at a frequency of ≥5% (regardless of causality) include:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (21%), accidental injury (12%), asthenia (12%), chest pain (15%), fever (12%), flu syndrome (29%), headache (37%), infection (24%), injection site reaction (38%), pain (35%) [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (21%), hypotension (19%), tachycardia (9%) [US].
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (35%), vomiting (21%), nausea (12%) [US].
- **Metabolic/Nutritional:** Hypercalcemia (15%), hypervolemia (12%) [US].
- **Nervous:** Dizziness (18%), paresthesia (12%) [US].
- **Respiratory:** Cough increase (18%), pharyngitis (27%) [US].

### Serious and Postmarketing Adverse Effects
- **Anaphylaxis:** [US].
- **Seizures:** [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; body odor and gastritis have been noted less frequently [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed with concurrent use. **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not assigned a lettered category in the provided source [US].
    - **Data:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate/controlled studies exist in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased milk concentrations of levocarnitine following administration. Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Weight-based dosing is provided for metabolic disorders; safety and efficacy are based on the same clinical parameters as adults [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Injection:** Specifically indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis.
    - **Oral Formulation:** Safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following a single IV dose (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; the mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine compounds that are quickly excreted [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Bioequivalence Study:** A study in 15 healthy male volunteers demonstrated that levocarnitine tablets and oral solution are bioequivalent [US].
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg post-dialysis), mean pre-dialysis levocarnitine concentrations increased from ~20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses in ESRD patients (N≈30 per group) showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations at 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing [US].
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservative [US].
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) at room temperature [US].

- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources (e.g., Australian S4 classification not provided in source).
