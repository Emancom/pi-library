# Editorial Product Information: Flutamide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official PI for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local regulatory documentation before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Flutamide (a non-steroidal anti-androgen).
- **Chemical Class:** Substituted anilide.
- **Brand Names:** **Flutamin** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L02BB01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 250 mg biconvex, round, yellow tablets [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Viatris (Alphapharm Pty Ltd) [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Advanced Prostatic Carcinoma:** For use in combination with an LHRH (luteinizing hormone-releasing hormone) agonist for the management of advanced disease in previously untreated patients [Australian].
- **Disease Flare Prevention:** To prevent the disease flare typically associated with the initiation of LHRH agonist therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The recommended dose is 250 mg (one tablet) administered three times daily at 8-hourly intervals [Australian].
- **LHRH Combination:** Therapy should be initiated up to 24 hours prior to the first dose of the LHRH agonist [Australian].
- **Administration:** To be taken orally.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known sensitivity to **flutamide** or any excipients in the formulation (including sulfites and lactose) [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with severe hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatic Injury:** Post-marketing reports indicate hospitalisations and rare fatalities due to liver failure. Hepatic injury is often reversible upon prompt discontinuation. 
    - *Monitoring:* Liver function tests (LFTs) must be performed monthly for the first 4 months and periodically thereafter.
    - *Thresholds:* Therapy should not be initiated if transaminases exceed 2–3 times the upper limit of normal (ULN). Discontinue immediately if the patient develops jaundice or if transaminases rise to 2–3 times ULN, even if asymptomatic [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Risk:** Combined androgen blockade may increase the risk of myocardial infarction, cardiac failure, and sudden cardiac death. It adversely affects lipoproteins, insulin sensitivity, and obesity markers [Australian].
- **QT/QTc Prolongation:** While not directly studied with **flutamide**, androgen deprivation therapy may prolong the QT interval. Benefits versus risks should be weighed in patients with congenital long QT syndrome or those taking antiarrhythmic medications [Australian].
- **Endocrine/Metabolism:** Reduced glucose tolerance and increased HbA1c have been observed; blood glucose monitoring is recommended for diabetic patients [Australian].
- **Bone Density:** Long-term use is associated with decreased bone mineral density and increased fracture risk [Australian].
- **Hematologic:** Anemia is a known consequence of testosterone suppression [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Note: Frequencies provided are based on combination therapy with LHRH agonists unless otherwise stated.*

| Frequency | System Organ Class | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Endocrine/Vascular | Hot flushes, decreased libido, impotence |
| | Gastrointestinal | Diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Reproductive (Monotherapy) | Gynaecomastia and/or breast tenderness (sometimes with galactorrhoea) |
| | Vascular | Hypertension |
| **Rare (<0.1%) / Very Rare** | Hepatic | Cholestatic jaundice, hepatic encephalopathy, hepatic necrosis |
| | Endocrine | Hyperglycaemia, aggravated diabetes |
| | Respiratory | Interstitial lung disease |

- **Other Reported Effects:** Insomnia, tiredness, headache, dizziness, anorexia, constipation, peripheral oedema, and photosensitivity reactions (erythema, ulcerations, epidermal necrolysis) [Australian].
- **Laboratory Changes:** Elevated BUN and rarely elevated serum creatinine [Australian].
- **Urine Discolouration:** Patients may observe amber or yellow-green urine due to **flutamide** metabolites [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Oral Anticoagulants (e.g., Warfarin):** **Flutamide** may increase prothrombin time (PT). Close monitoring of PT/INR is required; anticoagulant dose adjustment may be necessary [Australian].
- **Theophylline:** Reports indicate increased plasma theophylline concentrations [Australian].
- **Cytochrome P-450 Metabolism:** **Flutamide** may inhibit liver microsomes; caution is advised when co-administered with other drugs metabolised by the liver (e.g., paracetamol, opioids, NSAIDs) [Australian].
- **QT Prolonging Drugs:** Careful evaluation is required when used with Class IA, III, or IC antiarrhythmics, antipsychotics, certain macrolides, or quinolone antibiotics [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category:** Not specified/No data available. 
    - **General Note:** Indicated only for use in male patients. There is a potential for fetal harm if administered to a pregnant woman [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been established; potential presence in breast milk must be considered [Australian].
- **Fertility:** **Flutamide** does not decrease sexual activity or spermatogenesis in animal models at pharmacologically active doses, though libido is reduced in humans via the LHRH agonist combination [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No data available [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data provided in available sources [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** See **Section 4 (Contraindications)** and **Section 5 (Warnings)**.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Flutamide** is a potent anti-androgen that inhibits androgen uptake and/or inhibits the nuclear binding of androgens in target tissues (specifically the prostate) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly and completely absorbed [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Undergoes rapid and extensive metabolism. The primary active metabolite is **2-hydroxyflutamide** [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Excreted mainly in the urine. The elimination half-life of the active metabolite is approximately 8.1 hours [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Lacks estrogenic, anti-estrogenic, progestational, and anti-progestational activities [Australian].
