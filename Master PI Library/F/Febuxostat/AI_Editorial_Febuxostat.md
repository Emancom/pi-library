# Editorial Product Information: Febuxostat

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **febuxostat** Product Information for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a single clinical reference.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Febuxostat (present as febuxostat hemihydrate).
- **Chemical Class:** 2-arylthiazole derivative; non-purine selective inhibitor of xanthine oxidase.
- **Brand Names:** ADENUBEX [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** M04AA03.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 80 mg film-coated, immediate-release tablets. The 80 mg tablet includes a break line to facilitate 40 mg dosing [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pharmacor Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Symptomatic Hyperuricaemia:** Indicated for the treatment of adults with gout where urate deposition has occurred, manifesting as gouty arthritis and/or tophus formation [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The recommended dose is 40 mg or 80 mg once daily, administered with or without food [Australian].
- **Initiation and Titration:**
    - Initial dose: 40 mg once daily.
    - If serum uric acid (sUA) remains >0.357 mmol/L (6 mg/dL) after 2–4 weeks, the dose should be increased to 80 mg once daily [Australian].
    - Target sUA levels (<0.357 mmol/L) may be verified as early as two weeks after starting therapy.
- **Gout Flare Prophylaxis:** Prophylaxis with an NSAID or colchicine is recommended for at least 6 months upon initiation of therapy to manage mobilisation-related flares [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets may be taken regardless of food or antacid use. The 80 mg tablet is designed to be split into equal halves for 40 mg doses [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to **febuxostat** or any excipient in the formulation (e.g., lactose) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiovascular Death:** A significant warning exists regarding increased rates of cardiovascular (CV) death. In the CARES study, patients with established CV disease treated with **febuxostat** had a higher rate of CV death compared to those on allopurinol. **Febuxostat** is not recommended for patients with pre-existing major CV disease (e.g., MI, stroke, unstable angina) [Australian].
- **Serious Skin Reactions:** Rare but serious reports of Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN), and DRESS have been documented. Treatment must be discontinued immediately at the first sign of a rash. Patients who develop these reactions must never be restarted on **febuxostat** [Australian].
- **Hepatic Effects:** Reports of fatal and non-fatal hepatic failure have occurred. Liver function tests (LFTs) are recommended at baseline and periodically thereafter. Treatment should be interrupted if ALT exceeds 3x ULN [Australian].
- **Gout Flares:** Treatment should not be initiated during an acute gout attack.
- **Thyroid Function:** Increased TSH values (>5.5 microIU/mL) have been observed in 5.5% of patients during long-term use. Caution is advised in patients with existing thyroid dysfunction [Australian].
- **Specific Populations:** Not recommended for use in organ transplant recipients or patients with markedly increased urate production (e.g., Lesch-Nyhan syndrome or malignant disease) [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally mild to moderate.

### Table: Frequency of Adverse Effects [Australian]
| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Gout flares, headache, dizziness, diarrhoea, nausea, LFT abnormalities, rash, pruritus, arthralgia, myalgia, oedema, fatigue. |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Diabetes mellitus, hyperlipidaemia, insomnia, libido decrease, paraesthesia, somnolence, palpitations, hypertension, dyspnoea, vomiting, abdominal pain, cholelithiasis, renal failure. |
| **Rare** (<0.1%) | Pancytopenia, thrombocytopenia, anaphylaxis, retinal artery occlusion, sudden cardiac death, gastrointestinal perforation, hepatitis, SJS/TEN, rhabdomyolysis, tubulointerstitial nephritis. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Mercaptopurine and Azathioprine:** Concomitant use is not recommended. Inhibition of xanthine oxidase (XO) can lead to a ~500% increase in the AUC of these cytotoxic drugs, causing severe haematological toxicity. If unavoidable, significant dose reduction of the cytotoxic agent and intensive monitoring are required [Australian].
- **Theophylline:** Co-administration results in a ~400-fold increase in the metabolite 1-methylxanthine. While no immediate dose adjustment is required for theophylline, caution is advised during long-term exposure [Australian].
- **UGT Inducers (e.g., Phenytoin):** May increase **febuxostat** metabolism and decrease efficacy. Monitor sUA 1–2 weeks after starting an inducer [Australian].
- **Glucuronidation Inhibitors (e.g., Naproxen, Probenecid):** May increase **febuxostat** exposure, though no dose adjustment is typically necessary [Australian].
- **Warfarin:** No significant pharmacokinetic interaction; no dose adjustment required [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **[Australian Category B1]**. There is very limited data in humans. Animal studies showed no foetal abnormalities at high doses. However, **febuxostat** is not recommended for use during pregnancy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if the drug is excreted in human milk (excretion is confirmed in rat milk). A risk to the infant cannot be excluded; use is not recommended while breastfeeding [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no dose-dependent effects on male or female fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children under 18 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment is generally required, though greater sensitivity in some older individuals cannot be ruled out [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - Mild to Moderate (Clcr 30–89 mL/min): No dose adjustment required.
    - Severe (Clcr <30 mL/min): Caution is advised; the maximum recommended dose is 40 mg once daily [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - Mild: No dose adjustment required.
    - Moderate: Limited data available.
    - Severe (Child-Pugh Class C): Not studied; use with caution [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Febuxostat** is a potent, non-purine, selective inhibitor of both the oxidised and reduced forms of xanthine oxidase. It prevents the conversion of hypoxanthine to xanthine and xanthine to uric acid [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed (tmax 1.0–1.5 h) with at least 84% absorption. High-fat meals reduce Cmax but do not clinically impact sUA reduction [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is ~99.2% (primarily to albumin).
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised via the UGT system (conjugation) and CYP system (oxidation, mainly CYP1A1, 1A2, 2C8, 2C9).
    - **Elimination:** Dual pathways; approximately 49% excreted in urine and 45% in faeces [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies (APEX, FACT, CONFIRMS):** These trials demonstrated that **febuxostat** (80 mg) was superior to allopurinol (300/200 mg) in achieving sUA levels <0.357 mmol/L. In the CONFIRMS study, 67% of patients on **febuxostat** 80 mg reached the target versus 42% on allopurinol [Australian].
- **CV Safety Studies:**
    - **CARES:** Found a higher rate of CV death and all-cause mortality with **febuxostat** compared to allopurinol in high-risk CV patients [Australian].
    - **FAST:** A prospective study in patients 60+ years with CV risk factors showed **febuxostat** was non-inferior to allopurinol regarding a composite CV endpoint and showed no increase in CV death in the overall study population [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Packaging:** Available in Aclar/PVC/Aluminium blisters in pack sizes of 14 or 28 tablets [Australian].
