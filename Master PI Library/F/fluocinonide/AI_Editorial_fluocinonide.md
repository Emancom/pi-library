# Editorial Product Information: Fluocinonide

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: US (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview designed for use by healthcare professionals to provide a concise clinical summary of fluocinonide.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information mandated for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Fluocinonide (a 21-acetate ester of fluocinolone acetonide).
- **Chemical Class:** Topical corticosteroid.
- **Brand Name(s):** Fluocinonide Ointment [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Ointment, 0.05% (0.5 mg/g) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** 
    - Manufactured by Laboratorios Liconsa SAU (Spain); Distributed by Xiromed, LLC; Repackaged by Bryant Ranch Prepack [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- Relief of inflammatory and pruritic manifestations of corticosteroid-responsive dermatoses [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** Apply a thin film to the affected area two to four times daily, depending on the severity of the condition [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Gently massage the medication into the affected area [US].
    - For external use only; avoid contact with eyes [US].
    - Patients should wash hands after application [US].
    - **Occlusive Dressings:** May be utilised for the management of psoriasis or recalcitrant conditions. If an infection develops, occlusive therapy must be discontinued and appropriate antimicrobial treatment started [US].
- **Titration/Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to fluocinonide or any components of the ointment base (e.g., glyceryl monostearate, white petrolatum, propylene carbonate, propylene glycol, white wax) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
- **Systemic Absorption:** Significant percutaneous absorption of potent topical steroids can result in reversible hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis suppression, Cushing’s syndrome, hyperglycemia, and glucosuria. Risk increases with high potency, use over large surface areas, prolonged application, and the use of occlusive dressings [US].
- **Monitoring:** Patients applying high doses to large areas or under occlusion should be periodically screened for HPA axis suppression via urinary free cortisol and ACTH stimulation tests [US].
- **Local Atrophy:** Prolonged use may lead to skin and subcutaneous tissue atrophy, particularly on the face or in intertriginous/flexor areas, even with short-term application [US].
- **Concomitant Infections:** If dermatological infections are present, appropriate antifungal or antibacterial agents must be used. If the infection does not resolve promptly, fluocinonide should be paused until the infection is controlled [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following local adverse reactions are reported infrequently but may increase in frequency with the use of occlusive dressings [US]. Frequencies are not specified in available sources, but are listed in approximate decreasing order of occurrence:
- Burning and itching
- Irritation and dryness
- Folliculitis and hypertrichosis
- Acneiform eruptions
- Hypopigmentation
- Perioral dermatitis
- Allergic contact dermatitis
- Skin maceration and secondary infection
- Skin atrophy and striae
- Miliaria

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Management:** No specific drug-drug interactions are listed in the provided documentation; however, the systemic effects of corticosteroids should be considered if significant absorption occurs.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned a letter category in the source, but noted as follows: Corticosteroids are generally teratogenic in laboratory animals at low systemic and dermal doses. There are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use only if potential benefits justify the potential fetal risk. Avoid extensive use, large amounts, or prolonged periods in pregnant patients [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Caution should be exercised. It is unknown if topical administration results in sufficient systemic absorption to produce detectable levels in breast milk, though systemically administered corticosteroids are secreted into breast milk [US].
- **Fertility:** Long-term animal studies to evaluate the effect on fertility have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - Pediatric patients are more susceptible to HPA axis suppression and Cushing’s syndrome due to a higher skin surface area to body weight ratio. 
    - Reported effects in children include linear growth retardation, delayed weight gain, and low plasma cortisol levels. 
    - Intracranial hypertension (bulging fontanelles, headaches, bilateral papilledema) has been reported [US].
    - Treatment should be limited to the minimum amount required for efficacy [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources; however, mature patients are noted to have lower susceptibility to systemic toxicity compared to children [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Specific dose adjustments are not provided, though corticosteroids are primarily metabolised in the liver [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Possesses anti-inflammatory, anti-pruritic, and vasoconstrictive properties. The exact mechanism of topical anti-inflammatory activity remains unclear [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Determined by vehicle, epidermal integrity, and use of occlusive dressings. Inflammation increases absorption [US].
    - **Distribution:** Bound to plasma proteins in varying degrees once absorbed [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised in the liver [US].
    - **Elimination:** Excreted by the kidneys and in the bile [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Summary:** Not specified in available sources. (Potency and efficacy are often predicted via vasoconstrictor assays for this class) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at room temperature. Avoid excessive heat above 30°C (86°F) [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Handling:** Keep out of reach of children. Use the cap to puncture the tube seal before first use [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** 
    - **US:** Rx-only (Prescription only).
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** Not specified in available sources.
