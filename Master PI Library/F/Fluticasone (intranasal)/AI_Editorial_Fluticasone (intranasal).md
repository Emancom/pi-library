## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US]; Manufactured by Baxter Pharmaceuticals India Private Ltd [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Dosing Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; should not be administered less frequently than every 6 hours [US].
- **Subsequent Daily Dosing:** Typically 50 mg/kg/day, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised based on clinical requirement [US].
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Monitor weekly and monthly, including blood chemistry and vital signs. Target plasma free carnitine concentrations are 35–60 µmol/L [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Slow 2–3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., reduction to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use [US].
- Discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, often within minutes of administration. These are most frequently reported in patients with ESRD on dialysis. Discontinue immediately if a reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity of seizures may occur [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral vs. IV):** The safety of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal failure. High-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]), which are normally excreted in urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of metabolic disorders or ESRD.

### Common Adverse Effects (≥5% in ESRD clinical trials)
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, back pain, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia [US].
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting [US].
- **Metabolic/Nutritional:** Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia [US].
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia [US].
- **Respiratory:** Cough increase, dyspnea, pharyngitis, rhinitis [US].

### Other Reported Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis, transient nausea/vomiting, and a "fishy" body odor have been observed [US].
- **Serious Effects:** Anaphylaxis, seizures, and laryngeal edema [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Clinical Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not explicitly lettered, but indicates used "only if clearly needed"]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women exist [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows suggest levocarnitine concentrations in milk increase with exogenous administration. Weigh the benefits of maternal supplementation against the potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg), which applies to paediatric populations [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** See Section 5. Intravenous levocarnitine is used specifically to treat deficiency in ESRD; however, chronic high-dose *oral* therapy is cautioned against due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine facilitates the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are then eliminated [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following a 20 mg/kg IV bolus, the mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Study:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly post-dialysis showed that trough levocarnitine levels (initially ~20 µmol/L) rose to between 54 and 180 µmol/L after one week (3 doses). Levels remained stable or increased over the study duration [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses in ESRD patients (N≈30 per group) demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Avoid excessive heat [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Discard unused portions [US].
- **Stability (Admixture):** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL, stored at 25°C [US].
