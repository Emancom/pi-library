# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document provided for the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinical overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official PI. Clinicians should consult the specific approved PI in their jurisdiction before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule for fatty acid transport).
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **Carnitine Deficiency in ESRD:** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease (ESRD) undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 to 4 hours (q3h or q4h); frequency should never be less than every 6 hours (q6h) [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses range from 50 mg/kg or as clinically required. Maximum reported dose is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags [US].
- Discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservative [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, occasionally within minutes of administration. Primarily reported in ESRD patients on dialysis. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and do not re-administer without a rigorous risk-benefit assessment [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with a history, frequency or severity may increase [US].
- **Monitoring:** In metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** Chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustment in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned under current labeling, but reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human surface area dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm.] Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine levels following supplementation. Weigh the risks of infant carnitine exposure against the benefits of maternal treatment. Consider discontinuation of nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal reproductive studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy are established for the acute and chronic treatment of inborn errors of metabolism resulting in carnitine deficiency [US]. (Refer to Section 3 for dosing).
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources; general dosing principles apply.
- **Renal Impairment:** No specific IV dose adjustments provided for non-dialysis renal impairment; however, the drug is used specifically to treat deficiency in ESRD. Note the risk of metabolite (TMA/TMAO) accumulation with chronic high-dose *oral* therapy in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Corrects carnitine deficiency (defined as <20 µmol/L at one week post-term). In ESRD, it normalizes the acylcarnitine/carnitine ratio and increases plasma concentrations [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. 
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
    - **Bioavailability:** Oral bioavailability is very low (~15%) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Study:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly showed a significant increase in plasma carnitine. Baseline pre-dialysis levels of ~21.3 µmol/L increased to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks of therapy. Trough concentrations remained stable or increased over the study duration [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Special Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Discard unused portion of single-dose vials immediately after opening [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only (Prescription only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
