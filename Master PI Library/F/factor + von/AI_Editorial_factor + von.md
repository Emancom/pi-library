# Editorial Product Information: factor + von

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **factor + von** (human coagulation factor VIII and human von Willebrand factor complex; plasma‑derived)
- Chemical class: Plasma‑derived coagulation factor complex (human von Willebrand factor [VWF] complexed with human coagulation factor VIII [FVIII])
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intravenous

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Prophylaxis and treatment of non‑surgical and surgical bleeding in patients with von Willebrand disease (VWD) when desmopressin (DDAVP) is ineffective or contraindicated.
- Prophylaxis and treatment of non‑surgical and surgical bleeding associated with FVIII deficiency due to haemophilia A.

(If an indication is jurisdiction‑specific in the original sources, that fact is not separately stated in the available sources. If such distinctions apply, they are not specified in available sources.)

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General principles
- Dosage should be expressed in International Units (IU) and individualized according to patient clinical condition, bleeding severity, and laboratory monitoring of plasma FVIII:C and VWF activity (VWF:RCo or VWF:Ac).
- The product contains VWF and FVIII in an approximate ratio of 2.4:1 (VWF:Ac : FVIII:C).
- Use aseptic technique for reconstitution and administration. Single‑patient single‑occasion use; discard any unused portion in the vial.

### Presentations / strengths (nominal clinical strengths)
- After reconstitution: approximately 50 IU FVIII/mL with ~120 IU VWF/mL (50 IU FVIII/mL formulation) and 100 IU FVIII/mL with ~240 IU VWF/mL (100 IU FVIII/mL formulation).
- Example vial contents (nominal):
  - 250 IU FVIII / 600 IU VWF (reconstitution volume 5 mL)
  - 500 IU FVIII / 1200 IU VWF (reconstitution volume 10 mL or 5 mL presentation)
  - 1000 IU FVIII / 2400 IU VWF (reconstitution volume 10 mL)

### Von Willebrand disease — dosing guidelines (patients with severe VWD, <10% normal VWF)
- Spontaneous bleeding episodes
  - Initial: VWF 10–20 IU/kg; FVIII 25–50 IU/kg equivalent targets
    - Aim for VWF peak >50% and FVIII >30%
  - Subsequent: 10 IU/kg VWF / 25 IU/kg FVIII every 12–24 h
    - Maintain VWF/FVIII trough >30% until bleeding stops (usually 2–4 days)
- Minor surgery
  - Dose: ~25 IU/kg VWF (equivalent) aiming VWF/FVIII trough >30% until healing (usually 2–4 days)
- Major surgery
  - Initial: VWF 25–35 IU/kg; FVIII 60–80 IU/kg equivalent; aim VWF peak >100%, FVIII >60%
  - Subsequent: 15–25 IU/kg VWF; 30–60 IU/kg FVIII every 12–24 h
    - Maintain VWF/FVIII trough >50% until healing complete (usually 5–10 days)
- Prophylaxis (selected patients)
  - 10–15 IU/kg VWF (25–40 IU/kg FVIII equivalent) 1–3 times weekly to maintain trough >1% (IU/dL). In young patients or patients with gastrointestinal bleeding or menorrhagia, shorter intervals or higher doses may be required.
- Paediatric VWD prophylaxis (based on a paediatric trial): consider 40–80 IU VWF:Ac/kg body weight (equivalent to VWF:RCo) 1–3 times per week to achieve haemostasis.

### Haemophilia A — dosing guidelines
- Minor bleeding episodes
  - 10–15 IU FVIII/kg every 12–24 h for 1–2 days to achieve peak 20–30% (IU/dL)
- Moderate to severe bleeding (e.g., haemarthroses)
  - 15–40 IU FVIII/kg every 8–24 h for 1–4 days to achieve peak 30–80%
- Life‑threatening bleeding (e.g., intracranial haemorrhage)
  - 50–60 IU FVIII/kg initially (single dose 8–12 hourly), then 20–25 IU/kg every 8–12 h; achieve peak levels 80–100% and trough >100%/80–100% respectively as clinically indicated
- Minor surgery
  - Pre‑op: 20–30 IU FVIII/kg (single dose), maintain levels with 12‑24 hourly dosing; aim pre‑op peak 40–60%, trough 20–30%
  - For dental extractions and invasive dental procedures use antifibrinolytic therapy in addition to factor replacement (strongly recommended)
- Major surgery
  - Pre‑op: 40–50 IU FVIII/kg single dose; post‑op dosing varies (see full table) to maintain peak 80–100% and troughs as specified for post‑op days 1–3 and beyond
- Prophylaxis
  - 25–40 IU FVIII/kg three times weekly to maintain trough levels (ongoing therapy)

- Paediatric haemophilia A: dosing based on body weight using the adult guidelines; shorter dosing intervals or higher doses may be necessary depending on clinical response.

### Continuous infusion
- No clinical studies using continuous infusion in patients; suggested as suitable for surgical coverage if required.
- Reconstitute product in the same diluent and volume as for bolus administration and administer using a suitable infusion pump; maintain aseptic technique and device sterility.

### Reconstitution and administration (key practical points)
- Bring product and diluent to room temperature before use.
- Use the supplied sterile transfer device/adapter to transfer diluent into the product vial; follow the device instructions for attachment and transfer.
- Gently swirl to dissolve; do not shake vigorously. Inspect reconstituted solution visually for particulate matter or discoloration; do not use if cloudy, particulate, or if clot/gel has formed.
- Do not refrigerate reconstituted product. Although physico‑chemical stability demonstrated up to 8 hours at room temperature (below 25°C), the product contains no antimicrobial preservative and should be used as soon as practicable.
- Withdraw reconstituted solution into syringe(s). Pooling of multiple vials into a single syringe or sterile container is permitted if done aseptically.
- Administer intravenously slowly—typically within 5–6 minutes (do not exceed 6 mL per minute). Observe the patient during administration; slow or stop infusion if hypersensitivity or other reactions occur.
- Do not mix the solution with other medicinal products or whole blood in the same infusion line.

### Titration and monitoring
- Adjust dose and dosing interval based on clinical response and laboratory monitoring of plasma FVIII:C and VWF activity (VWF:RCo/VWF:Ac). If expected factor levels are not achieved or bleeding is not controlled, investigate for inhibitors.

## 5. Contraindications
- History of anaphylactic or severe systemic reaction to coagulation FVIII and/or VWF preparations.
- Known hypersensitivity to any component of the product (including stabilisers/excipients).
- No additional jurisdiction‑specific contraindications are specified in the available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources

### General warnings and precautions
- Hypersensitivity reactions, including severe anaphylaxis, are possible. Patients should be informed of early signs (urticaria, chest tightness, wheeze, hypotension). If hypersensitivity occurs, discontinue product and initiate appropriate medical management.
- In haemophilia A, development of neutralising antibodies (inhibitors) to FVIII is a known complication; risk is highest within first ~50 exposure days but may occur later. Monitor for reduced clinical response and perform inhibitor testing if expected FVIII activity is not attained.
  - Recurrent low‑titre inhibitors have been reported after switching FVIII products in previously treated patients with prior inhibitor history.
- In VWD, especially type 3 patients, neutralising antibodies to VWF may develop; inhibitors may render therapy ineffective and may be associated with anaphylactoid reactions. Test for inhibitors if expected VWF activity is not achieved or if bleeding persists despite dosing.
- Thrombotic risk: Use of FVIII‑containing VWF products can lead to excessive elevation of FVIII:C. Monitor plasma FVIII:C to avoid sustained excessive levels which may increase thrombotic risk. Patients with clinical or laboratory risk factors for thrombosis should be monitored closely and venous thromboembolism prophylaxis instituted according to standard practice when indicated.
- Pathogen safety: This is a plasma‑derived product. Although donor selection, screening, and manufacturing inactivation/removal steps (e.g., solvent/detergent and dry heat treatments) reduce viral transmission risk, the possibility of transmission of infectious agents (including unknown or emerging pathogens) cannot be completely excluded. The manufacturing steps have been demonstrated in vitro to inactivate/remove relevant enveloped viruses (e.g., HIV, HBV, HCV) and HAV models, with some contribution to parvovirus B19 reduction; non‑enveloped viruses may be more resistant. Consider appropriate vaccination (hepatitis A and B) for patients receiving regular/repeated plasma‑derived products.
- Record‑keeping: Each administration should be recorded with product name and batch number to maintain traceability.

### Monitoring requirements
- Measure plasma FVIII:C and/or VWF activity (VWF:RCo or VWF:Ac) at appropriate intervals during therapy, particularly for severe bleeding, perioperative management, prophylaxis initiation, product switching, or suspected inadequate response.
- Monitor for signs of hypersensitivity and thrombosis during and after administration.
- In patients with suspected reduced efficacy, perform inhibitor assays (Bethesda or equivalent) for FVIII and appropriate assays for VWF inhibitors.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Use with caution in patients with known thrombotic risk factors.
- Use antifibrinolytic agents (e.g., tranexamic acid) as adjunctive therapy for dental extractions and other surgical procedures when clinically appropriate.
- Safety in elderly: not established in adequate studies; use with caution.
- Paediatric patients: apply same warnings and precautions as adults; dosing is weight‑based and clinical response should guide therapy.

## 7. Adverse Effects

- The following adverse reactions were reported in clinical trials and postmarketing surveillance. Frequency categories as presented in available sources:
  - Very common: ≥1/10
  - Common: ≥1/100 and <1/10
  - Uncommon: ≥1/1000 and <1/100
  - Rare: ≥1/10,000 and <1/1000
  - Very rare: <1/10,000
  - Not known: cannot be estimated from available data

### Adverse reactions by System Organ Class (SOC)

| System Organ Class | Adverse reaction | Frequency (clinical trials) | Frequency (postmarketing) |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Blood and lymphatic system disorders | FVIII inhibition (neutralising antibodies/inhibitors) | Common | Very rare |
| Blood and lymphatic system disorders | VWF inhibition (neutralising antibodies/inhibitors) | Not known | Very rare |
| Immune system disorders | Hypersensitivity (including tachycardia, chest pain, chest discomfort, back pain; angioedema; anaphylaxis) | Common | Very rare |
| Nervous system disorders | Dysgeusia | Uncommon | Very rare |
| Vascular disorders | Thromboembolic event | Uncommon | Very rare |
| Investigations | Liver function test abnormal | Uncommon | Very rare |
| General disorders and administration site conditions | Pyrexia | Common | Very rare |
| General disorders and administration site conditions | Headache | Very common | Very rare |

- Serious adverse effects highlighted:
  - Development of neutralising inhibitors to FVIII in haemophilia A leading to loss of efficacy.
  - Development of neutralising inhibitors to VWF in VWD, possibly associated with anaphylactic/anaphylactoid reactions.
  - Severe hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis, potentially life‑threatening.
  - Thromboembolic events, particularly in patients with risk factors or sustained excessive FVIII:C levels.

- Description/notes:
  - FVIII/VWF inhibitors will manifest clinically as inadequate haemostatic response; perform appropriate laboratory testing (e.g., Bethesda assay) if suspected.
  - Hypersensitivity reactions reported include urticaria, angioedema, chills, flushing, nausea, vomiting, hypotension, tachycardia, chest tightness, wheeze, and in some cases shock.
  - Thromboembolic events have been observed particularly in VWD patients with additional risk factors; monitor FVIII:C to avoid sustained excessive levels.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Pharmacokinetic interactions: Interaction of VWF and FVIII with other medicinal products has not been studied; no pharmacokinetic interaction data are available.
- Pharmacodynamic/clinical interactions:
  - Antifibrinolytic agents are recommended as adjunctive therapy after dental extractions; concomitant use is common practice to support haemostasis.
- Management notes:
  - When concomitant therapies which affect coagulation or thrombosis risk are used, monitor clinical status and laboratory parameters closely.
- Jurisdiction‑specific interactions: Not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- Pregnancy:
  - Experience in pregnant women with VWD or haemophilia A is limited or not available. Use only if clearly indicated, taking into account increased bleeding risk at delivery and maternal/fetal considerations.
  - Pregnancy category systems (e.g., Australian categories, US letter categories) are not specified in the available sources.
- Breastfeeding:
  - Experience in breastfeeding women is not available. Use only if clearly indicated.
- Fertility:
  - Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted with this product. Effect on fertility: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Use in Special Populations

### Paediatric use
- Paediatric dosing for haemophilia A: weight‑based dosing generally follows adult guidelines; shorter dosing intervals or higher doses may be necessary depending on clinical response.
- Paediatric dosing for VWD prophylaxis: a paediatric trial suggests 40–80 IU VWF:Ac/kg 1–3 times weekly may be considered to achieve haemostasis.
- General paediatric safety: listed warnings and precautions apply to children as to adults.

### Elderly
- Safety in the elderly population has not been established in appropriate studies. Use with caution; monitor for adverse events and thrombotic risk.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Key pivotal trials: Not fully detailed in the available sources.
- Paediatric haemostasis trial: A paediatric trial in patients <12 years to achieve haemostasis informed a prophylactic recommendation of 40–80 IU VWF:Ac/kg 1–3 times weekly.
- Continuous infusion: No patient studies; continuous infusion has not been evaluated clinically although suggested as a method for surgical coverage based on product properties.
- Viral inactivation/removal validation: Manufacturing includes solvent/detergent treatment and dry heat (80°C for 72 h) and partitioning steps; these measures have demonstrated in vitro reduction/inactivation for relevant enveloped viruses (HIV, HBV, HCV) and HAV models and contribute to parvovirus B19 reduction. Clinical trial designs, sample sizes, and primary outcomes for pivotal efficacy studies are not specified in the available sources.
