# Editorial Product Information: Fesoterodine Fumarate

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived solely from the following Product Information (PI) source: **US (Food and Drug Administration)**. 
- **Note on Jurisdictional Coverage:** While this document is intended to be a harmonised overview across multiple regions (including Australia, Europe, UK, Canada, and Japan), the provided source material was limited to the United States. Consequently, information specific to other jurisdictions is marked as "Not specified in available sources."
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Fesoterodine fumarate (a competitive muscarinic receptor antagonist).
- **Brand Names:** **Toviaz** (referenced in text) [US].
- **ATC Code:** G04BD11 (Not explicitly stated in source, but standard pharmaceutical classification).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Extended-release (ER) tablets: 4 mg (light blue, oval, biconvex, film-coated, debossed with 'FS' [US]) and 8 mg (blue, oval, biconvex, film-coated, debossed with 'FT' [US]).
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Dr. Reddy’s Laboratories Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Adult Overactive Bladder (OAB):** Treatment of OAB in adults presenting with symptoms of urge urinary incontinence, urgency, and frequency [US].
- **Pediatric Neurogenic Detrusor Overactivity (NDO):** Treatment of NDO in pediatric patients aged 6 years and older weighing more than 25 kg [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing (OAB)
- **Initial Dosage:** 4 mg orally once daily.
- **Titration:** Dosage may be increased to a maximum of 8 mg once daily based on individual response and tolerability [US].

### Pediatric Dosing (NDO)
- **Patients > 25 kg to 35 kg:** 4 mg once daily. Maximum dosage is 8 mg once daily if required [US].
- **Patients > 35 kg:** Initial dosage 4 mg once daily. Increase to 8 mg once daily after one week [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets must be swallowed whole with liquid. Do not chew, divide, or crush.
- May be taken with or without food [US].

### Missed Dose
- If a dose is missed, the patient should take the ER tablet as soon as remembered. Do not take more than one dose per day [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known or suspected hypersensitivity to **fesoterodine fumarate**, **tolterodine tartrate** tablets, or **tolterodine tartrate** extended-release capsules [US].
- Urinary retention [US].
- Gastric retention [US].
- Uncontrolled narrow-angle glaucoma [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Angioedema:** Reports include involvement of the face, lips, tongue, and larynx. May occur after the first dose or multiple doses. Discontinue immediately if suspected [US].
- **Urinary Retention:** Use with caution in patients with clinically significant bladder outlet obstruction due to the risk of retention and subsequent kidney injury [US].
- **Decreased Gastrointestinal Motility:** Not recommended in patients with severe constipation or decreased GI motility [US].
- **Controlled Narrow-Angle Glaucoma:** Use with caution; may worsen the condition [US].
- **Central Nervous System (CNS) Effects:** Associated with anticholinergic effects including somnolence, dizziness, and headache. Patients should be monitored, particularly after initiation or dose escalation. Caution is advised when driving or operating machinery [US].
- **Myasthenia Gravis:** Use with caution due to the risk of exacerbating disease symptoms [US].
- **Heat Prostration:** Decreased sweating may lead to heat exhaustion in high-temperature environments [US].

### Pediatric Patients (NDO)
- **Common (≥2%):** Diarrhea, urinary tract infection, dry mouth, constipation, abdominal pain, nausea, weight increase, and headache [US].
- **Heart Rate:** Dose-dependent increases in heart rate were observed, more pronounced in children < 12 years old [US].

### Postmarketing Experience
- **Cardiac:** Palpitations.
- **CNS:** Confusional state.
- **Skin:** Urticaria, pruritus [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Antimuscarinic Agents:** Coadministration with other anticholinergics may increase the frequency and severity of dry mouth, constipation, and urinary retention [US].
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, clarithromycin):**
    - **Adults:** Maximum dose 4 mg once daily.
    - **Pediatric (> 35 kg):** Maximum dose 4 mg once daily.
    - **Pediatric (> 25 kg to 35 kg):** Use is not recommended [US].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., rifampin, carbamazepine):** May significantly decrease exposure to the active metabolite (5-HMT). No dose adjustment provided, but efficacy may be reduced [US].
- **CYP2D6 Inhibitors:** Poor metabolizers of CYP2D6 show higher exposure, but no specific dose adjustment is recommended [US].
- **Warfarin:** No clinically significant effect on INR or prothrombin time [US].
- **Oral Contraceptives:** No significant interaction with ethinyl estradiol or levonorgestrel [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified in available sources (Lettered categories phased out; source notes no available data in humans). 
    - **Animal Data:** Fetotoxicity observed in mice (6x MRHD) and rabbits (3x MRHD) based on AUC [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Presence in human milk is unknown. Consider the developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding alongside the mother’s clinical need [US].
- **Fertility:** No effect on male fertility in mice. Reduced corpora lutea and viable fetuses in female mice at 45 mg/kg/day [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established for NDO in patients $\ge$ 6 years and $> 25$ kg. Not established for patients < 6 years or $\le 25$ kg [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required. However, higher incidences of antimuscarinic effects (UTI, dizziness at 8 mg) were noted in patients $\ge$ 75 years [US].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Adults:** 
        - Mild/Moderate (ClCr 30–89 mL/min): No adjustment (8 mg max).
        - Severe (ClCr < 30 mL/min): Maximum 4 mg once daily [US].
    - **Pediatric (> 25 kg to 35 kg):**
        - Severe/End-stage: Not recommended.
    - **Pediatric (> 35 kg):**
        - Severe (eGFR 15–29): Maximum 4 mg once daily [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild/Moderate (Child-Pugh A/B):** No dose adjustment required.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Use is not recommended [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Fesoterodine is a competitive muscarinic receptor antagonist. It is rapidly hydrolysed by non-specific esterases to its active metabolite, 5-hydroxymethyl tolterodine (5-HMT), which inhibits bladder contractions [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Increases volume at first detrusor contraction and increases bladder capacity in a dose-dependent manner. Does not prolong QT interval at therapeutic doses [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Bioavailability of 5-HMT is 52%. Peak plasma levels reached in ~5 hours.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP2D6 and CYP3A4.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 70% recovered in urine; 7% in feces. Terminal half-life is ~7 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adult OAB:** Two Phase 3 trials (n=1,964) demonstrated statistically significant reductions in urge urinary incontinence episodes and micturition frequency compared to placebo over 12 weeks [US].
- **Pediatric NDO:** One Phase 3 open-label study (n=124) showed significant improvement in maximum cystometric bladder capacity (MCBC) at Week 12 for both 4 mg and 8 mg doses in patients weighing > 25 kg [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from moisture. Keep container tightly closed [US].
