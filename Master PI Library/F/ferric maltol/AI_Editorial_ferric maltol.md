# Editorial Product Information: Ferric Maltol

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions’ Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **US (Food and Drug Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of ferric maltol for healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, regulatory requirements, and prescribing considerations.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference and is not a substitute for the official Product Information approved for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ferric maltol, an iron replacement product. Ferric maltol is a 3-hydroxy-2-methyl-4H-pyrane-4-one iron (III) complex (3:1) with a molecular mass of 431.2.
- **Brand Name(s):** **ACCRUFER** [US].
- **ATC Code:** B03AB10 (ferric maltol).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Oral capsules containing 30 mg of iron (equivalent to 201.5 mg of maltol) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Shield Therapeutics Inc. (Distributed in the US); Shield TX (UK) Ltd (Labeler).

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Iron Deficiency:** Indicated for the treatment of iron deficiency in adults and paediatric patients 10 years of age and older [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric $\ge$ 10 years):** 30 mg taken orally twice daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Administer on an empty stomach, either 1 hour before or 2 hours after a meal [US].
    - Capsules must be swallowed whole; do not open, break, or chew [US].
- **Duration of Therapy:** Guided by the severity of iron deficiency. Generally, at least 12 weeks of treatment is required. Therapy should continue until ferritin levels return to the normal range [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
Ferric maltol is contraindicated in patients with:
- A history of hypersensitivity to the active substance or any excipient. Reactions may include shock, hypotension, loss of consciousness, or collapse [US].
- Hemochromatosis and other iron overload syndromes [US].
- A history of receiving repeated blood transfusions [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [US]. However, the label contains a prominent warning regarding the leading cause of fatal poisoning in children under 6: accidental overdose of iron-containing products.
- **Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD) Flare:** Avoid use in patients with an active IBD flare, as there is a potential risk of exacerbating gastrointestinal inflammation [US].
- **Iron Overload:** Excessive iron therapy can lead to iatrogenic hemosiderosis. Iron parameters should be assessed prior to and during therapy. Avoid administration to patients with evidence of iron overload or those receiving concomitant intravenous iron [US].
- **Accidental Ingestion:** Keep out of reach of children. Accidental overdose is a leading cause of fatal poisoning in children under 6 years of age [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following gastrointestinal adverse effects were reported in $\ge$ 1% of patients and at a rate higher than placebo:

| **Flatulence** | 4.6% | 0.0% |
| **Diarrhoea** | 4.0% | 1.7% |
| **Constipation** | 4.0% | 0.8% |
| **Feces discoloured** | 4.0% | 0.8% |
| **Abdominal pain** | 2.9% | 2.5% |
| **Nausea** | 1.7% | 0.8% |

- **Discontinuation:** 4.6% of patients discontinued treatment due to adverse reactions; abdominal pain was the most common cause (1.7%) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Dimercaprol:** Concomitant use with iron products may increase the risk of nephrotoxicity. Concomitant use should be avoided [US].
- **Bioavailability-Sensitive Oral Medications:** Ferric maltol may decrease the bioavailability of certain drugs, including **mycophenolate**, **ethinyl estradiol**, **ciprofloxacin**, and **doxycycline**.
    - *Management:* Separate administration by at least 4 hours [US].
- **Oral Iron/Supplements:** Monitor clinical response as concomitant use may reduce iron bioavailability [US].
- **No Significant Interaction Observed:** Lisinopril, metoprolol, warfarin, atorvastatin, and norgestimate did not show significant interactions in vitro or in limited screenings [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified (Pregnancy and Lactation Labeling Rule (PLLR) format used). 
    - **Risk Summary:** Ferric maltol is not absorbed systemically as an intact complex. Maternal use is not expected to result in fetal exposure. Untreated iron deficiency anemia in pregnancy is associated with adverse outcomes, including preterm delivery and low birth weight [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on the presence of the drug in human milk or its effects on the breastfed child. Systemic absorption of the intact complex by the mother does not occur, thus exposure to the child via breastfeeding is not expected [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies using maltol at 6 times the recommended human dose showed no effects on mating, fertility, or early embryonic development [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have been established in patients aged 10 to <18 years. Use is supported by adult data and the FORTIS trial. The safety profile in this group is consistent with adults. Safety and efficacy have **not** been established in children under 10 years [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed between patients $\ge$ 65 years (39% of trial population) and younger patients [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No clinically meaningful change in exposure to maltol or its glucuronide was observed in patients with non-dialysis dependent chronic kidney disease (eGFR $\ge$ 15 mL/min/1.73 $m^2$ and < 60 mL/min/1.73 $m^2$) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Ferric maltol delivers iron for uptake across the intestinal wall. The complex dissociates in the gastrointestinal tract, allowing iron and maltol to be absorbed separately [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Increases serum iron parameters, including ferritin and transferrin saturation (TSAT) [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Dissociates upon uptake. Food reduces the bioavailability of iron.
    - **Distribution:** Iron is transferred to transferrin and ferritin.
    - **Metabolism:** Maltol is primarily metabolised through glucuronidation (UGT1A6) and sulphation in vitro.
    - **Elimination:** 39.8% to 60% of ingested maltol is excreted in the urine as maltol glucuronide [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adult IBD (AEGIS 1 & 2):** Two randomized, placebo-controlled trials in patients with quiescent IBD and iron deficiency anemia. The mean difference in hemoglobin (Hb) from baseline to week 12 was 2.18 g/dL (p<0.0001) in favor of ferric maltol [US].
- **Adult CKD (AEGIS 3):** A trial in patients with non-dialysis dependent CKD. The LS mean difference in Hb from baseline to week 16 was 0.52 g/dL (p=0.0149) [US].
- **Pediatrics (FORTIS):** A study in 24 patients aged 10 to <18 years. A clinically significant increase in Hb (mean change 1.10 g/dL) was observed at week 12 [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Excursions:** Permitted between 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Dispense in the original child-resistant container [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** 
    - **US:** Prescription-only (Rx only).
