# Editorial Product Information: Fulvestrant

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of fulvestrant for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is intended as an editorial reference and does not replace the official PI or local regulatory guidance required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Fulvestrant (an antioestrogen).
- **Brand Names:** Fulvestrant Intas [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Solution for injection in pre-filled syringes. Each 5 mL syringe contains 250 mg of fulvestrant (50 mg/mL) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intramuscular (IM) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Accord Healthcare Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Fulvestrant is indicated for the treatment of postmenopausal women with:
- Hormone-receptor (HR) positive, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) negative, locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer who have not been previously treated with endocrine therapy [Australian].
- HR-positive, locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer with progressive disease following prior endocrine (anti-oestrogen or aromatase inhibitor) therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult Females)
- **Recommended Dose:** 500 mg administered intramuscularly at one-month intervals [Australian].
- **Loading Dose:** An additional 500 mg dose is administered two weeks after the initial dose [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Fulvestrant must be administered as two 5 mL injections (250 mg each), one in each buttock (gluteal area) [Australian].
- The injection should be administered slowly over 1–2 minutes [Australian].
- **Caution:** Use caution when injecting at the dorsogluteal site due to the proximity of the underlying sciatic nerve [Australian].
- The product is for single use only; any residue must be discarded. It must not be mixed with other drugs [Australian].

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to fulvestrant or any of the excipients (e.g., ethanol, benzyl alcohol, benzyl benzoate, castor oil) [Australian].
- Pregnancy and lactation [Australian].
- Severe hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Osteoporosis:** Due to the mechanism of action, there is a potential risk of osteoporosis; however, long-term data on bone effect is currently absent [Australian].
- **Visceral Disease:** Efficacy and safety have not been evaluated in patients with critical visceral disease [Australian].
- **Coagulation Disorders:** Caution is required in patients with bleeding diatheses, thrombocytopenia, or those receiving anticoagulants due to the intramuscular route of administration [Australian].
- **Thromboembolic Events:** Commonly observed in women with advanced breast cancer; clinicians should consider this risk when prescribing [Australian].
- **Injection Site Events:** Reports include sciatica, neuralgia, neuropathic pain, and peripheral neuropathy. Caution is advised at the dorsogluteal site [Australian].
- **Laboratory Interference:** Due to structural similarity, fulvestrant may interfere with antibody-based oestradiol assays, potentially resulting in falsely elevated oestradiol levels [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP450 Interactions:** Fulvestrant does not significantly inhibit major CYP isoenzymes in vitro. Clinical trials with midazolam suggest no inhibitory effect on CYP3A4 [Australian].
- **Inducers/Inhibitors:** Co-administration with rifampicin (CYP3A4 inducer) showed no change in fulvestrant clearance. No dose adjustments are required when co-administered with CYP3A4 inhibitors or inducers [Australian].
- **Overall Profile:** There are no known drug-drug interactions requiring dose adjustment [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Fulvestrant may cause foetal harm. It is contraindicated in pregnant women. In animal studies, it caused reduced embryonic survival and foetal abnormalities at doses below clinical levels.
- **Contraception:** Patients of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during treatment and for **2 years** after the final dose [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Studies in rats show high levels of fulvestrant in milk. The risk to nursing infants is unknown; breastfeeding should be avoided [Australian].
- **Fertility:** In animal studies, fulvestrant reduced female fertility and caused adverse effects on male reproductive organs (seminiferous tubular atrophy). Effects in females were largely reversible [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended; safety and efficacy have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment for CrCl >30 mL/min. Caution is advised for CrCl <30 mL/min as safety/efficacy have not been evaluated [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild:** No dose adjustment required. 
    - **Moderate to Severe:** Use with caution as clearance may be reduced. Fulvestrant is contraindicated in severe hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Fulvestrant is a competitive oestrogen receptor (ER) antagonist with an affinity comparable to oestradiol. It downregulates the ER and lacks agonist activity [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** It inhibits the growth of oestrogen-sensitive breast cancer cell lines and downregulates ER expression in breast cancer tissue [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Slowly absorbed after IM injection; peak plasma levels reached in ~7 days. Steady state is achieved after approximately 6 monthly doses (with 2-fold accumulation) [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Large apparent volume of distribution (3 to 5 L/kg); 99% bound to plasma proteins (VLDL, LDL, HDL) [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic via pathways analogous to endogenous steroids (including CYP3A4), though non-P450 routes are predominant [Australian].
    - **Excretion:** Primarily via faeces; <1% excreted in urine. Terminal half-life is approximately 50 days [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **CONFIRM Trial:** Compared 500 mg vs 250 mg in postmenopausal women with advanced breast cancer. The 500 mg dose significantly extended median progression-free survival (PFS) (6.5 vs 5.4 months) and showed a 4.1-month increase in median overall survival (OS) (26.4 vs 22.3 months) [Australian].
- **FALCON Trial:** Compared 500 mg fulvestrant vs 1 mg anastrozole in endocrine-naive patients. Fulvestrant showed superior median PFS (16.6 vs 13.8 months; HR 0.797). Benefit was most pronounced in patients without visceral metastasis [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate) [Australian].
- **Handling:** Do not freeze [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Expiry date is found on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Schedule S4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
