# Editorial Product Information: Fidaxomicin

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the clinical and pharmaceutical profile of fidaxomicin for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial summary and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory guidelines before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Fidaxomicin, a macrocyclic antibacterial agent.
- **Brand Name(s):** **DIFICID** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A07AA12.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 200 mg film-coated tablets. The tablets are white to off-white, oblong, and debossed with "FDX" on one side and "200" on the other [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Merck Sharp & Dohme (Australia) Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Clostridium difficile Infection (CDI):** Treatment of confirmed CDI in adult patients [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** 200 mg (one tablet) administered twice daily (every 12 hours) for a duration of 10 days.
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets may be taken with or without food (before, during, or after meals).
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Titration:** Not applicable; fixed-dose regimen.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Fidaxomicin is contraindicated in patients with a known hypersensitivity to the active ingredient or any of the excipients (including microcrystalline cellulose, pregelatinised maize starch, hyprolose, butylated hydroxytoluene, sodium starch glycollate, magnesium stearate, and the components of the polyvinyl alcohol-based tablet coating) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
- **Not for Systemic Infections:** Due to minimal systemic absorption, fidaxomicin is not indicated for the treatment of systemic infections.
- **Development of Drug-Resistant Bacteria:** Use should be limited to cases where *C. difficile* is proven or strongly suspected to avoid the risk of antibiotic resistance.
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Acute reactions including dyspnoea, rash, pruritus, and angioedema (mouth, throat, and face) have been reported. Fidaxomicin should be discontinued if severe reactions occur.
- **Macrolide Allergy:** Caution is advised in patients with a known macrolide allergy, as some patients with hypersensitivity to fidaxomicin also reported a history of macrolide allergy.
- **Monitoring Requirements:** No specific laboratory monitoring is mandated based on the PI, though clinical response should be monitored.
- **Cardiovascular Safety:** Clinical studies showed no evidence of QTc prolongation or significant ECG changes.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The safety profile was evaluated in Phase 3 trials involving 564 patients treated with fidaxomicin 200 mg twice daily. The overall incidence of adverse events was approximately 68.3%, similar to vancomycin.

| Frequency | System Organ Class | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Common (≥1/100 to <1/10)** | Gastrointestinal | Nausea, constipation, vomiting |
| **Uncommon (≥1/1000 to <1/100)** | Gastrointestinal | Abdominal distension, flatulence, dry mouth |
| | Metabolism/Nutrition | Anorexia |
| | Nervous System | Dysgeusia, headache, dizziness |
| | Investigations | Increased alanine aminotransferase (ALT) |
| **Frequency Unknown** | Immune System | Hypersensitivity (rash, pruritus, angioedema, dyspnoea) |

**Serious Adverse Effects:**
- **Neutropenia:** While neutropenia-related events occurred more frequently with fidaxomicin (2.4%) than vancomycin (1.0%) in trials, these were not considered drug-related by investigators.
- **Vomiting:** Identified as the primary adverse event leading to discontinuation (0.5% incidence).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Cytochrome P450 (CYP) System:** Fidaxomicin does not clinically interact with the CYP system. It does not induce major CYP enzymes (1A2, 2B6, 2C9, 2C19, 3A4/5) and does not inhibit them systemically. Local inhibition of CYP2C9 and CYP3A4/5 in the GI tract is possible but has not shown clinical relevance with substrates like warfarin, midazolam, or omeprazole.
- **P-glycoprotein (P-gp) Interactions:**
    - Fidaxomicin is a substrate and a mild-to-moderate inhibitor of intestinal P-gp.
    - **Ciclosporin (P-gp Inhibitor):** Co-administration increased fidaxomicin Cmax (4-fold) and AUC (2-fold), and metabolite OP-1118 levels (9.5-fold Cmax; 4-fold AUC). However, no dose adjustment is recommended as systemic levels remain very low.
    - **Digoxin (P-gp Substrate):** Co-administration resulted in a minor increase in digoxin Cmax (14%) and AUC (12%), which is not considered clinically relevant.
- **Management Note:** Caution is warranted for P-gp substrates with low bioavailability and a narrow therapeutic index (e.g., dabigatran etexilate).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B1:** (Drugs which have been taken by only a limited number of pregnant women and women of childbearing age, without an increase in the frequency of malformation or other direct or indirect harmful effects on the human fetus having been observed).
    - Animal studies (rats/rabbits) via intravenous route showed no evidence of embryo-fetal toxicity at high exposure multiples.
    - Recommendation: Use during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if fidaxomicin or its metabolites are excreted in human milk. A risk-benefit assessment should consider the health benefits of breastfeeding and the mother's clinical need for the drug.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no effects on mating or fertility in rats at exposures ~50 times the recommended human dose.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients under 18 years of age have not been established; use is not recommended in this population [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment is required for patients ≥65 years. While plasma concentrations are 2–4 times higher in the elderly compared to younger adults, concentrations remain in the ng/mL range and are not clinically significant.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is recommended for any stage of renal impairment or patients on dialysis. However, clinical data are limited; use with caution in patients with severe renal impairment.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is recommended. Due to limited clinical data, use with caution in patients with moderate-to-severe hepatic impairment. Exposure may be 2–3 fold higher in patients with active chronic hepatic cirrhosis.
- **Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD):** Data from retrospective and Phase III/IV studies suggest fidaxomicin is effective and well-tolerated in patients with co-morbid IBD, with plasma levels remaining within expected ranges.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Fidaxomicin is a bactericidal antibiotic that inhibits RNA synthesis by binding to RNA polymerases. Its site of action is distinct from that of rifamycins. It specifically inhibits *C. difficile* and also reduces sporulation *in vitro*.
- **Microbiology:** Narrow-spectrum activity against *C. difficile*. Gram-negative organisms are intrinsically resistant. It has a prolonged post-antibiotic effect (6–10 hours).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Minimal systemic absorption; plasma concentrations are in the ng/mL range.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily transformed via hydrolysis at the isobutyryl ester to the active metabolite **OP-1118**. This process is independent of CYP enzymes.
    - **Distribution:** Confined primarily to the gastrointestinal tract. Fecal concentrations are high (e.g., 5.0–7630.0 µg/g).
    - **Elimination:** Excreted mainly in the feces (>92% as fidaxomicin and OP-1118). Less than 1% is recovered in urine.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Acts locally; time-dependent bactericidal activity. It shows minimal impact on normal intestinal microflora (e.g., *Bacteroides*).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** Two Phase 3, multi-national, double-blind, non-inferiority trials (101.1.C.003 and 101.1.C.004) compared fidaxomicin (200 mg BID) to vancomycin (125 mg QID) for 10 days.
- **Primary Outcomes:** Fidaxomicin was non-inferior to vancomycin for clinical response at the end of therapy (~88% vs ~86%).
- **Sustained Clinical Response:** Fidaxomicin was **superior** to vancomycin for sustained response 30 days post-therapy (~75% vs ~64%) due to significantly lower recurrence rates (e.g., 12.6%–15.7% for fidaxomicin vs 25.1%–27.0% for vancomycin).
- **Additional Findings:** Fidaxomicin maintained efficacy in patients requiring concomitant antibiotics and was associated with lower rates of vancomycin-resistant *Enterococcus* (VRE) colonization.
