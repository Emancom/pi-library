# Editorial Product Information: Ferumoxytol

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial summary and does not replace the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Clinicians should consult the local approved PI for legal and regulatory compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ferumoxytol (a non-stoichiometric magnetite/superparamagnetic iron oxide).
- **Chemical Class:** Parenteral iron replacement product.
- **Brand Name(s):** Feraheme [US].
- **ATC Code:** B03AC (Iron, parenteral preparations).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Injection: 510 mg elemental iron per 17 mL (30 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AMAG Pharmaceuticals, Inc [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Iron Deficiency Anemia (IDA):** Indicated for the treatment of IDA in adult patients who have:
    - Demonstrated intolerance to oral iron or had an unsatisfactory response to oral iron therapy [US].
    - Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The recommended course consists of two doses of 510 mg.
- **Schedule:** An initial 510 mg dose followed by a second 510 mg dose 3 to 8 days later [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Administer only as an intravenous infusion.
    - Dilute in 50–200 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride or 5% Dextrose.
    - Infuse over at least 15 minutes [US].
    - Administer with the patient in a reclined or semi-reclined position.
    - **Extravasation:** Take precautions to avoid extravasation; it may lead to long-lasting skin discoloration/staining. If it occurs, stop administration at that site immediately [US].
- **Monitoring:** Observe the patient for signs of hypersensitivity for at least 30 minutes following the infusion. Monitor blood pressure and pulse during and after administration [US].
- **Hemodialysis Patients:** Administer once blood pressure is stable and at least one hour of dialysis is completed. Monitor for hypotension [US].
- **Re-administration:** The dose may be repeated in patients with persistent or recurrent IDA [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **ferumoxytol** or any of its excipients (e.g., mannitol) [US].
- History of allergic reaction to any intravenous iron product [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning - Serious Hypersensitivity/Anaphylaxis [US]:** Fatal and serious hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis, have occurred. Initial symptoms may include syncope, hypotension, cardiac arrest, or unresponsiveness. Risk is higher in patients with multiple drug allergies. Administer only when personnel and resuscitation therapies are immediately available. 
- **Hypotension:** Clinically significant hypotension may occur. Monitor for signs/symptoms after each dose [US].
- **Iron Overload:** Excessive iron therapy can lead to iatrogenic hemosiderosis. Monitor hematologic response (hemoglobin, ferritin, transferrin saturation) regularly. Do not administer to patients with evidence of iron overload [US].
- **MRI Test Interference:** **Ferumoxytol** is superparamagnetic and can alter MRI studies for up to 3 months. Use T1- or proton density-weighted sequences if imaging is required within this window. T2-weighted sequences should not be used within 4 weeks of administration [US].
- **Laboratory Interference:** Within 24 hours of administration, laboratory assays may overestimate serum iron and transferrin bound iron [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Data (Clinical Trials)
| Adverse Reaction | Frequency [US] |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Headache (1.8–3.4%), Nausea (1.8–3.1%), Dizziness (1.5–2.6%), Hypotension (2.5%), Diarrhea (1–4%), Constipation (2.1%), Peripheral edema (2.0%) |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Pyrexia, Pruritus, Rash, Muscle spasms, Abdominal pain, Dyspnea |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, cardiac/cardiorespiratory arrest, syncope, acute kidney injury, seizure, and acute myocardial ischemia (the latter reported in postmarketing contexts involving hypersensitivity) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Drug-drug interaction studies have not been formally conducted.
- **Oral Iron:** **Ferumoxytol** may reduce the absorption of concomitantly administered oral iron preparations [US].
- **Drug-Device Interference:** Interacts with MRI imaging (see Section 5).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned under current labeling (Risk Summary provided).
    - **Risk Summary:** Limited data in pregnant women. Animal studies (rabbits) at maternally toxic doses (6x human dose) showed fetal malformations and decreased weights. Severe maternal hypersensitivity reactions can lead to fetal bradycardia, especially in the 2nd and 3rd trimesters [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** No data on the presence of the drug in human milk. It is detected in the milk of lactating rats. Consider the developmental/health benefits of breastfeeding against the mother's clinical need for the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility or general reproductive performance were observed in animal studies (rats) [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials included 833 patients $\ge 65$ years. While no overall differences in safety/efficacy were noted, caution is advised due to the higher frequency of co-morbidities and decreased organ function in this population [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Approved for use in patients with CKD, including those on hemodialysis. No specific dose adjustment is required, but monitoring for hypotension during dialysis is essential [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Consists of a superparamagnetic iron oxide core coated with a carbohydrate shell (polyglucose sorbitol carboxymethylether). The complex is taken up by macrophages in the reticuloendothelial system (liver, spleen, bone marrow). Iron is released from the complex and either stored as ferritin or bound to transferrin for erythropoiesis [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is consistent with plasma volume (approx. 3.16 L).
    - **Metabolism/Elimination:** Capacity-limited, dose-dependent elimination. 
    - **Half-life:** Approximately 15 hours [US].
    - **Excretion:** Not removed by hemodialysis.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Does not prolong the QT interval at supratherapeutic doses (1.02 g) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **IDA Trial 3:** A randomized, double-blind trial (n=1997) compared **ferumoxytol** to ferric carboxymaltose (FCM). **Ferumoxytol** demonstrated non-inferiority regarding the incidence of hypersensitivity/hypotension and showed comparable increases in hemoglobin [US].
- **CKD Trials:** Three randomized, open-label trials compared **ferumoxytol** to oral iron in patients with CKD. **Ferumoxytol** (2 x 510 mg) was superior to oral iron (200 mg/day for 21 days) in increasing hemoglobin levels at Day 35 across non-dialysis and dialysis populations [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store at $20^\circ\text{C}$ to $25^\circ\text{C}$ ($68^\circ\text{F}$ to $77^\circ\text{F}$); excursions permitted to $15^\circ\text{C}$ to $30^\circ\text{C}$ [US].
- **Diluted Solution:** Once added to infusion bags (concentrations 2–8 mg/mL), the solution should be used immediately. It may be stored at room temperature for up to 4 hours or refrigerated ($2^\circ\text{C}$ to $8^\circ\text{C}$) for up to 48 hours [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only Medicine (Rx-only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources (refer to local jurisdiction).
