# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis based on the provided Product Information (PI) for **levocarnitine** (specifically the Baxter Healthcare Corporation US PI). It is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine [(R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt].
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Levocarnitine).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with an inborn error of metabolism which results in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing hemodialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 to 4 hours; frequency should not be less than every 6 hours.
- **Dose Range:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilized [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if concentrations are supra-normal [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizures:** Reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma free carnitine levels (target range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5%)
In clinical trials of ESRD patients on hemodialysis, the following events occurred at a frequency of ≥5% (regardless of causality) [US]:

| Body System | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain (9%), Asthenia (9%), Chest pain (12%), Fever (7%), Headache (22%), Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%) |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea (16%), Nausea (8%), Vomiting (15%) |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia (9%), Hyperkalemia (6%), Hypervolemia (5%) |
| **Nervous System** | Dizziness (13%), Paresthesia (5%) |
| **Respiratory** | Cough increase (9%), Dyspnea (7%), Pharyngitis (23%), Rhinitis (9%) |

### Serious/Other Adverse Effects
- **Neurologic:** Seizures.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; body odor and gastritis (less frequent).
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels in patients on warfarin therapy following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US]: Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate or well-controlled studies exist in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** [US]: Not specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations of levocarnitine after exogenous administration. Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of maternal supplementation.
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Refer to Dosage and Administration; dosing is weight-based (mg/kg).
- **Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may result in the accumulation of toxic metabolites: trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism, levocarnitine clears accumulated toxic acyl-CoA compounds by forming acylcarnitine, which is rapidly excreted.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
    - **Bioavailability:** Absolute bioavailability of oral formulations (tablets/solution) is approximately 15% [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Efficacy:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg in ESRD patients demonstrated dose-dependent increases in trough concentrations at 12 and 24 weeks.
- **Clinical Outcome Limitation:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the effect on specific signs, symptoms, or clinical outcomes in this population has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Stability (Admixture):** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified (US context).
