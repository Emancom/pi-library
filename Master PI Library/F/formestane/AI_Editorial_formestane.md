# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection, synthesised for healthcare professionals to support clinical decision-making. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Metabolic Disorders:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 to 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours.
- **Dose Range:** Subsequent daily doses typically range from 50 mg/kg as required by therapy, up to a maximum reported dose of 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels and monitor weekly/monthly. Monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Oral Metabolite Accumulation:** While specific to chronic high-dose oral administration, clinicians should be aware that in patients with severely compromised renal function, metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) may accumulate [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common and Very Common Adverse Effects (ESRD Trials)
Based on placebo-controlled trials in chronic hemodialysis patients, the following events occurred at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) [US]:
- **General:** Pain (30%), injection site reaction (33%), chest pain (12%), flu syndrome (25%), headache (22%), fever (7%), abdominal pain (9%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), palpitations (5%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), anorexia (5%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%).
- **Neurological/Nervous:** Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), depression (5%), paresthesia (5%).

### Other Reported Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; gastritis.
- **Other:** Body odor [US].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Anaphylaxis/Hypersensitivity:** Laryngeal edema, bronchospasm [US].
- **Neurological:** Seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. **Recommendation:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients on warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not assigned a lettered category in provided text, but data suggests use only if clearly needed]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate, well-controlled studies exist in pregnant women [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after administration. The benefits of maternal therapy must be weighed against potential risks to the infant; consideration may be given to discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was observed in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg for metabolic disorders); specific age-related safety data not further detailed [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** The drug is specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients undergoing dialysis. Safety and efficacy of high-dose *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule essential for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic/fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, it alleviates metabolic abnormalities caused by toxic organic acid accumulation (acylCoA) and increases plasma carnitine concentrations [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (n=12), 20 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma carnitine from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations [US].
- **Clinical Outcomes:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the specific effects on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, cardiomyopathy) in this population have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].

- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources (US document).
