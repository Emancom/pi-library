# Editorial Product Information: Fexinidazole

## Editorial Note
*   **Jurisdictions contributing to this editorial:** United States (FDA).
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of fexinidazole for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data to support safe prescribing and administration.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labeling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Fexinidazole, a nitroimidazole antimicrobial.
*   **Brand Name(s):** Not specified in available sources [US].
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Dosage Form and Strength:** Oral tablets, 600 mg.
*   **Tablet Description:** Pale-yellow, round, biconvex tablets, debossed with "4512" on one side [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Oral.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sanofi-Aventis U.S. LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Human African Trypanosomiasis (HAT):** Treatment of both first-stage (hemolymphatic) and second-stage (meningoencephalitic) HAT caused by *Trypanosoma brucei gambiense*.
*   **Approved Population:** Patients aged 6 years and older weighing at least 20 kg [US-approved].
*   **Limitations of Use:** Fexinidazole has demonstrated decreased efficacy in patients with severe second-stage HAT (defined as cerebrospinal fluid white blood cell count [CSF-WBC] >100 cells/µL). In this subgroup, fexinidazole should only be utilised if no other treatment options are available [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Paediatrics $\ge$ 6 years)
Fexinidazole is administered once daily for a total of 10 days (4 days of loading doses followed by 6 days of maintenance doses).

| Body Weight | Loading Dose (Days 1–4) | Maintenance Dose (Days 5–10) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **$\ge$ 35 kg** | 1,800 mg (3 tablets) daily | 1,200 mg (2 tablets) daily |
| **20 kg to < 35 kg** | 1,200 mg (2 tablets) daily | 600 mg (1 tablet) daily |

### Administration Instructions
*   **Food Requirement:** Must be administered with food at approximately the same time each day to ensure adequate absorption (AUC increases 4- to 5-fold with food).
*   **Integrity:** Tablets must be swallowed whole; do not break or crush.
*   **Vomiting:** If vomiting occurs after the first dose event, do **not** re-dose. Resume with the next scheduled dose the following day. If a second event of vomiting occurs, the healthcare provider should be contacted immediately.
*   **Missed Doses:** If a dose is missed, resume normal dosing the following day until the 10-day course is complete.

## 4. Contraindications
*   Known hypersensitivity to fexinidazole or any nitroimidazole-class drug (e.g., metronidazole, tinidazole) [US].
*   Severe hepatic impairment [US].
*   Patients with Cockayne syndrome (risk of severe irreversible hepatotoxicity/acute liver failure) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Decreased Efficacy in Severe HAT:** Lower success rates and higher mortality were observed in patients with CSF-WBC >100 cells/µL compared to nifurtimox-eflornithine combination therapy (NECT) [US].
*   **QT Interval Prolongation:** Fexinidazole prolongs the QT interval in a concentration-dependent manner (average increase of 19 msec). Avoid use in patients with a QTcF >470 msec, history of Torsade de Pointes, congenital long QT, or uncompensated heart failure.
*   **Neuropsychiatric Reactions:** Agitation, anxiety, depression, abnormal behavior, nightmares, and suicidal ideation have been reported. Increased monitoring or hospitalization may be required for patients with pre-existing psychiatric disorders.
*   **Neutropenia:** Avoid concomitant use with other drugs that cause neutropenia. Monitor leukocyte counts periodically.
*   **Hepatotoxicity:** Elevations in liver transaminases have been observed. Liver-related laboratory tests should be evaluated at baseline and monitored during treatment.
*   **Disulfiram-like Reactions:** Avoid alcohol during treatment and for 48 hours after the final dose.
*   **Psychotic Reactions with Disulfiram:** Avoid fexinidazole if disulfiram has been used within the previous two weeks.

### Serious and Specific Reactions
*   **Paediatric-specific:** Vomiting within 30 minutes of administration was significantly more frequent in children (20%) than adults (6.1%) [US].
*   **Rare (<2%):** Hallucinations, psychotic disorder, personality change, suicidal ideation [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
*   **QT Prolonging Drugs:** Avoid co-administration with antiarrhythmics, neuroleptics, fluoroquinolones, and certain antifungals. If necessary, observe a washout period of 5 half-lives for the QT-prolonging drug or 7 days for fexinidazole.
*   **Alcohol:** Disulfiram-like reaction (flushing, rash, abdominal cramps).
*   **Herbal Medicines:** Avoid use due to potential pharmacodynamic toxicities.

### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
*   **CYP3A4/5 Substrates:** Fexinidazole is a moderate CYP3A4 inducer. It may reduce the efficacy of sensitive substrates, including **hormonal contraceptives**. Use alternative or additional non-hormonal contraception during treatment and for 5 days after the final dose [US].
*   **CYP1A2 and CYP2C19 Substrates:** Fexinidazole inhibits these enzymes; monitor for increased toxicity of substrates (e.g., duloxetine, omeprazole).
*   **CYP2B6 Substrates:** May decrease systemic exposure of substrates (e.g., bupropion, efavirenz).
*   **Transporters:** Inhibits OCT2, OAT1, OAT3, MATE1, and MATE2-K. Avoid concomitant use with substrates like metformin or dofetilide if possible.
*   **Effect of other drugs on Fexinidazole:** CYP450 inducers (e.g., rifampin, St. John's wort) may increase M1 and M2 metabolites, increasing QT prolongation risk. Avoid concomitant use [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   **US Category:** Not specified as a lettered category [US].
    *   Data in humans are insufficient. Animal studies showed no effects at clinical doses but demonstrated abortions and reduced fetal weight at maternally toxic doses (2x clinical exposure in rats; <0.02x in rabbits).
    *   **Clinical Consideration:** Untreated HAT poses significant risks to the mother and fetus (including vertical transmission). Treatment timing should weigh these risks.
*   **Breastfeeding:** Fexinidazole and its metabolites are present in rat milk; human data are absent. The health benefits of breastfeeding should be considered alongside the mother's clinical need for the drug.
*   **Fertility:** No effect on fertility parameters was observed in rats at doses up to 1.03x clinical exposure.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established for $\ge$ 6 years and $\ge$ 20 kg. Not recommended for those younger or lighter [US].
*   **Geriatric Use:** Limited data (only 11 subjects $\ge$ 65 years in trials). No specific PK differences observed based on age (up to 71 years).
*   **Renal Impairment:** No adjustment for mild-to-moderate impairment (eGFR $\ge$ 30 mL/min). Avoid use in severe renal impairment (eGFR <30 mL/min) as data are unavailable [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:**
    *   **Mild (Child-Pugh A):** No dosage adjustment.
    *   **Moderate (Child-Pugh B):** Increased exposure to M1 metabolite; monitor for adverse effects.
    *   **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Contraindicated [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Antiprotozoal. Bioactivated by the nitroreductase (NTR) enzyme of *Trypanosoma brucei* into reactive amines that damage DNA and proteins.
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised by CYP3A4 and flavin monooxygenases to active metabolites M1 (sulfoxide) and M2 (sulfone). M2 is associated with QT prolongation.
    *   **Elimination:** Half-lives are 15h (fexinidazole), 16h (M1), and 23h (M2). Less than 3.2% is excreted in urine.
    *   **Distribution:** 98% protein bound. M1 and M2 cross the blood-brain barrier (CSF concentrations are significant).

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Trial 1 (Late-stage HAT):** A randomized, open-label trial (N=394) comparing fexinidazole to NECT. Success rate at 18 months was 91.2% for fexinidazole vs 97.6% for NECT. Efficacy was lower in the severe subgroup (86.9% vs 98.7% for NECT) [US].
*   **Trial 2 & 3 (Early-stage/Paediatrics):** Single-arm trials showing success rates of 98.7% (Trial 2, adults) and 97.6% (Trial 3, children) at 12 months.

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store below 30°C (86°F) [US].
*   **Handling:** Store in the original package to protect from light and moisture.
