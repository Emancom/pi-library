# Editorial Product Information: Fluconazole

## Editorial Note
- This editorial document has been developed using the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **fluconazole** for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data to support safe and effective prescribing.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or clinical guidelines required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Fluconazole
- **Chemical Class:** Bis-triazole antifungal agent
- **Brand Name(s):** APO-Fluconazole [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Hard gelatin capsules, 200 mg [Australian]
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral (The pharmacological properties are similar for both oral and intravenous routes; bioavailability is >90% in normal volunteers).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
The following indications are approved [Australian]:
- **Cryptococcal meningitis:** Treatment in patients unable to tolerate amphotericin B (noting fluconazole may have lower efficacy in the acute phase).
- **Cryptococcal meningitis (Maintenance):** Prevention of relapse in patients with AIDS.
- **Candidiasis (Oropharyngeal and Oesophageal):** Treatment in AIDS and other immunocompromised patients.
- **Oropharyngeal Candidiasis (Prophylaxis):** Secondary prophylaxis in patients with HIV infection.
- **Serious Candida infections:** Treatment of life-threatening infections in patients unable to tolerate amphotericin B.
- **Vaginal Candidiasis:** Treatment when topical therapy has failed.
- **Dermatomycosis:** Treatment of extensive tinea corporis, tinea cruris, or tinea pedis in immunocompetent patients when topical therapy is impractical or has failed.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Cryptococcal Meningitis:** 400 mg on day 1, followed by 200–400 mg once daily. Treatment typically continues for 10–12 weeks after CSF cultures are negative.
- **Prevention of Cryptococcal Relapse (AIDS):** 100–200 mg once daily.
- **Oropharyngeal Candidiasis:** 100 mg on day 1, followed by 50 mg once daily for at least 2–3 weeks.
- **Oesophageal Candidiasis:** 200 mg on day 1, followed by 100 mg once daily. Doses up to 400 mg daily may be used based on response.
- **Secondary Prophylaxis (HIV):** 150 mg as a single dose once weekly.
- **Systemic Candida Infections:** 400 mg on day 1, followed by 200–400 mg once daily for at least 4 weeks (and 2 weeks post-symptom resolution).
- **Vaginal Candidiasis:** A single oral dose of 150 mg.
- **Tinea Infections:** 150 mg once weekly for 4 weeks.

### Administration Instructions
- Capsules should be swallowed whole.
- Absorption is not affected by gastric pH or food intake.
- Fluconazole capsules are not suitable for children weighing less than 35 kg [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to fluconazole, related azole compounds, or any excipients [Australian].
- **Terfenadine:** Co-administration is contraindicated in patients receiving multiple doses of fluconazole ≥400 mg/day [Australian].
- **QT-prolonging drugs (CYP3A4 substrates):** Co-administration with cisapride, astemizole, erythromycin, pimozide, and quinidine is strictly contraindicated due to the risk of cardiotoxicity [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Rare cases of serious hepatic toxicity, including fatalities, have occurred. Monitor liver function tests (LFTs) if abnormalities develop during therapy. Discontinue if clinical signs of liver disease emerge.
- **Dermatologic Reactions:** Exfoliative cutaneous reactions (e.g., Stevens-Johnson Syndrome, Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis) and Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS) have been reported. Discontinue if a rash develops in patients with superficial infections; monitor closely in those with systemic infections.
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** Fluconazole is associated with QT interval prolongation. Use with extreme caution in patients with proarrhythmic conditions (e.g., structural heart disease, electrolyte imbalances like hypokalaemia, or concomitant use of other QT-prolonging drugs).
- **Adrenal Insufficiency:** Cases have been reported in patients receiving fluconazole.
- **CYP Inhibition:** Fluconazole is a potent inhibitor of CYP2C9 and CYP2C19, and a moderate inhibitor of CYP3A4. Significant monitoring is required for drugs with narrow therapeutic windows metabolised by these enzymes.
- **Excipients:** Contains lactose; avoid in patients with rare hereditary galactose intolerance or glucose-galactose malabsorption [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Categorisation
- **Common (1–10%):** Headache, abdominal pain, diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting, rash, and elevation of LFTs (ALT, AST, alkaline phosphatase).
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Seizures, dizziness, paraesthesia, taste perversion, dyspepsia, flatulence, dry mouth, cholestasis, jaundice, bilirubin increase, pruritus, urticaria, insomnia, and somnolence.
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Leukopenia (including neutropenia/agranulocytosis), thrombocytopenia, anaphylaxis, angioedema, hypercholesterolaemia, hypertriglyceridaemia, hypokalaemia, tremor, hepatic failure, hepatocellular necrosis, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, alopecia, and QT prolongation/Torsade de Pointes.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Contraindicated Combinations
- **Cisapride, Astemizole, Pimozide, Quinidine, Erythromycin:** Increased risk of QT prolongation and Torsade de Pointes.
- **Terfenadine:** Contraindicated with fluconazole doses ≥400 mg.

### Significant Interactions Requiring Monitoring/Adjustment
- **Anticoagulants (Warfarin):** Fluconazole increases prothrombin time; careful monitoring is required.
- **Rifampicin:** Decreases fluconazole AUC by 25% and half-life by 20%; consider increasing fluconazole dose.
- **Hydrochlorothiazide:** Increases fluconazole plasma levels by approximately 40%.
- **Sulphonylureas:** Fluconazole prolongs the half-life of glibenclamide, glipizide, and tolbutamide; monitor for hypoglycaemia.
- **Phenytoin:** Significant increases in phenytoin concentrations; monitor levels to avoid toxicity.
- **Ciclosporin/Tacrolimus/Sirolimus:** Fluconazole increases concentrations of these immunosuppressants; dose reductions and level monitoring are often necessary.
- **Theophylline:** 18% decrease in mean plasma clearance; monitor for toxicity.
- **Zidovudine:** Increased AUC and Cmax; monitor for zidovudine-related adverse effects.
- **HMG-CoA Reductase Inhibitors (Statins):** Increased risk of myopathy and rhabdomyolysis (particularly with CYP3A4-metabolised statins like atorvastatin).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category D]. Use should be avoided except in life-threatening infections where benefits outweigh fetal risks. High-dose therapy (400–800 mg/day) in the first trimester has been associated with a distinct pattern of birth defects (e.g., brachycephaly, cleft palate, femoral bowing). Single 150 mg doses for vaginal candidiasis have also been associated with a small increased risk of musculoskeletal malformations.
- **Breastfeeding:** Fluconazole is excreted in human milk at concentrations similar to plasma. Use in nursing mothers is not recommended [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal data shows delayed parturition at high doses, but no effect on fertility in rats.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on clinical/mycological response. 
    - *Mucosal Candidiasis:* 3 mg/kg daily.
    - *Systemic Candidiasis:* 6–12 mg/kg daily.
    - *Neonates (0–2 weeks):* Same mg/kg dose as older children but administered every 72 hours due to slow clearance.
    - *Neonates (3–4 weeks):* Same dose every 48 hours.
- **Elderly Use:** Adjust dose based on renal function.
- **Renal Impairment:** No adjustment for single-dose therapy. For multiple-dose therapy, after loading doses on days 1 and 2, adjust as follows [Australian]:
    - **CrCl >50 mL/min:** 100% of recommended dose.
    - **CrCl 21–50 mL/min:** 50% of dose or double the dosing interval.
    - **CrCl 11–20 mL/min:** 25% of dose or quadruple the dosing interval.
    - **Regular Dialysis:** One dose after every dialysis session.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Highly selective inhibitor of fungal cytochrome P-450 sterol C-14 alpha demethylation, leading to the loss of normal sterols and accumulation of 14 alpha-methyl sterols in fungi.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - *Absorption:* >90% bioavailable; peak plasma levels in 1–2 hours (fasted).
    - *Distribution:* Apparent volume of distribution approximates total body water; low protein binding (11–12%). Excellent penetration into CSF, saliva, sputum, and skin.
    - *Metabolism:* Minor; approximately 11% excreted as metabolites in urine.
    - *Elimination:* Predominantly renal (80% unchanged drug). Terminal half-life is approximately 30 hours (range 20–50 hours).
