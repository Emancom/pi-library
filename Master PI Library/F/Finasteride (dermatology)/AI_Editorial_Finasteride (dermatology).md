# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction:
    - **United States** (Food and Drug Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview designed for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data into a single, coherent reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/amino acid derivative ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance haemodialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
- **Loading Dose:** Often utilised in patients presenting with severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Maintenance Frequency:** Doses should be administered every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
- **Dose Range:** Subsequent daily doses are generally 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered.
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine levels prior to treatment. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is advised.

### ESRD Patients on Haemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are supranormal.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal oedema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately.
- **Neurological Effects (Seizures):** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity may occur.
- **Monitoring Requirements:** In ESRD patients, monitoring of pre-dialysis carnitine levels is essential for dose titration.
- **General Precaution:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in those with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalised Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. 
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not assigned, but comparable to B]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. Risk-benefit analysis is required; consideration may be given to discontinuing nursing or the drug.
- **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, Saccharomyces, Schizosaccharomyces) were negative. Long-term carcinogenicity studies have not been performed.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use is approved; see Section 3 for weight-based dosing (50 mg/kg) in metabolic disorders.
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is specifically indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. However, caution is advised regarding the use of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, it alleviates metabolic abnormalities resulting from carnitine loss during dialysis and reduced intake/synthesis.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (faeces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x weekly after dialysis), trough concentrations increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg in dialysis patients showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks.
- **Bioequivalence:** Levocarnitine tablets and oral solution were found to be bioequivalent in healthy volunteers (Cmax ~80 µmol/L after 2g BID dosing).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Stability (Admixture):** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL).
