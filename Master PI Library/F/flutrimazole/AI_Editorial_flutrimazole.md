# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following jurisdictional Product Information (PI) source: United States (FDA).
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current official PI approved in their specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Metabolic Disorders)
- **Adult and Paediatric:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Initial Management:** In severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 or 4 hours and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg may be required based on clinical response [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administer as a 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations (normal range: 40 µmol/L to 50 µmol/L) [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Hypersensitivity Reactions
- **Boxed Warning:** None [US].
- Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have occurred, often within minutes of administration, particularly in ESRD patients [US].
- Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs. Re-administration should only occur after a careful risk-benefit assessment and under close monitoring [US].

### General Precautions
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed. Recommendation: Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- **US Category:** Not assigned (narrative descriptive provided). 
- Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm [US].
- There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women; use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].

### Breastfeeding
- Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. 
- Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after exogenous administration. 
- Risks of excess carnitine intake to the infant should be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See Section 3 (Dosage and Administration). Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
- Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. 
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and [³H]-γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Following intravenous administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine over 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours, and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- In ESRD patients on hemodialysis, administration of levocarnitine at 20 mg/kg three times per week significantly increased plasma carnitine concentrations. After 12 to 24 weeks of therapy, trough concentrations remained stable and above normal levels [US].
- Note: While carnitine concentrations increased, the effects on clinical outcomes and signs/symptoms of carnitine deficiency in the ESRD population have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of an opened vial as the formulation is preservative-free [US].
