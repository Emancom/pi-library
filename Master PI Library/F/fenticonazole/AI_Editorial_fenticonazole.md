# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule; an amino acid derivative [(R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium].
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism which result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the next 24 hours.
    - Dosing frequency: Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less frequently than every 6 hours [US].
    - Maximum recorded dose: 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - Administration: Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - Dose adjustments: May be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [US].
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring:** 
    - For metabolic disorders: Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
    - For ESRD: Monitor trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations (normal range: 40 to 50 µmol/L) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events reported in clinical trials of ESRD patients on hemodialysis (occurring at ≥5% regardless of causality) include:

| Body System | Adverse Event (Frequency ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia |
| **Respiratory** | Cough increase, dyspnea, pharyngitis, rhinitis |

- **Postmarketing Experience:** 
    - **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent: body odor, gastritis [US].
    - **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating treatment or after dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified by letter, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits (up to 3.8 times the human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration. Risks to the child must be weighed against the benefits to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See Section 3 (Dosage and Administration). Clinical data suggests use in newborns and premature infants for secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. 
    - Chronic high doses of **oral** levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US]. 
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with metabolic disorders, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine complexes [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Dialysis Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (n=12), 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma carnitine concentrations from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L by week 8. While carnitine concentrations increased, the effect on clinical outcomes in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat. Discard unused portions [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) [US].
