# Editorial Product Information: Fludeoxyglucose F 18

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **Fludeoxyglucose F 18 Injection** for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical and regulatory data into a single reference point.
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** 2-deoxy-2-[¹⁸F]fluoro-D-glucose (Fludeoxyglucose F 18)
- **Chemical Class:** Positron-emitting radiopharmaceutical; glucose analog
- **Brand Names:** Fludeoxyglucose F 18 Injection, USP [US]
- **ATC Code:** V09IX04 (Not specified in available source, provided for clinical context)
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
  - Multiple-dose 50 mL and 100 mL glass vials [US]
  - Concentration: 0.74 to 7.40 GBq/mL (20 to 200 mCi/mL) at the end of synthesis (EOS) [US]
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous injection
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** PETNET Solutions, Inc. [US]

## 2. Approved Indications
**Fludeoxyglucose F 18 Injection** is indicated for positron emission tomography (PET) imaging in the following clinical settings:

*   **Oncology [US-approved]:** Assessment of abnormal glucose metabolism to assist in evaluating malignancy in patients with known or suspected abnormalities (identified via other modalities) or in those with an existing cancer diagnosis.
*   **Cardiology [US-approved]:** Identification of left ventricular myocardium with residual glucose metabolism and reversible loss of systolic function in patients with coronary artery disease and left ventricular dysfunction, when used adjunctively with myocardial perfusion imaging.
*   **Neurology [US-approved]:** Identification of regions of abnormal glucose metabolism associated with foci of epileptic seizures.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
*   **Recommended Dose:** 5 to 10 mCi (185 to 370 MBq) administered as a single intravenous injection [US].

### Dosing by Indication
*   **Oncology/Cardiology/Neurology:** Same as general adult dosing (5 to 10 mCi) [US].

### Administration Instructions
*   **Aseptic Technique:** Withdraw the dose aseptically and assay in a calibrated dose calibrator prior to administration [US].
*   **Visual Inspection:** Inspect for particulate matter and discoloration; do not use if present [US].
*   **Radiation Safety:** Use waterproof gloves and effective shielding. The dose should be the minimum necessary for diagnostic purposes [US].
*   **Expiration:** Must be used within 12 hours of the end of synthesis (EOS) [US].
*   **Imaging Timing:** Initiate imaging within 40 minutes of injection; acquire static emission images 30 to 100 minutes post-injection [US].

### Patient Preparation
*   **Oncology and Neurology:** Patients should fast for 4 to 6 hours prior to injection. Aim for normoglycemia for at least two days prior to the study [US].
*   **Cardiology:** Administration of 50 to 75 grams of glucose-containing food or liquids prior to injection facilitates the localization of cardiac ischemia [US].
*   **Hydration:** Encourage adequate hydration before the study and frequent voiding (for at least one hour post-study) to reduce bladder radiation exposure [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   None specified in available US source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Radiation Risk:** As a radiation-emitting product, **Fludeoxyglucose F 18** may increase the risk of cancer. This risk is notably higher in paediatric populations. Use the smallest dose consistent with imaging objectives [US].
*   **Blood Glucose Abnormalities:** Inadequately regulated blood glucose levels (e.g., in diabetes or non-fasting states) can lead to suboptimal imaging in oncology and neurology settings [US].
*   **Adverse Reaction Readiness:** While rare, hypersensitivity reactions (pruritus, edema, rash) have occurred. Emergency resuscitation equipment must be available [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*   **Frequency Not Specified (Post-marketing):**
    *   **Immune System Disorders:** Hypersensitivity reactions including pruritus, edema, and rash [US].
*   **Serious Adverse Effects:** Potential for radiation-induced complications (long-term risk) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Interaction Studies:** Specific interactions between **Fludeoxyglucose F 18** and other medications have not been formally studied [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   **Category:** Not specifically assigned a letter category in the current US label, but noted to cross the placenta [US].
    *   **Clinical Considerations:** Radiopharmaceuticals have the potential to cause fetal harm. Benefit-risk analysis should consider the gestational stage and radiation dose [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** 
    *   **Recommendation:** Temporarily discontinue breastfeeding. Pump and discard breast milk for at least 9 hours following administration to minimize infant radiation exposure. Avoid close physical contact with the infant during this period [US].
*   **Fertility:** Animal studies evaluating effects on fertility have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** 
    *   **Neurology:** Safety and effectiveness established for the identification of seizure foci; recommended dose is 2.6 mCi (96.2 MBq) [US].
    *   **Oncology/Cardiology:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** No specific dose adjustments provided; however, the general principle of using the lowest effective dose applies [US].
*   **Renal Impairment:** Not specifically studied. However, as the drug is primarily eliminated renally, impairment may increase radiation exposure to the bladder and adjacent tissues. Ensure adequate hydration and frequent voiding [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specifically studied [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
**Fludeoxyglucose F 18** is a glucose analog. It is transported into cells via facilitative glucose transporter (GLUT) proteins and phosphorylated by hexokinase to [¹⁸F]-FDG-6-phosphate. Because it cannot be further metabolised in the glycolytic pathway and has low membrane permeability, it remains trapped in tissues in proportion to their rate of glucose utilization [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** Rapidly distributes to all organs. In healthy volunteers, arterial blood levels decay triexponentially (half-lives: 0.2–0.3 min, 10–13 min, and 80–95 min) [US].
*   **Metabolism:** Phosphorylated within cells but not further metabolised. It is cleared from non-cardiac tissues within 3 to 24 hours [US]. 
*   **Elimination:** Primarily renal (unchanged). Within 33 minutes of injection, approximately 3.9% of the dose is excreted in urine; by 2 hours, approximately 20.6% is present in the bladder [US].
*   **Physical Half-life:** 109.7 minutes [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Oncology:** Efficacy supported by 16 independent prospective studies involving various malignancies (e.g., lung, colorectal, breast, melanoma). Doses ranged from 200 MBq to 740 MBq. Results showed variable performance based on cancer type and size [US].
*   **Cardiology:** Demonstrated in 10 prospective studies of patients with CAD and left ventricular dysfunction. **Fludeoxyglucose F 18** accumulation in areas of reduced perfusion (mismatch) was predictive of reversible systolic dysfunction [US].
*   **Neurology:** Evaluated in a trial of 86 patients with epilepsy. PET imaging confirmed previous diagnostic findings in 16% and provided new findings in 34% of cases [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59–86°F) [US].
*   **Shielding:** Store upright within a lead-shielded container [US].
*   **Shelf Life:** Use within 12 hours of the end of synthesis (EOS) [US].
