# Editorial Product Information: Fluorescein

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of fluorescein sodium for healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory instructions into a single reference.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the Australian (TGA) source document was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Fluorescein sodium (disodium 2-(3-oxo-6-oxido-3H-xanthen-9-yl)benzoate)
- **Chemical Class:** Diagnostic dye
- **Brand Names:** Fluorescein SERB [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** SO1JA 01
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for intravenous (IV) injection; 500 mg/5 mL (100 mg/mL) [Australian]
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous injection
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Clinect Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Diagnostic Angiography:** Indicated for fluorescein angiography of the fundus and the iris vasculature [Australian].
- **Diagnostic Use:** This medicinal product is for diagnostic purposes only [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** A single dose of 500 mg (one 5 mL ampoule) [Australian].
- **Paediatric Dosing:** While formal studies have not been conducted in the paediatric population, a weight-based dose adjustment is recommended (e.g., 5 mg/kg) if used in children [Australian].
- **Geriatric Dosing:** No specific dose modification is required for the elderly, though consideration should be given to pre-existing comorbidities [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Administer via intravenous injection [Australian].
    - Visually inspect for particulate matter and discolouration prior to use [Australian].
    - Do not mix or dilute with other medications in the same syringe [Australian].
    - Intravenous cannulas should be flushed before and after administration to avoid physical incompatibility [Australian].
    - The product contains no antimicrobial preservatives and is intended for single use in one patient only [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to fluorescein sodium or any excipients [Australian].
- **Prohibited Routes:** Absolute contraindication for intrathecal or intra-arterial use [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Rare but potentially fatal cases of anaphylactic/anaphylactoid shock have been reported. A survey suggested a severe reaction rate of 1 in 220,000 angiographies.
    - **Monitoring:** Patients must be observed closely for at least 30 minutes post-angiography [Australian].
    - **Emergency Preparedness:** An emergency tray with resuscitation equipment (including adrenaline for IV/IM use, oxygen, IV fluids, and corticosteroids) must be immediately available [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Disease:** Caution is required in patients with cardiovascular disease or a history of cardio-pulmonary disease. Rare complications include chest pain, myocardial infarction, and death [Australian].
- **Beta-blocker Concomitance:** Use with beta-blockers (including ophthalmic drops) may increase the risk of lethal anaphylaxis and reduce the efficacy of adrenaline during resuscitation [Australian].
- **Extravasation Risk:** The high pH of fluorescein solution can cause severe tissue damage, pain, thrombophlebitis, and necrosis. Ensure correct needle placement before injection. If extravasation occurs, stop the injection immediately and treat local tissue [Australian].
- **Skin and Urine Discolouration:** Patients should be advised that skin and urine will turn yellow. Skin discolouration typically fades within 6–12 hours; urine discolouration (chromaturia) resolves within 24–36 hours [Australian].
- **Contact Lenses:** Fluorescein can stain soft contact lenses; contact should be avoided [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are categorised by frequency based on clinical trials and post-marketing surveillance [Australian].

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (≥10%) | Nausea |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Vomiting, syncope, dysgeusia, pruritus, urticaria, extravasation |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Abdominal pain, headache, dizziness, paresthesia, cough, throat tightness, hypersensitivity, thrombophlebitis, feeling hot, pain, dysphagia |
| **Rare** (0.01–0.1%) | Cardiac arrest, anaphylactic reaction, bronchospasm, shock, hypotension |
| **Very Rare** (<0.01%) | Death, angina pectoris, bradycardia, tachycardia, seizures, dyspnoea, pulmonary oedema, respiratory arrest, laryngeal/nasal oedema, hypertension, vasodilatation |

- **Other Reactions (Frequency Not Known):** Acute myocardial infarction, tremor, loss of consciousness, cerebrovascular accident, dermatitis, erythema, chills, and malaise [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Beta-blockers:** May potentiate the severity of anaphylactic reactions and impair the response to emergency adrenaline [Australian].
- **Organic Anion Transporter (OAT) Inhibitors:** Compounds like probenecid may interfere with the systemic profile of fluorescein [Australian].
- **Physicochemical Incompatibilities:** Avoid mixing with acidic solutions (e.g., antihistamines like promethazine) or citric acid, as these may cause fluorescein to precipitate [Australian].
- **Laboratory Test Interference:** Fluorescence may interfere with blood and urinary parameters. It specifically interferes with serum digoxin and cortisol determination when using fluorescence polarization immunoassay (FPIA). Use caution during therapeutic drug monitoring of narrow-therapeutic-index drugs [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B2:** Animal studies showed no foetal harm at 9 times the human dose.
    - **Recommendation:** No adequate human studies exist. Use should be avoided unless deemed absolutely necessary [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Fluorescein is excreted in human milk for up to 7 days. It is recommended to discontinue breastfeeding for at least 4 days post-administration, pumping and discarding milk during this time. Complete clearance from milk may take up to 2 weeks [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data currently available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No specific clinical trials conducted. Dosing of 5 mg/kg is recommended if use is necessary [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy compared to younger adults; however, the benefit-to-risk ratio must consider age-related comorbidities like diabetes and cardiovascular disease [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Limited experience is available in renally impaired subjects [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources (though hepatic clearance via conjugation is noted in the pharmacology) [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A diagnostic dye that fluoresces yellow-green (520–530 nm) when stimulated by blue light (465–490 nm). This allows for the visualization of retinal vascular structures [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Rapidly distributes; appears in the central artery of the eye within 7–14 seconds of IV injection. Volume of distribution is 0.5 to 0.8 L/kg. Plasma protein binding is moderate (~70–80%) [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly converted to fluorescein monoglucuronide, which retains approximately 1/3 to 1/4 of the fluorescence of the parent compound [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal excretion. Urine remains fluorescent for 24–36 hours. Systemic clearance is typically complete within 48–72 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Data:** Based on a study of 7 healthy subjects receiving 14 mg/kg, showing approximately 80% conversion to glucuronide within one hour [Australian].
- **General Efficacy:** Pivotal trial summaries were not detailed in the available source document.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Handling:** Single-use ampoule; discard any unused portion in accordance with local requirements [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
- **Registration Number:** AUST R 124565 [Australian].
