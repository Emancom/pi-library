# Editorial Product Information: Fosinopril

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of fosinopril sodium for healthcare professionals, synthesized from the available official Product Information (PI).
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference by clinicians and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the United States (FDA) source was available for analysis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Fosinopril sodium (the ester prodrug of the active ACE inhibitor fosinoprilat).
- **Chemical Class:** Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitor containing a phosphinate group.
- **Brand Names:** Fosinopril Sodium [US].
- **ATC Code:** C09AA09 (Note: Not explicitly in source but standard classification).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
  - Oral Tablets: 10 mg, 20 mg, and 40 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Chartwell RX, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypertension:** For the treatment of hypertension in adults and children weighing more than 50 kg. It may be used as monotherapy or in combination with thiazide diuretics [US].
- **Heart Failure:** For the management of heart failure as adjunctive therapy when added to conventional treatments, including diuretics with or without digitalis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Hypertension
- **Adults:**
  - **Initial Dose:** 10 mg once daily, whether used as monotherapy or added to a diuretic.
  - **Maintenance Dose:** Usually 20 mg to 40 mg once daily.
  - **Dose Titration:** Adjust according to blood pressure response at peak (2–6 hours) and trough (24 hours). Some patients may require up to 80 mg daily. If the antihypertensive effect diminishes at the end of the dosing interval, consider dividing the daily dose.
- **Pediatric Patients (>50 kg):**
  - **Recommended Dose:** 5 mg to 10 mg once daily as monotherapy.
  - **Note:** Appropriate dosage strengths for children weighing less than 50 kg are not available from the manufacturer [US].

### Heart Failure
- **Initial Dose:** 10 mg once daily.
- **Initial Dose (High-Risk):** 5 mg once daily is preferred for patients with moderate-to-severe renal failure or those who have undergone vigorous diuresis.
- **Maintenance Dose:** Increase over several weeks to a maximum tolerated dose, not exceeding 40 mg once daily. The typical effective range is 20 mg to 40 mg daily.

### Administration and Missed Doses
- **Instructions:** May be taken without regard to food [US].
- **Concomitant Diuretic Use:** To reduce the risk of hypotension, diuretics should ideally be discontinued 2–3 days prior to starting fosinopril. If the diuretic cannot be stopped, initiate fosinopril under close medical supervision for several hours until blood pressure stabilizes.
- **Antacid Interaction:** Separate administration of fosinopril and antacids by at least 2 hours [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to fosinopril sodium or any other ACE inhibitor (e.g., a history of angioedema with any ACE inhibitor therapy) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning
- **Fetal Toxicity:** Drugs that act on the renin-angiotensin system (RAS) can cause injury and death to the developing fetus. Discontinue fosinopril immediately if pregnancy is detected [US].

### General Warnings
- **Anaphylactoid Reactions and Angioedema:** 
  - **Head and Neck Angioedema:** Can involve the face, extremities, lips, tongue, glottis, or larynx. Laryngeal involvement may be fatal and requires immediate emergency management (e.g., subcutaneous epinephrine 1:1000).
  - **Intestinal Angioedema:** May present as abdominal pain (with or without nausea/vomiting); should be considered in the differential diagnosis of abdominal pain.
  - **Dialysis/Apheresis:** Increased risk of anaphylactoid reactions in patients using high-flux membranes (e.g., AN69) or undergoing LDL apheresis with dextran sulfate.
- **Hypotension:** Most likely in volume- or salt-depleted patients (e.g., those on diuretics, dialysis, or with vomiting/diarrhea). In heart failure, excessive hypotension may lead to oliguria, azotemia, and rarely, acute renal failure.
- **Neutropenia/Agranulocytosis:** Reported with ACE inhibitors (notably captopril); monitoring of white blood cell counts should be considered in patients with collagen-vascular disease, especially if renal function is impaired.
- **Hepatic Failure:** Rarely, ACE inhibitors are associated with a syndrome starting with cholestatic jaundice and progressing to fulminant hepatic necrosis. Discontinue if jaundice or marked liver enzyme elevations occur.

### Precautions and Monitoring
- **Renal Function:** Monitor closely, especially in patients with severe heart failure or renal artery stenosis (bilateral or in a solitary kidney). 
- **Hyperkalemia:** Risk factors include renal insufficiency, diabetes mellitus, and use of potassium-sparing diuretics or supplements. Serum potassium should be monitored.
- **Cough:** A persistent, nonproductive cough is common with ACE inhibitors and resolves after discontinuation.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Generally, adverse events are mild and transient. Frequency does not appear to be dose-related within the recommended range.

| Adverse Effect | Hypertension (n=688) | Heart Failure (n=361) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cough** | 2.2% | 9.7% |
| **Dizziness** | 1.6% | 11.9% |
| **Nausea/Vomiting** | 1.2% | 2.2% |
| **Diarrhea** | <1.0% | 2.2% |
| **Orthostatic Hypotension** | 1.4% | 1.9% |
| **Weakness/Fatigue** | <1.0% | 1.4% |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Angioedema (0.2% in heart failure), hypotension, acute renal failure, neutropenia, and hepatic necrosis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Dual Blockade of the RAS:** Combining ACE inhibitors with ARBs or aliskiren increases the risk of hypotension, hyperkalemia, and renal impairment. Avoid aliskiren in patients with diabetes or GFR <60 mL/min [US].
- **Diuretics:** Potential for additive hypotensive effects.
- **Potassium Supplements/Sparing Diuretics:** Increased risk of hyperkalemia. Use with caution and frequent monitoring.
- **Lithium:** Increased serum lithium levels and toxicity have been reported. Frequent monitoring is recommended.
- **Antacids:** May impair fosinopril absorption. Separate dosing by 2 hours.
- **Gold (Sodium Aurothiomalate):** Nitritoid reactions (flushing, nausea, vomiting, hypotension) may occur more frequently in patients on ACE inhibitors.
- **Laboratory Interactions:** May cause a false low measurement of serum digoxin with certain RIA kits (e.g., Digi-Tab) [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D [US].** Exposure during the second and third trimesters is associated with fetal renal dysfunction, oligohydramnios, fetal lung hypoplasia, skeletal deformations, and death.
- **Breastfeeding:** Fosinoprilat is detectable in breast milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants, fosinopril should not be administered to nursing mothers [US].
- **Fertility:** No adverse reproductive effects were seen in rat studies at doses up to 60 mg/kg/day [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for hypertension in children >50 kg. Safety and efficacy in children <6 years or those with GFR <25 mL/min have not been established [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy were observed between elderly and younger patients, though greater sensitivity in some older individuals cannot be ruled out.
- **Renal Impairment:** Due to dual hepatobiliary and renal excretion, the total body clearance of fosinoprilat remains relatively constant across varying degrees of renal failure (including end-stage). No dosage adjustment is generally needed unless the patient has heart failure or severe reduction in function [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** In patients with cirrhosis, the rate (but not extent) of hydrolysis may be slowed. The total body clearance of fosinoprilat is reduced to approximately half of normal.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Fosinopril is a prodrug that is hydrolyzed by esterases to fosinoprilat. Fosinoprilat competitively inhibits ACE, preventing the conversion of angiotensin I to angiotensin II. This leads to reduced vasopressor activity, decreased aldosterone secretion, and potentially increased levels of bradykinin.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Approximately 36% absorbed; rate (not extent) slowed by food.
  - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (~99.4%). Does not cross the blood-brain barrier.
  - **Metabolism:** Converted to fosinoprilat in the liver and gut wall.
  - **Elimination:** Fosinoprilat is eliminated equally by the liver and kidneys. This dual pathway is unique among ACE inhibitors.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Serum ACE activity is inhibited by ≥90% at 2 to 12 hours after a single dose. Antihypertensive effects begin within 1 hour and persist for 24 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Hypertension:** Placebo-controlled trials showed that doses of 20–80 mg daily lowered systolic/diastolic BP by an average of 8–9/6–7 mmHg at trough. Effects were maintained for at least 2 years.
- **Heart Failure:** Randomized trials in patients receiving diuretics and digitalis demonstrated that fosinopril (10–40 mg) improved exercise tolerance and reduced symptoms of HF (dyspnea, NYHA class) compared to placebo.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C.
- **Handling:** Dispense in a tight container with a child-resistant closure. Protect from moisture [US].
