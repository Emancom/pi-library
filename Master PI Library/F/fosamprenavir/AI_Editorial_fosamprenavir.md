# Editorial Product Information: Fosamprenavir Calcium

**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been compiled by an expert editorial team. It integrates clinical data and regulatory guidance from the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). In instances where recommendations vary between agencies, the more comprehensive or conservative guidance has been provided and attributed accordingly.

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** Fosamprenavir Calcium (Brand Names: Telzir, Lexiva)
**Active Ingredient:** Fosamprenavir calcium.

Each 700 mg tablet contains fosamprenavir calcium, which is the mono-calcium salt of the phosphate ester of amprenavir. Each tablet provides approximately 600 mg of the active moiety, amprenavir.

---

## 2. Approved Indications
Fosamprenavir is a protease inhibitor (PI) indicated for the treatment of Human Immunodeficiency Virus Type 1 (HIV-1) infection in combination with other antiretroviral medications.

*   **Adults:** Indicated for both treatment-naïve and protease inhibitor-experienced patients.
*   **Pediatrics:** Indicated for patients aged 4 weeks and older [FDA]. The TGA approval specifically covers children and adolescents from 6 years of age and older.

**Clinical Considerations:**
In adults previously treated with protease inhibitors, fosamprenavir (when boosted with ritonavir) has not demonstrated equivalent efficacy to the lopinavir/ritonavir combination [TGA].

---

## 3. Contraindications
Fosamprenavir is contraindicated in:
*   Patients with a history of clinically significant hypersensitivity to fosamprenavir, amprenavir, or ritonavir (when co-administered).
*   Concurrent use of drugs highly dependent on CYP3A4 for clearance, where elevated levels may lead to life-threatening events. These include:
    *   **Alpha 1-adrenoreceptor antagonists:** Alfuzosin.
    *   **Antiarrhythmics:** Flecainide and propafenone (when used with ritonavir).
    *   **Antipsychotics/Neuroleptics:** Lurasidone (when used with ritonavir), pimozide.
    *   **Ergot derivatives:** Dihydroergotamine, ergotamine, ergonovine, methylergonovine.
    *   **GI motility agents:** Cisapride.
    *   **Lipid-modifying agents:** Lovastatin, simvastatin, lomitapide [FDA].
    *   **PDE5 inhibitors:** Sildenafil (specifically when used for pulmonary arterial hypertension).
    *   **Sedatives/Hypnotics:** Triazolam, orally administered midazolam.
    *   **Antimycobacterials:** Rifampin (due to significant reduction in amprenavir levels).
    *   **Herbal products:** St. John’s wort (*Hypericum perforatum*).
    *   **NNRTIs:** Delavirdine.

---

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Skin Reactions:** Severe or life-threatening rashes, including Stevens-Johnson Syndrome, have been reported in <1% of patients. Treatment must be discontinued permanently for severe rashes or moderate rashes accompanied by systemic symptoms.
*   **Sulfa Allergy:** Fosamprenavir contains a sulfonamide moiety. While cross-reactivity is not established, the drug should be used with caution in patients with known sulfonamide allergies.
*   **Hepatic Toxicity:** Use of higher-than-approved doses of the ritonavir-boosted regimen has resulted in transaminase elevations. Patients with Hepatitis B or C are at increased risk for hepatic adverse events.
*   **Diabetes and Hyperglycemia:** New-onset diabetes or exacerbation of pre-existing diabetes and hyperglycemia have been reported with protease inhibitor therapy.
*   **Immune Reconstitution Syndrome:** Inflammatory responses to residual opportunistic infections (e.g., *MAC*, CMV, PCP) may occur during the initial phase of treatment. Autoimmune disorders (e.g., Graves' disease) may also occur.
*   **Lipid Elevations:** Increases in total cholesterol and triglycerides are common. Testing should be performed prior to therapy and monitored regularly.
*   **Hemophilia:** Increased spontaneous bleeding has been reported in patients with hemophilia A and B.
*   **Nephrolithiasis:** Cases of kidney stones have been reported during post-marketing surveillance [FDA].
*   **Hemolytic Anemia:** Rare cases of acute hemolytic anemia have been reported with amprenavir.

## 5. Dosage and Administration
Therapy should be initiated by a physician experienced in HIV management. The tablets may be taken with or without food.

### 5.1 Adult Dosing
*   **Antiretroviral-Naïve Patients:**
    *   1400 mg once daily (without ritonavir) [FDA].
    *   1400 mg once daily plus 200 mg ritonavir once daily.
    *   1400 mg once daily plus 100 mg ritonavir once daily [FDA].
    *   700 mg twice daily plus 100 mg ritonavir twice daily.
*   **Protease Inhibitor-Experienced Patients:**
    *   700 mg twice daily plus 100 mg ritonavir twice daily. Once-daily dosing is not recommended in this population.

### 5.2 Pediatric Dosing (4 weeks to 18 years)
Dosage should be weight-based (mg/kg) and must not exceed the maximum adult dose. Tablets may be used for patients weighing at least 39 kg who can swallow them whole. For younger children or those unable to swallow tablets, an oral suspension is required. Dosing regimens range from 18 mg/kg to 45 mg/kg depending on weight and ritonavir use [FDA].

### 5.3 Special Populations
*   **Renal Impairment:** No initial dose adjustment is required.
*   **Hepatic Impairment:**
    *   **Mild (Child-Pugh 5-6):** 700 mg twice daily with 100 mg ritonavir once daily.
    *   **Moderate (Child-Pugh 7-9):** 450 mg twice daily with 100 mg ritonavir once daily [FDA]. The TGA notes that the 700 mg tablet cannot achieve the required reduced dose for moderate/severe impairment.
    *   **Severe (Child-Pugh 10-15):** 300 mg twice daily with 100 mg ritonavir once daily [FDA].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most common adverse reactions ($\ge$4%) in adults include:
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain.
*   **Nervous System:** Headache, dizziness, oral paraesthesia.
*   **Skin:** Rash.
*   **General:** Fatigue.

**Pediatric-specific findings:** Vomiting and neutropenia occur more frequently in pediatric patients compared to adults [FDA].

**Laboratory Abnormalities:** Grade 3 or 4 elevations in ALT, AST, serum lipase, triglycerides, and cholesterol have been observed.

---

## 7. Drug Interactions
Fosamprenavir (via its metabolite amprenavir) is a substrate for and an inducer/inhibitor of CYP3A4. When co-administered with ritonavir, the interaction profile is also influenced by ritonavir's potent inhibition of CYP3A4 and CYP2D6.

*   **PDE5 Inhibitors:** Use with caution and reduced doses (e.g., sildenafil 25 mg every 48 hours) for erectile dysfunction.
*   **HMG-CoA Reductase Inhibitors:** Atorvastatin dose should not exceed 20 mg/day. Pravastatin and fluvastatin are preferred alternatives.
*   **Corticosteroids:** Fluticasone co-administration with ritonavir-boosted fosamprenavir is not recommended due to the risk of Cushing's syndrome and adrenal suppression.
*   **HCV Antiviral Agents:** Co-administration with boceprevir, simeprevir, or telaprevir is not recommended due to altered plasma concentrations and potential loss of virologic response.

---

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### 8.1 Pregnancy
*   **Regulatory Category:** Category B3 [TGA].
*   **Clinical Guidance:** Use during pregnancy only if the potential benefit outweighs the risk to the fetus. Dosing of 700 mg twice daily with 100 mg ritonavir twice daily should only be considered for pregnant patients already stable on this regimen with a viral load <50 copies/mL [FDA].
*   **Registry:** Healthcare providers are encouraged to register pregnant patients with the Antiretroviral Pregnancy Registry (APR).

### 8.2 Breastfeeding
HIV-infected mothers should be instructed **not** to breastfeed to avoid the risk of postnatal HIV transmission and potential adverse reactions in the infant.

---

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Fosamprenavir is a prodrug rapidly converted to amprenavir by gut epithelium phosphatases during absorption.
*   **Distribution:** Amprenavir is approximately 90% protein-bound, primarily to alpha1-acid glycoprotein (AAG).
*   **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized in the liver via the CYP3A4 enzyme system. Ritonavir increases amprenavir concentrations by inhibiting this pathway.
*   **Elimination:** The plasma half-life of amprenavir is approximately 7.7 hours. Excretion is primarily fecal (~75%) with minimal renal excretion (~14%).

---

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Fosamprenavir is a prodrug of amprenavir, a non-peptide competitive inhibitor of the HIV-1 protease enzyme. By binding to the active site of the protease, it prevents the cleavage of viral Gag and Gag-Pol polyprotein precursors. This results in the formation of immature, non-infectious viral particles, thereby halting the viral replication cycle.

---

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Tablets:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [FDA]. The TGA recommends storage below 30°C. To ensure maximum stability, store in the original container and keep tightly closed to protect from moisture.
