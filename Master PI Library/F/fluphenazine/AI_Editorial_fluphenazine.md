# Editorial Product Information: Fluphenazine Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of fluphenazine hydrochloride, synthesised from official product information provided by the US Food and Drug Administration. 
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for use by healthcare professionals and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Fluphenazine hydrochloride, a trifluoromethyl phenothiazine derivative.
*   **Brand Names:** Specific brand names are not listed in the provided source; the product is identified as **Fluphenazine Hydrochloride Tablets, USP**.
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    *   **Tablets (Oral):** 1 mg, 2.5 mg, 5 mg, and 10 mg [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Oral.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** RemedyRepack Inc. (repackager/distributor); Rising Pharma Holdings, Inc. (manufacturer) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Management of Psychotic Disorders:** Indicated for the management of manifestations of psychotic disorders [US].
*   **Clinical Limitations:**
    *   Not shown to be effective for behavioral complications in patients with mental retardation [US].
    *   Not approved for the treatment of patients with dementia-related psychosis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
*   **Initial Dosage:** Usually ranges from 2.5 mg to 10 mg per day.
*   **Administration:** The total daily dose should be divided and administered at 6 to 8-hour intervals.
*   **Maintenance:** Once symptoms are controlled, the dosage can often be reduced to a maintenance level of 1 mg to 5 mg daily, frequently administered as a single daily dose [US].
*   **Maximum Dose:** Daily doses up to 40 mg may be required for severely disturbed patients, though safety for prolonged administration at this level has not been established via controlled clinical studies [US].

### Titration and Conversion
*   **Initial Approach:** Treatment should begin with a low dose and be titrated upward until the desired clinical effect is reached. Most patients achieve a therapeutic effect at doses under 20 mg daily [US].
*   **Conversion to Injectable:** For patients stabilized on oral fluphenazine, conversion to long-acting fluphenazine decanoate may be indicated [US].

### Geriatric Dosing
*   **Suggested Starting Dose:** 1 mg to 2.5 mg daily, with subsequent adjustments based on patient response [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to fluphenazine; cross-sensitivity to other phenothiazine derivatives may occur [US].
*   **Central Nervous System:** 
    *   Suspected or established subcortical brain damage [US].
    *   Comatose or severely depressed states [US].
    *   Concurrent use of large doses of hypnotics [US].
*   **Organ Dysfunction:**
    *   Presence of liver damage [US].
    *   Presence of blood dyscrasias [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning [US]
*   **Increased Mortality in Elderly Patients with Dementia-Related Psychosis:** Elderly patients treated with antipsychotic drugs for dementia-related psychosis are at an increased risk of death (1.6 to 1.7 times the risk of placebo). Most deaths are cardiovascular or infectious in nature. Fluphenazine is not approved for this use.

### General Warnings
*   **Tardive Dyskinesia:** A potentially irreversible and involuntary dyskinetic syndrome. Risk increases with treatment duration and cumulative dose, though it can occur at low doses after brief periods. Discontinuation should be considered if signs appear [US].
*   **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** A potentially fatal complex characterized by hyperpyrexia, muscle rigidity, altered mental status, and autonomic instability. Immediate discontinuation of the drug is required [US].
*   **Falls:** May cause somnolence, motor instability, and postural hypotension, leading to fractures or other injuries. Fall risk assessments are recommended [US].
*   **Hematologic Effects:** Cases of leukopenia, neutropenia, and agranulocytosis (sometimes fatal) have been reported. Frequent CBC monitoring is required during the first few months of therapy if the patient has a history of low WBC or drug-induced leukopenia [US].

### Clinical Precautions
*   **CNS Impairment:** May impair mental and physical abilities required for driving or operating heavy machinery [US].
*   **Hypotension:** Use with caution in patients with pheochromocytoma, cerebrovascular or renal insufficiency, or severe cardiac reserve deficiency (e.g., mitral insufficiency) [US].
*   **Medical Conditions:** Use cautiously in patients with a history of convulsive disorders or those exposed to extreme heat or phosphorus insecticides [US].
*   **Prolactin Levels:** Elevates prolactin; approximately one-third of human breast cancers are prolactin-dependent *in vitro* [US].
*   **Ocular Effects:** Long-term use may lead to pigmentary retinopathy, lenticular and corneal deposits [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common and Serious Effects
*   **Extrapyramidal Symptoms (EPS):** Most frequently reported. Includes pseudoparkinsonism, dystonia, dyskinesia, akathisia, oculogyric crises, and hyperreflexia [US].
*   **Autonomic Nervous System:** Hypertension, blood pressure fluctuations, nausea, loss of appetite, dry mouth, headache, constipation, polyuria, blurred vision, and tachycardia [US].
*   **Endocrine/Metabolic:** Weight change, peripheral edema, gynecomastia, abnormal lactation, menstrual irregularities, and impotence in men [US].
*   **Hepatic:** Cholestatic jaundice, typically appearing within the first months of therapy [US].
*   **Allergic:** Itching, erythema, urticaria, seborrhea, photosensitivity, and exfoliative dermatitis [US].
*   **Hematologic:** Agranulocytosis, leukopenia, thrombocytopenic or nonthrombocytopenic purpura, and pancytopenia [US].
*   **Other:** Sudden, unexpected death has been reported in hospitalized psychotic patients [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **CNS Depressants:** Potentiation of alcohol, opiates, analgesics, antihistamines, and barbiturates may occur [US].
*   **Anticholinergics:** The effects of atropine may be potentiated due to additive anticholinergic activity [US].
*   **Epinephrine:** Phenothiazines may reverse the action of epinephrine, leading to a further lowering of blood pressure; epinephrine should **not** be used to treat fluphenazine-induced hypotension [US].
*   **Anesthetics:** Reduced amounts of anesthetics may be necessary in patients on large doses of phenothiazines [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   **Risk Summary:** Safety has not been established. Benefit must outweigh potential risk [US].
    *   **Non-teratogenic Effects:** Neonates exposed to antipsychotics during the third trimester are at risk for EPS and/or withdrawal symptoms (e.g., agitation, hypertonia, tremor, respiratory distress) following delivery [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Fertility:** Impotence in men and increased libido in women have been reported with phenothiazine therapy [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children have not been established [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** See Boxed Warning regarding dementia. Increased risk of tardive dyskinesia in elderly women. Lower starting doses (1 mg to 2.5 mg) are recommended [US].
*   **Renal Impairment:** Monitor renal function during long-term therapy; if BUN becomes abnormal, treatment should be discontinued [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in the presence of liver damage [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** A trifluoromethyl phenothiazine derivative with activity at all levels of the central nervous system. The exact mechanism of therapeutic action is unknown [US].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption/Distribution:** Not specified in available sources.
    *   **Metabolism/Elimination:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Pharmacodynamics:** Exhibits potent antipsychotic effects and may produce significant extrapyramidal symptoms [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Dementia-Related Psychosis:** Meta-analyses of 17 placebo-controlled trials (modal duration 10 weeks) identified the increased mortality risk in elderly patients [US].
*   **General Psychosis:** Safety and efficacy are based on established clinical use; specific pivotal trial data for the original approval are not detailed in the current US PI [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15° to 30°C (59° to 86°F) [US].
*   **Handling:** Protect from light. Avoid excessive heat. Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container with a child-resistant closure [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription Only) [US].
