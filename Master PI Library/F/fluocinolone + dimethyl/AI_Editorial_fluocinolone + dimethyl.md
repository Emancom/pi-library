# Editorial Product Information: fluocinolone + dimethyl

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **fluocinolone + dimethyl**
- Chemical class:
  - Fluocinolone acetonide — topical corticosteroid (synthetic glucocorticoid)
  - Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) — alkyl sulfoxide solvent with penetration-enhancing properties
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Auricular (otic)

## 2. Regulatory History
- First approved / marketing start date (as stated in source): 07/20/1972
- Last revision(s) of the source PI as stated in the source document:
  - Revised: August 2024
  - Revised: May 2025
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Relief of pruritus and inflammation associated with acute and chronic otitis in the dog.
  - Indication limited to topical application in the ear canal of dogs.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
- General dosing (clinical recommendation from source):
  - 4 to 6 drops (approximately 0.2 mL) per ear, administered twice daily into the ear canal.
  - Maximum recommended duration: 14 days.
  - Cumulative total dosage limit as stated: should not exceed 17 mL.
- Dosing by indication:
  - Indication-specific dosing is limited to treatment of pruritus and inflammation in acute and chronic canine otitis as above.
- Administration instructions:
  - Cleanse the affected ear canal by an appropriate method prior to instillation.
  - Instill the recommended number of drops into the ear canal; gentle external massage of the ear canal after dosing may aid distribution.
  - Avoid contact of the product with the animal’s eyes.
  - Avoid direct contact of bare hands with the product.
  - If hearing loss or vestibular dysfunction is noted during treatment, discontinue use immediately and flush the ear canal thoroughly with a nonototoxic solution.
- Titration schedules: Not specified in available sources
- Missed dose instructions: Not specified in available sources
- Route restriction:
  - For topical (otic) use only; do not administer by any other route.
  - Recommended for use in dogs only (topical application to the ear canal).

## 5. Contraindications
- Contraindicated wherever systemic corticosteroid therapy would be dangerous.
- Preparations containing DMSO should not be used in pregnant animals; DMSO has shown teratogenic and embryotoxic effects in some species (chick embryos, guinea pigs).
- Not to be administered by any route other than topical auricular; recommended for canine ear canal only.
- Other contraindications explicitly stated in source documents: Not specified beyond the items above.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- Boxed / major warning:
  - **Use in pregnancy**: Corticosteroids (systemic or parenteral) administered to pregnant animals may induce parturition if used during the last trimester and may precipitate premature parturition with associated complications (dystocia, fetal death, retained placenta, metritis). Corticosteroid exposure during pregnancy has been associated with congenital malformations (including cleft palate and other anomalies) in some species. Preparations containing DMSO have demonstrated teratogenic and embryotoxic effects in certain species; do not use in pregnant animals.
- General warnings and precautions:
  - Evaluate and follow infected ears carefully. Lack of response or worsening may reflect an infection requiring identification and appropriate antimicrobial therapy (including sensitivity testing) before or concurrent with corticosteroid use.
  - Do not treat animals with a generalized infection with this product unless appropriate systemic or local antimicrobial/supportive therapy is used.
  - DMSO enhances percutaneous absorption of other compounds (including corticosteroids and many topical/systemic agents); clinicians should be aware of the potential for increased systemic exposure and potentiation of other drugs.
  - DMSO may potentiate effects of concurrently administered systemic medications, particularly those affecting the cardiovascular and central nervous systems; use caution when co-administered.
  - Other topical medications should not be present at the site of application when this product is applied; if other topical agents are indicated, they should not be applied until the DMSO-containing preparation is thoroughly dry.
  - DMSO is a potent solvent and may damage fabrics, plastics, and other materials — avoid contact with such materials until the treated area is dry.
  - Hearing loss and vestibular dysfunction have been reported; discontinue and flush with a nonototoxic solution if these occur.
- Monitoring requirements:
  - Monitor for local adverse reactions (stinging, increased local temperature, signs of sensitization).
  - Monitor for systemic corticosteroid effects (signs of adrenal suppression, weight loss, increased susceptibility to infections).
  - Monitor hepatic/renal function or other laboratory parameters only if clinically indicated — specific laboratory monitoring schedules are not specified in available sources.
- Specific clinical precautions:
  - Avoid contact with eyes and avoid handling product with bare hands.
  - Do not exceed recommended dose or duration; systemic effects have been observed with higher or excessive exposure, particularly when DMSO facilitates increased absorption.

  - Teratogenic/congenital effects when corticosteroids are used in pregnancy; DMSO embryotoxicity/teratogenicity in some species.
  - Ocular lens changes/cataracts noted in animals after high-dose or prolonged oral DMSO exposure.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Pharmacodynamic / absorption interactions:
  - DMSO enhances percutaneous absorption of many substances; concurrent topical or systemic drugs applied at or near the site may have increased absorption and effect.
  - DMSO has been shown to enhance absorption of steroids, vasoconstrictors, antiperspirants, dyes, thiabendazole, hexachlorophene and others in experimental settings.
  - DMSO may potentiate systemically administered drugs, particularly those affecting the cardiovascular and central nervous systems; exercise caution when co-administering such agents.
- Practical management notes:
  - Do not apply other topical medications at the treatment site at the same time; wait until the DMSO-containing preparation is thoroughly dry before applying other topicals.
  - Evaluate co-administered systemic therapies for potential potentiation by enhanced absorption (no quantitative interaction data provided).
- Pharmacokinetic interactions: Not specified in available sources (no CYP, transporter, or specific PK interaction parameters provided).

## 11. Pharmacology
- Mechanism of action:
  - Fluocinolone acetonide: a synthetic glucocorticoid with marked topical anti-inflammatory activity; reduces edema, inflammation, erythema, cellular infiltration and pruritus. Described to have greater than 100-fold anti-inflammatory potency compared with hydrocortisone in experimental settings.
  - Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO): a polar solvent with strong penetration-enhancing properties that increases percutaneous absorption of many compounds; also acts as a solvent for a broad range of polar and nonpolar substances.
- Pharmacokinetics:
  - Absorption: Systemic absorption of fluocinolone acetonide from the ear canal has been demonstrated (evidence: reversible adrenal changes, weight loss in treated dogs indicative of systemic corticosteroid exposure). DMSO enhances percutaneous absorption and can increase systemic uptake of co-administered compounds.
  - Distribution, metabolism, elimination: Specific clinical pharmacokinetic parameters (plasma half-life, volume of distribution, metabolic pathways, routes of elimination) are not specified in available sources.
- Pharmacodynamics:
  - Anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive effects consistent with topical corticosteroid class. DMSO potentiates dermal/aural absorption and may enable systemic pharmacodynamic effects from topically applied corticosteroids.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Key trial / study findings described in available sources:
  - Safety/toxicology studies in dogs:
    - Dogs treated at 12 drops (0.4 mL) per ear daily for 21 consecutive days (a regimen used in a study) had no significant changes in clinical chemistry, urinalysis, or hematology but showed reversible adrenal cortical atrophy; ACTH stimulation tests suggested reversible adrenal suppression with recovery after one week off therapy.
    - Dogs treated at higher volumes (e.g., 15 drops/ear/day for 7 days; 45 drops/ear/day for 21 days) showed small to slight decreases in body weight and adrenal changes consistent with systemic corticosteroid exposure—indicating absorption from the ear canal and potentiation by DMSO.
  - Toxicology of DMSO:
    - Oral DMSO in animals at high doses has been associated with lens refractive changes and nuclear cataracts; topical high-concentration DMSO in a dog study (90% solution, 20–60 mL/day for 21 days) did not show clinically meaningful ophthalmological effects in that study.
  - Efficacy trials:
