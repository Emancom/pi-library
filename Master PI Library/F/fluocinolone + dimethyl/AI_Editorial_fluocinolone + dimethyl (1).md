# Editorial Product Information: fluocinolone + dimethyl

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **fluocinolone + dimethyl**
- Chemical class:
  - Fluocinolone (fluocinolone acetonide): synthetic corticosteroid (topical glucocorticoid)
  - Dimethyl (dimethyl sulfoxide, DMSO): organic sulfoxide solvent and percutaneous absorption enhancer
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Auricular (otic) — topical application to the ear canal (dog)

## 2. Regulatory History
- [US] Original approval: NADA 045-512 — 07/20/1972
- Source PI revision dates reported in available materials: August 2024 and May 2025
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources
- Other jurisdictions' registration dates: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Relief of pruritus and inflammation associated with acute and chronic otitis in the dog (indicated use is limited to the auricular canal of dogs).
- Use limited to veterinary (animal) practice; product is restricted for use by or on the order of a licensed veterinarian in the United States [US].

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (dog)
- Recommended dose: 4–6 drops (~0.2 mL) per ear administered twice daily.
- Maximum recommended duration: 14 days.
- Maximum total product volume per course: Do not exceed 17 mL (total cumulative dose).

### Dosing observations from non-clinical studies (informational; not recommended therapeutic regimens)
- Single‑daily administration of 12 drops (0.4 mL) per ear for 21 days was used in a toxicity study.
- Higher experimental exposures reported in toxicity studies (not therapeutic recommendations): 15 drops (0.5 mL)/ear/day for 7 days and 1.5 mL (45 drops)/ear/day for 21 days produced systemic effects.

### Administration instructions
- Cleanse the affected ear canal by an appropriate method prior to instillation.
- Instill recommended number of drops into the ear canal; gentle external massage of the ear canal after instillation may aid distribution.
- Avoid contact of the product with the animal’s eyes.
- Avoid bare-hand contact with the product; DMSO enhances skin penetration and may transfer contaminants or the drug into human skin.
- If hearing loss or vestibular dysfunction is noted during treatment, discontinue product immediately and flush the ear canal thoroughly with a non-ototoxic solution.

### Titration / missed dose
- Titration schedules: Not specified in available sources.
- Missed dose instructions: Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Do not administer by any route other than topical auricular application to the ear canal of the dog.
- Preparations containing DMSO should not be used in pregnant animals because studies in chick embryos and guinea pigs showed teratogenicity and embryotoxicity.
- Contraindicated wherever systemic corticosteroid administration would be dangerous (i.e., conditions in which systemic glucocorticoids are contraindicated).
- Animals with generalized infection should not be treated with this product without appropriate supportive antimicrobial therapy.
- Use is recommended only for topical application to the ear canal of the dog; use in other species or for other routes is contraindicated or not recommended in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed/Highlighted warnings
- [US] Federal restriction: Product is for veterinary use and is restricted to use by or on the order of a licensed veterinarian.

### General warnings and precautions
- Systemic absorption: DMSO enhances percutaneous absorption of co-applied substances and the corticosteroid component; systemic corticosteroid effects (including adrenal suppression and weight loss) may occur, especially with elevated doses or prolonged use.
- Adrenal effects: Monitor for signs of adrenal suppression; corticosteroid systemic effects may be reversible on withdrawal.
- Infection: Corticosteroid therapy can mask or exacerbate infections; perform careful initial evaluation of infected ears and follow up. If incomplete response or worsening occurs, identify the infecting organism and consider antimicrobial sensitivity testing and appropriate antimicrobial therapy.
- Pregnancy: Avoid use in pregnant animals due to risks associated with both corticosteroids (may induce parturition and produce congenital anomalies in animal studies) and DMSO (teratogenic/embryotoxic in some species).
- Local irritation/sensitization: Local reactions may represent sensitization to the corticosteroid or vehicle.
- Hearing/vestibular risk: Hearing loss and vestibular dysfunction have been reported in a small number of dogs; discontinue and irrigate ear canal if these occur.
- Human exposure: Avoid contact of product with human skin and eyes; DMSO facilitates absorption through human skin and may carry contaminants. Prevent contact with fabrics, plastics and other materials as DMSO is a potent solvent.
- Concomitant topical medications: Other topical medications should not be present at the application site; do not apply other topical agents until the DMSO-containing product has thoroughly dried.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor treated animals for:
  - Signs of adrenal suppression (e.g., decreased responsiveness to ACTH testing in study settings)
  - Weight loss or failure to gain weight
  - Increased susceptibility or worsening of infections
  - Local skin/ear reactions or sensitization
  - Auditory or vestibular changes (hearing loss, abnormal gait, head tilt)
  - Unusual breath odor (garlic/oyster-like) and taste related to DMSO absorption

### Specific clinical precautions
- Exercise caution when animals are receiving concomitant systemic medications, especially those affecting the cardiovascular and central nervous systems, as DMSO may potentiate effects.
- Prevent contact of the applied area with fabrics and plastics until drying has occurred.
- In the presence of local and/or systemic side effects, withdraw the drug; local therapy can often be resumed at a lower dose once systemic signs abate (based on study observations).

## 7. Adverse Effects
- All adverse effects listed below are taken from available source material. Frequency estimates are not provided in the sources; where frequency is not specified, the table notes "Not specified in available sources."

### Summary of reported adverse effects
- Local transient mild stinging on denuded areas
- Temporary increase in local temperature at application site
- Hearing loss and vestibular dysfunction (reported in a small number of animals)
- Odorous breath (garlic- or oyster-like) and unpleasant taste following topical application of DMSO
- Adrenal suppression and adrenal cortical atrophy (reversible)
- Weight loss; decreased body weight
- Increased susceptibility to infections
- Local sensitization or local adverse reactions
- Diarrhea and intestinal inflammation (reported with oral/ingested fluocinolone in animal studies)
- Ocular lens changes and nuclear cataracts (observed in animals with high-dose oral DMSO exposure)
- Hematologic changes observed in toxicology studies: lymphocytopenia (general finding), erythrocytosis in cats in one study

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic / pharmacokinetic interaction potential
- DMSO markedly enhances percutaneous absorption of topically applied compounds and may increase systemic exposure to concomitantly applied topical agents and to the corticosteroid itself.
- DMSO may potentiate effects of concurrently administered systemic drugs (notably those affecting the cardiovascular and central nervous systems).

### Specific examples (from experimental/clinical observations)
- DMSO has been shown to enhance topical/percutaneous absorption of steroids, vasoconstrictors, antiperspirants, dyes, thiabendazole (anthelmintic), and hexachlorophene (antiseptic).
- Medications containing DMSO should be used judiciously when animals are receiving other pharmaceutical preparations, especially cardioactive and CNS-active agents.

### Management notes
- Avoid applying other topical medications at the site of application until the DMSO-containing product has thoroughly dried.
- Exercise caution and monitor closely if the animal is receiving concurrent systemic therapies with narrow therapeutic indices or significant CNS/cardiovascular effects.
- If additional topical therapy is required, delay until the DMSO formulation has dried to reduce risk of enhanced absorption.

### Jurisdiction-specific interactions
- No region-specific interaction differences specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Corticosteroid risks: Clinical and experimental data demonstrate that corticosteroids administered systemically to animals may induce parturition if used during the last trimester and may cause premature parturition, dystocia, fetal death, retained placenta, metritis, and congenital anomalies (e.g., cleft palate, deformed forelegs, phocomelia, anasarca) in animal studies.
- DMSO risks: Preparations containing DMSO should not be used in pregnant animals; studies in chick embryos and guinea pigs indicate teratogenicity and embryotoxicity.
- Recommendation: Avoid use in pregnant animals due to combined risks of corticosteroid and DMSO exposure.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Fluocinolone (fluocinolone acetonide): a potent topical glucocorticoid that reduces inflammation by decreasing edema, erythema, cellular infiltration and pruritus associated with inflammatory dermatoses; reported to have markedly greater anti‑inflammatory potency than hydrocortisone (~100× in comparative assays).
- Dimethyl (DMSO): a polar organic solvent that acts as a percutaneous absorption enhancer; facilitates transport of co-applied compounds across skin and mucosa and can potentiate local and systemic absorption of drugs applied to the treated area.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Anti-inflammatory actions of fluocinolone lead to reduced signs of allergic or inflammatory otitis (pruritus, erythema, edema).
- DMSO can permit topically applied steroids to exert systemic effects at elevated dosages or with prolonged exposure, demonstrated by adrenal suppression and other corticosteroid-related systemic effects in animal studies.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: Systemic absorption from auricular application has been demonstrated in animal studies (evidence: reversible adrenal cortical atrophy, weight changes), and DMSO increases percutaneous absorption.
- Distribution/metabolism/elimination: Detailed pharmacokinetic parameters (Cmax, Tmax, half-life, metabolic pathways, excretion) are not specified in available sources.
- Note: Systemic absorption of DMSO can produce a characteristic garlic/oyster-like breath odor and taste.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Pivotal clinical trial data for efficacy/safety in field populations are not described in the available sources.
- Available evidence in the product literature is comprised of:
  - Pharmacology and absorption-enhancement studies (historical references demonstrating DMSO-enhanced percutaneous absorption of various compounds).
  - Toxicology and safety studies in animals including:
    - Oral DMSO in dogs at high daily doses produced lens changes and nuclear cataracts over weeks to months; topical high-concentration DMSO applied to dogs (90% solution at total daily dose 20–60 mL for 21 days) did not produce clinically meaningful ophthalmologic effects in one study.
    - Fluocinolone acetonide fed to dogs caused weight loss and intestinal inflammation at higher dietary doses; repeated topical/otic administration at therapeutic and supra-therapeutic exposures produced reversible adrenal cortical changes consistent with systemic corticosteroid exposure.
    - Topical application of fluocinolone acetonide in DMSO enhanced systemic corticosteroid effects in immature animal models for other steroids (demonstrating the absorption-enhancing property of DMSO).
- Trial designs, primary endpoints, randomized controlled data, and numerical efficacy results are not specified in available sources.
