# Editorial Product Information: Fluorometholone

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of fluorometholone, synthesising clinical data for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the local approved PI for definitive regulatory guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Fluorometholone (a topical anti-inflammatory glucosteroid).
- **Brand Name(s):** **FML** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Ophthalmic suspension (eye drops) containing 1 mg/mL (0.1%) fluorometholone [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Ocular (topical).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AbbVie Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- Treatment of steroid-responsive inflammatory conditions of the palpebral and bulbar conjunctiva, cornea, and the anterior segment of the globe [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** Instil 1 to 2 drops into the conjunctival sac two to four times daily.
- **Initial Intensive Dosing:** During the first 24 to 48 hours, the frequency may be increased to 2 drops every hour [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - The bottle must be shaken well before use.
    - To reduce systemic absorption, apply pressure to the tear duct (nasolacrimal occlusion) immediately following administration.
    - To avoid contamination, ensure the dropper tip does not touch the eye or any other surface.
- **Duration of Therapy:** Treatment should not be discontinued prematurely. However, use should not exceed 10 days except under strict ophthalmic supervision [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
Fluorometholone is contraindicated in the following conditions [Australian]:
- Acute epithelial (superficial) *Herpes simplex* keratitis (dendritic keratitis).
- Fungal diseases of the ocular structures.
- Viral diseases of the cornea and conjunctiva, including vaccinia and varicella.
- Mycobacterial infections of the eye.
- Tuberculosis of the eye.
- Known hypersensitivity to any component of the formulation.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified.
- **General Warnings:**
    - **Duration Limit:** Use for more than 10 days requires regular intraocular pressure (IOP) monitoring and strict ophthalmic supervision [Australian].
    - **Herpes Simplex History:** Use with extreme caution in patients with a history of *Herpes simplex* keratitis; frequent slit-lamp microscopy is mandatory.
    - **Corticosteroid-Induced Effects:** Prolonged use may result in increased IOP (glaucoma), optic nerve damage, visual field defects, and posterior subcapsular cataract formation.
    - **Infection Risk:** Steroids may mask or enhance acute purulent untreated infections. Prolonged use may encourage secondary fungal or viral ocular infections. Fungal invasion should be suspected in any persistent corneal ulceration.
    - **Tissue Thinning:** In conditions causing thinning of the cornea or sclera, topical steroids may lead to perforation.
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Frequent monitoring of IOP is required, especially in patients with a history of glaucoma.
- **Visual Disturbances:** Patients reporting blurred vision or visual disturbances should be evaluated for cataracts, glaucoma, or rare conditions such as central serous chorioretinopathy (CSCR) [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by system organ class below. Frequencies were not explicitly categorised by percentage in the source material [Australian].

| System Organ Class | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Eye Disorders** | Elevated IOP, glaucoma, optic nerve damage, cataract (including posterior subcapsular), visual acuity/field defects, blurred vision, eye irritation, conjunctival/ocular hyperaemia, eye pain, foreign body sensation, eyelid oedema, eyelid ptosis, discharge, pruritus, increased lacrimation, mydriasis, ulcerative keratitis, punctate keratitis, secondary infection, and globe perforation (in cases of tissue thinning). |
| **Immune System** | Hypersensitivity reactions. |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Dysgeusia (bitter taste). |
| **Skin/Subcutaneous** | Rash, delayed wound healing. |
| **General/Systemic** | Rare occurrences of systemic hypercorticoidism. |

*Note: Cases of corneal calcification have been reported very rarely in association with the phosphate component of the drops in patients with significant corneal damage [Australian].*

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** 
    - **CYP3A Inhibitors:** While systemic exposure is expected to be low, concurrent treatment with CYP3A inhibitors may increase the risk of systemic corticosteroid-related side effects [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B3.** 
    - Animal studies (rabbits) have shown fluorometholone to be teratogenic, fetotoxic, and embryocidal when administered ocularly at doses approximating the human dose. 
    - Use is only recommended if the potential benefit outweighs the potential risk to the fetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if topical ocular administration results in sufficient systemic absorption to produce detectable levels in human milk. Because systemically absorbed steroids can suppress growth or interfere with endogenous production, a decision must be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug, based on the clinical importance of the medication to the mother [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available in source documents.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in children under 2 years of age [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available in source documents.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No specific data or dose adjustments specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Fluorometholone is a corticosteroid that inhibits the inflammatory response (mechanical, chemical, or immunological). It is thought to act by inducing lipocortins (phospholipase A2 inhibitory proteins), which inhibit the release of arachidonic acid, thereby controlling the biosynthesis of prostaglandins and leukotrienes [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Fluorometholone has demonstrated a lower propensity to increase intraocular pressure compared to dexamethasone in clinical studies [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C. Protect from freezing. Store the bottle in an upright position [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** 18 months.
- **In-use Stability:** Discard any unused contents 4 weeks after opening the bottle [Australian].
