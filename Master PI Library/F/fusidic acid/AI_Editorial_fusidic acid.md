# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonized editorial overview of Levocarnitine Injection, synthesized for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** A naturally occurring carrier molecule/amino acid derivative involved in fatty acid metabolism.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic management of patients with metabolic defects resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Metabolic Disorders)
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
- **Acute Management:** In cases of severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose may be administered, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) via IV injection or infusion [US].
- **Dose Range:** Subsequent daily doses typically fall within the 50 mg/kg range, though therapy must be individualized. The maximum recorded dose is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus (2–3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Monitoring
- **Metabolic Patients:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- **Dialysis Patients:** Therapy is often prompted by trough plasma concentrations falling below the normal range of 40–50 µmol/L [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, and laryngeal edema have been reported, primarily in dialysis patients. Some reactions occurred within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued immediately [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **INR Monitoring:** Increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) has been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. Monitor INR levels upon initiation or dose adjustment [US].
- **Product Inspection:** Parenteral products must be visually inspected for discoloration or particulate matter prior to use [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common Effects:** Transient nausea and vomiting are frequently observed. Less common effects include body odor and gastritis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in INR when used concurrently with levocarnitine. Frequent monitoring of INR is recommended [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Oral levocarnitine (not the IV formulation) should be used with caution in patients with renal impairment due to the potential accumulation of toxic metabolites like trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not specifically assigned in text]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of fetal harm or impaired fertility. Because animal studies are not always predictive of human response, it should be used in pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies in humans are unavailable. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine in milk following administration. Risks of excess intake in the infant should be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is based on mg/kg body weight (refer to Section 3) [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. However, safety and efficacy of high-dose *oral* levocarnitine have not been established in patients with severe renal compromise [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
- **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites (via oral route) include trimethylamine N-oxide and γ-butyrobetaine [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Bioequivalence:** Studies in healthy volunteers confirmed bioequivalence between oral tablets and solution [US].
- **ESRD Studies:** Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) in hemodialysis patients demonstrated a dose-dependent increase in trough levocarnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks. While carnitine levels increased, the impact on specific clinical outcomes in this population remains to be determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Once opened, any unused portion of the single-dose vial must be discarded, as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only (Rx-only) [US].
