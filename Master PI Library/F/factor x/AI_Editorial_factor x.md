**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been synthesized from the official regulatory filings of the Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA, Australia) for Beriplex AU and the Food and Drug Administration (FDA, United States) for Coagadex. While both products are plasma-derived therapies involving Coagulation Factor X, they represent different therapeutic classes: Beriplex AU is a 4-factor Prothrombin Complex Concentrate (PCC) and Coagadex is a purified Human Coagulation Factor X. This editorial document combines their clinical profiles to provide a comprehensive overview of Factor X and Prothrombin Complex replacement therapy.

# Unified Product Information: Human Coagulation Factor X / Prothrombin Complex

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
This PI covers plasma-derived human coagulation products containing Factor X, specifically:
*   **Human Prothrombin Complex (PCC):** Containing Coagulation Factors II, VII, IX, and X, as well as Proteins C and S [TGA].
*   **Human Coagulation Factor X:** A purified, sterile concentrate of human Coagulation Factor X [FDA].

## 2. Approved Indications
These products are indicated for the management of coagulation factor deficiencies under the following circumstances:

*   **Acquired Deficiency (Vitamin K Antagonist Reversal):** For the treatment and perioperative prophylaxis of bleeding in patients with an acquired deficiency of prothrombin complex factors induced by vitamin K antagonists (e.g., warfarin), when rapid correction of the deficiency is required [TGA].
*   **Hereditary Factor X Deficiency:** 
    *   Routine prophylaxis to minimize the frequency of bleeding events [FDA].
    *   On-demand treatment and control of active bleeding [FDA].
    *   Perioperative management of bleeding in patients with mild, moderate, or severe hereditary Factor X deficiency [FDA].

## 3. Contraindications
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known life-threatening hypersensitivity or anaphylactic reactions to the active substance or any excipients [TGA, FDA].
*   **Heparin-Induced Thrombocytopenia (HIT):** Products containing heparin are contraindicated in patients with a history of HIT [TGA].
*   **Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC):** Prothrombin complex preparations should only be used after the consumptive state has been terminated [TGA].

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Thromboembolic Risk:** There is a significant risk of thrombosis and DIC with prothrombin complex concentrates, especially with repeated dosing or in patients with pre-existing risk factors (e.g., coronary heart disease, liver disease, or neonates). Patients must be monitored closely for signs of intravascular coagulation or thrombosis [TGA].
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Allergic-type reactions, including anaphylaxis, may occur. If symptoms such as hives, chest tightness, or wheezing develop, administration should be stopped immediately [TGA, FDA].
*   **Neutralizing Antibodies:** Patients treated for hereditary Factor X deficiency may develop neutralizing antibodies (inhibitors). If expected plasma Factor X levels are not reached or bleeding is not controlled, an assay for Factor X inhibitors should be performed [FDA].
*   **Transmissible Infectious Agents:** As these products are manufactured from human plasma, they carry a potential risk of transmitting infectious agents, including viruses and the Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease (CJD) agent. Stringent donor screening and viral inactivation steps (e.g., nanofiltration, pasteurization, solvent/detergent treatment) are employed to minimize this risk [TGA, FDA].
*   **Sodium Content:** The sodium content of the reconstituted solution (up to 343 mg/100 mL in some preparations) should be considered for patients on a controlled sodium diet [TGA].

## 5. Dosage and Administration
### 5.1 Acquired Deficiency (VKA Reversal)
Dosage is individualized based on the patient's pre-treatment International Normalised Ratio (INR) and target INR. 
*   **INR 2.0–3.9:** ~25 IU/kg body weight.
*   **INR 4.0–6.0:** ~35 IU/kg body weight.
*   **INR >6.0:** ~50 IU/kg body weight.
Doses are generally based on a maximum body weight of 100 kg. Vitamin K should be administered concurrently to maintain long-term factor levels [TGA].

### 5.2 Hereditary Factor X Deficiency
*   **Routine Prophylaxis:** 
    *   **Adults/Adolescents (≥12 years):** 25 IU/kg twice weekly.
    *   **Children (<12 years):** 40 IU/kg twice weekly, with trough levels monitored to ensure they stay ≥5 IU/dL [FDA].
*   **On-Demand Treatment:** 25 IU/kg (Adults) or 30 IU/kg (Children) at the first sign of bleeding, repeated every 24 hours until the bleed is resolved [FDA].
*   **Perioperative Management:** Dosing is calculated to raise plasma Factor X levels to 70–90 IU/dL pre-surgery and maintain them at ≥50 IU/dL post-surgery [FDA].

### 5.3 Administration Instructions
Reconstitution must be performed using the provided diluent and transfer device (e.g., Mix2Vial). The solution should be clear or slightly opalescent. Administer by slow intravenous injection or infusion; for PCC, the rate should not exceed 3 IU/kg/min (max 210 IU/min) [TGA]. For purified Factor X, the recommended infusion rate is 10 mL/min (max 20 mL/min) [FDA].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*   **Common (≥5%):** Infusion site pain, erythema, headache, fatigue, and back pain [FDA, TGA].
*   **Vascular/Immune:** Thromboembolic events (arterial and venous), disseminated intravascular coagulation, and hypersensitivity/anaphylactic reactions [TGA].
*   **Other:** Increase in body temperature and the development of neutralizing antibodies (inhibitors) [TGA, FDA].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **VKA Neutralization:** These products directly neutralize the anticoagulant effect of vitamin K antagonists [TGA].
*   **Factor Xa Inhibitors:** The efficacy of Factor X products may be counteracted by direct or indirect Factor Xa inhibitors (e.g., rivaroxaban, apixaban) [FDA].
*   **Heparin Sensitivity:** Clotting tests sensitive to heparin may be affected when using products containing heparin [TGA].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
The safety of these products during pregnancy and lactation has not been established in controlled clinical trials. Use should only be considered if the clinical benefit clearly outweighs the potential risks. There is no evidence from animal studies that these factors cross the placental barrier, but high-quality data regarding excretion into human milk is unavailable [TGA, FDA].

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Incremental Recovery:** In healthy volunteers, the incremental in vivo recovery (IVR) for Factor X is approximately 2.0 IU/dL per IU/kg in adults. In children under 12, the recovery is lower, at approximately 1.7 IU/dL per IU/kg [FDA].
*   **Half-Life:** The median plasma half-life of Factor X is approximately 30–31 hours [FDA, TGA].
*   **Metabolism:** These products are distributed and metabolized in the same manner as endogenous coagulation factors [TGA].

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Factor X is a vitamin K-dependent zymogen that plays a central role in the coagulation cascade. It is converted to its active form (Factor Xa) via either the extrinsic or intrinsic pathway. Factor Xa then associates with Factor Va to form the prothrombinase complex, which converts prothrombin (Factor II) into thrombin. Thrombin subsequently converts fibrinogen into fibrin to form a stable blood clot. Replacement therapy restores these levels to achieve adequate hemostasis [TGA, FDA].

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store in the original package to protect from light. Most products should be stored at 2°C to 25°C [TGA, FDA]. 
*   **Stability:** Do not freeze. Reconstituted solutions should be used immediately (or within 1 hour) as they contain no antimicrobial preservatives [TGA, FDA].
