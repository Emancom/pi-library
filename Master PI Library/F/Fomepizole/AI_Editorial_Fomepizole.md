# Editorial Product Information: Fomepizole

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) documents from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Note on Scope:** While this editorial is designed to harmonize multiple sources, only the Australian PI was provided for this specific review. Data from other major jurisdictions (e.g., US, European, Canadian) are noted as "Not specified in available sources" unless referenced within the provided Australian documentation.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is not a substitute for the official PI and should not be used as the sole basis for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Fomepizole (4-methylpyrazole)
- **Chemical Class:** Alcohol dehydrogenase inhibitor
- **Brand Name(s):** **Antizol** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** V03AB34 (Not explicitly stated in source, but standard for fomepizole)
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile, preservative-free solution for intravenous infusion.
    - Each 1.5 mL vial contains 1.5 g of fomepizole (1 g/mL) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AFT Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Ethylene Glycol Poisoning:** For the treatment of confirmed or suspected ethylene glycol ingestion [Australian].
- **Methanol Poisoning:** For the treatment of confirmed or suspected methanol ingestion [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Treatment should commence immediately upon clinical suspicion of ingestion (e.g., anion gap metabolic acidosis, osmolar gap, or visual disturbances) or documented serum concentrations >20 mg/dL.

- **Loading Dose:** 15 mg/kg.
- **Maintenance Dosing:** 10 mg/kg every 12 hours for four doses.
- **Escalated Maintenance Dosing:** After the initial four maintenance doses, the dose should be increased to 15 mg/kg every 12 hours. This compensates for the drug’s ability to induce its own metabolism [Australian].

### Dosing during Haemodialysis
Fomepizole is dialyzable. During haemodialysis, the dosing frequency must be increased to every 4 hours [Australian].
- **At start of dialysis:** If <6 hours since last dose, do not dose. If ≥6 hours, administer next scheduled dose.
- **During dialysis:** Dose every 4 hours.
- **At completion of dialysis:** Based on time since last dose (<1 hour: no dose; 1–3 hours: 1/2 next dose; >3 hours: full next dose).

### Administration Instructions
- **CAUTION:** Must be diluted prior to use.
- **Preparation:** Draw the dose from the vial and dilute in at least 100 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride or 5% dextrose. 
- **Infusion Rate:** Administer as a slow intravenous infusion over 30 minutes.
- **Physical State:** Fomepizole solidifies at room temperature (melting point 25°C). If solid, liquefy by running the vial under warm water or holding it in the hand. Solidification does not affect stability [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Documented serious hypersensitivity to fomepizole or other pyrazoles [Australian].
- Concurrent ethanol intoxication (ethanol competitively inhibits the metabolism of fomepizole) [Australian].
- Exposure to poisons other than ethylene glycol or methanol (including diethylene glycol) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Administration Warning:** Do not administer undiluted or via bolus injection. Bolus injections have been associated with venous irritation and phlebosclerosis [Australian].
- **Allergic Reactions:** Anaphylaxis and minor reactions (rash, eosinophilia) have been reported. Monitor patients closely.
- **Muscle Damage:** Risk of creatine kinase (CK) elevation and potential muscle damage.
- **Neurological/Metabolic:** Potential risk of seizures and hypoglycemia.
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Frequent monitoring of blood gases, pH, electrolytes, BUN, creatinine, and urinalysis is essential. Hepatic enzymes and white blood cell counts should also be monitored.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorized by frequency based on clinical trial data (n=141) [Australian].

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Headache, Fever (in poisoned patients), Nausea (in healthy volunteers) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Abdominal pain, vomiting, dizziness, drowsiness, agitation, phlebosclerosis, rash, hypotension, anaemia, eosinophilia, metallic taste |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Multi-organ failure, anuria, nystagmus, anxiety, tachycardia, phlebitis, hiccups |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Post-marketing reports include anaphylaxis, brain herniation, brain oedema, renal failure, and respiratory distress [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Ethanol:** Significantly reduces the rate of elimination for both ethanol (by ~40%) and fomepizole (by ~50%) through competitive inhibition of alcohol dehydrogenase [Australian].
- **CYP450 System:** Fomepizole induces CYP2E1 and may inhibit other CYP enzymes. Reciprocal interactions may occur with drugs like phenytoin, carbamazepine, cimetidine, or ketoconazole, though these are not formally studied [Australian].
- **Psychiatric Medications:** Interaction with disulfiram, antidepressants, and anxiolytics has not been established.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B1:** (Note: The provided PI lists Category B2). Fomepizole has been shown to cross the placenta in rats. It should only be used in pregnant women if clearly needed [Australian].
    - **US Category:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if fomepizole is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution when administering to breastfeeding women [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed an 8% reduction in testicular mass at doses 0.1 times the human maximum daily exposure; this effect was amplified when combined with ethanol [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 12 years of age have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Pharmacokinetics in the elderly have not been sufficiently studied [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Metabolites are excreted renally. Fomepizole is removed by haemodialysis. Specific pharmacokinetic studies in renal impairment are lacking [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Fomepizole is metabolized in the liver; however, studies in patients with hepatic disease have not been performed [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Fomepizole is a competitive inhibitor of alcohol dehydrogenase (ADH). It prevents the formation of toxic metabolites (glycolic/oxalic acid from ethylene glycol; formic acid from methanol) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Rapidly distributes to total body water (Vd: 0.6–1.02 L/kg).
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic. The main metabolite is 4-carboxypyrazole (80–85%). It induces its own metabolism via the CYP450 system after 30–40 hours.
    - **Elimination:** Follows Michaelis-Menten kinetics after acute doses and first-order kinetics after enzyme induction [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Efficacy was primarily established in two prospective, non-controlled U.S. clinical trials involving patients over 12 years of age with ethylene glycol or methanol poisoning [Australian].
- **Ethylene Glycol Study:** In 16 patients, fomepizole administration (with or without dialysis) blocked the rise of glycolate concentrations once ethanol levels were negligible.
- **Methanol Study:** In 11 patients, fomepizole effectively blocked the formation of formate [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C.
- Protect from light.
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any residue after single use. After dilution, use as soon as practicable; if storage is necessary, hold at 2-8°C for no more than 24 hours [Australian].
