# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Metabolic Errors:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Standard Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Acute Management:** In cases of severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose may be administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Dosing Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically remain at 50 mg/kg or as clinically required. The maximum recorded dose is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect parenterals visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate appropriate medical care [US].
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine levels prior to therapy. For metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring is advised (target free carnitine range: 35 to 60 µmol/L). For ESRD, monitor trough concentrations regularly [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects listed below were observed in clinical trials of ESRD patients on hemodialysis. Frequency is reported regardless of causality [US].

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site reaction (33–59%), Pain (30–49%), Pharyngitis (23–33%), Headache (22–37%), Hypertension (20–21%), Diarrhea (16–35%), Flu syndrome (25–40%), Nausea/Vomiting (8–21%), Chest pain (6–15%), Dizziness (10–18%), Dyspnea (3–19%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Abdominal pain, Asthenia, Back pain, Fever, Infection, Palpitations, Tachycardia, Vascular disorder, Anorexia, Constipation, Melena, Anemia, Hypercalcemia, Hyperkalemia, Peripheral edema, Weight changes, Leg cramps, Depression, Insomnia, Paresthesia, Rhinitis, Pruritus, Rash, Eye disorders, Urinary tract infection |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm [US].
- **Postmarketing Experience:** Seizures (both new-onset and increased frequency in known seizure disorders) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when **levocarnitine** is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of **levocarnitine** or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not specified by letter, but narrative provided]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess intake should be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See **Dosage and Administration** for general weight-based dosing; specific age-range approvals were not detailed [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral formulations in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]), which are normally excreted in urine [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn metabolic errors, it promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Study:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly showed that trough levocarnitine concentrations increased from a baseline of ~20 µmol/L to a mean of 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg in ESRD patients (N≈30 per group) demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough plasma concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when diluted to concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s [US].

- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
