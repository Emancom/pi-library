# Editorial Product Information: Flumazenil

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of the official Product Information provided for flumazenil. It is intended for use by healthcare professionals as a clinically accurate, consolidated reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial is not a substitute for the official Product Information approved in a specific jurisdiction. Prescribers should consult the current local approved labelling for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Flumazenil (an imidazobenzodiazepine).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Anexate** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** V03AB25.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Solution for injection/infusion: 0.5 mg/5 mL ampoules [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) injection or infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pharmaco (Australia) Ltd under license of CHEPLAPHARM Arzneimittel GmbH [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- Reversal of acute benzodiazepine effects resulting from therapeutic use (e.g., anaesthesia or sedation) or overdose [Australian].
- **Restriction:** Use is restricted to hospitalised patients under continued professional observation. It is not indicated for outpatient use, short-stay patients, or as a diagnostic tool [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Administration must be performed by an anaesthetist or experienced physician. The drug may be diluted in 5% glucose or 0.9% NaCl [Australian].

**Reversal of Therapeutic Sedation/Anaesthesia:**
- **Initial Dose:** 0.2 mg administered IV over 15 seconds.
- **Titration:** If consciousness is not achieved within 60 seconds, a second dose of 0.1 mg may be injected. This may be repeated at 60-second intervals.
- **Maximum/Usual Dose:** Total dose is typically 0.3–0.6 mg; maximum total dose is 1 mg [Australian].

**Reversal of Known or Suspected Overdose:**
- **Initial Dose:** 0.3 mg administered IV.
- **Titration:** If consciousness is not achieved within 60 seconds, repeat injections may be given until the patient awakes.
- **Maximum Dose:** Total dose up to 2 mg.
- **Recurrent Drowsiness:** An IV infusion of 0.1–0.4 mg/hour may be used, adjusted to the desired level of arousal [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing (> 1 year of age)
- **Initial Dose:** 0.01 mg/kg (up to 0.2 mg) IV over 15 seconds.
- **Titration:** If consciousness is not achieved after 60 seconds, further injections of 0.01 mg/kg (up to 0.2 mg) may be repeated at 60-second intervals.
- **Maximum Dose:** 0.05 mg/kg or 1 mg, whichever is lower [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Infusions should be completed within 24 hours of preparation to reduce microbial hazards.
- Ensure neuromuscular blockade is fully reversed before administration [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to flumazenil.
- Patients who have been administered a benzodiazepine for the control of potentially life-threatening conditions (e.g., status epilepticus or control of intracranial pressure).
- **Mixed Intoxication:** In cases where benzodiazepine reversal may unmask the toxicity of cyclic antidepressants (e.g., tricyclics/tetracyclics), particularly in the presence of autonomic, neurological, or cardiovascular symptoms of severe intoxication [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in provided source.
- **Benzodiazepine Withdrawal:** Flumazenil can precipitate withdrawal symptoms or convulsions in patients with known or suspected dependency or those on high-dose benzodiazepines. If withdrawal occurs, slow IV injection of 5 mg diazepam or 5 mg midazolam is recommended [Australian].
- **Resedation:** Patients must be monitored for an appropriate period due to the risk of resedation and respiratory depression, as the duration of flumazenil may be shorter than the benzodiazepine [Australian].
- **Head Injury:** May precipitate convulsions or increase intracranial pressure in patients with head injuries or unstable intracranial pressure.
- **Epilepsy:** Abrupt suppression of the protective effect of benzodiazepines may cause convulsions in patients who have received long-term benzodiazepine treatment for epilepsy [Australian].
- **Driving/Machinery:** Patients should not engage in hazardous activities (driving or operating machinery) for 24 hours post-administration [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common (1–10%)
- Nausea and vomiting (more frequent when used with opioids or following anaesthesia reversal).

### Infrequent/Rare (< 1%)
- Dizziness, vertigo.
- Anxiety, fearfulness, agitation, depressed mood, or tearfulness.
- Palpitations.
- Hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis.
- Panic attacks (specifically in patients with a history of panic disorder).

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Convulsions:** Reported in patients with epilepsy, severe hepatic impairment, or mixed drug overdose (particularly cyclic antidepressants) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Benzodiazepine Receptors:** Specifically blocks the central effects of benzodiazepines and non-benzodiazepine agonists that act at the benzodiazepine receptor (e.g., zopiclone).
- **Other CNS Depressants:** No interactions have been observed with barbiturates, ethanol, or meprobamate.
- **Mixed Overdose:** Reversal of benzodiazepines can unmask the toxic effects (e.g., cardiac dysrhythmias and convulsions) of other drugs taken in overdose, specifically cyclic antidepressants [Australian].

### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- The pharmacokinetics of benzodiazepines are not altered by flumazenil [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category B3. Safety in human pregnancy has not been established. Use only if clearly needed, weighing benefits against potential fetal risks. Animal studies showed no teratogenicity, but embryotoxicity was noted at high doses (> 500 times clinical exposure) [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if flumazenil is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution when administering to breastfeeding women [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies (rats) showed no effect on fertility at doses > 300 times the maximum clinical dose [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for children > 1 year. Agitation (3%) and aggressive reactions (2%) were noted in clinical study. PK parameters show high variability [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Pharmacokinetics are not significantly affected in the elderly [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Pharmacokinetics are not significantly affected by renal failure or haemodialysis [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Clearance is reduced by 57–74%, and elimination half-life is prolonged up to 2-fold in moderate to severe impairment. Careful dose titration and extended observation periods are required [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Flumazenil is a competitive antagonist at the benzodiazepine receptor, specifically blocking the central sedative effects of benzodiazepine agonists [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Onset of reversal occurs within 1–2 minutes of IV injection. It may possess weak intrinsic anticonvulsant activity [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Approximately 50% plasma protein bound; Vss is 0.95 L/kg.
    - **Metabolism:** Almost completely metabolised in the liver; the primary carboxylic acid metabolite is inactive.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily hepatic (99% non-renal). Elimination half-life is approximately 53 minutes (range 40–80 minutes) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Paediatric Study:** A single-arm study (n=107) in children aged 1–17 years following midazolam sedation for procedures (e.g., endoscopy) supported weight-based dosing [Australian].
- No further clinical trial data provided in the source document [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Protect from light.
- Store in a cool, dry place below 30°C [Australian].
- Shelf life is specified on the packaging and the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG) [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
