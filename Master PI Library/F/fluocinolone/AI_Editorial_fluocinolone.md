# Editorial Product Information: Fluocinolone Acetonide (Topical Oil)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the local PI for the most up-to-date regional requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Fluocinolone acetonide (a synthetic corticosteroid).
- **Chemical Class:** Corticosteroid; Acetal.
- **Brand Names:** Not specified in available sources (Repackaged/Relabeled by Bryant Ranch Prepack) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Topical oil, 0.01% (Scalp Oil). Each gram contains approximately 0.11 mg of fluocinolone acetonide.
- **Excipients of Note:** Refined peanut oil NF (48%), isopropyl alcohol, isopropyl myristate, light mineral oil, and oleth-2 [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Topical (Scalp).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Quagen Pharmaceuticals LLC; Repackaged by Bryant Ranch Prepack [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Scalp Psoriasis:** Treatment of psoriasis of the scalp in adult patients [US].
- *Note:* While this specific product is labeled as "Scalp Oil," other formulations of fluocinolone acetonide 0.01% topical oil are marketed for atopic dermatitis (adults/pediatrics) and chronic eczematous external otitis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Scalp Psoriasis (Adults)
- **General Dosing:** Apply a thin film to the affected areas of the scalp.
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Wet or dampen hair and scalp thoroughly before application.
    - Massage the oil well into the scalp.
    - Cover the scalp with the supplied shower cap.
    - Leave the application on overnight or for a minimum of 4 hours before washing off.
    - Wash hair with regular shampoo and rinse thoroughly [US].
- **Duration:** Therapy should be discontinued once control is achieved. If no improvement is observed within 2 weeks, the patient should be re-evaluated [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to fluocinolone acetonide or any other component of the preparation [US].
- **Peanut Allergy:** Due to the inclusion of 48% refined peanut oil NF, use is contraindicated or requires extreme caution in patients with known peanut sensitivities [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **HPA Axis Suppression:** Systemic absorption of topical corticosteroids can produce reversible hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis suppression, leading to glucocorticosteroid insufficiency upon withdrawal. Manifestations of Cushing’s syndrome, hyperglycemia, and glucosuria may occur [US].
- **Monitoring:** Patients applying the steroid to large surface areas or under occlusion (such as the provided shower cap) should be evaluated periodically for HPA axis suppression using ACTH stimulation, A.M. plasma cortisol, and urinary free cortisol tests [US].
- **Peanut Sensitivity:** While the peanut oil used is heated to 475°F to decompose allergenic proteins, caution should still be exercised. Clinical studies have shown potential for exacerbation of atopic dermatitis in peanut-sensitive individuals [US].
- **Infection:** If concomitant skin infections are present or develop, appropriate antifungal or antibacterial agents should be used. If the infection does not respond promptly, the corticosteroid should be discontinued until the infection is controlled [US].
- **Application Site Precautions:** 
    - For external use only; avoid contact with eyes.
    - Do not use on the face, underarm, or groin unless directed by a physician.
    - Avoid use in the diaper area (diapers/plastic pants act as occlusive dressings) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Local adverse reactions are reported infrequently but may occur more often with occlusive dressings.

### General Topical Reactions
- Burning, itching, irritation, and dryness.
- Folliculitis, acneiform eruptions, and hypopigmentation.
- Perioral dermatitis and allergic contact dermatitis.
- Skin atrophy, striae, and miliaria [US].

### Pediatric Post-Marketing Safety Data (Face Application)
In a safety study of 58 children (ages 2–12) applying the oil twice daily for 4 weeks:
| Adverse Event | Incidence (%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Any Adverse Event | 25.9% |
| Telangiectasia | 8.6% |
| Erythema | 5.2% |
| Itching | 5.2% |
| Irritation | 5.2% |
| Burning | 5.2% |
| Hypopigmentation | 3.5% |

Serious adverse effects related to systemic absorption include intracranial hypertension (bulging fontanelles, headaches, bilateral papilledema) and growth retardation in children [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.
- **General Note:** Significant interactions are rare for topical corticosteroids but systemic absorption should be considered if used with other agents that affect the HPA axis [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not assigned/specified under current labeling]. Corticosteroids are generally teratogenic in animals. There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Systemically administered corticosteroids appear in human milk. It is unknown if topical administration results in sufficient systemic absorption to produce detectable quantities in milk. Exercise caution when administering to a nursing woman [US].
- **Fertility:** Long-term animal studies to evaluate the effect on fertility have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - Children are more susceptible to systemic toxicity and HPA axis suppression due to a higher skin surface area to body mass ratio.
    - Reported effects in children include linear growth retardation, delayed weight gain, and low plasma cortisol levels.
    - Not recommended for diaper dermatitis [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Induction of phospholipase A₂ inhibitory proteins (lipocortins). These proteins control the biosynthesis of potent mediators of inflammation, such as prostaglandins and leukotrienes, by inhibiting the release of arachidonic acid [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Exhibits anti-inflammatory, antipruritic, and vasoconstrictive properties. It is classified as a low to medium potency corticosteroid [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Extent of percutaneous absorption is determined by the vehicle, epidermal integrity, and use of occlusion. 
    - **Distribution/Metabolism/Elimination:** Once absorbed through the skin, topical corticosteroids follow similar pharmacokinetic pathways to systemically administered corticosteroids, being metabolised primarily in the liver and excreted by the kidneys [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Scalp Psoriasis (Adults):** In a vehicle-controlled study, after 21 days of treatment, 60% of patients using fluocinolone acetonide topical oil achieved an "excellent to cleared" clinical response, compared to 21% in the vehicle group [US].
- **Peanut Allergy Safety:** A study of 13 patients (6–17 years) with known peanut allergies showed negative prick and patch test results for the refined peanut oil used in this formulation. However, one patient experienced an exacerbation of atopic dermatitis [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (68° to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°–30°C (59°–86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Keep the bottle tightly closed [US].
