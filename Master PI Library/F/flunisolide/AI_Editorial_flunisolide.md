# Editorial Product Information: Flunisolide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (Food and Drug Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised clinical overview of flunisolide nasal solution for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Flunisolide (a synthetic fluorinated corticosteroid/glucocorticoid).
- **Brand Names:** Flunisolide Nasal Solution USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Nasal solution 0.025% (25 mcg per metered spray). Each 25 mL bottle contains 6.25 mg of flunisolide and delivers 200 sprays [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intranasal.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Bausch & Lomb Incorporated [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Rhinitis:** Management of nasal symptoms associated with seasonal or perennial rhinitis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Starting Dose:** 2 sprays (50 mcg) in each nostril twice daily (total 200 mcg/day) [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** Up to 2 sprays in each nostril three times daily (total 300 mcg/day) if required. The maximum total daily dose should not exceed 8 sprays per nostril (400 mcg/day) [US].
- **Maintenance:** Once symptoms are controlled, the dose should be titrated to the lowest effective amount. Some patients with perennial rhinitis may be maintained on 1 spray per nostril per day [US].

### Dosing in Paediatrics (Ages 6–14 years)
- **Starting Dose:** 1 spray (25 mcg) in each nostril three times daily OR 2 sprays (50 mcg) in each nostril twice daily (total 150–200 mcg/day) [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** 4 sprays per nostril per day (total 200 mcg/day) [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Priming:** The pump must be primed before initial use (5–6 sprays until a fine mist appears). Repriming is required if the unit has not been used for 5 days or more, or after cleaning [US].
- **Technique:** Patients should clear nasal passages prior to use. The spray tip should be directed toward the back and outer side of the nose while the patient sniffs gently. The head should be bent forward during administration and tilted back for a few seconds afterward [US].
- **Cleaning:** The pump unit should be removed and soaked in warm water if clogged; sharp objects should never be used to clear the nozzle [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to flunisolide or any inactive ingredients (including benzalkonium chloride) [US].
- **Infection:** Presence of untreated localized infection involving the nasal mucosa [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Systemic Steroid Replacement:** Transferring from systemic corticosteroids to topical flunisolide requires caution due to the risk of adrenal insufficiency. Monitoring is essential in patients with asthma or other conditions where rapid systemic steroid withdrawal may cause severe exacerbation [US].
- **Immunosuppression:** Patients on immunosuppressant doses of corticosteroids are more susceptible to infections. Exposure to chickenpox or measles should be avoided; if exposure occurs, medical advice/prophylaxis (e.g., VZIG or IG) is recommended [US].
- **Local Nasal Effects:** Localized *Candida albicans* infections of the nose and pharynx may occur. Corticosteroids may inhibit wound healing; avoid use in patients with recent nasal septal ulcers, nasal surgery, or trauma until healing is complete [US].
- **HPA Axis Suppression:** While minimal at recommended doses, excessive doses can suppress hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) function [US].
- **Growth Monitoring:** Intranasal corticosteroids may cause a reduction in growth velocity in paediatric patients. Growth should be monitored routinely via stadiometry [US].
- **Ocular Effects:** Caution in patients with ocular herpes simplex [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Side effects are primarily localized to the nasal mucosa.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Mild transient nasal burning and stinging (approx. 45%) [US] |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Nasal congestion, sneezing, epistaxis (or bloody mucus), nasal irritation, sore throat, headache, nausea, vomiting, watery eyes [US] |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Nasal septal perforation, temporary or permanent loss of smell or taste [US] |

**Note:** The incidence of burning/stinging often decreases with continued treatment [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Prednisone:** Concurrent use of flunisolide with alternate-day systemic prednisone may increase the likelihood of HPA axis suppression compared to either treatment alone [US].
- **Other Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category C]. Flunisolide has shown teratogenic and fetotoxic effects in rabbits and rats at 2–4 times the human dose. Use during pregnancy only if the potential benefit outweighs the risk to the fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if flunisolide is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution when administering to nursing women [US].
- **Fertility:** In animal studies (rats), oral doses 4 times the maximum human intranasal dose impaired female fertility. Doses approximately equal to the human dose showed no effect [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for children under 6 years of age [US]. 
- **Geriatric Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65+ to determine different responses. Generally, dose selection for elderly patients should be cautious, typically starting at the low end of the range [US].
- **Hepatic/Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources; however, caution is advised in the elderly due to the higher frequency of decreased hepatic or renal function [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A potent glucocorticoid with weak mineralocorticoid activity. It exerts direct local anti-inflammatory effects on the nasal mucosa with minimal systemic activity at recommended doses [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Well absorbed following intranasal administration; approximately 50% reaches systemic circulation unmetabolized [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly converted by the liver (first-pass metabolism) to a much less active primary metabolite (6β-hydroxy) and glucuronate/sulfate conjugates [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately half is recovered in urine and half in stool.
    - **Half-life:** 1 to 2 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy:** Controlled studies in 488 adults and 127 paediatric patients demonstrated effectiveness in reducing symptoms of stuffy nose, runny nose, and sneezing. Improvement is typically observed within a few days, though full relief may take 1–2 weeks [US].
- **HPA Axis Studies:** At recommended doses, no unequivocal depression of plasma cortisol was observed. However, at doses >2 times the recommended limit, evidence of decreased endogenous cortisol production was noted [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store between 15°C and 25°C (59°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Keep the dust cover on the pump when not in use. Keep out of reach of children [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** [US] Rx-only (Prescription only).
