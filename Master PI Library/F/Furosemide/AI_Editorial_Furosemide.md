# Editorial Product Information: Furosemide

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the clinical and regulatory profile of furosemide for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial reference and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for final prescribing decisions. *Note: At the time of compilation, only the Australian (TGA) PI was provided for synthesis.*

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Furosemide (an anthranilic acid derivative).
- **Chemical Class:** Loop diuretic.
- **Brand Name(s):** APO-Frusemide [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** C03CA01 (not specified in available sources, but standard classification).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
  - Oral Tablets: 20 mg and 40 mg [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral (parenteral routes mentioned as indications for transition but not detailed as available products in the provided source).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Oedema:** Treatment of oedema associated with congestive heart failure, cirrhosis of the liver, and renal disease (including nephrotic syndrome) in adults, infants, and children [Australian].
- **Hypertension:** Management of hypertension in adults, either as monotherapy or in combination with other antihypertensive agents [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Oedema:** The usual initial dose is 20 mg to 80 mg as a single dose. If required, dosage may be increased by 20 mg to 40 mg increments every 6 to 8 hours until a diuretic response is achieved. The effective dose may be administered once or twice daily. Maximum titration up to 400 mg/day is permitted in severe cases, except in advanced renal failure [Australian].
- **Hypertension:** The usual initial dose is 80 mg daily, typically divided into 40 mg twice daily. Dose adjustments should be based on clinical response [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- It is recommended that oral furosemide be taken on an empty stomach to ensure optimal absorption [Australian].

### Titration and Maintenance
- **Mobilisation of Oedema:** May be achieved most safely by intermittent dosing (e.g., 2 to 4 consecutive days per week) [Australian].
- **Combination Therapy:** When adding furosemide to an existing antihypertensive regimen, the dose of other agents should be reduced by at least 50% to prevent excessive hypotension [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to furosemide or sulfonamides (due to potential cross-sensitivity) [Australian].
- Complete renal shutdown, anuria, or impaired renal function; treatment must be discontinued if increasing azotaemia or oliguria occurs during the treatment of progressive renal disease [Australian].
- Severe electrolyte imbalances, including hypokalaemia and hyponatraemia [Australian].
- Hypovolaemia, dehydration, or hypotension [Australian].
- Hepatic coma or pre-coma [Australian].
- Pregnancy and breastfeeding [Australian].
- Newborns with jaundice or infants at risk of hyperbilirubinaemia/kernicterus (due to bilirubin displacement from albumin) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in provided source.
- **Electrolyte Monitoring:** Mandatory periodic determination of serum electrolytes (sodium, potassium, chloride), creatinine, and BUN is required, particularly in high-risk patients [Australian].
- **Ototoxicity:** Hearing impairment and tinnitus may occur, often associated with rapid administration, renal impairment, or concomitant ototoxic drugs [Australian].
- **Lactose Intolerance:** Tablets contain lactose; not suitable for patients with rare hereditary galactose intolerance or glucose-galactose malabsorption [Australian].
- **Risperidone Co-administration:** Caution is advised in elderly patients with dementia; clinical trials noted an increased mortality rate when risperidone was used with furosemide compared to either agent alone [Australian].
- **Systemic Lupus Erythematosus:** Potential for exacerbation or activation of SLE [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by frequency as follows:

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Electrolyte disturbances (symptomatic), dehydration, hypovolaemia (especially in the elderly), increased blood creatinine, increased blood triglycerides. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Hyponatraemia, hypochloremia, hypokalaemia, increased blood cholesterol, hyperuricaemia/gout, increased urine volume, haemoconcentration, hepatic encephalopathy in hepatic insufficiency. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Impaired glucose tolerance, manifest or latent diabetes mellitus, thrombocytopaenia, gastrointestinal reactions (nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea), allergic skin reactions (pruritus, urticaria, rashes). |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Eosinophilia, leukopaenia, agranulocytosis, vasculitis, paraesthesia, fever, anaphylactic/anaphylactoid reactions. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Irreversible deafness (reported after oral use), Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN), acute pancreatitis, and rhabdomyolysis (typically in the context of severe hypokalaemia) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Ototoxic/Nephrotoxic Agents:** Increased risk of toxicity when used with aminoglycosides, certain cephalosporins (e.g., cephaloridine), or cisplatin [Australian].
- **Lithium:** Furosemide reduces lithium excretion, significantly increasing the risk of lithium toxicity [Australian].
- **ACE Inhibitors/ARBs:** Potential for severe hypotension and renal deterioration; consider pausing furosemide 3 days prior to starting these agents [Australian].
- **NSAIDs:** May reduce the natriuretic and antihypertensive effects; risk of acute renal failure in dehydrated patients [Australian].
- **Sucralfate:** Reduces furosemide absorption; doses should be separated by at least 2 hours [Australian].
- **Risperidone:** Increased mortality observed in elderly patients with dementia [Australian].
- **Levothyroxine:** High doses of furosemide may transiently increase free thyroid hormones followed by a decrease in total levels [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category C. Furosemide crosses the placental barrier and should only be used if there are compelling medical reasons. Use requires monitoring of foetal growth. In pregnancy, it should only be used in patients with a marked reduction in GFR [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Furosemide passes into breast milk and may inhibit lactation [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available in source documents [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Initial dose is 2 mg/kg body weight; do not exceed 6 mg/kg. Premature infants are at risk of nephrocalcinosis/nephrolithiasis and persistence of patent ductus arteriosus [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of circulatory collapse, thrombosis, and complications when combined with risperidone in dementia [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Requires careful monitoring of BUN and creatinine; dose adjustments needed for severe states [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Initiation of therapy for cirrhosis with ascites is best performed in a hospital setting due to the risk of sudden fluid/electrolyte shifts precipitating hepatic coma [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Potent loop diuretic that inhibits sodium and chloride absorption in the ascending limb of Henle's loop, as well as the proximal and distal tubules [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; bioavailability is approximately 64% from tablets in fasted subjects [Australian].
  - **Distribution:** Extensively bound to plasma proteins (91–99%), primarily albumin [Australian].
  - **Metabolism:** Furosemide glucuronide is the primary biotransformation product [Australian].
  - **Elimination:** Approximately 80% is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (via glomerular filtration and tubular secretion). Terminal half-life is biphasic, up to 100 minutes [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No specific pivotal trial data was provided in the source documents [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C.
- Protect from light [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
