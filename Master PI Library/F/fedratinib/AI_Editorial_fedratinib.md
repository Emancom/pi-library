**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document for fedratinib (marketed as INREBIC) is an editorial synthesis of the official prescribing information provided by the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). In instances of varying clinical criteria or safety observations, the most comprehensive and conservative guidance has been selected, with the source agency indicated in brackets.

# Unified Editorial Product Information: Fedratinib

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** INREBIC  
**Active Ingredient:** Fedratinib (as dihydrochloride monohydrate)  
**Formulation:** 100 mg hard capsules. Each capsule contains 100 mg of fedratinib, which is equivalent to 117.30 mg of fedratinib dihydrochloride monohydrate.

---

## 2. Approved Indications
INREBIC is indicated for the treatment of adult patients with intermediate-2 or high-risk [FDA] primary myelofibrosis (MF), post-polycythaemia vera myelofibrosis, or post-essential thrombocythaemia myelofibrosis. 

The indication includes patients who are Janus Associated Kinase (JAK) inhibitor naïve or those who have previously received treatment with ruxolitinib [TGA].

---

## 3. Contraindications
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to fedratinib or any of the inactive ingredients (including gelatin, titanium dioxide, and iron oxide red) [TGA]. 

---

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Encephalopathy and Wernicke’s Encephalopathy (WE)
**Boxed Warning:** Serious and potentially fatal encephalopathy, including Wernicke’s, has been reported in approximately 1.3% of patients treated with fedratinib in clinical trials [FDA, TGA]. WE is a medical emergency caused by thiamine (Vitamin B1) deficiency. 

*   **Clinical Presentation:** Healthcare providers should monitor for ataxia, mental status changes, and ophthalmoplegia (e.g., nystagmus, diplopia). Confusion or memory impairment requires immediate evaluation.
*   **Management:** Do not initiate treatment in patients with existing thiamine deficiency. Replenish thiamine levels prior to starting therapy. All patients must receive daily thiamine prophylaxis during treatment. If WE is suspected, immediately discontinue the drug and initiate parenteral thiamine.

### Hematologic Toxicities
Fedratinib may cause significant anemia, thrombocytopenia, and neutropenia. 
*   **Monitoring:** Obtain a complete blood count (CBC) at baseline, periodically during treatment, and as clinically indicated. 
*   **Risk Factors:** Patients with a baseline hemoglobin <10.0 g/dL or platelet count <100 x 10⁹/L are at higher risk for Grade 3 or 4 toxicities [TGA].

### Gastrointestinal Toxicity
Nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea are frequent. Prophylactic anti-emetics (e.g., 5-HT3 receptor antagonists) are recommended. Severe diarrhea should be treated promptly with antidiarrheals.

### Hepatic and Pancreatic Elevation
Elevations in ALT, AST, amylase, and lipase have occurred. Monitor these levels at baseline and periodically. Treatment should be interrupted or discontinued for Grade 3 or higher elevations.

### Uveitis
Uveitis has been reported in post-marketing surveillance. The incidence may be higher in specific ethnic groups, such as Japanese patients (up to 55% of reported cases in some datasets) [FDA, TGA]. Patients reporting eye pain, redness, or vision changes require prompt ophthalmologic evaluation.

### Major Adverse Cardiac Events (MACE) and Thrombosis
Though fedratinib-specific rates were similar to placebo in trials, other JAK inhibitors have shown increased risks of MACE (myocardial infarction, stroke) and thrombosis (DVT/PE). Use caution in patients with cardiovascular risk factors or those who are current/past smokers.

### Symptom Exacerbation
Discontinuing or interrupting JAK inhibitors can lead to a flare of myeloproliferative symptoms, potentially causing fever, respiratory distress, or multi-organ failure. A gradual dose taper should be considered when stopping for reasons other than life-threatening toxicity [FDA].

---

## 5. Dosage and Administration
### Recommended Dose
The standard dose is **400 mg taken orally once daily**.  
Patients must have a baseline platelet count of $\ge$50 x 10⁹/L before starting treatment. Fedratinib can be taken with or without food, though a high-fat meal may improve gastrointestinal tolerance.

### Pre-treatment Requirements
*   Assess thiamine (Vitamin B1) levels.
*   Perform a CBC, hepatic panel, and amylase/lipase tests.
*   Taper and discontinue ruxolitinib according to its specific prescribing information before starting fedratinib.

### Administration Information
*   **Missed Doses:** Skip the missed dose and take the next dose at the regular time the following day. Do not double the dose.
*   **Swallowing Difficulties:** For patients unable to swallow capsules, the contents may be dispersed in 180 mL of Ensure Plus and administered orally or via a French size 14 or 16 nasogastric tube [FDA].

### Dose Modifications
*   **Renal Impairment:** For severe renal impairment (CrCl 15–29 mL/min), reduce the dose to 200 mg daily.
*   **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Reduce the dose to 200 mg daily if co-administered with strong inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, ritonavir).
*   **Hematologic Toxicity:** Interrupt dose for Grade 4 neutropenia or Grade 3/4 thrombocytopenia until resolved to $\le$ Grade 2, then restart at 100 mg lower than the previous dose.

---

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most common adverse reactions ($\ge$20%) include diarrhea, nausea, anemia, and vomiting.

**Significant Adverse Events:**
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (up to 66%), Nausea (62%), Vomiting (39%).
*   **Hematologic:** Anemia (up to 74% all grades), Thrombocytopenia (47%), Neutropenia (23%).
*   **Infection:** Pneumonia and urinary tract infections have been reported.
*   **Cardiac:** Fatal cardiogenic shock occurred in 1% of patients in the JAKARTA trial [FDA].

---

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Strong inhibitors increase fedratinib exposure (e.g., a 3-fold increase in AUC with ketoconazole). Reduce fedratinib dose to 200 mg.
*   **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Strong and moderate inducers (e.g., rifampicin, efavirenz) significantly decrease fedratinib exposure and should be avoided.
*   **Effect on Other Drugs:** Fedratinib is a moderate inhibitor of CYP3A4, CYP2C19, and CYP2D6. It may increase concentrations of drugs like midazolam, omeprazole, and metoprolol, requiring dose monitoring of those agents.
*   **Metformin:** Fedratinib reduces the renal clearance of metformin by 36% [TGA]. Monitor blood glucose and adjust metformin doses as necessary.

---

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
**Australian Category D:** Fedratinib may cause fetal harm. Animal studies showed embryofetal lethality and skeletal variations at exposures below the recommended human dose. Women of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for at least one month after the final dose.

### Breastfeeding
There are no data regarding the presence of fedratinib in human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in the infant, breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for at least one month following the last dose.

---

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Peak plasma concentrations ($C_{max}$) are reached within 3 hours at steady state. Absolute bioavailability is approximately 77% [TGA].
*   **Distribution:** High volume of distribution (1770 L), indicating extensive tissue distribution. Protein binding is approximately 92% (mainly to $\alpha$1-acid glycoprotein).
*   **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized by CYP3A4, with minor contributions from CYP2C19 and FMO3.
*   **Elimination:** Effective half-life is approximately 41 hours; terminal half-life is approximately 114 hours. Excretion is primarily via feces (77%).

---

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Fedratinib is an oral kinase inhibitor with selective activity against wild-type and mutationally activated Janus Associated Kinase 2 (JAK2) and FMS-like tyrosine kinase 3 (FLT3). It inhibits JAK2-mediated phosphorylation of STAT3/5 proteins, which are critical for cell proliferation in myeloproliferative neoplasms.

---

## 11. Storage Conditions
Store below 30°C (86°F). Keep the capsules in the original bottle to protect them from moisture and out of reach of children. Discard any dispersed medication if not used within 2 hours of preparation [FDA].
