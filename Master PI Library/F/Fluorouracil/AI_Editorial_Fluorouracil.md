# Editorial Product Information: Fluorouracil and Salicylic Acid

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for educational and reference purposes for healthcare professionals. It does not replace the official, legally approved Product Information required for prescribing within any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Fluorouracil (a pyrimidine antimetabolite) and Salicylic Acid (a phenolic aromatic acid/keratolytic).
- **Brand Name:** **Actikerall** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** D06BB01 (Fluorouracil, combinations).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Topical solution containing 5 mg/g (0.5%) fluorouracil and 100 mg/g (10%) salicylic acid [Australian].
- **Excipients of Note:** Contains 19% v/v ethanol absolute and dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Topical use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Mayne Pharma International Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Solar (Actinic) Keratosis:** Indicated for the topical treatment of solar keratosis of mild to moderate severity (Grade I and II according to Olsen 1991) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** Apply once daily to the affected area or individual lesions until cleared or for a maximum of 12 weeks.
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Use the provided brush applicator.
    - WIPE the brush on the neck of the bottle before application to avoid overloading.
    - For individual lesions: Apply to the lesion and a maximum 0.5 cm rim of healthy surrounding skin. Up to ten lesions may be treated simultaneously.
    - For field treatment: Treat a contiguous area of skin, ensuring the total treatment area at any one time does not exceed 25 cm² (e.g., 5 cm x 5 cm).
    - If the required area exceeds 25 cm², treat the most clinically relevant section first, then proceed to the next area once the first course is complete.
    - **Film Formation:** The solution dries to form a white film. Before each reapplication, the existing film must be removed by peeling it off (warm water may assist).
- **Titration:** If severe local skin reactions occur, application frequency may be reduced to three times per week until symptoms improve.
- **Thin Epidermis:** Areas with thin skin (e.g., around eyes and temples) should be treated less frequently and monitored closely.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to fluorouracil, salicylic acid, acetylsalicylic acid (aspirin), or any excipients.
- **Pregnancy and Lactation:** Contraindicated during pregnancy, in women for whom pregnancy cannot be excluded, and during breastfeeding [Australian].
- **Renal Insufficiency:** Must not be used in patients with renal impairment.
- **DPD Status:** Contraindicated in patients with known dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase (DPD) deficiency or in those receiving potent DPD inhibitors (e.g., certain nucleoside analogues).
- **Site of Application:** Must not come into contact with eyes or mucous membranes.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [Australian]:** **Actikerall** should only be used on a total area no greater than 25 cm² at a time.
- **Application Limits:** Use is restricted to the face, forehead, and bald scalp. Do not use on bleeding lesions.
- **Local Skin Responses:** Erythema, inflammation, burning, and crusting are common and generally indicate a therapeutic response. However, severe reactions (ulceration, erosion) may occur.
- **UV Exposure:** Solar keratosis is caused by chronic UV damage. Patients must protect treated areas from direct sunlight using clothing, hats, and broad-spectrum sunscreen (SPF 30+).
- **Systemic Absorption:** Risk increases if applied to thin epidermis or large areas. DMSO in the formulation may act as a skin irritant.
- **DPD Deficiency:** 5-fluorouracil is metabolised by DPD. Deficiency leads to accumulation and potential systemic toxicity. DPD activity should be checked if clinically indicated before starting treatment.
- **Sensory Disturbances:** Patients with conditions like diabetes mellitus require close monitoring due to potential impaired wound healing or reduced sensation at the application site.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are primarily localized to the site of administration and are related to the pharmacological action of the components.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Application site irritation, burning, inflammation, pruritus, pain, erythema. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Application site erosion, scab formation, bleeding, skin exfoliation, headache. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Dermatitis, oedema, ulcer, skin plaque, dry eye, eye pruritus, lacrimation increased, gastroenteritis, influenza. |

- **Post-marketing reports:** Isolated cases of allergic reactions, contact dermatitis, and application site scarring.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **DPD Inhibitors:** Nucleoside analogues (e.g., brivudine, sorivudine) significantly increase plasma fluorouracil levels and toxicity. A minimum 4-week interval must be observed between the use of fluorouracil and these agents.
- **Phenytoin:** Systemic fluorouracil can increase phenytoin plasma levels, leading to toxicity. While topical absorption is low, monitoring is advised.
- **Methotrexate and Sulphonylureas:** Absorbed salicylic acid may potentially interact with these agents, though systemic absorption from this product is typically minimal.
- **Dermatological Agents:** Concurrent use of other peeling or abrasive agents (e.g., benzoyl peroxide, tretinoin, medicated cleansers) may cause cumulative irritation.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D [Australian]**. There are no data on topical use in pregnant women, but systemic 5-FU is teratogenic in animals. The risk to the human foetus is unknown; therefore, use is strictly contraindicated.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if fluorouracil or its metabolites are excreted in human milk. Use is contraindicated.
- **Fertility:** Systemic 5-FU may cause transient male infertility and reduced pregnancy rates in rodents, but this is considered unlikely to be relevant to humans given the very low systemic absorption of this topical product.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established; solar keratosis is not a paediatric condition.
- **Elderly Use:** The usual adult dose may be used.
- **Renal Impairment:** Use is contraindicated due to the salicylic acid component and potential for systemic accumulation.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specifically addressed in available sources, though systemic 5-FU is primarily metabolised in the liver.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:**
    - **Fluorouracil (5-FU):** A cytostatic antimetabolite. It mimics thymine, inhibiting DNA and RNA synthesis. This primarily affects cells with accelerated growth, such as those in solar keratoses.
    - **Salicylic Acid:** A keratolytic and corneolytic agent. It reduces corneocyte adhesion and removes intercellular lipids, which facilitates the penetration of fluorouracil into hyperkeratotic lesions.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Percutaneous absorption of 5-FU is very low (approx. 0.0085% to 0.035%). Salicylic acid absorption is also minimal (~15% of the exposure seen with oral aspirin). Absorption increases significantly on damaged or eczematous skin.
    - **Distribution:** Systemically absorbed 5-FU enters cells via passive diffusion and distributes to all tissues, including the brain.
    - **Metabolism:** Absorbed 5-FU is metabolised in the liver via DPD to dehydrofluorouracil. Salicylic acid is conjugated in the liver to salicyluric acid and glucuronides.
    - **Elimination:** 5-FU is partly exhaled as $CO_2$. Salicylic acid is excreted renally.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Focal Study (Study 98605101-0702):** A randomised, double-blind trial (n=470) showed that **Actikerall** achieved histological clearance in 70.1% of patients compared to 54.1% for diclofenac 3% gel and 42.7% for placebo. Complete clinical clearance (all lesions) was 55.4% for **Actikerall**.
- **Field Directed Study (Study 98605101-1401):** Targeted a 25 cm² area with 4-10 lesions. Complete clinical clearance 8 weeks post-treatment was 49.5% for **Actikerall** vs. 18.2% for vehicle.
- **Comparison with Cryotherapy (Study 1005-6002-1007):** **Actikerall** once daily for 6 weeks achieved a histological clearance rate of 62.1% vs. 41.9% for cryotherapy. Recurrence rates at 6 months were lower with **Actikerall** (27.3%) compared to cryotherapy (67.7%).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C. 
- **Handling:** Do not freeze or refrigerate. Keep the bottle tightly closed to prevent the solution from drying out.
- **Shelf Life:** Discard 3 months after the first opening of the bottle.
- **Cautions:** The solution is flammable; keep away from fire or flames. It may cause permanent stains on textiles or acrylics.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Subject to additional monitoring in Australia to identify new safety information.
