# Editorial Product Information: Famciclovir

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing or dispensing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Famciclovir (a synthetic guanine derivative).
- **Chemical Class:** Nucleoside analogue antiviral.
- **Brand Name(s):** APO-Famciclovir [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J05AB09.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 125 mg, 250 mg, and 500 mg [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Famciclovir is indicated for the following conditions [Australian-approved]:
- **Herpes Zoster (Shingles):** Treatment in adults; therapy should ideally commence within 72 hours of rash onset.
- **Genital Herpes:** 
    - Treatment of recurrent episodes in adults and adolescents (12 years and older).
    - Chronic suppression of recurrent genital herpes.
- **Herpes Labialis (Cold Sores):** Treatment of recurrent episodes in immunocompetent adults.
- **Immunocompromised Patients:** 
    - Treatment of uncomplicated herpes zoster.
    - Treatment and suppression of recurrent herpes simplex.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult Immunocompetent)
- **Herpes Zoster:** 250 mg three times daily for 7 days.
- **Recurrent Genital Herpes:** 
    - 500 mg initially, followed by 250 mg every 12 hours for 3 doses; OR
    - 125 mg twice daily for 5 days; OR
    - 1000 mg twice daily for 1 day.
- **Suppression of Genital Herpes:** 250 mg twice daily.
- **Herpes Labialis:** 1500 mg as a single dose or 750 mg twice daily for 1 day.

### Immunocompromised Dosing
- **Herpes Zoster:** 500 mg three times daily for 10 days.
- **Recurrent HSV:** 500 mg twice daily for 7 days.
- **Suppression of Genital Herpes (HIV patients):** 500 mg twice daily.

### Administration Instructions
- May be taken with or without food. 
- Therapy should be initiated at the first sign or symptom (e.g., tingling, itching, burning) or as soon as a rash or lesion appears.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **famciclovir** or any of its excipients.
- Known hypersensitivity to **penciclovir** (the active metabolite of famciclovir).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Efficacy Limits:** Not studied in ophthalmic zoster, chickenpox, or zoster encephalomyelitis.
- **Transmission Risk:** Genital herpes is sexually transmitted. Patients should practice safer sex (condoms) and avoid intercourse when symptoms are present, as transmission can occur even during antiviral therapy or asymptomatic shedding.
- **CNS Effects:** May cause dizziness, somnolence, or confusion. Patients experiencing these symptoms should avoid driving or operating heavy machinery.
- **Severe Hepatic Impairment:** Not studied; conversion to penciclovir may be impaired, potentially reducing efficacy.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Famciclovir is generally well-tolerated. Frequencies are based on [Australian] clinical trial and post-marketing data.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Headache |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Dizziness, vomiting, nausea, abdominal pain, diarrhoea, abnormal liver function tests, rash, pruritus |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Confusion (predominantly in the elderly), somnolence (predominantly in the elderly), angioedema (face, eyelid, periorbital, pharyngeal), urticaria |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Hallucinations, palpitations, cholestatic jaundice |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Thrombocytopenia, serious skin reactions (Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, erythema multiforme), arthralgia, myalgia |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Probenecid:** Concurrent use may increase plasma concentrations of penciclovir.
- **Aldehyde Oxidase Inhibitors:** Conversion of the precursor 6-deoxy penciclovir to penciclovir is mediated by aldehyde oxidase. **Raloxifene** (a potent inhibitor) may reduce penciclovir formation; monitor efficacy if co-administered.
- **Other Renal Drugs:** Medications affecting renal physiology may alter penciclovir levels.
- **No significant interactions:** Zidovudine, cimetidine, and promethazine showed no relevant clinical interactions.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B1]. Animal studies show no embryotoxic or teratogenic effects. However, safety in human pregnancy is not established. Use only if potential benefits outweigh risks.
- **Breastfeeding:** Excreted in rat milk; human data is unavailable. Breastfeeding is not recommended unless clinical benefit justifies the risk.
- **Fertility:** No significant effects on sperm count, morphology, or motility were observed in men treated with 250 mg twice daily for 18 weeks.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children under 12 (genital herpes) or for other indications in children/adolescents have not been established.
- **Elderly Use:** No specific precautions required if renal function is normal.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for mild-to-moderate impairment. No data for severe impairment.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dose adjustments are mandatory based on creatinine clearance (CrCl).

**Dosage Adjustment Examples (Immunocompetent):**
- **CrCl 10–29 mL/min:** 
    - Zoster: 250 mg every 24 hours.
    - Genital Herpes (5-day): 125 mg every 12 hours.
- **Haemodialysis:** For conditions other than herpes labialis, a 48-hour dosing interval is recommended; administer a full dose immediately after dialysis.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Famciclovir is a prodrug of **penciclovir**. In virus-infected cells, viral thymidine kinase phosphorylates penciclovir to its triphosphate form. This inhibits viral DNA polymerase by competing with deoxyguanosine triphosphate, terminating the DNA chain and halting replication.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; bioavailability of penciclovir is 77%.
    - **Distribution:** Low plasma protein binding (<20%).
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly converted to penciclovir; no unchanged famciclovir is found in urine.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal via tubular secretion and glomerular filtration. Terminal half-life is ~2 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Herpes Zoster:** Trials showed famciclovir 500 mg t.i.d. reduced the time to full crusting (5 days vs 7 days for placebo) and reduced the duration of post-herpetic neuralgia.
- **Genital Herpes:** A 1-day 1000 mg b.i.d. regimen was shown to be as effective as longer courses for episodic treatment. Suppressive therapy (250 mg b.i.d.) maintained approximately 70% of patients recurrence-free over 12 months.
- **Herpes Labialis:** A single 1500 mg dose significantly reduced healing time compared to placebo (4.4 days vs 6.2 days).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C.
- Keep in the original container to protect from light and moisture.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
