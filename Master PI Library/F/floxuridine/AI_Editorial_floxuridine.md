# Editorial Product Information: Floxuridine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA). 
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **floxuridine** for healthcare professionals, synthesising information from official Product Information (PI) sources. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and not a replacement for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing. At the time of this review, only the United States (FDA) PI was provided for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Floxuridine (a fluorinated pyrimidine antimetabolite; 2’-deoxy-5-fluorouridine).
- **Brand Names:** Floxuridine for Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** L01BC03.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Lyophilized powder for reconstitution: 500 mg per 5 mL vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Continuous regional intra-arterial infusion only [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Fresenius Kabi USA, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Gastrointestinal Adenocarcinoma Metastatic to the Liver:** Indicated for the palliative management of patients considered incurable by surgery or other means, provided the disease is confined to an area capable of infusion via a single artery [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The recommended therapeutic dosage range is 0.1 mg/kg/day to 0.6 mg/kg/day [US].
- **Dosing by Site:** 
    - **Hepatic Artery Infusion:** Higher dosage ranges (0.4 mg/kg to 0.6 mg/kg) are typically utilized for hepatic artery infusion because the liver metabolizes the drug, reducing systemic toxicity [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - **Reconstitution:** Reconstitute the 500 mg vial with 5 mL of sterile water for injection to reach a concentration of 100 mg/mL [US].
    - **Dilution:** Further dilute the calculated daily dose with 5% Dextrose or 0.9% Sodium Chloride injection to a volume suitable for the infusion pump [US].
    - **Apparatus:** Use an appropriate pump to ensure uniform infusion rates and to overcome arterial pressure [US].
- **Maintenance/Titration:** Therapy should be continued until adverse reactions appear. Once toxicities subside, therapy may be resumed [US]. 
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Patients in a poor nutritional state [US].
- Patients with depressed bone marrow function [US].
- Patients with potentially serious infections [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [US]:** **Floxuridine** must be administered under the supervision of a physician experienced in cancer chemotherapy and intra-arterial therapy. Due to the high risk of severe toxic reactions, patients must be hospitalized for the initiation of the first course of therapy.
- **General Warnings:** 
    - **Toxicity Profile:** The drug has a narrow margin of safety. Severe hematological toxicity and gastrointestinal hemorrhage may occur [US].
    - **Poor Risk Patients:** Use with extreme caution in patients with impaired hepatic or renal function, history of high-dose pelvic irradiation, or previous use of alkylating agents [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Careful monitoring of white blood cell (WBC) count and platelet count is mandatory [US].
- **Mandatory Discontinuation Criteria:** Therapy must be stopped immediately upon the first sign of any of the following:
    - Myocardial ischemia [US].
    - Stomatitis or esophagopharyngitis [US].
    - Leukopenia (WBC < 3,500/mm³) or a rapidly falling WBC count [US].
    - Intractable vomiting or frequent diarrhea/watery stools [US].
    - Gastrointestinal ulceration or bleeding [US].
    - Thrombocytopenia (Platelets < 100,000/mm³) [US].
    - Hemorrhage from any site [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are frequently related to the arterial infusion procedure or the drug's antimetabolic activity.

- **Very Common (>10%) / Common (1–10%):** 
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, enteritis, stomatitis [US].
    - **Dermatologic:** Localized erythema [US].
    - **Laboratory:** Anemia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, elevations in alkaline phosphatase, serum transaminase, bilirubin, and LDH [US].
- **Other Reported Reactions:**
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Duodenal ulcer, duodenitis, gastritis, glossitis, pharyngitis, anorexia, abdominal cramps; intra- and extrahepatic biliary sclerosis; acalculous cholecystitis [US].
    - **Dermatologic:** Alopecia (usually transient), dermatitis, nonspecific rash [US].
    - **Cardiovascular:** Myocardial ischemia [US].
    - **Procedural Complications:** Arterial aneurysm, arterial ischemia/thrombosis, embolism, fibromyositis, thrombophlebitis, hepatic necrosis, abscesses, and catheter-related issues (infection, blockage, leakage) [US].
- **Potential (Based on 5-FU similarity):** Pancytopenia, agranulocytosis, anaphylaxis, acute cerebellar syndrome, nystagmus, and photosensitivity [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Any therapy that increases patient stress, interferes with nutrition, or depresses bone marrow function will increase the toxicity of **floxuridine** [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category D:** Floxuridine can cause fetal harm when administered to pregnant women. Animal studies show teratogenicity (cleft palate, skeletal defects) at doses significantly lower than human therapeutic doses [US].
    - **Recommendation:** Women of childbearing potential should be advised to avoid pregnancy. If the drug is used during pregnancy, the patient must be apprised of the potential risk to the fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if the drug is excreted in human milk. Because **floxuridine** inhibits DNA and RNA synthesis, breastfeeding is contraindicated while receiving the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal data for 5-fluorouracil (a metabolite) indicates induced chromosomal aberrations and inhibited spermatogonia differentiation. **Floxuridine** may have similar adverse effects on gametogenesis [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in pediatric patients have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed; however, general cautions regarding "poor risk" patients apply [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Use with extreme caution. The liver is the primary site of metabolism; impaired function may increase the risk of systemic toxicity [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A fluorinated pyrimidine antimetabolite. When administered via rapid intra-arterial injection, it is catabolized to 5-fluorouracil (5-FU). Its primary effect is the interference with DNA synthesis and, to a lesser extent, RNA inhibition. Continuous infusion enhances its direct anabolism to floxuridine-monophosphate, increasing DNA inhibition [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized in the liver [US].
    - **Elimination:** Excreted in the urine as intact drug, urea, 5-FU, and various urea metabolites; also expired as respiratory CO₂ [US].
    - **Pharmacokinetic data** for continuous intra-arterial infusion is currently unavailable [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Trial Summary:** Not specifically named in the source document. Clinical efficacy is established based on the drug's effectiveness in the palliative management of gastrointestinal adenocarcinoma metastatic to the liver in incurable patients [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vial Storage:** Store sterile powder at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Reconstituted vials should be refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) and used within 2 weeks [US].
- **Handling:** Procedures for the proper handling and disposal of anticancer drugs should be followed [US].

- **Legal Classification:** 
    - **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription only) [US].
