# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **United States (FDA)**. 
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine for use by healthcare professionals.
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to the local approved labeling for their region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn metabolic errors [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Administer intravenously.
- Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use [US].

### Inborn Errors of Metabolism
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg, administered as a slow 2- to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often given, followed by an equivalent dose over the next 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours (q3h or q4h) and never less frequently than every 6 hours (q6h) [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** Up to 300 mg/kg has been administered in clinical practice [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
- **Administration Timing:** Administer as a slow 2- to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Monitoring
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels prior to therapy.
- Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations is recommended. 
- Target plasma free carnitine concentration: 35 to 60 µmol/L [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Hypersensitivity Reactions
- **Serious Reactions:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration [US].
- **Management:** Discontinue treatment immediately upon signs of severe hypersensitivity. Re-administration should only occur after a careful risk-benefit analysis and under close monitoring [US].

### Seizures
- Levocarnitine may increase the frequency or severity of seizures in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a history of seizure activity [US].

### General Precautions
- **Oral Levocarnitine in Renal Impairment:** The safety of the oral formulation has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse event data are largely derived from trials in ESRD patients on chronic hemodialysis.

### Common to Very Common Adverse Effects (≥5%)
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (21–35%), headache (3–37%), chest pain (6–15%), fever (5–12%), abdominal pain (5–21%), flu syndrome (15–29%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), rhinitis (6–11%), dyspnea (3–11%).
- **Metabolic/Nutritional:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hypervolemia (3–12%).
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%).

### Other Reported Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea/vomiting; body odor and gastritis have been noted less frequently.
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (see Warnings and Precautions).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels in patients on warfarin therapy after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- **US Category:** Not specified as a lettered category in source. 
- **Data:** Animal reproductive studies (rats/rabbits) at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm.
- **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].

### Breastfeeding
- **Data:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration [US].
- **Recommendation:** The risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefit of supplementation for the mother. Consideration may be given to discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg for metabolic disorders); see Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in provided source.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily studied in ESRD patients on dialysis. Precautions regarding metabolite accumulation apply specifically to chronic high-dose oral therapy [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production [US].

### Pharmacodynamics
- In patients with inborn errors of metabolism, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are quickly eliminated.
- In ESRD, it replenishes low plasma carnitine levels caused by reduced dietary intake (meat/dairy), decreased renal synthesis, and losses during dialysis [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. 
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Following a single IV dose (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. 
- **Half-life:** Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Studies:** Healthy volunteers receiving IV levocarnitine (20 mg/kg bolus) followed a two-compartment model. ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly reached stable trough concentrations (between 54 and 180 µmol/L) within one week [US].
- **Dose-Ranging in ESRD:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Clinical Outcomes Note:** While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations is proven, the effects on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, cardiomyopathy) in the ESRD population have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription only (Rx only). Classified as a Human Prescription Drug [FDA].
