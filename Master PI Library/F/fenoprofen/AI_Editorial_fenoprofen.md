# Editorial Product Information: Fenoprofen

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of fenoprofen, synthesised for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Fenoprofen calcium (arylacetic acid derivative)
- **Brand Names:** Fenoprofen calcium capsules (e.g., Atland Pharmaceuticals) [US]
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Capsules: 200 mg and 400 mg [US]
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Atland Pharmaceuticals, LLC [US]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Analgesia:** Relief of mild to moderate pain in adults [US-approved].
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis:** Relief of signs and symptoms [US-approved].
- **Osteoarthritis:** Relief of signs and symptoms [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Principle:** Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration necessary to achieve treatment goals.
- **Analgesia (Mild to Moderate Pain):** 200 mg orally every 4 to 6 hours as needed [US].
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis and Osteoarthritis:** 
    - Recommended dose: 400 mg to 600 mg orally, 3 or 4 times daily [US].
    - Adjust dosage based on patient response and symptom severity.
    - Full therapeutic benefits may require 2 to 3 weeks of continuous therapy [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** Total daily dosage should not exceed 3,200 mg [US].
- **Administration:** May be taken with meals or milk to improve tolerability; however, this may delay and diminish peak blood levels [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to fenoprofen or any product excipients.
- **Aspirin/NSAID Sensitivity:** History of asthma, urticaria, or other allergic-type reactions following the use of aspirin or other NSAIDs. Severe, rarely fatal, anaphylactic reactions have been reported [US].
- **Surgical Setting:** Use in the setting of coronary artery bypass graft (CABG) surgery [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Cardiovascular and Gastrointestinal Risk [US]:**
    - **Cardiovascular (CV) Thrombotic Events:** NSAIDs increase the risk of serious CV thrombotic events, including myocardial infarction and stroke, which can be fatal. Risk may occur early and increases with duration of use.
    - **Gastrointestinal (GI) Events:** Increased risk of serious GI adverse events including bleeding, ulceration, and perforation of the stomach or intestines. These can be fatal and may occur without warning. The elderly and those with a history of peptic ulcer disease or GI bleeding are at higher risk.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Elevations in ALT/AST (3x ULN) occur in ~1% of patients. Discontinue if liver disease signs develop or systemic manifestations (e.g., eosinophilia, rash) occur [US].
- **Hypertension:** NSAIDs can lead to new-onset or worsening of pre-existing hypertension. Monitor blood pressure throughout therapy [US].
- **Heart Failure and Edema:** Avoid use in patients with severe heart failure unless benefits outweigh risks. NSAIDs may blunt the effects of diuretics and ACE inhibitors [US].
- **Renal Toxicity:** Long-term use may cause renal papillary necrosis. Monitor renal function in patients with impairment, heart failure, liver dysfunction, or the elderly. Avoid use in advanced renal disease [US].
- **Anaphylactic Reactions:** May occur in patients without prior exposure [US].
- **Serious Skin Reactions:** Discontinue at the first sign of skin rash. Includes Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN), and Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS) [US].
- **Fetal Toxicity:** Avoid use after 30 weeks gestation (ductus arteriosus closure). Limit use between 20–30 weeks gestation due to risk of oligohydramnios/fetal renal dysfunction [US].
- **Hematologic Toxicity:** Anemia and increased bleeding risk may occur. Monitor hemoglobin/hematocrit if symptoms of anemia arise [US].
- **Masking of Infection:** Antipyretic and anti-inflammatory activity may mask signs of infection [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Anticoagulants (e.g., Warfarin):** Synergistic effect on bleeding; monitor closely [US].
- **Antiplatelets/SSRIs/SNRIs:** Increased risk of GI bleeding [US].
- **ACE Inhibitors, ARBs, Beta-blockers:** Diminished antihypertensive effect. In elderly or volume-depleted patients, co-administration may cause acute renal failure [US].
- **Diuretics:** Reduced natriuretic effect of loop and thiazide diuretics [US].
- **Digoxin:** Increased serum concentration and prolonged half-life of digoxin [US].
- **Lithium:** Increased plasma lithium levels (mean 15% increase) and reduced renal clearance [US].
- **Methotrexate:** Increased risk of methotrexate toxicity (neutropenia, renal dysfunction) [US].
- **Cyclosporine:** Increased nephrotoxicity [US].
- **Pemetrexed:** Increased risk of myelosuppression and GI toxicity. Avoid fenoprofen 2 days before and after pemetrexed administration [US].
- **Phenobarbital:** May decrease the plasma half-life of fenoprofen; dosage adjustment may be required [US].
- **Albumin-Bound Drugs (Hydantoins, Sulfonamides):** Fenoprofen may displace these drugs, increasing their activity and potential toxicity [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:**
    - **First/Second Trimester:** No adequate human studies. Animal data showed embryo-fetal lethality in rabbits at 0.6x the maximum human daily dose [US].
    - **Week 20 Gestation:** Limit use and monitor for oligohydramnios if use exceeds 48 hours [US].
    - **Week 30 Gestation and Later:** Contraindicated due to risk of premature closure of the fetal ductus arteriosus [US].
    - **Labor/Delivery:** No studies; however, NSAIDs may delay parturition and increase stillbirth risk [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Breastmilk levels are ~1.6% of maternal plasma levels. Due to limited experience, other agents may be preferred, especially with newborns or preterm infants [US].
- **Fertility:** NSAIDs may delay or prevent ovulation; consider withdrawal in women having difficulty conceiving [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established in patients under 18 years [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Increased risk of CV, GI, and renal adverse reactions. Start at the low end of the dosing range and monitor closely [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not recommended in advanced renal disease. Monitor renal function closely if used [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Monitor for worsening liver function; peak plasma levels are not significantly altered in stable alcoholic cirrhosis [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibition of cyclooxygenase (COX-1 and COX-2), leading to reduced prostaglandin synthesis. It possesses analgesic, anti-inflammatory, and antipyretic properties [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed under fasting conditions; peak levels in ~2 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (99%), primarily to albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily conjugated to glucuronides.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 90% excreted in urine within 24 hours. Plasma half-life is ~3 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
Clinical studies in rheumatoid arthritis and osteoarthritis showed fenoprofen to be comparable to aspirin in controlling disease activity, with a lower frequency of tinnitus and mild GI reactions (nausea, dyspepsia) compared to aspirin [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Preserve in well-closed containers [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription-only (Rx-only).
