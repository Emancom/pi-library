# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) sourced from the **United States (FDA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a cohesive reference.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for educational and professional reference. It does not replace the official PI required for prescribing within any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dose:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (over 2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Frequency:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically in the range of 50 mg/kg or as clinically required. Doses as high as 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Therapy should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be implemented as early as the third or fourth week of treatment if plasma levels are adequate (normal range: 40–50 µmol/L) [US].

### Monitoring and Instructions
- **Plasma Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine levels prior to starting parenteral therapy, followed by weekly and monthly monitoring. Ideal free carnitine concentrations should be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L.
- **Inspection:** Parenteral products should be visually inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occurred within minutes of injection. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and monitor for recurrence if re-administration is attempted [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine has been associated with reports of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Levocarnitine):** While the injection is used in ESRD, the safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting are common. Gastritis and abdominal pain have also been reported.
- **Body Odor:** Body odor has been noted as a less frequent adverse effect.
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (see Section 5).
- **ESRD Clinical Trial Data:** In double-blind, placebo-controlled trials of ESRD patients on hemodialysis, the following events occurred at a frequency of ≥5% (regardless of causality):

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. However, adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women are unavailable. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate levocarnitine concentrations in milk increase following administration. The benefits of maternal supplementation must be weighed against the potential risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing guidelines for metabolic disorders are applicable to paediatric patients. Safety and efficacy in this population are consistent with the adult profile [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used for carnitine deficiency in ESRD patients on dialysis (see Section 3). Caution is advised regarding the accumulation of metabolites if oral formulations are used in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are then cleared from the body.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Efficacy:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly, mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine concentrations rose from 20 µmol/L (baseline) to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While the increase in concentrations is proven, the impact on specific clinical outcomes in this population remains undetermined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C.
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Stability:** The formulation contains no preservatives; discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription only (Rx-only). NDC 36000-368-10.
