# Editorial Product Information: Ferric Citrate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of ferric citrate for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource only. It is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing decisions in any particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ferric citrate (equivalent to ferric iron).
- **Chemical Class:** Phosphate binder; Iron replacement product.
- **Brand Name(s):** Ferric Citrate (generic) [US].
- **ATC Code:** V03AE03 (Not explicitly specified in source, but standard classification).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Oral Tablet (Film-coated):** 210 mg of ferric iron, equivalent to 1 g of ferric citrate [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Teva Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hyperphosphatemia:** Indicated for the control of serum phosphorus levels in adult patients with chronic kidney disease (CKD) on dialysis [US].
- **Iron Deficiency Anemia:** Indicated for the treatment of iron deficiency anemia in adult patients with chronic kidney disease not on dialysis (CKD-NDD) [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 General Dosing (Adult)
- **Hyperphosphatemia (CKD on Dialysis):**
    - **Starting Dose:** 2 tablets orally, 3 times per day with meals [US].
    - **Titration:** Adjust dose by 1 to 2 tablets as needed to maintain target serum phosphorus levels. Titration may occur at intervals of 1 week or longer [US].
    - **Maximum Dose:** 12 tablets daily [US].
- **Iron Deficiency Anemia (CKD Not on Dialysis):**
    - **Starting Dose:** 1 tablet orally, 3 times per day with meals [US].
    - **Titration:** Adjust dose as needed to achieve and maintain hemoglobin targets [US].
    - **Maximum Dose:** 12 tablets daily [US].

### 3.2 Administration Instructions
- Tablets must be swallowed whole. 
- **Warning:** Do not chew or crush tablets, as this may cause discoloration of the mouth and teeth [US].
- Administration should always occur with meals [US].

### 3.3 Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Iron Overload Syndromes:** Contraindicated in patients with conditions such as hemochromatosis [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Iron Overload:** Treatment with ferric citrate increases iron stores (ferritin and TSAT). Healthcare providers should assess iron parameters prior to initiation and monitor them during therapy. Patients receiving concomitant intravenous (IV) iron may require a dose reduction or discontinuation of the IV therapy to prevent overload [US].
- **Accidental Pediatric Overdose:** Accidental ingestion of iron-containing products is a leading cause of fatal poisoning in children under 6 years of age. The product must be kept out of reach of children [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Periodic monitoring of serum phosphorus (for dialysis patients), hemoglobin (for anemia patients), and iron parameters (ferritin/TSAT) is essential [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### 6.1 Clinical Trial Experience
Adverse reactions are common, particularly gastrointestinal effects.

| Adverse Reaction | Frequency (CKD on Dialysis) | Frequency (CKD Not on Dialysis) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Discolored Feces** | 19% | 22% |
| **Diarrhea** | 21% | 21% |
| **Constipation** | 8% | 18% |
| **Nausea** | 11% | 10% |
| **Vomiting** | 7% | Not specified (>5%) |
| **Abdominal Pain** | Not specified (>5%) | 5% |
| **Hyperkalemia** | Not specified (>5%) | 5% |
| **Cough** | 6% | Not specified |

- **Discolored Feces:** Patients should be advised that dark-colored stools are normal for oral iron medications and do not indicate GI bleeding [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Gastrointestinal reactions are the most frequent reason for treatment discontinuation (up to 14% in dialysis patients) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### 7.1 Separation of Dosing
When interactions are suspected or when medications have a narrow therapeutic range, consider separating the administration time of ferric citrate from the concomitant drug.
- **Doxycycline:** Administer at least 1 hour before ferric citrate [US].
- **Ciprofloxacin:** Administer at least 2 hours before or after ferric citrate [US].

### 7.2 Clinically Significant Compatibility
The following drugs were studied in vivo and showed no clinically significant changes in exposure (AUC/Cmax) when co-administered with ferric citrate:
- Amlodipine, Aspirin, Atorvastatin, Calcitriol, Clopidogrel, Digoxin, Diltiazem, Doxercalciferol, Enalapril, Fluvastatin, Glimepiride, Levofloxacin, Losartan, Metoprolol, Pravastatin, Propranolol, Sitagliptin, and Warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not specified by letter category in current US labelling. 
    - **Summary:** There are no available human data regarding use in pregnant women. Animal studies using other iron compounds (ferric gluconate) showed malformations at high doses, though ferric/ferrous compounds generally do not. Iron overdose during pregnancy carries risks of spontaneous abortion and fetal malformation [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** No human data exists regarding transfer into milk or effects on the infant. Animal data (rats) indicate iron transfer into milk via specific transporters. Decisions should weigh the clinical need of the mother against the potential risks to the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Not evaluated in human trials [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established. There is a potential risk of increased gastrointestinal toxicity in infants under 6 months of age who are not yet consuming solid food [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies included a significant number of patients aged 65 and older. No overall differences in safety or efficacy were observed between elderly and younger patients [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Ferric citrate is specifically indicated for patients with CKD; no further dose adjustments beyond the indicated titration are specified [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### 10.1 Mechanism of Action
- **Phosphate Binding:** Ferric iron binds dietary phosphate in the GI tract, forming insoluble ferric phosphate, which is excreted in the feces. This reduces serum phosphorus levels [US].
- **Iron Replacement:** Ferric iron is reduced to the ferrous form by ferric reductase in the GI tract and is subsequently absorbed into the systemic circulation, where it binds to transferrin for incorporation into hemoglobin [US].

### 10.2 Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Systemic absorption of iron occurs; however, formal PK studies for ferric citrate have not been conducted [US].
- **Elimination:** Unbound iron/phosphate precipitates are excreted in the feces [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Hyperphosphatemia:** In a 52-week active-controlled trial, ferric citrate effectively maintained serum phosphorus levels compared to active controls (sevelamer/calcium acetate). In a randomized withdrawal phase, serum phosphorus rose by 2.2 mg/dL in the placebo group compared to those remaining on ferric citrate [US].
- **Iron Deficiency Anemia:** In a 16-week placebo-controlled trial of CKD-NDD patients, 52% of patients treated with ferric citrate achieved a hemoglobin increase of ≥1.0 g/dL, compared to 19% in the placebo group [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from moisture [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription only).
