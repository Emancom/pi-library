# Editorial Product Information: Fampridine

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the Product Information (PI) for fampridine. It is designed as a clinical reference for healthcare professionals.
*   **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is not a substitute for the official, legally binding Product Information approved in a specific jurisdiction. Prescribers must consult their local regulatory authority’s approved PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Fampridine (also known as 4-aminopyridine)
*   **Chemical Class:** Potassium channel blocker
*   **Brand Name(s):** **FAMPYRA** [Australian]
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 10 mg modified-release (MR) tablets [Australian].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Link Medical Products Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Symptomatic Walking Improvement in Multiple Sclerosis (MS):** Indicated for adult patients with MS to improve walking ability. Use is restricted to patients who demonstrate a clinical response after an initial 8-week treatment period [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
*   **General Dosing (Adults):** One 10 mg modified-release tablet taken twice daily, approximately 12 hours apart.
*   **Administration Instructions:** Tablets must be swallowed whole. Do not crush, divide, dissolve, suck, or chew the tablets, as this may compromise the modified-release properties. Fampridine may be taken with or without food [Australian].
*   **Treatment Evaluation:** A clinical review of the benefit-risk profile should occur 8 weeks after therapy initiation. Treatment should only continue if a walking test demonstrates a response [Australian].
*   **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, patients should not take a double dose to compensate. The standard 12-hour interval between doses must be maintained.

## 4. Contraindications
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to fampridine or any of the product’s excipients.
*   **Renal Impairment:** Moderate to severe renal impairment, defined as Creatinine Clearance (CrCl) <50 mL/min or eGFR <59 mL/min/1.73m² [Australian].
*   **Seizure History:** Any history of seizures [Australian].
*   **Concomitant Therapy:** Concurrent use with other forms or formulations of fampridine/4-aminopyridine [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Seizures:** A dose-dependent increase in seizure risk has been observed. The recommended 10 mg twice-daily dose must not be exceeded. Use with caution in patients with factors that may lower the seizure threshold. Therapy must be discontinued if a seizure occurs [Australian].
*   **Renal Function Monitoring:** Because fampridine is primarily excreted unchanged by the kidneys, patients with renal impairment are at risk of higher plasma concentrations and associated neurological toxicity. Caution is required in patients with mild renal impairment (CrCl 50–80 mL/min or eGFR 60–89 mL/min/1.73m²), and renal function should be monitored [Australian].
*   **Effects on Driving and Operating Machinery:** Due to potential CNS side effects such as dizziness, vertigo, and seizures, patients should be advised to exercise caution when driving or operating machinery [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are primarily related to nervous system excitation.

| Frequency Category | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (>10%) | Urinary tract infection (UTI) |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Insomnia, anxiety, dizziness, headache, balance disorder, paraesthesia, tremor, nasopharyngitis, dyspnoea, pharyngolaryngeal pain, nausea, vomiting, constipation, dyspepsia, back pain, asthenia |

*   **Serious Adverse Effects:** Seizures and hypersensitivity reactions (including anaphylaxis) have been reported in post-marketing experience [Australian].
*   **Note on UTIs:** While UTIs are reported frequently, the mechanism may involve neuronal stimulation of the bladder mimicking infection symptoms [Australian].
*   **Post-Marketing Reports:** Trigeminal neuralgia (new or exacerbated) and vertigo have been reported [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** 
    *   **OCT2 Inhibitors:** Fampridine is actively secreted via the Organic Cation Transporter-2 (OCT2). Concomitant use with OCT2 inhibitors (e.g., cimetidine) may increase systemic exposure, though studies suggest this may not be clinically meaningful [Australian].
    *   **Renally Secreted Drugs:** Theoretical risk of interaction with other drugs that undergo active renal secretion.
    *   **CYP450 Enzymes:** In vitro data suggest fampridine is unlikely to inhibit or induce major CYP450 enzymes at therapeutic concentrations.
*   **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Use with caution when combined with medications that lower the seizure threshold [Australian].
*   **Specific Clinical Studies:** No pharmacokinetic interactions were observed with interferon-beta or baclofen [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   **Australian Category C.** Fampridine should only be used if the potential benefit outweighs the risk to the foetus. 
    *   Animal studies (rats/rabbits) showed no embryotoxicity at doses up to 10 mg/kg/day, but reduced pup survival and weight gain were noted in rats at doses exceeding 1.5 times the human exposure at MRHD [Australian].
*   **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if fampridine is excreted in human milk. Due to the potential for serious reactions in infants, a choice must be made between discontinuing breastfeeding or discontinuing the drug, based on the clinical importance of the medicine to the mother [Australian].
*   **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in rat studies at doses 8 times the human exposure [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [Australian].
*   **Elderly Use:** Clearance may decrease modestly with age. While no specific dose adjustment is required based on age alone, renal function monitoring is essential in this population [Australian].
*   **Renal Impairment:** 
    *   **Mild:** Use with caution; monitor renal function.
    *   **Moderate to Severe:** Contraindicated [Australian].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment required, as fampridine is primarily excreted renally [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** A non-selective potassium channel blocker. In Multiple Sclerosis, it is thought to block potassium channels in demyelinated axons, reducing current leakage and restoring neuronal conduction and action potential formation.
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption:** Rapidly and completely absorbed. Relative bioavailability of the MR tablet is 95% compared to oral solution.
    *   **Distribution:** Largely unbound to plasma proteins (>90%). Volume of distribution is 2.6 L/kg.
    *   **Metabolism:** Metabolised by oxidation to 3-hydroxy-4-aminopyridine (primarily via CYP2E1) and subsequently conjugated. Metabolites have negligible activity.
    *   **Elimination:** Primarily renal (90% as unchanged drug within 24 hours). Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 6 hours [Australian].
*   **Pharmacodynamics:** Fampridine does not significantly prolong the QTc interval or affect QRS duration [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Pivotal Phase III Studies (MS-F203, MS-F204):** Demonstrated a significantly higher proportion of "Timed Walk Responders" (consistent improvement in Timed 25-foot Walk) compared to placebo. Responder rates were 34.8%–42.9% for fampridine vs. 8.3%–9.3% for placebo.
*   **Study 218MS305:** A 24-week study showing significant improvement in walking ability as measured by the 12-item Multiple Sclerosis Walking Scale (MSWS-12) and the Timed Up and Go (TUG) test.
*   **Observational Study (218MS401):** A Phase IV study of 4,646 patients confirmed the safety profile in routine practice, with a seizure incidence rate of 0.9 per 100 patient-years [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Storage Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
*   **Special Instructions:** Store in the original bottle to protect from moisture.
*   **Shelf Life After Opening:** Use within 7 days of opening the bottle [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **Poison/Schedule Classification:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
