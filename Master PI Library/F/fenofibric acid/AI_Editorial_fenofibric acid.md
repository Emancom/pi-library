# Editorial Product Information: Fenofibric Acid

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a concise, structured format.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local regulatory documentation before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Fenofibric acid (as choline fenofibrate).
- **Chemical Class:** Peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor (PPAR) alpha agonist (Fibrate).
- **Brand Names:** Trilipix [US]; generic formulations available (e.g., Amneal).
- **ATC Code:** C10AB11.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Delayed-release capsules: 45 mg and 135 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Amneal Pharmaceuticals of New York LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
Fenofibric acid is indicated as an adjunct to diet for the following [US]:
- **Severe Hypertriglyceridemia:** To reduce triglyceride (TG) levels in adults with TG ≥ 500 mg/dL.
- **Primary Hyperlipidemia:** To reduce elevated low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) in adults when use of recommended LDL-C lowering therapy is not possible.

**Limitations of Use [US]:**
- Fenofibrate has not been determined to reduce the risk of pancreatitis in patients with markedly elevated TG (e.g., >2,000 mg/dL).
- Large randomized controlled trials (FIELD, ACCORD Lipid) did not demonstrate a reduction in coronary heart disease morbidity or mortality with fenofibrate therapy in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** Administered as a single daily dose, at any time of day, with or without food [US].
- **Severe Hypertriglyceridemia:** 45 mg to 135 mg once daily. Dosage should be adjusted based on patient response following repeat lipid determinations at 4 to 8-week intervals [US].
- **Primary Hyperlipidemia:** 135 mg once daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** Capsules must be swallowed whole. Do not crush, break, dissolve, or chew [US].
- **Monitoring/Discontinuation:** Assess TG levels 4 to 8 weeks after initiation. Discontinue therapy if an adequate response is not achieved after 2 months of treatment at the maximum dose [US].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, patients should take the next dose at the regular time. Do not double the dose [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Renal Impairment:** Severe renal impairment, including end-stage renal disease (ESRD) and patients receiving dialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Disease:** Active liver disease, including unexplained persistent liver function abnormalities [US].
- **Gallbladder Disease:** Pre-existing gallbladder disease [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to fenofibric acid, fenofibrate, or any excipients [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Serious drug-induced liver injury (DILI), including cases requiring transplantation or resulting in death, has occurred. Monitor serum ALT, AST, and total bilirubin at baseline and periodically. Discontinue if enzyme levels persist >3x the upper limit of normal (ULN) or if signs of liver injury develop [US].
- **Myopathy and Rhabdomyolysis:** Risks are increased in the elderly (≥65 years), patients with renal impairment, hypothyroidism, or when co-administered with a statin or colchicine. Discontinue if CK levels are markedly elevated or myopathy is suspected [US].
- **Renal Function:** Reversible increases in serum creatinine have been reported. Monitor renal function in patients with renal impairment and consider monitoring for those at risk (e.g., elderly, diabetes) [US].
- **Cholelithiasis:** Fenofibrates increase cholesterol excretion into the bile, potentially leading to gallstones. Discontinue if gallstones are confirmed [US].
- **Coumarin Anticoagulants:** Potentiates the anticoagulant effect (prolongs PT/INR). Monitor INR frequently and adjust coumarin dosage as needed [US].
- **Pancreatitis:** Reported in patients taking fibrates; may be due to failure of efficacy in severe hypertriglyceridemia or secondary to biliary obstruction [US].
- **Hematologic Changes:** Possible mild-to-moderate decreases in hemoglobin, hematocrit, and white blood cell counts. Periodic monitoring is recommended during the first 12 months [US].
- **Paradoxical HDL Decrease:** Severe decreases in HDL-C (as low as 2 mg/dL) have occurred weeks to years after initiation. Check HDL-C within the first few months; if severely depressed, discontinue and do not restart [US].
- **Venothromboembolic Disease:** Increased risk of pulmonary embolism and deep vein thrombosis has been observed in clinical trials [US].

**Other Adverse Reactions (≥3% for Fenofibric Acid):** Diarrhea, dyspepsia, pain, nasopharyngitis, sinusitis, upper respiratory tract infection, arthralgia, myalgia, pain in extremity, and dizziness [US].

### Postmarketing Experience (Frequency Not Established)
- **Serious:** Anaphylaxis, angioedema, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN), DRESS.
- **Other:** Rhabdomyolysis, acute renal failure, interstitial lung disease, photosensitivity (may cross-react with ketoprofen), hepatitis, and cirrhosis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Statins and Colchicine:** Increased risk of myopathy and rhabdomyolysis. Use with caution; monitor for muscle symptoms [US].
- **Coumarin Anticoagulants:** Potentiation of PT/INR. Reduce dose of anticoagulant and monitor INR frequently [US].
- **Immunosuppressants (e.g., Cyclosporine, Tacrolimus):** Increased risk of nephrotoxicity. Use the lowest effective dose and monitor renal function [US].
- **Bile-Acid Binding Resins:** May bind fenofibric acid and impede absorption. Administer fenofibric acid at least 1 hour before or 4 to 6 hours after the resin [US].
- **CYP Inhibition:** Fenofibric acid is a mild-to-moderate inhibitor of CYP2C9 and a weak inhibitor of CYP2C8, CYP2C19, and CYP2A6 [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - [US]: There are insufficient data in pregnant women to determine a drug-associated risk. Animal studies showed skeletal malformations and embryo-fetal toxicity only at maternally toxic doses. Use only if potential benefit outweighs risk [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** 
    - [US]: No data on presence in human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions (e.g., disruption of lipid metabolism) in infants, breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for 5 days after the final dose [US].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in rat studies at doses up to 10 times the maximum human dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Dose selection should be based on renal function; elderly patients are more likely to have renal impairment [US].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Mild to Moderate (eGFR 30–59 mL/min/1.73m²):** Start at 45 mg once daily. Increase only after evaluating renal function and lipid response [US].
    - **Severe (eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73m²):** Contraindicated [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not evaluated in this population; contraindicated in active liver disease [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Active moiety is fenofibric acid. It activates PPARα, which increases lipolysis and elimination of triglyceride-rich particles by activating lipoprotein lipase and reducing production of apoprotein C-III [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Reduces Total-C, TG, and VLDL-C; increases HDL-C [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is ~81%. Peak levels (Tmax) in 4–5 hours (fasted) [US].
    - **Distribution:** ~99% protein bound [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily conjugated with glucuronic acid. Not significantly metabolized by CYP450 [US].
    - **Elimination:** Half-life of ~20 hours. Primarily excreted in urine as fenofibric acid and its glucuronide [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Hypertriglyceridemia:** Trials showed significant reductions in TG (up to 54.5%) and VLDL-TG (up to 50.6%) compared to placebo [US].
- **Primary Hyperlipidemia:** Trials showed mean reductions in LDL-C (~20.6%) and Total-C (~18.7%) and increases in HDL-C (~11%) [US].
- **Cardiovascular Outcomes:** The ACCORD Lipid trial (statin + fenofibrate) and the FIELD trial (fenofibrate monotherapy) failed to show a significant reduction in major adverse cardiovascular events in patients with type 2 diabetes [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C (59°F–86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from moisture. Keep out of reach of children [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription only (Rx-only).
