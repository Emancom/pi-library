# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine (Intravenous)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single, structured reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidelines required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **Carnitine Deficiency in End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing hemodialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Metabolic Disorders:**
    - **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - **Titration:** Dose adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be implemented as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are adequate [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use. Discard any unused portions as the formulation contains no preservatives.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** No specific contraindications are listed in the available source; however, serious hypersensitivity reactions have been reported during use [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These can occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and monitor for recurrence if re-administration is attempted [US].
- **General Precautions:** The safety/efficacy of oral levocarnitine has not been established in renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:**
    - **Plasma Levels:** It is recommended to obtain a baseline plasma carnitine concentration. Weekly and monthly monitoring should follow, including blood chemistries and vital signs.
    - **Target Range:** Free carnitine concentrations should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** 
    - [US ESRD Trials]: Injection site reaction (33%), pharyngitis (23%), headache (22%), hypertension (20%), diarrhea (16%), infection (15%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8-12%), dizziness (13%), accidental injury (10%).
- **Common (1–10%):**
    - [US ESRD Trials]: Abdominal pain (9%), fever (7%), anemia (6%), hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%), cough (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%), pruritus (5%), kidney failure (6%).
- **Neurologic Reactions:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting. Body odor and gastritis have been noted less frequently [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Clinical Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of **levocarnitine** or after any dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:**
    - **US Classification:** Not explicitly assigned a letter category in the current source, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits (up to 3.8 times the human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows suggest carnitine concentrations in milk increase with exogenous administration. Risks to the child from excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg), applicable to the paediatric population [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the available source.
- **Renal Impairment:** **Levocarnitine** is easily removed by dialysis. Use caution with oral formulations in patients with severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Levocarnitine** is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, where they undergo oxidation for energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with specific metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and [³H]-γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (n=12), treatment with 20 mg/kg after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis (trough) levocarnitine levels from 21.3 µmol/L at baseline to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While the drug successfully increases carnitine concentrations, the impact on specific clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, malaise) in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) when mixed in PVC bags with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8 mg/mL [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [United States].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
