# Editorial Product Information: Fezolinetant

## Editorial Note
This editorial product information document synthesizes details regarding **fezolinetant** from the Australian (TGA) product information. Its purpose is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating key prescribing information in a structured, clinically relevant format. This document is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information issued by a regulatory authority for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
-   **Active ingredient**: Fezolinetant
-   **Chemical class**: Nonhormonal selective neurokinin-3 (NK3) receptor antagonist [Australian]
-   **Brand name(s)**: VEOZA™ [Australian]
-   **ATC code**: Not specified in available sources.
-   **Dosage form(s) and strength(s)**:
    -   Film-coated tablets: 45 mg [Australian]
-   **Route(s) of administration**: Oral [Australian]
-   **Manufacturer/sponsor**: Astellas Pharma Australia Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
-   Treatment of moderate to severe vasomotor symptoms (VMS) associated with menopause [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
-   **General dosing (adult)**: The recommended dose of **fezolinetant** is 45 mg once daily [Australian].
    -   The benefit and duration of treatment should be periodically assessed based on the natural history and course of VMS associated with menopause. Clinical data beyond 12 months is not available [Australian].
-   **Administration instructions**:
    -   Administer orally once daily at approximately the same time each day, with or without food [Australian].
    -   Tablets should be swallowed whole with liquids. Do not cut, crush, or chew [Australian].
-   **Titration schedules**: Not applicable [Australian].
-   **Missed dose instructions**: If a dose is missed, administer it as soon as possible, unless there is less than 12 hours before the next scheduled dose. In that case, skip the missed dose and return to the regular schedule the following day [Australian].
-   **Pre-treatment monitoring**:
    -   Perform baseline hepatic laboratory tests to evaluate for hepatic function and injury, including serum alanine aminotransferase (ALT), serum aspartate aminotransferase (AST), serum alkaline phosphatase (ALP), and serum bilirubin (total and direct) [Australian].
    -   Do not initiate **fezolinetant** if ALT or AST is ≥ 2 times the upper limit of normal (ULN) or if total bilirubin is ≥ 2 times the ULN [Australian].
-   **Follow-up monitoring**:
    -   Perform follow-up hepatic laboratory tests monthly for the first 3 months, then at 6 months and 9 months after initiation of therapy. Thereafter, monitor periodically based on clinical judgment [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
-   Known hypersensitivity to **fezolinetant** or to any of its excipients [Australian].
-   Concomitant use of moderate or strong CYP1A2 inhibitors [Australian].
-   Patients with pre-existing Child-Pugh Class B (moderate) or C (severe) chronic hepatic impairment [Australian].
-   Patients with severe or end-stage renal impairment (eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m²) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
-   **Boxed warnings**: Not specified in available sources.
-   **General warnings and precautions**:
    -   **Hepatotoxicity and Liver Function Monitoring**:
        -   Elevations in serum ALT (≥ 3 x ULN) occurred in 2.1% of **fezolinetant** patients vs 0.8% in placebo during clinical trials [Australian]. AST elevations (≥ 3 x ULN) occurred in 1.0% vs 0.4% in placebo [Australian]. These were generally asymptomatic and resolved with continued treatment or dose interruption/discontinuation [Australian].
        -   Post-marketing, serious but reversible hepatotoxicity has been reported within 40 days of treatment. Patients experienced transaminase elevations (>10 x ULN) with concurrent elevations in bilirubin and/or ALP, sometimes associated with symptoms such as fatigue, pruritus, jaundice, dark urine, or abdominal pain [Australian].
        -   Patients should discontinue **fezolinetant** immediately and seek medical attention if signs or symptoms suggestive of hepatotoxicity occur [Australian].
    -   **Discontinuation Criteria**:
        -   Discontinue **fezolinetant** if transaminase elevations are greater than 5 times the ULN [Australian].
        -   Discontinue **fezolinetant** if transaminase elevations are greater than 3 times the ULN and total bilirubin level is greater than 2 times the ULN [Australian].
        -   Monitoring of liver function tests should continue until they have normalised, and other causes of liver injury should be excluded [Australian].
    -   **Patients with Existing Liver Disease**:
        -   Not studied in patients with active liver disease, or Child-Pugh Class B or C chronic hepatic impairment. Monitoring of liver function is advised for women with known or suspected hepatic disorder [Australian].
    -   **Patients with Oestrogen-Dependent Tumours**:
        -   Not studied in patients with current or previous breast cancer or other oestrogen-dependent tumours. Decision to treat should be based on individual benefit-risk assessment [Australian].
    -   **Patients Treated with Anti-Oestrogen Therapy**:
        -   Not studied in patients undergoing anti-oestrogen therapy. Decision to treat should be based on individual benefit-risk assessment [Australian].
    -   **Concomitant use with Hormone Replacement Therapy (HRT)**:
        -   Not investigated and not recommended (local vaginal preparations excluded) [Australian].
    -   **Medicinally Induced Menopause**:
        -   Only studied in patients after natural or surgical menopause. No efficacy or safety data are available for pharmacologically induced menopause (e.g., using GnRH analogues). Decision to treat should be based on individual benefit-risk assessment [Australian].
-   **Specific clinical precautions**: Perimenopausal women of childbearing potential should use an effective non-hormonal contraceptive method [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions observed during clinical studies and from spontaneous reporting are listed by frequency category: very common (≥ 1/10), common (≥ 1/100 to < 1/10), uncommon (≥ 1/1,000 to < 1/100), rare (≥ 1/10,000 to < 1/1,000), very rare (< 1/10,000), and not known (cannot be estimated from available data) [Australian].

-   **Common (≥ 1% incidence in clinical trials) [Australian]**:
    -   Headache (4.5% with **fezolinetant** vs 6.2% with placebo)
    -   Nausea (1.8% with **fezolinetant** vs 1.6% with placebo)
    -   Diarrhoea (1.7% with **fezolinetant** vs 1.8% with placebo)
    -   Fatigue (1.8% with **fezolinetant** vs 1.4% with placebo)
    -   Insomnia (1.7% with **fezolinetant** vs 0.8% with placebo)
    -   Back pain (1.3% with **fezolinetant** vs 0.5% with placebo)
    -   Urinary tract infection (1.1% with **fezolinetant** vs 0.9% with placebo)
    -   Uterine haemorrhage (1.1% with **fezolinetant** vs 0.6% with placebo)
    -   Nasopharyngitis (1.2% with **fezolinetant** vs 1.6% with placebo)
    -   Upper respiratory tract infection (1.7% with **fezolinetant** vs 2.6% with placebo)
    -   Arthralgia (1.3% with **fezolinetant** vs 1.4% with placebo)
    -   Abdominal pain
    -   Alanine aminotransferase (ALT) increased
    -   Aspartate aminotransferase (AST) increased
-   **Not known (frequency cannot be estimated)**:
    -   Hepatotoxicity [Australian].

-   **Most frequent adverse reactions (≥3%)**: Diarrhoea and insomnia [Australian].
-   **Most frequent adverse reactions leading to discontinuation**: ALT increased (0.3%) and insomnia (0.2%) [Australian].
-   **Serious adverse effects**: Serious cases of drug-induced hepatotoxicity have been reported post-marketing, as described in Warnings and Precautions [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
-   **Pharmacokinetic interactions**:
    -   **CYP1A2 Inhibitors**: Concomitant use with moderate or strong CYP1A2 inhibitors is **contraindicated** [Australian]. **Fezolinetant** is a substrate of CYP1A2.
        -   Strong CYP1A2 inhibitors (e.g., fluvoxamine) significantly increase **fezolinetant** exposure (AUC increased 9.4-fold) [Australian].
        -   Moderate CYP1A2 inhibitors (e.g., mexiletine) are predicted to increase **fezolinetant** AUC by 4.6-fold [Australian].
        -   Weak CYP1A2 inhibitors (e.g., cimetidine) are predicted to increase **fezolinetant** AUC by 2-fold, but this is not considered clinically relevant [Australian].
    -   **CYP1A2 Inducers**: Smoking (a moderate CYP1A2 inducer) decreased **fezolinetant** Cmax by 71.74% and AUC by 48.29% [Australian].
    -   **Effect of fezolinetant on other medicinal products**:
        -   In vitro, **fezolinetant** and its major metabolite (ES259564) are not inhibitors of CYP1A2, CYP2B6, CYP2C8, CYP2C9, CYP2C19, CYP2D6, CYP3A4, and are not inducers of CYP1A2, CYP2B6, or CYP3A4 [Australian].
        -   Neither **fezolinetant** nor ES259564 inhibit P-glycoprotein (P-gp), BCRP, OATP1B1, OATP1B3, OCT2, OAT1, OAT3, MATE1, or MATE2-K transporters [Australian].
        -   ES259564, but not **fezolinetant**, is a substrate of P-gp [Australian].
-   **Pharmacodynamic interactions**: Not specified in available sources, beyond the caution against concomitant use with HRT.
-   **Clinically significant interactions with management notes**: Concomitant use with moderate or strong CYP1A2 inhibitors is contraindicated due to significant increases in **fezolinetant** exposure [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
-   **Pregnancy**:
    -   Use of **fezolinetant** in pregnant women is not indicated. There are no data on its use in pregnant women. Perimenopausal women of childbearing potential are recommended to use an effective non-hormonal contraceptive method [Australian].
    -   **Pregnancy Category B3 [Australian]**: Animal studies have shown embryofetal lethality in rats and rabbits at exposures more than 128-times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) AUC. Decreased fetal weight, impaired ossification, and increased skeletal variations were observed in rabbits at high doses. **Fezolinetant** did not produce malformations in either species [Australian].
-   **Breastfeeding**:
    -   Use of **fezolinetant** in breastfeeding women is not recommended. There are no data to assess its effects on the breastfed child or on milk production. It is not known if **fezolinetant** is present in human milk. Excretion into rat milk was observed, with concentrations higher than in maternal plasma [Australian].
-   **Fertility data**: No human data are available on the effect of **fezolinetant** on fertility. Animal studies in female rats showed no effect on fertility at oral doses up to 140-times the MRHD AUC [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
-   **Paediatric use**: **Fezolinetant** is not indicated for paediatric patients. No data are available [Australian].
-   **Elderly use**: Clinical studies have not been conducted for safety and efficacy in women initiating **fezolinetant** treatment over 65 years of age. No data are available for commencing treatment in patients aged over 65 years [Australian].
-   **Renal impairment**:
    -   No dose adjustment is required in patients with mild or moderate renal impairment (eGFR ≥ 30 mL/min/1.73 m²) [Australian].
    -   **Fezolinetant** should not be used in patients with severe renal impairment or end-stage renal disease (eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m²) [Australian].
    -   There are no data for patients with end-stage renal disease. While **fezolinetant** exposure is not significantly affected, the AUC of its major metabolite (ES259564) increased approximately 1.7- to 4.8-fold in moderate and severe renal impairment [Australian].
-   **Hepatic impairment**:
    -   No dose modification is recommended for individuals with Child-Pugh Class A (mild) chronic hepatic impairment [Australian].
    -   **Fezolinetant** should not be used in individuals with Child-Pugh Class B (moderate) or C (severe) chronic hepatic impairment [Australian].
    -   In Child-Pugh Class A, mean **fezolinetant** Cmax increased by 1.2-fold and AUCinf by 1.6-fold. In Child-Pugh Class B, Cmax decreased by 15% and AUCinf increased by 2-fold. **Fezolinetant** has not been studied in individuals with Child-Pugh Class C severe chronic hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
-   **Mechanism of action**: **Fezolinetant** is a nonhormonal, selective neurokinin-3 (NK3) receptor antagonist. It blocks the binding of neurokinin B (NKB) to NK3 receptors on kisspeptin/neurokinin B/dynorphin (KNDy) neurons in the thermoregulatory centre of the hypothalamus. This modulation restores neuronal activity disrupted by declining oestrogen during menopause, thereby reducing VMS [Australian].
-   **Pharmacokinetics**:
    -   **Absorption**: Peak plasma concentrations (Cmax) are usually achieved 1 to 4 hours post-dose. Steady-state plasma concentrations are generally reached by day 2 with minimal accumulation following once-daily dosing [Australian].
    -   **Distribution**: The mean apparent volume of distribution (Vd/F) is 189 L. Plasma protein binding is low (51%). **Fezolinetant** distribution into red blood cells is almost equal to plasma [Australian].
    -   **Metabolism**: Primarily metabolised by CYP1A2 to an oxidised major metabolite, ES259564, which is approximately 20-fold less potent than **fezolinetant**. To a lesser extent, it is also metabolised by CYP2C9 and CYP2C19 [Australian].
    -   **Elimination**: Apparent clearance at steady-state is 10.8 L/h. Elimination is primarily via urine (76.9%) and to a lesser extent faeces (14.7%). In urine, 1.1% of the administered dose is excreted unchanged and 61.7% as ES259564. The terminal elimination half-life is less than 15 hours [Australian].
-   **Pharmacodynamics**: **Fezolinetant** treatment provides relief from VMS over 24 hours. It does not cause clinically relevant changes in sex hormone levels. A transient decrease in luteinising hormone (LH) levels has been observed at peak concentrations. No clinically relevant QT prolongation is predicted [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
-   **Key pivotal trial summaries**: The efficacy of **fezolinetant** was evaluated in two 12-week, randomised, placebo-controlled, double-blind Phase 3 studies (SKYLIGHT 1 and SKYLIGHT 2), followed by a 40-week extension period. These studies included 1022 postmenopausal women with moderate to severe VMS. The co-primary efficacy endpoints were change from baseline in VMS frequency and severity at weeks 4 and 12 [Australian].
-   **Outcomes**: In both studies, **fezolinetant** 45 mg daily demonstrated a statistically significant and clinically meaningful reduction (≥2 hot flashes per 24 hours) in the frequency and severity of moderate to severe VMS compared to placebo at weeks 4 and 12 [Australian].
-   **Safety**: Long-term safety was evaluated in SKYLIGHT 4 (a 52-week study), which assessed endometrial safety via transvaginal ultrasound and endometrial biopsies. These assessments did not identify an increased risk of endometrial hyperplasia or malignancy, nor a clinically relevant effect on endometrial thickness [Australian].
