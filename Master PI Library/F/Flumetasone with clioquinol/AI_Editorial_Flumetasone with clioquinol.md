# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data into a single, coherent reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing in any particular region. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (Generic name); chemical class: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter).
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available source.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection for intravenous use.
    - Strength: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose: 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    - A loading dose is often given in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the next 24 hours [US].
    - Frequency: Every 3 to 4 hours (q3h or q4h), and never less than every 6 hours (q6h) [US].
    - Max dose: The highest administered dose reported is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
    - Administration: Slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

### Monitoring and Titration
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- **ESRD:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels below 40 to 50 µmol/L. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Levocarnitine may increase the frequency or severity of seizures in patients with pre-existing seizure activity. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a prior history of seizures [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** Chronic high-dose oral administration (not specifically injection) in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse event frequency was assessed in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials of ESRD patients on hemodialysis.

| Frequency | Adverse Event (Regardless of Causality) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Headache (22%), Hypertension (20%), Nausea (8%), Vomiting (15%), Diarrhea (16%), Pharyngitis (23%), Fever (7%), Infection (15%), Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Abdominal pain (9%), Accidental injury (10%), Asthenia (9%), Chest pain (12%), Flu syndrome (25%), Cardiovascular disorder (5%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%), Hypercalcemia (9%), Hyperkalemia (6%), Peripheral edema (5%), Dizziness (13%), Dyspnea (7%), Rhinitis (9%), Pruritus (5%) |
| **Serious/Notable** | **Anaphylaxis**, **Laryngeal edema**, **Bronchospasm**, **Seizures** |

- **Note:** Frequency of nausea and vomiting is reported as "transient" in some cases but occurred at rates of 8% and 15% respectively in trials [US].
- **Body Odor:** Less frequent but noted as an adverse reaction [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine.
- **Management:** It is recommended to monitor INR levels in patients receiving warfarin after starting levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not formally letter-categorized in source, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits (up to 3.8 times the human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm [US].
    - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against maternal benefit; consideration may be given to discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies revealed no evidence of impaired fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders are based on mg/kg, applicable to paediatric patients (see Section 3) [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - Primarily indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis (see Section 2 and 3). 
    - Safety of oral levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, facilitating oxidation and energy production.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine facilitates the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids (as acyl-CoA esters) by forming acylcarnitine, which is rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Trough Levels:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg post-dialysis), trough levels increased from a baseline of 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses in ESRD patients showed dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the effect on clinical outcomes in this specific population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat [US].
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
- **Preservative:** None; discard unused portions [US].
