# Editorial Product Information: Foscarnet

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial synthesis: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration).
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory guidance.
- This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing in any particular region. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Foscarnet sodium hexahydrate (trisodium phosphonoformate hexahydrate).
- **Chemical Class:** Pyrophosphate analogue / Antiviral.
- **Brand Names:** **FOSCAVIR** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available source.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Solution for injection, 24 mg/mL (6 g in 250 mL) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Link Medical Products Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Cytomegalovirus (CMV) Retinitis:** Treatment in patients with Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome (AIDS) [Australian].
- **Aciclovir-resistant Herpes Simplex Virus (HSV) Infections:** Treatment of infections (clinically or *in vitro* documented resistance) in immunocompromised patients with Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
Dosing must be individualised based on renal function, assessed by estimated creatinine clearance. **Foscarnet** must not be administered by rapid intravenous infusion.

### CMV Retinitis
- **Induction Therapy:** 60 mg/kg every 8 hours for 2 to 3 weeks, depending on clinical response [Australian].
- **Maintenance Therapy:** 90 mg/kg to 120 mg/kg once daily, infused over 2 hours. It is recommended to start at 90 mg/kg and increase to 120 mg/kg if the disease progresses and the lower dose is well-tolerated [Australian].

### HSV Infections (Aciclovir-resistant)
- **Induction Therapy:** 40 mg/kg every 8 hours by slow infusion (over 1 hour) for 2 to 3 weeks or until lesions have healed [Australian].
- **Maintenance Therapy:** Efficacy has not been established for this indication [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Peripheral Vein:** Must be diluted to a concentration of 12 mg/mL with glucose 5% or normal saline immediately prior to use to prevent local irritation [Australian].
- **Central Venous Line:** May be administered undiluted (24 mg/mL) [Australian].
- **Hydration:** To reduce renal toxicity, patients should be hydrated with 0.5–1.0 litre of normal saline before the first infusion, and 0.5–1.0 litre added to each subsequent infusion. Clinically dehydrated patients must have their condition corrected before starting therapy [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to **foscarnet** [Australian].
- **Prognosis-based Restriction:** Long-term treatment is contraindicated in patients with a reasonable prognosis (e.g., bone marrow transplant recipients) due to limited long-term safety data regarding bone deposition [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in provided source.
- **Renal Impairment:** **Foscarnet** is highly nephrotoxic. Serum creatinine must be monitored every second day during induction and once weekly during maintenance. Dosage must be adjusted if renal function declines [Australian].
- **Mineral and Electrolyte Abnormalities:** **Foscarnet** chelates bivalent metal ions (calcium, magnesium). This may lead to acute decreases in ionised serum calcium, which may not be reflected in total calcium levels. Patients must be monitored for hypocalcaemia, hypomagnesaemia, and hypokalaemia [Australian].
- **QT Prolongation:** Rare cases of QT prolongation and Torsade de pointes have been reported. Caution is advised in patients with existing cardiac conduction intervals, bradycardia, or underlying cardiac disease [Australian].
- **Seizures:** Associated with plasma mineral/electrolyte imbalances. Status epilepticus has been reported [Australian].
- **Barium Toxicity:** Barium levels in glass bottles may increase during storage. Barium can inhibit potassium channels and induce neuromuscular blockade. Monitor ECG and potassium levels [Australian].
- **Genital Ulceration:** High concentrations in urine can cause significant local irritation and ulceration. Meticulous personal hygiene and cleaning after micturition are recommended [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse event frequencies are based on patients with CMV retinitis who may be severely immunocompromised.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (≥10%) | Granulocytopenia, anaemia, decreased appetite, hypomagnesaemia, hypokalaemia, hypocalcaemia, headache, paraesthesia, dizziness, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, rash, asthenia, chills, fatigue, pyrexia, increased blood creatinine. |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Thrombocytopenia, leukopenia, neutropenia, sepsis, electrolyte imbalances (hyper/hypophosphataemia, hyponatraemia, hypercalcaemia), psychiatric disturbances (anxiety, confusion, aggression), convulsions, tachycardia, palpitations, hypertension, hypotension, abdominal pain, pancreatitis, renal failure, dysuria, genital ulceration. |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Pancytopenia, acidosis, urticaria, angioedema, renal tubular disorder, glomerulonephritis, nephrotic syndrome. |
| **Not Known** (Post-marketing) | Anaphylactic reactions, diabetes insipidus, Torsade de pointes, ventricular arrhythmia, oesophageal ulceration, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, rhabdomyolysis, Fanconi syndrome (acquired). |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Intravenous Pentamidine:** Concomitant use significantly increases the risk of severe, potentially fatal hypocalcaemia and renal impairment. Avoid combination [Australian].
- **Nephrotoxic Agents:** Additive toxicity with aminoglycosides, amphotericin B, ciclosporin A, aciclovir, methotrexate, and tacrolimus [Australian].
- **Loop Diuretics:** Should be avoided as they may inhibit renal tubular secretion and increase **foscarnet** toxicity. Thiazides are preferred if a diuretic is required [Australian].
- **Protease Inhibitors:** Abnormal renal function has been reported when used with ritonavir or saquinavir [Australian].
- **Zidovudine/Ganciclovir:** No pharmacokinetic interactions have been observed [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. Animal studies show skeletal abnormalities in offspring. There is no clinical experience in pregnant women; therefore, **foscarnet** should not be used in pregnancy. Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Animal studies show **foscarnet** passes into breast milk at 3 times maternal plasma concentrations. Breastfeeding is not recommended [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Men should not father a child during or up to 6 months after therapy [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established. Effect on skeletal development is unknown, but bone deposition is observed in animal studies [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific efficacy studies; however, due to the high likelihood of decreased renal function in the elderly, cautious dose selection and frequent renal monitoring are required [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Mandatory dose reduction based on creatinine clearance (CrCl). Treatment is not recommended if CrCl <0.4 mL/kg/min [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required [Australian].
- **Haemodialysis:** Use is not recommended as dosage guidelines have not been established [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Directly inhibits viral-specific DNA polymerase and reverse transcriptase. It does not require activation by thymidine kinase (TK), making it effective against TK-deficient HSV mutants [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Distributed into cerebrospinal fluid (10–70% of plasma levels). High affinity for bone tissue [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** No metabolic conversion [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal via glomerular filtration and tubular secretion. Initial half-life of 2–4 hours; terminal half-life of 1–8 days (release from bone) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **HSV:** An open-label comparative study of 14 HIV patients with aciclovir-resistant lesions showed **foscarnet** (40 mg/kg every 8 hours) was superior to vidarabine in healing time and pain loss [Australian].
- **CMV Retinitis:** Induction therapy resulted in stabilisation of retinal lesions in approximately 90% of cases. Maintenance therapy significantly delays the time to disease progression [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C. Do not refrigerate [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Discard solution within 24 hours of breaking the sterility seal. Pharmacy-prepared bags are stable for 24 hours [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Prescription Only Medicine (S4) [Australian].
