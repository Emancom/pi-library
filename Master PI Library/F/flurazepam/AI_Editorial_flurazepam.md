# Editorial Product Information: Flurazepam Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of flurazepam hydrochloride for healthcare professionals. It synthesises the available Product Information (PI) into a single, clinically focused reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory authorities for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Flurazepam hydrochloride, a 1,4-benzodiazepine derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Flurazepam Hydrochloride Capsules, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** N05CD01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Hard gelatin capsules: 15 mg (blue opaque cap/white opaque body) and 30 mg (blue opaque cap/white opaque body) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Chartwell RX, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Insomnia:** Treatment of insomnia characterised by difficulty falling asleep, frequent nocturnal awakenings, and/or early morning awakenings [US].
- **Duration of Use:** Since insomnia is often transient, short-term use is typically sufficient. Prolonged use should only be undertaken following appropriate clinical evaluation [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 General Dosing (Adult)
Prescribers must use the lowest effective dose to minimise dose-related adverse effects.
- **Women:** Recommended initial dose is 15 mg [US].
- **Men:** Recommended initial dose is 15 mg or 30 mg [US].
- **Titration:** The 15 mg dose may be increased to 30 mg if necessary for efficacy [US].

### 3.2 Specific Populations
- **Elderly or Debilitated Patients:** Recommended dose is 15 mg to reduce the risk of oversedation, dizziness, confusion, and ataxia [US].

### 3.3 Administration Instructions
- Capsules should be taken immediately before retiring to bed [US].
- Patients must ensure they are able to get a full night’s sleep (7 to 8 hours) before they must be active again [US].

### 3.4 Discontinuation and Tapering
- **Gradual Taper:** To mitigate withdrawal reactions, a gradual, patient-specific taper should be used for discontinuation or dose reduction [US].
- **Management of Withdrawal:** If withdrawal symptoms emerge, the taper should be paused or the dose increased to the previous level, followed by a slower reduction schedule [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to flurazepam hydrochloride or any other benzodiazepines [US].
- **Angioedema:** Patients who have experienced angioedema (involving the tongue, glottis, or larynx) following the first or subsequent doses of flurazepam should not be rechallenged [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### 5.1 Boxed Warnings [US]
- **Concomitant Use with Opioids:** Simultaneous use of benzodiazepines and opioids may result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death. Reserve concomitant use for patients with inadequate alternative treatment options; limit dosages and durations to the minimum required.
- **Abuse, Misuse, and Addiction:** Use exposes patients to risks of abuse, misuse, and addiction, which can lead to overdose or death. Assess risk before prescribing and monitor throughout treatment.
- **Dependence and Withdrawal:** Continued use may lead to clinically significant physical dependence. Abrupt discontinuation or rapid dose reduction can precipitate life-threatening withdrawal reactions (e.g., seizures). Use a gradual taper for discontinuation.

### 5.2 General Warnings
- **CNS Depressant Effects:** May cause daytime impairment, even when used as prescribed. Risk of next-day psychomotor impairment increases with higher doses, co-administration with other CNS depressants, or if less than a full night's sleep remains after ingestion [US].
- **Complex Sleep Behaviours:** Reports of "sleep-driving," preparing/eating food, making phone calls, or having sex while not fully awake (often with amnesia for the event). Discontinue immediately if these occur [US].
- **Worsening of Depression:** Benzodiazepines may exacerbate pre-existing depression. Suicidal thinking and behavior may occur; prescribe the smallest feasible quantity to prevent intentional overdose [US].
- **Severe Anaphylactic Reactions:** Rare cases of fatal airway obstruction due to angioedema have been reported [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### 6.1 Common Adverse Effects (Frequency not quantified in source)
- Dizziness
- Drowsiness
- Light-headedness
- Staggering
- Ataxia
- Loss of coordination/Falling

### 6.2 Other Reported Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, GI pain, heartburn, upset stomach.
- **Neurological/Psychiatric:** Nervousness, talkativeness, apprehension, irritability, weakness, confusion, restlessness, hallucinations, slurred speech, euphoria, depression.
- **General:** Palpitations, chest pain, body/joint pain, headache, sweating, flushes, blurred vision, burning eyes, faintness, hypotension, shortness of breath, pruritus, skin rash, dry mouth, bitter taste, excessive salivation.
- **Rare/Serious:** Leukopenia, granulocytopenia, elevated SGOT/SGPT, total/direct bilirubin elevations, and elevated alkaline phosphatase [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### 7.1 Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Opioids:** Increased risk of fatal respiratory depression and sedation due to additive effects at GABA and mu-receptor sites [US].
- **CNS Depressants:** Additive effects with alcohol, other benzodiazepines, psychotropic medications, anticonvulsants, antihistamines, and tricyclic antidepressants. Downward dose adjustment of flurazepam may be necessary [US].

### 7.2 Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Persistence of Effect:** Due to the long half-life of psychoactive metabolites, potential drug interactions may persist for several days after flurazepam is discontinued [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Risk Summary:** Use late in pregnancy can cause neonatal sedation (respiratory depression, lethargy, hypotonia) and/or neonatal withdrawal symptoms (hyperreflexia, irritability, tremors).
    - **Data:** Observational studies do not report a clear association with major birth defects. Animal studies at relevant doses did not indicate morphological developmental risks [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. Due to flurazepam's long half-life, it may accumulate in breast milk, potentially causing sedation and poor feeding in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Studies assessing the effects of flurazepam on fertility have not been conducted [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in children have not been established [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Elderly patients are more sensitive to oversedation and confusion. There is a higher risk of falls. Initial dosing should be conservative (15 mg), and clearance is notably slower in elderly males compared to younger males [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Sensitivity may be increased in patients with decreased hepatic or renal function; however, specific eGFR-based dosing is not specified in available sources [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
### 10.1 Mechanism of Action
Flurazepam is a 1,4-benzodiazepine that binds to stereo-specific GABA$_A$ receptors at several sites within the CNS. The exact mechanism of its hypnotic action is unknown [US].

### 10.2 Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract.
- **Metabolism:** Rapidly metabolised. The primary active metabolite in the blood is **N1-desalkyl-flurazepam**.
- **Steady State:** N1-desalkyl-flurazepam reaches steady state after 7 to 10 days of dosing, at levels 5 to 6 times higher than those seen after a single dose.
- **Elimination:** Primarily excreted in the urine.
    - **Parent half-life:** ~2.3 hours.
    - **Active metabolite (N1-desalkyl-flurazepam) half-life:** 47 to 100 hours [US].

### 10.3 Pharmacodynamics
- **Clinical Performance:** Increased effectiveness is often observed on the second or third night of use. Due to the long half-life of metabolites, sleep latency and total wake time may remain decreased for 1 to 2 nights after treatment is discontinued [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Sleep Laboratory Studies:** Objectively determined that flurazepam hydrochloride is effective for at least 28 consecutive nights of administration [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from light and moisture. Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container with a child-resistant closure [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Schedule IV (CIV) Controlled Substance [US].
