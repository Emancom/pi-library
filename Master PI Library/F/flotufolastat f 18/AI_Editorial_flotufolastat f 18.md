# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Administered every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    - **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Administration:** Slow bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Adjustment:** Dose reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine concentrations and may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate appropriate medical management [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with and without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Monitoring:** 
    - Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels.
    - Monitor weekly and monthly, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine level: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Levocarnitine):** The safety of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with renal insufficiency has not been established. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are often confounded by the underlying pathology (e.g., ESRD or inborn errors of metabolism).

### Very Common (>10%) [US-reported in dialysis trials]
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%), Chest pain (12%), Flu syndrome (25%), Headache (22%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%).

### Common (1–10%) [US-reported in dialysis trials]
- **General:** Abdominal pain, fever, accidental injury, asthenia.
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, palpitations, arrhythmia, atrial fibrillation.
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight changes.
- **Neurological/Psychiatric:** Dizziness (13%), insomnia, depression, paresthesia.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea (8%), dyspepsia, anorexia, constipation.
- **Respiratory:** Cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis.
- **Skin:** Pruritus, rash.

### Other Reported Reactions
- **Body Odour:** Reported with levocarnitine use.
- **Gastritis:** Observed less frequently.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Classification:** Not explicitly assigned a letter category in the current source, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of fetal harm or impaired fertility. 
    - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after exogenous administration. Benefits to the mother must be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing guidelines for metabolic disorders apply to paediatric patients (see Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for patients with ESRD on dialysis. Dose adjustments for dialysis patients are based on trough plasma carnitine levels (see Section 3).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Trials:** Two double-blind, placebo-controlled trials were conducted in patients on chronic hemodialysis. Results showed that IV administration of 20 mg/kg three times weekly resulted in pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine concentrations increasing from approximately 20 µmol/L at baseline to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks of therapy [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses three times weekly showed dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability (Admixture):** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL, stored at room temperature (25°C) [US].
