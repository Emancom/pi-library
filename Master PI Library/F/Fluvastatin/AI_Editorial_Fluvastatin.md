# Editorial Product Information: Fluvastatin Sodium

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information provided by the Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration) authority. 
*   **Purpose:** This document serves as a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a concise reference format. 
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved PI for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Fluvastatin sodium
*   **Chemical Class:** HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor (Statin)
*   **Brand Name(s):** **Lescol XL** [Australian]
*   **ATC Code:** C10AA04
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    *   Prolonged-release tablets: 80 mg (equivalent to 84.24 mg fluvastatin sodium) [Australian]. 
    *   *Note:* 20 mg and 40 mg capsule formulations are no longer marketed in Australia but are referenced in clinical titration guidelines.
*   **Route of Administration:** Oral
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Novartis Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Limited [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Hypercholesterolaemia:** As an adjunct to diet in the treatment of primary hypercholesterolaemia in adults. [Australian]
*   **Heterozygous Familial Hypercholesterolaemia (HeFH) in Paediatrics:** As an adjunct to diet in adolescent boys and girls (at least one year post-menstruation) aged 10–16 years with HeFH, where dietary response is inadequate and LDL-C remains ≥4.9 mmol/L, or ≥4.1 mmol/L with a positive family history of premature cardiovascular disease or ≥2 other cardiovascular risk factors. [Australian]
*   **Secondary Prevention of Cardiac Events:** Prevention of major adverse cardiac events (MACE) in adults with coronary heart disease following successful coronary transcatheter therapy. [Australian]

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
*   **Standard Range:** 40 mg once or twice daily, or 80 mg once daily. A 20 mg dose may be sufficient in mild cases. [Australian]
*   **Secondary Prevention (Post-transcatheter therapy):** 80 mg daily. [Australian]
*   **Dose Individualisation:** Titrate based on baseline LDL-C and therapeutic goals. Maximum LDL-C reduction is typically achieved within four weeks. [Australian]

### Administration Instructions
*   **Lescol XL 80 mg Tablets:** Must be swallowed whole with a glass of water. Do not chew, crush, split, or cut the tablets. [Australian]
*   **Timing:** May be taken at any time of day, with or without food. [Australian]
*   **Combination Therapy:** If used with colestyramine or other resins, fluvastatin must be administered at bedtime, at least four hours after the resin to avoid binding interactions. [Australian]

### Missed Dose
*   Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
*   Known hypersensitivity to fluvastatin or any excipients. [Australian]
*   Active liver disease or unexplained, persistent elevations in serum transaminases. [Australian]
*   **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated due to potential fetal harm. [Australian]
*   **Lactation:** Contraindicated in breastfeeding mothers. [Australian]
*   **Women of Childbearing Potential:** Contraindicated unless adequate contraceptive precautions are employed. [Australian]

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Hepatic Effects
*   **Liver Failure:** Rare post-marketing reports of fatal and non-fatal hepatic failure have occurred. Discontinue treatment if clinical signs of liver injury (e.g., jaundice, nausea, easy bruising) develop. [Australian]
*   **Monitoring:** Perform liver function tests (LFTs) prior to initiation, at 6 and 12 weeks post-initiation or dose escalation, and periodically (e.g., every 6 months) thereafter. Discontinue if AST or ALT levels exceed 3x the upper limit of normal (ULN) and persist. [Australian]

### Skeletal Muscle Effects
*   **Myopathy/Rhabdomyolysis:** Rarely reported. Risk increases with higher doses or concurrent use of ciclosporin, fibrates, erythromycin, or nicotinic acid. [Australian]
*   **Fusidic Acid:** **Statins must not be co-administered with systemic fusidic acid.** Discontinue fluvastatin during fusidic acid therapy and for seven days after the last dose. Fatal rhabdomyolysis has been reported with this combination. [Australian]
*   **Creatine Kinase (CPK) Monitoring:** Measure baseline CPK in high-risk patients (e.g., renal impairment, age >70, alcohol abuse, personal/family history of muscle disorders). During treatment, measure CPK if muscle pain, weakness, or cramps occur. Discontinue if CPK >5x ULN. [Australian]
*   **Immune-Mediated Necrotizing Myopathy (IMNM):** Rare autoimmune myopathy reported with statins, characterized by proximal muscle weakness and elevated CPK persisting despite discontinuation. [Australian]

### General Precautions
*   **Glucose Metabolism:** Statins may increase HbA1c and fasting plasma glucose; new-onset diabetes has been reported in patients with pre-existing risk factors. [Australian]
*   **Interstitial Lung Disease:** Reported with long-term statin therapy. Symptoms include dyspnoea, non-productive cough, and fatigue. [Australian]
*   **Myasthenia Gravis:** Statins may induce or aggravate pre-existing myasthenia gravis or ocular myasthenia. [Australian]

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally mild and similar to placebo.
*   **Common (1–10%):** Dyspepsia, abdominal pain, nausea, insomnia, headache, increased blood CPK, increased transaminases. [Australian]
*   **Rare (0.1–1%):** Hypersensitivity (rash, urticaria), myalgia, muscle weakness, myopathy. [Australian]
*   **Very Rare (<0.1%):** Thrombocytopenia, anaphylaxis, pancreatitis, hepatitis, vasculitis, angioedema, rhabdomyolysis, lupus erythematosus-like reactions. [Australian]
*   **Frequency Not Known:** Erectile dysfunction, depression, interstitial lung disease, IMNM. [Australian]

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Ciclosporin:** Bioavailability of fluvastatin 80 mg increases approximately 2-fold when co-administered. Use combination with caution. [Australian]
*   **Fluconazole:** CYP2C9 inhibition increases fluvastatin AUC by ~84% and Cmax by ~44%. Use caution. [Australian]
*   **Colestyramine:** Reduces fluvastatin bioavailability by >50%. Administer fluvastatin at least 4 hours after the resin to achieve additive lipid-lowering effects. [Australian]
*   **Warfarin:** Fluvastatin may increase warfarin exposure (AUC ↑31%, Cmax ↑67%). Monitor prothrombin time/INR when starting or stopping fluvastatin. [Australian]
*   **Phenytoin:** Fluvastatin may increase phenytoin AUC by ~20%. Monitor phenytoin levels. [Australian]
*   **Glibenclamide (Glyburide):** Fluvastatin increases glibenclamide exposure. Monitor patients when fluvastatin dose is increased to 80 mg daily. [Australian]
*   **CYP3A4 Inhibitors (Erythromycin, Itraconazole):** Minimal effect on fluvastatin due to primary CYP2C9 metabolism. [Australian]
*   **Grapefruit Juice:** No interaction expected based on fluvastatin's metabolic profile. [Australian]

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** **Category D [Australian]**. HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors decrease cholesterol synthesis, which is essential for fetal development. Several cases of fetal abnormalities have been reported. Therapy must be discontinued if pregnancy occurs.
*   **Breastfeeding:** Fluvastatin is excreted in animal milk; data in humans is unavailable. Use is contraindicated in breastfeeding mothers due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in infants. [Australian]
*   **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in animal studies at therapeutic doses. [Australian]

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Approved for HeFH in ages 10–16. **Lescol XL 80 mg** should not be used for initiation; starting dose should be 20 mg once daily. Adjust at intervals of ≥6 weeks up to 80 mg daily. Long-term safety (>2 years) is not established. [Australian]
*   **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustments required. Patients >65 years demonstrated enhanced treatment response for LDL-C reduction compared to younger patients. [Australian]
*   **Renal Impairment:** Pharmacokinetics are unchanged in mild to severe renal insufficiency; no dose adjustments are necessary. [Australian]
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in active liver disease or unexplained transaminase elevations. Use with caution in those with a history of liver disease or heavy alcohol use. [Australian]

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Competitive inhibitor of HMG-CoA reductase. It limits the conversion of HMG-CoA to mevalonate, increasing LDL-receptor expression in the liver and enhancing the clearance of circulating LDL-C. [Australian]
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption:** Rapid and complete (IR formulation <1 hr). Food reduces the rate but not the extent. **Lescol XL** absorption is 60% slower than IR, with a 4-hour increase in mean residence time. [Australian]
    *   **Distribution:** 98% protein bound; targeted to the liver. [Australian]
    *   **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic via hydroxylation of the indole ring. Major pathways involve CYP2C9 (~75%), followed by CYP3A4 (~20%) and CYP2C8 (~5%). [Australian]
    *   **Elimination:** ~93% excreted in faeces; terminal half-life is ~2.3 hours. [Australian]

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **LCAS (Lipoprotein and Coronary Atherosclerosis Study):** Demonstrated that fluvastatin significantly slowed the progression of coronary atherosclerosis (judged by minimum lumen diameter) over 2.5 years in patients with mild-to-moderate hypercholesterolaemia. [Australian]
*   **LIPS (Lescol Intervention Prevention Study):** Evaluated fluvastatin 80 mg daily in patients post-transcatheter therapy. The study showed a significant reduction in the risk of MACE over a median of 3.9 years (22.6% reduction in risk vs. placebo). [Australian]

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store below 25°C. [Australian]
*   **Handling:** Keep out of reach of children. [Australian]
*   **Shelf Life:** Consult the expiry date on the packaging. [Australian]

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
