# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine [(R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt].
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative/Carrier molecule.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (generic); Carnitor [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg.
- **Administration:** Delivered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours (q3h or q4h) and never less frequently than every 6 hours (q6h) [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered.

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustments:** Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when diluted in Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks/benefits before any attempt at re-administration [US].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Monitoring:** For patients with metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (>10%):** Transient nausea and vomiting. 
- **Less Frequent:** Body odor, gastritis.
- **Serious Effects:** Seizures (see below) and hypersensitivity reactions.

### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) include:
| System Organ Class | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, abdominal pain, vomiting, nausea |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, paresthesia |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea |

### Post-Marketing Experience
- **Neurologic:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concomitantly with levocarnitine.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels in patients on warfarin therapy following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dosage adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned by letter, but label states "use only if clearly needed"]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm.
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows suggest carnitine concentrations in milk increase with administration. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine must be weighed against maternal benefit [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies revealed no evidence of impaired fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg body weight as specified in the "Dosage and Administration" section [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** See "Dosage and Administration" for ESRD-specific protocols. Oral levocarnitine is cautioned in renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, it may alleviate metabolic abnormalities caused by the accumulation of toxic organic acids.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** Efficacy was demonstrated in a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients where 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis significantly increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Trough levels rose from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L over 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging:** A study of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses in ESRD patients (N≈30 per group) showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservative [US].
