# Editorial Product Information: Fenofibrate

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial is derived from the official Product Information (PI) document provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **fenofibrate** for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data into a single, structured reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource. It is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing within a particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Fenofibrate (nanoparticles)
- **Chemical Class:** Fibric acid derivative
- **Brand Names:** APO-FENOFIBRATE [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** C10AB05
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Oral Tablet:** 48 mg and 145 mg [Australian]
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Southern Cross Pharma Pty Ltd; Distributed by Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
Fenofibrate is indicated as an adjunct to diet for the treatment of:
- Hypercholesterolaemia [Australian]
- Dyslipidaemia (Fredrickson Types II, III, IV, and V) [Australian]
- Dyslipidaemia associated with type 2 diabetes mellitus [Australian]

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Standard Dose:** 145 mg taken once daily [Australian].
- **Dose Equivalence:** One 145 mg tablet is equivalent to three 48 mg tablets. However, the 48 mg strength is specifically intended for use when dose reduction is required (e.g., renal impairment) [Australian].
- **Restriction:** Patients should not combine 48 mg and 145 mg tablets. The maximum daily dose is 145 mg [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets must be swallowed whole with a glass of water.
- **Food Effect:** The nanosized formulation (48 mg and 145 mg) may be taken at any time of day regardless of food intake [Australian].
- Patients should maintain a standard cholesterol-lowering diet during treatment.

### Titration and Monitoring
- Lipid levels should be monitored periodically. If an adequate response is not achieved after three months of treatment at the maximum dose, alternative or complementary therapies should be considered [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
Fenofibrate is contraindicated in:
- **Paediatrics:** Children [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Liver dysfunction, including primary biliary cirrhosis and unexplained, persistent liver function abnormalities [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Severe renal dysfunction (eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73m²) [Australian].
- **Gallbladder Disease:** Existing gallbladder disease [Australian].
- **Combination Therapy:** Concurrent use with other fibrates [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to fenofibrate, or a history of photoallergy/phototoxic reactions during treatment with fibrates or ketoprofen [Australian].
- **Pancreatitis:** Chronic or acute pancreatitis, except for acute pancreatitis resulting from severe hypertriglyceridaemia [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Myopathy and Rhabdomyolysis:** Muscle toxicity (myalgia, myositis, and rare rhabdomyolysis) has been reported. Risk increases with co-administration of HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors (statins) or other fibrates. CPK levels should be monitored if muscle symptoms occur; therapy must be discontinued if CPK exceeds 5x the upper limit of normal (ULN) [Australian].
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Elevations in serum transaminases (AST/ALT) are dose-related. LFTs should be monitored every 3 months for the first 12 months of therapy. Discontinue if levels exceed 3x ULN [Australian].
- **Renal Function:** Reversible elevations in serum creatinine are common. Treatment should be interrupted if creatinine levels increase to >50% ULN. Caution is advised in patients with pre-existing renal insufficiency, the elderly, and those with diabetes [Australian].
- **Cholelithiasis:** Fenofibrate may increase cholesterol excretion into the bile, leading to gallstones. Therapy should be stopped if gallstones are confirmed [Australian].
- **Paradoxical HDL-C Decrease:** Severe, paradoxical reductions in HDL-cholesterol (as low as 2 mg/dL) have been reported, usually occurring 2 weeks to years after initiation. If a severe drop is detected, therapy must be withdrawn and not re-initiated [Australian].
- **Venothromboembolic Disease:** Higher rates of pulmonary embolism (PE) and deep vein thrombosis (DVT) were observed in clinical trials (FIELD study) [Australian].
- **Hematologic Changes:** Mild to moderate decreases in haemoglobin, haematocrit, and white blood cell counts may occur, typically stabilising over time [Australian].
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Cases of Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS) and Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN) have been reported post-marketing [Australian].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Rhabdomyolysis:** Potential for acute renal failure.
- **Hepatitis:** Jaundice and cirrhosis (rare cases).
- **Pancreatitis:** Reported even in the absence of biliary obstruction.
- **Severe Skin Reactions:** SJS, TEN, and erythema multiforme.
- **Interstitial Lung Disease:** Reported post-marketing (Very Rare) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Oral Anticoagulants:** Fenofibrate enhances the anticoagulant effect, increasing bleeding risk. The anticoagulant dose should be reduced by approximately one-third at therapy initiation, with frequent INR monitoring [Australian].
- **HMG-CoA Reductase Inhibitors (Statins):** Increased risk of serious muscle toxicity. Use with extreme caution and monitor for signs of myopathy [Australian].
- **Ciclosporin:** Potential for severe, reversible renal impairment. Closely monitor renal function [Australian].
- **Glitazones:** Potential for paradoxical HDL-C reduction; monitor HDL levels if co-administered [Australian].
- **Cytochrome P450:** Fenofibrate is a weak-to-moderate inhibitor of CYP2C9 and a weak inhibitor of CYP2C19 and CYP2A6. Monitor drugs with a narrow therapeutic index metabolised by these enzymes [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category B3. Animal studies have shown embryofetal toxicity, including skeletal abnormalities and increased stillbirths at doses associated with maternal toxicity. There are no adequate studies in pregnant women; use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if fenofibrate is excreted in human milk. Administration to breastfeeding women is not recommended [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in rat studies at doses up to 300 mg/kg/day [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use in children is contraindicated; safety and efficacy data are unavailable [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** In the absence of renal impairment, the standard adult dose is recommended. Renal function should be monitored [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Moderate (eGFR 30–60 mL/min/1.73m²):** Start with one 48 mg tablet once daily. Dose may be increased to two 48 mg tablets daily only after assessing lipid response and renal stability [Australian].
    - **Severe (eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73m²):** Contraindicated [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not studied; use is contraindicated due to potential hepatotoxicity [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Fenofibrate is a PPARα (Peroxisome Proliferator Activated Receptor type α) agonist. Activation of PPARα increases lipolysis and elimination of triglyceride-rich particles by activating lipoprotein lipase and reducing Apoprotein C-III. It also increases the synthesis of Apoproteins A-I and A-II, leading to higher HDL-cholesterol levels [Australian].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Nanosized tablets (48/145 mg) have high bioavailability that is not significantly affected by food [Australian].
- **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound to albumin (>99%). Peak plasma levels are reached within 2–4 hours [Australian].
- **Metabolism:** Rapidly hydrolysed by esterases to the active metabolite, fenofibric acid. No parent drug is detected in plasma [Australian].
- **Elimination:** Elimination half-life is approximately 20 hours. Primarily excreted in urine (70% within 24 hours; 88% within 6 days) as fenofibric acid and its glucuronide conjugate [Australian].
- **Dialysis:** Fenofibric acid is not eliminated by haemodialysis [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **FIELD Study:** A 5-year study of 9,795 patients with T2DM. Fenofibrate did not significantly reduce the primary outcome of major coronary events but did significantly reduce total cardiovascular events (11% relative reduction). There was a non-significant increase in CHD mortality [Australian].
- **ACCORD-Lipid Trial:** Evaluated fenofibrate + simvastatin vs. simvastatin alone in T2DM. No significant difference was found in the primary composite outcome for the overall population. However, a pre-specified subgroup with high TG and low HDL showed a 31% relative reduction in events [Australian].
- **DAIS Study:** A 3-year angiographic study in T2DM showing that fenofibrate significantly reduced the progression of focal coronary atherosclerosis [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Protection:** Protect from moisture [Australian].
