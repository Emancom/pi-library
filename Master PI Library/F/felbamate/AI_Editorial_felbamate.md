# Editorial Product Information: Felbamate

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview of felbamate, designed for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical and regulatory data into a single, structured reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is intended for informational purposes and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) mandated for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved labeling for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Felbamate (INN); a dicarbamate derivative (2-phenyl-1,3-propanediol dicarbamate).
- **Brand Names:** Felbatol (historical reference); current source reflects generic Felbamate [US].
- **ATC Code:** N03AX10.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Oral Suspension: 600 mg/5 mL [US].
    - Oral Tablet: (Mentioned as bioequivalent in pharmacokinetics, though suspension is the primary focus of the source document) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sun Pharmaceutical Industries, Inc. (Labeler); Taro Pharmaceutical Industries Ltd. (Manufacturer) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
Felbamate is not indicated as a first-line antiepileptic treatment. It is recommended only for patients who respond inadequately to alternative treatments and whose epilepsy is sufficiently severe that the substantial risks of aplastic anemia and/or liver failure are deemed acceptable.

- **Adults (Monotherapy or Adjunctive):** For the treatment of partial seizures, with and without secondary generalisation, in patients 14 years of age and older [US-approved].
- **Children (Adjunctive):** For the treatment of partial and generalised seizures associated with Lennox-Gastaut syndrome in children aged 2 to 14 years [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults ≥14 years)
The majority of patients in clinical trials received 3600 mg/day. Dosage of concomitant antiepileptic drugs (AEDs) should be reduced by 20% to 33% when initiating felbamate to minimise side effects.

- **Initial Monotherapy:** Start at 1200 mg/day in 3 or 4 divided doses. Titrate under close clinical supervision by 600 mg increments every 2 weeks to 2400 mg/day, and then to 3600 mg/day if clinically indicated [US].
- **Conversion to Monotherapy:** Start at 1200 mg/day in 3 or 4 divided doses while reducing concomitant AEDs by one-third. At Week 2, increase felbamate to 2400 mg/day and reduce other AEDs by another one-third. At Week 3, increase to 3600 mg/day and continue reducing other AEDs as indicated [US].
- **Adjunctive Therapy:** Add 1200 mg/day in 3 or 4 divided doses. Increase by 1200 mg/day increments at weekly intervals to 3600 mg/day [US].

### Pediatrics (Lennox-Gastaut Syndrome, 2-14 years)
- **Adjunctive Therapy:** Add 15 mg/kg/day in 3 or 4 divided doses. Increase by 15 mg/kg/day increments at weekly intervals to a maximum of 45 mg/kg/day (or 3600 mg/day) [US].

### Administration and Missed Dose
- **Administration:** Shake the suspension well before use [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to felbamate, its ingredients, or other carbamates [US].
- History of any blood dyscrasia [US].
- History of hepatic dysfunction [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
- **Aplastic Anemia:** Associated with a marked increase in incidence (estimated 2 to 5 per million per year in the general population vs. >100-fold increase with felbamate). Case fatality rates are estimated at 20% to 30%. Onset typically occurs 5 to 30 weeks after initiation.
- **Hepatic Failure:** Postmarketing experience suggests a rate of roughly 6 cases of fatal/transplant-requiring liver failure per 75,000 patient-years. 67% of reported cases resulted in death or transplant, usually within 5 weeks of symptom onset.

### General Warnings
- **Sudden Withdrawal:** AEDs should not be discontinued abruptly due to the risk of increased seizure frequency [US].
- **Suicidal Behavior and Ideation:** AEDs increase the risk of suicidal thoughts or behavior. Patients should be monitored for the emergence of depression or unusual changes in mood [US].
- **Written Acknowledgement:** In the US, felbamate should not be used until there has been a complete discussion of risks and the patient/guardian has signed an acknowledgement form.

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Hematologic:** Perform full hematologic evaluations (including platelets and reticulocytes) at baseline, frequently during therapy, and for a significant period after discontinuation. Discontinue if bone marrow depression occurs [US].
- **Hepatic:** Monitor serum transaminase levels (AST, ALT) at baseline and periodically. Discontinue if AST or ALT levels increase to ≥2 times the Upper Limit of Normal (ULN) [US].

| **CNS** | Headache (37%), Somnolence (19%), Dizziness (18%), Insomnia (18%) | Somnolence (48%), Insomnia (16%), Nervousness (16%) |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Aplastic anemia and pancytopenia [US].
- Acute liver failure and hepatitis [US].
- Stevens-Johnson Syndrome and Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (Rare/Postmarketing) [US].
- Anaphylactoid reactions [US].
- Status epilepticus [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
Felbamate is a potent modulator of cytochrome P450 and other metabolic pathways.

- **Effects of Felbamate on other AEDs:**
    - **Phenytoin:** Increases steady-state plasma concentrations (approx. 40% dose reduction often required) [US].
    - **Valproate:** Increases steady-state concentrations [US].
    - **Carbamazepine:** Decreases steady-state concentrations of the parent drug but **increases** levels of the active metabolite (carbamazepine epoxide) [US].
    - **Phenobarbital:** Increases plasma concentrations [US].
- **Effects of other AEDs on Felbamate:**
    - **Phenytoin/Carbamazepine:** Decrease felbamate steady-state concentrations by ~45% and ~40% respectively [US].
    - **Valproate:** No significant effect on felbamate clearance [US].
- **Oral Contraceptives:** Felbamate may decrease the AUC of gestodene by 42%, potentially reducing contraceptive efficacy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category C]. Animal studies showed decreased pup weight and increased pup deaths during lactation, but no malformations. Use only if clearly needed. Prescribers are encouraged to enroll patients in the North American Antiepileptic Drug (NAAED) Pregnancy Registry [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Felbamate is excreted in human milk. The effect on the nursing infant is unknown; caution is advised [US].
- **Fertility:** No effects on male or female fertility were observed in rat studies at doses up to 13.9 times the human dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Pediatric Use:** Safety/efficacy not established in children other than those with Lennox-Gastaut syndrome. Approved for ages 2–14 years [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of patients over 65. Dose selection should be cautious, generally starting at the low end of the range [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Felbamate clearance is reduced and half-life is prolonged in proportion to declining renal function. **Reduce starting and maintenance doses by one-half** in patients with renal dysfunction [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with a history of hepatic dysfunction [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Exact mechanism is unknown. In vitro studies indicate felbamate is an antagonist at the strychnine-insensitive glycine recognition site of the NMDA receptor-ionophore complex. It has weak inhibitory effects on GABA and benzodiazepine receptor binding [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Well-absorbed (>90%). Food does not affect tablet absorption (suspension not evaluated) [US].
    - **Distribution:** 22–25% protein bound, primarily to albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Approx. 40–50% excreted unchanged in urine; 40% unidentified metabolites/conjugates; 15% as para-hydroxyfelbamate, 2-hydroxyfelbamate, and monocarbamate metabolites (inactive) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is 20 to 23 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adult Monotherapy:** Two double-blind trials compared felbamate (3600 mg/day) to low-dose valproate. Felbamate was significantly superior in preventing "escape" (seizure frequency increase) [US].
- **Adult Adjunctive Therapy:** Crossover and add-on trials demonstrated significant reductions in seizure frequency compared to placebo in patients with refractory partial-onset seizures [US].
- **Pediatric Lennox-Gastaut:** A 70-day trial (45 mg/kg/day) showed felbamate was superior to placebo in reducing atonic seizures (44% vs 7% reduction) and total seizures [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Shake well before using. Dispense in a tight container [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
