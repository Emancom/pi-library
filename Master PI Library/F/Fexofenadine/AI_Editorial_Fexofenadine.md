# Editorial Product Information: Fexofenadine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Fexofenadine hydrochloride (a carboxylic acid metabolite of terfenadine).
- **Chemical Class:** Orally-active, non-sedating histamine H1-receptor antagonist.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Fexotabs** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - 180 mg film-coated tablets [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sanofi Consumer Healthcare [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Seasonal Allergic Rhinitis:** Relief of symptoms associated with seasonal allergic rhinitis in adults and children aged 12 years or older [Australian].
- **Urticaria:** Relief of symptoms associated with urticaria in adults and children aged 12 years or older [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Children $\ge$ 12 Years)
- **Standard Dose:** 180 mg taken once daily as required [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Antacid Interaction:** It is advised to leave a 2-hour interval between the administration of fexofenadine and antacids containing aluminium and magnesium hydroxide [Australian].
- **Food:** Coadministration with food has no clinically significant effect on the absorption of fexofenadine [Australian].

### Special Populations
- **Elderly/Renal/Hepatic:** No dosage adjustment is required for these populations based on current clinical data [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to **fexofenadine**, terfenadine, or any of the inactive excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
- **General Precautions:**
    - **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** While dosage adjustment is not required, clinicians should be aware that peak plasma levels and half-life may be increased (54% and 19% respectively in renal disease) [Australian].
- **Ability to Drive/Use Machines:** The effects on driving performance were not specifically assessed during registration [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Fexofenadine is generally well-tolerated. Adverse event incidences in clinical trials were similar to placebo.

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (>1%)** | Headache, fatigue, dizziness, drowsiness, nausea. |
| **Uncommon (<1%)** | Nervousness, insomnia, sleep disorders, or paroniria (abnormal dreams). |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Rash, urticaria, pruritus, and hypersensitivity reactions (including angioedema, chest tightness, dyspnoea, flushing, and systemic anaphylaxis). |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Erythromycin and Ketoconazole:** Coadministration results in a 2- to 3-fold increase in fexofenadine plasma levels. This is attributed to increased gastrointestinal absorption and decreased biliary/gastrointestinal secretion. These changes are not associated with QTc interval prolongation or increased adverse events [Australian].
- **Antacids (Aluminium/Magnesium Hydroxide):** Administration 15 minutes prior to fexofenadine reduces bioavailability, likely due to binding in the GI tract. Space doses by 2 hours [Australian].
- **Apalutamide:** As a weak P-gp inducer, coadministration may result in a 30% decrease in fexofenadine AUC [Australian].
- **Omeprazole:** No clinically significant interaction observed [Australian].
- **Pseudoephedrine:** Pharmacokinetics are not altered when coadministered [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B2.** 
    - Animal studies (rats/rabbits) showed no evidence of teratogenicity. However, decreased pup weight and survival were noted in rats at high doses (150 mg/kg/day). There are no adequate studies in pregnant women; use is only recommended if the potential benefit justifies the risk [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Fexofenadine has been found to cross into human breast milk when terfenadine was administered. Use in nursing women is not recommended unless the physician determines the benefit outweighs the potential risk to the infant [Australian].
- **Fertility:** In rat studies, dose-related reductions in implants and increases in post-implantation losses were observed at high doses of terfenadine (equivalent to >3x human therapeutic exposure) [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy of the 180 mg tablet have not been established in children under 12 years of age [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment is required, although peak plasma levels may be increased by 68% in this population [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment required [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dosage adjustment required [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A selective, peripheral H1-receptor antagonist. It inhibits antigen-induced bronchospasm and histamine release from mast cells at high concentrations. It does not cross the blood-brain barrier [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Antihistaminic effect begins within one hour, reaches maximum at 2–4 hours, and lasts at least 12 hours. No evidence of tolerance after 28 days of dosing [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; $T_{max}$ occurs 1–3 hours post-dose. Absolute bioavailability is approximately 33%.
    - **Distribution:** 60% to 70% plasma protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Negligible hepatic metabolism.
    - **Elimination:** Mean terminal half-life of 14–15 hours. Primary excretion is via faeces (80%) and urine (11%) as unchanged drug [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Seasonal Allergic Rhinitis (SAR):** Double-blind studies (up to 861 patients) demonstrated that 120 mg and 180 mg once daily were significantly superior to placebo in reducing total symptom scores (sneezing, rhinorrhoea, itchy nose/eyes). Both doses showed significant improvements in quality-of-life and work/activity impairment scores [Australian].
- **Chronic Idiopathic Urticaria (CIU):** A 6-week trial of 208 patients showed that 180 mg and 240 mg once daily significantly reduced the number of wheals and pruritus compared to placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Expiry date is located on the packaging [Australian].
- **Packaging:** Available in PVC/PE/PVDC/Al blister packs [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 2** (Pharmacy Medicine) [Australian].
