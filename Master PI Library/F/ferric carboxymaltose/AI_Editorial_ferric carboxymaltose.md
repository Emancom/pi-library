# Editorial Product Information: Ferric Carboxymaltose

**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been synthesized by combining regulatory data and clinical guidelines from the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). It is intended for use by healthcare professionals as a comprehensive reference. Where recommendations or approved uses differ between jurisdictions, the source agency is indicated in brackets (e.g., [TGA] or [FDA]).

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** Ferric Carboxymaltose (FCM)  
**Active Ingredient:** Ferric carboxymaltose (a colloidal iron(III) hydroxide complex with a carbohydrate polymer).  
**Concentration:** 50 mg of elemental iron per mL.

## 2. Approved Indications
Ferric carboxymaltose is an intravenous iron replacement product indicated for:

*   **Iron Deficiency Anemia (IDA):** In adult and pediatric patients (1 year of age and older) who have demonstrated an intolerance to oral iron or have had an unsatisfactory clinical response to oral iron therapy.
*   **Iron Deficiency in Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD):** Specifically for adult patients with non-dialysis dependent CKD [FDA].
*   **Heart Failure:** To improve exercise capacity in adult patients with iron deficiency and New York Heart Association (NYHA) class II/III heart failure [FDA].
*   **General Iron Deficiency:** Treatment of iron deficiency in adults and adolescents (14 years and older) when oral iron is ineffective, unusable, or when rapid iron delivery is clinically necessary [TGA].

The diagnosis of iron deficiency must be confirmed by appropriate laboratory testing (e.g., serum ferritin, transferrin saturation).

## 3. Contraindications
The administration of ferric carboxymaltose is contraindicated in:
*   Patients with a known history of hypersensitivity to ferric carboxymaltose or any of its excipients.
*   Anemia not caused by iron deficiency (e.g., other microcytic or megaloblastic anemias) [TGA].
*   Evidence of iron overload or disturbances in iron utilization [TGA].

## 4. Warnings and Precautions

### Hypersensitivity Reactions
Serious and potentially fatal anaphylactic-type reactions have been reported. Patients may present with shock, loss of consciousness, collapse, or clinically significant hypotension. Observation is required for at least 30 minutes following every administration. FCM should only be administered in facilities equipped to manage advanced resuscitation.

### Symptomatic Hypophosphatemia
FCM can cause a reduction in serum phosphate levels. While often transient, cases of symptomatic hypophosphatemia leading to osteomalacia and fractures—sometimes requiring surgical intervention—have been reported in the post-marketing setting [TGA/FDA]. 
*   **Risk Factors:** These include Malabsorption, hyperparathyroidism, Vitamin D deficiency, and inflammatory bowel disease. 
*   **Monitoring:** Clinicians should monitor serum phosphate in patients requiring repeat courses of treatment or those with existing risk factors.

### Hypertension
Transient elevations in systolic blood pressure, often resolving within 30 minutes, have been observed following FCM administration. Blood pressure should be monitored after each dose [FDA].

### Paravenous Leakage
Caution must be exercised to avoid extravasation. Leakage at the injection site may lead to irritation and potentially long-lasting, irreversible brown skin discoloration. If extravasation occurs, the administration must be stopped immediately.

### Infections
Parenteral iron should be used with caution in patients with acute or chronic infections. It is recommended to suspend treatment in patients with ongoing bacteremia [TGA].

## 5. Dosage and Administration
FCM must be administered only by the intravenous route, either as a slow bolus injection or an infusion. It must not be given via subcutaneous or intramuscular routes.

### Adult and Adolescent (14+ years) Dosing [TGA]
Dosing is determined by a stepwise approach:
1.  **Cumulative Dose:** Calculated using the **Ganzoni Method** (based on weight and Hb deficit) or the **Simplified Method**. 
    *   *Simplified Method:* Patients <70 kg (1000–1500 mg total); patients ≥70 kg (1500–2000 mg total) depending on Hb levels.
2.  **Maximum Limits:** A single dose must not exceed 1,000 mg of iron (20 mg/kg body weight). No more than 1,000 mg should be administered within a single week.

### US Standard Dosing Regimen [FDA]
*   **Patients ≥50 kg:** Two doses of 750 mg separated by at least 7 days (Total 1,500 mg). Alternatively, a single dose of 15 mg/kg (max 1,000 mg) may be administered.
*   **Patients <50 kg:** Two doses of 15 mg/kg body weight separated by at least 7 days.

### Heart Failure Specific Dosing [FDA]
Dosing is based on baseline Hb and body weight (<70 kg or ≥70 kg), with doses typically given at Day 1 and Week 6, followed by maintenance doses of 500 mg at 12, 24, and 36 weeks if iron stores remain low.

### Pediatric Dosing (Ages 1–13 years)
*   **TGA:** Two doses of 15 mg/kg (max 750 mg per dose) separated by at least 7 days.
*   **FDA:** Two doses of 15 mg/kg separated by at least 7 days for a total cumulative dose of 30 mg/kg.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most frequently reported adverse drug reactions (ADR) include:
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea (most common), vomiting, abdominal pain, constipation, or diarrhea.
*   **Vascular/Nervous System:** Hypertension, flushing, dizziness, and headache.
*   **Local Reactions:** Injection site reactions (discoloration, pain, or irritation).
*   **Metabolic:** Hypophosphatemia (decreased serum phosphate).
*   **Rare/Serious:** Anaphylactic reactions, Kounis syndrome (acute allergic coronary arteriospasm), and tachycardia.

## 7. Drug Interactions
Parenteral iron reduces the absorption of oral iron preparations. If oral iron therapy is to be implemented, it should not be initiated for at least 5 days following the final FCM administration.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

### Pregnancy
*   **Category B3 [TGA]:** Data on use in the first trimester is limited. It is recommended that treatment be confined to the second and third trimesters if the benefits outweigh the risks.
*   **Clinical Note:** FCM should be avoided before 16 weeks gestation unless oral treatment is ineffective or contraindicated [TGA]. 
*   **Fetal Risk:** Maternal hypersensitivity reactions may lead to fetal bradycardia.

### Breastfeeding
FCM is excreted in human milk in negligible amounts (≤1%). Clinical studies have not shown adverse effects on breastfed infants, although constipation and diarrhea have been noted.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** Following injection, the iron is rapidly cleared from the plasma and distributed primarily to the liver, spleen, and bone marrow. The volume of distribution is approximately 3 L.
*   **Elimination:** The terminal half-life ranges from 7 to 12 hours. Renal excretion of iron is negligible.
*   **Utilization:** Iron is incorporated into red blood cells within 6–9 days, with 91–99% utilization in patients with IDA after 24 days.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Ferric carboxymaltose is a complex of a polynuclear iron(III)-hydroxide core with a carbohydrate ligand. The complex is designed to be stable and release iron in a controlled manner to iron-binding proteins (transferrin and ferritin), minimizing the risk of toxicity from non-transferrin bound iron.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   Store below 25°C.
*   Do not freeze.
*   Keep vials in the original carton to protect from light.
*   **After Dilution:** If diluted in sterile 0.9% NaCl, the solution is stable for up to 72 hours at room temperature [FDA], though TGA recommends use within 12 hours if stored at 2–8°C for microbiological safety.
