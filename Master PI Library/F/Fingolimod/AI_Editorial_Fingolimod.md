# Editorial Product Information: Fingolimod

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of fingolimod, intended for use by healthcare professionals as a clinically accurate reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information approved by the regulatory authority in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the local PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Fingolimod hydrochloride (chemical class: sphingosine 1-phosphate (S1P) receptor modulator).
- **Brand Name(s):** **AKM Fingolimod** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L04AE01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Hard capsules: 0.5 mg (equivalent to 560 micrograms of fingolimod hydrochloride). 
    - 0.25 mg capsules (available in other brands) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pharmacor Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Relapsing Multiple Sclerosis (MS):** Indicated for the treatment of adults and paediatric patients aged 10 years and older with relapsing forms of MS. The primary clinical goals are to reduce the frequency of clinical relapses and delay the progression of physical disability [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The recommended dose is 0.5 mg taken orally once daily.
- **Dosing in Paediatric Patients (10 years and older):** Dosing is based on body weight:
    - **$\le$ 40 kg:** 0.25 mg orally once daily.
    - **> 40 kg:** 0.5 mg orally once daily.
- **Administration Instructions:** May be taken with or without food.
- **First-Dose Monitoring (Critical Safety Requirement):**
    - Initiation of therapy requires observation for at least 6 hours post-dose for signs of bradycardia. 
    - Monitoring must include hourly pulse and blood pressure measurements.
    - An electrocardiogram (ECG) must be performed prior to the first dose and at the end of the 6-hour observation period.
    - If a patient is switched from 0.25 mg to 0.5 mg daily, the first-dose monitoring protocol must be repeated.
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** If a dose is missed, treatment should continue with the next scheduled dose. However, if treatment is interrupted for specific durations (e.g., more than 2 weeks after the first month of treatment), the first-dose monitoring protocol must be re-instituted upon re-initiation.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Recent Cardiovascular/Cerebrovascular Events (within the last 6 months):** Myocardial infarction, unstable angina, stroke, transient ischaemic attack (TIA), decompensated heart failure requiring hospitalisation, or Class III/IV heart failure.
- **Cardiac Conduction Abnormalities:** History or presence of Mobitz Type II second-degree or third-degree atrioventricular (AV) block, or sick sinus syndrome (unless the patient has a functioning pacemaker).
- **QT Interval:** Baseline QTc interval $\ge$ 500 ms.
- **Concomitant Medications:** Co-administration with Class Ia or Class III anti-arrhythmic drugs during treatment initiation.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to fingolimod or any of the excipients.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Infections:** Fingolimod reduces peripheral lymphocyte counts (to 20–30% of baseline). This increases the risk of serious and opportunistic infections. A recent complete blood count (CBC) should be available prior to initiation. 
    - **PML:** Cases of Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy (PML) have occurred, typically after 2-3 years of treatment. The incidence appears higher in patients in Japan [Australian/Japanese observation].
    - **Cryptococcal Infections:** Fatal cases of cryptococcal meningitis have been reported.
- **Bradyarrhythmia:** Initiation causes a transient decrease in heart rate. Caution is required in patients with sino-atrial heart block, history of syncope, or symptomatic bradycardia.
- **Macular Oedema:** Occurs in approximately 0.4% of patients at the 0.5 mg dose. Ophthalmologic evaluation is required at baseline and 3–4 months after initiation. Patients with diabetes or uveitis are at higher risk.
- **Liver Function:** Elevations in transaminases (ALT/AST) are common. Liver function tests (LFTs) should be obtained within 6 months prior to initiation and monitored periodically.
- **Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES):** Rare cases have been reported; fingolimod should be discontinued if PRES is suspected.
- **Skin Cancer:** Increased risk of basal cell carcinoma (BCC), melanoma, and squamous cell carcinoma. Periodic skin examinations are recommended.
- **Rebound Effect:** Severe exacerbation of disease activity has been reported after stopping therapy, typically within 12–24 weeks of discontinuation.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by frequency from pooled clinical trial data:

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Influenza, sinusitis, headache, cough, diarrhoea, back pain, hepatic enzyme elevation (ALT, GGT, AST). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Bronchitis, herpes zoster, tinea versicolor, basal cell carcinoma, lymphopenia, leucopenia, dizziness, migraine, blurred vision, bradycardia, hypertension, dyspnoea, eczema, pruritus, asthenia, weight decrease. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Pneumonia, macular oedema, malignant melanoma, seizures, thrombocytopenia. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Lymphoma, Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES). |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Kaposi’s sarcoma. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy (PML), cryptococcal meningitis, serious hepatic injury, and clinically significant bradyarrhythmia.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Anti-neoplastic/Immunosuppressive Therapies:** Risk of additive immune system effects. Caution is required when switching from long-acting therapies (e.g., natalizumab, teriflunomide).
- **Anti-arrhythmics:** Class Ia (e.g., quinidine) and Class III (e.g., amiodarone) are contraindicated during initiation.
- **Heart Rate Lowering Agents:** Beta-blockers, diltiazem, verapamil, digoxin, and ivabradine may exacerbate bradycardia.
- **Vaccination:** Live attenuated vaccines should be avoided during and for 2 months after stopping treatment.
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** 
    - **Ketoconazole:** Increases fingolimod AUC by 1.7-fold via CYP4F2 inhibition.
    - **Carbamazepine:** May decrease fingolimod concentrations.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category D [Australian].** Fingolimod is associated with an increased risk of major congenital malformations (7.2% in prospective registry data). 
    - **Contraception:** Females of reproductive potential must use effective contraception during and for **2 months** after stopping treatment. 
    - **Washout:** Fingolimod should be stopped 2 months before planning pregnancy.
- **Breastfeeding:** Fingolimod is excreted in the milk of treated rats. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants, breastfeeding is not recommended.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no effect on fertility or sperm counts, but human data are unavailable.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for patients $\ge$ 10 years of age. Safety/efficacy in children < 10 years not established. Seizures were reported more frequently in paediatric trials (5.6%) compared to adults.
- **Elderly Use ($\ge$ 65 years):** Limited clinical experience; use with caution.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild to Moderate (Child-Pugh A and B):** No dose adjustment needed, but use with caution.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Not recommended [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Fingolimod is metabolised to fingolimod-phosphate, which acts as a functional antagonist at S1P receptors (S1P1, 3, 4, 5). This prevents lymphocytes from egressing from lymph nodes, reducing the infiltration of pathogenic cells into the CNS.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Slow ($T_{max}$ 12–16 hours); bioavailability 93%.
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein bound (>99.7%); distributes extensively into tissues ($V_d$ ~1200 L).
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP4F2; also involves CYP3A4 under strong induction.
    - **Elimination:** Average half-life is 6–9 days. 81% excreted in urine as inactive metabolites.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **FREEDOMS (D2301):** A 2-year placebo-controlled study in 1,272 patients. Fingolimod 0.5 mg reduced the annualised relapse rate (ARR) by 54% and significantly delayed disability progression compared to placebo.
- **TRANSFORMS (D2302):** A 1-year study vs. interferon beta-1a. Fingolimod 0.5 mg showed a 52% relative reduction in ARR compared to the active comparator.
- **PARADIGMS (D2311):** A paediatric study (ages 10–18) where fingolimod showed an 81.9% reduction in ARR compared to interferon beta-1a over 24 months.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Packaging:** PVC/ACLAR-Al blister packs.
- **Handling:** Protect from moisture.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Prescription Only Medicine (**S4**) [Australian].
