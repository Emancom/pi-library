# Editorial Product Information: Fosfomycin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- Information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., US, European, Canadian, Japanese) was not provided in the source material and is therefore not included or is marked as "Not specified in available sources."
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of fosfomycin trometamol for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference and is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Fosfomycin (as fosfomycin trometamol).
- **Chemical Class:** Phosphonic acid derivative; antibacterial.
- **Brand Names:** CIPLA FOSFOMYCIN [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Granules for oral solution containing 5.631 g of fosfomycin trometamol, equivalent to 3.0 g of fosfomycin. 
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Cipla Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Uncomplicated Lower Urinary Tract Infection (Acute Cystitis):** Indicated for treatment in females aged 12 years and older [Australian].
- **Pathogen Specificity:** Indicated for infections caused by susceptible strains of *Escherichia coli* or *Enterococcus faecalis* [Australian].
- **Limitations of Use:** 
    - Not indicated for the treatment of pyelonephritis or perinephric abscess.
    - Not recommended where resistance is likely (e.g., previous treatment failure or known non-susceptible organisms).
    - Not recommended for use in males due to limited efficacy data [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult Women >12 years):** A single 3.0 g sachet administered once.
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Must be taken on an empty stomach (approximately 2–3 hours after a meal).
    - Preferably administered before bedtime after emptying the bladder.
    - Dissolve the contents of one sachet in a glass of cold water; do not use hot water.
    - Consume immediately after preparation.
- **General Instruction:** Do not use more than one single dose to treat a single episode of acute cystitis. Repeated daily dosing does not improve clinical success but increases the risk of adverse events [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to fosfomycin trometamol or any excipients (including sucrose).
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with severe renal failure (creatinine clearance <10 mL/min).
- **Renal Replacement Therapy:** Contraindicated in patients undergoing haemodialysis, peritoneal dialysis, or haemofiltration [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious and occasionally fatal anaphylaxis or anaphylactic shock may occur. If a reaction occurs, the drug must not be re-administered, and emergency treatment must be initiated.
- **Clostridioides difficile-associated diarrhoea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all antibacterial agents, including fosfomycin. Severity can range from mild diarrhoea to fatal colitis. Consider CDAD in patients presenting with severe or persistent diarrhoea during or several weeks after treatment.
- **Sucrose Content:** Contains 2.213 g of sucrose per sachet. Not recommended for patients with rare hereditary problems of fructose intolerance, glucose-galactose malabsorption, or sucrase-isomaltase insufficiency.
- **Central Nervous System:** Dizziness has been reported; patients should exercise caution when driving or operating machinery.
- **Repeat Dosing:** Avoid multiple doses for a single infection episode as it does not improve microbiological eradication [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most common adverse reactions involve the gastrointestinal tract and are generally self-limiting.

### Frequency of Adverse Reactions (Pivotal Trial Data)
| Adverse Event | Fosfomycin (n=1233) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Diarrhoea | 9.0% |
| Vaginitis | 5.5% |
| Nausea | 4.1% |
| Headache | 3.9% |
| Dizziness | 1.3% |
| Asthenia | 1.1% |
| Dyspepsia | 1.1% |

### System Organ Class Listing
- **Common (1–10%):** Vulvovaginitis, headache, dizziness, diarrhoea, nausea, dyspepsia, abdominal pain.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Vomiting, anorexia, dry mouth, flatulence, rash, urticaria, pruritus.
- **Not Known (Frequency cannot be estimated):** Anaphylactic reactions/shock, angioedema, antibiotic-associated colitis.
- **Rare/Isolated Reports:** One case of unilateral optic neuritis was reported in pivotal trials, considered possibly treatment-related [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Metoclopramide:** Concomitant administration significantly lowers serum and urinary concentrations of fosfomycin and should be avoided.
- **Gastrointestinal Motility Agents:** Other drugs that increase GI motility may similarly reduce fosfomycin levels.
- **Urinary Alkalinisers:** Not recommended, as fosfomycin exhibits optimal bactericidal activity at typical (acidic) urinary pH.
- **Anticoagulants (Coumarins):** While fosfomycin does not bind to plasma proteins or undergo metabolism, both increases and decreases in INR have been reported post-marketing. Monitoring of coagulation parameters is recommended in patients taking warfarin or related agents.
- **Cimetidine:** Does not alter the pharmacokinetics of fosfomycin [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:**
    - **Australian Category B2:** Limited data (n=152) available for the first trimester. While human data (n>1500) do not currently suggest malformations or foetal/maternal toxicity, single-dose treatments are generally not considered suitable for UTIs in pregnancy according to some clinical perspectives [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Fosfomycin is excreted in human breast milk. Use is not recommended unless the potential benefit to the mother outweighs the risk to the infant [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies (rats) showed no effect on fertility at doses 3 times the human clinical dose. No human data available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children under 12 years of age have not been established. Use is not recommended in this population [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment is required in elderly patients with good renal function.
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Mild to Moderate (CrCl >10 mL/min):** No dosage adjustment necessary for a single dose, but clinical monitoring is advised.
    - **Severe (CrCl <10 mL/min):** Use is contraindicated.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specifically studied; however, since fosfomycin is not metabolised by the liver and is primarily excreted renally, no dosage adjustment is anticipated [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Fosfomycin inhibits the first stage of bacterial cell wall synthesis. It irreversibly blocks the condensation of uridine diphosphate-N-acetylglucosamine with p-enolpyruvate by inhibiting the phosphoenolpyruvate transferase enzyme. It also reduces bacterial adhesion to the bladder mucosa.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Exhibits bactericidal activity. It is most effective at acidic pH. Action is likely concentration-dependent.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute oral bioavailability is approximately 35–40%. Food delays absorption but does not affect the total amount excreted in urine.
    - **Distribution:** Distributed to the kidneys, bladder wall, prostate, and seminal vesicles. It crosses the placental barrier. Plasma protein binding is negligible.
    - **Metabolism:** Not metabolised.
    - **Elimination:** Excreted unchanged mainly via the kidneys (40–50% of dose in urine) and to a lesser extent in faeces (18–28%). Elimination half-life is approximately 4 hours, though significantly prolonged in renal impairment (up to 50 hours in severe impairment) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** Three phase 3, prospective, double-blind trials (MON-US-01, -02, -03) compared a single 3 g dose of fosfomycin to ciprofloxacin, trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole, and nitrofurantoin.
- **Outcomes:** 
    - Microbiologic eradication rates for fosfomycin were approximately 82% at 5–11 days post-therapy.
    - Clinical success (symptom resolution) was approximately 70%.
    - Fosfomycin was found to be microbiologically inferior to ciprofloxacin (7 days) and trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole (10 days) but equivalent to nitrofurantoin (7 days) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].
