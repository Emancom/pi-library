# Editorial Product Information: Flecainide

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of flecainide product information for use by healthcare professionals. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in specific jurisdictions. Clinicians should refer to their local regulatory authority's current approved documentation.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Flecainide acetate
- **Chemical Class:** Benzamide class antiarrhythmic; membrane-stabilising (Class 1c) agent.
- **Brand Names:** APO-FLECAINIDE [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** C01BC04
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - 50 mg Tablets: White to off-white, circular, biconvex, debossed with 'F1'. [Australian]
    - 100 mg Tablets: White to off-white, circular, biconvex, debossed with 'F & 2' separated by a scoreline. [Australian]
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Apotex Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Supraventricular Arrhythmias:** 
    - Arrhythmias associated with pre-excitation syndromes (e.g., Wolff-Parkinson-White and Lown-Ganong-Levine syndromes).
    - Arrhythmias due to dual AV nodal pathways in patients with debilitating symptoms.
    - Paroxysmal atrial fibrillation/flutter (PAF) associated with disabling symptoms.
- **Ventricular Arrhythmias:** 
    - Maintenance of normal rhythm in life-threatening ventricular arrhythmias not controlled by other therapies.
- **Note on Chronic Atrial Fibrillation:** Use in chronic atrial fibrillation is not recommended as it has not been adequately studied. [Australian]

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
Dosage must be individualised based on therapeutic response and patient tolerance.

### Dosing by Indication
- **Supraventricular Arrhythmias (PSVT and PAF):** 
    - Recommended starting dose: 50 mg every 12 hours.
    - Incremental increases: 50 mg twice daily every four days until efficacy is achieved.
    - Maximum dose: 300 mg/day.
- **Sustained Ventricular Tachycardia (VT):**
    - Recommended starting dose: 100 mg every 12 hours.
    - Incremental increases: 50 mg twice daily every four days. 
    - Usual maintenance: 150 mg every 12 hours (300 mg/day).
    - Maximum dose: 400 mg/day.

### Administration and Titration
- **Steady State:** Due to a long half-life (12–27 hours), steady-state plasma levels may take 3 to 5 days to achieve. Dose adjustments should occur no more frequently than every four days.
- **Loading Dose:** Not recommended due to an increased risk of adverse effects.
- **Transferring Therapy:** When switching from another antiarrhythmic, allow at least two plasma half-lives of the discontinued drug to elapse before starting flecainide.

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Second- or third-degree AV block (unless a pacemaker is present).
- Right bundle branch block (RBBB) when associated with a left hemi-block (bifascicular block), unless a pacemaker is present.
- Cardiogenic shock.
- Asymptomatic premature ventricular contractions (PVCs) or asymptomatic non-sustained ventricular tachycardia in patients with a history of myocardial infarction.
- Known hypersensitivity to flecainide.
- Severe renal or hepatic impairment, unless plasma level monitoring is available. [Australian]

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Mortality Risk (CAST Study):** In the Cardiac Arrhythmia Suppression Trial (CAST), flecainide was associated with a higher incidence of mortality or non-fatal cardiac arrest in patients post-myocardial infarction with asymptomatic non-life-threatening ventricular arrhythmias. Prophylactic use of Class I antiarrhythmics in the post-MI setting is potentially hazardous.
- **Proarrhythmic Effects:** Flecainide can cause new or worsened arrhythmias (e.g., increased PVCs or severe VT). Risk is higher in patients with sustained VT, congestive heart failure (CHF), or low ejection fraction.
- **Structural Heart Disease:** Use with extreme caution in patients with impaired left ventricular function (EF ≤ 40%) due to the risk of life-threatening ventricular arrhythmias.
- **Heart Failure:** Flecainide has a mild negative inotropic effect and may worsen CHF.
- **Cardiac Conduction:** Commonly increases PR, QRS, and QT intervals. Dosage reduction should be considered if the PR interval exceeds 0.20 seconds or the QRS complex widens by more than 25%.
- **Pacemaker Thresholds:** Flecainide can reversibly increase endocardial pacing thresholds. Use with caution in patients with permanent pacemakers.
- **Electrolyte Disturbances:** Hypokalaemia or hyperkalaemia must be corrected before starting flecainide.

### Monitoring Requirements
- Periodic monitoring of trough plasma levels is recommended, especially in patients with severe renal failure or CHF. The therapeutic range is typically 200 to 1,000 ng/mL. [Australian]

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Cardiac Adverse Effects
- **Serious:** New or exacerbated ventricular arrhythmias (6.8% in VT patients), worsened CHF (3.9% in VT patients), AV block (2nd or 3rd degree: 0.9%), and sinus bradycardia/arrest (1.2%). [Australian]

### Non-Cardiac Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Dizziness (27–37%), visual disturbances (26–38% including blurred vision/diplopia), headache (10–18%), nausea (10–18%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Dyspnoea (9–13%), fatigue (7–13%), palpitations (6–11%), chest pain (6–12%), tremor (6%), asthenia (5%), constipation (4%), rash (4%), abdominal pain (3%), paraesthesia (3%) |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Impotence, decreased libido, gynaecomastia, malaise, hypotension, hypertension, myocardial infarction, dyspepsia, anxiety, convulsions, hallucination, renal failure |

- **Post-marketing/Rare:** Hepatic dysfunction (cholestasis, hepatic failure), pulmonary fibrosis, interstitial lung disease, and blood dyscrasias (pancytopenia, anaemia). [Australian]

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Amiodarone:** Increases flecainide plasma levels. Reduce flecainide dose by 50% and monitor plasma levels closely.
- **Digoxin:** Flecainide increases plasma digoxin levels by approximately 13%.
- **Beta-blockers:** Potential for additive negative inotropic effects. Co-administration of flecainide and propranolol may increase levels of both drugs by 20–30%.
- **Cimetidine:** Increases flecainide plasma levels by approximately 30% and prolongs half-life.
- **Enzyme Inducers (Phenytoin, Phenobarbital, Carbamazepine):** May increase the rate of flecainide elimination by approximately 30%.
- **Alkaline Urine:** pH > 7.0 (due to diet or medication) may slow flecainide elimination, necessitating dose reduction.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. Animal studies show teratogenic and embryotoxic effects at high doses. Flecainide should only be used in pregnancy as a drug of last resort in life-threatening arrhythmias.
- **Breastfeeding:** Flecainide is excreted in human breast milk. The benefit to the mother must be weighed against potential risks to the infant.
- **Fertility:** Effect on human labour, delivery, or long-term fertility is unknown.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children have not been established; use is not recommended. [Australian]
- **Elderly Use:** Rate of elimination may be reduced. Starting dose of 100 mg twice daily is recommended with cautious titration.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - Severe (CrCl ≤ 20 mL/min/m²): Initial dose 100 mg once daily (or 50 mg twice daily). Requires frequent plasma level monitoring.
    - Less severe: Initial dose 100 mg every 12 hours.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use only if benefits outweigh risks; monitor plasma levels closely as flecainide is extensively metabolised in the liver.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Class 1c antiarrhythmic that inhibits the fast sodium channel, slowing the upstroke of the myocardial action potential.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** 
    - Depresses conduction in all heart tissues, most notably the His-Purkinje system (H-V conduction). 
    - Minimal effect on the duration of the action potential in Purkinje fibres.
    - Possesses local anaesthetic activity.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Nearly complete and prompt; peak levels in 3 hours. Food does not affect absorption.
    - **Distribution:** Widely distributed (Vd: 5 to 13.4 L/kg); approximately 40% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Genetically determined (CYP2D6); poor metabolisers have lower clearance.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 30% excreted unchanged in urine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Cardiac Arrhythmia Suppression Trial (CAST):** A pivotal multi-centre, randomised, placebo-controlled trial. It demonstrated that flecainide increased the risk of death or non-fatal cardiac arrest in patients with asymptomatic, non-life-threatening ventricular arrhythmias following a myocardial infarction. [Australian]
