# Editorial Product Information: Fludrocortisone Acetate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial harmonises information from the following jurisdiction:
    - **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis designed for use by healthcare professionals. It provides a consolidated clinical overview of fludrocortisone acetate based on official regulatory sources.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial summary and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Fludrocortisone acetate
- **Chemical Class:** Synthetic adrenocortical steroid; potent mineralocorticoid.
- **Brand Name(s):** Florinef [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** H02AA02
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - 0.1 mg (100 micrograms) oral tablets [Australian]
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aspen Pharmacare Australia Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Primary adrenocortical insufficiency:** Partial replacement therapy in Addison's disease [Australian].
- **Salt-losing adrenogenital syndrome:** Treatment of salt-wasting forms of congenital adrenal hyperplasia [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
The dosage must be individualised based on the severity of the disease and the specific patient response.

### Dosing by Indication
- **Addison’s Disease:** 
    - The standard dose is **0.1 mg daily**. 
    - Acceptable ranges include 0.1 mg three times weekly up to 0.2 mg daily [Australian].
    - If transient hypertension occurs, the dose should be reduced to 0.05 mg daily [Australian].
    - Fludrocortisone is typically administered in conjunction with a glucocorticoid (e.g., hydrocortisone 10–30 mg daily or cortisone 10–37.5 mg daily in divided doses) to approximate physiological adrenal activity [Australian].
- **Salt-losing Adrenogenital Syndrome:** 
    - The recommended dosage is **0.1 mg to 0.2 mg daily** [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- The lowest effective dose should be utilised to manage the condition.
- When discontinuing or reducing therapy, a gradual taper is recommended where possible [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Systemic Fungal Infections:** Use is contraindicated in the presence of systemic mycotic infections [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known or suspected hypersensitivity to fludrocortisone or any excipients in the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Sodium Retention:** Due to its marked potency in promoting sodium retention, fludrocortisone should not be used for conditions other than those specifically indicated [Australian].
- **Infection Risk:** Corticosteroids may mask signs of infection and decrease resistance to new infections. Viral illnesses (e.g., chickenpox, measles) may follow a more severe or fatal course in non-immune patients [Australian].
- **Visual Disturbances:** Risk of cataracts (posterior subcapsular), glaucoma, and rare conditions such as central serous chorioretinopathy (CSCR). Patients reporting blurred vision should be referred for ophthalmological evaluation [Australian].
- **Pheochromocytoma Crisis:** Life-threatening crises have been reported following systemic corticosteroid use in patients with suspected or identified pheochromocytoma [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular/Electrolyte Effects:** High doses can cause hypertension, oedema, and potassium loss. Monitoring of blood pressure, weight, and serum electrolytes is essential during prolonged therapy [Australian].
- **Stress Response:** Patients may require increased (supportive) doses during periods of trauma, surgery, or severe illness, both during treatment and for up to one year following cessation [Australian].
- **Tuberculosis:** Use in active TB should be limited to fulminating cases and used only alongside appropriate antituberculous regimens. Latent TB requires close monitoring for reactivation [Australian].
- **Vaccination:** Smallpox vaccination is contraindicated. Other immunisation procedures should be avoided, particularly at high doses, due to the risk of neurological complications and impaired antibody response [Australian].
- **Psychiatric Effects:** Potential for euphoria, insomnia, mood swings, personality changes, severe depression, or frank psychosis [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Frequencies are not explicitly defined in available sources (e.g., >10%). Effects are generally minimal at small doses but increase with prolonged use or co-administration with other glucocorticoids.

- **Fluid and Electrolyte:** Sodium and fluid retention, hypertension, potassium loss, hypokalaemic alkalosis, congestive heart failure in susceptible patients.
- **Musculoskeletal:** Muscle weakness, steroid myopathy, loss of muscle mass, osteoporosis, vertebral compression fractures, aseptic necrosis (femoral/humeral heads), pathological long bone fractures.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Peptic ulcer (with potential perforation/haemorrhage), pancreatitis, abdominal distension, ulcerative oesophagitis.
- **Dermatological:** Impaired wound healing, thin fragile skin, bruising, petechiae, ecchymoses, facial erythema, increased sweating, subcutaneous fat atrophy, striae, hyperpigmentation, hirsutism, acne-form eruptions.
- **Neurological:** Convulsions, increased intracranial pressure with papilloedema (pseudotumour cerebri), vertigo, headache.
- **Endocrine:** Cushingoid state, menstrual irregularities, growth suppression in children, secondary adrenocortical/pituitary unresponsiveness, decreased carbohydrate tolerance, manifestation of latent diabetes.
- **Ophthalmic:** Glaucoma, cataracts, exophthalmos, blurred vision.
- **Metabolic:** Hyperglycaemia, glycosuria, negative nitrogen balance.
- **Post-Marketing:** Panniculitis (frequency not known) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Amphotericin B & Potassium-depleting Diuretics:** Enhanced risk of hypokalaemia; monitor serum potassium closely [Australian].
- **CYP3A Inhibitors (e.g., Cobicistat):** May increase systemic corticosteroid side effects; avoid combination unless benefits outweigh risks [Australian].
- **Anticoagulants (Oral):** Corticosteroids may increase or decrease anticoagulant activity; monitor INR/prothrombin time [Australian].
- **Antidiabetics:** Diminished hypoglycaemic effect; dose adjustments for insulin or oral agents may be required [Australian].
- **Hepatic Enzyme Inducers (e.g., Phenytoin, Rifampicin, Carbamazepine, Barbiturates):** Increased clearance of fludrocortisone; dose increases may be necessary [Australian].
- **Digitalis Glycosides:** Increased risk of arrhythmias or digitalis toxicity associated with hypokalaemia [Australian].
- **Estrogens/Oral Contraceptives:** May increase corticosteroid half-life and concentration; dose reduction may be needed [Australian].
- **NSAIDs/Aspirin:** Increased ulcerogenic effect. Use aspirin cautiously in patients with hypoprothrombinaemia [Australian].
- **Cyclosporin:** Potential for increased activity of both drugs when used concurrently [Australian].
- **Thyroid Drugs:** Clearance is decreased in hypothyroidism and increased in hyperthyroidism [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category C** [Australian]: Corticosteroids have caused malformations (cleft palate) in animal studies, though this is not clearly established in humans. Long-term use may cause reduced placental/birth weight or adrenal suppression in the neonate. Use only if benefits to the mother outweigh risks to the foetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Use with caution; weigh the potential benefits to the mother against potential hazards to the child [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No specific human data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Growth and development must be closely observed during prolonged therapy [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of adverse effects, specifically hypertension and osteoporosis. Close clinical supervision is recommended [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Enhanced corticosteroid effects may occur in patients with cirrhosis [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A synthetic adrenocortical steroid with extremely potent mineralocorticoid activity and high glucocorticoid activity. It acts primarily on the distal tubule of the kidney to increase sodium reabsorption and promote potassium excretion [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Small doses produce marked sodium retention and increased urinary potassium excretion, leading to a rise in blood pressure [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic; physiological action is similar to hydrocortisone but significantly more prolonged.
    - **Half-life:** 18–36 hours [Australian].
    - **Excretion:** Eliminated by the kidneys, mostly as inactive metabolites.
    - **Duration of Action:** 1 to 2 days [Australian].
    - **Protein Binding:** Highly protein bound [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No specific pivotal trial data was detailed in the source documentation [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Primary Storage:** Store refrigerated between **2°C and 8°C** [Australian].
- **Dispensing Allowance:** Once dispensed, the product may be stored below **25°C for a maximum of 3 months**. The dispensing pharmacist must record the "Start date (below 25°C)" and "Expiry date (below 25°C)" on the label. Any tablets kept out of the refrigerator beyond this 3-month period must be discarded [Australian].
- **Container:** Amber glass bottle with a polypropylene cap and induction seal [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
