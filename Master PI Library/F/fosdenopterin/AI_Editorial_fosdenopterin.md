# Editorial Product Information: Fosdenopterin

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived solely from the Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **fosdenopterin** intended for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises available regulatory data into a single clinical reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official, full Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labeling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Fosdenopterin (as hydrobromide dihydrate).
- **Chemical Class:** Cyclic pyranopterin monophosphate (cPMP).
- **Brand Name:** **NULIBRY** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Lyophilized powder or cake for injection in a single-dose vial containing 9.5 mg of **fosdenopterin** (equivalent to 12.5 mg of fosdenopterin hydrobromide dihydrate) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sentynl Therapeutics, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Molybdenum Cofactor Deficiency (MoCD) Type A:** Indicated to reduce the risk of mortality in patients with MoCD Type A [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 General Dosing
**Fosdenopterin** therapy should be initiated if a diagnosis of MoCD Type A is known or presumed. If the diagnosis is not confirmed by genetic testing, treatment should be discontinued promptly [US].

### 3.2 Dosing by Age and Gestation
The dosage is based on actual body weight. For patients less than one year of age, a titration schedule based on gestational age is required [US].

**Table 1: Dosage Regimen for Patients < 1 Year (by Gestational Age) [US]**
| Titration Duration | Preterm Neonates (<37 Weeks) | Term Neonates (≥37 Weeks) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Initial Dosage** | 0.4 mg/kg once daily | 0.55 mg/kg once daily |
| **Month 1 and 2** | 0.7 mg/kg once daily | 0.75 mg/kg once daily |
| **Month 3 to 12** | 0.9 mg/kg once daily | 0.9 mg/kg once daily |

**Patients One Year of Age or Older:**
- Recommended dosage: 0.9 mg/kg administered intravenously once daily [US].

### 3.3 Preparation and Administration
- **Reconstitution:** Reconstitute each 9.5 mg vial with 5 mL of Sterile Water for Injection, USP. Swirl gently; do not shake. The resulting concentration is 1.9 mg/mL [US].
- **Inspection:** The solution should be clear and colorless to pale yellow. Discard if particulate matter or discoloration is present [US].
- **Infusion:** Administer via infusion pump at a rate of 1.5 mL/minute using non-DEHP tubing and a 0.2-micron filter [US].
- **Slow IV Push:** For volumes less than 2 mL, administration by slow intravenous push over 1 to 2 minutes may be considered [US].
- **Compatibility:** Do not mix with other drugs or administer as an infusion with other drugs [US].

### 3.4 Missed Dose
Administer a missed dose as soon as possible. The subsequent scheduled dose must be administered at least 6 hours after the completion of the missed dose [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None listed in available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Potential for Photosensitivity:** Animal studies indicate phototoxic potential. Healthcare providers should advise patients/caregivers to avoid exposure to direct sunlight and artificial UV light (e.g., UVA/UVB phototherapy). 
- **Protective Measures:** Patients should wear protective clothing, hats, and sunglasses. For patients 6 months and older, broad-spectrum sunscreen with high SPF should be used.
- **Monitoring:** If photosensitivity occurs, immediate medical attention and dermatological evaluation are recommended [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### 6.1 Clinical Trial Experience
The following adverse reactions were reported in pediatric patients with MoCD Type A treated with **fosdenopterin** or recombinant cPMP (rcPMP).

**Very Common (>10%) [US]:**
- Catheter-related complications (89%) (includes infection, site abscess, extravasation, and occlusion)
- Pyrexia (78%)
- Viral infection (56%)
- Pneumonia (44%)
- Otitis media (44%)
- Vomiting (44%)
- Cough/Sneezing (44%)
- Viral upper respiratory infection (33%)
- Gastroenteritis (33%)
- Diarrhea (33%)
- Bacteremia (33%)
- Abdominal pain (22%)
- Influenza (22%)
- Lower respiratory tract infection (22%)
- Viral tonsillitis (22%)
- Oropharyngeal pain (22%)
- Rash maculo-papular (22%)
- Anemia (22%)
- Eye swelling (22%)
- Seizure (22%)
- Agitation (22%)

### 6.2 Serious Adverse Effects
- **Necrotizing enterocolitis:** One death was reported in an observational study of rcPMP [US].
- **Sepsis:** Reported in more than one patient in observational studies [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### 7.1 Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Cytochrome P450 (CYP) Enzymes:** **Fosdenopterin** does not inhibit CYP1A2, 2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, or 3A4/5. It does not induce CYP1A2, 2B6, or 3A4 [US].
- **Transporter Systems:** 
    - **Fosdenopterin** is a weak inhibitor of MATE2-K and OAT1.
    - It is a weak substrate for MATE1.
    - It does not inhibit P-gp, BCRP, OATP1B1, OATP1B3, OCT2, OAT3, or MATE1 [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified (Pregnancy and Lactation Labeling Rule [PLLR] format used). There are no available human data. Animal studies in rabbits (IV) and mice (SC) at doses up to 89 and 32 times the human exposure, respectively, showed no adverse developmental effects [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on the presence of **fosdenopterin** in human milk or its effects on the breastfed infant. The clinical need for the mother should be weighed against potential risks to the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in mice showed no adverse effects on fertility or reproductive performance at doses 18 times the human exposure [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness established from birth [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Not studied, as MoCD Type A is primarily a pediatric disease [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** The effect of renal impairment on pharmacokinetics is unknown [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** The effect of hepatic impairment on pharmacokinetics is unknown [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
### 10.1 Mechanism of Action
Patients with MoCD Type A have mutations in the *MOCS1* gene, resulting in a deficiency of cPMP. **Fosdenopterin** provides an exogenous source of cPMP, which is converted to molybdopterin and then to molybdenum cofactor. This cofactor is essential for activating molybdenum-dependent enzymes, including sulfite oxidase (SOX), which detoxifies neurotoxic sulfites [US].

### 10.2 Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Administered intravenously; Cmax and AUC increase proportionally with dose [US].
- **Distribution:** Volume of distribution (Vd) is approximately 300 mL/kg. Plasma protein binding is low (6% to 12%) [US].
- **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized via non-enzymatic degradation to Compound Z (an inactive oxidation product) [US].
- **Elimination:** Mean half-life is 1.2 to 1.7 hours. Total body clearance is 167 to 195 mL/h/kg. Renal clearance accounts for approximately 40% of total clearance [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
Efficacy was established based on 13 treated patients with MoCD Type A compared to a genotype-matched historical control group (n=18).
- **Survival Outcome:** Treated patients (NULIBRY or rcPMP) showed a significant improvement in overall survival. The hazard ratio for the risk of death was 0.18 (95% CI: 0.04, 0.72) compared to the historical control [US].
- **Biomarkers:** Treatment resulted in a sustained reduction in urinary S-sulfocysteine (SSC) levels normalized to creatinine [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Frozen Vials:** Store between -25°C and -10°C (-13°F and 14°F) in the original carton to protect from light [US].
- **Reconstituted Solution:** If not used immediately, store at room temperature (15°C to 25°C) or refrigerate (2°C to 8°C) for up to 4 hours, including infusion time. Do not freeze or shake [US].
