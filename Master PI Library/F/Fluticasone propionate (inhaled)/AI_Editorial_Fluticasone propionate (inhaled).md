# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine (Intravenous)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official, legally approved Product Information in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved labeling before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Levocarnitine).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Severe Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest administered dose reported is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Titration:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations.
- **Dose Reduction:** Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on plasma levels [US].

### Monitoring and Administration Instructions
- **Baseline Monitoring:** Obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to starting therapy. 
- **Routine Monitoring:** Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Visual Inspection:** Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration. Discard any unused portion as the product contains no preservatives.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** While the source states "None known" in the dedicated section, subsequent warnings highlight serious hypersensitivity reactions as a clinical risk [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, often within minutes of administration. These are reported primarily in patients with ESRD on dialysis. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been reported [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral vs. IV):** The safety of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. High doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **General:** Transient nausea and vomiting are common. Body odor and gastritis are reported less frequently.
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin anticoagulation [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned under current format, but animal data provided]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk. The risks to the child from excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, Saccharomyces, Schizosaccharomyces) were negative. Long-term animal carcinogenicity studies have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders (50 mg/kg) applies to paediatric patients; see Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD patients undergoing dialysis. Monitor for metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO) if considering oral formulations in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** Clinical trials demonstrated that 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly after dialysis significantly increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Trough levels typically stabilized between 54 and 243 µmol/L (Normal range: 40–50 µmol/L).
- **Note:** While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations is proven, the impact on specific clinical outcomes or signs/symptoms of carnitine deficiency in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature (25°C) when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL [US].
