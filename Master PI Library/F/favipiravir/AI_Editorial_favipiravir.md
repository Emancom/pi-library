# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory information into a clinically relevant format.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic management of patients with metabolic defects resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **Carnitine Deficiency in End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance haemodialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It should be administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **ESRD on Haemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].

### Dosing by Indication
- **Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered in severe cases, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **ESRD Maintenance:** Administer as a slow bolus (2–3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Monitoring and Titration
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Monitor plasma carnitine concentrations weekly or monthly. Target free carnitine levels are between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **ESRD:** Dose adjustments should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. If levels are below 40–50 µmol/L, initiation or adjustment is warranted. Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be performed as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on clinical response [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect parenteral products for particulate matter or discoloration before use.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal oedema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, particularly in ESRD patients. Some reactions manifest within minutes of administration. In the event of a severe reaction, discontinue treatment immediately and assess the risks before considering re-administration [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Monitoring:** In ESRD patients, chronic administration of high oral doses (not typically applicable to this IV formulation) may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5% Frequency in ESRD Trials)
Based on placebo-controlled trials in haemodialysis patients, the following effects were reported regardless of causality:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), chest pain (12%), flu syndrome (25%), headache (22%), infection (15%), abdominal pain (9%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhoea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), anorexia (5%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcaemia (9%), hyperkalaemia (6%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%).
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), paresthesia (5%).

### Other Reported Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; gastritis; body odour (infrequent).
- **Neurologic:** Seizures.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalised Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels upon initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine in patients on anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits using doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration. Clinicians must weigh the benefits to the mother against the potential risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant; discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing in paediatric patients follows the mg/kg protocols established for adults in metabolic and ESRD settings [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific data not detailed; generally, dose selection should be cautious, reflecting the greater frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for ESRD patients. The safety of long-term high-dose *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function is not established due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism, it promotes the excretion of excess organic acids and clears acyl-CoA compounds by forming acylcarnitine, which is rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in faeces).
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (n=12) receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis, mean trough levocarnitine concentrations increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) when mixed in PVC bags with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8 mg/mL [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
