# Editorial Product Information: Fremanezumab

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory specifics into a single reference.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for final prescribing authority.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Fremanezumab (a fully humanised IgG2Δa/kappa monoclonal antibody).
- **Chemical Class:** Calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) antagonist.
- **Brand Name:** **Ajovy** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N02CD03.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):**
    - Solution for injection in a single-dose autoinjector: 225 mg in 1.5 mL (150 mg/mL) [Australian].
    - Solution for injection in a single-dose pre-filled syringe: 225 mg in 1.5 mL (150 mg/mL) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Subcutaneous injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Teva Pharma Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Migraine Prophylaxis:** Indicated for the preventive treatment of migraine in adult patients [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** Treatment should be initiated by a clinician experienced in migraine management. Two subcutaneous dosing options are available:
    - **Monthly:** 225 mg administered once every month.
    - **Quarterly:** 675 mg administered every three months (given as three consecutive injections of 225 mg).
- **Switching Regimens:** When transitioning between monthly and quarterly dosing, the first dose of the new schedule should be administered on the next scheduled date of the original regimen [Australian].
- **Assessment of Benefit:** Clinical benefit should be evaluated within 8 to 12 weeks of starting therapy. The necessity for ongoing treatment should be reassessed regularly thereafter [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Inject into the abdomen, thigh, or upper arm. Avoid areas that are red, bruised, indurated, or tender.
    - If multiple injections are required (for the 675 mg dose), use different injection sites.
    - Prior to use, allow the device to reach room temperature for 30 minutes. Do not shake or freeze [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, administer it as soon as possible. Resume the regular schedule from that point; do not double the dose to compensate for a missed one [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known clinically significant hypersensitivity to the active substance (fremanezumab) or any of the excipients (histidine, sucrose, disodium edetate, polysorbate 80) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Reactions such as rash, urticaria, and pruritus have been reported (typically <1% of trial participants). While most occur within 24 hours, some reactions may be delayed. **Anaphylaxis** has been reported rarely. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue the drug and initiate appropriate therapy [Australian].
- **Hypertension:** New-onset or worsening of pre-existing hypertension has been reported with CGRP antagonists. Monitoring of blood pressure is required. Hypertension may occur at any time but is most frequently reported within the first 7 days of initiation [Australian].
- **Major Cardiovascular Disease:** Safety has not been established in patients with significant cardiovascular disease, as these individuals were excluded from clinical trials [Australian].
- **Traceability:** To ensure the traceability of biological products, the specific name and batch number of the administered product must be clearly recorded [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Injection Site Reactions:** These are the most frequent adverse events and are typically mild to moderate.
- **Immunogenicity:** Anti-drug antibodies (ADA) were detected in 0.4% of patients in short-term trials and up to 2.3% in long-term studies. Neutralising antibodies were rare (<1%). ADA development did not appear to impact safety or efficacy [Australian].

- **Post-marketing Reports:** Anaphylactic reactions (rare), constipation, fatigue, and hypertension [Australian].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** One case of Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS) was reported post-marketing in a patient taking multiple medications, including lamotrigine [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP450 Interactions:** As a monoclonal antibody, fremanezumab is not metabolised by cytochrome P450 enzymes. Therefore, pharmacokinetic interactions with small-molecule drugs are not expected [Australian].
- **Concomitant Migraine Therapy:** Use with acute migraine medications (triptans, ergots, analgesics) or other preventive treatments did not influence the exposure of fremanezumab in population pharmacokinetic analyses [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B1.**
    - Data in pregnant women are limited. Animal studies (rats/rabbits) showed no evidence of reproductive toxicity or harm to the fetus. 
    - Fremanezumab is known to cross the placenta, with higher fetal exposure expected during the second and third trimesters. Due to the long half-life (31 days), this should be considered for women planning pregnancy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if fremanezumab is excreted in human milk. A risk to the infant cannot be excluded. The decision to continue breastfeeding or therapy should weigh the developmental health benefits of breastfeeding against the mother’s clinical need for the drug [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Human data are unavailable. Animal studies showed no impact on male or female fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children and adolescents under 18 years of age have not been established. No data are available [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Limited data are available for patients ≥65 years. Population pharmacokinetic analysis suggests no dose adjustment is required [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required. Monoclonal antibodies are not eliminated via renal pathways [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required. Fremanezumab is not metabolised in the liver [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Fremanezumab binds selectively to the CGRP ligand, preventing its binding to the CGRP receptor. This modulates the trigeminal system to prevent migraine attacks, though the exact mechanism remains fully elucidated [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The drug is highly specific for CGRP and does not bind to related molecules such as amylin or calcitonin [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Subcutaneous bioavailability is approximately 55%. Median $T_{max}$ is 5 to 7 days.
    - **Distribution:** Apparent volume of distribution is approximately 6 L, indicating minimal extravascular distribution.
    - **Metabolism:** Degraded by enzymatic proteolysis into peptides and amino acids.
    - **Elimination:** Estimated half-life is 31 days. Clearance is approximately 0.141 L/day. Steady state is reached in roughly 168 days (6 months) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Episodic Migraine (Study 1):** In a 12-week study of 875 patients, both monthly and quarterly dosing significantly reduced the monthly average number of migraine days compared to placebo. Approximately 44–48% of patients achieved a ≥50% reduction in migraine days [Australian].
- **Chronic Migraine (Study 2):** In a 12-week study of 1,130 patients with ≥15 headache days per month, fremanezumab significantly reduced moderate-to-severe headache days compared to placebo. Approximately 38–41% achieved a ≥50% reduction in headache days [Australian].
- **Difficult-to-Treat Migraine (Study 4):** Efficacy was demonstrated in 838 patients who had failed 2 to 4 classes of prior preventive treatments, showing a significant reduction in migraine days compared to placebo from the first month [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store in a refrigerator between 2°C and 8°C. Do not freeze [Australian].
- **Light:** Keep in the original carton to protect from light [Australian].
- **Excursions:** May be stored unrefrigerated for up to 7 days at temperatures up to 30°C. If not used within 7 days of removal from the refrigerator, the product must be discarded [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Restricted to prescription use by a medical practitioner [Australian].
