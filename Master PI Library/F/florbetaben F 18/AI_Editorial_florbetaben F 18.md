# Editorial Product Information: Florbetaben F 18

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial product information has been synthesized from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **United States (FDA)**. 
- **Note:** At the time of this editorial synthesis, only the United States (FDA) documentation was available for review. Consequently, regional variations for other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian, Japanese) are not specified.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a clinical synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Florbetaben F 18 (radioactive diagnostic agent).
- **Chemical Class:** Stilbene derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** **NEURACEQ** [US].
- **ATC Code:** V09AX06.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Clear, sterile, non-pyrogenic solution for injection in a multiple-dose vial. Strength ranges from 50 MBq/mL to 5,000 MBq/mL (1.4 mCi/mL to 135 mCi/mL) at the end of synthesis (EOS) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Lantheus Biosciences Ltd. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
**NEURACEQ** is indicated for Positron Emission Tomography (PET) imaging of the brain to estimate amyloid beta neuritic plaque density in adult patients with cognitive impairment who are being evaluated for:
- Alzheimer’s disease (AD) and other causes of cognitive decline [US].
- Selection for amyloid beta-directed therapy, as defined by the prescribing information of specific therapeutic products [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Radioactivity Dose:** 300 MBq (8.1 mCi) [US].
- **Volume:** Up to 10 mL administered as a slow intravenous bolus (6 seconds/mL) [US].
- **Maximum Mass Dose:** 30 micrograms [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Use aseptic technique and appropriate radiation shielding.
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration; do not use if the solution is not clear or contains particles.
- **NEURACEQ** must not be diluted.
- Follow the bolus with an intravenous flush of approximately 10 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride injection to ensure full delivery of the dose.
- Verify catheter patency with a saline flush prior to administration.

### Image Acquisition
- **Timing:** Acquire PET images starting 45 minutes to 130 minutes after drug administration.
- **Duration:** 15 to 20 minutes of imaging time.
- **Positioning:** Patient should be supine with the head centered in the scanner field of view.

## 4. Contraindications
- None specified in available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Risk of Image Misinterpretation:** Interpretation errors (false positives/negatives) may occur. Visual assessment of **NEURACEQ** scans should be performed independently of clinical features. Errors are more likely in cases of severe brain atrophy or motion artifacts.
- **Reader Training:** Images should only be interpreted by readers who have successfully completed specific training provided by the manufacturer.
- **Radiation Risk:** As a radiopharmaceutical, **NEURACEQ** contributes to a patient’s long-term cumulative radiation exposure, which is associated with an increased risk of cancer. 
- **Hydration:** Instruct patients to hydrate significantly before and after administration and to void frequently to reduce radiation exposure to the bladder.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The safety of **NEURACEQ** was evaluated in 872 subjects (1,090 total administrations). Adverse reactions are generally mild and localized to the injection site.

| Adverse Reaction | Frequency (N=1,090) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Injection site pain | 3.4% |
| Injection site erythema | 1.7% |
| Injection site irritation | 1.1% |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** None highlighted in the provided clinical trial data [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** *In vitro* studies using human liver microsomes indicate that florbetaben does not inhibit cytochrome P450 enzymes at clinically relevant concentrations.
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Management Notes:** No clinically significant drug-drug interactions have been identified in provided sources [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned (narrative summary used). There are no data on use in pregnant women. Radiopharmaceuticals have the potential to cause fetal harm. Clinicians should consider the radiation dose and gestational timing if administration is necessary.
- **Breastfeeding:** 
    - It is unknown if florbetaben F 18 is excreted in human milk. To minimize radiation exposure to a breastfed infant, lactating women should pump and discard breast milk for **24 hours** after administration [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies have not been performed to evaluate the impact on male or female fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established in paediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** In clinical studies, 69% of subjects were ≥65 years and 35% were ≥75 years. No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed between elderly and younger adult subjects.
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Florbetaben F 18 binds to amyloid beta plaques in the brain. The F 18 isotope emits positrons that are detected by PET. It does not bind to tau or α-synuclein.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The drug crosses the blood-brain barrier and shows differential retention in regions containing amyloid beta deposits.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Approximately 6% of injected radioactivity reaches the brain within 10 minutes.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily catalyzed by CYP2J2 and CYP4F2.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily hepatobiliary. Approximately 30% of radioactivity is excreted in urine (mostly as polar metabolites) within 12 hours.
    - **Half-life:** Mean biological half-life is approximately 1 hour. The physical half-life of F 18 is 109.8 minutes.
    - **Protein Binding:** 98.5%.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** The efficacy was established in two single-arm studies comparing premortem PET scans to postmortem histopathology (the "truth standard").
- **Performance:** In subjects who underwent autopsy (n=82), the sensitivity was 96.2% and specificity was 80.0% for detecting moderate-to-frequent amyloid plaques using in-person trained readers.
- **Reproducibility:** Inter-reader agreement was high, with a Fleiss kappa statistic of 0.80 across 454 subjects.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F). Excursions are permitted between 2°C and 42°C (36°F to 108°F) [US].
- **Shelf Life:** The multiple-dose vial expires **10 hours** after the end of synthesis (EOS). 
- **Handling:** Store in the original container within radiation shielding. Dispose of unused product as radioactive waste in accordance with local regulations.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** 
    - **United States:** Rx-only; Radioactive Material.
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** Not specified in available sources (handled under nuclear regulatory licenses).
