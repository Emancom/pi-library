**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document for Recombinant Coagulation Factor IX (Nonacog alfa) has been synthesized from official regulatory documents provided by the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) for BeneFIX and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for RIXUBIS. While both products contain recombinant factor IX (rFIX), healthcare professionals should note that specific formulations, stabilization excipients, and pharmacokinetic recovery values may vary slightly between brands.

# Product Information: Coagulation Factor IX (Recombinant)

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Active Ingredient:** Nonacog alfa (Recombinant Coagulation Factor IX).

Coagulation Factor IX (Recombinant) is a purified protein produced via recombinant DNA technology. The product consists of a single-chain glycoprotein with a primary amino acid sequence identical to the allelic form of plasma-derived Factor IX. It is produced in genetically engineered Chinese Hamster Ovary (CHO) cell lines and is inherently free from the risk of transmission of human blood-borne pathogens.

## 2. Approved Indications
Recombinant Factor IX is indicated for use in adults and children with haemophilia B (congenital Factor IX deficiency or Christmas disease) for:
*   **Control and prevention of bleeding episodes:** On-demand treatment and control of haemorrhagic events.
*   **Perioperative management:** Prevention and control of bleeding in surgical settings.
*   **Routine prophylaxis:** Regular administration to reduce the frequency of spontaneous bleeding episodes [FDA].

**Note:** This product is not indicated for the treatment of other factor deficiencies (e.g., Factors II, VII, or X), nor for the reversal of coumarin-induced anticoagulation or the treatment of liver-dependent coagulation factor deficiencies [TGA]. It is not indicated for the induction of immune tolerance in patients with haemophilia B [FDA].

## 3. Contraindications
Administration is contraindicated in patients with:
*   Known hypersensitivity to the active substance, any excipients, or hamster-derived proteins.
*   Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC) [FDA].
*   Signs of active fibrinolysis [FDA].

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Hypersensitivity Reactions
Allergic-type hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis, have been reported. Initial administrations (approximately the first 10–20 exposures) should be performed under medical supervision where emergency care is available. Symptoms may include hives, angioedema, chest tightness, hypotension, and dyspnoea. If such reactions occur, discontinue administration immediately. Patients may also develop hypersensitivity to trace amounts of CHO proteins present in the product.

### Inhibitor Formation
Patients should be monitored for the development of Factor IX neutralizing antibodies (inhibitors). If the expected plasma Factor IX activity levels are not attained or if bleeding is not controlled with an appropriate dose, an assay should be performed to determine inhibitor concentration (measured in Bethesda Units). There is a reported correlation between the presence of major deletion mutations in the Factor IX gene and an increased risk of inhibitor formation and acute hypersensitivity.

### Thromboembolic Risks
Although recombinant Factor IX contains only Factor IX, the use of Factor IX-containing products has been historically associated with thromboembolic complications. Caution is advised when administering the product to patients with liver disease, post-operative patients, neonates, or patients at risk of DIC or thromboembolic phenomena. 

### Nephrotic Syndrome
Cases of nephrotic syndrome have been reported following attempted immune tolerance induction with Factor IX products in patients with Factor IX inhibitors and a history of allergic reactions.

### Agglutination of Red Blood Cells
Reports have indicated the agglutination of red blood cells in the tubing or syringe during administration. To minimize this risk, limit the amount of blood entering the tubing and do not allow blood to enter the syringe [TGA].

## 5. Dosage and Administration
### General Principles
Treatment must be individualized and initiated under the supervision of a physician experienced in haemophilia B management. Dosage depends on the severity of the deficiency, the location/extent of the bleed, and the patient’s clinical condition and Factor IX recovery.

### Calculation of Dose
The potency is expressed in International Units (IU). One IU is equivalent to the amount of Factor IX activity in 1 mL of normal pooled human plasma. 
The calculation of the required dose should be based on the empirical finding that:
*   **Adults (≥12–15 years):** 1 IU/kg increases circulating Factor IX by approximately 0.8–0.9 IU/dL.
*   **Children (<12 years):** 1 IU/kg increases circulating Factor IX by approximately 0.7 IU/dL [FDA/TGA].

**Required Units (IU) = Body Weight (kg) × Desired Factor IX Increase (% or IU/dL) × Reciprocal of Observed Recovery.**

### Dosing Guidelines
*   **Minor Bleeds (e.g., uncomplicated joint/soft tissue):** Maintain Factor IX levels at 20–30% for 1–2 days.
*   **Moderate Bleeds (e.g., muscle/haematuria):** Maintain levels at 25–50% until healing is achieved (approx. 2–7 days).
*   **Major Bleeds/Surgery (e.g., CNS, pharynx, major trauma):** Maintain levels at 50–100% for 7–10 days.
*   **Routine Prophylaxis:** 
    *   **Adults/Adolescents:** 40–60 IU/kg twice weekly [FDA].
    *   **Children (<12 years):** 60–80 IU/kg twice weekly [FDA].

### Administration
Administer via intravenous bolus infusion over several minutes after reconstitution. The maximum infusion rate should not exceed 10 mL/minute [FDA]. Recombinant Factor IX should not be administered by continuous infusion as safety and efficacy for this method have not been established.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Common adverse reactions (frequency >1%) include:
*   Dysgeusia (altered taste).
*   Pain in extremities.
*   Positive test for furin antibodies [FDA].
*   Injection site reactions (burning, erythema).

Rare or post-marketing reactions include:
*   Anaphylaxis and hypersensitivity.
*   Nausea, vomiting, and diarrhoea.
*   Dizziness, headache, and lethargy.
*   Inadequate Factor IX recovery.
*   Thromboembolic events (e.g., DVT, pulmonary embolism).
*   Renal infarct [TGA].

## 7. Drug Interactions
No formal drug interaction studies have been conducted. There are no known interactions with other medicinal products. Reconstituted product must not be mixed with other medicinal products or infused in the same tubing.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy (Category B2)
Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. It is unknown whether recombinant Factor IX can cause foetal harm or affect reproduction capacity. Use during pregnancy only if clearly indicated.

### Breastfeeding
It is unknown whether the protein is excreted in human milk. A decision should be made whether to discontinue breastfeeding or the drug, taking into account the benefit of the drug to the mother and the benefit of breastfeeding to the infant.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Half-life:** The mean terminal half-life is approximately 22 to 26 hours.
*   **Recovery:** Recovery is generally lower for recombinant Factor IX compared to plasma-derived products.
*   **Pediatrics:** Clearance is higher in children (especially those <6 years) compared to adults, necessitating dose adjustments and potentially more frequent dosing in younger populations.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Factor IX is a vitamin K-dependent coagulation factor. It is activated by the Factor VII/tissue factor complex (extrinsic pathway) or Factor XIa (intrinsic pathway). Activated Factor IX (IXa), in combination with activated Factor VIII, activates Factor X. This sequence ultimately converts prothrombin to thrombin, which transforms fibrinogen into fibrin to form a stable blood clot.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Unopened Vials:** Store under refrigeration at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze. 
*   **Room Temperature Excursion:** Vials may be stored at room temperature (up to 30°C) for a single period of up to 6 months [TGA] or up to the expiration date (36 months) [FDA/RIXUBIS specific]. Patients should record the date the product is moved to room temperature.
*   **Reconstituted Solution:** The product does not contain preservatives. To reduce microbiological hazard, use as soon as possible or within 3 hours of reconstitution. Store at room temperature (up to 30°C); do not refrigerate after reconstitution.
