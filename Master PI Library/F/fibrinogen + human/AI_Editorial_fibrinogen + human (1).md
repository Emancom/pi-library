## 3. Approved Indications
- Adjunct to hemostasis in adult and pediatric patients undergoing surgery when control of bleeding by conventional surgical techniques (suture, ligature, cautery) is ineffective or impractical. Effective in heparinized patients.
- Adjunct to standard surgical techniques to prevent leakage from colonic anastomoses following reversal of temporary colostomies in adult and pediatric patients.
- Use outside these indications or in other surgical settings: Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General dosing (adult)
- Dosage is based on the surface area to be covered. Ensure sufficient product is applied to entirely cover the intended area.
- Use as a thin layer; excessive thickness may interfere with wound healing.
- Typical applied volumes observed in clinical studies: mean 5.3 mL (range 0.2–20.0 mL). (Clinical-trial exposure data.)

### Dosing by indication
- Hemostasis (surgical adjunct): Apply sufficient volume to cover bleeding surface; hemostasis should be assessed clinically after application.
- Sealing colonic anastomoses (adjunct): Apply thin layer to the anastomotic site as adjunct to standard technique.

### Preparation and reconstitution (clinical/practical points)
- Reconstitute and prepare the two component solutions (Sealer Protein solution containing aprotinin and Thrombin solution) using aseptic technique and separate syringes/cannulas to avoid premature clotting.
- Reconstituted components should be visually checked and should be clear or slightly opalescent; do not use if cloudy, discolored, or containing deposits.
- After reconstitution or thawing/warming for clinical use, product must be used within the time limit specified in the handling instructions (use within 4 hours after warming/once opened). Exact handling times and device-specific instructions are part of manufacturer instructions and must be followed.

### Method of application
- For topical use only. Do not inject intravascularly or into highly vascular tissue (e.g., nasal mucosa).
- Apply as a thin layer by dripping or by approved cannula or spray device cleared for use with the product.
- Dry the application site as much as possible before application (standard techniques). Do not use pressurized gas/air to dry the site.
- Allow at least ~2 minutes after application for polymerization; if repeat application is required, dry the site as much as possible and remove residues before reapplication (reapply before polymerization of prior layer).
- Expel any initial drops when very small volumes are required to ensure delivery of adequately mixed material.
- Vials/pre-filled syringes are for single-patient use only; discard unused product. Record product name and batch number in patient record to maintain traceability.

### Spray application parameters (device-dependent)
- Use only spray catheters/applicators approved for use with the product.
- Use only the recommended gas and regulator, within the recommended pressure ranges and application distances supplied with the delivery device to reduce risk of gas embolism.
- General recommended device/gas/pressure/distance guidance (device-specific instructions must be followed precisely):
  - Open surgery: use medical-grade CO2 (preferred), compressed air or nitrogen; recommended distance about 10–15 cm; spray pressure approximately 1.5–2.0 bar (21.8–29.0 psi).
  - Minimally invasive/laparoscopic procedures: use CO2 only with a designated MIS spray device; recommended distances vary by applicator (examples: 2–5 cm for some MIS applicators; specific applicator guidance should be followed); suggested pressures in the range of ~1.18–1.50 bar (17–22 psi) for certain MIS applicators.
- Do not spray where the minimum recommended distance from applicator tip to tissue cannot be assured.

### Titration and missed doses
- Not applicable for a single-procedure topical haemostat/sealant; no titration schedule or missed-dose guidance specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Intravascular application: Do not inject **fibrinogen + human** directly into the circulatory system or highly vascularized tissue (e.g., nasal mucosa).
- Known hypersensitivity to aprotinin (component of the product): Do not use in individuals with a known hypersensitivity to aprotinin.
- Severe or brisk arterial or venous bleeding: Do not use for control of severe brisk bleeding (product will be washed away by high-flow bleeding).
- Application where minimum recommended spray distance cannot be assured: Do not spray when minimum distance cannot be maintained.
- Any other contraindications: Not specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed / prominent warnings
- **Hypersensitivity / anaphylaxis**: Aprotinin in the product is associated with hypersensitivity reactions, including life-threatening anaphylactic reactions. Patients with prior exposure to systemic aprotinin or repeated applications of the product are at increased risk.
- **Air or gas embolism**: Life‑threatening or fatal air/gas embolism, tissue rupture, or gas entrapment with compression have occurred when fibrin sealants were administered using pressurized gas with open-regulator spray devices at higher-than-recommended pressures or too close to tissue.

### General warnings and precautions
- Monitor for hypersensitivity signs (bradycardia, tachycardia, hypotension, flushing, bronchospasm, wheeze, dyspnea, nausea, urticaria, angioedema, pruritus, erythema, paresthesia). Discontinue use and manage clinically if hypersensitivity occurs; remove remaining product from site.
- To reduce risk of gas embolism: use only recommended regulator/gas, device, pressure range and applicator distance; use devices designed for enclosed body cavities for MIS procedures (with venting features to prevent overpressurization).
- Do not use in enclosed body areas with open-spray devices not designed for that purpose.
- Product components (Sealer Protein and Thrombin solutions) may be denatured by alcohol, iodine, or heavy metal ions; if such substances were used to clean the wound, rinse thoroughly before application.
- When used during surgery, avoid intravascular application; intravascular application may cause intravascular coagulation and thromboembolic events.
- The product is manufactured from pooled human plasma and may carry a risk of transmitting infectious agents (including viruses and agents of variant Creutzfeldt–Jakob disease). Report any suspected infections potentially transmitted by the product to the relevant authorities/manufacturer.
- Safety and effectiveness have not been evaluated in neurosurgical procedures or other surgeries involving confined spaces; use in these settings is not evaluated/approved.
- Document use of the product and batch number in patient records.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor vital signs during and after application: blood pressure, heart rate, oxygen saturation, and end-tidal CO2, particularly when spray application is used (risk of gas embolism).
- Monitor for signs of hypersensitivity during and after administration.
- Consider monitoring coagulation markers as clinically indicated; increased D‑dimer levels were observed in clinical experience (clinical significance to be interpreted in context).

### Specific clinical precautions
- Avoid application of excessive thickness of fibrin layer.
- Use separate syringes and cannulas for preparation of the two solutions to prevent premature clotting.
- Discard any solutions that are cloudy, discolored, or contain deposits.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Oxidized cellulose-containing preparations (e.g., certain topical carrier materials) can reduce efficacy of **fibrinogen + human** and should not be used as carrier materials.
- Solutions containing alcohol, iodine, or heavy metal ions can denature the sealer protein and/or thrombin components and reduce product activity — rinse wound areas thoroughly if such substances have been used.
- Prior systemic exposure to aprotinin increases risk of hypersensitivity to this product.
- No pharmacokinetic interaction data (systemic) provided in available sources because product is used topically and systemic exposure is limited/not specified.
- Other clinically significant interactions: Not specified in available sources.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Summary exposure: Data reflect use of the product in five clinical trials (Study 1–Study 5) with a total of 452 patients receiving the product as an adjunct to hemostasis or as a sealant adjunct in colonic anastomoses.
- Mean volume applied in trials: 5.3 mL (range 0.2–20.0 mL).
- Follow-up durations varied by study:
  - Study 1: 30 days follow-up.
  - Study 2 and Study 3: 6 months follow-up.
  - Study 4: up to 167 days follow-up.
  - Study 5: up to 69 days follow-up.
- Observed safety signals included increases in D‑dimer levels and hypersensitivity/anaphylactic reactions; additional adverse events consistent with risks of topical fibrin sealants and effects related to gas spray application were observed.
- Detailed trial designs, primary endpoints and efficacy outcomes: Not specified in available sources.
