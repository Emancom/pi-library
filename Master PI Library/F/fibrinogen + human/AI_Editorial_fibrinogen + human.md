# Editorial Product Information: fibrinogen + human

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **fibrinogen + human**
- Chemical class: Human plasma‑derived fibrinogen combined with human thrombin (fibrin sealant) with added fibrinolysis inhibitor (aprotinin) in the sealer protein component
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Topical application only (local surgical application). Do not inject intravascularly.

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Last revision stated as 09/2025 in one source [date of PI revision].  
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Adjunct to hemostasis in adult and pediatric patients undergoing surgery when control of bleeding by conventional surgical techniques (e.g., suture, ligature, cautery) is ineffective or impractical. Product is effective in heparinized patients.
- Adjunct to standard surgical techniques to prevent leakage from colonic anastomoses following reversal of temporary colostomies in adult and pediatric patients.
- If any indication is limited to specific jurisdictions, that limitation is not specified in available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General principles
- For topical use only. Do not inject into the vasculature or into highly vascularized mucosa (e.g., nasal mucosa).
- Dose is determined by surface area of wound to be covered. Ensure the amount applied fully covers intended application area.
- Apply as a thin layer by dripping or spraying with a delivery system or applicator approved/cleared for use with this product.
- Allow at least 2 minutes after application for sufficient polymerization. If repeat application is required, dry site as much as possible and remove residues from prior application prior to reapplying; reapplication onto an already polymerized layer may not adhere firmly.
- Use aseptic technique during preparation and application. Single‑patient use only; discard any unused product and record product name and batch number in the patient record.

### Surface‑area guidance (approximate)
- 2 mL: spray coverage ≈ 100 cm²; cannula application coverage ≈ 8 cm²
- 4 mL: spray coverage ≈ 200 cm²; cannula application coverage ≈ 16 cm²
- 10 mL: spray coverage ≈ 500 cm²; cannula application coverage ≈ 40 cm²

### Preparation and reconstitution
- The fibrin sealant comprises two component solutions prepared separately:
  - Sealer Protein Solution: Sealer Protein Concentrate dissolved in the fibrinolysis inhibitor solution (contains aprotinin).
  - Thrombin Solution: Thrombin powder dissolved in calcium chloride solution.
- Always use separate syringes and cannulas for preparing the two solutions to avoid premature clotting.
- Visually inspect reconstituted solutions; they should be clear or slightly opalescent. Do not use if cloudy, discolored, contains deposits, or other visible changes.
- After reconstitution/warming, use within the time limits specified by the manufacturer for the prepared presentation (see device/product instructions). Do not expose components to temperatures above those recommended during preparation. Do not microwave.

### Thawed pre‑filled syringes / warmed product (administration timing)
- When using pre‑filled frozen syringes, ensure the product is thawed and warmed to the recommended application temperature range before use (manufacturer’s instructions provide thaw/warm guidance). Once opened or warmed to application temperature, use within the manufacturer‑specified time window (e.g., several hours); verify the exact allowable in‑use time per the supplied instructions.

### Application technique and equipment
- Use delivery systems and applicators that are approved/cleared for use with this product.
- Spray only onto visible application sites. Dry the wound surface as much as possible (e.g., compresses, suction); do not use pressurized gas to dry the site.
- Expel air from the syringe before connecting the application device. Do not expel air remaining in the joining piece or application cannula prematurely (this may cause clogging).
- If interruption causes cannula clogging, replace the cannula immediately before resuming application.
- Record product batch information in the patient chart for traceability.

### Recommended gases, distances and pressures for spray application (device‑specific parameters must be followed)
- Open surgical application:
  - Recommended gases: medical grade carbon dioxide (CO2), compressed air, or nitrogen.
  - Recommended distance: approximately 10–15 cm from tissue.
  - Recommended spray pressure range: approximately 1.5–2.0 bar (≈21.8–29.0 psi).
- Minimally invasive / laparoscopic application (enclosed body cavity):
  - Use only systems and applicators designed and cleared for enclosed cavities and specifically designed to prevent overpressurization; where specified, CO2 is the recommended gas.
  - Typical recommended distance for minimally invasive applicators: 2–5 cm (3 cm commonly recommended).
  - Typical recommended spray pressure range for minimally invasive applicators: approximately 1.18–1.50 bar (≈17–22 psi).
- Always follow the specific applicator/regulator Instructions for Use for gas type, pressure and distance. Do not spray where minimum recommended distance cannot be assured.

### Contraindicated application situations (administration)
- Do not use for severe or brisk arterial or venous bleeding (product will be washed away before hemostasis is achieved).
- Do not inject intravascularly.
- Do not spray in enclosed body areas unless using a delivery system specifically designed for enclosed spaces with appropriate venting/pressure control.

### Missed dose / titration
- Not applicable for single‑use, topical surgical applications. No titration schedules specified.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to aprotinin (present in the fibrinolysis inhibitor component).  
- Intravascular application (i.e., injection directly into the circulatory system) or use in highly vascularized mucosa (e.g., nasal mucosa).
- Use for treatment of severe or brisk arterial or venous bleeding.
- Spraying when the minimum recommended distance from applicator tip to target site cannot be assured.
- If any jurisdiction‑specific contraindications exist, they are not specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed / highlighted warnings
- **Hypersensitivity / anaphylaxis:** Contains aprotinin; life‑threatening anaphylactic reactions, including anaphylactic shock, have occurred. Patients with prior systemic aprotinin exposure or repeated applications of the product in the same setting may be at increased risk.
- **Air / gas embolism:** Life‑threatening or fatal air or gas embolism, tissue rupture, or gas entrapment can occur with spray application using pressurized gas if recommended gases, pressures and distances are not observed.

### General warnings and precautions
- Monitor patients for hypersensitivity signs and symptoms (see Monitoring). If hypersensitivity occurs, discontinue application, remove remaining product from the site and treat appropriately.
- To reduce risk of gas embolism, use only the recommended regulator, gas, pressure range and applicator distance for the chosen delivery system. Prefer use of CO2 for closed/enclosed cavities where indicated due to higher solubility.
- Use only spray catheters/applicators approved for use with this product. Devices designed for enclosed body cavities should include means to prevent overpressurization (e.g., dedicated vent lines).
- Sealer Protein and Thrombin solutions can be denatured by exposure to alcohol, iodine or heavy metal ions. If such agents have been used on the wound, thoroughly rinse the area before applying the product.
- Safety and effectiveness have not been evaluated in neurosurgical procedures or in other surgeries involving confined spaces unless specifically cleared; use in these settings is not approved.
- The product is derived from pooled human plasma and may carry a risk of transmitting infectious agents, including viruses and agents of transmissible spongiform encephalopathies (e.g., vCJD/CJD). Report suspected transmission events per local requirements.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor for signs of hypersensitivity: bradycardia, tachycardia, hypotension, flushing, bronchospasm, wheeze, dyspnea, nausea, urticaria, angioedema, pruritus, erythema, paresthesia.
- During and after spray application, monitor blood pressure, pulse, oxygen saturation and end‑tidal CO2 given the risk of air/gas embolism.
- Maintain documentation of product name and batch number for each application.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Avoid intravascular exposure; intravascular application can result in intravascular coagulation and thromboembolic events.
- When very small volumes are required (1–2 drops), expel and discard initial drops from the application cannula to ensure delivery of adequately mixed product.
- Do not combine or carry the product on oxidized cellulose carrier materials (oxidized cellulose may reduce efficacy).
- Do not use if reconstituted solutions show cloudiness, discoloration, deposits, or other abnormal appearance.

## 7. Adverse Effects

- Serious adverse events reported include life‑threatening air/gas embolism, thromboembolic events and intravascular coagulation when applied intravascularly, tissue rupture or gas entrapment from improper spray use, and potential transmission of infectious agents from human plasma‑derived products.

- Increased D‑dimer levels were observed during clinical trials.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Oxidized cellulose‑containing materials (e.g., certain topical haemostatic carriers) can reduce the efficacy of the fibrin sealant and should not be used as carrier materials.
- Topical agents containing alcohol, iodine, or heavy metal ions can denature the sealer protein and thrombin components; rinse wound areas thoroughly if these agents have been applied before using the product.
- Prior or concomitant exposure to systemic aprotinin increases the risk of hypersensitivity reactions when this product (which contains aprotinin in the fibrinolysis inhibitor component) is used; exercise caution.
- No systemic pharmacokinetic interaction data are specified in available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- The product is a fibrin sealant composed of human‑derived fibrinogen and human thrombin with an antifibrinolytic component (aprotinin) in the sealer protein preparation. On mixing and application, fibrinogen is converted to fibrin by thrombin leading to polymerization and local fibrin clot formation to achieve hemostasis and tissue sealing. Allow at least ~2 minutes for polymerization to be sufficient.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Safety exposure described across five clinical trials (Study 1–5) with a total of 452 patients receiving the product as an adjunct for hemostasis or for sealing colonic anastomoses after reversal of temporary colostomies.
- Mean volume of product applied in studies: 5.3 mL (range 0.2–20.0 mL).
- Follow‑up durations reported in the clinical programs:
  - Study 1: 30 days
  - Study 2 and Study 3: 6 months
  - Study 4: up to 167 days
  - Study 5: up to 69 days
- Increased D‑dimer levels were observed during clinical studies.
