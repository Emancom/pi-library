# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of the Product Information for **levocarnitine** injection. It is intended for use by healthcare professionals as a concise clinical reference.
- **Note:** This editorial synthesis does not replace the official Product Information mandated in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for mitochondrial fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Name:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US]. 
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) sterile solution in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metabolic Disorders:** For the management (acute and chronic) of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - **Administration:** Delivered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion. 
    - **Frequency:** Administered every 3 to 4 hours (q3h or q4h), but never less than every 6 hours (q6h).
    - **Loading Dose:** Often utilised in patients presenting with severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported administered dose is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** Recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg of dry body weight.
    - **Administration:** Delivered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Adjustment:** Dose adjustments (e.g., reduction to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels [US].

### Monitoring
- **Plasma Concentrations:** It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations. Weekly and monthly monitoring is advised. 
- **Target Range:** Free carnitine levels should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None Known:** There are no known contraindications for levocarnitine injection [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, treatment must be discontinued immediately and appropriate medical management initiated [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In patients with a known history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity of activity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulation Caution):** While the injection is used for ESRD, high doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (>10%):** Transient nausea and vomiting. 
- **Other Reported Effects:** Gastritis and body odor (rare).
- **Dialysis-Specific Adverse Events (Frequency ≥5% regardless of causality):**
    - **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain.
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia.
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea.
    - **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia.
    - **Nervous System:** Dizziness, paresthesia.
    - **Respiratory:** Cough increase, dyspnea, pharyngitis, rhinitis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not explicitly assigned a letter category in source, but animal reproductive studies (rats and rabbits) at 3.8 times the human dose revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm.
    - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal constituent of human milk. Studies in dairy cows suggest exogenous administration increases milk concentrations. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant should be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing guidelines for metabolic disorders are applicable to paediatric patients; however, specific age-range safety profiles beyond metabolic indications are not detailed [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used therapeutically in ESRD for carnitine deficiency. High-dose oral therapy is cautioned against in severe impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing the necessary substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, it alleviates metabolic abnormalities related to the accumulation of toxic organic acids and corrects biochemical deficiency (defined as plasma concentrations <20 µmol/L) [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following IV administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Bioequivalence:** Studies in healthy volunteers established bioequivalence between oral tablets and solution (absolute bioavailability ~15%).
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg thrice weekly after dialysis showed that trough levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) demonstrated a dose-dependent increase in trough concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].

- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only (Rx Only) [US].
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** Not specified in available sources.
