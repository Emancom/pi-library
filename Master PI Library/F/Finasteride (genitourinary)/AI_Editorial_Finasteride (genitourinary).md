# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial document was developed using official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It aims to provide a clinically accurate overview of the drug's properties, usage, and safety profile across the provided regulatory source.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing or dispensing in any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP).
- **Chemical Class:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt; a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Clear, colorless sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic management of patients presenting with secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from congenital metabolic defects [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease who are undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Acute Management:** Often initiated with a loading dose in patients with severe metabolic crisis, followed by equivalent dosing over the subsequent 24 hours. Administration should occur every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though higher doses may be required. The maximum reported dose is 300 mg/kg/day [US].
- **Monitoring:** Plasma carnitine concentrations should be obtained prior to therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Initial Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration Timing:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Adjustment:** Therapy initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels <40–50 µmol/L. Dose adjustments should be guided by trough concentrations, with downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) potentially made by the third or fourth week of treatment [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None Known:** There are currently no listed contraindications for levocarnitine injection in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported. These often occur within minutes of administration, primarily in ESRD patients. Treatment must be discontinued immediately if severe reactions occur [US].
- **TMA/TMAO Accumulation:** While specific to oral formulations, clinicians should note that chronic high-dose administration in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine (both oral and IV) has been associated with reports of seizures. In patients with pre-existing seizure disorders, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disorders.

| Frequency | Adverse Event [US] |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site reaction (33-38%), Pharyngitis (15-27%), Chest pain (12-15%), Hypertension (18-21%), Headache (12-37%), Nausea/Vomiting (8-21%), Diarrhea (9-35%), Abdominal pain (5-21%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Fever, Asthenia, Dizziness, Dyspnea, Rhinitis, Pruritus, Hypercalcemia, Peripheral edema, Tachycardia |
| **Post-marketing/Other** | Body odor, Gastritis, Seizures, Anaphylaxis |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended to monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US]: Not formally assigned a letter category in the provided source, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine intake must be weighed against maternal benefits [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was observed in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy are established for the treatment of inborn errors of metabolism and carnitine deficiency in ESRD. Specific dosing is weight-based (see Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in the available source.
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety and efficacy of the *oral* formulation have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. The injection is specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients undergoing dialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This provides substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Efficacy:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly showed that trough concentrations (initially ~20 µmol/L) increased and stabilized between 54 and 180 µmol/L after one week of therapy [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses in ESRD patients (N≈30 per group) demonstrated dose-proportionate increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
- **Handling:** Vials are single-dose; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].
