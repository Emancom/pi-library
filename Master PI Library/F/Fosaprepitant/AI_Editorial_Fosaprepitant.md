# Editorial Product Information: Fosaprepitant

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of fosaprepitant for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory and clinical data into a concise reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current approved PI in their respective region before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Fosaprepitant (as dimeglumine), a water-soluble phosphoryl prodrug of aprepitant.
- **Chemical Class:** Substance P/neurokinin 1 (NK1) receptor antagonist.
- **Brand Names:** Fosaprepitant-AFT [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A04AD12.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Lyophilised powder for injection, 150 mg (as free acid) per vial [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AFT Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Prevention of Chemotherapy-Induced Nausea and Vomiting (CINV):** Indicated in combination with other antiemetic agents for the prevention of acute and delayed nausea and vomiting associated with initial and repeat courses of:
    - Highly emetogenic cancer chemotherapy (HEC).
    - Moderately emetogenic cancer chemotherapy (MEC).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
The recommended dose is **150 mg** administered as an intravenous infusion on **Day 1 only**.

### Dosing by Indication
- **Highly Emetogenic Chemotherapy (HEC):** 150 mg IV on Day 1, administered approximately 30 minutes prior to chemotherapy.
- **Moderately Emetogenic Chemotherapy (MEC):** 150 mg IV on Day 1, administered approximately 30 minutes prior to chemotherapy.

### Co-administration Regimen
Fosaprepitant must be used in conjunction with a corticosteroid (typically dexamethasone) and a 5-HT3 antagonist. 
- **Dexamethasone Adjustment:** When co-administered with fosaprepitant 150 mg, the oral dose of dexamethasone should be reduced by approximately 50% on Days 1 and 2 to account for drug interactions [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Reconstitution:** Reconstitute with 5 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride. Avoid shaking or jetting to prevent foaming.
- **Dilution:** The reconstituted volume should be added to an infusion bag containing 145 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride (final volume 150 mL).
- **Infusion Rate:** Administer over **20 to 30 minutes**.
- **Incompatibilities:** Incompatible with solutions containing divalent cations (e.g., Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺), such as Hartmann's or Lactated Ringer's Solution.
- **Restrictions:** Do **not** administer as a bolus, or via intramuscular/subcutaneous routes.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to fosaprepitant, aprepitant, polysorbate 80, or any excipients.
- **Concurrent use with:**
    - Pimozide
    - Terfenadine
    - Astemizole
    - Cisapride
  *Rationale: Inhibition of CYP3A4 by the active metabolite (aprepitant) can significantly elevate plasma levels of these drugs, leading to serious or life-threatening reactions.*

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **CYP3A4 Interactions:** Fosaprepitant is rapidly converted to aprepitant, a dose-dependent inhibitor of CYP3A4. Use caution with concomitant drugs metabolised via this pathway.
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Immediate reactions (flushing, erythema, dyspnoea, anaphylaxis/anaphylactic shock) have occurred during or shortly after infusion. Discontinue immediately and do not re-challenge if hypersensitivity occurs.
- **Infusion Site Reactions (ISRs):** ISRs, including thrombophlebitis and vasculitis, are reported. Risk increases with concomitant vesicant chemotherapy (e.g., anthracyclines). If local irritation occurs, terminate the infusion and restart in a different vein.
- **Warfarin Interaction:** Co-administration may cause a clinically significant decrease in INR. Monitor INR closely for 2 weeks post-treatment, specifically at the 7–10 day mark [Australian].
- **Hormonal Contraceptives:** Efficacy may be reduced during and for 28 days following administration. Patients should use effective alternative or back-up contraception methods.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The safety profile of fosaprepitant 150 mg is generally similar to that of oral aprepitant, though ISRs are unique to the IV route.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Constipation, infusion site pain. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Palpitations, abdominal pain/distension, dyspepsia, asthenia, decreased appetite, cough, pharyngeal pain, hot flush, flushing, hypertension, infusion site erythema/pruritus. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Infusion site induration. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN) have been identified during post-marketing use.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibition:** Aprepitant (the active metabolite) is a weak-to-moderate inhibitor of CYP3A4. It can increase concentrations of midazolam and corticosteroids.
- **CYP2C9 Induction:** Aprepitant is a moderate inducer of CYP2C9. It can decrease concentrations of S-warfarin and tolbutamide.
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Strong inducers (e.g., Rifampicin, St. John’s Wort) may significantly reduce aprepitant plasma concentrations and efficacy.

### Specific Management Notes
- **Corticosteroids:** Reduce oral dexamethasone dose by 50% and methylprednisolone dose (IV by 25%, oral by 50%).
- **Chemotherapeutic Agents:** Use caution with agents metabolised by CYP3A4 (e.g., docetaxel, paclitaxel, etoposide, irinotecan, vinca alkaloids). Post-marketing neurotoxicity has been reported with ifosfamide.
- **5-HT3 Antagonists:** No clinically significant interactions noted with ondansetron or granisetron.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B2]. There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use only if potential benefit outweighs potential risk to the foetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** Aprepitant is excreted in the milk of lactating rats. It is unknown if it is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment.
- **Fertility:** No effects on mating performance or fertility were observed in rat studies at doses up to 1.5 times the human exposure.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in patients under 18 years of age [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment required.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment required, including for patients with severe renal insufficiency or end-stage renal disease (ESRD) on haemodialysis.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dosage adjustment required for mild to moderate impairment (Child-Pugh score 5–9). No clinical data available for severe impairment (Child-Pugh score >9).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Fosaprepitant is a prodrug that is rapidly converted to aprepitant. Aprepitant is a selective high-affinity antagonist of human substance P/neurokinin 1 (NK1) receptors. It has little to no affinity for serotonin, dopamine, or corticosteroid receptors.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** NK1 receptor antagonists act centrally to inhibit emesis. Positron Emission Tomography (PET) studies indicate that aprepitant crosses the blood-brain barrier, with >95% receptor occupancy at peak plasma levels.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** >95% protein bound. Volume of distribution (Vdss) is ~66 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly converted to aprepitant in the liver and extrahepatic tissues (kidney, lung, ileum) within 30 minutes of infusion. Aprepitant is further metabolised primarily by CYP3A4.
    - **Elimination:** Eliminated primarily via metabolism; not excreted unchanged in urine. Terminal half-life is approximately 14 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **HEC Trial:** A randomised, double-blind study (N=2322) compared a single dose of fosaprepitant 150 mg to a 3-day oral aprepitant regimen. Fosaprepitant was shown to be non-inferior to the oral regimen, with a Complete Response (no vomiting and no rescue therapy) of 71.9% vs 72.3%.
- **MEC Trial:** A randomised, double-blind study (N=1000) compared fosaprepitant 150 mg (with ondansetron/dexamethasone) to a control regimen (ondansetron/dexamethasone alone). The fosaprepitant regimen was superior, with a Complete Response in the delayed phase of 78.9% vs 68.5% (p < 0.001).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vial (Unopened):** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze) [Australian].
- **Reconstituted/Diluted Solution:** To avoid microbiological hazard, use as soon as practicable. If storage is necessary, the diluted solution is stable for up to 24 hours at 2°C to 8°C.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4) [Australian].
