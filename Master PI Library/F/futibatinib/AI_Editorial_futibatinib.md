# Product Information: LYTGOBI (futibatinib)

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Trade Name:** LYTGOBI®
**Active Ingredient:** Futibatinib

LYTGOBI is available as film-coated tablets for oral administration. Strengths include 4 mg [TGA, FDA] and 16 mg [FDA].

## 2. Approved Indications
LYTGOBI is indicated as monotherapy for the treatment of adult patients with locally advanced or metastatic intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (iCCA) who have been previously treated with at least one line of systemic therapy. Eligible patients must harbor a fibroblast growth factor receptor 2 (FGFR2) gene fusion or other rearrangement.

**Note:** This indication has been granted provisional approval [TGA] or accelerated approval [FDA] based on objective response rate and duration of response. Continued approval may be contingent upon the verification of clinical benefit in confirmatory trials.

## 3. Contraindications
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to futibatinib or any of the inactive ingredients (including lactose monohydrate) [TGA].
*   **FDA Labeling:** The US PI identifies no specific contraindications [FDA].

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Hyperphosphataemia and Soft Tissue Mineralization
Increased serum phosphate is a common pharmacodynamic consequence of FGFR inhibition. Persistent hyperphosphataemia may result in ectopic soft tissue mineralization, including vascular, myocardial, and cutaneous calcification (calcinosis or nonuremic calciphylaxis) [FDA]. Complications may include muscle cramps, QT interval prolongation, and arrhythmias [TGA].
*   **Management:** Monitor phosphate levels throughout treatment. Implement a low-phosphate diet and initiate phosphate-lowering therapy when levels reach ≥5.5 mg/dL. Dose adjustment or interruption is required for levels >7 mg/dL.

### Ocular Toxicity
LYTGOBI can cause retinal pigment epithelial detachment (RPED) or serous retinal detachment, which may manifest as blurred vision, floaters, or photopsia.
*   **Monitoring:** Perform a comprehensive ophthalmic exam, including optical coherence tomography (OCT) of the macula, before starting therapy, every 2 months for the first 6 months, and every 3 months thereafter.
*   **Dry Eye:** Futibatinib is associated with dry eye and corneal keratitis. Patients should use ocular demulcents as needed.

### Embryofetal Toxicity
Based on animal data and its mechanism of action, LYTGOBI can cause fetal harm or pregnancy loss. Verify pregnancy status prior to initiation.
*   **Contraception:** Female patients of reproductive potential and male patients with female partners of reproductive potential must use effective contraception during treatment and for one week following the final dose.

### Excipients
The tablets contain lactose monohydrate. Patients with rare hereditary conditions such as galactose intolerance, total lactase deficiency, or glucose-galactose malabsorption should avoid this product [TGA].

## 5. Dosage and Administration
### Recommended Dose
The standard starting dose is **20 mg orally once daily**. This can be administered as five 4 mg tablets or one 16 mg tablet plus one 4 mg tablet [FDA].

### Administration Instructions
*   Take at approximately the same time each day, with or without food.
*   Tablets must be swallowed whole; do not crush, chew, split, or dissolve [FDA].
*   **Missed Doses:** If a dose is missed by more than 12 hours, or if vomiting occurs after ingestion, do not take an additional dose. Resume the regular schedule the following day.

### Dose Modifications
Reductions should follow these levels:
1.  **First reduction:** 16 mg daily.
2.  **Second reduction:** 12 mg daily.
3.  **Discontinuation:** Permanently stop treatment if the patient cannot tolerate 12 mg daily.

Detailed modification protocols for hyperphosphataemia and RPED/retinal detachment (ranging from withholding to permanent discontinuation) are based on the severity and duration of the adverse event.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The safety profile of LYTGOBI is based on clinical trials involving patients with iCCA and other advanced solid tumors.

*   **Very Common (≥20%):** Hyperphosphataemia, nail toxicities (onychomadesis, paronychia), musculoskeletal pain, constipation, diarrhea, fatigue, dry mouth, alopecia, stomatitis, abdominal pain, dry skin, arthralgia, dysgeusia, nausea, decreased appetite, and vomiting.
*   **Serious Adverse Reactions:** Reported in 39% of patients [FDA]. Common serious events include pyrexia, gastrointestinal hemorrhage, ascites, and bile duct obstruction.
*   **Laboratory Abnormalities:** Frequently observed changes include increased creatinine, decreased hemoglobin, increased glucose, and increased AST/ALT.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Effect of Other Drugs on Futibatinib
*   **Strong CYP3A/P-gp Inhibitors:** Avoid concomitant use (e.g., itraconazole), as these increase futibatinib exposure. If unavoidable, consider a dose reduction of futibatinib [TGA].
*   **Strong or Moderate CYP3A Inducers:** Avoid concomitant use (e.g., rifampin), as these significantly decrease futibatinib plasma concentrations. If unavoidable, a gradual increase in futibatinib dose may be considered under close monitoring [TGA].
*   **Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs):** No clinically significant interaction has been observed with PPIs like lansoprazole.

### Effect of Futibatinib on Other Drugs
*   **Transporter Substrates:** Futibatinib may increase the exposure of P-gp substrates (e.g., digoxin) and BCRP substrates (e.g., rosuvastatin).
*   **CYP Enzymes:** Futibatinib has the potential to induce CYP1A2. It does not appear to have clinically significant effects on CYP3A4 substrates like midazolam.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
**Pregnancy Category D [TGA].** There are no human data on LYTGOBI use in pregnant women. Animal studies showed 100% embryofetal mortality at 5 times the recommended human dose and significant malformations at 0.02 times the dose.

### Breastfeeding
It is unknown if futibatinib is excreted in human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in the infant, breastfeeding should be discontinued during treatment and for one week after the final dose.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Median Tmax is approximately 2 hours. A high-fat, high-calorie meal reduces Cmax by 42% but does not significantly affect the total exposure (AUC).
*   **Distribution:** 95% bound to plasma proteins (primarily albumin and α1-acid glycoprotein). Apparent volume of distribution is approximately 66 L.
*   **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized via CYP3A and glutathione conjugation.
*   **Elimination:** The mean half-life is approximately 2.9 hours. Excretion occurs mainly through feces (64% to 91%) and to a lesser extent in urine (6% to 9%).
*   **Special Populations:**
    *   **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild to moderate impairment (CLcr 30–89 mL/min). Data are insufficient for severe impairment or ESRD.
    *   **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild to moderate impairment. Patients with cirrhosis and elevated bilirubin may experience higher unbound exposure, increasing the risk of adverse reactions [TGA].

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Futibatinib is a small-molecule, selective, and irreversible inhibitor of FGFR types 1, 2, 3, and 4. It works by covalently binding to the FGFR kinase domain, thereby blocking constitutive signaling. This inhibition suppresses the proliferation and survival of cancer cells that harbor FGFR alterations, including fusions, rearrangements, and mutations.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **TGA:** Store below 25°C.
*   **FDA:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F), with excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F).
*   **General:** Keep in original packaging and out of reach of children.
