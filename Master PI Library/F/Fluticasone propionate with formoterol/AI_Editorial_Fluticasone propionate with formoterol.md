# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI. Prescribers should consult the local approved PI for the specific jurisdiction in which they are practicing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not specified in source, inferred by class).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism which result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose may be given, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered.
- **Monitoring:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are adequate [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring:** In ESRD patients, pre-dialysis (trough) levocarnitine levels should be monitored to guide dosing [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** While specific to chronic high-dose *oral* levocarnitine, clinicians should be aware that in patients with severely compromised renal function, metabolites such as trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) may accumulate as they are normally excreted in urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events reported in clinical trials for ESRD patients (frequency ≥5%) regardless of causality [US]:

| Body System | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Injection site reaction (33–38%), Pain (21–35%), Chest pain (6–15%), Flu syndrome (15–29%), Headache (3–37%), Abdominal pain (5–21%), Fever (5–12%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (18–21%), Hypotension (3–19%), Tachycardia (5–9%) |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea (9–35%), Vomiting (9–21%), Nausea (5–12%) |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia (6–15%), Hypervolemia (3–12%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (15–27%), Cough increase (10–18%), Dyspnea (3–11%) |
| **Nervous System** | Dizziness (10–18%), Paresthesia (3–12%) |

**Serious/Postmarketing Adverse Effects:**
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].
- **Immune System:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis, transient nausea/vomiting, body odor [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended that INR levels be monitored after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not explicitly assigned in provided source; text-based]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after exogenous administration. Clinicians should weigh the benefits of maternal supplementation against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is applicable to paediatric patients. Specific age-related safety profiles were not detailed beyond standard dosing protocols [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. High-dose oral therapy is cautioned in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation, but IV administration is the standard of care for dialysis-related deficiency [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Total body clearance is approximately 4.00 L/h [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x/week post-dialysis), mean pre-dialysis levocarnitine concentrations rose from ~20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While IV levocarnitine effectively increases carnitine concentrations, the source notes that the effect on specific clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, arrhythmias) has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature (25°C) when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].
