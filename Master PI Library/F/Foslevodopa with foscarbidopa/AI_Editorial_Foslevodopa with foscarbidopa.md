## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Pediatrics)
- **Metabolic Disorders:**
    - **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Administered every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Maximum Dose:** Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - **Administration:** Slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
    - **Adjustment:** Dose adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations.

### Monitoring
- **Baseline:** Obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to starting parenteral therapy.
- **Maintenance Monitoring:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine levels between 35 and 60 µmol/L) [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use. 
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions manifest within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and do not re-administer without careful risk-benefit analysis [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Levocarnitine (oral or IV) has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been reported [US].
- **Monitoring for ESRD:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine in patients with renal failure/ESRD on dialysis have not been established. Chronic high-dose oral use in these patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO) normally excreted in urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse event data are derived from clinical trials in ESRD patients on chronic hemodialysis.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%), Flu syndrome (25%), Headache (22%), Hypertension (20%), Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%), Infection (15%), Hypotension (14%), Dizziness (13%), Chest pain (12%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Abdominal pain (9%), Asthenia (9%), Accidental injury (10%), Nausea (8%), Fever (7%), Dyspnea (7%), Anemia (6%), Hypercalcemia (9%), Hyperkalemia (6%), Tachycardia (6%) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and other severe hypersensitivity reactions [US].
- **Post-marketing Experience:** Seizures and various hypersensitivity symptoms (laryngeal edema, bronchospasm).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels in patients on warfarin therapy after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specifically assigned a letter, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits (up to 3.8 times human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. A decision should be made whether to discontinue nursing or the drug, weighing the benefits of carnitine to the mother against potential risks of excess intake to the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg). Clinical trials in ESRD patients predominantly involved adults.
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65 and over to determine if they respond differently than younger subjects [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosing in ESRD is specific (10-20 mg/kg post-dialysis). The risk of metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO) is elevated with oral administration in patients with severely compromised renal function [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) and γ-butyrobetaine.
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), the mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous carnitine) [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Administration to ESRD patients on hemodialysis results in increased plasma concentrations of levocarnitine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x weekly post-dialysis), mean pre-dialysis trough levels increased from ~21 µmol/L at baseline to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses over 12 and 24 weeks demonstrated a dose-dependent increase in trough carnitine concentrations [US].
- *Note:* While levocarnitine increases carnitine levels in ESRD patients, the effect on specific clinical outcomes or signs/symptoms of deficiency in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial as it contains no preservative.
