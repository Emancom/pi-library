# Editorial Product Information: Pindolol

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: US (FDA).
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of pindolol clinical data for healthcare professionals, synthesised from official Product Information. 
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Pindolol (a synthetic nonselective beta-adrenergic receptor antagonist).
- **Chemical Class**: Beta-blocker with intrinsic sympathomimetic activity (ISA).
- **Brand Names**: Pindolol Tablets [US]; historically marketed as Visken.
- **ATC Code**: C07AA03.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**: 
    - Oral tablets: 5 mg and 10 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Westminster Pharmaceuticals, LLC; Bryant Ranch Prepack (Repackager) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypertension**: Indicated for the management of hypertension, either as monotherapy or in combination with other antihypertensive agents (specifically thiazide-type diuretics) [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult)**: The initial recommended dose is 5 mg twice daily.
- **Titration**: If an adequate antihypertensive response is not achieved within 3 to 4 weeks, the dose may be increased in 10 mg/day increments at intervals of 3 to 4 weeks.
- **Maximum Dose**: 60 mg/day [US].
- **Administration**: Tablets are administered orally. Bioavailability is not significantly affected by food co-administration [US].
- **Clinical Pearl**: While some response may be seen within one week, the maximal effect often requires 2 weeks or longer [US].

## 4. Contraindications
Pindolol is contraindicated in patients with:
- Bronchial asthma [US].
- Overt cardiac failure [US].
- Cardiogenic shock [US].
- Second and third-degree heart block [US].
- Severe bradycardia [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Abrupt Withdrawal**: Discontinuing therapy abruptly in patients with coronary artery disease can exacerbate angina or lead to myocardial infarction and ventricular arrhythmias. Therapy should be tapered over 1 to 2 weeks [US].
- **Cardiac Failure**: Inhibition of sympathetic drive by beta-blockade may precipitate more severe heart failure. Use with caution in well-compensated patients (e.g., those on digitalis or diuretics) [US].
- **Nonallergic Bronchospasm**: Patients with bronchospastic diseases (e.g., chronic bronchitis, emphysema) should generally not receive beta-blockers. If necessary, use pindolol with extreme caution as it may inhibit bronchodilation [US].
- **Major Surgery**: Routine withdrawal of beta-blockers prior to surgery is controversial; however, impaired reflex stimuli response may increase risks under general anaesthesia. The anesthesiologist must be informed [US].
- **Diabetes and Hypoglycemia**: Beta-blockers may mask premonitory signs of acute hypoglycemia (e.g., tachycardia, blood pressure changes) and may inhibit insulin release in response to hyperglycemia [US].
- **Thyrotoxicosis**: Pindolol may mask clinical signs of hyperthyroidism (e.g., tachycardia). Abrupt withdrawal should be avoided to prevent thyroid crisis [US].
- **Anaphylactic Risk**: Patients with a history of severe anaphylaxis may be more reactive to repeated allergen challenges and may be unresponsive to standard doses of epinephrine [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Catecholamine-depleting drugs (e.g., Reserpine)**: Potential additive effects; monitor for hypotension or marked bradycardia [US].
- **Thioridazine**: Pindolol may increase serum thioridazine levels; thioridazine may also increase pindolol levels [US].
- **Antihypertensive Agents**: Used safely with hydrochlorothiazide, hydralazine, and guanethidine without unexpected interactions [US].
- **Digoxin**: Small transient decreases in plasma digoxin levels noted, but no effect on clinical efficacy [US].
- **Bioavailability**: No significant effect from food, aspirin, or hydralazine [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: [US Category B]. Animal studies at 100x human doses showed no embryotoxicity. No adequate, well-controlled studies in humans. Use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: Pindolol is secreted in human milk; nursing is not recommended for mothers receiving the drug [US].
- **Fertility**: High doses in male rats (30 mg/kg/day) were associated with testicular atrophy, though a clear dose-response was not established [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and effectiveness in children have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use**: Half-life is more variable, averaging 7 hours but reaching up to 15 hours [US].
- **Renal Impairment**: Clearance of the drug is significantly reduced in uremic patients (CrCl <20 mL/min). However, the elimination half-life remains similar to healthy subjects due to compensatory mechanisms, though volume of distribution decreases [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Elimination is significantly slower and more variable in patients with cirrhosis. Half-lives ranging from 2.5 to >30 hours have been observed. Dose adjustment and caution are required [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Pindolol is a nonselective beta-adrenergic antagonist. It possesses **Intrinsic Sympathomimetic Activity (ISA)**, meaning it exerts a low-level agonist effect at the receptor while blocking more potent agonists (catecholamines).
- **Pharmacodynamics**: Reduces heart rate, systolic blood pressure, and cardiac output during exercise. Due to ISA, pindolol causes a smaller reduction in resting heart rate (4–8 beats/min) compared to beta-blockers without ISA [US]. It lacks quinidine-like membrane-stabilizing activity.
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Rapid and near-complete (>95%); peak plasma levels within 1 hour.
    - **Distribution**: 40% protein bound; volume of distribution is ~2 L/kg.
    - **Metabolism**: Extensive hepatic metabolism (60–65%) to hydroxy-metabolites.
    - **Elimination**: 35–40% excreted unchanged in urine. Half-life is 3–4 hours in healthy adults [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Hypertension Trials**: Controlled, double-blind studies demonstrated pindolol's efficacy as monotherapy or with thiazides. Monotherapy was found as effective as propranolol, methyldopa, and hydrochlorothiazide in reducing blood pressure. Long-term studies (up to 4 years) showed no diminution of the antihypertensive effect [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Protection**: Protect from light [US].
- **Container**: Dispense in tight, light-resistant containers with child-resistant closures [US].
