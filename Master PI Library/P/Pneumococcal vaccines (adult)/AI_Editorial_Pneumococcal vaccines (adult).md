# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
This editorial product information document synthesises information from the official Product Information document for **Levocarnitine Injection** provided, which originates from the **US (FDA)**. This document serves as a harmonised editorial overview intended for healthcare professionals. Please note that this is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. The provided document was titled "Product Information — Pneumococcal vaccines (adult)" but the content was clearly for Levocarnitine Injection.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active ingredient (INN/generic name) and chemical class**: Levocarnitine (L-carnitine); a carrier molecule in the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. Chemically, it is (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. [US]
- **Brand name(s) by jurisdiction if available**: Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US]
- **ATC code if available**: Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage form(s) and strength(s) by jurisdiction**:
    - Solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US]
- **Route(s) of administration**: Intravenous [US]
- **Manufacturer/sponsor by jurisdiction if available**:
    - Manufactured by: Baxter Pharmaceuticals India Private Ltd [US]
    - Manufactured for: Baxter Healthcare Corporation, Deerfield, IL, USA [US]

## 2. Approved Indications
- For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with an inborn error of metabolism which results in secondary carnitine deficiency. [US]
- For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease (ESRD) who are undergoing dialysis. [US]

## 3. Dosage and Administration
**General Dosing [US]:** Levocarnitine injection is administered intravenously.
- **Parenteral Drug Product Inspection**: Inspect visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- **Metabolic Disorders [US]**:
    - Recommended dose: **50 mg/kg** given as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection or by infusion.
    - Loading Dose: Often given in severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent total daily dose over the following 24 hours.
    - Administration frequency: Administer q3h or q4h, and never less than q6h, either by infusion or intravenous injection.
    - Maximum daily dose administered: **300 mg/kg**.
    - Monitoring: Plasma carnitine concentration should be obtained prior to initiating therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (free carnitine concentration should be between 35 and 60 µmol/L) and overall clinical condition.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis [US]**:
    - Recommended starting dose: **10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg** dry body weight, given as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - Initiation of therapy: May be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations below normal (40 µmol/L to 50 µmol/L).
    - Dose adjustments: Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) levocarnitine concentrations. Downward dose adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg after dialysis) may be considered as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.
- **Paediatric use**: Dosage and administration for paediatric patients is not specifically detailed, but the metabolic disorder dosing regimen would typically apply to all ages including paediatric patients, given the inborn error indication. [US]
- **Missed dose instructions**: Not specified in available sources. [US]

## 4. Contraindications
- None known. [US]

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed warnings**: None. [US]
- **General warnings and precautions [US]**:
    - **Hypersensitivity Reactions**: Serious hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported following levocarnitine injection, predominantly in patients with end-stage renal disease undergoing dialysis. Some reactions occurred within minutes after intravenous administration.
    - **Management of Severe Hypersensitivity**: Discontinue treatment and initiate appropriate medical treatment. The risks and benefits of re-administering levocarnitine must be considered for individual patients. If re-administration is decided, monitor patients for recurrence of signs and symptoms.
    - **Renal Insufficiency**: Chronic administration of high doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD on dialysis may lead to accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO)), which are normally excreted by the kidneys. The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in this population.

**Postmarketing Experience [US]**:
- **Neurologic Reactions**: Seizures have been reported in patients, with or without pre-existing seizure activity. An increase in seizure frequency and/or severity has been reported in patients with pre-existing seizure activity.
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions**: Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm. (See **Warnings** section).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin**: Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) with the use of warfarin have been observed. [US]
- **Management**: INR levels should be monitored in patients on warfarin therapy after the initiation of levocarnitine treatment or after dose adjustments. [US]
- **Other Interactions**: Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy [US]**:
    - **Pregnancy Category**: Not specified using lettered categories.
    - **Animal Data**: Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or harm to the fetus due to levocarnitine injection.
    - **Human Data**: No adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women.
    - **Recommendation**: Since animal reproduction studies are not always predictive of human response, this drug should be used during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding [US]**:
    - Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied.
    - Studies in dairy cows show increased levocarnitine concentration in milk following exogenous administration.
    - **Recommendation**: In nursing mothers receiving levocarnitine, any risks to the child from excess carnitine intake must be weighed against the benefits of levocarnitine supplementation to the mother. Discontinuation of nursing or levocarnitine treatment may be considered.
- **Fertility data**: Animal reproductive studies showed no evidence of impaired fertility. [US]

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of action [US]**: Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance essential for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, where they undergo oxidation to produce energy. Fatty acids are a primary energy substrate in most tissues, particularly skeletal and cardiac muscle. Levocarnitine can also promote the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with defects in fatty acid metabolism and/or specific organic acidopathies, by forming acylcarnitine which is quickly excreted.
- **Pharmacokinetics [US]**:
    - **Absorption**: Levocarnitine tablets were bio-equivalent to levocarnitine oral solution. Maximum plasma concentration (Cmax) of about 80 µmol/L achieved at 3.3 hours following oral administration of 330 mg b.i.d. tablets or 2g b.i.d. oral solution. The absolute bioavailability from oral formulations was approximately 15.1 ± 5.3% (tablets) and 15.9 ± 4.9% (oral solution).
    - **Distribution**: Not bound to plasma protein or albumin in human or other species. After a slow 3-minute intravenous bolus dose of 20 mg/kg, the plasma concentration profile is described by a two-compartment model. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours.
    - **Metabolism**: Not extensively metabolized; functions as a carrier molecule.
    - **Elimination**: Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 0-24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. In a study with an oral dose of [3H-methyl]-L-carnitine, 58% to 65% of the radioactive dose was recovered in urine and feces in 5-11 days. Major metabolites found were trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and [3H]-γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces). Urinary excretion of levocarnitine was about 4% to 8% of the dose; fecal excretion of total carnitine was less than 1% of the administered dose. Total body clearance was a mean of 4.00 L/h.
- **Pharmacodynamics**: Levocarnitine can alleviate metabolic abnormalities in patients with inborn errors leading to accumulation of toxic organic acids, promoting their excretion as acylcarnitine. It increases plasma levocarnitine concentrations in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. [US]

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic and Clinical Studies in ESRD [US]**: Clinical studies with levocarnitine injection in ESRD patients on hemodialysis demonstrated increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations.
    - In a 9-week study (N=12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg 3 times/week after dialysis): Mean plasma levocarnitine concentrations significantly increased from ~20 µmol/L (pre-dialysis, pre-treatment) to ~190 µmol/L (trough after 8 weeks).
    - In similar studies, 12- and 24-week mean pre-dialysis (trough) levocarnitine concentrations were 189 µmol/L (N=25) and 243 µmol/L (N=23) respectively.
    - A dose-ranging study showed dose-dependent increases in mean trough levocarnitine concentrations after 12 and 24 weeks of therapy (e.g., 10 mg/kg: 116 ± 69 µmol/L at 12 weeks; 40 mg/kg: 371 ± 111 µmol/L at 12 weeks).
- **Clinical Outcomes in ESRD**: While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations was demonstrated, the effects of supplemental carnitine on the signs and symptoms of carnitine deficiency and clinical outcomes in this population have not been determined. [US]

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [USP Controlled Room Temperature]. [US]
- Avoid excessive heat. [US]
- Protect from freezing. [US]
- Discard any unused portion of an opened vial, as the formulation does not contain a preservative. [US]
- Compatibility and Stability: Levocarnitine injection is compatible and stable when mixed in parenteral solutions of Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's in concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL and stored at room temperature (25°C) for up to 24 hours in PVC plastic bags. [US]
