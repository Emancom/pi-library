# Editorial Product Information: Pralidoxime

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from the official Product Information (PI) provided for the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonized editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing in any particular region. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pralidoxime chloride (also known as 2-PAM Chloride).
- **Chemical Class:** Cholinesterase reactivator (Oxime).
- **Brand Names:** **PROTOPAM** Chloride [US].
- **ATC Code:** V03AB04.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Lyophilized powder for injection: 1000 mg single-dose vial [US].
- **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous (preferred), Intramuscular, or Subcutaneous (when IV is not feasible).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Organophosphate Poisoning:** Antidotal treatment for poisoning caused by pesticides and chemicals (including nerve agents) of the organophosphate class that possess anticholinesterase activity [US].
- **Anticholinesterase Overdosage:** Control of overdosage by anticholinesterase drugs (e.g., neostigmine, pyridostigmine, ambenonium) used in the treatment of myasthenia gravis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Organophosphate Poisoning:**
    - **Intravenous (IV):** Initial dose of 1000 to 2000 mg. Administer as an infusion in 100 mL of normal saline over 15 to 30 minutes. If IV infusion is not practical or pulmonary edema is present, give slowly (not less than 5 minutes) as a 50 mg/mL solution.
    - **Repeat Dosing:** A second dose of 1000 to 2000 mg may be given after approximately one hour if muscle weakness is not relieved. Subsequent doses may be repeated every 10–12 hours if weakness persists.
- **Intramuscular (IM) Dosing (based on severity):**
    - **Mild Symptoms:** 600 mg (2 mL). If symptoms persist after 15 minutes, repeat the 600 mg dose. A third 600 mg dose may be given after another 15 minutes (Total: 1800 mg).
    - **Severe Symptoms:** Administer three 600 mg doses in rapid succession (Total: 1800 mg).
- **Anticholinesterase Overdosage (Myasthenia Gravis):**
    - 1000 to 2000 mg IV, followed by increments of 250 mg every five minutes [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Reconstitution for IV Infusion:** Add 20 mL Sterile Water for Injection to the 1000 mg vial (50 mg/mL). Further dilute with Normal Saline to a concentration of 10 to 20 mg/mL [US].
- **Reconstitution for IM Injection:** Add 3.3 mL Sterile Water for Injection to the 1000 mg vial for an approximate concentration of 300 mg/mL [US].
- **Infusion Rate:** Intermittent IV infusion rate should not exceed 200 mg/minute [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Absolute Contraindications:** None known [US].
- **Relative Contraindications:** Known hypersensitivity to the drug or situations where the risk of use outweighs the potential benefit [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Ineffectiveness:** Not effective for poisoning due to phosphorus, inorganic phosphates, or organophosphates lacking anticholinesterase activity [US].
- **Carbamate Poisoning:** **PROTOPAM** is not indicated for carbamate class pesticides, as it may increase the toxicity of carbaryl [US].
- **Rapid IV Administration:** May cause temporary worsening of cholinergic manifestations (e.g., tachycardia, cardiac arrest, laryngospasm, muscle rigidity).
- **Renal Impairment:** Pralidoxime is excreted in the urine; dosage should be reduced in patients with renal insufficiency [US].
- **Myasthenia Gravis:** Use with extreme caution as it may precipitate a myasthenic crisis [US].
- **Monitoring:** Treatment should begin immediately without waiting for laboratory results. Monitor red blood cell and plasma cholinesterase levels. Observation should be maintained for 48 to 72 hours post-exposure [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Note: Frequencies are not established in clinical trials; the following have been reported in volunteers or during therapy.*
- **General:** Dizziness, headache, drowsiness, blurred vision, diplopia, impaired accommodation, nausea, tachycardia.
- **Cardiovascular:** Increased systolic and diastolic blood pressure.
- **Respiratory:** Hyperventilation.
- **Neuromuscular:** Muscular weakness (when given to normal volunteers).
- **Injection Site:** Mild to moderate pain 40–60 minutes after IM injection.
- **Laboratory:** Transient elevations in SGOT (AST), SGPT (ALT), and creatine phosphokinase [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Atropine:** When used concomitantly, signs of "atropinization" (mydriasis, tachycardia, dry mouth) may appear earlier than with atropine alone [US].
- **Barbiturates:** Potentiated by anticholinesterases; use with caution for seizure control [US].
- **Avoidance:** Morphine, theophylline, aminophylline, reserpine, and phenothiazine-type tranquilizers should be avoided in patients with organophosphate poisoning [US].
- **Succinylcholine:** Anticholinesterase activity may prolong paralysis; use with caution [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Not assigned a lettered category in the provided source; however, animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. It should be administered to a pregnant woman only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is not known if the drug is excreted in human milk; exercise caution during administration [US].
- **Fertility:** No investigations on fertility impairment have been conducted [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy are supported by extrapolation and case studies.
    - **IV Loading Dose:** 20–50 mg/kg (max 2000 mg/dose) over 15–30 minutes, followed by continuous infusion of 10–20 mg/kg/hour [US].
    - **IM Dosing (<40 kg):** 15 mg/kg per injection (Max 45 mg/kg per course) [US].
    - **IM Dosing (≥40 kg):** Follow adult dosing [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Dose selection should be cautious, generally starting at the low end of the range due to higher frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Reduce dosage due to decreased clearance [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources, though elevations in liver enzymes have been reported.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Reactivates cholinesterase (primarily outside the central nervous system) that has been inactivated by phosphorylation due to organophosphates. It also slows the "aging" process of phosphorylated cholinesterase to a nonreactivatable form and may directly detoxify certain organophosphates [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Relieves muscle paralysis, specifically the muscles of respiration. It is less effective against central respiratory depression, requiring concomitant atropine [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Distribution:** Throughout extracellular water; not protein bound. Volume of distribution (Vd): 0.60 to 2.7 L/kg.
    - **Metabolism:** Partially metabolized by the liver.
    - **Elimination:** Rapidly excreted in urine via renal tubular secretion. Plasma half-life is approximately 74 to 77 minutes (longer in children and poisoned patients) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- There are no adequate and well-controlled clinical studies establishing effectiveness; use is based on successful case reports, animal studies, and decades of clinical experience in pesticide and nerve agent poisoning [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Parenteral products should be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration before use. Discard unused portions [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in provided US source (Refer to regional schedules such as Australian S4 where applicable).
