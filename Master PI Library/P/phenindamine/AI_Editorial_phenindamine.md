# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **US (FDA)**.
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. 
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Always consult local regulatory guidelines for the most current information.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. 
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not specified in available source; standard reference used).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Metabolic):** For the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Severe Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose may be given, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing intervals should be every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically remain in the 50 mg/kg range, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Titration:** Dose adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma carnitine concentrations (target 40–50 µmol/L). Downward adjustments to 5 mg/kg may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Monitoring
- Plasma carnitine concentrations should be obtained prior to therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in patients with ESRD on dialysis. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate risks before re-administration [US].

### Clinical Precautions
- **Seizure Risk:** Seizures have been reported in patients receiving levocarnitine, regardless of pre-existing seizure history. In those with established seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** While this PI concerns the injection, clinicians should note that chronic high-dose oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### General Experience
- **Common Effects:** Transient nausea and vomiting have been observed. Less frequent effects include body odor and gastritis [US].

### ESRD Clinical Trial Data (≥5% Incidence)
In double-blind, placebo-controlled trials of ESRD patients on hemodialysis, the following events occurred at a frequency of ≥5% [US]:
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%), chest pain (6–15%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), abdominal pain (5–21%), nausea (5–12%).
- **Central Nervous System:** Headache (3–37%), dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%).
- **Body as a Whole:** Injection site reaction (27–38%), pain (21–35%), fever (5–12%), infection (10–24%), asthenia (8–12%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hypervolemia (3–12%).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not explicitly lettered, but evaluated under current labelling standards]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. However, there are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased milk concentrations of carnitine following exogenous administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake to the infant should be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders are applicable to paediatric patients. Specific age-related normal concentrations define carnitine deficiency in premature infants and newborns [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. For the injectable form, dose adjustments are specifically recommended for ESRD patients on dialysis (see Section 3) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with specific metabolic defects [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours, and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg three times weekly), trough levocarnitine levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, and 40 mg/kg) showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Clinical Outcomes:** While levocarnitine injection increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the impact on clinical outcomes in this specific population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservative [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature [US].
