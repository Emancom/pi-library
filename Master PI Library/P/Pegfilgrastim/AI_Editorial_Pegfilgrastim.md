# Editorial Product Information: Pegfilgrastim

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction:
    - **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**: Product Information for Pelgraz (pegfilgrastim).
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Not all regional dosage forms or indications may be represented if they were absent from the provided source.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Pegfilgrastim (rbe).
- **Chemical Class**: Recombinant human Granulocyte Colony-Stimulating Factor (G-CSF) covalently bound to a 20,000 dalton polyethylene glycol (PEG) molecule.
- **Brand Names**: 
    - **Pelgraz** [Australian]
    - **Neulasta** (Reference biological product)
- **ATC Code**: L03AA13.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**:
    - **Pre-filled Syringe**: 6 mg pegfilgrastim in 0.6 mL (10 mg/mL) solution for injection. Supplied with a needle guard [Australian].
- **Route of Administration**: Subcutaneous (SC) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Accord Healthcare Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Febrile Neutropenia Prophylaxis**: Indicated in cancer patients receiving cytotoxic chemotherapy to decrease the duration of severe neutropenia and reduce the incidence of infection, as manifested by febrile neutropenia [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult)**: A single 6 mg subcutaneous injection per chemotherapy cycle.
- **Timing of Administration**: 
    - Should be administered approximately 24 hours after the completion of cytotoxic chemotherapy.
    - Safety data indicates it may be administered up to 14 days before chemotherapy.
    - **Note**: It is not recommended for administration in the period between 24 hours after and 14 days before chemotherapy due to the sensitivity of rapidly dividing myeloid cells [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions**:
    - For single use in one patient only.
    - Inspect visually for particulate matter or discolouration.
    - Allow the syringe to reach room temperature before injection.
    - Do not shake.
- **Missed Dose**: Not specified in available sources; however, dosing is tied strictly to the chemotherapy cycle.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity**: Known hypersensitivity to **pegfilgrastim**, **filgrastim**, *E. coli*-derived proteins, or any component of the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings**: None listed in provided Australian source.
- **Splenomegaly and Splenic Rupture**: Reported cases include fatalities. Evaluate patients reporting left upper abdominal or shoulder tip pain for splenic enlargement or rupture [Australian].
- **Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS)**: Monitor for pulmonary signs (fever, cough, dyspnoea). Discontinue if ARDS occurs.
- **Sickle Cell Crisis**: Severe and sometimes fatal crises have occurred. Use only after careful risk-benefit evaluation in patients with sickle cell trait or disease [Australian].
- **Capillary Leak Syndrome (CLS)**: Characterised by hypotension, hypoalbuminaemia, oedema, and haemoconcentration. Requires close monitoring and standard symptomatic treatment [Australian].
- **Glomerulonephritis**: Reported in patients receiving pegfilgrastim; typically resolves upon drug withdrawal. Urinalysis monitoring is recommended.
- **MDS and AML**: Associated with use in breast and lung cancer patients when used in conjunction with chemotherapy/radiotherapy. Monitor for symptoms of myeloid malignancy [Australian].
- **Aortitis**: Reported in patients; may present with fever, abdominal pain, and malaise. 
- **Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS)**: Rare but can be life-threatening. If SJS occurs, pegfilgrastim must never be restarted [Australian].
- **Latex Sensitivity**: The needle cover contains dry natural rubber (a latex derivative), which may cause allergic reactions [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%)**: Medullary bone pain (26%), nausea, asthenia, alopecia, headache, neutropenia.
- **Common (1–10%)**: Myalgia, arthralgia, skeletal pain, injection site pain, diarrhoea, dizziness, fatigue, vomiting.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%)**: Capillary Leak Syndrome, glomerulonephritis.
- **Rare (<0.1%)**: Splenic rupture, sickle cell crisis, Sweet’s syndrome (acute febrile dermatosis), SJS.
- **Very Rare (<0.01%)**: Cutaneous vasculitis [Australian].
- **Serious Adverse Effects**: Splenic rupture (fatalities reported), ARDS, anaphylaxis, CLS, and SJS.
- **Laboratory Abnormalities**: Spontaneously reversible elevations in LDH, alkaline phosphatase, and uric acid are common and often attributed to post-cytokine bone marrow expansion [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Cytotoxic Chemotherapy**: Due to potential sensitivity of rapidly dividing myeloid cells, do not use in the 24 hours following chemotherapy.
- **Lithium**: While not specifically investigated, the pharmacodynamic effect of lithium (promoting neutrophil release) may potentiate the effect of pegfilgrastim [Australian].
- **Bone Imaging**: Increased hematopoietic activity may lead to transient positive bone imaging changes; consider this when interpreting results [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **Australian Category B3**. 
    - Animal studies (rats/rabbits) showed increased post-implantation loss and reversible structural changes (wavy ribs) at maternally toxic doses. Use only if potential benefit justifies the risk to the fetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding**: It is unknown if pegfilgrastim is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution when administering to breastfeeding women [Australian].
- **Fertility**: No effects on fertility in male or female rats were observed at doses up to 13x the recommended human dose [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: 
    - Studied in 37 paediatric patients with sarcoma. 
    - Systemic exposure (AUC) was higher in younger age groups (0-5 years) compared to older children and adults. 
    - The most common adverse effect was bone pain [Australian].
- **Elderly Use**: Pharmacokinetics in patients ≥65 years are similar to younger subjects; no dose adjustment is required [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment**: No effect on pharmacokinetics was observed in patients with various degrees of renal impairment, including end-stage renal disease. No dose adjustment is required [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Not specifically studied, but PK is not expected to be affected by impaired hepatic function [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: A glycoprotein that regulates the production and release of neutrophils from the bone marrow. It binds to specific surface receptors on hematopoietic cells, stimulating proliferation and differentiation.
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Time to peak serum concentration ($T_{max}$) ranges from 8 to 120 hours (median ~40 hours).
    - **Distribution**: Limited to the plasma compartment.
    - **Elimination**: Non-linear and dose-dependent. Clearance is primarily neutrophil-mediated (self-regulating); serum concentrations decline rapidly at the onset of neutrophil recovery following chemotherapy [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics**: Causes a marked increase in peripheral blood neutrophil counts within 24 hours. Neutrophils produced show normal or enhanced chemotactic and phagocytic function.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies (Reference Product)**: Three randomised, double-blind trials showed that a single 6 mg dose of pegfilgrastim was as effective as daily filgrastim injections in reducing the duration of severe neutropenia (DSN) and the incidence of febrile neutropenia [Australian].
- **Biosimilar Comparability (Pelgraz)**: 
    - **APO-Peg-02**: A Phase I PK/PD study in 66 healthy subjects demonstrated PK and PD (ANC) equivalence between Pelgraz and US/EU-licensed Neulasta.
    - **APO-Peg-03**: A Phase III study in 589 breast cancer patients undergoing TAC chemotherapy (docetaxel, doxorubicin, cyclophosphamide) confirmed therapeutic equivalence in DSN and safety profile [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Refrigeration**: Store at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze.
- **Light Protection**: Keep the pre-filled syringe in the outer carton to protect from light.
- **Excursion**: May be exposed to room temperature (up to 25°C) for a single period of up to 15 days. If not used within this period, it must be discarded.
- **Accidental Freezing**: If accidentally frozen, allow to thaw in the refrigerator once; if frozen a second time, discard [Australian].
