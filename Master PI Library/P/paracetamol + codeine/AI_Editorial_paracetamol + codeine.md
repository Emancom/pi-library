# Editorial Product Information: paracetamol + codeine

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **paracetamol + codeine**
- Chemical class:
  - Paracetamol: analgesic / antipyretic (aniline derivative)
  - Codeine: opioid (weak opioid analgesic; phenanthrene derivative)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- For temporary relief of acute moderate pain in patients over the age of 12 years.
  - Where specific age restrictions or additional indications apply in particular jurisdictions, these are noted elsewhere in this document.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
- Product strengths described in available sources: paracetamol 500 mg + codeine phosphate hemihydrate 15 mg per tablet.
- General dosing (adult and adolescents ≥12 years):
  - 2 tablets every 6 hours if required, up to a maximum of 8 tablets in 24 hours.
  - (This corresponds to a maximum daily paracetamol dose of 4000 mg and a maximum daily codeine dose of 120 mg when using the formulation described in the available sources.)
- Dosing by indication: Not further specified in available sources beyond the above for acute moderate pain.
- Administration instructions:
  - Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration necessary to control symptoms.
  - Avoid concomitant intake of alcoholic beverages while taking the combination.
  - Caution patients about activities requiring mental alertness and coordination until they know how the medicine affects them (sedation, drowsiness).
- Titration schedules:
  - Initiate opioid therapy at the lower end of recommended doses and titrate cautiously to effect.
  - When discontinuing in a person who may be physically dependent, do not cease abruptly; taper gradually. General tapering guidance: reduce dose by 10–25% every 2–4 weeks, with individualized adjustments as required.
- Missed dose instructions: Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to paracetamol, codeine, or any excipient in the formulation.
- Severe respiratory disease, acute respiratory disease, or respiratory depression.
- Chronic constipation.
- During labour when delivery of a premature infant is anticipated (may produce neonatal withdrawal symptoms).
- Active alcoholism.
- Diarrhoea caused by pseudomembranous colitis or poisoning (until causative organism/toxin cleared; codeine may slow elimination).
- Individuals who are CYP2D6 ultra-rapid metabolisers.
- Children younger than 12 years of age.
- Adolescents aged 12–18 years in whom respiratory function might be compromised (including post‑tonsillectomy and/or adenoidectomy for obstructive sleep apnoea) — increased risk of serious, life‑threatening adverse reactions.
- Women who are breastfeeding.
- Any other contraindications listed in jurisdictional labelling: Not specified in available sources beyond those above.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed / prominent warnings
- [Australian] Limitations of use: Because of opioid-associated risks, this combination should be used only when non-opioid analgesics are ineffective, not tolerated, or otherwise inadequate to manage pain.
- [Australian] Hazardous and harmful use: Contains an opioid (codeine) and carries risks of abuse, misuse, addiction, overdose, and death. Assess risk for abuse prior to prescribing and monitor regularly.
- [Australian] Life‑threatening respiratory depression: Serious, potentially fatal respiratory depression may occur, especially on initiation or with dose increases.

### General warnings and precautions
- Concomitant CNS depressants: Concurrent use with benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, anti-histamines, tricyclic antidepressants, antipsychotics, cannabis, other opioids, sedatives/hypnotics, centrally acting antiemetics, and alcohol may produce profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death. Reserve combined use for patients for whom alternative treatment options are not possible; if co-prescribed, use lowest effective doses and shortest duration, and monitor closely.
- Addiction, dependence, tolerance, withdrawal: Neuroadaptation may produce tolerance and physical dependence; withdrawal symptoms can occur after several days to weeks of continued use. Monitor for signs of misuse and dependence; taper rather than abrupt cessation.
- Hyperalgesia: Opioid‑induced hyperalgesia may occur, particularly with high doses and prolonged use; reduce dose and consider opioid rotation if suspected.
- Use in chronic non‑cancer pain: Evidence does not generally support long‑term opioid therapy for most chronic non‑cancer pain. Consider opioids only after optimisation of non‑pharmacological and non‑opioid therapies and as part of multidisciplinary care; set realistic goals and discontinue if no improvement in pain/function.
- Accidental ingestion: Accidental ingestion, especially by children, can be fatal. Counsel patients on safe storage and disposal.
- CYP2D6 metabolism: Codeine is metabolised to morphine via CYP2D6. Ultra‑rapid metabolisers convert codeine to morphine rapidly, with risk of opioid toxicity even at commonly prescribed doses. Conversely, poor or deficient metabolisers may obtain inadequate analgesia.
  - Prevalence of ultra‑rapid metabolisers (estimates): 1% in Chinese/Japanese/Hispanic populations; ~3% in African Americans; 1–10% in Caucasians; highest (16–28%) in some North African, Ethiopian and Arab populations.
- Hepatic and renal impairment: Use with caution in hepatic or renal dysfunction; specific dose adjustments are not provided in available sources.
- Elderly: Increased susceptibility to respiratory depression and likely to have renal impairment; use caution and monitor.
- Conditions requiring caution: raised intracranial pressure, head injury, prostatic hypertrophy, hypotension, hypothyroidism, recent gastrointestinal surgery. Codeine may obscure the diagnosis/course of gastrointestinal disease.
- Paediatric precautions: Respiratory depression and fatalities have been reported in children who are ultra‑rapid metabolisers given codeine, particularly post‑adenotonsillectomy. Contraindicated in children under 12 and in 12–18 year‑olds with compromised respiratory function as above.
- Monitoring requirements:
  - Monitor for respiratory depression at initiation and dose increases.
  - Monitor for signs of misuse, abuse, addiction, and diversion.
  - Monitor liver function if prolonged therapy or if combined with other hepatotoxic agents or alcohol.
  - For long‑term therapy, regularly reassess pain control, function, adverse effects and aberrant behaviours; discontinue or taper if goals not met or misuse suspected.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Paracetamol:
  - Absorption increased by drugs that increase gastric emptying (e.g. metoclopramide).
  - Absorption decreased by drugs that slow gastric emptying (e.g. propantheline), antidepressants with anticholinergic properties, and narcotic analgesics.
  - Plasma chloramphenicol concentrations may be increased when given with paracetamol.
  - Paracetamol excretion may be affected by probenecid.
  - Cholestyramine reduces paracetamol absorption if given within 1 hour of paracetamol.
  - Risk of paracetamol toxicity is increased by concomitant hepatotoxic drugs or liver microsomal enzyme inducers, and by alcohol or antiepileptic drugs.
- Codeine:
  - Codeine is metabolised to morphine by CYP2D6; inhibitors of CYP2D6 (e.g. quinidine, certain phenothiazines and antipsychotics) may reduce conversion and analgesic effect, while inducers or ultra‑rapid metabolism increase morphine exposure.
  - Concomitant administration with opioid receptor antagonists is usually inappropriate due to potential additive or antagonistic effects and risk of adverse outcomes.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Additive central nervous system (CNS) depression: concurrent use with benzodiazepines, other opioids, gabapentinoids, alcohol, sedatives/hypnotics, certain antidepressants (tricyclics), antipsychotics, antihistamines, and centrally-acting antiemetics may result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma and death. Limit doses and durations; monitor closely.
- Anticholinergics: increased risk of severe constipation and urinary retention when combined with codeine.
- Antiperistaltic antidiarrhoeals (e.g. kaolin, pectin, loperamide): may increase risk of severe constipation.
- Antihypertensives: hypotensive effects may be potentiated by concurrent use of codeine.
- Monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs): concurrent administration or use within 14 days of ceasing MAOIs may enhance respiratory depressant effects of codeine.
- Warfarin and other coumarin anticoagulants: paracetamol may potentiate the effect of warfarin and prolonged concomitant use may necessitate dose reduction or monitoring of anticoagulation parameters.

### Management notes
- Avoid or use extreme caution when co-prescribing with other CNS depressants; consider alternatives, use lowest necessary doses, and closely monitor respiratory and CNS function.
- Monitor INR when paracetamol is used regularly in patients on warfarin.
- Avoid use in patients receiving strong CYP2D6 inhibitors if clinically important (monitor analgesic response and toxicity).
- Allow adequate time between cholestyramine and paracetamol dosing to avoid reduced paracetamol absorption.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- [Australian] Category A: Both paracetamol and codeine have been taken by a large number of pregnant women and women of childbearing age without proven increase in frequency of malformations or other direct harmful effects on the fetus. However:
  - Opioid analgesics can cause respiratory depression in the newborn.
  - Prolonged high‑dose maternal codeine use prior to delivery may produce neonatal withdrawal.
  - Use during labour when delivery of a premature infant is anticipated is contraindicated (may produce neonatal withdrawal).
- Other jurisdictional pregnancy category information: Not specified in available sources.

### Breastfeeding
- Use in breastfeeding is contraindicated in the available sources.
- Rationale: Codeine is metabolised to morphine which is excreted in breast milk; in mothers who are CYP2D6 ultra‑rapid metabolisers, elevated morphine levels in breast milk have resulted in life‑threatening or fatal respiratory depression in breastfed infants.
- If breastfeeding is considered despite contraindication in some jurisdictions, counsel on risks, monitor infant for sedation and respiratory depression, and consider alternative analgesics.

### Fertility
- Effects on fertility: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Contraindicated in children younger than 12 years.
- Contraindicated in adolescents aged 12–18 years in whom respiratory function might be compromised (including post‑tonsillectomy and/or adenoidectomy for obstructive sleep apnoea).
- Respiratory depression and deaths have occurred in children who received codeine post‑tonsillectomy/adenoidectomy who were ultra‑rapid metabolisers.
- Dosing recommendations for children under 12 or for other paediatric indications: Not specified in available sources.

### Elderly
- Elderly patients are more likely to have age‑related renal impairment and may be more susceptible to respiratory depressant effects; use with caution and monitor closely. Specific dose reductions: Not specified in available sources.

### Renal impairment
- Use with caution in renal impairment. Specific dose adjustments by eGFR or stages of renal impairment: Not specified in available sources.

### Hepatic impairment
- Use with caution in hepatic impairment. Specific dose adjustments by severity of hepatic impairment: Not specified in available sources.

### CYP2D6 ultra‑rapid metabolisers
- Contraindicated in individuals known to be CYP2D6 ultra‑rapid metabolisers due to increased risk of rapid conversion of codeine to morphine and consequent opioid toxicity.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Paracetamol: analgesic and antipyretic; precise mechanism not fully described in available sources.
- Codeine: opioid analgesic that exerts analgesic effect partly via metabolic conversion to morphine (active metabolite) by hepatic CYP2D6; exhibits opioid‑class effects including analgesia, sedation and respiratory depression.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: Paracetamol absorption is affected by agents that alter gastric emptying (increased by prokinetics; decreased by anticholinergic agents); cholestyramine reduces paracetamol absorption if given within 1 hour.
- Metabolism:
  - Codeine is metabolised to morphine by CYP2D6; interindividual variability in CYP2D6 activity (poor, extensive, ultra‑rapid metabolisers) affects analgesic efficacy and toxicity risk.
  - Paracetamol metabolism and excretion can be altered by enzyme inducers and inhibitors; risk of hepatotoxic metabolites increased with enzyme induction, alcohol, or other hepatotoxins.
- Elimination: Paracetamol excretion may be affected by probenecid.
- Detailed ADME parameters (e.g. Tmax, half‑life, volume of distribution, clearance values): Not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Opioid pharmacodynamic effects include analgesia, sedation, respiratory depression, miosis, reduced gastrointestinal motility (constipation), and potential for dependence and withdrawal.
