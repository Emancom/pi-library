# Editorial Product Information: Paroxetine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the Product Information (PI) for **paroxetine**, specifically designed for use by healthcare professionals. It integrates clinical data, regulatory requirements, and safety information into a concise, structured format.
- **Prescribing Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, legally approved PI in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for prescribing within their region. At the time of this publication, only the Australian PI was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Paroxetine hydrochloride (a phenylpiperidine derivative).
- **Chemical Class:** Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitor (SSRI).
- **Brand Names:** APO-Paroxetine [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 20 mg film-coated tablets [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Apotex Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Paroxetine** is indicated for the treatment and prevention of relapse in the following conditions [Australian]:
- Major Depression and prevention of relapse of depressive symptoms.
- Obsessive Compulsive Disorder (OCD) and prevention of relapse.
- Panic Disorder (with or without agoraphobia) and prevention of relapse.
- Social Anxiety Disorder (Social Phobia).
- Generalised Anxiety Disorder (GAD).
- Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Instructions
- **Administration:** Administer once daily in the morning with food. Tablets should be swallowed whole and not chewed [Australian].
- **Titration:** Dose changes should occur at intervals of at least one week [Australian].

### Adult Dosing by Indication [Australian]
- **Depression:** Recommended dose is 20 mg/day. If required, increase in 10 mg increments to a maximum of 50 mg/day.
- **OCD:** Recommended dose is 40 mg/day. Initiate at 20 mg/day and increase weekly by 10 mg. Maximum dose is 60 mg/day.
- **Panic Disorder:** Recommended dose is 40 mg/day. Initiate at 10 mg/day to reduce the risk of transient increased anxiety; increase weekly by 10 mg. Maximum dose is 60 mg/day.
- **Social Anxiety Disorder:** Recommended dose is 20 mg/day. If required, increase by 10 mg increments to a maximum of 50 mg/day.
- **Generalised Anxiety Disorder:** Recommended dose is 20 mg/day. If required, increase by 10 mg increments to a maximum of 50 mg/day.
- **Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder:** Recommended dose is 20 mg/day. If required, increase by 10 mg increments to a maximum of 50 mg/day.

### Discontinuation
**Abrupt discontinuation must be avoided.** A gradual taper is required (e.g., reducing the dose by 10 mg/day at weekly intervals). If intolerable symptoms occur, resume the previous dose and taper more slowly [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to **paroxetine** or its excipients [Australian].
- **MAOIs:** Use in combination with Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (including linezolid and methylene blue). A 2-week washout period is required when switching between **paroxetine** and an MAOI [Australian].
- **Thioridazine:** Concomitant use is contraindicated due to the risk of QTc prolongation and serious ventricular arrhythmias [Australian].
- **Pimozide:** Concomitant use is contraindicated as **paroxetine** increases pimozide levels, raising the risk of QT prolongation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Suicidality:** Increased risk of suicidal thinking and behavior in children, adolescents, and young adults (18–24 years) during the initial 1–2 months of treatment. Close monitoring for clinical worsening and emergence of suicidality is mandatory [Australian].
- **Withdrawal Symptoms:** High incidence of symptoms upon discontinuation (30% vs 20% placebo). Common reactions include dizziness, sensory disturbances (e.g., electric shock sensations), and sleep disturbances [Australian].
- **Serotonin Syndrome/NMS:** Potentially life-threatening; risk increases with concomitant serotonergic or neuroleptic drugs. Monitor for hyperthermia, rigidity, and mental status changes [Australian].
- **Akathisia:** Most likely within the first few weeks of therapy [Australian].
- **Haemorrhage:** SSRIs increase the risk of bleeding (ecchymoses, GI bleed, postpartum haemorrhage). Caution with NSAIDs, aspirin, or anticoagulants [Australian].
- **Bone Fracture:** Epidemiological data suggest an increased risk of fractures during therapy [Australian].
- **Tamoxifen Interaction:** **Paroxetine** irreversibly inhibits CYP2D6, which may reduce the efficacy of tamoxifen. Avoid co-administration where possible [Australian].
- **Glaucoma:** Use with caution in patients with narrow-angle glaucoma due to potential mydriasis [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are typically mild and may decrease with continued treatment.
- **Very Common (>10%):** Nausea, sexual dysfunction [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Somnolence, insomnia, agitation, tremor, dizziness, headache, blurred vision, yawning, sweating, dry mouth, constipation, diarrhoea, decreased appetite, asthenia, weight gain, and increased cholesterol [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Abnormal bleeding (skin/mucous membranes), confusion, hallucinations, extrapyramidal disorders, sinus tachycardia, postural hypotension, and urinary retention [Australian].
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Seizures, hyponatremia (predominantly in the elderly), elevation of hepatic enzymes, and manic reactions [Australian].
- **Very Rare:** Serotonin syndrome, thrombocytopenia, severe allergic reactions (anaphylaxis), SIADH, acute glaucoma, and severe cutaneous adverse reactions (Stevens-Johnson syndrome, TEN) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic (CYP2D6):** **Paroxetine** is a potent inhibitor of CYP2D6. It significantly increases levels of tricyclic antidepressants (TCAs), phenothiazines, risperidone, atomoxetine, Type 1C antiarrhythmics (e.g., flecainide), and metoprolol [Australian].
- **Warfarin:** Pharmacodynamic interaction may increase bleeding risk without significantly altering INR [Australian].
- **Phenytoin:** Co-administration reduces **paroxetine** plasma levels and increases the incidence of adverse effects [Australian].
- **Fosamprenavir/Ritonavir:** Significant reduction in **paroxetine** plasma levels [Australian].
- **Procyclidine:** **Paroxetine** significantly increases procyclidine levels; reduce procyclidine dose if anticholinergic effects occur [Australian].
- **Neuromuscular Blockers:** SSRIs may prolong the action of mivacurium and suxamethonium [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category D]. Epidemiological studies indicate an increased risk of cardiovascular malformations (notably ventricular septal defects) following first-trimester exposure. Use in late pregnancy is associated with Persistent Pulmonary Hypertension of the Newborn (PPHN) and neonatal withdrawal symptoms [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Detected in breast milk at concentrations similar to plasma. Use only if benefits outweigh risks to the infant [Australian].
- **Fertility:** May affect sperm quality in humans; reversible upon discontinuation [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not indicated for patients < 18 years. Failed to demonstrate efficacy in MDD clinical trials and associated with increased suicidal ideation [Australian].
- **Elderly:** Increased plasma concentrations occur. Dosing should start at the adult initial dose and not exceed 40 mg/day [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Increased plasma concentrations occur in severe renal (CrCl < 30 mL/min) or severe hepatic impairment. Dosing should be restricted to the lower end of the therapeutic range [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Potent and selective inhibitor of 5-HT (serotonin) reuptake in brain neurons. It has low affinity for muscarinic, adrenergic, or histaminergic receptors [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Well absorbed orally; undergoes first-pass metabolism.
    - **Distribution:** 95% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised via CYP2D6. Kinetics are non-linear due to saturable metabolism.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 64% in urine and 36% in faeces. The elimination half-life is approximately 24 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Depression Relapse Prevention:** A 1-year study showed a 15% relapse rate for **paroxetine** vs 39% for placebo.
- **OCD:** 12-week trials demonstrated significant improvement in YBOCS scores at 40 mg and 60 mg doses.
- **Panic Disorder:** Studies confirmed superiority to placebo and comparable efficacy to clomipramine.
- **PTSD:** Efficacy demonstrated in 12-week trials; however, long-term efficacy (>12 weeks) has not been evaluated in controlled trials [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Precautions:** Protect from moisture [Australian].
