# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory jurisdiction: United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn metabolic errors resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Metabolic Disorders (Adult and Paediatric):**
    - **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the next 24 hours [US].
    - **Frequency:** Administered every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Maximum Dose:** Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
    - **Adjustments:** Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration [US].
    - Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels (target: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported. These can occur within minutes of intravenous administration, particularly in ESRD patients. Discontinue immediately if a reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizures:** Reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **General Precautions:** The safety/efficacy of oral **levocarnitine** has not been established in renal insufficiency; chronic high doses of the oral form in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Commonly Observed:** Transient nausea and vomiting. Body odor and gastritis are reported less frequently [US].
- **ESRD Clinical Trial Data (Frequency ≥5%):**
    - **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (5–21%), headache (3–37%), injection site reaction (27–38%), pain (21–35%), fever (5–12%), flu syndrome (15–29%) [US].
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), chest pain (6–15%), tachycardia (5–9%) [US].
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%) [US].
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%) [US].
    - **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hypervolemia (3–12%) [US].
    - **Nervous System:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%) [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, and seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have been observed when co-administered with **levocarnitine**. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiation or dose adjustment [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Classification:** Not explicitly letter-categorised in source, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased carnitine concentrations in milk after exogenous administration. Weigh the benefits to the mother against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No mutagenic effects seen in standard tests; long-term carcinogenicity studies have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing guidelines are provided for metabolic disorders; safety and efficacy for ESRD have been established based on adult data and the known pathophysiology of the disease in children [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in provided sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** See Section 3 for ESRD-specific dosing [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Levocarnitine** is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn metabolic errors, it promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly cleared [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following a 20 mg/kg IV bolus, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** Double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in chronic hemodialysis patients demonstrated that 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly significantly increased pre-dialysis (trough) plasma carnitine concentrations (from baseline ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Stability:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only (Prescription only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
