# Editorial Product Information: Procarbazine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the local approved documentation before initiating therapy.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Procarbazine (as hydrochloride).
- **Chemical Class:** Methylhydrazine derivative.
- **Brand Names:** Natulan [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01AD03.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Hard capsules containing 50 mg of procarbazine hydrochloride [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Link Medical Products Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hodgkin’s Disease:** Treatment of Hodgkin’s disease (multiple lymphadenoma) [Australian].
- **Other Malignant Lymphomas:** Including lymphosarcoma, reticulosarcoma, and Brill-Symmers disease [Australian].
- **Refractory Cases:** Procarbazine may be effective in cases resistant to other cytostatic drugs or radiation therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Treatment should be initiated with small doses that are titrated upwards to achieve therapeutic effect while monitoring for toxicity.

- **Initial Titration:** 
    - Day 1: 50 mg daily.
    - Subsequent Days: Increase daily dose by 50 mg until a maximum of 250 mg to 300 mg daily is reached (typically by day 5 or 6) [Australian].
    - This dose is maintained until maximum possible remission is achieved.
- **Maintenance Dosage:** 
    - 50 mg to 150 mg daily [Australian].
    - Therapy is generally continued until a total cumulative dose of 6 g has been reached [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- If leucopenia (<3,000/mm³) or thrombocytopenia (<80,000/mm³) occurs, treatment must be suspended until haematological recovery, then recommenced [Australian].
- Patients should be advised to avoid specific foods and alcohol (see Section 7).

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to procarbazine or any excipient in the formulation [Australian].
- Pre-existing leucopenia or thrombocytopenia [Australian].
- Severe hepatic or renal impairment [Australian].
- Pregnancy and breastfeeding [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Expert Supervision:** Therapy must only be administered under the supervision of a physician experienced in the use of potent antineoplastic agents [Australian].
- **Myelosuppression:** Significant bone marrow depression may occur. Full blood counts (FBC), liver, and renal function tests must be performed before each cycle [Australian].
- **Secondary Malignancies:** There is a risk of secondary non-lymphoid malignancies, such as acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) and lung cancer, particularly when used in combination with other chemotherapy or radiotherapy [Australian].
- **Immunosuppression:** Increased risk of opportunistic infections. Live vaccines should generally be avoided due to the risk of generalised infection [Australian].
- **Discontinuation Criteria:** Consider stopping treatment if the following occur:
    - Haematological toxicity (leucopenia, thrombocytopenia).
    - CNS toxicity (paraesthesia, neuropathy, confusion).
    - Severe hypersensitivity, stomatitis, diarrhoea, or persistent vomiting [Australian].
- **Comorbidities:** Use with caution in patients with phaeochromocytoma, epilepsy, cardiovascular disease, or cerebrovascular disease [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are most prominent in the gastrointestinal and haematological systems. GI effects often diminish after the first few weeks of therapy [Australian].

| Frequency | System Organ Class | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Common (>1/100)** | Blood/Lymphatic | Thrombocytopenia, leucopenia, anaemia |
| | Gastrointestinal | Nausea, vomiting, loss of appetite, anorexia |
| | Infections | Infection |
| | Skin | Alopecia (usually reversible) |
| **Uncommon (<1/100)** | Immune | Hypersensitivity (anaphylaxis, angioedema) |
| **Not Known** | Psychiatric | Insomnia, depression, confusion, lethargy |
| | Nervous System | Seizures, peripheral neuropathy, paraesthesia, dizziness, headache, tremor, somnolence |
| | Gastrointestinal | Stomatitis, constipation, diarrhoea, abdominal pain |
| | Hepato-biliary | Hepatotoxicity, jaundice, abnormal LFTs |
| | Renal | Renal failure, acute kidney injury |
| | Reproductive | Permanent azoospermia, ovarian failure |
| | Neoplasms | Secondary malignancies (AML, MDS, solid tumours) |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Sepsis, bone marrow failure, pancytopenia, Steven-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), and congestive heart failure [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **MAO Inhibition:** Procarbazine is a weak monoamine oxidase inhibitor (MAOI).
    - **Dietary Amines:** Patients must avoid foods high in tyramine (e.g., aged cheese, yoghurt, bananas) to prevent hypertensive crises [Australian].
    - **Sympathomimetics:** Avoid concomitant use with sympathomimetic drugs and decongestants [Australian].
    - **CNS Depressants:** Potentiates the effects of barbiturates, anaesthetics, and narcotic analgesics. Use with caution and at reduced dosage [Australian].
    - **Tricyclic Antidepressants/Anticholinergics:** Use with caution [Australian].
- **Alcohol:** Disulfiram-like reactions may occur; abstinence is advised [Australian].

### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP3A Inducers:** Concomitant use with enzyme-inducing antiepileptics is associated with an increased risk of hypersensitivity reactions, potentially due to reactive intermediates. Non-enzyme-inducing antiepileptics are preferred [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category D. Procarbazine is teratogenic. It is contraindicated in pregnancy as it may cause fetal malformations, miscarriage, and premature birth [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. It is unknown if procarbazine is excreted in human milk [Australian].
- **Fertility:** High risk of permanent sterility and azoospermia in both males and females. Patients must be counselled on these risks before starting therapy [Australian].
- **Contraception:** Required during and after treatment (up to 6 months for males and 9 months for females) [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing must be determined by a physician experienced in paediatric oncology. Children may be at increased risk of toxicity, including tremors, coma, and convulsions [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Caution is required due to age-related decline in organ function (renal, hepatic, cardiac) and reduced haematopoietic reserve. Close monitoring for intolerance is necessary [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe impairment. Use with caution in mild-to-moderate cases. Haematological status should be monitored twice weekly and organ function weekly in these patients [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A cytostatic methylhydrazine derivative. While the exact mechanism is complex, it is known to inhibit protein, DNA, and RNA synthesis [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Exhibits weak MAO inhibitory activity [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Not specified in available sources.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Bottle:** Store below 30°C in a dry place [Australian].
- **Blister Pack:** Store below 25°C in a dry place [Australian].
- **Special Handling:** Urine produced for up to 48 hours post-dose should be handled with protective clothing [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction

- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only.
