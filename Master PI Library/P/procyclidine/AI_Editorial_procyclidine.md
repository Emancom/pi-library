# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Note on Substance:** Although the introductory prompt mentioned procyclidine, the provided source documentation pertains exclusively to **levocarnitine**. This editorial reflects the levocarnitine data contained within the provided source.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- This synthesis is intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** For the management of acute and chronic treatment in patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The standard recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion.
    - **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose may be given, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (minimum every 6 hours) over the next 24 hours.
    - **Maintenance:** Daily doses typically remain at 50 mg/kg, though titration may be required. The maximum recorded dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** 
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Timing:** Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Titration:** Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels, initiated as early as the third or fourth week of treatment [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for discoloration or particulate matter prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known:** There are currently no listed contraindications in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Serious Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, and laryngeal edema, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Symptoms may occur within minutes of injection. In the event of a severe reaction, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate the risk-benefit ratio before considering re-administration [US].

### General Precautions
- **Seizure Risk:** Seizures have occurred in patients with and without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment & Oral Formulations:** The safety of **oral** levocarnitine has not been established in patients with severe renal impairment or ESRD. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may result in the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Plasma Carnitine Levels:** Prior to starting therapy, obtain baseline plasma concentrations. Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended. The therapeutic target for free carnitine is generally 35 to 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Clinical Monitoring:** Periodic assessment of blood chemistries, vital signs, and overall clinical status is advised [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (>10%):** Nausea, vomiting, and transient gastrointestinal distress.
- **Less Frequent:** Gastritis and a distinct "fishy" body odor. 
- **ESRD-Specific Observations (regardless of causality):** 
    - *General:* Injection site reactions (33–59%), pain (30–49%), headache (22%), fever (7–12%), abdominal pain (9–21%) [US].
    - *Cardiovascular:* Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%), chest pain (6–15%) [US].
    - *Digestive:* Diarrhea (9–35%) [US].
    - *Respiratory:* Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough (10–18%) [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
    - **Management:** Monitor INR levels closely upon initiation of levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not formally classified by letter in the source, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits (up to 3.8 times the human dose) showed no evidence of fetal harm or impaired fertility. 
    - **Recommendation:** Due to lack of adequate human trials, use during pregnancy only if clearly required [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations following exogenous administration.
    - **Recommendation:** The risks to the infant of excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefit of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was observed in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg), applicable to the paediatric population [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific data not provided in the source; however, caution is generally advised regarding renal function assessment.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used as a primary treatment in ESRD patients on dialysis. However, accumulation of metabolites (TMA/TMAO) is a concern with high-dose **oral** therapy in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, IV administration increases plasma carnitine concentrations, which are often depleted due to dialysis losses and reduced renal synthesis [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Trials:** Two double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in chronic hemodialysis patients demonstrated that 20 mg/kg doses significantly increased pre-dialysis (trough) levels from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks of therapy. While levocarnitine successfully increases plasma concentrations, the definitive impact on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been fully determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) when mixed in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s in PVC plastic bags (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription Only Medicine).
