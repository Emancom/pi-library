# Editorial Product Information: Propylthiouracil

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Propylthiouracil for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for clinical reference and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the Australian PI was provided; therefore, information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., US, European, Canadian) is noted as "Not specified in available sources" where applicable.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Propylthiouracil (Chemical class: Thiouracil derivative).
- **Brand Name(s):** Halycil [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** H03BA02 (Antithyroid preparations – Thiouracils).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Tablet:** 50 mg circular, biconvex, white tablet [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Halewood Chemicals Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hyperthyroidism:** Indicated for the treatment of hyperthyroidism in adults (including the elderly) who meet the following criteria [Australian]:
    - Patients who are intolerant of methimazole or carbimazole.
    - Patients for whom surgery or radioactive iodine therapy is not considered an appropriate treatment option.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
Dosage must be individualised based on the severity of the hyperthyroidism and the patient's clinical response. The objective is to use the lowest effective dose to maintain a euthyroid state and avoid inducing hypothyroidism.

- **Initial Dose:** 150 mg to 300 mg daily, administered in divided doses (typically every 8 hours). In severe cases, up to 500 mg per day may be required until the patient is euthyroid [Australian].
- **Maintenance Dose:** Once controlled (usually after 1–2 months), the dose is typically reduced to 50 mg to 150 mg daily [Australian].
- **Duration:** Treatment should be maintained for the shortest duration possible [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Doses should not be doubled to compensate for a missed dose.
- Caution is advised when switching between different brands of propylthiouracil due to a lack of comparability data [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to propylthiouracil or any excipients (e.g., lactose) [Australian].
- **Paediatrics:** Use in paediatric patients is contraindicated due to the risk of severe hepatotoxicity [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Serious Warnings
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Severe, potentially fatal hepatic reactions and cases requiring liver transplant have been reported in both adults and children. Onset typically occurs within the first six months. Treatment must be discontinued immediately if significant hepatic enzyme abnormalities develop [Australian].
- **Agranulocytosis:** A potentially life-threatening reaction typically occurring within the first few months of therapy. Patients must report sore throat, fever, or mouth ulcers immediately [Australian].
- **ANCA-Associated Vasculitis (AAV):** Use is associated with antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibodies (ANCA) positivity. Severe cases may involve pulmonary haemorrhage or renal failure [Australian].

### General Precautions
- **Haematological Monitoring:** Propylthiouracil can adversely affect prothrombin and platelets. Full blood count (FBC) and coagulation profiles should be monitored [Australian].
- **Thyroid Function:** Regular monitoring of TSH and thyroid hormones is required to prevent overdosage and subsequent hypothyroidism [Australian].
- **Sugar Intolerance:** Contains lactose; not recommended for patients with rare hereditary galactose intolerance, total lactase deficiency, or glucose-galactose malabsorption [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most serious reactions are hepatotoxicity, agranulocytosis, and systemic vasculitis.

| Frequency | Adverse Event(s) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (≥10%) | Urticaria, Pyrexia |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Leukopenia, Neutropenia, Gastrointestinal disturbances (nausea, vomiting), Taste disturbances, Rash, Abnormal liver function tests |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Aplastic anaemia, Agranulocytosis, Thrombocytopenia, Headache, Bleeding, Vasculitis, Stomach pain, Liver disorders (hepatitis, hepatomegaly) |
| **Rare** (0.01–0.1%) | Pancytopenia, Hypoprothrombinaemia, Dizziness, Paraesthesia, Hearing impairment, Interstitial pneumonia, Jaundice, Alopecia, SLE |
| **Very Rare** (<0.01%) | Haemolysis, Asthma, Arthralgia, Acute renal failure |
| **Not Known** | Liver failure, Pulmonary haemorrhage, Sepsis, ANCA-positivity, Polymyositis |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Theophylline, Digoxin, and Beta-blockers:** As the patient moves from a hyperthyroid to a euthyroid state, the clearance of these drugs may decrease, necessitating a dose reduction [Australian].
- **Anticoagulants (e.g., Warfarin):** Propylthiouracil may inhibit Vitamin K activity, potentially increasing the effect of oral anticoagulants. Enhanced monitoring of PT/INR is required [Australian].
- **Radio-iodine ($^{131}$I) Therapy:** Propylthiouracil may have a radioprotective effect. It should be withdrawn 2–4 days prior to radio-iodine treatment to avoid reducing the therapy's effectiveness [Australian].
- **Agranulocytosis-Inducing Meds:** Concurrent use increases the risk of haematological toxicity [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category D [Australian]:** Known to cause or suspected of causing human foetal malformations.
    - **1st Trimester:** May be preferred over methimazole if antithyroid therapy is essential, due to the higher risk of congenital anomalies associated with methimazole during organogenesis.
    - **2nd/3rd Trimester:** Switching to an alternative may be considered due to the risk of maternal hepatotoxicity from propylthiouracil.
    - **Monitoring:** Use the lowest effective dose; high doses can cause foetal goitre and hypothyroidism. Monitor thyroid function every 4–6 weeks [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Transfers into breast milk (approx. 10% of maternal serum concentration). Neonatal development and infant thyroid function must be closely monitored [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Hyperthyroidism reduces fertility in both sexes; treatment with propylthiouracil typically results in the normalisation of fertility/sperm count as thyroid function is controlled [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** **Contraindicated** due to the risk of liver failure [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Dose selection should be cautious, starting at the lower end of the range, reflecting the higher frequency of decreased hepatic/renal function [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dose adjustments based on GFR [Australian]:
    - **GFR 10–50 mL/min:** Administer 75% of the standard dose.
    - **GFR < 10 mL/min:** Administer 50% of the standard dose.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Caution is required; a reduced dose should be considered [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits the synthesis of thyroid hormones (T4 and T3) by interfering with the incorporation of iodine into tyrosyl residues (mediated by peroxidase inhibition). It also inhibits the peripheral conversion of T4 to the more active T3 [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Clinical effects are delayed (3–4 weeks) until preformed hormone stores are depleted [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Rapid; peak levels in ~1 hour. Bioavailability 50–75% due to first-pass metabolism.
    - **Distribution:** ~80% protein bound; crosses placenta; enters breast milk.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily glucuronidation via UGT1A9.
    - **Elimination:** Half-life of 1–3 hours; excreted in urine as glucuronic acid conjugates [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C.
- **Conditions:** Protect from light; store in the original container.
- **Shelf Life:** 4 years [Australian].
