# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (Food and Drug Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the local approved PI for full legal and prescribing compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; frequency should never be less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically remain in the 50 mg/kg range, adjusted based on clinical response. The maximum recorded dose is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels <40 µmol/L to 50 µmol/L. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and do not re-administer without careful risk-benefit assessment and close monitoring [US].
- **Seizures:** Seizures have been reported in patients with and without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to therapy and monitor weekly or monthly. Monitoring should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma free carnitine levels (target range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulations):** Safety of oral levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5%)
In clinical trials of ESRD patients on dialysis, the following events occurred at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) [US]:
- **General:** Abdominal pain (21%), accidental injury (12%), asthenia (12%), chest pain (15%), fever (12%), flu syndrome (29%), headache (37%), infection (24%), injection site reaction (38%), pain (35%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (21%), hypotension (19%), tachycardia (9%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (35%), vomiting (21%), nausea (12%).
- **Nervous:** Dizziness (18%), paresthesia (12%).
- **Respiratory:** Cough increase (18%), pharyngitis (27%).

### Other Reported Effects
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea, vomiting, and body odor have been observed [US].
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. Monitoring of INR is recommended after initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine in patients on warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned/specified in updated labelling]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal constituent of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. In dairy cows, carnitine concentrations in milk increase following administration. Weigh the benefits of maternal treatment against the potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is based on mg/kg body weight, similar to adults [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** See **Section 5 (Warnings and Precautions)** regarding the accumulation of metabolites with oral dosing. IV levocarnitine is removed by dialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also facilitates the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, IV administration increases plasma carnitine concentrations. Trough levels typically stabilise or increase over 8–24 weeks of therapy [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg post-dialysis), mean plasma levocarnitine increased from 20 µmol/L (pre-dialysis) to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) showed dose-proportional increases in trough levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
