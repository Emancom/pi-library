# Editorial Product Information: Pitavastatin

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial overview was developed using the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of pitavastatin clinical and regulatory data for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not replace the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing within a particular region. Clinical practice should always be guided by the current approved labeling in the prescriber's jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pitavastatin calcium (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor).
- **Brand Names:** Not explicitly named in the source text, though distributed/marketed by Upsher-Smith and GSMS, Inc. [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Tablets (Oral):** 1 mg, 2 mg, and 4 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Distributed by Upsher-Smith Laboratories, LLC; Marketed by Golden State Medical Supply, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Primary Hyperlipidemia:** As an adjunct to diet in adults to reduce low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) [US].
- **Heterozygous Familial Hypercholesterolemia (HeFH):** As an adjunct to diet to reduce LDL-C in adults and pediatric patients aged 8 years and older [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The recommended range is 2 mg to 4 mg once daily [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** 4 mg once daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** Take orally once daily at the same time each day, with or without food [US].
- **Monitoring and Titration:**
    - Assess LDL-C levels as early as 4 weeks after initiation or titration; adjust dosage based on clinical response [US].
    - For patients failing to reach LDL-C goals on the 4 mg maximum dose or those requiring high-intensity statin therapy, alternative treatments should be considered [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Concomitant Cyclosporine Use:** Significantly increases pitavastatin exposure [US].
- **Hepatic Disease:** Active liver failure or decompensated cirrhosis [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to pitavastatin or any inactive tablet excipients (e.g., angioedema, rash, urticaria) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Myopathy and Rhabdomyolysis:** Risk is dose-dependent and increased in patients $\ge$ 65 years, those with uncontrolled hypothyroidism, or renal impairment. 
    - Pitavastatin should be discontinued if CK levels become markedly elevated or if myopathy is suspected/diagnosed [US]. 
    - Therapy should be temporarily withheld during acute/serious conditions predisposing to renal failure (e.g., sepsis, shock, major surgery, trauma) [US].
- **Immune-Mediated Necrotizing Myopathy (IMNM):** Rare autoimmune myopathy reported with statin use; characterized by proximal muscle weakness and elevated CK persisting despite statin discontinuation [US].
- **Hepatic Dysfunction:** Persistent increases in serum transaminases may occur. Liver enzyme testing should be performed before initiation and as clinically indicated thereafter [US].
- **Endocrine Effects:** Increases in HbA1c and fasting serum glucose levels have been reported [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common Adverse Effects ($\ge$ 2%):** Myalgia, constipation, diarrhea, back pain, and pain in extremity [US].
- **Clinical Laboratory Abnormalities:** Elevated CK, transaminases, alkaline phosphatase, bilirubin, and glucose [US].
- **Postmarketing Reports (Frequency not established):**
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Abdominal pain, nausea, dyspepsia.
    - **Neurological:** Peripheral neuropathy, dizziness, hypoesthesia, rare reports of cognitive impairment (reversible), and new-onset or exacerbated myasthenia gravis.
    - **Psychiatric:** Insomnia, depression.
    - **Respiratory:** Interstitial lung disease.
    - **Reproductive:** Erectile dysfunction.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Rhabdomyolysis with acute kidney injury, fatal hepatic failure (rare), and angioedema [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Cyclosporine:** Contraindicated; increases pitavastatin AUC by 4.6-fold [US].
- **Erythromycin:** Limits pitavastatin dose to a **maximum of 1 mg once daily** [US].
- **Rifampin:** Limits pitavastatin dose to a **maximum of 2 mg once daily** [US].
- **Gemfibrozil:** Concomitant use should be avoided due to increased risk of myopathy [US].
- **Other Fibrates/Niacin ($\ge$ 1 g/day):** Use with caution; weigh benefits against the increased risk of myopathy [US].
- **Colchicine:** Increased risk of myopathy reported [US].
- **Antiretrovirals:** 
    - Atazanavir: Increases pitavastatin exposure.
    - Darunavir/Ritonavir or Lopinavir/Ritonavir: May slightly decrease pitavastatin exposure [US].
- **Warfarin:** No significant pharmacokinetic or pharmacodynamic (INR/PT) interaction [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Risk Summary:** May cause fetal harm based on the mechanism of action (decreased cholesterol synthesis necessary for fetal development). Discontinue pitavastatin if pregnancy is recognized unless the clinical need for the mother outweighs the potential risk to the fetus [US].
    - **Data:** Human observational studies have not identified a drug-associated risk of major malformations [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. There is no data on pitavastatin presence in human milk, but other statins are known to pass into milk, and there is potential for serious adverse reactions in infants [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal data showed decreased implantations and decreased fetal viability in rabbits at high doses (30x human exposure) [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for Heterozygous Familial Hypercholesterolemia (HeFH) in patients **8 years and older**. Safety/efficacy not established in patients < 8 years or for other types of hyperlipidemia [US].
- **Elderly Use ($\ge$ 65 years):** No overall difference in efficacy compared to younger patients, but age is a recognized risk factor for statin-associated myopathy [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Moderate/Severe (eGFR 15–59 mL/min/1.73m²) and ESRD on Hemodialysis:** Recommended starting dose is **1 mg once daily**; maximum dose is **2 mg once daily** [US].
    - **Mild impairment:** No dosage adjustment recommended [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in active liver failure or decompensated cirrhosis. Pitavastatin exposure (AUC) is 3.8-fold higher in moderate impairment (Child-Pugh B) [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Competitive inhibitor of HMG-CoA reductase, the rate-limiting enzyme in cholesterol biosynthesis. This increases LDL-receptor expression on hepatocytes, enhancing LDL uptake from the circulation [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak plasma concentration ($C_{max}$) reached in ~1 hour. Absolute bioavailability is 51%. A high-fat meal decreases $C_{max}$ by 43% but does not significantly affect AUC [US].
    - **Distribution:** >99% protein bound (mainly albumin) [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via glucuronidation (UGT1A3 and UGT2B7) to form pitavastatin lactone. Minimal CYP450 metabolism (marginal 2C9 and 2C8) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 79% excreted in feces and 15% in urine. Mean plasma half-life is ~12 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Primary Hyperlipidemia (Adults):**
    - **Vs. Atorvastatin:** Non-inferiority demonstrated; 2 mg and 4 mg pitavastatin resulted in LDL-C reductions of -38% and -45% respectively, compared to -38% (10 mg) and -44% (20 mg) for atorvastatin [US].
    - **Vs. Simvastatin:** Non-inferiority demonstrated; 2 mg and 4 mg pitavastatin resulted in LDL-C reductions of -39% and -44% respectively [US].
    - **Geriatric Patients:** In comparison with pravastatin, pitavastatin demonstrated significantly greater LDL-C reductions at all dose levels [US].
- **Paediatric HeFH:** A 12-week trial in patients 8–16 years showed dose-dependent LDL-C reductions (1 mg: -21%; 2 mg: -30%; 4 mg: -38%) compared to placebo (-1%) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from light. Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container with a child-resistant closure [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only (Prescription only) [US].
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** Not specified in available sources (e.g., Australian S4 classification is not mentioned in the US document).
