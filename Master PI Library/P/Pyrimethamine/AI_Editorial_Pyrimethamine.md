# Editorial Product Information: Pyrimethamine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States** (Food and Drug Administration/NorthStar Rx LLC).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a concise reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidance required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pyrimethamine (a folic acid antagonist).
- **Chemical Class:** 5-(4-chlorophenyl)-6-ethyl-2, 4-pyrimidinediamine.
- **Brand Names:** Noted generically as **Pyrimethamine Tablets, USP** [US].
- **ATC Code:** P01BD01 (Antiprotozoal).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Scored 25 mg tablets [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** NorthStar Rx LLC (Manufactured by APL Healthcare Limited) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Toxoplasmosis:** Indicated for the treatment of toxoplasmosis when administered concurrently with a sulfonamide, due to the synergistic effect of the combination [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Initial Dose:** 50 mg to 75 mg daily, combined with 1 g to 4 g daily of a sulfapyrimidine-type sulfonamide (e.g., sulfadoxine) [US].
- **Duration:** This regimen is typically maintained for 1 to 3 weeks, depending on clinical response and patient tolerance [US].
- **Maintenance:** Following the initial phase, the dose for both drugs may be reduced by half and continued for an additional 4 to 5 weeks [US].

### Paediatric Dosing
- **Initial Dose:** 1 mg/kg/day divided into two equal daily doses [US].
- **Duration/Reduction:** After 2 to 4 days, the dose may be reduced to 0.5 mg/kg/day and continued for approximately one month [US].
- **Co-administration:** Use in conjunction with standard paediatric sulfonamide dosing [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Mitigating GI Effects:** Anorexia and vomiting may be minimised by taking the medication with meals [US].
- **Folinic Acid Supplementation:** Concurrent administration of folinic acid (leucovorin) is strongly recommended for all patients to mitigate bone marrow suppression [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to pyrimethamine or any component of the formulation [US].
- **Haematologic Disorders:** Documented megaloblastic anaemia secondary to folate deficiency [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Therapeutic Index:** Pyrimethamine possesses a narrow therapeutic window for the treatment of toxoplasmosis [US].
- **Folate Deficiency:** If signs of folate deficiency occur, the dose should be reduced or the drug discontinued. Folinic acid (5–15 mg daily) should be administered until normal haematopoiesis returns [US].
- **Carcinogenicity:** Data in humans (two case reports) suggest a potential link to chronic granulocytic leukaemia or reticulum cell sarcoma following long-term use (14–24 months). Significant increases in lung tumours have been observed in murine models [US].
- **Pediatric Safety:** Pyrimethamine must be kept out of reach of children; accidental ingestion has resulted in fatalities [US].

### Precautions
- **Convulsive Disorders:** A small "starting" dose is recommended in patients with a history of seizures to avoid potential neurotoxicity [US].
- **Organ Impairment:** Use with caution in patients with impaired renal or hepatic function [US].
- **High-Risk Populations:** Caution is required in patients with pre-existing folate deficiency (e.g., alcoholism, malabsorption syndrome, or pregnancy) and those taking medications that affect folate levels (e.g., phenytoin) [US].
- **Patient Monitoring:** Patients should be advised to stop the medication and seek immediate medical attention if a skin rash, sore throat, pallor, purpura, or glossitis develops [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- In patients receiving high dosages, semiweekly blood counts (including platelets) should be performed [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often dose-related and may be exacerbated by concurrent sulfonamide therapy.

| System Organ Class | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Haematologic** | Megaloblastic anaemia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, pancytopenia, neutropenia. |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Anorexia, vomiting, atrophic glossitis. |
| **Dermatologic/Immune** | Hypersensitivity reactions (occasionally severe): Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), erythema multiforme, anaphylaxis. |
| **Respiratory** | Pulmonary eosinophilia (reported rarely). |
| **Other** | Hyperphenylalaninemia, haematuria, cardiac rhythm disorders. |

*Note: Serious haematologic effects may occur even at low doses in sensitive individuals [US].*

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Antifolic Agents:** Concomitant use with other antifolic drugs or myelosuppressive agents (e.g., sulfonamides, trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, proguanil, zidovudine, methotrexate) increases the risk of bone marrow suppression [US].
- **Lorazepam:** Mild hepatotoxicity has been reported when administered concurrently with lorazepam [US].
- **Quinine/Antibiotics:** Pyrimethamine may be used with quinine and other antimalarials or antibiotics [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not assigned a letter category in this PI]. Teratogenic effects (cleft palate, microphthalmia, etc.) have been demonstrated in rats, hamsters, and pigs at 2.5 to 5 times the human dose. Use only if the potential benefit justifies the risk to the foetus. Concurrent folinic acid is strongly recommended [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Pyrimethamine is excreted in human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants, a decision should be made whether to discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the drug's importance to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** Information on impairment of fertility is not specified beyond the mutagenicity data [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Highly susceptible to adverse effects from overdose. Refer to Section 3 for specific toxoplasmosis dosing [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects over 65 to determine if they respond differently. Dosing should generally start at the low end of the range, reflecting the higher frequency of hepatic, renal, or cardiac dysfunction [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution; specific dosage adjustments are not provided in available sources [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A folic acid antagonist that selectively inhibits nucleic acid precursors in *Toxoplasma gondii*. Its activity is significantly enhanced (synergised) when used with sulfonamides [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Well absorbed with peak levels occurring 2 to 6 hours post-administration [US].
    - **Distribution:** 87% bound to human plasma proteins [US].
    - **Elimination:** Eliminated slowly with a plasma half-life of approximately 96 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Pivotal clinical trial data are not detailed in the provided PI. Reference is made to historical studies (Eyles and Coleman, 1953; Jacobs et al., 1964) establishing the synergistic effect of pyrimethamine and sulfonamides in animal models of toxoplasmosis [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Keep in a dry place and protect from light [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources (refer to local jurisdiction for specific scheduling) [US].
