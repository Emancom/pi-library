# Editorial Product Information: Pyrazinamide

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of pyrazinamide, synthesized for use by healthcare professionals. 
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference; it is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the Australian PI was provided for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pyrazinamide (pyrazinoic acid amide or pyrazine carboxylamide)
- **Chemical Class:** Antituberculous agent; pyrazine derivative
- **Brand Name(s):** Pyrazinamide-AFT [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** J04AK01
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 500 mg tablets (white to off-white, round, scored) [Australian]
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AFT Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Tuberculosis:** Treatment of active, drug-sensitive tuberculosis caused by *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* in patients older than 12 years of age [Australian].
- **Combination Therapy:** Must be used in conjunction with other effective antituberculosis agents, typically during the initial 2-month intensive phase of treatment [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Children >12 years)
- **Daily Regimen:** 15 to 30 mg/kg administered once daily [Australian].
- **Maximum Dose:** While older regimens allowed higher limits, current recommendations generally advise a maximum of 3 g per day. Note that CDC guidelines suggest a more conservative maximum of 2 g per day for daily regimens [Australian].
- **Twice Weekly Regimen:** 50 to 75 mg/kg based on lean body weight to facilitate outpatient compliance. In clinical evaluations, doses exceeding 3 g twice weekly have been used without a reported increase in adverse reactions [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Pyrazinamide should be administered with at least one other effective antituberculous medication. Its use in combination does not require dose modification of the companion agents [Australian].

### Special Instructions
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to pyrazinamide or any excipients in the formulation [Australian].
- Pre-existing hepatic disease [Australian].
- Hyperuricaemia and/or acute gouty arthritis [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Pyrazinamide can cause severe hepatic damage. It should only be used where close clinical observation and laboratory monitoring are available [Australian].
- **Hepatocellular Damage:** Therapy must be permanently discontinued if signs of hepatocellular injury appear [Australian].
- **Gouty Arthritis:** If hyperuricaemia is accompanied by acute gouty arthritis, therapy must be discontinued and not reinstated [Australian].

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Baseline Testing:** Liver function tests (specifically ALT and AST) and serum uric acid levels must be determined prior to initiating therapy [Australian].
- **Periodic Monitoring:** Laboratory testing should be repeated at regular intervals throughout treatment and immediately if clinical symptoms of dysfunction occur [Australian].
- **Susceptibility Testing:** Pre-treatment in vitro sensitivity tests of *M. tuberculosis* cultures should be performed [Australian].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Diabetes Mellitus:** Use with caution, as management of diabetes may become more difficult during therapy [Australian].
- **Patient Education:** Patients should be advised to report symptoms such as fever, malaise, anorexia, nausea, vomiting, dark urine, jaundice, or joint pain immediately [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** 
    - Hepatotoxicity (10–25%) [Australian].
    - Hyperuricaemia (frequent, often asymptomatic) [Australian].
    - Nongouty polyarthralgia (up to 40%) [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):**
    - Jaundice (2–3%) [Australian].
    - Mild arthralgia and myalgia [Australian].
- **Rare (<0.1%):**
    - Acute yellow atrophy of the liver and death [Australian].
    - Thrombocytopenia, sideroblastic anaemia (with erythroid hyperplasia), and vacuolation of erythrocytes [Australian].
    - Interstitial nephritis and dysuria [Australian].
    - Photosensitivity (often with reddish-brown skin discolouration), maculopapular rash, and acne [Australian].
    - Porphyria [Australian].
- **Frequency Not Defined:**
    - Gastrointestinal: Nausea, vomiting, anorexia, and aggravation of peptic ulcers [Australian].
    - Hypersensitivity: Rash, urticaria, and pruritus [Australian].
    - Other: Fever, splenomegaly, and liver tenderness [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Probenecid:** Known to inhibit the renal excretion of pyrazinamide [Australian].
- **Xanthine Oxidase Inhibitors (e.g., Allopurinol):** May increase plasma concentrations of pyrazinamide and its active metabolite (pyrazinoic acid), as they are metabolised via xanthine oxidase [Australian].
- **Hepatic Transporters:** Pyrazinamide may inhibit BSEP and OATP1A1 (based on rat studies), potentially reducing the elimination of drugs cleared via these pathways [Australian].
- **Uricosuric Agents:** Concurrent administration may help reduce pyrazinamide-induced hyperuricaemia [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B2.** 
    - There are no well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Animal studies (mice) showed no embryofetal abnormalities at high doses. Use only if the potential benefit outweighs the risk to the fetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Pyrazinamide is excreted in breast milk in small amounts. If treatment is deemed essential, the patient should discontinue nursing [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats and mice have shown significant disruption of endocrine balance, oxidative stress in reproductive organs, and reduced fertility indexes at subclinical or high doses [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for patients >12 years of age. There is limited clinical information for younger children; use with caution and only when essential for TB control [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Reduction in dose size and/or frequency is recommended in the presence of renal insufficiency [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with hepatic disease [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Pyrazinamide is a prodrug converted into its active form, pyrazinoic acid (POA), by the enzyme pyrazinamidase/nicotinamidase (encoded by the *pncA* gene in *M. tuberculosis*). It is primarily active at acidic pH. Potential mechanisms include cell envelope stress responses and inhibition of fatty acid synthesis [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Readily absorbed from the GI tract; peak serum levels reached at approximately 2 hours [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Widely distributed to liver, lungs, and CSF. It is excreted in breast milk [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic hydrolysis to pyrazinoic acid, subsequently hydroxylated to 5-hydroxypyrazinoic acid [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Mainly via glomerular filtration. Approximately 70% of a dose is recovered in urine within 24 hours (mostly as metabolites) [Australian].
    - **Half-life:** 9–10 hours in normal renal/hepatic function; prolonged in impairment [Australian].
