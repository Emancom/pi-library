# Editorial Product Information: Pramipexole

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically accurate editorial overview for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing in a particular region. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pramipexole dihydrochloride monohydrate.
- **Chemical Class:** Non-ergot dopamine agonist; aminobenzothiazole derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** **APO-PRAMIPEXOLE** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N04BC05.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Oral tablets: 0.125 mg, 0.25 mg, and 1 mg [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Southern Cross Pharma Pty Ltd (Distributed by Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd) [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Parkinson's Disease:** Treatment of the signs and symptoms of idiopathic Parkinson's disease. It may be used as monotherapy or as adjunctive therapy in combination with levodopa [Australian].
- **Restless Legs Syndrome (RLS):** Symptomatic treatment of primary Restless Legs Syndrome [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Parkinson's Disease
**Titration Schedule:**
Dosing should start low and increase gradually every 5 to 7 days to achieve maximum therapeutic effect without intolerable side effects. The daily dose is administered in three equally divided doses [Australian].
- **Week 1:** 0.125 mg three times daily (0.375 mg total/day).
- **Week 2:** 0.25 mg three times daily (0.75 mg total/day).
- **Week 3:** 0.5 mg three times daily (1.5 mg total/day).
- **Further Titration:** If necessary, increase by 0.75 mg/day at weekly intervals to a maximum of 4.5 mg/day [Australian].

**Maintenance:** 0.375 mg to 4.5 mg daily. In advanced disease, doses >1.5 mg/day may allow for a reduction in levodopa dosing [Australian].

### Restless Legs Syndrome (RLS)
**Titration Schedule:**
Administer once daily, 2 to 3 hours before bedtime [Australian].
- **Initial Dose:** 0.125 mg once daily.
- **Titration:** If needed, increase every 4 to 7 days.
- **Titration Steps:** 0.125 mg → 0.25 mg → 0.5 mg → 0.75 mg (Max dose) [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets may be taken with or without food, swallowed with water [Australian].

### Treatment Discontinuation
- **Parkinson's Disease:** Taper at a rate of 0.75 mg/day until the dose is 0.75 mg, then reduce by 0.375 mg/day [Australian].
- **RLS:** May be discontinued without tapering, though rebound symptoms may occur in approximately 10% of patients [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to **pramipexole** or any excipients in the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Sudden Onset of Sleep and Somnolence:** **Pramipexole** is associated with somnolence and episodes of sudden sleep onset, sometimes without warning signs or awareness. Patients must be cautioned regarding driving or operating machinery [Australian].
- **Impulse Control Disorders:** Patients and caregivers should be monitored for compulsive behaviours, including pathological gambling, hypersexuality, binge eating, and compulsive shopping. Dose reduction or tapering should be considered if these occur [Australian].
- **Hallucinations and Confusion:** These are known side effects, occurring more frequently in advanced Parkinson's disease when combined with levodopa [Australian].
- **Postural Hypotension:** Especially during dose initiation; blood pressure monitoring is recommended, particularly in patients with severe cardiovascular disease [Australian].
- **Dystonia:** Axial dystonia (e.g., antecollis, camptocormia) has been reported following initiation or dose adjustment [Australian].
- **Retinal Changes:** While animal studies (albino rats) showed retinal degeneration, human clinical trials have not demonstrated similar evidence over 2.5–4 years of treatment [Australian].
- **Augmentation in RLS:** Long-term use in RLS may result in earlier onset of symptoms in the evening/afternoon and spread to other extremities [Australian].
- **Drug Withdrawal Syndrome:** Tapering may cause apathy, anxiety, depression, fatigue, sweating, and pain. These symptoms do not typically respond to levodopa [Australian].
- **Melanoma:** Epidemiological data suggest Parkinson's patients have a higher risk of melanoma; regular skin monitoring is advised [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Parkinson’s Disease
- **Very Common (>10%):** Nausea, constipation, somnolence, dizziness, dyskinesia (advanced disease), postural hypotension (advanced disease), accidental injury (advanced disease) [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Hallucinations, confusion, insomnia, peripheral oedema, amnesia, dystonia, visual abnormalities, urinary frequency [Australian].

### Restless Legs Syndrome
- **Very Common (>10%):** Nausea, headache [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Fatigue, somnolence, dizziness, constipation, diarrhoea, dry mouth, influenza [Australian].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Cardiac Failure:** Pharmaco-epidemiological studies suggest an increased risk of cardiac failure associated with **pramipexole** use [Australian].
- **Rhabdomyolysis:** A single case has been reported in a patient with advanced Parkinson's disease [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Levodopa:** **Pramipexole** increases the Cmax of levodopa by approximately 40%. It is recommended to reduce the levodopa dose during **pramipexole** titration [Australian].
- **Renal Tubular Secretion Inhibitors:** Basic (cationic) drugs that inhibit this pathway (e.g., cimetidine, diltiazem, quinidine, quinine, ranitidine, triamterene, verapamil, digoxin, procainamide, trimethoprim) may reduce the clearance of **pramipexole**. Consider dose reduction of **pramipexole** if co-administered [Australian].
- **Dopamine Antagonists:** Antipsychotics (phenothiazines, butyrophenones) and metoclopramide may diminish **pramipexole** efficacy; concurrent use is not recommended [Australian].
- **CNS Depressants and Alcohol:** Possible additive somnolent effects; caution is advised [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category B3.** Studies in rats showed embryotoxicity at maternotoxic doses. There are no adequate studies in pregnant women. Use only if potential benefit outweighs risk [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. **Pramipexole** inhibits prolactin secretion and is excreted in the milk of rats. If use is unavoidable, breastfeeding should be discontinued [Australian].
- **Fertility:** In rats, high doses inhibited nidation and prolonged oestrus cycles due to prolactin reduction. No human data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in patients under 18 years of age [Australian].
- **Elderly:** Clearance is approximately 30% lower in patients ≥65 years, primarily due to age-related renal decline. Hallucination risk is increased in the elderly [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **CrCl >50 mL/min:** No dose reduction [Australian].
    - **CrCl 20–50 mL/min:** Parkinson's initiation dose 0.125 mg twice daily (0.25 mg/day). Max 2.25 mg/day [Australian].
    - **CrCl <20 mL/min:** Parkinson's initiation dose 0.125 mg once daily. Max 1.5 mg/day [Australian].
    - **RLS:** Not studied in RLS patients with renal impairment [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Dose reduction is likely unnecessary as 90% of the drug is renally excreted; however, it has not been specifically investigated [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A non-ergot dopamine agonist with high selectivity for D2-subfamily receptors and preferential affinity for D3 receptors. It alleviates Parkinsonian motor deficits by stimulating striatal dopamine receptors [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid; bioavailability >90%. Tmax is ~2 hours (delayed 1 hour by food) [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Extensive (Vd ~500 L); 15% protein bound. Distributes into erythrocytes [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Minimal biotransformation [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (90% as unchanged drug). Elimination half-life is ~8 hours in young healthy adults and ~12 hours in the elderly [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Parkinson's Disease:** Pivotal studies using the UPDRS scale showed significant improvements in activities of daily living (Part II) and motor performance (Part III). In advanced disease, **pramipexole** significantly reduced "off" time and allowed for levodopa dose reduction [Australian].
- **Restless Legs Syndrome:** Efficacy demonstrated in 12-week trials using the IRLS Rating Scale and CGI-I. Sustained efficacy was shown in a 9-month maintenance study where 85% of placebo-switched patients failed treatment vs 21% of those continuing **pramipexole** [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Protection:** Protect from light [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **S4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
