# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine (Intravenous)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on provided Product Information from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a clinical synthesis intended for reference and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.
- Information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian, Japanese) was not provided in the source materials; where information is absent across all provided sources, it is noted as "Not specified in available sources."

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule for fatty acid transport).
- **Brand Name(s):** Not specified in available sources [US: marketed as Levocarnitine Injection].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
  - **Injection (Solution):** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metabolic Disorders:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:**
  - Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
  - In severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose may be given, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours).
  - Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
  - Recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
  - Administration: Slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
  - Dose adjustments: May be reduced (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect parenteral products for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives.

### Monitoring
- **Baseline:** Obtain plasma carnitine concentrations before initiating parenteral therapy.
- **Routine:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations.
- **Target:** Normal plasma free carnitine levels are between 35 and 60 µmol/L.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate appropriate medical care.
- **Monitoring Requirements:** In patients with ESRD, trough levocarnitine levels should be monitored. Therapy initiation may be prompted by levels below 40 to 50 µmol/L.
- **Metabolic Accumulation:** While not evaluated for the IV form, chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in the urine [US].

### Serious and Other Notable Reactions
- **Neurologic:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea, vomiting, gastritis, and body odor have been noted.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned a letter category in the provided source, but noted that reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after administration. The risks of excess intake by the infant should be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation.
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy are supported by use in the treatment of inborn errors of metabolism (secondary carnitine deficiency). See Dosage and Administration for dosing guidance.
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in provided sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for ESRD patients on hemodialysis. Note that the safety of the *oral* formulation has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency, and high oral doses may lead to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
- **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours.
- **Dialysis:** Levocarnitine is easily removed from plasma by hemodialysis.

### Pharmacodynamics
In ESRD patients, IV administration increases plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Normal trough concentrations (40–50 µmol/L) are typically exceeded within one week of starting therapy at 20 mg/kg three times weekly.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic/Dose-Ranging Studies:** In ESRD patients receiving 10, 20, or 40 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis, trough concentrations increased in a dose-dependent manner. After 24 weeks, mean trough concentrations were 148 ± 50 µmol/L (10 mg/kg), 240 ± 60 µmol/L (20 mg/kg), and 456 ± 162 µmol/L (40 mg/kg) [US].
- **Note:** While IV levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the effect on clinical outcomes or specific signs/symptoms of deficiency in this population has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) at room temperature (25°C).
