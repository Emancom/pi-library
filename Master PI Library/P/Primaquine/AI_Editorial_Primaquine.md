# Editorial Product Information: Primaquine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory information regarding **primaquine**.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active ingredient:** Primaquine phosphate (an 8-aminoquinoline antimalarial).
- **Brand name(s):** **Primacin** [Australian].
- **ATC code:** P01BA03.
- **Dosage form(s) and strength(s):** 
    - Tablets: 7.5 mg primaquine base (as 13.2 mg primaquine phosphate) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Boucher & Muir Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Radical Cure (Prevention of Relapse):** Prevention of relapses in malaria caused by *Plasmodium vivax* and *Plasmodium ovale*.
- **Gametocytocidal Therapy:** Adjunctive treatment to reduce gametocytemia caused by *Plasmodium falciparum* in patients residing in malaria-receptive areas.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Standard Radical Treatment:** 15 mg once daily for 14 days.
- **Resistant Strains:** Up to 30 mg daily for 14 days may be required in areas with resistant strains or where lower-dose therapy has failed.
- **Regional Variation (WHO Advice):** For radical cure in most of South East Asia and the Pacific regions, a treatment period of 21 days is recommended [Australian/WHO].
- **G6PD Deficient Patients:** For patients with known glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency, a regimen of up to 45 mg once weekly for 8 weeks is suggested, provided close monitoring for haemolysis is maintained.
- **P. falciparum Gametocyte Reduction:** A single dose of 45 mg.

### Administration Instructions
- **Primaquine** should be taken with food to minimise gastrointestinal side effects.

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to **primaquine**, other 8-aminoquinolines, or any excipients (e.g., lactose monohydrate).
- Severe G6PD deficiency.
- Pregnancy [Australian].
- Acute illness involving serious systemic disease characterised by a tendency toward granulocytopenia (e.g., active rheumatoid arthritis, systemic lupus erythematosus).
- Concurrent use of other potentially haemolytic medications or agents that depress myeloid elements of the bone marrow.
- Concurrent use or recent use of quinacrine [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- None listed in provided sources.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Haemolytic Anaemia and G6PD Deficiency:** **Primaquine** can cause severe haemolytic anaemia in G6PD-deficient individuals. G6PD testing must be performed prior to administration. Due to test limitations, a residual risk of haemolysis remains even with "normal" results.
- **Methaemoglobinaemia:** Risk is increased in individuals with NADH methaemoglobin reductase deficiency. Discontinue if signs of methaemoglobinaemia (e.g., cyanosis) appear.
- **Cardiac Effects:** Potential for QT interval prolongation. Monitor ECG in patients with cardiac disease, long QT syndrome, electrolyte imbalances (hypokalaemia/hypomagnesaemia), bradycardia (< 50 bpm), or those taking concurrent QT-prolonging agents.
- **Blood Monitoring:** Routine blood cell counts and haemoglobin determinations are advisable during therapy.
- **Lactose Intolerance:** Tablets contain lactose; use with caution in sensitive patients.

### Monitoring Requirements
- Baseline haematocrit and haemoglobin.
- Close haematological monitoring (e.g., at day 3 and day 8) in patients with mild-to-moderate G6PD deficiency or where G6PD status is unknown but treatment is deemed necessary.
- Immediate discontinuation if dark urine or a sudden drop in haemoglobin or leukocyte count occurs.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often dose-related, particularly gastrointestinal symptoms.

| System Organ Class | Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Common | Abdominal cramps/pain, nausea, vomiting, epigastric distress. |
| **Nervous System** | Common | Dizziness, headache. |
| **Blood and Lymphatic** | Frequency Not Stated | Haemolytic anaemia (especially in G6PD deficiency), methaemoglobinaemia, leukopenia. |
| **Blood and Lymphatic** | Rare (High Dose) | Neutropenia, agranulocytosis (observed at 120 mg/day). |
| **Cardiac** | Frequency Not Stated | Cardiac arrhythmia, QT interval prolongation. |
| **Skin** | Frequency Not Stated | Rash, pruritus. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Quinacrine:** Potentiates the toxicity of **primaquine**; concurrent or recent use is contraindicated.
- **Proguanil:** Interaction not assessed *in vivo*, but other 8-aminoquinolines have shown 5- to 10-fold increases in concentration when combined.
- **Bone Marrow Depressants/Haemolytic Agents:** Must not be administered concurrently with **primaquine**.
- **QT-Prolonging Agents:** Use with caution; requires ECG monitoring.
- **Ketoconazole:** Reduced metabolism of **primaquine** in *in vitro* studies; *in vivo* effects are not fully established.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category D**. Safe use has not been established. **Primaquine** is contraindicated during pregnancy as the fetus may be G6PD deficient even if the mother is not.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety data are unavailable. It is unknown if **primaquine** is excreted in human milk. Given the risk of serious adverse effects in nursing infants (especially potential haemolysis), a decision must be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug.
- **Fertility:** No data available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - Radical cure: 0.3 mg/kg/day for 14 days.
    - *P. falciparum* gametocyte reduction: 0.7 to 1.0 mg/kg as a single dose.
- **Elderly Use:** Dosing should be cautious, typically starting at the lower end of the range due to increased frequency of hepatic, renal, or cardiac impairment.
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution; specific dosing adjustments not provided in source.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution; specific dosing adjustments not provided in source.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** An antimalarial schizontocide targeting the hypnozoite (liver) stage of *P. vivax* and *P. ovale*. It also acts against primary exoerythrocytic stages of *P. falciparum* and is highly effective against gametocytes, thereby disrupting malaria transmission.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly and almost completely absorbed (Tmax ~2 hours).
    - **Distribution:** Widely distributed (Vd 260–300 L).
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly metabolised in the liver. The primary metabolite is carboxyprimaquine, which accumulates during a 14-day course.
    - **Elimination:** Elimination half-life ranges from 4.3 to 7.4 hours.
