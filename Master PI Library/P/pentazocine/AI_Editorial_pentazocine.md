# Editorial Product Information: Pentazocine and Naloxone

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a structured reference.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulations and the current official PI for their region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Pentazocine hydrochloride (equivalent to 50 mg base) and Naloxone hydrochloride (equivalent to 0.5 mg base).
- **Chemical Class:** Pentazocine is a member of the benzomorphan (benzazocine) series; Naloxone is an opioid antagonist.
- **Brand Names:** Pentazocine and Naloxone Tablets, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** N02AF01 (Pentazocine, combinations).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Oral tablets containing 50 mg pentazocine HCl and 0.5 mg naloxone HCl [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Actavis Pharma, Inc. (Teva Pharmaceuticals) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Pain Management:** Indicated for the management of pain severe enough to require an opioid analgesic and for which alternative treatment options (e.g., non-opioid analgesics) are inadequate, not tolerated, or otherwise insufficient [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Initial Dose:** 1 tablet every three to four hours as needed for pain.
- **Titration:** Dosage should be titrated based on individual patient response and the lowest effective dose should be used for the shortest duration.
- **Maximum Dose:** The dosage may be increased to 2 tablets when necessary; however, the total daily dose must not exceed 12 tablets [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets are for oral use only. 
- Due to the presence of naloxone, which acts as an antagonist if administered parenterally, these tablets are designed to discourage misuse by injection [US].

### Discontinuation and Tapering
- Do not abruptly discontinue in physically dependent patients. Gradually taper the dose (e.g., 10% to 25% reduction every 2 to 4 weeks) to avoid withdrawal symptoms [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Significant respiratory depression [US].
- Acute or severe bronchial asthma in unmonitored settings or without resuscitative equipment [US].
- Known or suspected gastrointestinal obstruction, including paralytic ileus [US].
- Hypersensitivity to pentazocine, naloxone, or any excipients [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
- **Addiction, Abuse, and Misuse:** Use exposes patients to the risk of opioid addiction, which can lead to overdose and death. Assess risk prior to prescribing and monitor regularly.
- **Life-Threatening Respiratory Depression:** Serious or fatal respiratory depression may occur, particularly during treatment initiation or following dose increases.
- **Accidental Ingestion:** Even one dose, especially by children, can result in fatal overdose.
- **Risks from Concomitant Use with Benzodiazepines or CNS Depressants:** May result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death.
- **Neonatal Opioid Withdrawal Syndrome (NOWS):** Prolonged use during pregnancy can result in life-threatening withdrawal in the neonate.

### General Warnings
- **Opioid-Induced Hyperalgesia and Allodynia:** May occur if pain increases despite dose escalation [US].
- **Adrenal Insufficiency:** Reported with opioid use, typically following >1 month of therapy [US].
- **Severe Hypotension:** Risk of orthostatic hypotension and syncope in ambulatory patients [US].
- **Seizure Disorders:** Pentazocine may increase seizure frequency in patients with a history of seizure disorders [US].
- **Acute CNS Manifestations:** Hallucinations, disorientation, and confusion have been reported at therapeutic doses; these typically clear spontaneously within hours [US].
- **Biliary Surgery:** While opioids generally increase biliary pressure, pentazocine may have a lesser effect, though clinical significance is unclear [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Note: Frequencies are often not precisely specified in available source documents.*

- **Very Common (>10%):** Nausea, vomiting, lightheadedness, dizziness, sedation, sweating [US].
- **Common (1–10%):** Constipation, headache, euphoria, visual blurring [US].
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Respiratory depression, agranulocytosis (usually reversible), anaphylaxis, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis [US].
- **Frequency Not Specified:** 
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension, tachycardia, syncope.
    - **CNS:** Hallucinations, disorientation, disturbed dreams, insomnia, tremors, miosis.
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea, dry mouth, abdominal distress, biliary tract spasm.
    - **Dermatologic/Allergic:** Pruritus, rash, urticaria, facial edema.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Benzodiazepines and other CNS Depressants (including Alcohol):** Potentiates respiratory depression and sedation. Reserve for cases where alternatives are inadequate [US].
- **Serotonergic Drugs:** Increased risk of serotonin syndrome when used with SSRIs, SNRIs, TCAs, triptans, or MAOIs [US].
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** May cause CNS excitation and hypertension. Avoid use in patients who have taken MAOIs within the last 14 days [US].
- **Mixed Agonist/Antagonist and Partial Agonist Opioids:** (e.g., buprenorphine, butorphanol). May reduce the analgesic effect or precipitate withdrawal [US].
- **Muscle Relaxants:** May enhance neuromuscular blocking action and increase respiratory depression [US].
- **Diuretics:** Opioids may reduce diuretic efficacy by inducing antidiuretic hormone release [US].
- **Anticholinergic Drugs:** Increased risk of urinary retention and severe constipation [US].
- **Tobacco:** Smoking may increase the metabolic clearance of pentazocine, potentially reducing clinical effectiveness [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specifically lettered in current labeling, but carries a boxed warning for Neonatal Opioid Withdrawal Syndrome (NOWS). 
    - Animal data: Pentazocine has shown embryo-fetal toxicity (neural tube defects in hamsters) at doses 2.6 times the maximum human dose [US].
- **Labor and Delivery:** Not recommended immediately prior to or during labor; opioids cross the placenta and may cause neonatal respiratory depression [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Pentazocine is excreted in human milk. Monitor infants for excess sedation and respiratory depression. Withdrawal can occur in the infant if maternal use is stopped [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 12 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased sensitivity to adverse effects; start at the low end of the dosing range. Monitor renal function as pentazocine is substantially excreted by the kidneys [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Extensive liver disease decreases pentazocine metabolism; use with caution to avoid side effect accentuation [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution; drug is primarily excreted by the kidneys [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
- **Pentazocine:** A mixed agonist-antagonist. It acts as a partial agonist at the mu-opioid receptor and an agonist at the kappa-opioid receptor [US].
- **Naloxone:** A pure opioid antagonist. When taken orally at the 0.5 mg dose, it has no pharmacologic activity but is included to antagonize the effects of pentazocine if the product is crushed and injected [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Well absorbed from the GI tract.
- **Onset of Action:** 15 to 30 minutes.
- **Duration of Action:** Usually 3 hours or longer.
- **Peak Plasma Time:** Approximately 1.7 hours (range 0.5 to 4 hours).
- **Metabolism:** Metabolized in the liver via oxidation and glucuronidation.
- **Elimination:** Approximately 60% excreted in urine within 24 hours. Mean plasma half-life is 3.6 hours (range 1.5 to 10 hours) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Pivotal trial data for the specific combination are not detailed in the available PI beyond pharmacokinetic studies in normal volunteers [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15° to 30°C (59° to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container. Secure storage is required to prevent accidental ingestion or diversion [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as a **Schedule IV** controlled substance (Rx-only) [US].
