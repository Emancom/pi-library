# Editorial Product Information: Phenylpropanolamine Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of phenylpropanolamine hydrochloride (veterinary) for use by healthcare professionals (veterinarians and pharmacists). 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing or dispensing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Phenylpropanolamine Hydrochloride (PPA).
- **Chemical Class:** Sympathomimetic amine (chemically related to ephedrine).
- **Brand Name:** Phenylpropanolamine Hydrochloride Chewable Tablets [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Scored, chicken-flavored chewable tablets available in 25 mg, 50 mg, and 75 mg strengths [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Manufactured by Zydus Animal Health and Investments Limited; Distributed by ZyVet Animal Health Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Urinary Incontinence (Canine):** Specifically indicated for the control of urinary incontinence associated with urethral sphincter hypotonus in dogs [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Dogs):** The recommended dosage is 2 mg/kg (0.91 mg/lb) of body weight administered twice daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets are scored to allow for accurate dosing; calculations should be made in half-tablet increments.
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** If a dose is missed, it should be administered as soon as remembered. If the next scheduled dose is near, the missed dose should be skipped to resume the regular schedule. Do not double the dose [US].
- **Clinical Onset:** It may require several days of consistent therapy before a clinical improvement in urinary incontinence is observed [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Not specified in available sources. (Note: While no absolute contraindications are listed under a specific heading, see **Section 5** for critical patient exclusions and cautionary uses).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
- **Human Safety:** Not for human use. Keep out of reach of children. In case of accidental human ingestion, immediate medical consultation is required [US].
- **Hypertension Risk:** PPA may cause significant increases in blood pressure. It should be used with extreme caution in dogs with pre-existing hypertension or conditions predisposed to high blood pressure [US].
- **Cardiovascular and Organ Disease:** Use with caution in patients with heart disease, kidney insufficiency, liver disease, diabetes, or glaucoma [US].
- **Incontinence Differential Diagnosis:** 
    - PPA is not effective for incontinence caused by neurological disease or structural malformations.
    - Use in dogs with urinary tract infections (UTI) may mask clinical symptoms of the infection [US].
- **Monitoring:** Periodic monitoring of blood pressure and protein levels in urine (proteinuria) is recommended, particularly in dogs with underlying cardiovascular or renal concerns.
- **Storage and Overdose Prevention:** Due to the flavored nature of the tablets, dogs may seek out and consume large quantities if the bottle is accessible. Overdose has occurred when dogs chewed through closed bottles. Store in a secured location out of animal reach [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects were evaluated in a 28-day placebo-controlled study and a 6-month open-label study [US].

| Adverse Reaction | Frequency (PPA-Treated) | Frequency (Placebo) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Emesis (Vomiting)** | 20.3% | 8.2% |
| **Hypertension (≥ 160 mmHg)** | 19.5% | 14.7% |
| **Anorexia** | 16.3% | 3.3% |
| **Weight Loss (> 5%)** | 16.1% | 6.8% |
| **Proteinuria** | 13.0% | 8.2% |
| **Anxiety/Aggression/Behavior Change** | 9.7% | 3.2% |
| **Polydipsia (Increased Thirst)** | 6.5% | 9.8% |
| **Lethargy** | 5.7% | 1.6% |

- **Serious and Post-Approval Events:** 
    - **Neurologic:** Seizures, ataxia, tremors, confusion, and disorientation.
    - **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, bradycardia, arrhythmias, and collapse.
    - **Renal/Urinary:** Renal failure, hematuria, and urinary retention.
    - **Fatalities:** Sudden death and brain hemorrhage (subarachnoidal and intraventricular) have been reported in rare cases.
    - **Other:** Elevated liver enzymes (ALT, ALP), mydriasis, and erythema [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Sympathomimetics:** Use with other sympathomimetic drugs may result in increased toxicity.
- **Antidepressants:** Caution is advised when co-administered with Tricyclic Antidepressants (TCAs) or Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs) due to the risk of exaggerated sympathomimetic effects.
- **Anesthetics:** Use with halogenated gaseous anesthetics may increase the risk of cardiac arrhythmias.
- **Aspirin:** Laboratory studies on human blood suggest PPA may potentiate decreased platelet aggregation when used with aspirin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy/Breeding:** The safety of PPA has not been evaluated in dogs intended for breeding, or in pregnant or lactating bitches [US].
- **Fertility:** Data not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Geriatric Use:** While not explicitly categorized, use with caution due to the higher prevalence of underlying heart or kidney disease in older dogs.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution; specific dosing adjustments are not provided in the source documentation [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** PPA is an $\alpha$-adrenergic agent. It is believed to stimulate the release of norepinephrine, which acts on $\alpha$ and $\beta$-adrenergic receptors of the smooth muscle to increase the tone of the urethra, bladder neck, and internal urethral sphincter.
- **Pharmacokinetics (Dogs):** 
    - **Absorption:** Rapid following oral administration. Bioavailability from immediate-release capsules is approximately 98%.
    - **Elimination:** The terminal half-life averages 3.5 ± 0.5 hours following intravenous administration.
    - **Formulation Nuance:** Controlled-release formulations exhibit biphasic absorption (an initial rapid phase followed by a second phase lasting over 16 hours) [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Increases urethral pressure and tone to prevent involuntary urine leakage.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **28-Day Effectiveness Study:** A multi-site US study of 184 dogs demonstrated that PPA (2 mg/kg BID) significantly reduced the mean number of urinary accidents per week compared to placebo. 
- **6-Month Safety Study:** Long-term administration in 157 dogs showed continued effectiveness with 98.1% owner satisfaction. One dog with pre-existing heart disease developed systolic failure, though the relationship to treatment was unclear [US].
- **Target Animal Safety Studies:** Healthy Beagles were administered up to 5 times the recommended dose for 26 weeks. Findings included dose-dependent increases in blood pressure and decreased heart rate. At 3X and 5X doses, some dogs exhibited gallop heart sounds and anxious behavior [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at controlled room temperature between 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Excursions:** Permitted between 15°C to 40°C (59°F to 104°F) [US].
- **Safety Note:** Store in a secure, tamper-proof location to prevent accidental ingestion by pets.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as a Prescription Animal Drug (Rx-only). Restricted to use by or on the order of a licensed veterinarian [US].
