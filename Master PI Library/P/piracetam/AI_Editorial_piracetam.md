# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical and regulatory data to provide a concise reference for prescribing and administration.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical guide; it is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine [(R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt].
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection solution: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism leading to secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing maintenance haemodialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often utilised in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Maintenance:** Dosing frequency should be every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
- **Dose Range:** Subsequent daily doses typically range from 50 mg/kg; the highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations (normal range: 40 µmol/L to 50 µmol/L). Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on clinical response and plasma levels [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal oedema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, particularly in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate risks before re-administration [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed following levocarnitine administration [US].
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation):** Note that the safety of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal failure; chronic high doses may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD)
The following events occurred at a frequency of ≥5% in placebo-controlled trials, regardless of causality [US]:

| Body System | Adverse Effects (≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting, dyspepsia |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcaemia, hypervolaemia, peripheral oedema |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, depression |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnoea, rhinitis |
| **Skin** | Pruritus |

### Other Reported Effects
- **Common (<10% but reported):** Body odour, gastritis, and transient nausea/vomiting.
- **Serious:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal oedema, bronchospasm, and seizures (postmarketing) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. 
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels at treatment initiation and following any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified by letter, but reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8x human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm]. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies in nursing mothers have not been conducted. Levocarnitine is present in dairy cow milk. A risk-benefit assessment should weigh the importance of treatment to the mother against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing guidelines are included under the general Metabolic Disorders section; safety and efficacy are established based on clinical use [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** See **Section 3: Dosage and Administration** for specific ESRD dialysis protocols.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule essential for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for β-oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Carnitine deficiency is biochemically defined as plasma concentrations <20 µmol/L at one week post-term [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound (including albumin).
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) and γ-butyrobetaine.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, IV levocarnitine (20 mg/kg post-dialysis) significantly increased plasma carnitine levels from 20 µmol/L to trough levels of 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg in ESRD patients demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
