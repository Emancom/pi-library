# Editorial Product Information: Prazosin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis has been compiled based on the official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of prazosin for use by healthcare professionals. 
- This document serves as a clinical resource and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 
- Note: At the time of this publication, only the Australian PI was provided for review. Information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., US, European, Canadian) is marked as "not specified in available sources."

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Prazosin hydrochloride
- **Chemical Class:** Quinazoline derivative; selective post-synaptic alpha-1 adrenoceptor antagonist.
- **Brand Names:** APO-Prazosin [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** C02CA01
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Tablets (Scored): 1 mg, 2 mg, and 5 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypertension:** Treatment of hypertension of various etiologies and severities. It may be used as monotherapy or as part of a multi-agent antihypertensive regimen [Australian].
- **Congestive Heart Failure (CHF):** Treatment of severe refractory CHF; used as adjunctive therapy when patients are unresponsive to conventional treatment with diuretics and cardiac glycosides [Australian].
- **Raynaud's Phenomenon and Raynaud's Disease:** Symptomatic treatment [Australian].
- **Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (BPH):** Adjunctive treatment for urinary obstruction in patients awaiting surgery [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing Principles
Therapy should be initiated at a low dose to enhance patient tolerability. Doses should be titrated based on the individual patient’s blood pressure and clinical response.

### Hypertension
- **Initial Dose:** 0.5 mg twice daily for three days, then increased to 1.0 mg two or three times daily for an additional three days [Australian].
- **Titration:** Increase gradually based on response. Optimal response may take up to six weeks.
- **Maintenance Dose:** Typically 3.0 mg to 20 mg daily in divided doses [Australian].

### Congestive Heart Failure
- **Initial Daily Dose:** 0.5 mg increasing to 4.0 mg in divided doses [Australian].
- **Recumbent Patients:** Start with 0.5 mg three or four times daily.
- **Maintenance Dose:** 4.0 mg to 20 mg daily in divided doses [Australian].
- **Note:** Close medical supervision and hemodynamic monitoring are recommended during titration, especially in severely ill or decompensated patients.

### Raynaud’s Phenomenon/Disease
- **Initial Dose:** 0.5 mg twice daily for 3 to 7 days [Australian].
- **Maintenance Dose:** 1 mg to 2 mg twice daily [Australian].

### Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia
- **Initial Dose:** 0.5 mg twice daily for 3 to 7 days [Australian].
- **Maintenance Dose:** 2 mg twice daily. Doses exceeding 4 mg daily have not been studied for BPH [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- If adding a diuretic or other antihypertensive to an existing prazosin regimen, the prazosin dose should be reduced to 1 mg or 2 mg twice daily and then retitrated [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to prazosin, other quinazolines, or any excipients in the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Syncope:** Prazosin can cause sudden loss of consciousness, typically due to excessive postural hypotension (the "first-dose effect"). This usually occurs 30 to 90 minutes after the initial dose or following rapid dose escalation. Incidence is approximately 1% with initial doses of ≥2 mg.
- **Priapism:** Alpha-1 blockers are associated with prolonged erections and priapism. Immediate medical intervention (within 4 hours) is required to prevent permanent tissue damage [Australian].
- **Intraoperative Floppy Iris Syndrome (IFIS):** Observed during cataract surgery in patients currently or previously treated with alpha-1 blockers. Surgeons should be informed of past or current use [Australian].
- **Angina:** Use with caution in patients with ischemic heart disease; prazosin may exacerbate angina [Australian].
- **CHF Obstruction:** Not recommended for heart failure caused by mechanical obstructions (e.g., aortic/mitral valve stenosis, pulmonary embolism) [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Both standing and supine blood pressure should be monitored during initiation and titration [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects
| System Organ Class | Adverse Effect (Frequency) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Cardiovascular** | Postural hypotension (14%), palpitations (5%), oedema (4%) |
| **Nervous System** | Headaches (8%), drowsiness (8%), dizziness/faintness |
| **General** | Lack of energy (7%), weakness (7%), asthenia (7%) |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea (5%), dry mouth (4%) |
| **Ocular/Respiratory**| Blurred vision (4%), Nasal congestion (4%) |

### Serious or Less Common Reactions
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia (1%), syncope (1%), bradycardia, vasculitis, angina pectoris, myocardial infarction (causal relationship not established) [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Vomiting (3%), constipation (3%), diarrhoea (2%), liver function abnormalities, pancreatitis.
- **Genitourinary:** Urinary incontinence, impotence, urinary frequency.
- **Psychiatric/CNS:** Depression (2%), nervousness (2%), hallucinations, paraesthesia, tinnitus, worsening of narcolepsy.
- **Dermatological:** Rash/pruritus (1%), alopecia, lichen planus, urticaria.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **PDE-5 Inhibitors:** Concomitant use with sildenafil, tadalafil, or vardenafil may lead to symptomatic hypotension. Use with caution [Australian].
- **Antihypertensives:** Additive hypotensive effects occur when combined with diuretics, beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors, or calcium channel blockers. Initial prazosin doses must be low when these are added [Australian].
- **Laboratory Interferences:** May cause false-positive results in screening tests for phaeochromocytoma (urinary VMA and MHPG) [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category B2. Safety in human pregnancy is not established. Use only when the potential benefit outweighs the risk to the fetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Small amounts of prazosin are excreted in breast milk. Use with caution; consider the benefit to the mother versus the risk to the infant [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed impairment of fertility at high doses (75 mg/kg/day). Human studies on sperm morphology and 17-ketosteroid excretion showed no significant drug-related effects [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for children under 12 years of age as safety and efficacy have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Higher risk of dangerous postural hypotension, particularly in those with cerebrovascular disease. Close monitoring of blood pressure is required, especially in BPH patients [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Prazosin does not appear to further compromise renal function. However, as some patients are highly sensitive, initiation should start at 0.5 mg daily with cautious titration [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Prazosin is primarily metabolised by the liver. Patients with impaired hepatic function may require lower doses [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selective competitive antagonism of post-synaptic alpha-1 adrenoceptors, resulting in peripheral vasodilation of both resistance vessels (arterioles) and capacitance vessels (veins).
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Reduces total peripheral resistance and blood pressure in both supine and standing positions. Unlike non-selective alpha-blockers, it typically does not cause reflex tachycardia. In CHF, it reduces left ventricular filling pressure and impedance while increasing cardiac output [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Peak plasma levels in 1–2 hours (2.5 hours in CHF). Bioavailability is increased in CHF.
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised in the liver via demethylation and conjugation.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily via bile and faeces. Plasma half-life is 2–3 hours (increased to ~7 hours in CHF) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Summary:** The source document notes that specific pivotal trial data was not available for review within the PI text. Clinical use is based on established hemodynamic and antihypertensive data [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Conditions:** Protect from light [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].
