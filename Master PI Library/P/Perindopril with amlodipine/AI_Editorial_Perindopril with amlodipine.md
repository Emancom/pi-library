# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Prescriber Notice:** This is an editorial synthesis for clinical reference and does not substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (generic); Carnitor [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL).
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Doses should be administered every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    - **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported administered dose is 300 mg/kg.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - **Administration:** Slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### Monitoring
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations before initiating parenteral therapy.
- Conduct weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma concentrations.
- **Target Range:** Plasma free carnitine concentration should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
- Use only clear, colorless solutions.
- Discard any unused portion as the product contains no preservatives.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have occurred, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions manifest within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur.
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** Safety of oral levocarnitine has not been established in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted renally [US].

### Serious/Specific Reactions
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (receiving either oral or IV therapy).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; abdominal pain; gastritis.
- **Body Odor:** Noted as a less frequent reaction.
- **Cardiovascular:** Arrhythmia, palpitations, tachycardia, hypotension.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not assigned a lettered category in this source]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine levels following administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impairment found in animal reproductive studies.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (mg/kg). Specific age ranges for approval are not restricted in the provided source for metabolic or ESRD indications.
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** IV administration is indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. High-dose oral therapy is cautioned against due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This provides the necessary substrate for β-oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated.

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not protein bound. Distribution half-life is approximately 0.585 hours.
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
- **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours.
- **Dialysis:** Levocarnitine is easily removed from plasma by hemodialysis.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Study:** In 15 healthy volunteers, oral tablets and solutions were bioequivalent, though absolute bioavailability was low (~15%).
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg post-dialysis showed that trough carnitine levels increased significantly (from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L) over 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Evaluated 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses in ESRD patients, showing dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations. Note: While carnitine levels increased, the effect on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population was not determined in these trials.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion after opening.
