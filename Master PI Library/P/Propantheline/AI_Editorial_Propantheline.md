# Editorial Product Information: Propantheline Bromide

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)** regulatory authority. 
- At the time of this publication, supplementary documents from other jurisdictions (e.g., US FDA, European EMA, Health Canada) were not provided for comparison. Consequently, the clinical recommendations reflect the current approved status and evidence base within the Australian jurisdiction.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Propantheline bromide.
- **Chemical Class:** Quaternary ammonium antimuscarinic agent.
- **Brand Names:** **Pro-Banthine** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A03AB05.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 15 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Peptic Ulcer:** Adjunctive therapy for the treatment of gastric and duodenal ulcers [Australian].
- **Genitourinary Disorders:** Treatment of neurogenic bladder and urinary incontinence associated with detrusor hyperactivity [Australian].
- **Hyperhidrosis:** Management of excessive sweating [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing
- **Peptic Ulcer:** The standard regimen is 15 mg (one tablet) taken three times daily, 30 minutes before meals, and 30 mg (two tablets) at bedtime. The total daily dose is typically 75 mg. Dosage should be titrated to individual response, often until dryness of the mouth occurs, though a slightly lower dose is considered optimal. The maximum daily dose must not exceed 120 mg [Australian].
- **Other Indications (Neurogenic Bladder, Hyperhidrosis):** 15 mg to 30 mg, administered four times daily [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets should be taken 30 minutes before meals to optimise absorption, as food significantly decreases the extent of absorption [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
Propantheline bromide is contraindicated in the following conditions [Australian]:
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to propantheline bromide, other anticholinergics, or excipients (including soya bean and lactose).
- **Ocular:** Glaucoma (risk of mydriasis and increased intra-ocular pressure).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Obstructive disease (e.g., pyloroduodenal stenosis, achalasia, paralytic ileus), intestinal atony (elderly or debilitated patients), toxic megacolon, and hiatal hernia associated with reflux oesophagitis.
- **Genitourinary:** Obstructive uropathy (prostatism) and prostatic enlargement.
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia secondary to cardiac insufficiency or thyrotoxicosis; unstable cardiovascular status in acute haemorrhage.
- **Neuromuscular:** Myasthenia gravis.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
- **General Cardiovascular Caution:** Use with extreme caution in patients with severe cardiac disease, coronary heart disease, congestive heart failure, cardiac arrhythmias, hypertension, or mitral stenosis, as increased heart rate may exacerbate these conditions [Australian].
- **Respiratory Risk:** Use cautiously in chronic lung disease; reduced bronchial secretions may lead to the formation of bronchial plugs [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal Precautions:** 
    - Diarrhoea in patients with ileostomy or colostomy may be a sign of incomplete obstruction; therapy should be avoided.
    - Caution in ulcerative colitis due to the risk of paralytic ileus and toxic megacolon.
    - Caution in gastrointestinal infections (e.g., *C. difficile*) as decreased motility may prolong infection by retaining toxins.
- **Autonomic & Neurological:** Caution in patients with autonomic neuropathy and Down’s syndrome.
- **Environmental Temperature:** Risk of hyperthermia and heat stroke due to suppressed sweating (anhidrosis) in patients with fever or those exposed to high ambient temperatures [Australian].
- **Excipients:** Contains lactose and sugars; contraindicated in patients with rare hereditary galactose/fructose intolerance, lactase deficiency, or glucose-galactose malabsorption [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally dose-related and reversible upon discontinuation.

| System Organ Class | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Cardiovascular** | Palpitations, arrhythmias, bradycardia (transient) followed by tachycardia. |
| **Neurological** | Drowsiness, dizziness, headache, insomnia, nervousness, mental confusion, weakness. |
| **Ophthalmic** | Blurred vision, mydriasis, cycloplegia, increased ocular tension, loss of accommodation, light sensitivity. |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Xerostomia (dry mouth), nausea, vomiting, constipation, bloatedness, loss of taste. |
| **Genitourinary** | Urinary hesitancy and retention, impotence. |
| **Dermatological** | Anhydrosis (decreased sweating), flushing, dry skin, urticaria, rashes, allergic dermatitis, anaphylaxis. |
| **Respiratory** | Reduced bronchial secretions. |
| **Other** | Thirst, difficulty swallowing/talking, suppression of lactation. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Absorption Alterations:** Antimuscarinics delay gastric emptying. 
    - **Reduced Absorption:** Antacids and absorbent antidiarrhoeals reduce propantheline absorption; space doses by 2–3 hours. Ketoconazole absorption is reduced due to increased gastric pH [Australian].
    - **Enhanced Absorption:** Digoxin (tablet form) and nitrofurantoin absorption may be increased [Australian].
    - **Delayed/Reduced Absorption:** Paracetamol absorption is delayed and retarded [Australian].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Additive Antimuscarinic Effects:** Increased risk of toxicity when combined with:
    - Belladonna alkaloids, antihistamines, tricyclic antidepressants, MAOIs, phenothiazines, disopyramide, procainamide, quinidine, amantadine, and memantine [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal Motility:** Propantheline antagonises the effects of prokinetics like metoclopramide and domperidone. Combined use with opioids increases the risk of severe constipation and paralytic ileus [Australian].
- **Miscellaneous:** 
    - **Levodopa:** May decrease levodopa absorption via increased gastric metabolism.
    - **Nitrates:** May reduce the efficacy of sublingual nitrates due to dry mouth (impaired dissolution).
    - **Potassium Chloride:** Increased risk of GI lesions, especially with wax-matrix preparations.
    - **Corticosteroids:** Increased risk of elevated intra-ocular pressure [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B2** [Australian]. Safe use has not been established. Cohort data suggests a possible association with minor malformations. Use only if the potential benefits outweigh risks to the foetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety data is limited. While propantheline is poorly lipid-soluble and incompletely absorbed, caution is advised. Anticholinergic agents may suppress lactation [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No human data available; animal studies on fertility have not been established [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of inducing urinary retention and precipitating undiagnosed glaucoma. Caution is required; patients should be advised to void at the time of dosing [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution; specific dosing adjustments are not provided in available sources, but monitoring is recommended [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Competitive inhibitor of acetylcholine at postganglionic parasympathetic effector sites and autonomic ganglia. It reduces gastric acid secretion, decreases GI motility, and possesses spasmolytic properties [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly but incompletely absorbed. Peak plasma concentration (total C-14 material) at 6 hours. Food significantly reduces absorption.
    - **Distribution:** Does not readily cross the blood-brain barrier or enter the eye due to high ionisation and low lipid solubility.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised by hydrolysis to inactive metabolites; ~50% occurs in the GI tract before absorption.
    - **Elimination:** Elimination half-life is 1.3 to 2.1 hours. Approximately 70% of a dose is excreted in urine, mostly as metabolites; only 5% of an oral dose is excreted as unchanged drug [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Summary:** The provided source document does not contain specific pivotal trial data for modern regulatory review; approval is based on long-standing clinical use [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Container:** Supplied in HDPE bottles with child-resistant closures [Australian].
