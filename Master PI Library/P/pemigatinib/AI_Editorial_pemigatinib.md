# Editorial Product Information: Pemigatinib

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Regulatory Agencies:** Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA — Australia); Food and Drug Administration (FDA — United States).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the official Product Information (PI) for **pemigatinib** across the noted jurisdictions. It is intended for use by healthcare professionals as a clinically accurate, unified reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI. Clinicians must consult the local approved PI before prescribing.

---

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pemigatinib (a small molecule kinase inhibitor of FGFR1, 2, and 3).
- **Brand Name:** **Pemazyre**.
- **ATC Code:** L01EX14.
- **Dosage Form:** Immediate-release, uncoated tablets.
- **Strengths:** 
    - 4.5 mg (Round, white/off-white, debossed with "I" and "4.5").
    - 9 mg (Oval, white/off-white, debossed with "I" and "9").
    - 13.5 mg (Round, white/off-white, debossed with "I" and "13.5").
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Sponsor/Manufacturer:** 
    - [TGA]: Specialised Therapeutics Alim Pty Ltd.
    - [FDA]: Incyte Corporation.

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Cholangiocarcinoma:** Treatment of adults with previously treated, unresectable locally advanced or metastatic cholangiocarcinoma with a fibroblast growth factor receptor 2 (**FGFR2**) fusion or other rearrangement.
    - *Note:* This indication is approved under provisional [TGA] or accelerated [FDA] pathways based on objective response rate (ORR) and duration of response (DOR).
- **Myeloid/Lymphoid Neoplasms (MLNs):** Treatment of adults with relapsed or refractory MLNs with an **FGFR1** rearrangement [FDA-approved; not specified in available TGA source].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 Standard Dosing
- **Cholangiocarcinoma:** 13.5 mg once daily for 14 consecutive days, followed by 7 days off (21-day cycle).
- **Myeloid/Lymphoid Neoplasms (MLNs):** 13.5 mg once daily on a continuous basis [FDA].
- **Duration of Therapy:** Continue until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.

### 3.2 Administration Instructions
- Take at approximately the same time every day.
- Swallow tablets whole; do not crush, chew, split, or dissolve.
- May be taken with or without food.

### 3.3 Missed Dose and Vomiting
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed by 4 hours or more, skip the dose and resume at the next scheduled time.
- **Vomiting:** If vomiting occurs after taking a dose, do not take an additional tablet; resume with the next scheduled dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to pemigatinib or any excipients [TGA, FDA].
- **Specific Drug Interactions:** Concomitant use with **St John’s wort** is strictly contraindicated [TGA].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Ocular Toxicity:**
    - **Retinal Pigment Epithelial Detachment (RPED) / Serous Retinal Detachment:** Can occur in approximately 5% [TGA] to 11% [FDA] of patients. 
    - **Monitoring:** Comprehensive ophthalmological examination (including OCT) is required at baseline, every 2 months for the first 6 months, and every 3 months thereafter.
    - **Management:** Withhold dose for symptomatic or worsening RPED; resume at a lower dose only if asymptomatic and improved [FDA].
    - **Dry Eye:** Reported in up to 31% of patients. Use ocular demulcents as needed.
- **Hyperphosphataemia and Soft Tissue Mineralisation:** 
    - Increases in serum phosphate are a pharmacodynamic effect of FGFR inhibition (reported in >90% of patients). 
    - Prolonged elevation can lead to calcium-phosphate crystal precipitation, resulting in hypocalcaemia, soft tissue mineralisation (e.g., cutaneous calcification, calcinosis, non-uremic calciphylaxis), muscle cramps, and arrhythmias.
    - **Monitoring:** Monitor serum phosphate levels regularly. 
    - **Management:** Initiate low-phosphate diet if >5.5 mg/dL. Consider phosphate-lowering therapy if >7 mg/dL. Adjust/withhold pemigatinib dose based on the severity of the elevation.
- **Hypophosphataemia:** Severe cases (Grade 3/4) reported in 12–14% of patients. Discontinue phosphate-lowering therapy during treatment breaks or if levels fall below normal.
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Based on animal data, pemigatinib can cause fetal harm. Pregnancy testing is mandatory before initiation.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequencies from the pivotal FIGHT-202 study (N=146/147) are categorised below:

| Frequency | System Organ Class | Adverse Reactions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (>10%) | Metabolism | Hyperphosphataemia (60–94%), hypophosphataemia (23%), decreased appetite, hyponatremia. |
| | Gastrointestinal | Diarrhoea (47%), stomatitis (35%), nausea (40–41%), constipation (35%), dry mouth (34%), vomiting (27%), abdominal pain. |
| | Skin/Tissue | Alopecia (49%), nail toxicity (43–45%), dry skin (20%), palmar-plantar erythrodysaesthesia (15%). |
| | General | Fatigue (42–44%), arthralgia (25%), back pain (20%), peripheral oedema. |
| | Eye | Dry eye (28–35%), blurred vision. |

| **Common** (1–10%) | Eye | Serous retinal detachment, punctate keratitis. |

| **Uncommon** (<1%) | Skin/Tissue | Cutaneous calcification. |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** The most frequent serious reactions include hyponatremia, urinary tract infection, abdominal pain, and acute kidney injury [FDA].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Strong/Moderate CYP3A Inhibitors (e.g., Itraconazole):** Increase pemigatinib exposure. Avoid concomitant use. If unavoidable, reduce pemigatinib dose:
    - 13.5 mg $\rightarrow$ 9 mg.
    - 9 mg $\rightarrow$ 4.5 mg.
- **Strong/Moderate CYP3A Inducers (e.g., Rifampicin):** Significantly decrease pemigatinib exposure, potentially reducing efficacy. Avoid concomitant use.
- **Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs):** [TGA] recommends avoidance, as significant exposure reduction was observed in one-third of patients. [FDA] notes no clinically significant change in overall PK, though individual variability exists.
- **P-gp Substrates:** Pemigatinib inhibits P-gp in vitro. Separate administration of P-gp substrates with a narrow therapeutic index (e.g., digoxin) by at least 6 hours.
- **Renal Transporters:** Pemigatinib inhibits **OCT2** and **MATE1**, which may increase serum creatinine without affecting actual glomerular function.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - [TGA]: **Category D**.
    - [FDA]: Risk Summary indicates potential for fetal harm/loss.
    - **Recommendation:** Do not use during pregnancy unless clinically essential. Effective contraception is required for females and males with partners of childbearing potential during treatment and for **1 week** after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety not established. Advise patients not to breastfeed during treatment and for **1 week** after the last dose.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients <18 years have not been established.
- **Geriatric Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy between patients $\ge$65 years and younger adults. Dose adjustment is not required.
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - Mild to Moderate (eGFR 30–89 mL/min): No dose adjustment.
    - End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) on haemodialysis: No dose adjustment.
    - **Severe (eGFR 15–29 mL/min):** Reduce dose to 9 mg (from 13.5 mg) or 4.5 mg (from 9 mg).
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - Mild or Moderate (Child-Pugh A or B): No dose adjustment.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Reduce dose to 9 mg (from 13.5 mg) or 4.5 mg (from 9 mg).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Pemigatinib is a reversible, small-molecule kinase inhibitor of FGFR1, 2, and 3. It inhibits FGFR phosphorylation and signalling, decreasing cell viability in lines with activating FGFR alterations.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Median $T_{max}$ is 1–2 hours. No clinically meaningful effect from high-fat, high-calorie meals.
    - **Distribution:** 90.6% protein bound (predominantly albumin). $V_d$ is 235 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by **CYP3A4**.
    - **Elimination:** Mean half-life ($t_{1/2}$) is 15.4 hours. Excretion is primarily faecal (82.4%), with 12.6% in urine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **FIGHT-202 (NCT02924376):** A single-arm, multicentre study of 107/108 patients with FGFR2-positive cholangiocarcinoma.
    - **Efficacy:** Objective Response Rate (ORR) was 36% [FDA] to 37% [TGA]. 
    - **Response Duration:** Median DOR was 9.1 months [FDA] or 8.08 months [TGA].
- **FIGHT-203 (NCT03011372):** A single-arm study of 28 patients with FGFR1-rearranged MLNs [FDA].
    - **Efficacy:** Complete Response (CR) rate was 78% for patients with chronic phase in marrow.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [TGA]; 20°C to 25°C (excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C) [FDA].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to expiry date on packaging.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction

- **United States [FDA]:** Prescription Only (Rx-only).
