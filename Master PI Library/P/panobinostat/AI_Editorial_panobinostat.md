# Editorial Product Information: Panobinostat

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of panobinostat for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and must not be used as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information when prescribing or dispensing in a particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Panobinostat (as panobinostat lactate).
- **Chemical Class:** Histone deacetylase (HDAC) inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** **Farydak** [US].
- **ATC Code:** L01XH03.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):**
    - Oral capsules: 10 mg (light green), 15 mg (orange), and 20 mg (red) [US].
    - Note: Strengths are expressed as free base; 10 mg, 15 mg, and 20 mg panobinostat are equivalent to 12.58 mg, 18.86 mg, and 25.15 mg of panobinostat lactate, respectively [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Secura Bio, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Multiple Myeloma:** Indicated in combination with bortezomib and dexamethasone for the treatment of patients with multiple myeloma who have received at least two prior regimens, including bortezomib and an immunomodulatory agent [US-approved].
    - *Editorial Note:* In the United States, this indication received accelerated approval based on progression-free survival. Continued approval may depend on verification of clinical benefit in confirmatory trials [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The recommended starting dose is 20 mg taken orally once every other day for three doses per week (Days 1, 3, 5, 8, 10, and 12) during Weeks 1 and 2 of a 21-day cycle [US].
- **Duration of Therapy:**
    - **Cycles 1–8:** Complete 8 cycles of the 21-day regimen [US].
    - **Cycles 9–16:** For patients showing clinical benefit without unresolved severe toxicity, treatment may continue for an additional 8 cycles (total duration up to 48 weeks) [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Administer at approximately the same time each day, with or without food [US].
    - Capsules must be swallowed whole with water; do not open, crush, or chew [US].
    - Avoid contact with the capsule powder; if contact occurs, wash thoroughly [US].
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** If a dose is missed, it may be taken up to 12 hours after the scheduled time. If vomiting occurs, the patient should not take an additional dose but should resume the schedule with the next planned dose [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Prior to initiation and during therapy, clinicians must monitor Complete Blood Count (CBC), ECG (QTc must be <450 msec at baseline), and serum electrolytes (potassium, magnesium, and phosphate) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None specified in available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Fatal and Serious Toxicities (Severe Diarrhea and Cardiac Toxicities) [US]:**
    - **Severe Diarrhea:** Occurred in 25% of treated patients. Monitor for symptoms, initiate anti-diarrheal treatment promptly, and interrupt or discontinue therapy as necessary.
    - **Cardiac Toxicities:** Severe and fatal cardiac ischemic events, arrhythmias, and ECG changes (including QTc prolongation) have occurred. Arrhythmias are exacerbated by electrolyte imbalances.
- **Hemorrhage:** Fatal and serious gastrointestinal and pulmonary hemorrhages have been reported. Often associated with Grade 3/4 thrombocytopenia [US].
- **Myelosuppression:** Significant neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, and anemia occur frequently. Monitor CBC weekly [US].
- **Infections:** Localized and systemic infections (including pneumonia and sepsis) are common. Do not initiate therapy in patients with active infections [US].
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Liver function abnormalities (elevated transaminases and bilirubin) have occurred. Monitor hepatic function at baseline and during treatment [US].
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Can cause fetal harm. Advise of potential risks and ensure effective contraception [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):**
    - Gastrointestinal: Diarrhea (68%), Nausea (36%), Vomiting (26%), Decreased appetite (28%), Abdominal pain [US].
    - General: Fatigue (60%), Peripheral edema (29%), Pyrexia (26%), Weight decreased [US].
    - Hematologic (Laboratory): Thrombocytopenia (97%), Lymphopenia (82%), Leukopenia (81%), Neutropenia (75%), Anemia (62%) [US].
    - Chemistry (Laboratory): Hypocalcemia (67%), Hypophosphatemia (63%), Hypoalbuminemia (63%), Hypokalemia (52%), Hyponatremia (49%), Increased creatinine (41%) [US].
- **Common (1–10%):**
    - Cardiac: Arrhythmia (12%), Palpitations [US].
    - Vascular: Hypotension, Hypertension [US].
    - Serious Adverse Effects: Pneumonia (18%), Diarrhea (11%), Sepsis (6%) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **Strong CYP3A Inhibitors:** (e.g., ketoconazole, clarithromycin) Increase panobinostat exposure (AUC increased by 73%). **Management:** Reduce panobinostat dose to 10 mg [US].
    - **Strong CYP3A Inducers:** (e.g., rifampin, carbamazepine) Expected to decrease panobinostat exposure by ~70%. **Management:** Avoid concomitant use [US].
    - **CYP2D6 Substrates:** Panobinostat inhibits CYP2D6. Exposure of sensitive substrates (e.g., dextromethorphan, venlafaxine) or those with a narrow therapeutic index (e.g., thioridazine, pimozide) may increase significantly. **Management:** Avoid co-administration if possible; monitor closely [US].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **QT-Prolonging Drugs:** Concomitant use with anti-arrhythmics (e.g., amiodarone, sotalol) or other QT-prolonging agents (e.g., chloroquine, clarithromycin) is not recommended [US].
- **Food Interactions:** Avoid star fruit, pomegranate, and grapefruit products as they inhibit CYP3A enzymes [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not assigned a lettered category in the current US labelling, but stated to cause fetal harm based on animal data (teratogenicity and embryo-fetal lethality in rats and rabbits) [US].
    - **Contraception:** Females of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during and for 3 months after the final dose. Males with partners of reproductive potential should use condoms during and for 6 months after the final dose [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Discontinue breastfeeding during treatment due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies indicate potential for impaired male and female fertility (prostate atrophy, testicular degeneration, and reduced pregnancies) [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in children [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Patients ≥65 years had a higher frequency of adverse events and treatment discontinuation. Monitor closely, particularly for gastrointestinal and cardiac toxicities [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild:** Reduce starting dose to 15 mg [US].
    - **Moderate:** Reduce starting dose to 10 mg [US].
    - **Severe:** Avoid use [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild to severe renal impairment (CrCl <30 mL/min). Not studied in end-stage renal disease (ESRD) or dialysis [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** HDAC inhibitor that inhibits the enzymatic activity of histone deacetylases at nanomolar concentrations. This results in increased acetylation of histone proteins, leading to chromatin relaxation and transcriptional activation, eventually inducing cell cycle arrest or apoptosis in cancer cells [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:**
    - **Cardiac Electrophysiology:** Panobinostat may prolong the QT interval. QTc prolongation >500 msec is more common at doses ≥60 mg [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is ~21%. Tmax is reached within 2 hours. High-fat meals decrease Cmax and AUC slightly (44% and 16% respectively) [US].
    - **Distribution:** Approximately 90% plasma protein bound [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolized via reduction, hydrolysis, oxidation, and glucuronidation. CYP3A accounts for ~40% of hepatic elimination [US].
    - **Elimination:** Excreted in feces (44–77%) and urine (29–51%). Terminal half-life is approximately 37 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Trial (PANORAMA-1):** A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled multicenter study in 768 patients with relapsed multiple myeloma (1–3 prior lines of therapy).
- **Outcome:** In a prespecified subgroup of 193 patients (who received prior bortezomib and an IMiD), the median progression-free survival (PFS) was 10.6 months for the panobinostat arm vs 5.8 months for the placebo arm (HR 0.52; 95% CI: 0.36, 0.76). The overall response rate was 58.5% in the panobinostat group vs 41.4% in the placebo group [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C (59°F–86°F) [US].
- **Protection:** Store in the original carton to protect from light [US].
- **Handling:** Panobinostat is a cytotoxic drug; follow applicable special handling and disposal procedures [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only (Rx-only) [US].
- **Poison/Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
