# Editorial Product Information: Prucalopride

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of prucalopride for healthcare professionals, clinical pharmacists, and medical writers. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Prucalopride (as succinate).
- **Chemical Class:** Selective, high-affinity 5-HT4 receptor agonist (dihydrobenzofurancarboxamide class).
- **Brand Names:** **CALOTRANS** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A03AE04.
- **Dosage Form and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 1 mg (white to off-white, debossed ‘PR1’) and 2 mg (pink, debossed ‘PR2’) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pharmacor Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Functional Constipation:** Indicated for the treatment of chronic functional constipation in adults in whom laxatives have failed to provide adequate relief [Australian].
- **Clinical Requirement for Use:** Before initiating prucalopride, patients must have attempted at least two different types of laxatives from different classes at the highest tolerated doses for at least six months without achieving adequate relief [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Standard Adult Dose:** 2 mg taken once daily [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets may be taken with or without food at any time of the day [Australian].
- **Efficacy Assessment:** If treatment does not show effectiveness within four weeks, the clinical benefit of continuing therapy should be reassessed [Australian].
- **Maximum Recommended Dose:** Due to the specific prokinetic mechanism, doses exceeding 2 mg per day are not expected to increase clinical efficacy [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to prucalopride or any of the inactive excipients [Australian].
- Renal impairment requiring dialysis [Australian].
- Intestinal perforation or obstruction due to structural or functional disorders of the gut wall [Australian].
- Obstructive ileus [Australian].
- Severe inflammatory conditions of the intestinal tract (e.g., Crohn’s disease, ulcerative colitis, toxic megacolon/megarectum) [Australian].
- Recent bowel surgery [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Psychiatric Disorders:** Suicidal ideation, suicide attempts, and completed suicides have been reported. While a causal link is not established, clinicians should monitor for worsening depression or emerging suicidal thoughts. Patients/caregivers should be advised to report mood changes immediately and discontinue the drug if these occur [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Health:** Use with caution in patients with a history of arrhythmias or ischaemic cardiovascular disease. Prucalopride should be used cautiously with medications known to prolong the QTc interval [Australian].
- **Secondary Constipation:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients with secondary causes of constipation (e.g., endocrine, metabolic, or neurologic disorders) or opioid-induced constipation; use in these populations is not recommended [Australian].
- **Renal and Hepatic Function:** Dose adjustments are required for severe impairment (see Section 9).
- **Oral Contraceptives:** In the event of severe diarrhoea during treatment, the efficacy of oral contraceptives may be diminished. Patients should be advised to use an additional contraceptive method [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are most common at the start of therapy and typically resolve within a few days of continued treatment.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Headache (21.6%), Nausea (17.0%), Diarrhoea (12.1%), Abdominal pain (10.3%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Dizziness, vomiting, dyspepsia, flatulence, abnormal bowel sounds, decreased appetite, fatigue, polyuria |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Tremors, migraine, palpitations, vertigo, rectal haemorrhage, fever, malaise |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Rare reports of suicidal behaviour and major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) have been monitored; however, clinical trial data and observational cohort studies did not show a statistically significant increased risk of MACE compared to placebo or PEG 3350 [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** 
    - **P-gp Inhibitors:** Potent inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, verapamil, cyclosporine A, quinidine) may increase prucalopride exposure by approximately 40% [Australian].
    - **Erythromycin:** Co-treatment may increase erythromycin plasma concentrations by 30% [Australian].
    - Prucalopride has a low potential for CYP-mediated interactions and does not significantly inhibit major transporters (OATP, OAT, BSEP) [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **Atropine-like substances:** May reduce the 5-HT4 receptor-mediated effects of prucalopride [Australian].
    - **QTc Prolonging Drugs:** Caution is advised during concomitant use [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B1.** 
    - Human data are limited; spontaneous abortions have been observed in trials (causality unknown). Prucalopride is not recommended during pregnancy. Women of childbearing potential should use effective contraception during treatment [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Prucalopride is excreted in breast milk. While no effects on infants are anticipated at therapeutic doses, use is not recommended during breastfeeding in the absence of more robust human data [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in animal studies at 100 times the clinical exposure [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for children and adolescents under 18 years of age [Australian].
- **Elderly (>65 years):** Initiate at 1 mg once daily; if necessary, the dose may be increased to 2 mg once daily [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - Mild to Moderate: No dose adjustment required.
    - Severe (GFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m²): 1 mg once daily.
    - Dialysis: Contraindicated [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - Mild to Moderate: No dose adjustment required.
    - Severe (Child-Pugh Class C): 1 mg once daily [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Prucalopride is a selective, high-affinity 5-HT4 receptor agonist. It stimulates propulsive gastrointestinal motility, increasing gastric emptying and colonic filling, and reducing small bowel transit time [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed with >90% bioavailability. Peak plasma concentration (Cmax) reached in 2-3 hours [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Extensively distributed (Vd 567 L); plasma protein binding is low (~30%) [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Minor route; the majority of the drug remains unchanged.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (60% excreted unchanged in urine). Terminal half-life is approximately 24 hours. Steady-state is reached within 3–4 days [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** Efficacy was established in three 12-week, multicentre, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials (PRU-INT-6, PRU-USA-11, PRU-USA-13).
- **Primary Endpoint:** Proportion of patients reaching an average of ≥3 spontaneous complete bowel movements (SCBM) per week.
- **Results:** In pooled data, 23.6% of patients on 2 mg prucalopride achieved the primary endpoint vs. 11.3% on placebo at 12 weeks. Clinical improvement (≥1 SCBM/week increase) was seen in 43.1% of patients vs. 24.6% on placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C [Australian].
- Keep in original packaging to protect from moisture.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4 - S4).
