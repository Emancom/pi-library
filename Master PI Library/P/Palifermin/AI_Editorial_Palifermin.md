# Editorial Product Information: Palifermin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following jurisdictional Product Information (PI) source:
    - **US (FDA)**: KEPIVANCE (palifermin) for injection, revised December 2025.
- Other regional documents (e.g., Australian (TGA), European (EMA), Canadian (Health Canada)) were not provided in the source files; therefore, jurisdiction-specific information for those regions is listed as "Not specified in available sources."
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonized editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a clinical synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Palifermin (a truncated recombinant human keratinocyte growth factor [KGF]).
- **Chemical Class**: Mucocutaneous epithelial human growth factor; fibroblast growth factor (FGF) family.
- **Brand Name(s)**: **Kepivance** [US].
- **ATC Code**: V03AF08.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s)**: 
    - Lyophilized powder for injection: 5.16 mg per single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration**: Intravenous (IV) bolus injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Swedish Orphan Biovitrum AB (publ) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Severe Oral Mucositis**: Indicated to decrease the incidence and duration of severe oral mucositis in patients with hematologic malignancies who are receiving myelotoxic therapy as part of an autologous hematopoietic stem cell transplant (HSCT) regimen [US].
- **Supportive Care**: Specifically indicated for preparative regimens predicted to result in WHO Grade 3 or 4 mucositis in the majority of patients [US].

**Limitations of Use [US]:**
- Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients with non-hematologic malignancies.
- Not effective in reducing severe mucositis in patients undergoing allogeneic HSCT.
- Not recommended for use with melphalan 200 mg/m² as a standalone conditioning regimen.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing**: 60 mcg/kg/day administered as an intravenous bolus injection [US].
- **Treatment Schedule**: A total of 6 doses are required, divided into two phases [US]:
    - **Pre-Myelotoxic Therapy**: 3 doses administered on consecutive days prior to starting the preparative regimen. The third dose must be given 24 to 48 hours before the initiation of myelotoxic therapy.
    - **Post-Myelotoxic Therapy**: 3 doses administered on consecutive days starting after the preparative regimen is complete. The first of these doses should be given on the day of hematopoietic stem cell infusion (after the infusion) and at least 7 days after the last "pre" dose of palifermin.
- **Administration Instructions**:
    - Administer as an IV bolus.
    - If a heparinized line is used, flush with saline before and after palifermin administration [US].
- **Reconstitution**: Reconstitute with 1.2 mL Sterile Water for Injection, USP, to reach a concentration of 5 mg/mL. Swirl gently; do not shake [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Potential for Stimulation of Tumor Growth**: Palifermin is a growth factor for epithelial cells. While the KGF receptor is not found on hematopoietic cells, it is expressed on various non-hematopoietic tumors. The safety of palifermin in patients with non-hematologic malignancies is unknown. In vitro and animal models have shown palifermin can enhance the growth of certain human epithelial tumor cell lines [US].
- **Timing of Administration**: Do not administer palifermin within 24 hours before, during, or within 24 hours after myelotoxic chemotherapy. Clinical data suggests that administration within this window may increase the severity and duration of oral mucositis [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally reversible. The most common reactions (incidence ≥20% and at least 5% higher than placebo) include rash, fever, and elevated serum amylase [US].

| Body System | Adverse Reaction | Frequency (Kepivance vs Placebo) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Skin** | Rash | 62% vs 50% |
| | Pruritus | 35% vs 24% |
| | Erythema | 32% vs 22% |
| **Metabolic** | Elevated serum amylase (mainly salivary) | 62% vs 54% (Grade 3/4: 38% vs 31%) |
| | Elevated serum lipase | 28% vs 23% (Grade 3/4: 11% vs 5%) |
| **General** | Fever | 39% vs 34% |
| | Edema | 28% vs 21% |
| | Pain | 16% vs 11% |
| **Nervous** | Dysesthesia (perioral) | 12% vs 7% |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Mouth/Tongue thickness or discoloration | 17% vs 8% |
| **Special Senses** | Taste altered | 16% vs 8% |

- **Serious Adverse Effects**: Grade 3 skin rashes occurred in 3% of patients.
- **Post-marketing Experience**: Vaginal edema and erythema; Palmar-plantar Erythrodysaesthesia Syndrome (Hand-foot syndrome); increased incidence of cataracts was noted in post-approval safety studies [US].
- **Immunogenicity**: Approximately 2% of patients tested positive for antibodies to palifermin; no neutralizing activity was detected [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Heparin**: Heparin binds to palifermin and increases systemic exposure (AUC). When administering palifermin through an IV line used for heparin, the line must be flushed with saline [US].
- **Myelotoxic Chemotherapy**: Pharmacodynamic interaction occurs if administered within 24 hours of chemotherapy, leading to increased mucosal sensitivity and worsened mucositis [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **US Category**: Not specified (Lettered categories phased out; however, animal data indicates potential fetal harm).
    - **Risk Summary**: Animal studies in rats and rabbits showed increased post-implantation loss, decreased fetal body weight, and skeletal variations at doses 5 to 35 times the human dose. There are no adequate data on use in pregnant women [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: It is unknown if palifermin is excreted in human milk. Because of the potential for serious adverse reactions in the infant, breastfeeding should be discontinued during treatment and for at least 2 weeks after the final dose [US].
- **Fertility**: Animal studies suggest potential impairment of fertility in both males and females (decreased sperm count and increased post-implantation loss) [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Supported in patients aged 1 to 16 years. Pharmacokinetics in this group are similar to adults, although clearance may be higher in younger children. The safety profile in a Phase 1 study was consistent with that seen in adults [US].
- **Elderly Use**: No dose adjustment is recommended for patients over 65, despite a 30% lower rate of clearance observed in a small study group [US].
- **Renal Impairment**: No significant influence on palifermin pharmacokinetics; no dose adjustment required [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: The pharmacokinetic profile has not been assessed in patients with hepatic insufficiency [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Palifermin binds to the KGF receptor, stimulating the proliferation, differentiation, and migration of epithelial cells. This results in increased thickness of the tongue, buccal mucosa, and gastrointestinal tract, providing a protective effect against chemotherapy and radiation-induced injury [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Distribution**: Follows a linear model with extravascular distribution. Clearance is 2- to 4-fold higher in cancer patients compared to healthy subjects.
    - **Elimination**: Mean half-life is approximately 4.5 hours (range 3.3 to 5.7 hours).
    - **Accumulation**: No accumulation observed after 3 consecutive daily doses [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Autologous HSCT (Study 1)**: A randomized, placebo-controlled trial (n=212) of patients with hematologic malignancies receiving TBI, etoposide, and cyclophosphamide. Palifermin (60 mcg/kg) significantly reduced the median duration of severe oral mucositis (3 days vs. 9 days, p < 0.001) and the incidence of WHO Grade 3/4 mucositis (63% vs. 98%) [US].
- **Melphalan Regimen (Study 3)**: A study (n=281) using high-dose melphalan (200 mg/m²) showed no significant difference between palifermin and placebo in the incidence of severe oral mucositis [US].
- **Allogeneic HSCT**: A study in allogeneic transplant patients showed no benefit in preventing acute graft-versus-host disease (aGVHD) and actually showed a numerically higher incidence of WHO Grade 3/4 mucositis compared to placebo [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials**: Store in the original carton to protect from light.
- **Temperature**: Refrigerate at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) [US].
- **Reconstituted Solution**: Should be used immediately if possible. If not, it may be stored refrigerated (2°C to 8°C) for up to 24 hours. Protect from light. Before injection, it may be kept at room temperature for a maximum of 1 hour [US]. Do not freeze reconstituted solution.
