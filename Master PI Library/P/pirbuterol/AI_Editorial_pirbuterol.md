# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from official Product Information (PI) source documents from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a concise, structured format.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (over 2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Acute Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often given, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Administration should occur every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically in the range of 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised as therapy requires.

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow (2 to 3 minute) bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustments:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations below the normal range (40–50 µmol/L). Adjustments (e.g., downward to 5 mg/kg) should be guided by trough carnitine levels and may be implemented as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration before use.
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to therapy and monitor weekly or monthly thereafter. Monitoring should include vital signs, blood chemistries, and plasma carnitine levels (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in patients with ESRD on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of IV administration. If a severe reaction occurs, treatment must be discontinued immediately.
- **Seizures:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a known seizure history, an increase in the frequency and/or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of the oral formulation in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of metabolic disorders or ESRD.

### Common Adverse Effects (Frequency ≥5% in ESRD clinical trials)
| System Organ Class | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%), Chest pain (12%), Flu syndrome (25%), Headache (22%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%) |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%), Abdominal pain (9%), Nausea (8%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (23%), Cough increase (9%), Dyspnea (7%), Rhinitis (9%) |
| **Neurological** | Dizziness (13%), Insomnia (4%), Paresthesia (5%) |

### Serious and Postmarketing Adverse Effects
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm.
- **Neurological:** Seizures.
- **Other:** Transient nausea and vomiting, gastritis, and "fishy" body odor have been observed.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category C]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. However, there are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake to the infant must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for inborn errors of metabolism apply to paediatric patients; however, specific age-range safety profiles for ESRD are not detailed.
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. High-dose oral levocarnitine is cautioned against in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This allows for fatty acid beta-oxidation and subsequent energy production in tissues (excluding the brain), particularly in skeletal and cardiac muscle. It also facilitates the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, it corrects carnitine deficiency (defined as <20 µmol/L) and improves the acylcarnitine/carnitine ratio.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and [³H]-γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Total body clearance is approximately 4.00 L/h [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on dialysis, IV levocarnitine (20 mg/kg post-dialysis) increased mean trough plasma concentrations from 21.3 µmol/L at baseline to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While increases in carnitine levels are demonstrated, the effects on specific clinical outcomes in the ESRD population have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL [US].
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservative.
