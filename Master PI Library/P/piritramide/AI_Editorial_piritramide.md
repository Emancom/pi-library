# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises the clinical and regulatory information provided in the official Product Information (PI) source.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and does not replace the official PI for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to the current local PI for the most up-to-date and legally binding information.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP)
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Generic), Carnitor (Reference Listed Drug) [US]
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection solution: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US]
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) only [US]
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn errors of metabolism [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow intravenous bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].

### Dosing by Indication
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose: 50 mg/kg as a bolus or infusion [US].
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is typically followed by equivalent doses every 3 or 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
    - Maintenance doses are typically 50 mg/kg/day, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg/day have been utilized [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - Timing: Administer into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
    - Dose Adjustments: May be initiated based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma carnitine levels <40–50 µmol/L. Adjustments (e.g., reduction to 5 mg/kg) can be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough levels [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use [US].
- Compatible with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].
- Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known:** There are no listed contraindications in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported. These often occur within minutes of IV administration, primarily in ESRD patients. Treatment should be discontinued immediately upon signs of a severe reaction [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with reports of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity of events has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring:** Periodic monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- **Oral Levocarnitine in Renal Impairment:** Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been established in renal insufficiency. High oral doses in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology (ESRD or metabolic disease).

### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD patients at ≥5% frequency)
| Body System | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Body as Whole** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, hypervolemia |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia |
| **Respiratory** | Cough increase, dyspnea, pharyngitis, rhinitis |

### Serious/Specific Adverse Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent reports of gastritis [US].
- **Body Odor:** Reported with levocarnitine therapy [US].
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (as noted in Warnings) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management Note:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin anticoagulation [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned in this PI]. Animal reproductive studies (rats/rabbits) at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies in nursing mothers are unavailable. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased milk carnitine concentrations after administration. The benefits of maternal therapy must be weighed against the potential risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Included in metabolic disorder dosing recommendations. Efficacy in ESRD is based on adult data, with monitoring of plasma levels recommended for all ages [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources, though general caution is advised regarding dose selection in the elderly.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used for carnitine deficiency in ESRD. High *oral* doses are cautioned due to metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine facilitates the clearance of toxic acyl-CoA compounds by forming acylcarnitine, which is rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide and γ-butyrobetaine [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine over 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Trials:** Data from two double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in chronic hemodialysis patients demonstrated that levocarnitine injection (10, 20, or 40 mg/kg) significantly increased plasma carnitine concentrations. Trough levels remained stable or increased over 24 weeks of therapy. However, the effect on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservative [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription only). Poison Schedule/Classification not applicable [US].
