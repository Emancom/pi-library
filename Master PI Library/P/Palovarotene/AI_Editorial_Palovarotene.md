# Editorial Product Information: Palovarotene

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) documentation from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised, clinically focused overview of palovarotene for healthcare professionals. It synthesises complex regulatory data into a structured format for rapid clinical reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official PI. Clinicians must consult the local approved Product Information and relevant state/territory legislation before prescribing or administering this medication.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Palovarotene (as a retinoic acid receptor gamma [RARγ] selective agonist).
- **Brand Name:** **SOHONOS** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** M09AX11.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Hard capsules: 1 mg, 1.5 mg, 2.5 mg, 5 mg, and 10 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Ipsen Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Fibrodysplasia Ossificans Progressiva (FOP):** Indicated to reduce the formation of heterotopic ossification (HO) in adults and children. Eligible paediatric populations include females aged 8 years and older and males aged 10 years and older [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Principles
Palovarotene therapy must be supervised by a specialist physician experienced in managing FOP. Treatment consists of a daily "chronic" dose, which is escalated during a "flare-up" (or following a high-risk traumatic event).

### Adult and Paediatric Dosing (Aged 14 Years and Over)
- **Chronic Dosing:** 5 mg once daily.
- **Flare-up Dosing:** 20 mg once daily for 4 weeks, followed by 10 mg once daily for 8 weeks (Total 12-week regimen).
- **Persistent Symptoms:** If flare-up symptoms persist after 12 weeks, the 10 mg dose may be extended in 4-week increments until resolution.
- **Recurrent Flare-ups:** If a new flare-up occurs during treatment, the full 12-week regimen should be restarted.

### Paediatric Dosing (Under 14 Years of Age)
Dosing is weight-adjusted to ensure consistent exposure across age groups:
- **10 to <20 kg:** Chronic 2.5 mg; Flare-up (Weeks 1-4) 10 mg; Flare-up (Weeks 5-12) 5 mg.
- **20 to <40 kg:** Chronic 3 mg; Flare-up (Weeks 1-4) 12.5 mg; Flare-up (Weeks 5-12) 6 mg.
- **40 to <60 kg:** Chronic 4 mg; Flare-up (Weeks 1-4) 15 mg; Flare-up (Weeks 5-12) 7.5 mg.
- **≥60 kg:** Use adult dosing.

### Administration Instructions
- **Food Requirement:** Must be taken with food, ideally at the same time each day. 
- **Alternative Administration:** Capsules may be opened and contents sprinkled on a teaspoon of soft food (e.g., applesauce, yogurt). The mixture must be consumed within 1 hour of opening and kept out of direct sunlight.
- **Missed Dose:** If missed by ≤6 hours, take immediately. If >6 hours, skip the dose and resume at the next scheduled time. Do not double doses.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy:** Absolute contraindication due to severe teratogenicity [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated during treatment and for 1 month post-discontinuation [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to palovarotene, other retinoids, or any excipients (e.g., lactose).
- **Females of Childbearing Potential:** Contraindicated unless all strict pregnancy prevention conditions are met.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [Australian]
- **Teratogenicity:** Causes severe, life-threatening birth defects. Females must not be pregnant or intend to become pregnant.
- **Premature Physeal Closure (PPC):** May cause irreversible premature fusion of growth plates in growing children. Close monitoring of linear growth and skeletal maturity is mandatory.

### General Warnings
- **Bone Safety:** Retinoids are associated with bone toxicity, including decreased bone mineral density (BMD) and increased risk of vertebral fractures. Periodic radiological assessment of the spine is recommended.
- **Mucocutaneous Effects:** Extremely common (occurring in ~98% of patients). Includes dry skin, pruritus, and cheilitis. May increase risk of secondary infections (e.g., paronychia).
- **Psychiatric Disorders:** Risk of depression, anxiety, and suicidal ideation/behaviour, consistent with the systemic retinoid class. Monitor for mood changes.
- **Night Blindness:** Nyctalopia has been reported. Caution is advised for night driving.
- **Photosensitivity:** Increased risk of severe sunburn; patients should use sun protection and avoid excessive UV exposure.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors (e.g., Ketoconazole, Itraconazole):** Increase palovarotene exposure ~3-fold. Concomitant use should be avoided.
- **Moderate CYP3A4 Inhibitors (e.g., Fluconazole, Erythromycin):** Increase exposure. Avoid or reduce palovarotene dose by 50% [Australian].
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., Rifampicin, St John’s Wort):** Decrease exposure ~10-fold. Avoid concomitant use.
- **Vitamin A & Oral Retinoids:** Risk of additive toxicity (hypervitaminosis A). Avoid co-administration.
- **Tetracyclines:** Association with benign intracranial hypertension. Avoid co-administration.
- **Grapefruit Products:** May inhibit CYP3A4; avoid during treatment.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy Category X [Australian]:** Palovarotene is a potent teratogen. Fetal malformations include cleft palate and skeletal abnormalities.
  - **Pregnancy Prevention:** Monthly pregnancy testing is required. Females of childbearing potential must use one highly effective method (e.g., IUD) or two effective methods (e.g., hormonal + barrier) for at least 1 month before, during, and 1 month after treatment.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety not established; potential for serious adverse reactions. Breastfeeding is contraindicated for 1 month after the final dose.
- **Fertility:** No human data; animal studies showed effects only at maternally toxic doses.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not indicated for females <8 years or males <10 years due to the high risk of PPC in skeletally immature patients. PPC risk is significantly higher in children <10 years (56%) compared to those ≥10 years (31%) [Australian].
- **Elderly:** No dose adjustment required based on limited data.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild or moderate impairment. Use in severe renal impairment is not recommended [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for mild impairment. Use with caution in moderate impairment; not recommended for severe hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selective RARγ agonist. In FOP, a mutation in the ACVR1 (ALK2) receptor causes overactive BMP signaling, leading to HO. Palovarotene binds to RARγ, which acts as a transcriptional repressor to downregulate the Smad1/5/8 pathway, thereby inhibiting chondrogenesis and subsequent heterotopic bone formation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ ~4.6 hours; exposure increased by 40% with high-fat meals.
  - **Distribution:** Highly protein bound (98-99.6%).
  - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic via CYP3A4.
  - **Elimination:** Mean half-life ~8.7 hours. Fecal excretion (97%), urinary excretion (3%).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **MOVE Study (PVO-1A-301):** A Phase 3, single-arm study comparing palovarotene to a Natural History Study (NHS) cohort. 
- **Efficacy:** Treatment resulted in a 54% reduction in annualized new HO volume compared to untreated subjects (p=0.0392) in the weighted linear mixed effect analysis.
- **Flare-up Efficacy:** Phase 2 data showed a 72% reduction in new HO volume at 12 weeks post-flare-up when treated with the 20/10 mg regimen compared to placebo/untreated flare-ups.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C. 
- **Protection:** Keep in original packaging to protect from light.
- **Handling:** Caregivers (especially if pregnant) should wear gloves if opening capsules to avoid accidental exposure.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
- **Additional Monitoring:** Subject to additional monitoring (Black Triangle Scheme) to facilitate rapid identification of new safety information [Australian].
