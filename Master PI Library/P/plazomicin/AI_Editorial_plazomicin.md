# Editorial Product Information: Plazomicin

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **United States (FDA)**.
- This document serves as a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 
- Clinical decisions should be guided by the current official documentation relevant to the practitioner’s region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Plazomicin (as plazomicin sulfate).
- **Chemical Class:** Semi-synthetic aminoglycoside antibacterial (derived from sisomicin).
- **Brand Name:** **ZEMDRI** [United States].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injection: 500 mg/10 mL (50 mg/mL) supplied as a sterile, clear, colorless-to-yellow solution in a single-dose vial [United States].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Cipla USA Inc. [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Complicated Urinary Tract Infections (cUTI):** Indicated for patients 18 years of age or older for the treatment of cUTI, including pyelonephritis, caused by susceptible isolates of *Escherichia coli, Klebsiella pneumoniae, Proteus mirabilis,* and *Enterobacter cloacae*.
- **Usage Restriction:** Due to limited clinical safety and efficacy data, plazomicin should be reserved for use in patients who have limited or no alternative treatment options [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 Adult Dosing (Normal Renal Function)
- **Standard Dose:** 15 mg/kg administered once every 24 hours.
- **Creatinine Clearance (CLcr):** $\ge$ 90 mL/min.
- **Duration of Treatment:** 4 to 7 days for cUTI, including pyelonephritis. Total duration (IV plus optional switch to oral) is 7 to 10 days.

### 3.2 Weight-Based Calculations
- Dosing should be calculated using **Total Body Weight (TBW)**. 
- For patients whose TBW exceeds their Ideal Body Weight (IBW) by 25% or more, **Adjusted Body Weight (ABW)** should be used: $ABW = IBW + 0.4 \times (TBW - IBW)$.

### 3.3 Administration Instructions
- Administer via IV infusion over 30 minutes. 
- Must be diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection or Lactated Ringer’s Injection to a final volume of 50 mL.
- Do not co-administer or mix with other drugs in the same IV line.

### 3.4 Monitoring and Titration
- **Renal Function:** Assess CLcr prior to initiation and daily during therapy.
- **Therapeutic Drug Monitoring (TDM):** Recommended for patients with CLcr < 90 mL/min. The goal is to maintain plasma trough concentrations below 3 mcg/mL. Measure the trough concentration approximately 30 minutes before the second dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to plazomicin or any other aminoglycoside [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [United States]:**
    - **Nephrotoxicity:** Reported with plazomicin; risk is higher in patients with renal impairment, the elderly, or those on concomitant nephrotoxic drugs.
    - **Ototoxicity:** May cause irreversible hearing loss, tinnitus, or vertigo. 
    - **Neuromuscular Blockade:** Aminoglycosides may exacerbate muscle weakness in patients with neuromuscular disorders (e.g., myasthenia gravis) or delay recovery in those receiving neuromuscular blocking agents.
    - **Fetal Harm:** May cause permanent harm to the fetus when administered to pregnant women.
- **Mitochondrial DNA Variants:** Increased risk of ototoxicity has been observed in patients with certain mitochondrial mutations (e.g., the *m.1555A>G* variant), even if serum levels are within range.
- **Clostridium difficile-Associated Diarrhea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all systemic antibacterials; severity may range from mild diarrhea to fatal colitis.
- **Development of Drug-Resistant Bacteria:** Use only for proven or strongly suspected bacterial infections to prevent the development of resistance.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### 6.1 Common Adverse Effects
The following table summarizes adverse reactions occurring in $\ge$ 1% of patients treated with plazomicin (based on Trial 1 data):

| Adverse Reaction | Frequency (%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Decreased Renal Function | 3.6% |
| Diarrhea | 2.3% |
| Hypertension | 2.3% |
| Headache | 1.3% |
| Nausea | 1.3% |
| Vomiting | 1.3% |
| Hypotension | 1.0% |

### 6.2 Other Reported Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Constipation, gastritis.
- **Metabolic/Laboratory:** Hypokalemia, increased alanine aminotransferase (ALT).
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness.
- **Renal/Urinary:** Hematuria.
- **Respiratory:** Dyspnea.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Metformin:** Plazomicin does not clinically affect the pharmacokinetics of metformin (an OCT and MATE substrate).
- **Transporters:** Plazomicin is a selective inhibitor of MATE1 and MATE2-K renal transporters *in vitro* at high concentrations, but it is not a substrate for P-gp or BCRP.
- **CYP450 Enzymes:** Plazomicin does not inhibit or induce major CYP450 isoforms.
- **Nephrotoxic Agents:** Concomitant use increases the risk of nephrotoxicity [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Risk Summary:** Aminoglycosides cross the placenta and can cause irreversible, bilateral congenital deafness in the fetus. There are no available data on plazomicin use in pregnant women. 
    - **Jurisdictional Classification:** US FDA labeling highlights potential fetal harm but does not use the traditional lettered category system (A, B, C, D, X) in the provided source.
- **Breastfeeding:** No data are available regarding the presence of plazomicin in human milk or its effects on the breastfed infant. In rats, plazomicin is excreted in milk at low levels (2–4% of maternal plasma concentrations).
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats showed reduced corpora lutea and implantation sites at high doses (25–50 mg/kg/day), though no effects were seen on mating or fertility indices.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [United States].
- **Geriatric Use:** Higher incidence of adverse reactions observed (27% in $\ge$ 65 years vs 13.3% in < 65 years). Dosage adjustments should be based on renal function.
- **Renal Impairment:** Initial dosing must be adjusted based on CLcr:
    - **CLcr 60 to < 90 mL/min:** 15 mg/kg every 24 hours.
    - **CLcr 30 to < 60 mL/min:** 10 mg/kg every 24 hours.
    - **CLcr 15 to < 30 mL/min:** 10 mg/kg every 48 hours.
    - **CLcr < 15 mL/min or Dialysis:** Insufficient information to recommend a dosage regimen.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Pharmacokinetics in this population are unknown; however, plazomicin is not metabolized by the liver.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Aminoglycoside that binds to the bacterial 30S ribosomal subunit, inhibiting protein synthesis. It retains activity against most Enterobacteriaceae in the presence of aminoglycoside-modifying enzymes (AMEs).
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Efficacy correlates with the ratio of Area Under the Curve to Minimum Inhibitory Concentration (AUC:MIC).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Low plasma protein binding (~20%). Vd is approximately 17.9 L in healthy adults.
    - **Metabolism:** Not significantly metabolized.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal excretion (97.5% recovered in urine as unchanged drug). Mean half-life is 3.5 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Trial 1 (NCT02486627):** A multinational, double-blind, noninferiority trial compared plazomicin (15 mg/kg Q24h) to meropenem (1 g Q8h) in 609 adults with cUTI. 
- **Outcomes:** Plazomicin demonstrated noninferiority. At Day 5, composite cure was 88% (plazomicin) vs 91.4% (meropenem). At the Test of Cure (TOC) visit (Day 17 $\pm$ 2), composite cure was 81.7% for plazomicin vs 70.1% for meropenem.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F).
- **Diluted Solution:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature or up to 7 days when refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only. 
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources for other jurisdictions.
