# Editorial Product Information: Phenytoin Sodium

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of phenytoin sodium product information for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Phenytoin sodium
- **Chemical Class:** Hydantoin derivative
- **Brand Names:** DBL Phenytoin Injection [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** N03AB02
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Injection:** 100 mg/2 mL and 250 mg/5 mL ampoules [Australian]
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV); Intramuscular (IM) is generally discouraged due to erratic absorption [Australian].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Status Epilepticus:** Control of status epilepticus of the grand mal type [Australian].
- **Seizure Control:** Control of tonic-clonic (grand mal) and psychomotor (temporal lobe) seizures [Australian].
- **Neurosurgical Prophylaxis:** Prevention and treatment of seizures occurring during or following neurosurgery [Australian].
- **Cardiac Arrhythmias:** Treatment of certain cardiac arrhythmias, particularly those induced by digitalis, when unresponsive to conventional antiarrhythmic agents or cardioversion [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Therapeutic Serum Levels:** 10 to 20 micrograms/mL (40 to 80 micromoles/L) [Australian]. 
- **Loading Dose (Status Epilepticus):** 10 to 15 mg/kg administered slowly via IV [Australian].
- **Maintenance Dose:** 100 mg IV or orally every 6 to 8 hours [Australian].

### Dosing by Indication
- **Neurosurgery:** 250 mg IV every 6 to 12 hours until oral therapy is feasible [Australian].
- **Cardiac Arrhythmias:** 3 to 5 mg/kg bodyweight via a single IV injection, repeated as necessary [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Rate of Infusion:** Must not exceed **50 mg/minute** in adults. In neonates and children, the rate should not exceed **1 to 3 mg/kg/min** (max 50 mg/min) [Australian].
- **Technique:** Administer slowly and directly into a large vein through a large-gauge needle or IV catheter.
- **Flushing:** Test catheter patency with a sterile saline flush before administration. Follow each injection with a 0.9% sodium chloride flush to reduce local venous irritation [Australian].
- **Incompatibility:** Do not dilute or mix with other drugs or IV infusion solutions, as precipitation may occur due to the high pH (pH 12) [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to phenytoin, other hydantoins, or any excipients (including propylene glycol and ethanol) [Australian].
- **Cardiac Disorders:** Sinus bradycardia, sino-atrial block, second and third-degree AV block, or Stokes-Adams syndrome [Australian].
- **Drug-Drug Interactions:** Coadministration with delavirdine [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** Rapid IV administration increases the risk of hypotension, severe cardiotoxic reactions (bradycardia, heart block, ventricular fibrillation), and cardiovascular collapse. Continuous ECG and blood pressure monitoring is essential during loading doses [Australian].
- **Local Toxicity (Purple Glove Syndrome):** Peripheral IV injection can cause "purple glove syndrome," characterized by edema, discoloration, and pain distal to the site. This may progress to skin necrosis or limb ischemia requiring surgical intervention [Australian].
- **Dermatologic Reactions:** Rare, life-threatening reactions including Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN), and DRESS/AHS.
    - **Genetic Risk:** Patients of Asian ancestry may have an increased risk of SJS/TEN if they carry the **HLA-B*1502** allele [Australian].
- **Suicidal Ideation:** Antiepileptic drugs increase the risk of suicidal thoughts or behavior; patients should be monitored for changes in mood or depression [Australian].
- **Abrupt Withdrawal:** Discontinuing phenytoin suddenly may precipitate status epilepticus [Australian].
- **Metabolic Effects:** Phenytoin may affect bone mineral metabolism, leading to Vitamin D deficiency, osteomalacia, and increased fracture risk [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
| System | Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Central Nervous System** | Common | Nystagmus, ataxia, slurred speech, decreased coordination, mental confusion [Australian]. |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Common | Nausea, vomiting, constipation [Australian]. |
| **Dermatologic** | Common | Measles-like (morbilliform) rash [Australian]. |
| **Hepatic** | Rare | Toxic hepatitis, acute hepatic failure [Australian]. |
| **Hematologic** | Rare | Thrombocytopenia, leukopenia, agranulocytosis, pancytopenia [Australian]. |
| **Other** | Frequent | Gingival hyperplasia (especially in children/young adults) [Australian]. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Cardiovascular collapse, CNS depression, Purple Glove Syndrome, SJS/TEN, and anaphylaxis [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Enzyme Induction:** Phenytoin is a potent inducer of CYP3A4, CYP2D6, CYP1A2, CYP2C9, and CYP2C19. It significantly increases the metabolism of many drugs (e.g., warfarin, oral contraceptives, corticosteroids, and certain antiretrovirals) [Australian].
- **Drugs Increasing Phenytoin Levels:** Capecitabine, chloramphenicol, cimetidine, disulfiram, fluoxetine, isoniazid, and salicylates [Australian].
- **Drugs Decreasing Phenytoin Levels:** Chronic alcohol abuse, carbamazepine, folic acid, rifampicin, and St. John’s Wort [Australian].

### Management Notes
- **Valproate:** Concomitant use may increase the risk of hyperammonemia [Australian].
- **Enteral Feeding:** May reduce the absorption of oral phenytoin; IV administration is preferred for patients on continuous enteral feeds [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Phenytoin crosses the placenta. Prenatal exposure increases the risk of major malformations (approx. 6%), including orofacial clefts and cardiac defects, and Fetal Hydantoin Syndrome.
- **Neonatal Risk:** Risk of coagulation defects/hemorrhage; prophylactic Vitamin K is recommended for the mother prior to delivery [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended, as phenytoin is excreted in low concentrations in breast milk [Australian].
- **Fertility:** High doses in animal studies (mice/rats) showed adverse effects on pregnancy rates [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatrics:** Metabolize phenytoin more rapidly than adults. Loading dose: 10 to 20 mg/kg IV [Australian].
- **Elderly:** Slower metabolism and decreased albumin binding may increase toxicity risk. Recommended infusion rate: **5 to 10 mg/min** (max 25 mg/min) [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Increased fraction of unbound phenytoin; total plasma concentrations must be interpreted with caution. Unbound (free) levels are more reliable (Target: 0.8 to 2 micrograms/mL) [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Stabilizes neuronal membranes and inhibits the spread of seizure activity by blocking voltage-gated sodium channels in the motor cortex. It also enhances the sodium pump and may inhibit calcium influx [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic via CYP2C9 and CYP2C19. Follows non-linear (saturable) kinetics; small dose increases can lead to disproportionate increases in serum levels [Australian].
    - **Protein Binding:** Approximately 90%, primarily to albumin [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Mostly via bile as inactive metabolites, then excreted in urine [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Protection:** Protect from light [Australian].
- **Visual Inspection:** Do not use if the solution is hazy or contains a precipitate. If refrigerated/frozen, a precipitate may form but should dissolve at room temperature [Australian].
