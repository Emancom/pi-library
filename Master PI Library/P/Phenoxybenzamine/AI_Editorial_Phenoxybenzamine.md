# Editorial Product Information: Phenoxybenzamine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Always consult the local regulatory authority’s approved labelling before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Phenoxybenzamine hydrochloride (a long-acting, adrenergic $\alpha$-receptor blocking agent).
- **Chemical Class:** Haloalkylamine (alkylating agent).
- **Brand Names:** Dibenyline [Australian].
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Hard gelatin capsules, 10 mg [Australian].
- **Description:** Size 3 capsules with a white opaque body and clear red cap, printed 'P10' in grey, containing a white powder [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Amdipharm Mercury (Australia) Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Phaeochromocytoma:** Management of hypertensive episodes associated with this condition [Australian].
- **Neurogenic Bladder:** Treatment of urinary retention resulting from a neuropathic bladder [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Phaeochromocytoma (Adults)
- **Initial Dose:** 10 mg twice daily [Australian].
- **Titration:** Increase the dose slowly until the desired clinical effect (blood pressure control) is achieved or side effects become intolerable.
- **Frequency of Adjustment:** Observe the patient at each dose level for at least four days before considering a further increase [Australian].
- **Maintenance Dose:** The range is typically 20 mg to 60 mg daily, administered in two divided doses. The dosage required for blood pressure control generally averages 1 to 2 mg/kg body weight daily [Australian].

### Urinary Retention/Neurogenic Bladder (Adults)
- **Standard Dose:** 10 mg twice daily is usually sufficient [Australian].
- **Treatment Duration:** If no clinical effect is observed within 2 to 3 weeks, alternative therapies should be considered [Australian].

### General Administration
- Concomitant $\beta$-adrenergic blockade may be required to manage tachycardia or arrhythmias in patients with phaeochromocytomas that secrete significant amounts of adrenaline [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to phenoxybenzamine hydrochloride or any excipients (including lactose) [Australian].
- **Hypotensive Risk:** Conditions where a drop in blood pressure is clinically undesirable, specifically:
    - Cerebrovascular accidents (CVA) [Australian].
    - Recovery period following acute myocardial infarction (MI) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Beta-Receptor Effects:** $\alpha$-adrenergic blockade leaves $\beta$-adrenergic receptors unopposed. Administration of compounds that stimulate both $\alpha$ and $\beta$ receptors (e.g., adrenaline) may result in an exaggerated hypotensive response and tachycardia [Australian].
- **Long-term Use and Carcinogenicity:** Long-term use is **not recommended**. Case reports of carcinoma in humans have been noted following prolonged treatment. Preclinical studies in rats showed drug-related ulcerative or erosive gastritis and positive bioassays for various sarcomas and carcinomas [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular/Renal Caution:** Use with caution in patients with marked cerebral or coronary arteriosclerosis or pre-existing renal damage [Australian].
- **Respiratory Infections:** The adrenergic blocking effect may exacerbate symptoms of respiratory infections [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Close monitoring of blood pressure (supine and erect) is necessary during dose titration.
- **Overdose Management:** Adrenaline is **contraindicated** in overdose because it will cause further vasodilation and blood pressure drops (adrenaline reversal). Use noradrenaline to combat severe hypotension [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Note: Frequencies are not quantified in the source document.*

### Autonomic Nervous System
- Postural hypotension (accompanied by dizziness and compensatory tachycardia).
- Nasal congestion.
- Miosis (constriction of the pupil).
- Inhibition of ejaculation.
- *Note:* These effects may diminish as therapy continues [Australian].

### General Disorders
- Drowsiness and fatigue.
- Gastrointestinal irritation [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Adrenaline (Epinephrine):** Produces an exaggerated hypotensive response and tachycardia due to unopposed $\beta$-receptor stimulation and "adrenaline reversal" [Australian].
- **Noradrenaline:** Phenoxybenzamine blocks the hyperthermia normally produced by noradrenaline [Australian].
- **Reserpine:** Phenoxybenzamine blocks the hypothermia normally produced by reserpine [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category: Not assigned]. Adequate human and animal data are unavailable. Use only if clearly needed [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety data are unavailable. Because many drugs are excreted in milk and there is a potential for serious adverse reactions, a decision must be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug, weighing the importance of the medication to the mother [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available; however, caution is advised due to the higher prevalence of cardiovascular and renal comorbidities in this population [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Administer with caution [Australian]. No specific dosing adjustments provided in available sources.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Phenoxybenzamine is an irreversible, long-acting $\alpha$-adrenergic receptor antagonist. It acts as an alkylating agent, forming a covalent bond with $\alpha$-receptors. It also provides non-competitive blockade of receptors for histamine, serotonin, and acetylcholine (muscarinic) [Australian].

### Pharmacodynamics
- Decreases peripheral resistance and prevents paroxysmal hypertension in phaeochromocytoma.
- Increases blood flow to the skin, mucosa, and abdominal viscera.
- Lowers both supine and erect blood pressure.
- Reduces bladder outflow resistance by blocking $\alpha$-adrenoreceptors in the distal urethral sphincter and bladder neck smooth muscle [Australian].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** 20% to 30% of an oral dose is absorbed in its active form [Australian].
- **Distribution:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Metabolism/Elimination:** The half-life of the intravenous form is approximately 24 hours. Effects are cumulative for nearly a week with daily administration and persist for 3 to 4 days after a single intravenous dose [Australian].
