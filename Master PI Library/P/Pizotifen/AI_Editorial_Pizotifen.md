# Editorial Product Information: Pizotifen

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration).
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pizotifen malate
- **Chemical Class:** Cycloheptathiophene derivative (structurally related to cyproheptadine and tricyclic antidepressants)
- **Brand Name(s):** Sandomigran [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** N02CX01
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sugar-coated tablets containing 500 micrograms of pizotifen (as 725 micrograms pizotifen malate) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AFT Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
Pizotifen is indicated for the prophylactic (interval) treatment of vascular headaches [Australian]:
- Typical and atypical migraine
- Vasomotor headache
- Cluster headache (Horton's syndrome)

**Note:** Pizotifen is not effective for treating acute migraine attacks.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** Initial dose is 0.5 mg daily. Dosage should be increased progressively. 
- **Maintenance Dose:** The average maintenance dose is 1.5 mg daily, which may be administered as a single dose at night or in three divided doses.
- **Titration for Refractory Cases:** Dosage may be gradually increased up to 3 mg to 4.5 mg daily, administered in two or three divided doses [Australian].
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to pizotifen or any excipients in the formulation (e.g., lactose, sucrose) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Hepatic injury (ranging from transaminase elevation to hepatitis) has been reported. Treatment must be discontinued if clinical evidence of hepatic dysfunction occurs [Australian].
- **Anticholinergic Effects:** Due to slight anticholinergic activity, caution is required in patients with narrow-angle glaucoma (unless surgically treated) or urinary retention (e.g., prostatic hypertrophy) [Australian].
- **Epilepsy:** Seizures have been observed more frequently in patients with epilepsy; use with caution in this population [Australian].
- **Withdrawal:** Abrupt cessation may lead to withdrawal symptoms (depression, tremor, nausea, anxiety, dizziness, sleep disorders). Gradual withdrawal is recommended [Australian].
- **Excipients:** Tablets contain lactose and sucrose; they are contraindicated in patients with rare hereditary problems of galactose intolerance, severe lactase deficiency, or glucose-galactose malabsorption [Australian].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Monitor liver function if hepatic dysfunction is suspected.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most common reactions are appetite stimulation, weight increase, and sedation.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Increased appetite, weight increase |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Sedation (somnolence, fatigue), dizziness, nausea, dry mouth |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Constipation |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Hypersensitivity, face oedema, depression, CNS stimulation (aggression, agitation), hallucinations, insomnia, anxiety, paraesthesia, urticaria, rash, myalgia |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Convulsions |

- **Post-Marketing Reports (Frequency Unknown):** Hepatic enzyme increase, jaundice, hepatitis, muscle cramps. 
- **Causal Relationship Not Established:** Headache, hypotension, and impotence have been reported [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CNS Agents:** Central effects of sedatives, hypnotics, antihistamines, and alcohol may be enhanced by pizotifen [Australian].
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Concomitant use should be avoided as MAOIs may prolong and intensify anticholinergic effects [Australian].
- **Cisapride:** Concomitant administration may lead to reduced efficacy of cisapride [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Pizotifen is extensively metabolised by N-glucuronidation; increased plasma concentrations may occur when co-administered with drugs exclusively undergoing this pathway [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B1** [Australian]. Animal studies show no embryotoxic or teratogenic effects. Clinical data in humans is very limited; use only if the expected benefits outweigh potential risks [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Although concentrations in milk are unlikely to affect the infant, use in nursing mothers is not recommended [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no impairment of fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Experience in children is limited [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available in provided sources [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Caution is required; dosage adjustment may be necessary [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Caution is required; dosage adjustment may be necessary. Treatment should be discontinued if liver dysfunction is evidenced [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Pizotifen is a competitive serotonin antagonist. It also exhibits antihistamine, antibradykinin, and weak anticholinergic properties. It is thought to prevent the loss of tone in extracranial vessels and inhibit serotonin reuptake by platelets [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Possesses appetite-stimulating and antidepressant properties.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid and nearly complete (approx. 80%). Peak plasma levels reached in 4–6 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Widely distributed; Volume of Distribution (Vd) is at least 7 L/kg. Protein binding is 91%.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensive hepatic metabolism, primarily via N-glucuronidation.
    - **Elimination:** Elimination half-life is approximately 23 hours. Less than 1% is excreted unchanged in urine; up to 55% is excreted as metabolites [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C. Protect from light [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging.
