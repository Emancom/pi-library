## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Phytonadione (Vitamin K1); 2-methyl-3-phytyl-1, 4-naphthoquinone.
- **Chemical Class:** Naphthoquinone derivative (Vitamin).
- **Brand Name(s):** Phytonadione Injectable Emulsion, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** B02BA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile, nonpyrogenic aqueous dispersion for injection (10 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Subcutaneous (preferred), Intramuscular, or Intravenous [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** HF Acquisition Co LLC dba HealthFirst (Distributor); Dr. Reddy's Laboratories Inc. (Original Manufacturer) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Phytonadione** is indicated for coagulation disorders resulting from impaired formation of factors II, VII, IX, and X, specifically when caused by Vitamin K deficiency or interference with Vitamin K activity [US]. Specific approved indications include:
- **Anticoagulant-induced prothrombin deficiency:** Caused by coumarin or indanedione derivatives [US].
- **Hemorrhagic disease of the newborn:** Prophylaxis and treatment [US].
- **Hypoprothrombinemia due to antibacterial therapy** [US].
- **Malabsorption-related hypoprothrombinemia:** Secondary to obstructive jaundice, biliary fistula, sprue, ulcerative colitis, celiac disease, intestinal resection, cystic fibrosis, and regional enteritis [US].
- **Drug-induced hypoprothrombinemia:** Where interference with Vitamin K metabolism is established (e.g., salicylates) [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Anticoagulant-Induced Prothrombin Deficiency:** 2.5 mg to 10 mg (up to 25 mg; rarely 50 mg) initially. If prothrombin time (PT) is not satisfactorily shortened within 6 to 8 hours, the dose should be repeated [US].
- **Other Hypoprothrombinemia Causes:** 2.5 mg to 25 mg or more (rarely up to 50 mg), with the dose and route determined by the severity of the condition and patient response [US].

### Newborn Dosing
- **Prophylaxis of Hemorrhagic Disease:** 0.5 mg to 1 mg administered intramuscularly (IM) within one hour of birth [US].
- **Treatment of Hemorrhagic Disease:** 1 mg administered subcutaneously (SC) or IM. Higher doses may be required if the mother was receiving oral anticoagulants [US].

### Administration and Dilution
- **Preferred Route:** Whenever possible, the subcutaneous (SC) route should be used due to safety risks associated with other routes [US].
- **Intravenous (IV) Administration:** Must be performed very slowly, not exceeding a rate of 1 mg per minute [US].
- **Dilution:** May be diluted with preservative-free 0.9% Sodium Chloride, 5% Dextrose, or 5% Dextrose and Sodium Chloride. Diluted solutions must be used immediately and the remainder discarded [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **phytonadione** or any excipients (including polyoxyethylated fatty acid derivatives or benzyl alcohol) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning [US]
**Severe reactions, including fatalities, have occurred during and immediately following intravenous and intramuscular injection. These reactions may resemble anaphylaxis (including shock and cardiac/respiratory arrest) and have occurred even with proper dilution and in patients receiving the drug for the first time. The IV and IM routes should be restricted to situations where subcutaneous administration is not feasible and the potential risk is clinically justified.**

### General Warnings
- **Heparin Reversal:** Phytonadione does not counteract the anticoagulant effects of heparin [US].
- **Coagulation Paradox:** Overzealous correction of anticoagulant-induced hypoprothrombinemia may return the patient to the thromboembolic risk state present prior to anticoagulation [US].
- **Response Time:** An immediate effect is not expected; measurable improvement in PT typically requires 1 to 2 hours [US].
- **Liver Disease:** If the initial response to Vitamin K is unsatisfactory, repeated large doses are not recommended, as failure to respond may indicate an inherent lack of response in liver disease [US].

### Specific Precautions
- **Benzyl Alcohol Toxicity:** Contains benzyl alcohol, which has been associated with "Gasping Syndrome" in premature infants. While the amount in standard doses is low, toxicity risks must be considered in neonates [US].
- **Aluminum Toxicity:** The product contains aluminum, which can reach toxic levels with prolonged parenteral administration if renal function is impaired. Premature neonates are at the highest risk [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Serious Reactions
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylactoid reactions, shock, and cardiac/respiratory arrest (primarily associated with IV/IM routes) [US].
- **Neonatal Hyperbilirubinemia:** Reported rarely, typically with doses exceeding recommendations [US].

### Frequency Not Specified [US]
- **Cardiovascular:** Brief hypotension, rapid/weak pulse, cyanosis.
- **Respiratory:** Dyspnea.
- **Dermatologic:** Transient flushing sensations, profuse sweating. Rarely, repeated injections may cause erythematous, pruritic plaques or scleroderma-like lesions.
- **Neurologic:** Dizziness, "peculiar" taste sensations.
- **Injection Site:** Pain, swelling, and tenderness.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Oral Anticoagulants (Coumarins/Indanediones):** Phytonadione induces temporary resistance to these agents, especially with large doses. If anticoagulation must be resumed, higher doses of the anticoagulant or a switch to a different class (e.g., heparin) may be necessary [US].
- **Interfering Drugs:** Antibacterials and salicylates can interfere with Vitamin K activity; reducing the dosage of these drugs is an alternative to phytonadione therapy where appropriate [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category C]. Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. Use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if the drug is excreted in human milk. Caution should be exercised when administered to nursing women [US].
- **Fertility:** Studies on the impairment of fertility have not been conducted [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Recommended for prophylaxis and treatment of hemorrhagic disease of the newborn. Care must be taken to avoid exceeding doses due to the risk of hemolysis and hyperbilirubinemia, especially in premature infants [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Patients with impaired renal function, especially neonates, are at risk for aluminum accumulation and associated CNS and bone toxicity if parenteral administration is prolonged [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Response may be poor in cases of severe liver disease [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Phytonadione possesses the same type of activity as naturally occurring Vitamin K. It is essential for the hepatic synthesis of active prothrombin (Factor II), proconvertin (Factor VII), plasma thromboplastin component (Factor IX), and Stuart factor (Factor X) [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** A prompt response is typically seen in 2 to 4 hours in newborns; measurable PT improvement in adults occurs within 1 to 2 hours [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Not specified in available sources [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from light at all times. Keep ampules in the carton until the time of use [US].
- **Inspection:** Parenteral products should be visually inspected for particulate matter and discoloration before use [US].
