# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information documents contributed to this editorial: United States (FDA).
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for healthcare professionals. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the United States (FDA) documentation was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn metabolic errors [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing hemodialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It may be administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion. In acute metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 or 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours. Daily maintenance doses typically remain at 50 mg/kg or as clinically required. Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). This should be administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].

### Monitoring and Titration
- It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels before therapy.
- Target plasma free carnitine concentrations should be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- In ESRD, dose adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known:** There are no listed contraindications for levocarnitine injection in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These can occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate the risks versus benefits before re-administration [US].
- **Neurologic Reactions (Seizures):** Seizures have been reported in patients receiving levocarnitine, regardless of whether they had a pre-existing seizure disorder. In those with existing epilepsy, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **TMA/TMAO Accumulation:** While this applies primarily to high-dose chronic oral administration, clinicians should note that in patients with severely compromised renal function, metabolites such as trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) may accumulate as they are normally excreted in urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported at a frequency of ≥5% in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials for ESRD patients include:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (21%), injection site reaction (38%), pain (21–35%), fever, chest pain, headache, and infection [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia, and palpitations [US].
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), nausea (5–12%), and vomiting (9–21%) [US].
- **Neurological:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia, and insomnia [US].
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase, and dyspnea [US].

### Postmarketing and Other Reactions
- **Transient Effects:** Nausea and vomiting are commonly observed.
- **Less Frequent:** Gastritis and a transient "fishy" body odor have been reported [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels at the initiation of therapy and following any dose adjustments [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine is not protein-bound, suggesting a low potential for protein-binding displacement interactions [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned under current labeling, but reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm]. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after exogenous administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefit of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders in children follows the weight-based guidelines (50 mg/kg) provided in the general dosing section [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency, and chronic high doses may lead to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with metabolic errors or ESRD, levocarnitine helps clear accumulated toxic acyl-CoA compounds by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are then excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Studies:** Trials in healthy volunteers and ESRD patients demonstrated that IV administration significantly increases plasma carnitine levels. In ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg after dialysis, trough levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks of therapy [US].
- **Clinical Outcomes:** While supplemental carnitine increases plasma concentrations in ESRD, the specific effects on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, cardiomyopathy) in this population have not been definitively determined in the provided trials [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL at room temperature [US].
