# Editorial Product Information: Pentetate Calcium Trisodium (Ca-DTPA)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of pentetate calcium trisodium (Ca-DTPA) for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pentetate calcium trisodium (trisodium calcium diethylenetriaminepentaacetate); commonly referred to as **Ca-DTPA**.
- **Chemical Class:** Chelating agent.
- **Brand Name(s):** Not specified in available sources (labeled as Pentetate calcium trisodium).
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injection, solution concentrate: 1000 mg per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-use clear glass ampoules [US].
- **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) injection, IV infusion, or inhalation (nebulised).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** hameln pharmaceuticals gmbh [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Internal Radioactive Contamination:** Treatment of individuals with known or suspected internal contamination with plutonium, americium, or curium to increase rates of elimination [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration

### 3.1 General Dosing (Adults and Adolescents)
- **Initial Dose:** A single 1.0 gram dose administered intravenously.
- **Maintenance Dose:** 1.0 gram once daily administered intravenously.
- **Duration:** Treatment duration depends on the extent of contamination and individual response.
- **Chelator Selection:** Chelation is most effective when administered within the first 24 hours of contamination. If continued therapy is required beyond the initial dose, it is preferable to switch to **Zn-DTPA** for maintenance to minimise the depletion of endogenous trace metals. If Zn-DTPA is unavailable, Ca-DTPA may continue with concomitant zinc supplementation [US].

### 3.2 Paediatric Dosing (Children <12 years)
- **Initial Dose:** 14 mg/kg administered intravenously, not to exceed a total dose of 1.0 gram.
- **Maintenance Dose:** 14 mg/kg once daily, not to exceed 1.0 gram per day [US].

### 3.3 Administration Instructions
- **Intravenous Push:** Administer the 5 mL solution (1 gram) slowly over 3–4 minutes.
- **Intravenous Infusion:** Dilute the 5 mL solution in 100–250 mL of 5% dextrose in water (D5W), Ringers Lactate, or Normal Saline.
- **Inhalation (Nebulisation):** For contamination via inhalation only (within the preceding 24 hours). Dilute the dose at a 1:1 ratio with sterile water or saline.
- **Hydration:** Patients must drink plenty of fluids and void frequently to promote radioactive chelate excretion and reduce radiation exposure to the bladder [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Trace Metal Depletion:** Ca-DTPA is associated with the depletion of endogenous metals (zinc, magnesium, manganese). Toxicity increases with split daily dosing, increased dosage, and prolonged duration. Only a single initial dose of Ca-DTPA is recommended before switching to Zn-DTPA for maintenance [US].
- **Hemochromatosis:** Use with caution. Deaths have been reported in patients with severe hemochromatosis receiving high doses (up to 4x recommended daily dose) for iron reduction, although a causal association with Ca-DTPA has not been established [US].
- **Asthma Exacerbation:** Nebulised therapy may trigger asthma attacks. Use with caution in patients with a history of reactive airway disease [US].
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline blood and urine samples (CBC with differential, electrolytes, BUN, urinalysis) and radioassays where possible. Monitor serum zinc and CBC regularly during treatment [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Clinical data from the U.S. Registry (n=632) indicates that approximately 6.1% of individuals reported at least one adverse event [US].

| System Organ Class | Adverse Effect | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **General/Site** | Headache, lightheadedness, chest pain, injection site reactions | Common (1–10%) |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea, diarrhoea, metallic taste | Common (1–10%) |
| **Respiratory** | Cough, wheezing (primarily with inhalation) | Rare (<0.1%) |
| **Skin** | Dermatitis, allergic reaction | Common (1–10%) |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Prolonged treatment leads to severe depletion of essential trace metals and potential suppression of haematopoiesis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Human Data:** Adequate drug-drug interaction studies have not been identified.
- **Multiple Contaminants:** If multiple radiocontaminants are present, additional therapies may be required (e.g., Prussian blue for caesium; potassium iodide for radioiodine) [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category C]. Animal data suggests Ca-DTPA is teratogenic and embryocidal (notably brain damage in canine studies). It is believed to affect DNA/RNA synthesis via zinc depletion. **Multiple doses are not recommended during pregnancy.** In cases of high internal contamination, Zn-DTPA should be used if available. A single dose of Ca-DTPA may be used in life-threatening contamination scenarios if Zn-DTPA is unavailable, ideally with zinc supplementation [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Radioactive metals are excreted in breast milk. Women with internal contamination should not breastfeed, regardless of chelation therapy. Precautions must be taken when discarding expressed milk to prevent secondary contamination [US].
- **Fertility:** Effects on human fertility and spermatogenesis have not been studied [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established for IV route via extrapolation from adult data. Safety and effectiveness for the nebulised route have not been established in children [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required. However, impaired renal function may decrease the rate of elimination and increase the serum half-life. In cases of severe contamination and renal failure, high-efficiency, high-flux dialysis may be used to assist elimination [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No specific data; however, clinical clearance is primarily renal [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Forms stable chelates with metal ions by exchanging calcium for metals with greater binding capacity (e.g., plutonium, americium, curium). These chelates are then excreted via glomerular filtration [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Poorly absorbed from the GI tract (~5%). Approximately 20% absorption following inhalation [US].
  - **Distribution:** Rapidly distributes into extracellular fluid; does not penetrate erythrocytes or other cells. No specific organ accumulation [US].
  - **Metabolism:** Minimal metabolic change [US].
  - **Elimination:** Excreted almost entirely (>99%) in urine within 24 hours. Plasma half-lives follow a tri-exponential model: 1.4 min, 14.5 min, and 94.4 min [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **U.S. Registry Data:** Clinical efficacy is based on observational data from 286 contaminated individuals. In 17 patients receiving a 1 g dose (IV or nebulised), the mean Urine Excretion Enhancement Factor (EEF) for transuranium elements was 25.7, indicating a significant increase in radioactivity elimination compared to pre-treatment levels [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store between 15°C and 30°C (59°F–86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Inspect for particulate matter and discoloration before use. Open the OPC ampoule by breaking the neck downward with the point facing upward [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription drug). Poison schedule/legal classification not otherwise specified [US].
