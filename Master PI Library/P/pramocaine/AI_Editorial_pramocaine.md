# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection, synthesized for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labelling for definitive guidance.
- **Note on Sources:** Although the request mentioned multiple potential regions, only the United States (FDA) source documentation was provided for this synthesis. Consequently, all regional attributions reflect [US] standards.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (quaternary ammonium compound).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (generic/proprietary names vary; e.g., Baxter [US]).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Administration:** Administer intravenously as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Metabolic Disorders:**
    - **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg [US].
    - **Initial Dosing:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Doses should be administered every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, adjusted based on clinical response. Doses as high as 300 mg/kg have been utilised.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Timing:** Administer after each dialysis session into the venous return line.
    - **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are adequate.
- **Monitoring:** 
    - Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels before parenteral therapy.
    - Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of IV administration. Discontinue treatment immediately if a reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizures:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation Caveat):** The safety of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal failure. High-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Parenteral Inspection:** Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting are common. Gastritis and body odor (related to metabolites) occur less frequently [US].
- **Serious Reactions:** Anaphylaxis and seizures [US].
- **ESRD Clinical Trial Data (≥5% Incidence):** In placebo-controlled trials of dialysis patients, the following events occurred regardless of causality [US]:

| Body System | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain (12%), Flu syndrome (25%), Headache (22%), Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%) |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%), Nausea (8%), Abdominal pain (9%) |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness (13%), Paresthesia (5%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (23%), Cough increase (9%), Dyspnea (7%) |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia (9%), Hyperkalemia (6%) |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. 
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving oral anticoagulants [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not explicitly assigned in source; however, reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm]. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Specific studies on supplementation in nursing mothers are unavailable. In dairy cows, milk concentrations increase following exogenous administration. Weigh the benefits of maternal treatment against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant; discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing guidelines for metabolic disorders apply to pediatric patients. In newborns and premature infants, carnitine deficiency is defined as plasma concentrations below age-related normal levels [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically in ESRD patients on dialysis (see Section 3). Safety of oral formulations is not established in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into the mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also facilitates the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Excretion:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Increases plasma carnitine concentrations; however, clinical outcomes (signs/symptoms) in the ESRD population specifically have not been definitively determined by carnitine supplementation alone [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Bioavailability:** Absolute bioavailability of oral formulations is low (approx. 15-16%).
- **ESRD Studies:** Clinical trials established that a dose of 20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis significantly increases plasma levocarnitine concentrations from subnormal levels (approx. 20 µmol/L) to trough levels well above normal (approx. 190-243 µmol/L) after 8 to 24 weeks of therapy [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Single-dose vial; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only. Poison schedule/classification not applicable for this category in the US, but requires a prescription [US].
