# Editorial Product Information: Pamidronate Disodium

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of pamidronate disodium for use by healthcare professionals, synthesising data from available regulatory sources.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for definitive regulatory guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pamidronate disodium (a member of the bisphosphonate chemical class).
- **Brand Names:** Pamisol [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** M05BA01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Concentrated injection for intravenous infusion. Available as 15 mg/5 mL, 30 mg/10 mL (3 mg/mL), 60 mg/10 mL (6 mg/mL), and 90 mg/10 mL (9 mg/mL) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous infusion only. **Pamidronate must never be administered as a bolus injection.**
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Pamidronate disodium is indicated for the treatment of conditions associated with increased osteoclast activity [Australian]:
- **Predominantly lytic bone metastases:** Associated with breast cancer or advanced multiple myeloma.
- **Tumour-induced hypercalcaemia (TIH):** Acute management of hypercalcaemia of malignancy.
- **Paget’s disease of bone:** Treatment of symptomatic cases.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Bone Metastases (Breast Cancer/Multiple Myeloma):** 90 mg via single infusion every 4 weeks. In patients receiving 3-weekly chemotherapy, a 3-weekly schedule may be used [Australian].
- **Paget’s Disease:** A single infusion of 60 mg [Australian].

### Tumour-induced Hypercalcaemia (TIH)
Rehydration with 0.9% sodium chloride is required before and during treatment. The total dose per course depends on initial serum calcium levels:
- **Serum Calcium up to 3.0 mmol/L:** 30 mg.
- **Serum Calcium 3.0–3.5 mmol/L:** 30 to 60 mg.
- **Serum Calcium 3.5–4.0 mmol/L:** 60 to 90 mg.
- **Serum Calcium >4.0 mmol/L:** 90 mg.
- *Note:* The maximum dose per treatment course is 90 mg [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Dilution:** Must be diluted in 0.9% sodium chloride or 5% glucose.
- **Infusion Rate:** Should not exceed 60 mg/hour (1 mg/min).
- **Concentration:** Should not exceed 90 mg/250 mL.
- **Specific Duration:** For breast cancer (90 mg), typically 2 hours in 250 mL. For multiple myeloma (90 mg), it is recommended to use 500 mL over 4 hours to reduce renal risk [Australian].
- **Site:** Use a relatively large vein to minimise local reactions.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to pamidronate disodium, other bisphosphonates, or any excipients [Australian].
- Pregnancy [Australian].
- Lactation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Renal Toxicity:** Deterioration of renal function and potential renal failure have been reported. Serum creatinine must be measured prior to each dose [Australian].
- **Osteonecrosis of the Jaw (ONJ):** Reported predominantly in cancer patients. Risk factors include invasive dental procedures, poor oral hygiene, and concomitant corticosteroid or chemotherapy use. A dental exam is recommended prior to treatment [Australian].
- **Atypical Femur Fractures:** Subtrochanteric and diaphyseal fractures may occur, often preceded by prodromal thigh or groin pain [Australian].
- **Osteonecrosis of the External Auditory Canal:** Associated with long-term therapy; consider in patients with chronic ear infections [Australian].
- **Cardiac Failure:** Use caution in patients with pre-existing cardiac disease, especially the elderly, as saline overload during rehydration may precipitate failure [Australian].
- **Electrolyte Shifts:** Onset of action is not immediate; convulsions have occurred due to electrolyte changes during hypercalcaemia treatment [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are usually mild and transient. Acute "influenza-like" symptoms typically occur only with the first infusion [Australian].

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Hypocalcaemia, hypophosphataemia, fever, influenza-like symptoms (malaise, chills, fatigue) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Hypomagnesaemia, hypokalaemia, anaemia, thrombocytopenia, lymphocytopenia, headache, somnolence, hypertension, nausea, vomiting, bone/joint pain, infusion site reactions (pain, redness, swelling) |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Acute renal failure, seizures, lethargy, hypotension, uveitis, iritis, dyspepsia, pruritus |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Focal segmental glomerulosclerosis, atypical femur fractures |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Anaphylactic shock, reactivation of herpes simplex/zoster, left ventricular failure, confusion, hallucinations |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Antitumour Agents:** No significant interactions noted with common drugs (e.g., cisplatin, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, tamoxifen) [Australian].
- **Thalidomide:** Increased risk of renal dysfunction when used concomitantly in multiple myeloma patients [Australian].
- **Calcitonin:** Synergistic effect in severe hypercalcaemia, leading to a more rapid fall in serum calcium [Australian].
- **Other Bisphosphonates:** Do not use concurrently [Australian].
- **Nephrotoxic Drugs:** Caution is warranted when used with other potentially nephrotoxic agents [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category B3. Pamidronate crosses the placenta in animal models and may cause fetal harm, including shortened bones and reduced ossification. It is contraindicated during pregnancy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. It is unknown if pamidronate passes into human milk, though animal studies indicate transmission via milk [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no impact on general fertility but noted abnormal parturition [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Limited experience; only recommended if life-threatening hypercalcaemia cannot be controlled by other measures [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific dose adjustments required unless cardiac disease is present (see Section 5) [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not recommended for patients with severe impairment (CrCl <30 mL/min) except in life-threatening TIH. In mild-to-moderate impairment, the infusion rate should not exceed 20-22 mg/h (90 mg over 4 hours) [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Pharmacokinetics are not significantly affected. A maximum infusion rate of 20 mg/h is suggested for mild-to-moderate impairment; no data exists for severe impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Potent inhibitor of osteoclastic bone resorption. It binds avidly to calcium apatite crystals, suppressing the transformation of osteoclast precursors into mature, resorbing cells [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Results in decreased urinary hydroxyproline, decreased calcium, and improved glomerular filtration rate (GFR) in hypercalcaemic patients [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Rapidly taken up by bone; 50% of a dose remains in the skeleton for years. Plasma protein binding is approximately 54%.
    - **Metabolism:** Not eliminated by biotransformation.
    - **Elimination:** 20–55% of the dose is excreted unchanged in the urine within 72 hours. Apparent plasma half-life is 0.8 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Multiple Myeloma:** A trial of 90 mg infusions (monthly) showed a significant reduction in skeletal-related events (SREs) compared to placebo (24% vs 41%) and a longer time to the first SRE [Australian].
- **Breast Cancer:** Two trials using 90 mg every 3–4 weeks demonstrated that pamidronate significantly reduced SREs, lowered the skeletal morbidity rate, and reduced pain scores compared to placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Undiluted:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Diluted Solution:** Chemically stable for 24 hours at 25°C (in light) or 2–8°C (in dark). To reduce microbiological risk, use immediately; if necessary, store at 2–8°C for no more than 24 hours [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** S4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
