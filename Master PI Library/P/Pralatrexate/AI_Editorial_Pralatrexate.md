# Editorial Product Information: Pralatrexate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive prescribing authority.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pralatrexate (an antineoplastic folate analogue).
- **Brand Name:** **Folotyn** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01BA05.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Solution for infusion. Each 1 mL contains 20 mg of pralatrexate. Available as a 20 mg in 1 mL single-use vial (a 40 mg in 2 mL presentation is registered but noted as unavailable in the source) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Mundipharma Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Peripheral T-cell Lymphoma (PTCL):** Indicated for the treatment of adult patients with PTCL (including nodal, extranodal, and leukaemic/disseminated subtypes) who have progressed after at least one prior therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Standard Dose:** 30 mg/m² administered once weekly for six weeks, followed by a one-week rest period (7-week cycle).
- **Treatment Duration:** Continue until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.

### Mandatory Premedication Regimen
To reduce the risk of haematological toxicity and mucosal inflammation, the following must be observed:
- **Folic Acid:** 1.0–1.25 mg orally daily, initiated 10 days before the first dose of pralatrexate and continued for 30 days after the final dose.
- **Vitamin B12:** 1 mg intramuscular (IM) injection no more than 10 weeks prior to the first dose and every 8–10 weeks thereafter.

### Administration Instructions
- Administer undiluted as an IV infusion over 3–5 minutes.
- Should be administered via the side port of a free-flowing 0.9% sodium chloride injection line.
- Do not administer by any other route.

### Dose Modifications
- **General Principle:** If a dose is omitted due to toxicity, it should not be made up at the end of the cycle. Once a dose is reduced, it should not be re-escalated [Australian].
- **Mucositis:** 
  - Grade 2 (first occurrence): Omit dose; resume at prior dose upon recovery to ≤ Grade 1.
  - Grade 2 (recurrence) or Grade 3: Omit dose; resume at 20 mg/m² upon recovery to ≤ Grade 1.
  - Grade 4: Discontinue therapy.
- **Haematological Toxicities:**
  - Platelets < 50,000/µL: Omit dose. Resume at prior dose if resolved in 1 week, or 20 mg/m² if resolved in 2 weeks. Discontinue if unresolved after 3 weeks.
  - ANC < 1,000/µL: Omit dose and/or implement G-CSF/GM-CSF support. Specific triggers for dose reduction to 20 mg/m² apply for prolonged or recurrent neutropenia.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to pralatrexate or any excipients.
- **Pregnancy:** Pralatrexate is contraindicated in pregnant women [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated during lactation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Bone Marrow Suppression:** Severe myelosuppression (thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, and anaemia) is common. Monitor full blood counts (FBC) weekly.
- **Mucosal Inflammation:** Frequent monitoring for mucositis is required. If ≥ Grade 2 is observed, doses must be omitted or reduced.
- **Dermatological Reactions:** Severe and potentially fatal skin reactions, including toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN) and skin exfoliation, have been reported. Reactions often occur at sites of known lymphoma and are more common in patients with extensive skin involvement. Discontinue if severe reactions occur.
- **Pneumonitis:** Cases of potentially fatal pneumonitis have been reported. Monitor for new or worsening respiratory symptoms.
- **Tumour Lysis Syndrome (TLS):** Reported in patients with lymphoma. Monitor closely and treat complications promptly.
- **Third Space Fluid Accumulation:** The effect of effusions (pleural, ascites) on pralatrexate is unknown; consideration should be given to draining significant effusions prior to treatment.
- **Sodium Content:** Contains less than 1 mmol (23 mg) of sodium per dose (essentially sodium-free) [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Data primarily based on the pivotal PDX-008 study.*

### Very Common (>10%)
- **Gastrointestinal:** Mucosal inflammation (68%), nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation.
- **Haematological:** Thrombocytopenia (40%), anaemia (32%), neutropenia (24%), leukopenia.
- **General:** Pyrexia, fatigue, peripheral oedema.
- **Other:** Musculoskeletal pain, epistaxis, anorexia, rash, abnormal liver function tests (AST/ALT).

### Common (1–10%)
- Sepsis, pneumonia, febrile neutropenia, dehydration, pruritus, alopecia, and dizziness.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Serious events include TEN, sepsis, febrile neutropenia, and TLS. Deaths from mucositis and sepsis have occurred [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Probenecid:** Avoid concomitant use. Probenecid significantly reduces pralatrexate clearance, increasing systemic exposure and toxicity [Australian].
- **Renal Secretion Inhibitors:** Use caution with NSAIDs, penicillins, and proton pump inhibitors (omeprazole, pantoprazole), as these may reduce the renal clearance of pralatrexate.
- **Nephrotoxic Drugs:** Avoid concurrent use of aminoglycosides, loop diuretics, platinum compounds, and cyclosporine.
- **Transporters:** Pralatrexate is a potent inhibitor of MRP3. Caution is advised when co-administered with etoposide, teniposide, or methotrexate.
- **Trimethoprim/Sulfamethoxazole:** Exercise caution due to potential additive antifolate effects and increased bone marrow suppression.
- **CYP450:** Low potential for interactions; pralatrexate is not a significant substrate, inhibitor, or inducer of major CYP enzymes.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. There is a risk of foetal harm and death. Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Safety in infants has not been established.
- **Fertility:** Antifolates may irreversibly affect fertility. Males should not father a child during treatment or for 6 months following the last dose.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients under 18 years; use is not recommended.
- **Elderly:** No overall differences in safety/efficacy compared to younger patients. However, dose reduction may be necessary if age-related renal impairment is present.
- **Renal Impairment:** Caution is advised in moderate-to-severe impairment (eGFR < 60 mL/min). Approximately 34% is excreted renally. Avoid use in end-stage renal disease (ESRD) and dialysis unless benefits clearly outweigh risks. Fatal skin reactions have been reported in dialysis patients [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not formally studied. Caution and close monitoring of liver function are required.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Pralatrexate is an antifolate designed to accumulate preferentially in cancer cells via the reduced folate carrier (RFC-1). Once inside, it is efficiently polyglutamated and inhibits dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR), disrupting DNA synthesis and leading to cell death.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Distribution:** 65% plasma protein bound.
  - **Metabolism:** Not significantly metabolised by CYP450 or glucuronidation enzymes.
  - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is 12–18 hours. Total systemic clearance is approximately 417 mL/min (S-diastereomer) and 191 mL/min (R-diastereomer).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study PDX-008:** An open-label, single-arm study of 111 treated patients with relapsed or refractory PTCL.
- **Outcomes:** The overall response rate (ORR) was 29% (n=32/109 evaluable). 11% achieved a complete response (CR/CRu). The median duration of response was 10.1 months.
- **Survival:** Median progression-free survival (PFS) was 3.5 months; median overall survival (OS) was 14.5 months.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C. Keep in the original carton to protect from light.
- **Temperature Excursion:** Unopened vials are stable at up to 30°C for a single period of up to 120 hours [Australian].
- **Post-Opening:** Contains no antimicrobial preservative; use immediately and discard any unused portion.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Poison Schedule S4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
