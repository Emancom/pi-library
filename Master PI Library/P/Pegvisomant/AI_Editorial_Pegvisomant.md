# Editorial Product Information: Pegvisomant

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Note on Jurisdictional Scope:** While this document is intended to harmonise information from multiple authorities, the current version is primarily derived from the Australian PI. Information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., US, European, Canadian, Japanese) is marked as "Not specified in available sources" where comparative data was not provided in the source material.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is an editorial synthesis and must not be used as a substitute for the official, current PI for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pegvisomant (rbe). It is a protein of recombinant DNA origin containing 191 amino acid residues, covalently bound to polyethylene glycol (PEG) polymers.
- **Chemical Class:** Growth hormone (GH) receptor antagonist.
- **Brand Name(s):** **SOMAVERT** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** H01AX01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):**
    - **Powder for Injection:** Available in single-dose vials containing 10 mg, 15 mg, or 20 mg of pegvisomant protein (equivalent to approximately 21 mg, 32 mg, and 43 mg of pegvisomant, respectively) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Subcutaneous injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acromegaly:** For the treatment of acromegaly in patients who have demonstrated an inadequate response to surgery, radiation, or other medical therapies, or for whom such treatments are deemed inappropriate. The clinical goal is the normalisation of serum Insulin-like Growth Factor-I (IGF-I) levels [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Loading Dose:** A single 80 mg dose must be administered subcutaneously under medical supervision [Australian].
- **Maintenance Dose:** Following the loading dose, the recommended starting dose is 10 mg once daily via subcutaneous injection [Australian].
- **Maximum Dose:** The maintenance dose should not exceed 30 mg per day [Australian].

### Titration Schedule
- Adjustments should be made in increments of 5 mg/day every 4 to 6 weeks, based on serum IGF-I levels. The objective is to maintain IGF-I within the age-adjusted normal range and manage clinical symptoms [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Reconstitution:** Reconstitute the lyophilised powder with 1 mL of the provided sterile Water for Injections. Aim the diluent at the vial wall and swirl gently. **Do not shake**, as this may denature the protein.
- **Injection Site:** Rotate injection sites daily to prevent lipohypertrophy.
- **Post-Reconstitution:** Use immediately or within 6 hours if stored at 2°C to 8°C [Australian].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** History of hypersensitivity to pegvisomant or any of the excipients (glycine, mannitol, dibasic sodium phosphate, and monobasic sodium phosphate monohydrate) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Pituitary Tumour Growth:** Growth hormone-secreting tumours may expand despite pegvisomant therapy, potentially causing visual field defects. Patients require regular monitoring (e.g., MRI) for tumour expansion [Australian].
- **GH Assay Cross-reactivity:** Pegvisomant is structurally similar to GH and cross-reacts in commercial GH assays. GH levels will appear elevated during treatment; therefore, clinical monitoring and dose adjustments must be based solely on IGF-I levels, not GH levels [Australian].
- **Functional GH Deficiency:** Excessive dosing may lead to a functional GH deficiency state if IGF-I levels fall below the normal range [Australian].

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Liver Function Tests (LFTs):** Baseline assessment of ALT, AST, TBIL, and ALP is mandatory.
- **Recommended Monitoring Frequency [Australian]:**
    - **Normal Baseline:** Monitor LFTs every 4–6 weeks for the first 6 months, then as clinically indicated.
    - **Baseline ≤3x ULN:** Monitor monthly for at least 1 year, then bi-annually.
    - **Baseline >3x ULN:** Do not initiate therapy until a full workup establishes the cause of dysfunction.

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Liver Injury:** Treatment should be discontinued immediately if ALT/AST is ≥5x ULN, or ≥3x ULN when associated with increased TBIL or symptoms of hepatitis [Australian].
- **Glucose Metabolism:** Pegvisomant increases insulin sensitivity. Diabetic patients may require dose reductions of insulin or oral hypoglycaemic agents to avoid hypoglycaemia [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (1–10% and higher)
- **General Disorders:** Injection site reactions (e.g., erythema, hypertrophy/lipohypertrophy), pain (8–14%), peripheral oedema, chest pain [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhoea (up to 14%), nausea (up to 14%).
- **Infections:** Influenza, sinusitis, upper respiratory infections.
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness, paresthesia.
- **Investigations:** Abnormal liver function tests (reported in up to 12% of patients in the 10 mg/day group) [Australian].

### Serious and Rare Adverse Effects
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Marked transaminase elevations (>10x ULN) occurred in 0.8% of clinical trial patients [Australian].
- **Systemic Hypersensitivity:** Rare reports of anaphylactic reactions, angioedema, and laryngospasm.
- **Immunogenicity:** Low-titre non-neutralising anti-GH antibodies were observed in 16.9% of patients; clinical significance is currently unknown [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Insulin and Oral Hypoglycaemics:** Increased insulin sensitivity may necessitate a reduction in the dosage of anti-diabetic medications [Australian].
- **Opioids:** Patients on concurrent opioid therapy may require higher doses of pegvisomant to achieve adequate IGF-I suppression. The mechanism is unknown [Australian].
- **Somatostatin Analogues:** Combined use has not been formally studied; clinicians should consider whether to continue analogues when initiating pegvisomant [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B3.** 
    - Animal studies (rabbits) showed no teratogenicity but noted increased post-implantation loss at high doses. There are no adequate studies in pregnant women. Pegvisomant is not recommended during pregnancy and should only be used if benefits clearly outweigh risks. IGF-I levels should be monitored closely if used [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if pegvisomant is excreted in human milk. Caution should be exercised when administered to breastfeeding women [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Therapeutic reduction of IGF-I may potentially increase fertility in female patients. Patients should be advised to use contraception if pregnancy is not desired [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include sufficient numbers of subjects ≥65 years to determine if they respond differently. Dosing should generally be cautious, starting at the low end of the range [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [Australian].
- **Body Weight:** Clearance increases with body weight; heavier individuals may require higher doses [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Pegvisomant is a selective GH receptor antagonist. It binds to GH receptors on cell surfaces, blocking endogenous GH binding and subsequent intracellular signal transduction. This results in decreased serum concentrations of IGF-I, free IGF-I, the acid-labile subunit (ALS), and IGFBP-3 [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Slow and prolonged. Peak concentrations are reached 33–77 hours post-dose. Bioavailability of a 20 mg SC dose is approximately 57% relative to IV [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Vd is relatively small (~7 L).
    - **Elimination:** Mean half-life is approximately 6 days. Renal clearance is negligible (<1%). Total body clearance is approximately 28 mL/h [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal RCT (12-week):** A double-blind study of 112 patients showed dose-dependent reductions in IGF-I. Normalisation of IGF-I was achieved in 38.5%, 75%, and 82% of patients receiving 10 mg, 15 mg, and 20 mg daily, respectively, compared to 9.7% in the placebo group [Australian].
- **Long-term Study:** An open-label titration study (n=38) showed that 94.7% of patients achieved normal IGF-I levels with a mean duration of 19 months of treatment [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials (Unopened):** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze). Protect from light by keeping vials in the original carton [Australian].
- **Room Temperature Allowance:** The carton may be stored at room temperature (up to 25°C) for a single period of up to 30 days. It must not be returned to the refrigerator after this period and must be discarded if not used [Australian].
- **Diluent:** Store at room temperature (below 25°C) or in the refrigerator (2°C to 8°C) [Australian].
