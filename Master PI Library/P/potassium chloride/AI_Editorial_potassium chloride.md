# Editorial Product Information: Potassium Chloride

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis incorporates provided Product Information (PI) from the **Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA)** of Australia and the **Food and Drug Administration (FDA)** of the United States.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of potassium chloride as it appears in distinct therapeutic contexts (haemofiltration and oral supplementation) for the reference of healthcare professionals.
- This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI when prescribing or administering therapy in a specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Potassium chloride. 
- **Chemical Class:** Electrolyte/Potassium Salt.
- **Brand Names:** 
    - **ACCUSOL 35** (K0, K2, and K4 formulations) [TGA].
    - Generic and various manufacturers [FDA].
- **ATC Code:** B05ZA (Haemodialysates); A12BA01 (Potassium chloride).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Haemofiltration Solution [TGA]:** 5000 mL two-chamber bags (after mixing):
        - **K0:** 0 mmol/L Potassium.
        - **K2:** 2 mmol/L Potassium.
        - **K4:** 4 mmol/L Potassium.
        - *Also contains:* Sodium (140 mmol/L), Calcium (1.75 mmol/L), Magnesium (0.50 mmol/L), Bicarbonate (35 mmol/L), Chloride (109.3–113.3 mmol/L), and Glucose (0 or 5.55 mmol/L).
    - **Oral Solution [FDA]:**
        - **10%:** 1.3 mEq (1.3 mmol) potassium per mL.
        - **20%:** 2.6 mEq (2.6 mmol) potassium per mL.
- **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous (via extracorporeal circulation) [TGA]; Oral [FDA].
- **Sponsor/Manufacturer:** AA-Med Pty Ltd [TGA]; Amneal Pharmaceuticals NY LLC [FDA].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Continuous Renal Replacement Therapy (CRRT):** Used as a replacement fluid in intensive care units for the treatment of acute kidney injury [TGA].
- **Hypokalemia:** Treatment and prophylaxis of hypokalemia (with or without metabolic alkalosis) in patients where dietary management or diuretic dose reduction is insufficient [FDA].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Haemofiltration [TGA]:**
    - Only prescribed by physicians experienced in CRRT.
    - **Effluent Volume:** Typically 20–35 mL/kg/h (to deliver 20–25 mL/kg/h).
    - **Maximum Volume:** 75 L/day.
    - **Duration:** Discontinued when renal function is restored. Maximum intended use for a single CRRT line is 72 hours.
- **Oral Supplementation (Adults) [FDA]:**
    - **Treatment:** 40 mEq to 100 mEq/day in 2 to 5 divided doses. Individual doses should be limited to 40 mEq. Maximum daily dose: 200 mEq.
    - **Maintenance/Prophylaxis:** Typically 20 mEq per day.
    - **Note:** If serum potassium is < 2.5 mEq/L, intravenous rather than oral supplementation is recommended.

### Administration Instructions
- **Haemofiltration [TGA]:** 
    - Infused intravenously via arterial or venous lines using a metering pump.
    - Must be warmed to 36.5°C – 38.0°C immediately before infusion.
    - Requires strict aseptic mixing of the two chambers (Electrolyte Chamber A and Buffer Chamber B) and inspection for leaks/precipitates.
- **Oral Solution [FDA]:** 
    - Must be diluted with at least 4 ounces (approx. 120 mL) of cold water or other beverage.
    - Should be taken with meals or immediately after eating to reduce gastrointestinal irritation.

## 4. Contraindications
- **General:** Metabolic alkalosis [TGA; FDA].
- **Haemofiltration Specific [TGA]:**
    - **K0 and K2 strengths:** Hypokalaemia without simultaneous adapted potassium supplementation.
    - **K4 strength:** Hyperkalaemia without simultaneous adapted potassium supplementation.
    - Renal failure with pronounced protein catabolism (if uraemic symptoms cannot be corrected by CRRT).
    - Inadequate blood flow or arterial pressure from vascular access.
    - States with high risk of haemorrhage (e.g., systemic anticoagulation).
- **Oral Solution Specific [FDA]:** 
    - Concomitant use with potassium-sparing diuretics.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
- **Monitoring Requirements:**
    - **Electrolytes and Balance:** Regular monitoring of serum potassium, sodium, chloride, calcium, magnesium, and phosphate is essential. 
    - **Potassium specifically:** Monitor daily (or more frequently) during acute treatment until levels return to normal; monitor monthly to biannually for maintenance [FDA].
    - **Fluid Balance:** Establish accurate fluid balance records and monitor patient weight to avoid hyper- or hypo-hydration [TGA].
    - **Acid-Base:** Regular monitoring of acid-base balance (e.g., chloraemia, bicarbonate) is required.
- **Gastrointestinal Irritation:** Oral solutions may cause irritation if administered undiluted [FDA].
- **Hypoglycaemia:** Potassium-free haemofiltration solutions (K0) do not contain glucose; blood glucose must be monitored, especially in diabetic patients [TGA].
- **Precipitation:** Precipitation of calcium carbonate may occur several hours after starting haemofiltration. If particles are observed, the fluid and CRRT lines must be replaced immediately [TGA].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Haemofiltration-Related [TGA]
| System Organ Class | Adverse Reaction | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Metabolic & Nutritional** | Hypoglycaemia | Rare (<0.1%) |
| **Metabolic & Nutritional** | Hypophosphataemia, Electrolyte disturbances, Fluid imbalance | Not specified |
| **Vascular** | Hypotension, Hypertension | Not specified |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea, vomiting | Not specified |
| **Musculoskeletal** | Muscle cramps | Not specified |
| **Procedural** | Bleeding, clotting, infection, air embolism | Not specified |

### Oral Supplementation-Related [FDA]
- **Common:** Nausea, vomiting, flatulence, abdominal pain/discomfort, and diarrhea.
- **Serious (Overdose/Rapid Infusion):** Hyperkalemia leading to characteristic ECG changes (T-wave peaking, P-wave loss, S-T depression, QT-prolongation), muscle paralysis, and potentially fatal cardiac arrest.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Potassium-Sparing Diuretics:** Avoid concomitant use; risk of severe hyperkalemia [FDA; TGA].
- **RAAS Inhibitors (ACE inhibitors, ARBs, Aliskiren):** Produce potassium retention; monitor potassium levels closely [FDA].
- **NSAIDs:** May impair the renin-angiotensin system and cause potassium retention [FDA].
- **Cardiac Glycosides (e.g., Digoxin):** Increased risk of arrhythmias if hypokalaemia occurs [TGA]. Rapid lowering of serum potassium in digitalis-stabilized patients can also induce toxicity [FDA].
- **Vitamin D/Calcium-containing medicines:** Increased risk of hypercalcaemia [TGA].
- **Drug Clearance (CRRT):** CRRT may reduce the blood concentration of drugs with low protein binding, small distribution volume, or those likely to adsorb to the haemofilter [TGA].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:**
    - **TGA:** No animal or clinical data; use only after assessment of risks and benefits.
    - **FDA:** Potassium supplementation that does not lead to hyperkalemia is not expected to cause fetal harm.
- **Breastfeeding:**
    - **TGA:** No animal or clinical data; use only after assessment of risks and benefits.
    - **FDA:** Normal potassium content in human milk is ~13 mEq/L. Supplementation should have little effect on milk levels as long as maternal serum levels are not excessive.
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility anticipated at physiological concentrations [TGA].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:**
    - **TGA:** No clinical experience or dosing recommendations available for haemofiltration.
    - **FDA:** Safety and efficacy established for children (birth to 16 years). Treatment: 2–4 mEq/kg/day (not to exceed 1 mEq/kg as single dose). Maintenance: 1 mEq/kg/day (not to exceed 3 mEq/kg/day).
- **Geriatric Use:** 
    - Use caution; typically initiate at the low end of the dosing range due to the higher frequency of decreased renal, hepatic, or cardiac function [FDA].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - Increased risk of hyperkalemia. Initiate therapy at the low end of the dosing range and monitor serum potassium frequently [FDA].
- **Hepatic Impairment (Cirrhosis):**
    - Initiate therapy at the low end of the dosing range and monitor serum potassium frequently [FDA].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Potassium is the principal intracellular cation. It is essential for maintaining intracellular tonicity, nerve impulse transmission, cardiac/skeletal muscle contraction, and normal renal function.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** 100% bioavailability for intravenous administration [TGA]. Oral solution absorption is faster during the first few hours compared to modified-release products [FDA].
    - **Distribution:** Regulated by osmotic and concentration gradients between intra- and extra-cellular spaces. 
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal; also eliminated via the gut, lung, and skin.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Haemofiltration:** A clinical trial of 16 patients requiring high-volume CRRT for severe acute kidney injury reported one episode of rare, solution-related hypoglycaemia [TGA].
- **Oral Supplementation:** Efficacy is documented through clinical experience and published literature [FDA].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Haemofiltration Solution [TGA]:**
    - Store below 30°C. 
    - Do not freeze. 
    - Once mixed, use within 24 hours.
- **Oral Solution [FDA]:**
    - Store at 20°C to 25°C (excursions permitted between 15°C to 30°C).
    - **Protect from light and freezing.**
    - Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia [TGA]:** Unscheduled (Poison Standard).
- **United States [FDA]:** Rx only.
