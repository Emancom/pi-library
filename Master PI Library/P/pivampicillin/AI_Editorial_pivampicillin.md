# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Note:** This synthesis is an editorial consolidation and does not replace the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing and dispensing in any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a naturally occurring carrier molecule involved in mammalian energy metabolism.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [United States].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not explicitly specified in the source document).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [United States].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from genetic metabolic disorders [United States].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients undergoing chronic hemodialysis [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It may be administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [United States].
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing intervals should be every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) [United States].
- **Maintenance Dosing:** Daily doses typically remain in the 50 mg/kg range, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilized if clinically required [United States].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Initial Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session [United States].
- **Dose Adjustments:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels. Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [United States].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [United States].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8 mg/mL [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. Treatment must be discontinued immediately if a severe reaction occurs [United States].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with reports of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [United States].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** While this PI concerns the intravenous formulation, it notes that chronic high-dose **oral** levocarnitine in patients with severely impaired renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [United States].
- **Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels (target: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is recommended [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common and Serious Effects
- **General:** Injection site reactions (33%), fever (7%), and body odor [United States].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting are common. In dialysis trials, the following were reported: diarrhea (16%), abdominal pain (9%), and vomiting (15%) [United States].
- **Neurological:** Dizziness (13%), headache (22%), and paresthesia (5%). Seizures have been reported in post-marketing surveillance [United States].
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), chest pain (12%), and tachycardia (6%) [United States].
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), and dyspnea (7%) [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. Monitoring of INR is recommended upon initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine in patients on anticoagulant therapy [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [United States]: Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits using up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate, well-controlled studies exist in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Animal data (dairy cows) shows increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine must be weighed against maternal benefits [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is weight-based (mg/kg), applicable across age groups [United States].
- **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency [United States].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This provides the substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Distribution half-life is approximately 0.585 hours [United States].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [United States].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis, IV administration of 20 mg/kg three times weekly significantly increased plasma carnitine levels. Trough levels rose from a baseline of 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks [United States].
- **Note:** While IV levocarnitine effectively increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the impact on specific clinical outcomes or signs/symptoms of deficiency in this population has not been definitively determined [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [United States].
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [United States].
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [United States].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** [United States]: Rx-only (Prescription Only).
