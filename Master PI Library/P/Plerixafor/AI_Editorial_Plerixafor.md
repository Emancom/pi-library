# Editorial Product Information: Plerixafor

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing and dispensing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for regional legal compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Plerixafor (a reversible CXCR4 chemokine receptor antagonist).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Mozobil** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Solution for injection. Each single-use vial contains 24 mg of plerixafor in 1.2 mL (20 mg/mL) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** sanofi-aventis australia pty ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Haematopoietic Stem Cell (HSC) Mobilisation:** Indicated in combination with granulocyte-colony stimulating factor (G-CSF) to mobilise HSCs to the peripheral blood for collection and subsequent autologous transplantation in patients with:
    - Non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma (NHL) [Australian].
    - Multiple myeloma (MM) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The recommended dose is 0.24 mg/kg body weight daily by SC injection.
- **Administration Timing:** Plerixafor should be administered 6 to 11 hours prior to the initiation of apheresis. 
- **Treatment Sequence:** Initiate treatment after the patient has received G-CSF once daily for four days. G-CSF should continue to be administered each morning prior to apheresis.
- **Duration:** Typically used for 2 to 4 consecutive days; it has been used for up to 7 consecutive days in clinical settings [Australian].
- **Calculation:** Dose is based on actual body weight (obtained within 1 week of the first dose). 
    - *Calculation formula:* 0.012 x actual body weight (kg) = volume to be administered (mL).
    - Data is limited for patients weighing more than 175% of ideal body weight [Australian].
- **Method of Administration:** SC injection into the abdomen is recommended; however, extremities may be used. 

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to plerixafor or any of the excipients (sodium chloride, water for injection) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Tumour Cell Mobilisation (Leukaemia):** Not recommended for HSC mobilisation in patients with leukaemia due to the risk of mobilising leukaemic cells and contaminating the apheresis product.
- **Splenic Effects:** Cases of splenic enlargement and/or rupture have been reported. Patients reporting left upper abdominal pain and/or scapular/shoulder pain must be evaluated for splenic integrity.
- **Haematological Monitoring:** 
    - **Leukocytosis:** Plerixafor increases circulating leukocytes. Monitor white blood cell (WBC) counts; exercise caution if neutrophil counts exceed 50 x 10⁹/L.
    - **Thrombocytopenia:** Monitor platelet counts in all patients undergoing apheresis.
- **Allergic Reactions:** Rare cases of anaphylactic reactions, including shock, have been reported. Most mild-to-moderate reactions (e.g., urticaria, dyspnoea) occur within 30 minutes of administration.
- **Vasovagal Reactions:** Orthostatic hypotension and/or syncope may occur (incidence <1%), typically within 1 hour of injection.
- **Driving and Machinery:** Caution is advised due to potential dizziness, fatigue, or vasovagal reactions [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (≥10%):** Diarrhoea, nausea, injection site reactions, fatigue, headache, dizziness, arthralgia, and insomnia.
- **Common (1–10%):** Vomiting, flatulence, abdominal pain, and hyperhidrosis.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** 
    - Anaphylactic shock.
    - Splenic rupture.
    - Myocardial infarction (observed in clinical trials, though a direct causal link to plerixafor was not established due to existing patient risk profiles).
- **Post-Marketing Experience:** Abnormal dreams and nightmares have been reported [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Profile:** Plerixafor is not a substrate or inhibitor of P-glycoprotein.
- **Cytochrome P450:** In vitro studies indicate no induction or inhibition of major CYP450 enzymes (1A2, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 3A4/5), suggesting a low potential for drug-drug interactions.
- **Rituximab:** No impact on safety or CD34+ cell yield was observed when combined with rituximab [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category D:** Drugs which have caused, are suspected to have caused, or may be expected to cause an increased incidence of human foetal malformations or irreversible damage. 
    - Plerixafor was teratogenic in animal studies. Females of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during treatment and for one week following the final dose. 
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if plerixafor is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment.
- **Fertility:** No evidence of accumulation in testes or histopathological changes in reproductive organs was observed in animal studies [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in controlled clinical studies [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No notable differences in safety or efficacy were observed in patients ≥65 years compared to younger patients.
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Moderate to Severe (CrCl 20–50 mL/min):** Reduce dose by one-third to **0.16 mg/kg**.
    - **CrCl <20 mL/min or Haemodialysis:** Insufficient information to make dosage recommendations [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources (metabolism is not hepatic).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A reversible antagonist of the CXCR4 chemokine receptor. It blocks the binding of stromal cell-derived factor-1α (SDF-1α/CXCL12), which normally anchors HSCs to the bone marrow matrix. Disruption of this binding results in the release of stem cells into the systemic circulation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed after SC injection (Tmax: 30–60 minutes). Bioavailability ≥70%.
    - **Distribution:** Moderately bound to plasma proteins (37–58%). Volume of distribution is 0.3 L/kg.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (70% excreted unchanged in urine). Half-life is 3–5 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:**
    - **AMD3100-3101 (NHL):** 59.3% of the plerixafor group achieved the primary endpoint (≥5 x 10⁶ CD34+ cells/kg in ≤4 days) vs 19.6% in the placebo group.
    - **AMD3100-3102 (MM):** 71.6% of the plerixafor group achieved the primary endpoint (≥6 x 10⁶ CD34+ cells/kg in ≤2 days) vs 34.4% in the placebo group.
- **Engraftment:** No differences were observed between treatment groups regarding time to neutrophil or platelet engraftment, or graft durability up to 12 months post-transplant [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 25°C; excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C.
- **Handling:** Single-use vial only. The solution should be clear and colourless to pale yellow. Any unused portion must be discarded.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
