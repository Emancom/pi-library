# Editorial Product Information: Penicillin G Benzathine and Penicillin G Procaine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information from the **United States (FDA)**.
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Penicillin G Benzathine and Penicillin G Procaine.
- **Chemical Class:** Penicillin-class antibacterial.
- **Brand Names:** **Bicillin C-R** [US].
- **ATC Code:** J01CE30 (Penicillins, combinations).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injectable suspension in a 2 mL disposable syringe containing 1,200,000 units of penicillin G (600,000 units as the benzathine salt and 600,000 units as the procaine salt) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Deep Intramuscular (IM) injection only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Laboratories Div Pfizer Inc [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Bicillin C-R** is indicated for the treatment of moderately severe to severe infections caused by penicillin-G-susceptible microorganisms when therapy is guided by bacteriological studies and clinical response.

- **Streptococcal Infections (Group A):** Upper-respiratory tract infections, scarlet fever, erysipelas, and skin/soft-tissue infections [US].
- **Pneumococcal Infections:** Moderately severe pneumonia and otitis media (excluding pneumococcal meningitis) [US].
- **Important Limitations of Use:**
    - Not indicated for the treatment of venereal diseases, including syphilis, gonorrhea, yaws, bejel, and pinta [US].
    - Not recommended for streptococcal infections involving bacteremia; penicillin G sodium or potassium should be used for acute stages of severe pneumonia, empyema, meningitis, and pericarditis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric)
Treatment is typically administered in a single session using multiple IM sites if necessary [US].

### Dosing by Indication [US]
- **Group A Streptococcal Infections:**
    - Adults and Pediatrics (>60 lbs): 2,400,000 units.
    - Pediatrics (30 to 60 lbs): 900,000 to 1,200,000 units.
    - Pediatrics (<30 lbs): 600,000 units.
- **Pneumococcal Infections (except meningitis):**
    - Adults: 1,200,000 units.
    - Pediatrics: 600,000 units.
    - Schedule: Repeated every 2 to 3 days until the patient has been afebrile for 48 hours.

### Administration Instructions
- **Strictly for Deep IM Injection:** Do not inject intravenously or near a major nerve/artery [US].
- **Site Selection:** Administer in the upper, outer quadrant of the buttock (dorsogluteal) or the ventrogluteal site. In neonates and small children, the midlateral aspect of the thigh may be preferable.
- **Technique:** Inject at a slow, steady rate to avoid needle blockage due to high suspended material concentration [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known previous hypersensitivity reaction to any penicillin or to procaine [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning [US]
- **NOT FOR INTRAVENOUS USE:** Inadvertent intravenous administration has been associated with cardiorespiratory arrest and death. Do not admix with other intravenous solutions.

### General Warnings
- **Anaphylaxis:** Serious and occasionally fatal hypersensitivity (anaphylactic) reactions have been reported. Inquiry into previous reactions to penicillins and cephalosporins is mandatory before initiation [US].
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCAR):** SJS, TEN, DRESS, and AGEP have been reported. Discontinue immediately if SCAR is suspected [US].
- **Methemoglobinemia:** Associated with local anesthetic use (procaine). Patients with G6PD deficiency or infants under 6 months are at higher risk. Monitor for cyanosis and treat promptly [US].
- **Clostridioides difficile-associated Diarrhea (CDAD):** May range from mild diarrhea to fatal colitis [US].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Neurovascular Damage:** Accidental intravascular injection can cause Nicolau syndrome, transverse myelitis, gangrene, and permanent neurological damage, particularly in infants and small children [US].
- **Procaine Sensitivity:** A small percentage of patients are sensitive to procaine. If suspected, an intradermal test (0.1 mL of 1-2% procaine) should be performed prior to use [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Anaphylaxis and SCAR:** Shock, death, SJS, and TEN [US].
- **Hoigne's Syndrome:** A pseudo-anaphylactic reaction (agitation, confusion, hallucinations, fear of death) reported after procaine penicillin administration [US].
- **Nicolau Syndrome:** Resulting from accidental intravascular injection [US].
- **Kounis Syndrome:** Acute myocardial ischemia as part of an allergic reaction [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Tetracycline:** May antagonize the bactericidal effect of penicillin; avoid concurrent use [US].
- **Probenecid:** Increases and prolongs serum penicillin levels by inhibiting renal tubular secretion [US].
- **Methemoglobinemia-inducing agents:** Increased risk when used concurrently with nitrates/nitrites, antimalarials (chloroquine), or anticonvulsants (phenobarbital) [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified, but text indicates Category B-like status]. Reproduction studies in animals (mice, rats, rabbits) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Soluble penicillin G is excreted in breast milk. Caution should be exercised when administering to nursing women [US].
- **Fertility:** No long-term animal studies have been conducted to evaluate carcinogenic or mutagenic potential or impairment of fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use is approved; however, repeated injections into the anterolateral thigh should be avoided due to risks of quadriceps femoris fibrosis and atrophy [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Start at the low end of the dosing range. Monitoring of renal function is recommended due to the high rate of renal excretion [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Excretion is considerably delayed in patients with impaired kidney function; dose adjustments may be necessary based on clinical judgment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal; inhibits the biosynthesis of cell-wall peptidoglycan during active multiplication, leading to osmotic instability and bacterial death [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Low solubility provides a slow release from IM sites. Peak levels (1.0 to 1.3 units/mL for 600k units) are reached within 3 hours [US].
    - **Distribution:** Approximately 60% protein bound. Widely distributed; highest levels in kidneys, lower in liver and skin. Limited penetration into spinal fluid [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Hydrolyzed to penicillin G [US].
    - **Elimination:** Excreted rapidly via tubular excretion in the kidneys [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store in a refrigerator between 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) [US].
- **Special Handling:** Keep from freezing. Protect from light [US].
