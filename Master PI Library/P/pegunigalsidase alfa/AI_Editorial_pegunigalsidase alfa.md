# Editorial Product Information: Pegunigalsidase alfa

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Regulatory Agencies:** Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA - Australia); Food and Drug Administration (FDA - United States).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of pegunigalsidase alfa, synthesising information from primary international regulatory sources for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information or Prescribing Information required for clinical practice within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pegunigalsidase alfa (pegunigalsidase alfa-iwxj [FDA])
- **Chemical Class:** Pegylated recombinant form of human alpha-galactosidase-A; hydrolytic lysosomal neutral glycosphingolipid-specific enzyme.
- **Brand Name:** **Elfabrio**
- **ATC Code:** A16AB20
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Concentrated solution for intravenous (IV) infusion (2 mg/mL).
    - 20 mg/10 mL single-dose vial [TGA, FDA].
    - 5 mg/2.5 mL single-dose vial [FDA].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Chiesi Australia Pty Ltd [TGA]; Chiesi USA, Inc. [FDA]. Produced in tobacco cells (*Nicotiana tabacum* BY2) using recombinant DNA technology.

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Fabry Disease:** Indicated for long-term enzyme replacement therapy (ERT) in adult patients with a confirmed diagnosis of Fabry disease.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Recommended Dose:** 1 mg/kg of body weight administered once every two weeks.

### Preparation and Administration
- **Dilution:** Must be diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride. The total infusion volume is determined by patient weight:
    - < 70 kg: 150 mL
    - 70–100 kg: 250 mL
    - > 100 kg: 500 mL
- **Filtration:** Administer via an in-line, low protein-binding 0.2 μm filter.
- **Incompatibilities:** Do not infuse in the same intravenous line with other products.

### Infusion Rates
- **Initial Infusions (First 4–6 doses):** 
    - **ERT-experienced:** Start at ~0.83–2.78 mL/min based on weight (target duration ≥ 3 hours).
    - **ERT-naïve:** Start at ~0.63–1.38 mL/min based on weight [FDA].
- **Maintenance:** If tolerated, infusion duration may be gradually decreased to a minimum of 1.5 hours.

### Pre-treatment
- Pre-treatment with antihistamines, antipyretics, and/or corticosteroids may be considered, particularly for ERT-naïve patients or those with a history of hypersensitivity to prior ERT.

### Home Administration
- May be considered for patients tolerating infusions well with no history of moderate-to-severe infusion-related reactions (IRRs) for several months. Must be performed by a healthcare professional.

### Missed Doses
- Restart treatment as soon as possible, maintaining the 2-week interval thereafter. Do not double the dose [FDA].

## 4. Contraindications
- **[TGA]:** Hypersensitivity to the active substance or any excipients (sodium citrate dihydrate, citric acid, sodium chloride).
- **[FDA]:** None listed in the Highlights section of the Prescribing Information.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [FDA]:** **Hypersensitivity Reactions Including Anaphylaxis.** Appropriate medical support, including cardiopulmonary resuscitation equipment, must be available. If severe reactions occur, discontinue immediately.
- **Infusion-Related Reactions (IRRs):** Defined as adverse events occurring during or within 2 hours [TGA] to 24 hours [FDA] post-infusion. Common symptoms include nausea, chills, dizziness, and rash. Management involves slowing or holding the infusion and administering symptomatic treatment.
- **Membranoproliferative Glomerulonephritis:** Immune complex deposition has been reported, leading to declined renal function. 
    - **Monitoring:** Monitor serum creatinine and urinary protein-to-creatinine ratio. Discontinue therapy if glomerulonephritis is suspected.
- **Immunogenicity:** Anti-drug antibodies (ADA), including IgE and IgG, may develop. ADA-positive patients are at a higher risk for IRRs and may exhibit altered pharmacokinetics.
- **Cardiac Function:** Patients with advanced Fabry disease and compromised cardiac function should be closely monitored due to the higher risk of severe complications from IRRs.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Infusion-associated reactions (approx. 32%), nasopharyngitis (21%), headache (21%), diarrhoea (19%), fatigue (17%), nausea (17%), back pain (15%), pain in extremity (15%), and sinusitis (15%).
- **Common (1–10%):** Abdominal pain, proteinuria, dizziness, vomiting, cough, pyrexia, muscle spasms, rash, and arthralgia.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Agitation, insomnia, peripheral neuropathy, vertigo, bradycardia, hypotension/hypertension, bronchospasm, and dyspnoea.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, Type I hypersensitivity, and membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No formal interaction studies have been performed. Pegunigalsidase alfa is a protein expected to undergo peptide hydrolysis; it is an unlikely candidate for cytochrome P450-mediated interactions.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:**
    - **[TGA]:** Category B3. (Drugs taken by only a limited number of pregnant women and women of childbearing age, without an increase in the frequency of malformation or other direct or indirect harmful effects on the human fetus having been observed).
    - **[FDA]:** No available human data on use during pregnancy to evaluate drug-associated risks. Animal studies showed no malformations but noted increased skeletal alterations and fetotoxicity at high exposures.
    - **Recommendation:** Preferably avoid during pregnancy unless clearly necessary [TGA].
- **Breastfeeding:** Milk excretion was demonstrated in rats. It is unknown if it is excreted in human milk. A decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the benefit to the mother and infant.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children aged 0–17 years have not been established.
- **Geriatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients ≥ 65 years have not been established [TGA]. The FDA notes clinical trials did not include sufficient numbers of patients in this age group to determine if they respond differently.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required [TGA]. As a protein, it is not expected to be cleared by the liver or filtered by the kidneys.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Supplements or replaces deficient lysosomal alpha-galactosidase A. This catalyses the hydrolysis of terminal α-galactosyl moieties, reducing the accumulation of globotriaosylceramide (Gb3) and its metabolite lyso-Gb3.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Distribution:** Total molecular weight approx. 116 kDa (prevents glomerular filtration). 
    - **Metabolism:** Degraded via peptide hydrolysis into small peptides.
    - **Elimination Half-life (t½):** Increases with treatment duration, ranging from approx. 79 hours (Day 1) to 121 hours (Week 52).
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Treatment results in significant reductions in plasma lyso-Gb3 in ERT-naïve patients.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Trial 1 (ERT-naïve/Open-label):** Demonstrated a reduction in the average number of Gb3 inclusions per renal peritubular capillary (PTC) via BLISS score. Median BLISS score reduced from 3.2 at baseline to 0.7 at 26 weeks.
- **BALANCE Trial (ERT-experienced/Active-controlled):** Compared pegunigalsidase alfa to agalsidase beta. The primary endpoint was the annualised rate of change in eGFR (slope).
    - **Results:** The eGFR slope was -2.4 mL/min/1.73 m²/year for pegunigalsidase alfa vs -2.3 mL/min/1.73 m²/year for agalsidase beta, demonstrating comparable efficacy in maintaining renal function.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze or shake.
- **Diluted Solution:** 
    - **[TGA]:** Use immediately. Can be stored for 24 hours at 2°C to 8°C or 6 hours below 25°C.
    - **[FDA]:** Can be stored for 24 hours at 2°C to 8°C or 8 hours at 20°C to 25°C (inclusive of infusion time).

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule [TGA]:** Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4).
- **Legal Classification [FDA]:** Rx Only.
