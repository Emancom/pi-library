# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection, synthesised for use by healthcare professionals. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive prescribing requirements. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP); chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion. In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours, and not less than every 6 hours. Subsequent daily doses typically remain in the 50 mg/kg range, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been used [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Monitoring and Titration
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- **ESRD:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels <40–50 µmol/L. Downward dose adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough carnitine concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known seizure disorder, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **Oral vs. Intravenous Use:** The safety/efficacy of oral levocarnitine in patients with renal insufficiency has not been established. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) normally excreted in urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported at a frequency of ≥5% in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials for ESRD patients (regardless of causality) include:

| Body System | Adverse Event (≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, palpitations |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, anorexia, dyspepsia |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight changes |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, depression, paresthesia |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis |
| **Skin** | Pruritus, rash |
| **Other** | Leg cramps, eye disorder, urinary tract infection |

### Other Reported Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis and transient nausea/vomiting.
- **Body Odor:** Reported as a less frequent reaction [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not assigned a specific letter category in provided text, but states use only if clearly needed]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. In dairy cows, levocarnitine concentrations in milk increase following exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is based on mg/kg body weight (see Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources; however, ESRD dosing is applicable to the elderly within that population.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for use in ESRD patients. Caution is advised regarding the use of oral formulations in this population due to metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine clears accumulated acyl-CoA compounds by forming acylcarnitine, which is rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), the mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD PK Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis), mean pre-dialysis trough concentrations increased from 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L at baseline to 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** Trough levocarnitine concentrations increase proportionally with doses of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing. 
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only [US].
