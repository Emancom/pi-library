# Editorial Product Information: Pozelimab

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of this publication, only the United States PI was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pozelimab-bbfg; a recombinant human monoclonal immunoglobulin G4 (IgG4) antibody.
- **Brand Name:** **Veopoz** [US]
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injection (solution); 400 mg/2 mL (200 mg/mL) in a single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion (loading dose) and Subcutaneous (SC) injection (maintenance) [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Regeneron Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **CD55-deficient protein-losing enteropathy (PLE):** Indicated for the treatment of adult and paediatric patients aged 1 year and older with CD55-deficient PLE, also known as CHAPLE disease [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Requirements
Prior to the first dose, clinicians must verify the patient’s meningococcal vaccination status. Vaccination should be completed at least 2 weeks before starting therapy unless the risk of delaying treatment outweighs the risk of infection [US].

### Dosing Schedule
- **Day 1 (Loading Dose):** A single 30 mg/kg dose administered via intravenous infusion after appropriate dilution [US].
- **Day 8 and Thereafter (Maintenance Dose):** 10 mg/kg administered via subcutaneous injection once weekly [US].
- **Dose Titration:** If clinical response is inadequate after at least three weekly doses (starting from Week 4), the maintenance dosage may be increased to 12 mg/kg once weekly [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The maximum maintenance dosage is 800 mg once weekly [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Intravenous Loading:** Must be diluted (0.9% Sodium Chloride or 5% Dextrose) to a concentration of 6.7 mg/mL to 20 mg/mL. Infuse over at least 1 hour (max rate 1000 mg/hour) through a 0.2 to 5-micron filter. Observe the patient for 30 minutes post-infusion [US].
- **Subcutaneous Maintenance:** Doses >400 mg require two separate injections. Rotate injection sites (abdomen, thigh, or upper arm). Observe the patient for 30 minutes after the first SC injection [US].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- If a maintenance dose is missed, administer it as soon as possible within 3 days. 
- If more than 3 days have passed, skip the missed dose and resume the regular weekly schedule on the next planned day. Do not administer two doses on the same day [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Veopoz** is contraindicated in patients with unresolved *Neisseria meningitidis* infection [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning: Serious Meningococcal Infections [US]
- Life-threatening and fatal meningococcal infections have occurred in patients treated with complement inhibitors. 
- Vaccination (Serogroups A, C, W, Y and Serogroup B) is required at least 2 weeks before the first dose.
- Vaccination does not eliminate infection risk. Monitor patients for early signs of meningitis (e.g., fever, headache, stiff neck, confusion) and evaluate immediately if suspected.

### General Warnings
- **Other Bacterial Infections:** Increased susceptibility to encapsulated bacteria (*S. pneumoniae*, *H. influenzae*). Interrupt treatment during serious infection resolution [US].
- **Systemic Hypersensitivity:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, can occur. If cardiovascular or respiratory compromise is observed, discontinue the infusion and provide supportive care [US].
- **Immune Complex Formation:** Switching from other complement inhibitors may result in transiently decreased drug concentrations or hypersensitivity. Monitor accordingly [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The safety profile is based on a single-arm study of 10 patients (age 3–19 years).

| Adverse Reaction | Frequency [US] |
| :--- | :--- |
| Upper respiratory tract infection | 30% |
| Fracture | 30% |
| Urticaria | 20% |
| Alopecia (hair loss) | 20% |

**Other reported reactions (n=1 each):** Injection site reactions (dermatitis, erythema), metabolic acidosis, gingival bleeding, hematuria, proteinuria, and elevated liver enzymes [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Intravenous Immunoglobulin (IVIG):** Concomitant use may decrease **Veopoz** concentrations due to interference with FcRn recycling. Avoid co-administration. If unavoidable, monitor for worsening clinical signs of PLE [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** No human data available. Monoclonal antibodies can cross the placenta. Animal studies (monkeys) at 3.3 to 3.8 times clinical exposure showed no adverse developmental effects [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** No data on presence in human milk or effects on the infant. Consider the benefits of breastfeeding against the maternal clinical need for the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No specific human studies. Animal data showed no adverse effects on reproductive organs or function [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established for patients ≥ 1 year old. Safety not established for infants < 1 year old [US].
- **Elderly Use:** CHAPLE disease is primarily a paediatric condition; **Veopoz** has not been studied in the geriatric population [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** As a monoclonal antibody, pozelimab-bbfg is not expected to undergo renal or hepatic excretion. Specific studies in these populations have not been conducted [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A terminal complement inhibitor that binds to protein C5. This blocks the cleavage of C5 into C5a (anaphylatoxin) and C5b, thereby preventing the formation of the membrane-attack complex (C5b-C9) and subsequent cell lysis [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Full inhibition of CH50 (total complement hemolytic activity) is typically achieved by the end of the IV loading dose and maintained with weekly SC dosing [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Bioavailability:** Approximately 51% (SC injection).
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution ~3.3 L (IV).
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 13.5 to 14.1 days. Elimination occurs via linear (proteolytic) and non-linear (target-mediated) pathways [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Study (NCT04209634):** A single-arm study in 10 patients (aged 3–19 years) with confirmed CD55-deficient PLE. 
- **Outcomes:** 100% of patients achieved serum albumin normalization (≥ 3.5 g/dL) by Week 12. The median time to normalization was 15.5 days. Reductions in hospitalizations and albumin transfusions were also observed compared to pre-treatment data [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F). Keep in the original carton to protect from light. Do not freeze or shake [US].
- **Diluted IV Solution:** Administer immediately. If necessary, store at room temperature for up to 8 hours or refrigerated (2°C to 8°C) for up to 24 hours from the time of preparation [US].
- **Prepared SC Syringe:** Administer within 4 hours of preparation [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription only (Rx-only). Initial approval 2023.
