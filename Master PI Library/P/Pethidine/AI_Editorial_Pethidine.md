# Editorial Product Information: Pethidine Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a clinical reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to local guidelines and the official PI of their respective region for legal and regulatory compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pethidine hydrochloride (INN: Meperidine hydrochloride).
- **Chemical Class:** Synthetic opioid analgesic (phenylpiperidine derivative).
- **Brand Name(s):** DBL Pethidine Hydrochloride Injection [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N02AB02.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile, clear solution for injection [Australian].
    - 50 mg in 1 mL ampoule [Australian].
    - 100 mg in 2 mL ampoule [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intramuscular (IM), Subcutaneous (SC), slow Intravenous (IV) bolus, IV infusion, and Patient-Controlled Analgesia (PCA).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Short-term Management of Severe Pain:** Specifically for 24 to 36 hours when other treatment options (non-opioid analgesics) are inadequate, not tolerated, or contraindicated [Australian].
- **Obstetric Analgesia:** Management of pain during regular labor [Australian].
- **Anaesthetic Adjunct:** For use as premedication or as an adjunct to anaesthesia [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Analgesia (IM/SC):** 25 mg to 100 mg every 3 to 4 hours. IM is the preferred parenteral route [Australian].
- **Analgesia (Slow IV Injection):** 25 mg to 50 mg every 3 to 4 hours. Injection must be performed very slowly, preferably using a diluted solution [Australian].
- **Continuous IV Infusion:** Recommended initial rate of 0.3 mg/kg/hr, provided adequate analgesia is established prior to the infusion [Australian].
- **Maximum IV Dose:** Usually 200 mg/day [Australian].

### Specific Indications
- **Obstetric Analgesia:** 50 mg to 100 mg by IM (preferred) or SC injection once pain becomes regular. May be repeated 3 to 4 times at 1–3 hour intervals. **Maximum:** 4 doses per 24 hours [Australian].
- **Premedication:** 50 mg to 100 mg IM (preferred) or SC, 30 to 90 minutes before anaesthesia [Australian].
- **Anaesthesia Adjunct:** Slow IV injection of fractional doses (diluted to 10 mg/mL). Dose should not exceed 25 mg to 50 mg [Australian].
- **Patient-Controlled Analgesia (PCA):** Demand doses of 5 mg to 20 mg with a lockout interval of 6 to 20 minutes [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Facilities for oxygenation, respiratory control, and opioid antagonists (naloxone) must be immediately available during IV administration [Australian].
- Due to neurotoxicity risks, use should not exceed 24 to 36 hours [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to pethidine [Australian].
- **Respiratory:** Severe or acute respiratory disease, respiratory depression, or depleted respiratory reserve (e.g., severe emphysema, asthma, chronic bronchitis, kyphoscoliosis) [Australian].
- **Neurological:** Head injury, raised intracranial pressure, or brain tumour [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular:** Cardiac arrhythmias, particularly supraventricular tachycardias [Australian].
- **Pharmacological:** Concurrent use of Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs), including selegiline, or use within the last 14 days [Australian].
- **Obstetric:** Pre-eclampsia or eclampsia [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Known or suspected GI obstruction, including paralytic ileus [Australian].
- **Other:** 
    - Convulsive states (e.g., status epilepticus, tetanus) [Australian].
    - Diabetic acidosis with danger of coma [Australian].
    - Acute alcoholism or delirium tremens [Australian].
    - Severe liver disease or incipient hepatic encephalopathy [Australian].
    - Low platelet count or coagulation disorders [Australian].
    - Use in PCA for young children or patients with poor cognitive function [Australian].
    - Continuous IV infusion or PCA in patients with renal impairment [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [Australian]
- **Limitations of Use:** Only for pain where other options are inadequate.
- **Hazardous and Harmful Use:** Potential for abuse, misuse, and addiction, leading to overdose and death.
- **Life-Threatening Respiratory Depression:** Serious risk; monitor closely, especially during initiation or dose titration.
- **Concomitant Use with CNS Depressants:** Use with benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, or alcohol may result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death.

### General Warnings
- **Pethidine Associated Neurotoxicity (PAN):** Related to the metabolite **norpethidine**. Risk increases with doses >1000 mg/24h or in renal impairment. Symptoms include twitching, tremors, and seizures [Australian].
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Potentially life-threatening; risk is higher when combined with other serotonergic drugs [Australian].
- **Adrenal Insufficiency:** Reported with chronic opioid use (>1 month) [Australian].
- **Cardiac Effects:** May increase heart rate and systemic vascular resistance; not recommended for pain relief in cardiac infarction [Australian].
- **Pheochromocytoma:** May trigger a hypertensive crisis [Australian].

### Monitoring Requirements
- Monitor regularly for signs of norpethidine toxicity (e.g., anxiety, twitching) if doses exceed 800 mg/24h [Australian].
- Monitor intra-ocular tension in patients with glaucoma [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### More Common Effects
- **CNS:** Dizziness, light-headedness, sedation, sweating, hallucinations, disorientation, psychosis [Australian].
- **GI:** Nausea, vomiting, constipation [Australian].

### Less Common / Serious Effects
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypotension, hypertension, tachycardia, bradycardia, vasodilation [Australian].
- **Nervous System (Neurotoxicity):** Tremor, twitching, hyperreflexia, complex partial seizures, vertigo, coma [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Decreased gastric emptying, oesophageal motility disorders, biliary tract spasm [Australian].
- **Genitourinary:** Urinary retention, anuria [Australian].
- **Dermatological:** Rash, pruritus, injection site complications (fibrosis with frequent IM use) [Australian].
- **Psychiatric:** Mental clouding, dysphoria, hyperactivity [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **MAOIs:** Contraindicated; risk of fatal reactions including hyperpyrexia, convulsions, and coma [Australian].
- **CNS Depressants (including Alcohol/Benzodiazepines):** Additive respiratory and sedative effects [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 & CYP2B6 Inhibitors (e.g., Ritonavir, Ketoconazole):** May increase pethidine levels, prolonging opioid effects [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 & CYP2B6 Inducers (e.g., Rifampicin, Phenytoin):** May decrease pethidine efficacy or increase the formation of the toxic metabolite norpethidine [Australian].
- **Serotonergic Drugs (SSRIs, SNRIs, Triptans):** Increased risk of Serotonin Syndrome [Australian].
- **Phenothiazines:** May increase CNS toxicity and hypotension; risk of seizures in eclampsia [Australian].
- **Phenytoin:** Specifically increases pethidine metabolism to norpethidine, increasing seizure risk [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Opioids can cause respiratory depression in the neonate. Use during labor must be weighed against risks. Prolonged use may result in Neonatal Opioid Withdrawal Syndrome [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Pethidine is excreted in breast milk. Due to the lack of definitive safety data, it is not recommended for nursing mothers [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No human data available in provided sources [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:**
    - **Analgesia (Children):** 0.5 mg to 2 mg/kg IM/SC; max 100 mg [Australian].
    - **Pre-operative:** 1 mg to 2 mg/kg IM/SC; max 100 mg [Australian].
    - **Neonates:** Safety not established; metabolism is significantly reduced compared to adults [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased sensitivity. Dose reduction to half the normal adult dose is recommended for patients over 70 years [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Norpethidine accumulates, significantly increasing seizure risk. Dose reduction or increased intervals are required. Continuous IV infusion and PCA are contraindicated [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Reduced pethidine clearance. Dose reduction and/or increased dosage intervals are recommended [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Synthetic opioid that interacts with stereo-specific opiate receptors in the CNS and peripheral nervous system [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Produces analgesia, respiratory depression, and sedation. Unlike morphine, pethidine has local anaesthetic activity and is not effective for cough or diarrhoea [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively hepatic (hydrolysis and N-demethylation) to **norpethidine**. Norpethidine has half the analgesic potency but twice the convulsive potency of pethidine [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Pethidine half-life is ~3.5 hours. Norpethidine half-life is 8–21 hours in healthy adults, but >30 hours in renal impairment [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Not specified in available sources (The provided PI notes "No data available" for clinical trials) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Condition:** Protect from light [Australian].
- **Incompatibilities:** Incompatible with thiopentone, alkalis, iodine, and iodides. Loss of clarity occurs when mixed with heparin, phenobarbital sodium, phenytoin sodium, or sodium bicarbonate [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 8 (Controlled Drug) [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only Medicine.
