# Editorial Product Information: Phenazopyridine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesised from the official product labeling available in the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational use by healthcare professionals. It does not replace the official Product Information (PI) or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Phenazopyridine hydrochloride (an azo dye).
- **Brand Name(s):** Phenazopyridine Hydrochloride (Westminster Pharmaceuticals, LLC; NuCare Pharmaceuticals, Inc.) [US].
- **ATC Code:** G04AK04.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Oral tablets: 100 mg and 200 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Westminster Pharmaceuticals, LLC; repacked by NuCare Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Symptomatic Relief of Urinary Tract Irritation:** Indicated for the prompt relief of pain, burning, urgency, frequency, and other discomforts resulting from irritation of the lower urinary tract mucosa [US]. 
- **Etiology:** Irritation may be caused by infection, trauma, surgery, endoscopic procedures, or the passage of sounds or catheters [US].
- **Clinical Limitation:** Phenazopyridine is intended for symptomatic relief only; it is not a substitute for definitive diagnosis or specific antimicrobial/surgical therapy [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing
- **100 mg Tablets:** Two tablets three times daily (600 mg/day total) [US].
- **200 mg Tablets:** One tablet three times daily (600 mg/day total) [US].
- **Administration:** Should be taken after meals to reduce gastrointestinal upset [US].

### Dosing by Indication
- **Concomitant with Antibacterials:** When used for the treatment of a urinary tract infection (UTI) in combination with an antimicrobial agent, administration should not exceed 2 days [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Patients should be advised not to crush or chew tablets; reports of teeth discoloration have occurred when the product was broken or held in the mouth prior to swallowing [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to phenazopyridine hydrochloride or any excipients [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with renal insufficiency [US].
- **Hepatic Disease:** Contraindicated in patients with severe liver disease or severe hepatitis [US].
- **Pregnancy-related:** Contraindicated in pyelonephritis of pregnancy [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Carcinogenicity:** Phenazopyridine is reasonably anticipated to be a human carcinogen based on animal data (hepatocellular adenomas/carcinomas in mice and colon/rectum adenomas in rats). Epidemiological data in humans are currently insufficient [US].
- **Renal Accumulation:** A yellowish color of the skin or sclera may indicate drug accumulation due to impaired renal function and requires immediate discontinuation [US].
- **G-6-PD Deficiency:** Use with caution in patients with Glucose-6-Phosphate Dehydrogenase deficiency, as they are susceptible to oxidative hemolysis and hemolytic anemia [US].
- **Gastrointestinal Disturbances:** Use cautiously in patients with existing GI issues [US].
- **Laboratory Interference:** Phenazopyridine is an azo dye that can interfere with urinalysis based on colorimetric, spectrophotometric, or fluorometric methods. Affected tests include but are not limited to: ketones, bilirubin, glucose (oxidase tests), 17-hydroxycorticosteroids, porphyrins, albumin, and urobilinogen [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are reported based on clinical experience; specific frequencies are not specified in available sources.

- **Dermatologic/Hypersensitivity:** Rash, pruritus, discoloration of skin/fluids, anaphylactoid-like reactions, and hypersensitivity hepatitis [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, and diarrhoea [US].
- **Hematologic:** Methemoglobinemia, hemolytic anemia, and sulfhemoglobinemia [US].
- **Central Nervous System:** Headache, aseptic meningitis [US].
- **Special Senses:** Visual disturbances, staining of contact lenses (due to fluid discoloration) [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic:** Renal calculi, renal/hepatic toxicity (usually associated with overdose), and jaundice [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Systemic Studies:** The interaction of phenazopyridine with other drugs has not been studied in a systematic manner [US].
- **Clinical Observation:** Medical literature suggests that no significant interactions have been reported to date [US].
- **Antimicrobial Compatibility:** Explicitly noted as compatible with antimicrobial therapy for UTI [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category B [US]:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits (in combination with sulfacytine) showed no evidence of harm. 
    - **Clinical Consideration:** The drug crosses the placenta into the fetal compartment. It should be used during pregnancy only if the potential benefit clearly outweighs the risk [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is not known if phenazopyridine or its metabolites are excreted in human milk. A decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the importance of the therapy to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** Long-term animal studies show tumor association, but specific data on impairment of human fertility are not provided [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Adequate and well-controlled studies have not been performed in children. No specific paediatric problems have been documented [US].
- **Elderly Use:** A decline in renal function is common in the elderly; because phenazopyridine is renally excreted, caution and monitoring for skin/sclera yellowing are required [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in renal insufficiency [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe liver disease/hepatitis [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Exerts a specific local analgesic effect on the mucosa of the urinary tract. The precise molecular mechanism is unknown [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Provides symptomatic relief of pain and urgency without possessing antimicrobial activity [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Metabolism/Elimination:** Rapidly excreted by the kidneys.
    - **Excretion:** In healthy subjects, 90% of a 600 mg daily dose is eliminated in the urine within 24 hours (41% as unchanged drug, 49% as metabolites) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Trial Data:** Not specified in provided source documents. One limited epidemiological study of 2,214 patients followed for 3 years showed no significant excess of cancer [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15° to 30°C (59° to 86°F) [US].
- **Packaging:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container with a child-resistant closure [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only (Prescription Medication) [US].
- **FDA Regulatory Status:** This drug has not been found by the FDA to be safe and effective, and the labeling has not been approved by the FDA (classified as "unapproved drug other") [US].
