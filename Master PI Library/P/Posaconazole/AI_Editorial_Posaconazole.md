# Editorial Product Information: Posaconazole

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (TGA)
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview of posaconazole, synthesised from official product information for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Prescribing Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Posaconazole (triazole derivative)
- **Brand Names:** AKM Posaconazole [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** J02AC04
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Modified-release (MR) tablets, 100 mg [Australian]
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pharmacor Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
Posaconazole is indicated for use in patients aged 13 years and older for the following [Australian]:
- **Prophylaxis of Invasive Fungal Infections (IFIs):** For patients at high risk, such as those with prolonged neutropenia or recipients of haematopoietic stem cell transplants (HSCT).
- **Treatment of Invasive Aspergillosis:** Specifically for patients whose disease is refractory to, or who are intolerant of, alternative therapy.
- **Treatment of Other Fungal Infections:** Including fusariosis, zygomycosis, coccidioidomycosis, chromoblastomycosis, and mycetoma in patients with refractory disease or intolerance to alternative therapies.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Paediatrics ≥ 13 years)
- **Prophylaxis of IFIs:** Loading dose of 300 mg (3 tablets) twice daily on Day 1, followed by 300 mg once daily thereafter. 
- **Refractory/Intolerant IFIs:** Loading dose of 300 mg twice daily on Day 1, followed by 300 mg once daily thereafter.

### Administration Instructions
- **Non-interchangeability:** Modified-release tablets and oral suspension are NOT interchangeable. They require different dosing regimens [Australian].
- **Method:** Tablets must be swallowed whole. Do not crush, chew, or divide.
- **Food Intake:** MR tablets may be taken without regard to food [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to posaconazole or any excipients.
- **Ergot Alkaloids:** Co-administration with ergotamine or dihydroergotamine (risk of ergotism).
- **HMG-CoA Reductase Inhibitors:** Co-administration with CYP3A4-metabolised statins (e.g., simvastatin, lovastatin, atorvastatin) due to the risk of rhabdomyolysis.
- **CYP3A4 Substrates (QT Risk):** Co-administration with terfenadine, astemizole, cisapride, pimozide, or quinidine (risk of QT prolongation and Torsade de Pointes).
- **Direct Oral Anticoagulants:** Co-administration with rivaroxaban or apixaban (increased bleeding risk) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatic Toxicity:** Infrequent cases of hepatic reactions (ALT/AST elevations, clinical hepatitis) occurred in trials. Rare fatal outcomes reported in patients with serious underlying conditions. Monitor LFTs at treatment initiation and throughout therapy [Australian].
- **QT Prolongation:** Caution is advised in patients with proarrhythmic conditions. Correct electrolyte disturbances (potassium, magnesium, calcium) before and during therapy.
- **Vincristine Toxicity:** Concomitant use with vinca alkaloids increases vincristine plasma levels, leading to neurotoxicity and other serious reactions (seizures, peripheral neuropathy). Reserve for use only when no alternative antifungal options exist.
- **Venetoclax Toxicity:** Posaconazole increases venetoclax toxicity (risk of tumour lysis syndrome and neutropenia). Refer to venetoclax PI for specific dose adjustments.
- **Photosensitivity:** Increased risk of reactions. Advise patients to use sun protection and avoid UV exposure [Australian].
- **Adrenal Insufficiency:** Azoles may inhibit adrenal steroid hormone synthesis; adrenal suppressive effects have been observed in animal studies.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect profiles for MR tablets are generally similar to the oral suspension [Australian].

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Nausea, diarrhoea, headache, pyrexia, hypokalaemia, febrile neutropenia, cough [Australian] |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Vomiting, abdominal pain, constipation, dyspepsia, flatulence, dizziness, fatigue, rash, anaemia, thrombocytopenia, elevated LFTs (ALT, AST, Bilirubin, ALP) |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | QT/QTc prolongation, convulsions, neuropathy, hypertension, hypotension, pancreatitis, acute renal failure, menstrual disorders |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | **Serious:** Hepatic failure, Torsade de Pointes, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, haemolytic uraemic syndrome (HUS), thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP), adrenal insufficiency |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** 8% of patients in refractory IFI pools reported treatment-related serious adverse events [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
Posaconazole is a potent inhibitor of **CYP3A4** and a substrate for **UDP-glucuronidation** and **P-gp** efflux.

### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Inhibitors of Posaconazole:** Rifabutin (decreases AUC by 49%), phenytoin (decreases AUC by 50%), and efavirenz (decreases AUC by 50%) should be avoided unless benefit outweighs risk [Australian].
- **Gastric pH:** MR tablet pharmacokinetics are not significantly affected by antacids, H2-receptor antagonists, or proton pump inhibitors [Australian].

### Posaconazole Effects on Other Drugs
- **Immunosuppressants:** Significant increases in ciclosporin (reduce dose to 3/4), tacrolimus (reduce dose to 1/3), and sirolimus (reduce dose to 1/10) require frequent blood level monitoring.
- **Benzodiazepines:** Increases midazolam AUC by ~5-fold. Consider dose adjustments for benzodiazepines metabolised by CYP3A4.
- **HIV Protease Inhibitors:** Increases atazanavir levels; monitor for toxicity.
- **Digoxin:** Increased plasma concentrations; monitor levels.
- **Tretinoin (ATRA):** May lead to hypercalcaemia due to inhibited metabolism [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian: Category B3]. Animal studies showed reproductive toxicity (skeletal variations, teratogenicity, and dystocia) at exposures below human therapeutic levels. Must not be used unless benefits clearly outweigh risks to the foetus. 
- **Contraception:** Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during and for 2 weeks after treatment [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Excreted in the milk of lactating rats. Human excretion is unknown; breastfeeding should be discontinued during therapy.
- **Fertility:** Animal data showed reduced testosterone and disrupted oestrus cycling in some species, but no effect on male rat fertility at 1.6x human clinical doses [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety/efficacy not established in children < 13 years. Limited data in 13-17 year olds suggests a profile similar to adults [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment recommended; pharmacokinetics are comparable between young and elderly subjects.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild to severe impairment. However, due to high variability in exposure, monitor patients with severe impairment closely for breakthrough infections [Australian]. Posaconazole is not removed by haemodialysis.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Limited data available; no specific dose adjustment recommendation can be made. Half-life increases with decreasing hepatic function [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits lanosterol 14α-demethylase, an enzyme essential for ergosterol biosynthesis. This impairs fungal membrane integrity and inhibits growth.
- **Microbiology:** Active against *Aspergillus* spp., *Candida* spp., *Fusarium* spp., and members of the *Zygomycetes* class (e.g., *Mucor*, *Rhizopus*).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability of MR tablet is ~54%. Steady-state reached by Day 6.
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein bound (>98%), predominantly to albumin. Large volume of distribution suggests extensive tissue penetration.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via UDP-glucuronidation; minimal oxidative (CYP450) metabolism.
    - **Elimination:** Mean half-life for MR tablets is 26–31 hours. Primarily excreted in faeces (77%), mostly as unchanged parent drug.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Prophylaxis (Studies 316 and 1899):** Posaconazole was superior to fluconazole or itraconazole in preventing invasive aspergillosis and reducing IFI-related mortality in high-risk patients (HSCT with GVHD and neutropenic AML/MDS) [Australian].
- **Treatment of Refractory IFI:** Posaconazole oral suspension demonstrated a 42% global response rate in refractory aspergillosis compared to 26% in an external control group.
- **MR Tablet Bridging (Study 5615):** A non-comparative study in 230 patients confirmed that 300 mg daily (following Day 1 loading) achieved target steady-state concentrations and a safety profile consistent with the oral suspension.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Container:** Store in the original container to protect from moisture.
- **In-use Shelf Life:** Once opened, bottles are stable for 30 days (24, 60, or 96-count) or 60 days (1000-count) [Australian].
