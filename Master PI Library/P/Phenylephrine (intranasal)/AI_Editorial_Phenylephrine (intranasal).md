# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Jurisdictions Contributed:** United States (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of the available official Product Information for levocarnitine injection. It is intended as a clinical reference for healthcare professionals and is not a substitute for the official PI used for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.
- **Note on Sources:** While the user instruction referenced multiple potential regions, the provided source material was limited to the United States (FDA) documentation for **Levocarnitine Injection** by Baxter Healthcare Corporation. Consequently, international variations are not specified except where common clinical knowledge or the provided text suggests iatrogenic differences.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP); also known as L-carnitine.
- **Chemical Class:** A naturally occurring carrier molecule (amino acid derivative) required for mammalian energy metabolism.
- **Brand Names:** Available as generic **Levocarnitine Injection** [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
  - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL).
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from genetic metabolic disorders (e.g., glutaric aciduria II, methyl malonic aciduria, propionic acidemia, and medium-chain fatty acylCoA dehydrogenase deficiency) [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients undergoing maintenance haemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion. In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose may be followed by an equivalent dose divided over the next 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and not less than every 6 hours.
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).

### Administration Instructions
- Administer as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or via the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.

### Monitoring
- Plasma free carnitine concentrations should be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels is recommended.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known:** There are no listed contraindications for levocarnitine injection in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal oedema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These often occur within minutes of intravenous administration, primarily in ESRD patients. Treatment should be discontinued immediately upon signs of hypersensitivity [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known epilepsy, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral vs. IV):** While the injection is indicated for ESRD, clinicians should note that chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disorders.

### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
| Adverse Event | Frequency (Levocarnitine 10–40 mg/kg) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Hypertension** | 20% |
| **Headache** | 22% |
| **Diarrhoea** | 16% |
| **Vomiting** | 15% |
| **Abdominal Pain** | 9% |
| **Chest Pain** | 12% |
| **Fever** | 7% |
| **Dizziness** | 13% |

### Other Notable Reactions
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; gastritis.
- **Other:** Body odour, injection site reactions (33%), and pharyngitis (23%) [US].
- **Serious:** Anaphylaxis and seizures (post-marketing reports).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalised Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. 
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels closely after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not explicitly lettered, but follows historical Category C language]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if levocarnitine is excreted in human milk. In dairy cows, milk concentrations increase after administration. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing in children for metabolic disorders is consistent with adult weight-based dosing (50 mg/kg). Safety and efficacy in ESRD paediatric patients are implied via weight-based dosing [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Not specifically addressed in the source material.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis to replace dialytic losses and manage iatrogenic deficiency [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects [US].

### Pharmacodynamics
In ESRD patients, levocarnitine injection increases plasma carnitine concentrations. Normal plasma free carnitine levels are defined as 40–50 µmol/L; therapy aims to maintain levels between 35–60 µmol/L [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. 
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (faeces).
- **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** Double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in ESRD patients showed that 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly after dialysis significantly increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations from baseline (~20 µmol/L) to stable trough levels (~190 µmol/L) after 8 weeks of therapy [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives.
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription Only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources (refer to local jurisdictional standards for Australia/UK).
