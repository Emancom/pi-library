# Editorial Product Information: Panitumumab

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction:
    - **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidance required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the Australian source document was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Panitumumab (a fully human IgG2 monoclonal antibody).
- **Chemical Class:** Antineoplastic and immunomodulatory agents; monoclonal antibodies.
- **Brand Name:** **VECTIBIX** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01XC08.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile, colourless, preservative-free concentrated solution for intravenous (IV) infusion (20 mg/mL). Available in 100 mg/5 mL and 400 mg/20 mL single-use vials [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Amgen Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metastatic Colorectal Cancer (mCRC):**
    - **Monotherapy:** Indicated for the treatment of patients with wild-type *RAS* mCRC following the failure of standard chemotherapy regimens [Australian].
    - **Combination Therapy (First-line):** In combination with FOLFOX for the treatment of patients with wild-type *RAS* mCRC [Australian].
    - **Combination Therapy (Second-line):** In combination with FOLFIRI for patients who have previously received first-line fluoropyrimidine-based chemotherapy (excluding irinotecan) [Australian].
- **Note on RAS Status:** Efficacy is restricted to patients with wild-type *RAS* (defined as both *KRAS* and *NRAS* wild-type).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:** 6 mg/kg administered once every 2 weeks [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - **Method:** Must be administered via an infusion pump using a low protein-binding 0.2 μm or 0.22 μm in-line filter.
    - **Prohibition:** Do **not** administer as an IV push or bolus.
    - **Preparation:** Dilute in 0.9% sodium chloride to a total volume of 100 mL (doses >1000 mg should be diluted in 150 mL). The final concentration must not exceed 10 mg/mL [Australian].
    - **Infusion Rate:** Initial infusion should be delivered over approximately 60 minutes (90 minutes for doses >1000 mg). If tolerated, subsequent infusions may be given over 30 to 60 minutes [Australian].
- **Dose Modifications (Dermatologic Reactions):**
    - **Grade 3 or Intolerable:** Withhold 1–2 doses. 
    - **First Recovery (< Grade 3):** Resume at 100% of the original dose.
    - **Second Recovery:** Resume at 80% of the original dose.
    - **Third Recovery:** Resume at 60% of the original dose.
    - **Fourth Occurrence:** Permanently discontinue treatment [Australian].
- **Infusion Reactions:** Reduce infusion rate by 50% for mild/moderate (Grade 1–2) reactions. Discontinue permanently for severe or life-threatening reactions [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** History of life-threatening hypersensitivity to panitumumab or any excipients [Australian].
- **Combination Therapy:** Panitumumab in combination with oxaliplatin-based chemotherapy is contraindicated in patients with mutant *RAS* mCRC or where *RAS* status is unknown [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Tumour Genetic Marker Testing:** Mutational status of *RAS* (*KRAS* and *NRAS* exons 2, 3, and 4) must be confirmed by an experienced laboratory using validated test methods prior to initiation [Australian].
- **Dermatologic Toxicity:** Reported in approximately 93% of patients. Serious complications include necrotising fasciitis, sepsis, and rare cases of Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS) and Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN) [Australian].
- **Pulmonary Toxicity:** Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD), both fatal and non-fatal, has been reported. Discontinue permanently if ILD is confirmed [Australian].
- **Electrolyte Disturbances:** Progressively decreasing serum magnesium levels are common. Periodic monitoring of magnesium and calcium is required during treatment and for 8 weeks post-completion [Australian].
- **Ocular Toxicities:** Serious cases of keratitis, ulcerative keratitis, and corneal perforation have been reported [Australian].
- **Venous Thromboembolism:** Increased risk of DVT and pulmonary embolism; monitor patients closely [Australian].
- **Performance Status:** Patients with ECOG 2 performance status have shown increased toxicity and shortened progression-free survival when using panitumumab in combination with chemotherapy [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** 
    - **Skin:** Rash (47%), dermatitis acneiform (39%), pruritus (36%), erythema (33%), dry skin (21%), paronychia (20%).
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhoea (46%), nausea (39%), vomiting (26%), constipation (23%), abdominal pain (23%), stomatitis.
    - **General:** Fatigue (35%), pyrexia (21%), asthenia.
    - **Metabolism:** Decreased appetite (30%), hypomagnesaemia, hypokalaemia [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Conjunctivitis, skin fissures, acne, alopecia, dehydration, headache, dizziness, hypotension/hypertension, pulmonary embolism, epistaxis [Australian].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Necrotising fasciitis, SJS/TEN, ILD, severe infusion reactions, acute renal failure (secondary to diarrhoea/dehydration) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Irinotecan:** Co-administration does not alter the pharmacokinetics of irinotecan or its metabolite SN-38. Irinotecan-containing regimens (IFL/FOLFIRI) do not affect panitumumab pharmacokinetics [Australian].
- **Oxaliplatin:** Use is contraindicated in mutant *RAS* patients due to decreased progression-free survival and overall survival [Australian].
- **Bevacizumab:** Combination with bevacizumab and chemotherapy is not indicated due to increased toxicity and no improvement in progression-free survival [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category C.** 
    - EGFR is essential for normal organogenesis. Panitumumab is IgG2 and can cross the placental barrier. Animal studies suggest embryo-lethality. 
    - **Contraception:** Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during treatment and for 6 months after the final dose [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Excretion in human milk is unknown but likely. Discontinue breastfeeding during treatment and for 8 weeks after the final dose [Australian].
- **Fertility:** May impair fertility in women; animal data showed prolonged menstrual cycles and amenorrhoea (reversible) [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients under 18 years [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy in patients ≥ 65 years as monotherapy, though increased serious adverse events were noted in elderly patients when used in combination with FOLFOX or FOLFIRI [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not studied in this population; however, population PK analysis suggests no impact of renal function on panitumumab PK [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not studied in this population; population PK analysis suggests no impact of hepatic function on panitumumab PK [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Panitumumab binds with high affinity to the ligand-binding domain of the human epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR). This competitively inhibits autophosphorylation induced by EGFR ligands, leading to receptor internalisation, inhibition of cell growth, and decreased pro-angiogenic factor production [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Proliferation of EGFR-expressing tumour cells is inhibited. Mutations in *RAS* genes act as downstream drivers, making the inhibition of EGFR ineffective in mutant *RAS* tumours [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Exhibits non-linear pharmacokinetics. Steady-state is reached by the third dose (6 mg/kg every 2 weeks). Mean half-life is approximately 7.5 days [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Monotherapy:** A Phase 3 trial in 463 patients with EGFR-expressing mCRC (post-chemotherapy failure) showed a 46% reduction in disease progression or death in the panitumumab group vs. best supportive care (HR 0.54). Efficacy was primarily driven by the wild-type *KRAS* subgroup [Australian].
- **First-line (FOLFOX):** The PRIME study (N=1183) demonstrated significantly improved PFS in wild-type *KRAS* mCRC patients receiving panitumumab + FOLFOX vs. FOLFOX alone (9.6 vs. 8.0 months, HR 0.80) [Australian].
- **Second-line (FOLFIRI):** Study 20050181 (N=1186) showed improved PFS in wild-type *KRAS* patients receiving panitumumab + FOLFIRI vs. FOLFIRI alone (5.9 vs. 3.9 months, HR 0.73) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C.
- **Handling:** Do **not** freeze. Do **not** shake. Protect from direct sunlight.
- **Shelf Life:** Vials are single-use only. Diluted solutions should be used immediately; if storage is necessary, it may be kept at 2°C to 8°C for up to 24 hours [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (S4)** - Prescription Only Medicine [Australian].
