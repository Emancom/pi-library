# Editorial Product Information: perflutren + albumin

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **perflutren + albumin**
- Chemical class: Ultrasound contrast agent — perflutren gas-filled human albumin microspheres (microbubble contrast)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intravenous only (do not administer intra-arterially)
- DO NOT include brand or trade names, dosage form physical descriptions, or schedule listings

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 1997 (Initial U.S. Approval: 1997)
- Most recent prescribing information revision (as stated): 5/2025
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources
- If additional jurisdictional registration or update dates are required: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Indicated to opacify the left ventricle and improve delineation of left ventricular endocardial borders in patients with suboptimal echocardiograms.
  - Approved for use in adults and pediatric patients with suboptimal echocardiograms (no regional variation stated in available sources).

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General adult dosing
- Recommended initial adult dose: 0.5 mL administered intravenously at a rate not exceeding 1 mL/second.
- If contrast enhancement is inadequate after 0.5 mL, additional 0.5 mL doses may be repeated as needed.
- Maximum total dose: 5 mL in any 10-minute period.
- Maximum total dose in a single patient study: 8.7 mL.
- Follow each perflutren + albumin injection with a flush of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection or 5% Dextrose Injection.

### Pediatric dosing (by body weight)
- Pediatric product must be diluted 1:1 with 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection prior to administration.
  - Body weight ≤ 28 kg: draw 0.2 mL of perflutren + albumin and dilute with 0.2 mL 0.9% NaCl (final diluted volume 0.4 mL).
  - Body weight 29–40 kg: draw 0.3 mL and dilute with 0.3 mL 0.9% NaCl.
  - Body weight ≥ 41 kg: draw 0.4 mL and dilute with 0.4 mL 0.9% NaCl.
- Administer diluted pediatric dose at a rate not exceeding 0.05 mL/second.
- If enhancement is inadequate after the initial diluted dose, up to four additional doses of the same diluted volume may be given as needed.
- Discard diluted product if not used within 10 minutes after dilution.

### Preparation and handling
- Visually inspect the vial prior to use. Do not use if container damaged, protective seal/rubber cap has been entered, or the expected upper white layer is absent (may indicate damaged microspheres).
- Resuspension: invert vial and gently rotate to resuspend microspheres and allow product to come to room temperature (20–25°C). After resuspension the product should be opaque and milky-white. Do not use if the suspension appears clear.
- Vent the vial with a sterile vent spike or a sterile 18‑gauge needle before withdrawing the suspension. Do not inject air into the vial.
- Use the product within one minute of suspension. If more than one minute elapses, resuspend by inverting and gently rotating the syringe for no less than 10 seconds before use.
- For pediatric dilution: draw equal volumes of perflutren + albumin and 0.9% NaCl into the same syringe and mix gently by rolling the syringe horizontally in the palms for at least 10 seconds. If not used within 10 minutes after dilution, discard.

### Administration instructions
- Intravenous administration only (do not administer intra-arterially).
- Inspect visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration; do not inject if suspension is not opaque and milky-white or if particulate matter is visible.
- Adult peripheral venous injection: through a 20‑gauge or larger angiocatheter into a peripheral vein at a rate not exceeding 1 mL/s.
- Pediatric peripheral venous injection: through a 24‑gauge or larger angiocatheter into a peripheral vein at a rate not exceeding 0.05 mL/s.
- Suggested administration methods: short extension tubing, heparin lock, or intravenous line with a 3‑way stopcock.
- Do not aspirate blood back into the perflutren-containing syringe (may promote clot formation in the syringe).
- For short extension tubing or heparin lock: flush the line for patency before and after injection using 10 mL 0.9% NaCl for adults or 5 mL 0.9% NaCl for pediatric patients.
- For continuous IV lines: maintain vascular patency with 0.9% NaCl or 5% Dextrose Injection and flush the line in its entirety immediately after injection.
- Each vial is for single-patient use only; discard any unused portion.

### Titration / Missed doses
- No titration schedules beyond the repeat dosing described above.
- Missed dose instructions: Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known or suspected hypersensitivity to perflutren or albumin.
- No other absolute contraindications are specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed warning
- **WARNING: SERIOUS CARDIOPULMONARY REACTIONS** — Serious cardiopulmonary reactions, including fatalities, have occurred uncommonly during or shortly following perflutren-containing microsphere administration. Most serious reactions occur within 30 minutes of administration. Always have resuscitation equipment and trained personnel readily available prior to administration. [US]

### General warnings and precautions
- Serious cardiopulmonary reactions reported include fatal cardiac or respiratory arrest, shock, syncope, symptomatic arrhythmias (atrial fibrillation, tachycardia, bradycardia, supraventricular tachycardia, ventricular fibrillation, ventricular tachycardia), hypertension, hypotension, dyspnea, hypoxia, chest pain, respiratory distress, stridor, wheezing, loss of consciousness, and convulsions.
- Patients with unstable cardiopulmonary conditions may be at higher risk (examples: acute myocardial infarction, acute coronary syndromes, worsening or unstable congestive heart failure, or serious ventricular arrhythmias). Assess patients for conditions that may preclude administration.
- Hypersensitivity/anaphylactic reactions have been observed, including shock, bronchospasm, throat tightness, angioedema, facial and upper airway swelling, rash, urticaria, pruritus and flushing. Monitor all patients for hypersensitivity reactions.
- Systemic embolization: in patients with an intracardiac right-to-left shunt or other cardiac shunt, microspheres may bypass the pulmonary filter and enter arterial circulation. Assess and monitor patients with shunts for evidence of embolic phenomena after administration.
- Ventricular arrhythmia related to high ultrasound mechanical index: high mechanical index may cause microsphere rupture and lead to ventricular arrhythmias. End-systolic triggering with high mechanical indices has been associated with ventricular arrhythmias. Not recommended for use at mechanical indices > 0.8.
- Do not administer intra-arterially.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor patients during and after administration, with particular vigilance during the first 30 minutes.
- Ensure immediate availability of cardiopulmonary resuscitation equipment and personnel trained in advanced life support.
- Assess patients with shunts for embolic signs following administration.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Inspect vial and suspension as described in Dosage and Administration before use.
- Use single-patient vial for one patient only; discard unused portion.
- Avoid injection at rates higher than recommended because faster rates may reduce microsphere performance.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

### Pregnancy
- Human data: No data with perflutren + albumin use in pregnant women to inform drug-associated risks.
- Animal data: Intravenous administration to rats (doses 0.25, 5, 10 mL/kg/day; approximately 0.2, 5, and 10 times the recommended maximum human dose based on body surface area) and to rabbits (0.25, 2.5, 5 mL/kg/day; approximately 0.5, 5, and 10 times the recommended maximum human dose based on body surface area) during organogenesis produced no significant findings attributable solely to a direct effect on the fetus.
- Regulatory pregnancy category/system: Not specified in available sources.

### Breastfeeding
- No data on presence of perflutren protein-type A microspheres or perflutren in human milk, effects on the breastfed infant, or effects on milk production.
- Clinical recommendation: Consider the developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding along with the mother's clinical need for the drug and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed infant from the drug or underlying maternal condition.

- Impairment of fertility: Not specified in available sources beyond genotoxicity findings.

## 10. Use in Special Populations

### Paediatric use
- The safety and effectiveness to opacify the left ventricle and improve delineation of left ventricular endocardial borders have been established in pediatric patients with suboptimal echocardiograms.
- Pediatric clinical study data: efficacy and safety support use in pediatric patients; one clinical study included 37 pediatric patients aged 9 to 17 years.

No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed between older and younger subjects; however, greater sensitivity of some older individuals cannot be ruled out. ## 11. Pharmacology

### Mechanism of action
- The albumin-shelled microspheres containing perflutren gas create an echogenic blood-borne contrast effect. The microsphere–blood acoustic impedance mismatch scatters and reflects ultrasound waves, enhancing ultrasound imaging. At adult echocardiography frequencies (2–5 MHz), the microspheres resonate, increasing ultrasound scattering and image enhancement.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Contrast enhancement duration (median) in adults at various doses:
  - 0.2 mL (40% of recommended adult dose): ~1 minute
  - 0.5 mL: ~2 minutes
  - 3 mL: ~4 minutes
  - 5 mL: ~5 minutes
- Pulmonary hemodynamic effects: In a prospective open-label study of 30 patients (including patients with elevated baseline pulmonary arterial systolic pressure), no clinically important pulmonary hemodynamic, systemic hemodynamic, or ECG changes were observed after administration.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Distribution: Binding of perflutren to plasma proteins and partitioning into blood cells are unknown; perflutren protein binding is expected to be minimal due to the gas’s low partition coefficient in blood. Pharmacokinetics of the intact microspheres in humans are unknown.
- Elimination: Perflutren is eliminated via pulmonary exhalation with a pulmonary elimination half-life of 1.3 ± 0.69 minutes (mean ± SD). Perflutren is eliminated through the lungs within approximately 10 minutes; mean recovery in expired air was 96% ± 23%, with peak expired-air concentration at ~30–40 seconds after administration.
- Metabolism: Perflutren is a chemically stable gas that is not metabolized. The albumin component is expected to be handled by normal metabolic routes.

## 12. Clinical Trials

### Adult pivotal studies
- Two identical multicenter, controlled, dose-escalation studies (Study A n=101; Study B n=102; total 203 adult patients) enrolled adults with suboptimal non‑contrast echocardiography (defined as at least two of six LV endocardial border segments inadequately delineated in the apical 4‑chamber view).
- Patient characteristics: Mean age ~61 years (range 21–83); majority male; diverse racial composition reported.
- Primary outcome: improvement in left ventricular opacification and delineation of left ventricular endocardial borders compared with noncontrast echocardiography. Efficacy was established in these studies.
- Safety population described in clinical trials: 279 adults across clinical studies used for safety evaluation; adverse events reported as in Section 7.

### Pediatric study
- A clinical study in pediatric patients (reported n=37 aged 9–17 years) supported pediatric safety and effectiveness for the approved pediatric dosing schedule described above.

### Additional clinical data
- A postmarketing prospective observational safety surveillance study included 1,039 patients receiving the product in routine clinical practice; 17% reported at least one adverse event; no deaths or serious adverse reactions were reported in that study.
