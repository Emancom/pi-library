- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AnazaoHealth Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
Pentagastrin is indicated as a diagnostic aid for the evaluation of gastric acid secretory function [US]. Specific clinical applications include:
- **Evaluation of Anacidity (Achlorhydria):** Used in the diagnostic workup of suspected pernicious anemia, atrophic gastritis, or gastric carcinoma [US].
- **Post-Surgical Assessment:** Determination of the reduction in gastric acid output following operations for peptic ulcers, such as vagotomy or gastric resection [US].
- **Evaluation of Hypersecretory Conditions:** Diagnostic aid for suspected gastric hypersecretion in patients with duodenal ulcers or postoperative stomal ulcers [US].
- **Diagnosis of Zollinger-Ellison Syndrome:** Used to identify the marked hypersecretion characteristic of gastrinomas [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Subcutaneous Injection:** 6 mcg (0.006 mg) per kg of body weight [US].
- **Intravenous Infusion:** The dosage ranges from 0.1 to 12 mcg (0.0001 to 0.012 mg) per kg of body weight per hour [US].

### Administration Instructions
- For IV infusion, the drug must be administered in a 0.9% sodium chloride injection [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
The following agents may antagonise or diminish the gastric acid secretory response to pentagastrin, potentially leading to false-negative results [US]:
- Antacids
- Anticholinergics
- Histamine H2-receptor antagonists
- Proton Pump Inhibitors (e.g., omeprazole)

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Pentagastrin is a synthetic analogue of the hormone gastrin. It is believed to excite the oxyntic (parietal) cells of the stomach, stimulating them to secrete gastric acid, pepsin, and intrinsic factor [US].

### Pharmacodynamics
- **Pancreatic Effects:** Stimulates pancreatic secretion, particularly when administered in large intramuscular doses [US].
- **Gastrointestinal Motility:** Increases motility by a direct effect on intestinal smooth muscle, though it may delay gastric emptying by stimulating terminal antral contractions (retropulsion) [US].
- **Vascular/Electrolyte Effects:** Increases blood flow in the gastric mucosa, inhibits water and electrolyte absorption in the ileum, and promotes sodium and chloride diuresis [US].
- **Other Effects:** Induces contraction of the lower esophageal sphincter (when given IV) and increases motor activity in the colon and rectum [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Onset of Action:** 10 minutes [US].
- **Time to Peak Effect:** 20 to 30 minutes [US].
- **Duration of Action:** 60 to 80 minutes [US].
