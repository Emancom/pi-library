# Editorial Product Information: Prasugrel

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of prasugrel product information for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data to support safe and effective prescribing.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal compliance. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Prasugrel (as hydrochloride), a thienopyridine-class adenosine diphosphate (ADP) receptor antagonist.
- **Brand Names:** **Prasugrel Lupin** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - 5 mg film-coated tablets: Yellow, biconvex, oblong-shaped, debossed with "F1" [Australian].
    - 10 mg film-coated tablets: Orange, biconvex, oblong-shaped, debossed with "F2" [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Southern Cross Pharma Pty Ltd / Generic Health Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Prasugrel**, co-administered with acetylsalicylic acid (aspirin), is indicated for the prevention of atherothrombotic events (including myocardial infarction, stroke, and cardiovascular death) in patients with acute coronary syndromes (ACS) who are to undergo percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI). This includes patients with:
- Unstable angina (UA) (moderate to high risk)
- Non-ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI)
- ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI)

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults ≥18 years)
- **Loading Dose:** A single 60 mg dose initiated at the time of PCI [Australian].
- **Maintenance Dose:** 10 mg once daily.
- **Concomitant Therapy:** Patients must also take aspirin (75 mg to 325 mg) daily.

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets may be taken with or without food.
- Tablets should not be crushed or broken [Australian].

### Specific Population Dosing
- **Body Weight <60 kg:** Following the 60 mg loading dose, a maintenance dose of 5 mg once daily is recommended. The 10 mg maintenance dose is not recommended due to increased bleeding risk and higher exposure to the active metabolite [Australian].
- **Elderly (≥75 years):** Generally not recommended. If used, a 5 mg maintenance dose should be considered following the 60 mg loading dose [Australian].

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Active pathological bleeding (e.g., peptic ulcer, intracranial haemorrhage).
- History of transient ischaemic attack (TIA) or stroke.
- Severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class C).
- Known hypersensitivity to the active ingredient or any excipients.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Bleeding Risk:** **Prasugrel** significantly increases the risk of major and minor bleeding. It should only be used if the potential benefit outweighs the risk in patients with a propensity to bleed (e.g., recent trauma, surgery, or moderate-to-severe renal impairment).
- **TIA/Stroke History:** Contraindicated due to a higher rate of stroke (ischaemic and haemorrhagic) and bleeding observed in clinical trials [Australian].
- **Loading Dose Timing (NSTEMI):** In NSTEMI patients, the loading dose should be given at the time of PCI rather than as pretreatment, as early administration increases bleeding risk without additional benefit [Australian].
- **Surgery:** If elective surgery is required, **Prasugrel** should be discontinued at least 7 days prior to the procedure to minimise bleeding.
- **Thrombotic Thrombocytopenic Purpura (TTP):** Has been reported and requires prompt treatment.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Cross-reactivity among thienopyridines has been reported; monitor patients with a history of allergy to clopidogrel or ticlopidine.
- **Lactose:** Contains lactose; not recommended for patients with rare hereditary galactose intolerance or glucose-galactose malabsorption [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Haemorrhagic Adverse Effects
- **Common (1–10%):** Contusion (6.9%), haematoma (6.5%), epistaxis (6.2%), ecchymosis (2.2%), vessel puncture site haematoma (2.0%), puncture site haemorrhage (1.8%), GI haemorrhage (1.5%), haematuria (1.5%).
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Post-procedural haemorrhage, subcutaneous haematoma, haemoptysis, gingival bleeding, rectal haemorrhage, haematochezia, retroperitoneal haemorrhage, eye haemorrhage.

### Non-Haemorrhagic Adverse Effects (Common 1–10%)
- Hypertension (7.5%)
- Hypercholesterolemia/Hyperlipidemia (7.0%)
- Headache (5.5%)
- Back pain (5.0%)
- Dyspnoea (4.9%)
- Nausea (4.6%)
- Dizziness (4.1%)
- Cough (3.9%)
- Hypotension (3.9%)
- Fatigue (3.7%)
- Rash (2.8%)
- Pyrexia (2.7%)

### Serious/Rare Adverse Effects
- **TTP:** Very rare (<0.01%).
- **Angioedema/Anaphylaxis:** Rare (0.01%–0.1%).
- **Thrombocytopenia:** Severe cases (0.06%) reported in trials.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Morphine and Opioids:** Co-administration leads to delayed and reduced exposure to **Prasugrel** and its active metabolite, potentially reducing efficacy. Consider parenteral P2Y12 inhibitors if rapid inhibition is required in patients receiving morphine [Australian].
- **NSAIDs and Warfarin:** Concomitant use may increase the risk of bleeding; use with caution.
- **Cytochrome P450 Interactions:**
    - **CYP3A inhibitors** (e.g., ketoconazole) and **CYP inducers** (e.g., rifampicin) do not significantly impact the AUC of the active metabolite.
    - **CYP2B6:** **Prasugrel** is a weak inhibitor. Caution is advised when co-administering with drugs that have a narrow therapeutic window and are solely metabolised by CYP2B6 (e.g., cyclophosphamide, efavirenz).
- **Gastric pH Elevating Agents:** PPIs and H2 blockers slightly decrease Cmax but do not affect the AUC or antiplatelet activity of **Prasugrel**.
- **Digoxin:** No clinically significant effect on digoxin pharmacokinetics.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category B1 [Australian]:** Drugs which have been taken by only a limited number of pregnant women and women of childbearing age, without an increase in the frequency of malformation or other direct or indirect harmful effects on the human fetus having been observed.
    - Use only if potential benefits justify potential risks.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if **Prasugrel** is excreted in human milk (excretion confirmed in rat milk). A decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the benefit to the mother.
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in animal studies at doses 1,500 times the human maintenance dose.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients <18 years.
- **Elderly (≥75 years):** Increased risk of fatal and intracranial bleeding. Generally not recommended unless the benefit is clearly established; consider a 5 mg maintenance dose.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is necessary, including for End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD). However, monitoring for bleeding is advised as experience is limited.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild to moderate (Child-Pugh A/B) impairment. Contraindicated in severe (Child-Pugh C) impairment.
- **Asian Populations:** Higher exposure (AUC) observed in Chinese, Japanese, and Korean subjects; monitor closely [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A prodrug that is metabolised to an active thiol metabolite. This metabolite irreversibly binds to the P2Y12 class of ADP receptors on platelets, inhibiting platelet activation and aggregation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed (≥79%), with peak plasma levels of the active metabolite reached in approximately 30 minutes.
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly hydrolysed in the intestine to a thiolactone, followed by a single CYP450 step (primarily CYP3A4 and CYP2B6) to the active metabolite.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 68% excreted in urine and 27% in faeces as inactive metabolites. The active metabolite has a half-life of ~7.4 hours.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Following a 60 mg loading dose, inhibition of platelet aggregation (IPA) occurs within 15–30 minutes. Steady-state IPA (69–74%) is reached after 3–5 days of maintenance dosing.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **TRITON-TIMI 38:** A Phase 3 trial (n=13,608) comparing **Prasugrel** to clopidogrel in ACS patients undergoing PCI. **Prasugrel** significantly reduced the primary composite endpoint (CV death, non-fatal MI, or non-fatal stroke) by 18% (p <0.001) and reduced stent thrombosis by 50% (p <0.001). However, it was associated with a significant increase in TIMI major bleeding (2.2% vs 1.7%).
- **ACCOAST:** Evaluated loading dose timing in NSTEMI patients. Pre-treatment with a 30 mg loading dose prior to angiography increased major bleeding without providing additional ischaemic benefit compared to a 60 mg dose given at the time of PCI.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C.
- Keep in the original package to protect from moisture.
- Do not crush or break tablets [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
