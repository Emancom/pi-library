# Editorial Product Information: Protriptyline

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following jurisdictional Product Information (PI):
    - **United States (FDA)**: Protriptyline Hydrochloride Tablets (Epic Pharma, LLC)
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a clinically relevant format.
- **Note**: This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official PI. Clinicians should refer to the local approved labelling in their specific jurisdiction before prescribing. As only the United States (FDA) PI was provided for this review, jurisdictional variations from other regions (e.g., Australia, Europe) are "Not specified in available sources."

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Protriptyline Hydrochloride
- **Chemical Class**: Dibenzocycloheptene derivative (Tricyclic Antidepressant)
- **Brand Names**: 
    - [US]: Generic (e.g., Epic Pharma)
- **ATC Code**: Not specified in available sources (Commonly N06AA11).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**:
    - [US]: 5 mg and 10 mg film-coated tablets.
- **Route of Administration**: Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: 
    - [US]: Epic Pharma, LLC.

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Mental Depression**: Indicated for the treatment of symptoms of mental depression in patients under close medical supervision. [US]
- **Clinical Nuance**: Due to its activating properties, it is specifically noted as suitable for patients who are withdrawn and anergic. [US]

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing
- **Initial Dose**: 15 mg to 40 mg daily, divided into 3 or 4 doses. [US]
- **Titration**: Increase gradually based on clinical response and tolerance.
- **Maximum Dose**: 60 mg per day. [US]
- **Administration Note**: Any dose increases should preferably be made in the morning dose. [US]
- **Maintenance**: Once a satisfactory response is achieved, the dose should be reduced to the minimum effective amount. [US]

### Special Instructions
- **Adolescent and Elderly Patients**: Start with 5 mg three times daily. Increase gradually if necessary. [US]
- **Missed Dose**: Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity**: Prior sensitivity to protriptyline hydrochloride. [US]
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs)**: Do not co-administer with MAOIs. A minimum 14-day washout period is required after discontinuing an MAOI before starting protriptyline. [US]
- **Cisapride**: Contraindicated due to the risk of additive cardiac interactions, including QT interval prolongation and arrhythmias. [US]
- **Myocardial Infarction**: Do not use during the acute recovery phase following a myocardial infarction. [US]

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- **Suicidality and Antidepressant Drugs [US]**: Antidepressants increase the risk of suicidal thinking and behavior in children, adolescents, and young adults (aged 18-24). Monitor all patients closely for clinical worsening and emergence of suicidal thoughts, especially during the first few months of therapy or at dose changes. [US]

### General Warnings
- **Cardiovascular Risk**: Tachycardia and postural hypotension occur more frequently with protriptyline than with other antidepressants. Use with extreme caution in patients with cardiovascular disorders. [US]
- **Seizure Disorder**: Use with caution in patients with a history of seizures; TCAs may lower the seizure threshold. [US]
- **Angle-Closure Glaucoma**: Pupillary dilation (mydriasis) may trigger an acute angle-closure attack in patients with anatomically narrow angles. [US]
- **Bipolar Disorder**: Screening for bipolar risk is essential; treating a depressive episode with an antidepressant alone may precipitate a mixed/manic episode. [US]
- **Psychosis/Schizophrenia**: May aggravate psychotic symptoms or induce a shift to mania in manic-depressive patients. [US]

### Monitoring
- **Cardiac**: Monitor the cardiovascular system closely in elderly patients if the daily dose exceeds 20 mg. [US]
- **Glycaemic Control**: Both elevation and lowering of blood sugar levels have been reported. [US]

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Note: Frequencies were not specified in the source document. Reactions are listed by system organ class.*

- **Cardiovascular**: Myocardial infarction, stroke, heart block, arrhythmias, hypotension (particularly orthostatic), hypertension, tachycardia, palpitations.
- **Psychiatric**: Confusional states (especially in the elderly), hallucinations, delusions, anxiety, agitation, hypomania, exacerbation of psychosis, insomnia, nightmares.
- **Neurological**: Seizures, incoordination, ataxia, tremors, peripheral neuropathy, extrapyramidal symptoms, drowsiness, dizziness, headache, tinnitus.
- **Anticholinergic**: Dry mouth (rarely sublingual adenitis), blurred vision, mydriasis, increased intraocular pressure, constipation, paralytic ileus, urinary retention, hyperpyrexia.
- **Hematologic**: Agranulocytosis, bone marrow depression, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, purpura, eosinophilia.
- **Gastrointestinal**: Nausea, vomiting, anorexia, epigastric distress, diarrhea, peculiar taste, stomatitis, black tongue.
- **Endocrine**: Impotence, libido changes, gynecomastia (male), breast enlargement/galactorrhea (female), testicular swelling, blood sugar fluctuations.
- **Other**: Jaundice, altered liver function, alopecia, flushing, weight gain/loss, perspiration.
- **Withdrawal Symptoms**: Abrupt cessation after prolonged therapy may produce nausea, headache, and malaise. [US]

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **MAOIs**: Risk of hyperpyretic crises, severe convulsions, and death. [US]
- **Cisapride**: Risk of serious arrhythmias and QT prolongation. [US]
- **CYP2D6 Inhibitors**: Drugs that inhibit Cytochrome P450 2D6 (e.g., SSRIs like fluoxetine, sertraline, paroxetine; quinidine; cimetidine) can significantly increase protriptyline plasma concentrations (up to 8-fold). [US]
- **Cimetidine**: Reduces hepatic metabolism, increasing TCA steady-state concentrations and anticholinergic side effects. [US]
- **Guanethidine**: Protriptyline may block the antihypertensive effects of guanethidine or similarly acting compounds. [US]
- **CNS Depressants**: Potentiates the effects of alcohol, barbiturates, and other CNS depressants. [US]
- **Anticholinergics/Sympathomimetics**: Requires close supervision and dose adjustment due to additive effects. [US]
- **Tramadol**: Concurrent use may increase the risk of seizures. [US]

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - [US]: Category not explicitly lettered in text, but "safe use has not been established." Use only if potential benefits outweigh hazards.
    - Animal Data: Doses 10x the human dose in mice, rats, and rabbits showed no apparent adverse effects on reproduction. [US]
- **Breastfeeding**: Safe use in nursing mothers has not been established. [US]
- **Fertility**: Not specified in available sources beyond animal reproductive data.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and effectiveness have not been established. Protriptyline is not approved for use in children. [US]
- **Elderly Use**: Start at a lower dose (5 mg tid). Increased risk of confusion and cardiovascular events. Closely monitor if doses exceed 20 mg/day. [US]
- **Renal Impairment**: Dose selection should be cautious in the elderly due to decreased renal function, but specific eGFR adjustments are not provided. [US]
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Use with caution; dose selection should reflect the greater frequency of decreased hepatic function in certain populations. [US]

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: The exact mechanism of antidepressant action in humans is unknown. It is not an MAOI and does not act primarily via CNS stimulation. [US]
- **Pharmacodynamics**: It possesses "activating" properties rather than sedative or tranquilizing effects. [US]
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Well absorbed from the GI tract.
    - **Distribution**: Rapidly sequestered in tissues; penetrates the brain quickly. 
    - **Metabolism**: Significant demethylation in the liver. Substrate for CYP2D6.
    - **Elimination**: Rate of excretion is slow. Approximately 50% is excreted in urine over 16 days. Fecal excretion is minor. [US]

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Specific pivotal trials for protriptyline were not detailed in the source. Information is derived from pooled analyses of antidepressant drugs regarding suicidality risks across various psychiatric disorders (MDD, OCD). [US]

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). [US]
- **Container**: Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container. [US]

- **Poison Schedule/Legal Classification**:

- **Packaging**: Available in bottles of 30, 100, and 1000 tablets. [US]
