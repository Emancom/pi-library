# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data, regulatory requirements, and pharmacological profiles into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not intended to replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule; amino acid derivative (Naturally occurring substance).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) on Dialysis:** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
    - **Frequency:** Administered every 3 or 4 hours (q3h or q4h), and never less than every 6 hours (q6h) [US].
    - **Maximum Dose:** Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
    - **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect parenteral products for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL. Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature (25°C) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of IV administration. Discontinue treatment immediately if a reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizures:** Reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral formulation precaution):** Chronic administration of high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].
- **Monitoring:** 
    - **Metabolic Disorders:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
    - **ESRD:** Monitor trough (pre-dialysis) concentrations [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic errors.

| Frequency | Adverse Event [US] |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site reaction (33%), pharyngitis (23%), headache (22%), hypertension (20%), nausea (8-12%), vomiting (15-21%), diarrhea (16%), abdominal pain (9-21%), fever (7-12%), chest pain (12-15%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Asthenia, back pain, flu syndrome, infection, palpitations, tachycardia, hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, peripheral edema, weight changes, dizziness, paresthesia, dyspnea, pruritus, rash |
| **Serious Reactions** | Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm, seizures [US] |

- **Body Odor:** Less frequent but noted in clinical experience [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting are common; gastritis is less frequent [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly. Monitor INR levels after initiating treatment or following dose adjustments [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not assigned/Not specified]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (BSA) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine in milk. Weigh the benefits of maternal treatment against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, etc.) indicate levocarnitine is not mutagenic [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg) as in adults [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in the provided source.
- **Renal Impairment:** Safely used in ESRD on dialysis via IV route; however, high-dose *oral* therapy is cautioned due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, facilitating oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Corrects biochemical carnitine deficiency (defined as <20 µmol/L free carnitine at one week post-term).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, IV levocarnitine (20 mg/kg post-dialysis) increased pre-dialysis trough concentrations from ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L by week 8. While biochemical correction is established, clinical outcome benefits for malaise and weakness in this population have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat. Discard any unused portion of single-dose vials as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** 
    - **United States:** Rx-only (Human Prescription Drug).
- **Poison/Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
