# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Always consult the current local regulatory approved document before clinical application.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine; chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Severe Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing intervals should be every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
- **Dose Range:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is recommended.

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Adjustments:** Dose adjustments (e.g., downward to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect parenteral products for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
- Discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate risks before re-administration [US].
- **Neurologic Effects (Seizures):** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed following levocarnitine administration [US].
- **Monitoring:** Clinical status and carnitine levels should be monitored regularly to ensure therapeutic efficacy and safety.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5% in ESRD trials)
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (9%), headache (22%), injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), fever (7%), accidental injury (10%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), chest pain (12%), tachycardia (6%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%).
- **Metabolic/Nutritional:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hypervolemia (5%).
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), paresthesia (5%), depression (5%).

### Other Reported Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis and transient nausea/vomiting.
- **Other:** Body odor has been reported during therapy [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not explicitly assigned in source]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentration in milk after exogenous administration. It is unknown if levocarnitine is excreted in human milk. Weigh the benefits of maternal treatment against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant; discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg); safety and efficacy follow adult guidelines for the specified indications [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide). The **intravenous** form is specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also facilitates the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While IV levocarnitine effectively increases carnitine concentrations, the impact on specific clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, arrhythmias) in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** When diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL), the solution is stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags [US].
