# Editorial Product Information: Pirfenidone

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a concise reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local clinical guidelines required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the Australian PI was available for review; therefore, regional variations for other jurisdictions (e.g., US, European) are "Not specified in available sources" unless otherwise noted.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pirfenidone
- **Chemical Class:** Pyridinone (5-methyl-1-phenylpyridin-2-(1H)-one)
- **Brand Name(s):** ARX-Pirfenidone [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** L04AX05
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 267 mg (light yellow to yellow, biconvex, oval-shaped) [Australian]
    - Film-coated tablets: 801 mg (light pink to pink, biconvex, capsule-shaped) [Australian]
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Cipla Australia Pty Ltd. [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis (IPF):** Indicated for the treatment of IPF in adults [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Recommended Daily Dose:** 801 mg three times daily (TID) with food, for a total of 2403 mg/day [Australian].
- **Maximum Dose:** Doses exceeding 2403 mg/day are not recommended for any patient [Australian].

### Titration Schedule
To improve tolerability, the dose should be titrated over 14 days using the 267 mg tablet [Australian]:
- **Days 1 to 7:** 267 mg TID (801 mg/day)
- **Days 8 to 14:** 534 mg TID (1602 mg/day)
- **Day 15 onwards:** 801 mg TID (2403 mg/day)

### Administration Instructions
- **Food:** Must be taken with food to reduce the incidence of nausea and dizziness [Australian].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- **Interruption <14 days:** Resume at the previous recommended daily dose without re-titration [Australian].
- **Interruption ≥14 days:** Re-initiate therapy by undergoing the initial 2-week titration regimen [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to pirfenidone or any excipients [Australian].
- Concomitant use of **fluvoxamine** [Australian].
- History of angioedema associated with pirfenidone [Australian].
- Severe hepatic impairment or end-stage liver disease [Australian].
- Severe renal impairment (CrCl <30 mL/min) or end-stage renal disease requiring dialysis [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Drug-Induced Liver Injury (DILI), including fatal cases, has been reported. Perform Liver Function Tests (LFTs: ALT, AST, bilirubin) prior to initiation, monthly for the first 6 months, and every 3 months thereafter [Australian].
- **Photosensitivity and Rash:** Exposure to direct sunlight (including sunlamps) should be avoided or minimised. Patients should use daily sunblock and protective clothing [Australian].
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCAR):** SJS, TEN, and DRESS have been reported. Permanent discontinuation is required if SCAR is confirmed [Australian].
- **Angioedema/Anaphylaxis:** Immediate discontinuation is required if signs of hypersensitivity or angioedema occur [Australian].
- **Dizziness and Fatigue:** May occur and impact the ability to perform tasks requiring mental alertness [Australian].
- **Weight Loss:** Physicians should monitor weight and encourage increased caloric intake if weight loss becomes clinically significant [Australian].
- **Hyponatraemia:** Reported post-marketing; regular monitoring of laboratory parameters is recommended in symptomatic patients [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Data (from Clinical Trials)
| Adverse Effect | Frequency (Pirfenidone 2403 mg/day) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Nausea (36%), Rash (30%), Upper Respiratory Tract Infection (27%), Diarrhoea (26%), Fatigue (26%), Abdominal Pain (24%), Headache (22%), Dyspepsia (19%), Dizziness (18%), Vomiting (13%), Anorexia (13%), GERD (11%), Sinusitis (11%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Insomnia (10%), Weight Decreased (10%), Arthralgia (10%), Photosensitivity reaction (9.3%) |

### Serious/Post-Marketing Adverse Effects
- **Hepatobiliary:** Clinically relevant DILI, elevated bilirubin in combination with AST/ALT [Australian].
- **Hematologic:** Agranulocytosis [Australian].
- **Metabolic:** Hyponatraemia (Uncommon) [Australian].
- **Skin:** SJS, TEN, DRESS [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP1A2 Inhibitors:** Pirfenidone is primarily metabolised via CYP1A2.
    - **Strong Inhibitors (e.g., Fluvoxamine):** Contraindicated. Increases pirfenidone exposure 4-fold [Australian].
    - **Moderate Inhibitors (e.g., Ciprofloxacin):** Use with caution. If ciprofloxacin 750 mg BID is unavoidable, reduce pirfenidone dose to 1602 mg/day (534 mg TID) [Australian].
- **CYP1A2 Inducers:**
    - **Cigarette Smoking:** Induces CYP1A2 and reduces pirfenidone exposure by 50%. Patients should stop smoking before and during treatment [Australian].
    - **Strong Inducers (e.g., Rifampicin):** Avoid due to significant lowering of plasma levels [Australian].
- **Transporters:** Pirfenidone may inhibit intestinal P-glycoprotein (P-gp) during clinical use [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. No data in pregnant women. Animal studies show placental transfer and potential accumulation in amniotic fluid. Avoid use during pregnancy as a precautionary measure [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Unknown if excreted in human milk. A risk to the breastfeeding child cannot be excluded. A decision must be made to either discontinue nursing or discontinue the drug [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal data showed irregular oestrous cycles in rats at doses ~1.7 times the MRHD [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in patients under 18 years [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment required in patients 65 years and older [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - Mild: No adjustment [Australian].
    - Moderate (CrCl 30-50 mL/min): Use with caution [Australian].
    - Severe (CrCl <30 mL/min) or Dialysis: Contraindicated [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - Mild to Moderate (Child-Pugh A & B): Use with caution; monitor closely for toxicity [Australian].
    - Severe or End-Stage Liver Disease: Contraindicated [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Not fully established. Displays anti-fibrotic and anti-inflammatory properties. Attenuates pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β) and pro-fibrotic cytokines in animal models [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability unknown in humans. Food reduces $C_{max}$ by 40% [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Binds to human plasma proteins (50–62%), primarily albumin. Volume of distribution $\approx$ 70 L [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily CYP1A2 ($>$75%), with minor contributions from CYP2C9, 2C19, 2D6, and 2E1 [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life $\approx$ 2.4 hours. 80% cleared in urine within 24 hours (mostly as 5-carboxy-pirfenidone metabolite) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
The efficacy of pirfenidone 2403 mg/day was established in three Phase 3 trials (PIPF-004, PIPF-006/CAPACITY, and PIPF-016/ASCEND):
- **Primary Endpoint:** Change from baseline in percent predicted Forced Vital Capacity (FVC).
- **Outcomes:** Pirfenidone significantly reduced the decline in FVC compared to placebo. In PIPF-016, 17% of pirfenidone patients had an FVC decline $\ge$10% vs. 32% in the placebo group [Australian].
- **Mortality:** A pooled analysis showed a 48% reduction in the risk of all-cause mortality within the first 12 months (HR 0.52) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Packaging:** PVC/Aclar aluminium foil blisters [Australian].
- **Handling:** Unused or expired medicine should be returned to a pharmacy for disposal [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
