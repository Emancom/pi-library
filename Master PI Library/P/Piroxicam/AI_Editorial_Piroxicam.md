# Editorial Product Information: Piroxicam

## Editorial Note
- This harmonised editorial overview has been developed using official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction:
    - **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**
- **Purpose:** This document provides a clinical synthesis of piroxicam pharmacology, dosing, and safety for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis for informational purposes and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labelling for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Piroxicam
- **Chemical Class:** Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID); N-heterocyclic carboxamide of 1, 2-benzothiazine-1, 1-dioxide.
- **Brand Names:** APO-Piroxicam [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Hard gelatin capsules: 10 mg and 20 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- Symptomatic treatment of rheumatoid arthritis (RA).
- Symptomatic treatment of osteoarthritis (OA).
- Symptomatic treatment of ankylosing spondylitis (AS).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** Use the minimum effective dose for the shortest possible duration. The recommended starting dose is 10 mg daily [Australian]. 
- **Standard Maintenance Dose:** Generally 20 mg administered as a single daily dose [Australian].
- **Maximum Dose:** 20 mg daily. Doses exceeding 20 mg are not recommended due to an increased risk of adverse effects [Australian].
- **Treatment Duration:** Treatment should preferably be limited to 14 days. If long-term therapy is required, clinical efficacy and risk factors must be re-evaluated at 14 days and at frequent subsequent intervals [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** Intended for oral use.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Gastrointestinal:** Active peptic ulceration, GI bleeding or perforation, active inflammatory disease of the GI tract, or a recent history of these conditions [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to piroxicam. Cross-sensitivity exists; contraindicated in patients who have experienced asthma, nasal polyps, angioedema, or urticaria following aspirin or other NSAIDs [Australian].
- **Severe Skin Reactions:** History of severe allergic drug reactions, particularly cutaneous reactions such as Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), erythema multiforme, or any prior skin reaction to piroxicam [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular:** Peri-operative pain management in the setting of coronary artery bypass graft (CABG) surgery; severe heart failure [Australian].
- **Organ Failure:** Severe renal failure or severe hepatic failure [Australian].
- **Concomitant Therapy:** Co-administration with other NSAIDs (including COX-2 selective inhibitors) or analgesic doses of aspirin [Australian].
- **Pregnancy/Lactation:** Third trimester of pregnancy; breastfeeding mothers [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiovascular Thrombotic Events:** NSAIDs may increase the risk of fatal myocardial infarction and stroke. Risk may increase with duration of use or in patients with existing CV disease/risk factors [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal Toxicity:** Piroxicam is associated with a high risk of serious GI toxicity (ulceration, bleeding, perforation) relative to other NSAIDs. Risk is highest in the elderly (>70 years); use in patients >80 years should be avoided [Australian].
- **Hypertension:** May cause new-onset hypertension or worsen pre-existing disease. Blood pressure monitoring is required during initiation and throughout therapy [Australian].
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** May cause fatal DRESS syndrome, SJS, or TEN. Discontinue immediately at the first sign of skin rash or mucosal lesions [Australian].
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Borderline elevations of LFTs occur in up to 15% of patients. Severe reactions, including fatal hepatitis and jaundice, have been reported [Australian].
- **Renal Effects:** Long-term use may lead to renal papillary necrosis. NSAIDs may precipitate overt renal decompensation in patients with compromised renal blood flow (e.g., heart failure, dehydration, or pre-existing renal disease) [Australian].
- **Ophthalmological:** Visual disturbances have been reported; patients developing symptoms should undergo an eye examination [Australian].
- **Masking Infection:** Anti-inflammatory and analgesic properties may mask signs of infection [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Approximately 30% of patients experience side effects at 20 mg/day, rising with higher doses [Australian].

| Frequency | System/Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Gastrointestinal (20% overall): Abdominal discomfort, dyspepsia [Australian]. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | GI: Flatulence (5.2%), nausea (4.8%), abdominal pain (4.7%), epigastric distress (4.1%), constipation (3.8%), diarrhoea (3.2%), peptic ulcer (~1%). CNS: Dizziness (4.1%), headache (4.1%). Genitourinary: Oedema (2.7%). Skin: Rash (2.4%), pruritus (1.1%). ENT: Stomatitis (1.0%) [Australian]. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | CNS: Sedation, drowsiness (2.1%). Laboratory: Elevated LFTs, BUN/creatinine [Australian]. |
| **Rare (<0.1%) / Frequency Not Defined** | Palpitations, tachycardia, tinnitus, deafness, alopecia, onycholysis, pancreatitis, hepatitis, aplastic anaemia, leucopenia, thrombocytopenia, interstitial nephritis, nephrotic syndrome, aseptic meningitis, DRESS, SJS, TEN, fixed drug eruption [Australian]. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Peptic ulceration, GI haemorrhage, fatal hepatitis, and severe cutaneous reactions (SJS/TEN) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Anticoagulants:** Increased risk of serious, potentially fatal haemorrhage with warfarin, coumarins, and NOACs (e.g., apixaban, dabigatran, rivaroxaban) [Australian].
- **Methotrexate:** Decreased elimination of methotrexate, leading to increased plasma levels and potential lethality [Australian].
- **Lithium:** Decreased renal clearance resulting in increased steady-state lithium concentrations [Australian].
- **Antihypertensives:** Reduced efficacy of ACE inhibitors, ARBs, beta-blockers, and diuretics. Combination with ACE inhibitors/ARBs and diuretics increases risk of acute renal failure, especially in the elderly/dehydrated [Australian].
- **Aspirin:** Concomitant use reduces piroxicam plasma levels to ~80% and increases the risk of GI adverse reactions. Piroxicam interferes with the anti-platelet effect of low-dose aspirin [Australian].
- **Protein-Bound Agents:** Piroxicam may displace other highly protein-bound drugs (e.g., hydantoins, sulfonamides, sulfonylureas) [Australian].
- **Corticosteroids and SSRIs:** Significantly increased risk of GI ulceration and bleeding [Australian].
- **Cardiac Glycosides:** May exacerbate heart failure and increase plasma glycoside levels [Australian].
- **Colestyramine:** Enhances oral clearance and decreases the half-life of piroxicam. Administer piroxicam 2 hours before or 6 hours after colestyramine [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category C.** 
    - **First/Second Trimester:** Use only if benefits outweigh risks; use lowest dose and shortest duration. 
    - **Second/Third Trimester:** Risk of foetal renal dysfunction leading to oligohydramnios and neonatal renal impairment.
    - **Third Trimester:** Contraindicated. Risk of premature closure of the ductus arteriosus, foetal renal impairment, and delayed labour/birth [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Piroxicam is excreted in breast milk at approximately 1% to 3% of maternal plasma concentrations [Australian].
- **Fertility:** NSAIDs may delay or prevent ovulation, potentially causing reversible infertility. Withdrawal of piroxicam should be considered in women having difficulty conceiving [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use in children under 12 years is not recommended as safety and efficacy have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** High risk of complications. Caution is required; monitor renal, hepatic, and GI function closely. Avoid use in patients over 80 years [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe renal failure. Use with caution and lower doses in patients with impaired renal function [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe hepatic impairment/failure [Australian].
- **CYP2C9 Poor Metabolisers:** Use with caution due to the risk of abnormally high plasma levels [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Reversible inhibition of cyclooxygenase (COX), leading to inhibited prostanoid synthesis. Additional mechanisms include inhibition of neutrophil aggregation, lysosomal enzyme release, and superoxide anion generation [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Exhibits analgesic, anti-inflammatory, and antipyretic properties. Does not stimulate the pituitary-adrenal axis [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Well absorbed; not affected by food.
    - **Distribution:** 99% bound to plasma proteins.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised via hydroxylation and conjugation with glucuronic acid.
    - **Elimination:** Half-life is approximately 36 to 45 hours. Primarily excreted in urine and faeces; <5% unchanged [Australian].
