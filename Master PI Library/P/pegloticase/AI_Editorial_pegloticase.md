# Editorial Product Information: Pegloticase

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory information into a clinically relevant format.
- **Note:** This synthesis is an editorial reference and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in specific jurisdictions. Clinicians should consult the full, current PI from their local regulatory authority before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pegloticase (a PEGylated uric acid-specific enzyme).
- **Chemical Class:** Recombinant modified mammalian urate oxidase (uricase).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Krystexxa** [US].
- **ATC Code:** M04AX02.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Ready-to-Use Injection:** 8 mg/50 mL (0.16 mg/mL) in a single-dose vial [US].
    - **To-be-Diluted Injection:** 8 mg/mL in a single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Horizon Therapeutics USA, Inc. (an Amgen subsidiary) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Gout:** Indicated for the treatment of chronic gout in adult patients refractory to conventional therapy. Refractory gout is defined as failure to normalize serum uric acid and inadequate control of signs and symptoms with xanthine oxidase inhibitors at the maximum medically appropriate dose, or where these agents are contraindicated [US-approved].
- **Limitations of Use:** Not recommended for the treatment of asymptomatic hyperuricemia [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:** 8 mg administered as an intravenous infusion every two weeks [US].
- **Co-administration with Methotrexate:** It is recommended to co-administer pegloticase with weekly oral methotrexate 15 mg and folic acid/folinic acid supplementation. Methotrexate and folate should be initiated at least 4 weeks prior to pegloticase and continued throughout treatment. Pegloticase monotherapy may be used if methotrexate is contraindicated or clinically inappropriate [US].
- **Pre-treatment Requirements:** 
    - Discontinue oral urate-lowering agents before starting therapy; do not initiate them during treatment [US].
    - Screen patients at risk for G6PD deficiency prior to initiation [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - **Do not** administer as an IV push or bolus [US].
    - Infuse over no less than 120 minutes [US].
    - **Pre-medication:** Patients must receive antihistamines and corticosteroids before each infusion to minimize the risk of anaphylaxis and infusion reactions [US].
    - If an infusion reaction occurs, the infusion may be slowed, stopped, or restarted at a slower rate at the physician's discretion [US].
- **Monitoring:** Monitor serum uric acid levels before **every** infusion. Discontinue treatment if levels rise above 6 mg/dL, particularly if two consecutive levels above 6 mg/dL are observed, as this indicates a loss of therapeutic response and increased risk of infusion reactions [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **G6PD Deficiency:** Contraindicated in patients with glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency due to the risk of hemolysis and methemoglobinemia [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Patients with a history of serious hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis, to pegloticase or any of its components [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning (US): Anaphylaxis and Infusion Reactions.** Anaphylaxis and infusion reactions can occur during and after administration, including the first infusion. Reactions typically manifest within 2 hours, but delayed reactions can occur. Pegloticase must be administered in a healthcare setting by providers prepared to manage anaphylaxis.
- **Boxed Warning (US): G6PD Deficiency-Associated Hemolysis and Methemoglobinemia.** Screen at-risk patients (e.g., those of African, Mediterranean, or Southern Asian ancestry) before starting therapy. Do not administer to patients with G6PD deficiency.
- **Gout Flares:** An increase in gout flares is common upon initiation due to mobilization of urate deposits. Gout flare prophylaxis (NSAIDs or colchicine) is recommended starting at least 1 week before therapy and continuing for at least 6 months [US].
- **Congestive Heart Failure (CHF):** Exacerbations have been reported. Exercise caution and monitor patients with CHF closely following infusion [US].
- **Re-treatment:** Safety and efficacy have not been established after a treatment gap >4 weeks. Re-treatment may increase the risk of immunogenicity and infusion reactions [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency of Adverse Reactions
| Adverse Reaction | Co-administered with Methotrexate (n=96) | Pegloticase Monotherapy (n=49-85) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gout Flare** | 67% | 71% - 77% |
| **Infusion Reaction** | 4% | 26% - 31% |
| **Arthralgia** | 14% | 10% |
| **Nausea** | 5% | 12% |
| **Anaphylaxis** | 1% | 5% |
| **COVID-19** | 9% | 6% |
| **Fatigue** | 5% | 4% |

- **Other Common Reactions (Monotherapy):** Contusion/ecchymosis (11%), nasopharyngitis (7%), constipation (6%), chest pain (6%), and vomiting (5-8%) [US].
- **Postmarketing Experience:** Asthenia, malaise, and peripheral swelling have been reported [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Methotrexate:** Co-administration increases pegloticase concentrations and decreases the incidence and titer of anti-pegloticase antibodies [US].
- **PEGylated Products:** Potential for binding with other PEGylated therapeutics; the clinical impact of anti-PEG antibodies on other drugs is unknown [US].
- **Urate-Lowering Agents:** Oral agents (e.g., allopurinol) may blunt the rise of serum uric acid, potentially masking the loss of pegloticase efficacy and increasing the risk of infusion reactions. Discontinue these prior to therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified under new labeling; however, there are no adequate/well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Animal data (rats/rabbits) at doses 50-75 times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) showed no structural abnormalities but did result in decreased fetal and pup body weights [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if pegloticase is excreted in human milk. Use only if the benefit to the mother outweighs the unknown risk to the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of fertility impairment was observed in rats at doses up to 50 times the MRHD [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy were observed between patients ≥65 years and younger patients, though greater sensitivity in some older individuals cannot be ruled out. No dose adjustment required [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required. eGFR was not a significant covariate impacting pharmacokinetics [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not formally studied; however, hepatic impairment is not expected to impact the clearance of a PEGylated protein [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Pegloticase is a recombinant uricase that catalyzes the oxidation of uric acid to allantoin. Allantoin is an inert, water-soluble metabolite readily excreted by the kidneys [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Mean plasma uric acid levels typically drop to 0.7 mg/dL within 24 hours of the first 8 mg dose, compared to 8.2 mg/dL for placebo [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Pegloticase levels are proportional to the dose. Significant covariates include body surface area and the presence of anti-pegloticase antibodies, which increase clearance [US].
- **Immunogenicity:** Anti-pegloticase antibodies developed in 92% of patients receiving monotherapy every 2 weeks. High antibody titers are associated with failure to maintain target uric acid levels and a higher incidence of infusion reactions [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **MIRROR Trial (Trial 1):** A 52-week study comparing pegloticase + methotrexate vs. pegloticase + placebo. The responder rate (uric acid <6 mg/dL for 80% of Month 6) was significantly higher in the co-administration group (71% vs. 39%, p < 0.0001). Tophi resolution was also significantly improved (54% vs. 31%) [US].
- **Trials 2 and 3:** Two 6-month replicate trials for monotherapy. Responder rates for pegloticase 8 mg every 2 weeks were 47% and 38% respectively, vs. 0% for placebo [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Maintain under refrigeration at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F). Store in the original carton to protect from light [US].
- **Handling:** Do not shake or freeze.
- **Diluted Solution:** For the "To-be-Diluted" presentation, the diluted solution is stable for 4 hours at room temperature or refrigerated. Do not freeze [US].
- **Ready-to-Use Vial:** Once the stopper is punctured, use immediately. The unopened vial may be kept at room temperature for up to 4 hours [US].
