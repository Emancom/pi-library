# Editorial Product Information: Pentetate Zinc Trisodium (Zn-DTPA)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonized editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory information into a clinically actionable format.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local regulatory labeling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pentetate zinc trisodium (also known as trisodium zinc diethylenetriaminepentaacetate or **Zn-DTPA**).
- **Chemical Class:** Chelating agent.
- **Brand Names:** Not explicitly named as a proprietary brand in the source; referred to by its generic name.
- **ATC Code:** V03AB26 (Note: Not explicitly listed in source but standard for this agent).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injection (concentrate), 200 mg/mL in 5 mL single-use ampoules (1000 mg total per ampoule) [US].
- **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) or Respiratory (Inhalation).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Hameln Pharmaceuticals [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Internal Radiocontamination:** Indicated for the treatment of individuals with confirmed or suspected internal contamination with plutonium, americium, or curium to increase the rate of elimination [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing Principles
- **Timing:** Chelation is most effective when administered within the first 24 hours of contamination. However, treatment should still be initiated even if delayed.
- **Initial Dose Note:** It is preferable to use **Pentetate Calcium Trisodium (Ca-DTPA)** as the initial dose if available within the first 24 hours, as it is more effective than Zn-DTPA during this window. After 24 hours, Zn-DTPA and Ca-DTPA are considered equally effective [US].

### Adult and Adolescent Dosage
- **Initial Dose:** 1.0 gram administered intravenously [US].
- **Maintenance Dose:** 1.0 gram once daily administered intravenously [US].

### Pediatric Dosage (<12 years of age)
- **Initial Dose:** 14 mg/kg intravenously (not to exceed 1.0 gram) [US].
- **Maintenance Dose:** 14 mg/kg once daily intravenously (not to exceed 1.0 gram) [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Intravenous Push:** May be administered undiluted as a slow IV push over 3–4 minutes.
- **Intravenous Infusion:** May be diluted in 100–250 mL of 5% dextrose (D5W), Ringers Lactate, or Normal Saline and infused over 30 minutes.
- **Inhalation (Nebulization):** If contamination is via inhalation only, Zn-DTPA may be nebulized (diluted 1:1 with sterile water or saline). Swallowing the expectorant should be avoided [US].

### Treatment Duration
- Depends on the extent of contamination and individual response (monitored via radioactivity elimination rates). Treatment may continue for days, months, or years until the excretion enhancement factor approaches 1 [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Asthma Exacerbation:** Nebulized chelation therapy may trigger or worsen asthma. Caution is required for the inhalation route [US].
- **Trace Metal Depletion:** Chronic treatment (over several months) can deplete endogenous metals such as magnesium and manganese. Monitor these levels and provide supplements as necessary [US].
- **Contamination Control:** Patients must follow strict hygiene (e.g., using toilets instead of urinals, frequent handwashing, separate laundering of contaminated clothing) to prevent the spread of radioactivity to others [US].
- **Hydration:** Patients should maintain high fluid intake and void frequently to minimize radiation exposure to the bladder [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **General Frequency:** Adverse events are rarely reported (recorded in approximately 6% of the registry population) [US].
- **Nervous System:** Headache, lightheadedness.
- **Musculoskeletal:** Pelvic pain.
- **Respiratory:** While cough and wheezing were reported with Ca-DTPA nebulization, they were not specifically reported with Zn-DTPA in the registry [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Not specified in available sources, though potential for mineral depletion with long-term use is noted.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic:** No formal human drug-drug interaction studies have been identified [US].
- **Adjuvant Therapy:** If multiple or unknown radiocontaminants are present, additional therapies (e.g., Prussian blue for cesium, potassium iodide for radioiodine) may be required. Zn-DTPA does not bind radioactive iodine [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy Category:** **Category B** [US]. 
- **Pregnancy Considerations:** No human data exists. Animal studies (mice) at 31 times the human dose showed no evidence of fetal harm or impaired fertility, though a slight reduction in birth weight was noted. Treatment should be initiated and continued if contamination is confirmed, weighing the risks of radiation toxicity against the drug's risks [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Radioactive contaminants are excreted in breast milk. Women with suspected or known contamination should NOT breastfeed regardless of chelation therapy status [US].
- **Fertility:** Studies on carcinogenesis, mutagenesis, and impairment of fertility have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety/efficacy extrapolated from adults for IV use; dose is based on body size. Safety/efficacy for the nebulized route has NOT been established in children [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required. However, impaired renal function may decrease the rate of elimination. In severe cases, high-efficiency, high-flux dialysis may be used to assist elimination; dialysis fluid must be handled with radiation precautions [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No specific studies identified; however, Zn-DTPA and its chelates are primarily cleared by the kidneys [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Zn-DTPA exchanges its zinc ion for metal ions with a higher binding affinity (plutonium, americium, curium). The resulting stable metal-chelate complex is then excreted in the urine [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Poorly absorbed from the GI tract (~5%). Approximately 20% absorption via the lungs [US].
    - **Distribution:** Rapidly distributes into the extracellular fluid; no significant penetration into cells or erythrocytes [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Minimal metabolic change [US].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily via glomerular filtration in the kidneys. Plasma half-lives follow a tri-exponential model (averages of 1.4 min, 14.5 min, and 94.4 min). Over 99% is excreted in urine within 24 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Data is derived from a U.S. Registry of 646 individuals (286 with bioassay data) who were accidentally contaminated, primarily in occupational settings. Maintenance therapy with 1.0g daily Zn-DTPA showed significant increases in the urinary excretion of plutonium (in one case by a factor of 45 after the first dose) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Inspect for particulate matter and discoloration before use. Open One-Point Cut (OPC) ampoules by breaking the neck downward [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as a Human Prescription Drug (Rx-only) [US].
- **Other Jurisdictions:** Not specified in available sources.
