# Editorial Product Information: Propafenone Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document serves as a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical data into a concise, structured format.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidelines required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Propafenone hydrochloride (Class 1C antiarrhythmic).
- **Chemical Class:** Phenylpropiophenone derivative with local anaesthetic and membrane-stabilising activity.
- **Brand Names:** Noted manufacturers include Northstar Rx LLC and Glenmark Pharmaceuticals Limited [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Extended-Release (ER) Capsules:** 225 mg, 325 mg, and 425 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Northstar Rx LLC (Distributed by); Glenmark Pharmaceuticals Limited (Manufactured by) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Atrial Fibrillation:** Indicated for the prolongation of time to recurrence of symptomatic atrial fibrillation (AF) in patients with episodic AF (paroxysmal or persistent) who do not possess structural heart disease [US].
- **Usage Considerations:** 
    - Efficacy in permanent AF, isolated atrial flutter, or paroxysmal supraventricular tachycardia (PSVT) has not been established [US].
    - Not indicated for the control of ventricular rate during active atrial fibrillation [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):**
    - **Initial Dose:** 225 mg administered every 12 hours [US].
    - **Titration:** Dosage may be increased at intervals of at least 5 days. Increments proceed to 325 mg every 12 hours, with a maximum dose of 425 mg every 12 hours if required for therapeutic effect [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - May be taken with or without food [US].
    - Capsules must be swallowed whole; do not crush, chew, or divide the contents [US].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, the next dose should be taken at the scheduled time. Do not double the dose [US].
- **Dose Reductions:** Consider dose reduction in patients with hepatic impairment, significant QRS widening, or second- or third-degree AV block [US].

## 4. Contraindications
Propafenone is contraindicated in the following conditions [US]:
- Heart failure.
- Cardiogenic shock.
- Sinoatrial, atrioventricular, and intraventricular disorders of impulse generation or conduction (e.g., sick sinus syndrome, AV block) in the absence of a functional artificial pacemaker.
- Known Brugada Syndrome.
- Bradycardia.
- Marked hypotension.
- Bronchospastic disorders or severe obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD).
- Marked electrolyte imbalance.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Mortality [US]:** 
    - Data from the Cardiac Arrhythmia Suppression Trial (CAST) indicated an increased rate of death or reversed cardiac arrest in patients with non-life-threatening ventricular arrhythmias and a history of myocardial infarction treated with Class 1C antiarrhythmics (encainide/flecainide). 
    - It is prudent to consider Class 1C agents as carrying a significant proarrhythmic risk in patients with structural heart disease.
- **Proarrhythmic Effects:** May cause new or worsened arrhythmias, including life-threatening ventricular tachycardia or fibrillation. ECG evaluation is essential prior to and during therapy [US].
- **Unmasking Brugada Syndrome:** Therapy may reveal Brugada Syndrome. ECG monitoring is required after initiation; discontinue if suggestive changes occur [US].
- **Conduction Disturbances:** Propafenone slows AV conduction and may cause first-degree AV block. Use with caution in patients with existing conduction defects [US].
- **Heart Failure:** Due to negative inotropic and beta-blocking effects, propafenone may provoke or worsen heart failure, particularly in patients with pre-existing structural disease [US].
- **Agranulocytosis:** Reported within the first 2 months of therapy. Patients should promptly report signs of infection (fever, sore throat) [US].
- **Pacemaker Function:** May alter pacing and sensing thresholds. Devices should be monitored and reprogrammed as necessary [US].
- **Myasthenia Gravis:** Exacerbation has been reported [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Dizziness, taste disturbance [US].
- **Common (1–10%):** Palpitations, chest pain, dyspnea, nausea, fatigue, anxiety, constipation, upper respiratory tract infection, edema, influenza, blurred vision, first-degree AV block, bradycardia, vomiting, diarrhea, flatulence [US].
- **Uncommon to Rare (<1%):** Agranulocytosis, new-onset heart failure, ventricular tachycardia, sinus arrest, hepatomegaly, thrombocytopenia, syncope, somnolence, alopecia [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Sudden cardiac death, torsade de pointes, asystole, and severe conduction blocks [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP2D6 and CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Drugs such as quinidine, ritonavir, ketoconazole, and erythromycin significantly increase propafenone levels. Simultaneous use with both a 2D6 and 3A4 inhibitor must be avoided [US].
- **Digoxin:** Propafenone increases steady-state serum digoxin exposure by 60% to 270%. Monitor levels and adjust digoxin dose accordingly [US].
- **Warfarin:** Increases warfarin plasma concentrations and prolongs prothrombin time (INR). Adjust warfarin dose as needed [US].
- **Beta-Antagonists:** Increases plasma levels of propranolol and metoprolol [US].
- **Orlistat:** May reduce propafenone absorption. Abrupt cessation of orlistat in stabilised patients has resulted in convulsions and circulatory failure [US].
- **Lidocaine:** Concomitant use may increase the risk of CNS side effects [US].
- **Grapefruit Juice:** Increases propafenone plasma levels and should be avoided [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Classification:** Narrative summary provided (previously Category C). 
    - There are no adequate studies in pregnant women. Data from animal studies show embryo-fetal mortality at maternally toxic doses (3–6 times the MRHD). Propafenone crosses the placenta [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Propafenone and its metabolite are present in human milk. Levels are likely low, but breastfeeding should be weighed against the mother’s clinical need [US].
- **Fertility:** May transiently impair spermatogenesis in males. Reductions in sperm count and semen volume have been observed but remained within normal reference ranges and were reversible upon discontinuation [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety were observed, but greater sensitivity at higher doses cannot be ruled out. Clinical trials included 46% of subjects aged 65 and older [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Propafenone metabolites are excreted in urine (50%). Monitor closely for signs of toxicity [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Propafenone is highly metabolised by the liver. Bioavailability is significantly increased in severe dysfunction. Use with extreme caution and consider dose reduction [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Class 1C antiarrhythmic with local anaesthetic effects. It reduces the fast inward sodium current (Phase 0), slows upstroke velocity, and prolongs the effective refractory period. It also possesses weak beta-blocking activity (approx. 1/40th the potency of propranolol) [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Reaches peak plasma levels in 3 to 8 hours (ER formulation). Undergoes extensive, saturable first-pass metabolism [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Two patterns based on CYP2D6 (extensive vs. poor metabolisers). Extensive metabolisers produce the active 5-hydroxypropafenone metabolite [US].
    - **Distribution:** >95% protein bound; large volume of distribution (approx. 252 L) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Half-life is 2–10 hours in extensive metabolisers and 10–32 hours in poor metabolisers [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **RAFT Trial:** A US multicenter, double-blind study of 523 patients. All three doses (225 mg, 325 mg, 425 mg BID) significantly prolonged the time to the first recurrence of symptomatic atrial arrhythmia compared to placebo [US].
- **ERAFT Trial:** A European multicenter trial of 293 patients. Both 325 mg and 425 mg BID doses were superior to placebo in prolonging the time to recurrence of symptomatic paroxysmal AF [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as a Prescription Drug (Rx-only) [US].
- **Other Jurisdictions:** Not specified in available sources.
