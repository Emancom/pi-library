# Editorial Product Information: phentermine + topiramate

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): phentermine + topiramate
- Chemical class:
  - Phentermine: sympathomimetic amine anorectic
  - Topiramate: antiepileptic agent; sulfamate-substituted monosaccharide with carbonic anhydrase inhibitory activity
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 2012.
- Last revision/update of the provided source PI: Revised: 4/2026; recent major changes noted in 03/2026 (contraindications; selected warnings and precautions).
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources (except distribution restriction described under Warnings and Precautions for the U.S. — see Section 6).
- If other jurisdictional registration dates or PI revision dates are required: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of excess body weight in conjunction with a reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity for:
  - Adults with obesity.
  - Pediatric patients aged 12 years and older with obesity.
  - Adults with overweight in the presence of at least one weight-related comorbid condition.
- Limitations of use:
  - The effect on cardiovascular morbidity and mortality has not been established.
  - Safety and effectiveness of combination with other weight-loss products (prescription, OTC, herbal) have not been established.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Take once daily in the morning with or without food. Avoid evening administration to reduce the risk of insomnia.
- Recommended initiation and titration regimen (general adult pathway):
  - Start: 3.75 mg phentermine / 23 mg topiramate once daily for 14 days.
  - Then increase to 7.5 mg / 46 mg once daily.
  - After 12 weeks on 7.5 mg / 46 mg, evaluate weight loss. If adult patient has not lost ≥3% of baseline body weight, escalate:
    - 11.25 mg / 69 mg once daily for 14 days, then
    - 15 mg / 92 mg once daily.
  - After 12 weeks on 15 mg / 92 mg, evaluate weight loss. If adult patient has not lost ≥5% of baseline body weight, discontinue therapy as continued treatment is unlikely to provide clinically meaningful weight loss.

### Dosing by indication / pediatrics
- Pediatric patients aged ≥12 years: same dosing algorithm and evaluation criteria apply but assessment is by BMI reduction rather than absolute weight loss.
  - If pediatric patients do not meet BMI-reduction thresholds (same percentage thresholds as adults), follow the same escalation and discontinuation rules.
  - Monitor rate of weight loss in pediatric patients; if weight loss exceeds 2 lb (0.9 kg)/week, consider dose reduction.

### Renal impairment
- Mild renal impairment (CrCl ≥50 to <80 mL/min): no change in recommended dosing.
- Moderate renal impairment (CrCl ≥30 to <50 mL/min): maximum recommended dosage is 7.5 mg / 46 mg once daily.
- Severe renal impairment (CrCl <30 mL/min): maximum recommended dosage is 7.5 mg / 46 mg once daily; avoid use in end-stage renal disease on dialysis.
  - (CrCl calculated by Cockcroft-Gault using actual body weight.)

### Hepatic impairment
- Mild hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh 5–6): no change in recommended dosing.
- Moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh 7–9): maximum recommended dosage is 7.5 mg / 46 mg once daily.
- Severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh 10–15): avoid use.

### Discontinuation / tapering
- For patients taking 15 mg / 92 mg dose, taper prior to stopping: take 15 mg / 92 mg every other day for at least 1 week before discontinuation to reduce the risk of precipitating seizures.
- In situations where immediate discontinuation is medically required, appropriate monitoring is recommended.

### Administration instructions
- Take orally once daily in the morning.
- Monitor and titrate based on weight loss (adults) or BMI reduction (pediatrics) per the schedule above.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Pregnancy (contraindicated).
- Glaucoma.
- Hyperthyroidism.
- Concomitant use of monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs) or within 14 days of stopping an MAOI.
- Known hypersensitivity to phentermine, topiramate, any component of the product, or idiosyncrasy to sympathomimetic amines.
  - Noted hypersensitivity reactions with topiramate include anaphylaxis and angioedema.
- Additional jurisdictional contraindications: Not specified in available sources beyond the above.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed / prominent warnings
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity** — phentermine + topiramate can cause fetal harm. Advise patients who can become pregnant of the teratogenic risk and require a negative pregnancy test prior to initiation and monthly during treatment. Use of effective contraception is recommended during therapy.
  - [US] Distribution is restricted under a Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy (REMS)/limited program due to teratogenic risk; only certified dispensing sites may dispense the product.

### General warnings and precautions
- **Suicidal Behavior and Ideation**: Topiramate is an antiepileptic drug (AED); AEDs increase the risk of suicidal thoughts and behavior. Monitor for new or worsening depression, suicidal ideation, or unusual behavioral changes. Avoid use in patients with active suicidal ideation or history of suicide attempts.
- **Ophthalmologic adverse reactions**: Acute myopia and secondary angle-closure glaucoma have been reported; symptoms typically present within the first month but may occur at any time. Immediate discontinuation is recommended if symptoms occur. Visual field defects have been reported; consider discontinuation if visual problems occur.
- **Mood and sleep disorders**: May precipitate or worsen depression, anxiety, and insomnia. Consider dose reduction or discontinuation for clinically significant or persistent symptoms.
- **Cognitive impairment**: May cause impairment of attention/concentration, memory, and speech/language (word-finding) difficulties. Caution patients about driving or operating machinery until the individual response is known. Avoid excessive alcohol.
- **Slowing of linear growth (pediatrics)**: Associated with decreased height velocity in pediatric patients aged 12–17 years; monitor growth and consider dose reduction or discontinuation if growth is not as expected.
- **Metabolic acidosis**: Non-anion gap, hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis (↓ serum bicarbonate) can occur. Measure serum bicarbonate prior to initiation and periodically during treatment. Reduce dose or discontinue if persistent metabolic acidosis develops.
  - Avoid concomitant use with other carbonic anhydrase inhibitors when possible; if unavoidable, monitor for worsened acidosis.
- **Decrease in renal function**: Increases in serum creatinine and decreases in GFR have been observed; measure creatinine prior to initiation and during treatment. Reduce dose or discontinue if persistent elevations occur.
- **Risk of seizures with abrupt withdrawal**: Abrupt discontinuation of topiramate has been associated with seizures, even in patients without prior seizure history. Taper when discontinuing higher doses.
- **Kidney stones (nephrolithiasis)**: Increased risk due to topiramate’s carbonic anhydrase inhibition (↓ urinary citrate, ↑ urine pH, possible ↑ urinary calcium/citrate ratio). Advise increased fluid intake and avoid concomitant drugs that inhibit carbonic anhydrase.
- **Oligohidrosis and hyperthermia**: Decreased sweating and elevated body temperature have been reported; more commonly reported in pediatric patients; monitor, especially in hot environments or when febrile.
- **Hypokalemia**: Concomitant use with non–potassium-sparing diuretics may potentiate hypokalemia; monitor potassium prior to and during therapy.
- **Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS) / Multiorgan hypersensitivity**: Serious systemic hypersensitivity reactions including DRESS, severe skin reactions (e.g., Stevens–Johnson syndrome/toxic epidermal necrolysis), anaphylaxis, and angioedema have been reported. Discontinue if alternative etiologies cannot be established.
- **Allergic reactions to dyes**: Hypersensitivity to FD&C Yellow No. 5 (tartrazine) or other inactive ingredients may occur.

### Monitoring requirements
- Pregnancy testing: negative pregnancy test prior to initiation and monthly during therapy in patients who can become pregnant.
- Laboratory tests prior to initiation and periodically during therapy:
  - Serum bicarbonate (electrolytes) before starting and periodically during treatment.
  - Serum creatinine before starting and periodically during treatment.
  - Serum potassium before starting and periodically during treatment if on diuretics or at risk of hypokalemia.
  - Serum glucose prior to starting and during treatment in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus receiving antidiabetic medications.
- Monitor:
  - Mental health status for depression, suicidal ideation, or changes in mood/behavior.
  - Visual symptoms, visual acuity, and visual fields.
  - Growth (height velocity) in pediatric patients.
  - Signs of metabolic acidosis, kidney stones, oligohidrosis/hyperthermia, and severe allergic reactions.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Avoid use in pregnancy. Counsel on effective contraception and monthly pregnancy testing for patients of reproductive potential.
- Avoid concomitant use with other carbonic anhydrase inhibitors unless medically necessary.
- Use caution with concomitant CNS depressants and with alcohol due to potential additive impairment.
- Avoid abrupt discontinuation of high doses; taper per recommendations.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Oral contraceptives:
  - Phentermine + topiramate may alter exposure to estrogen and progestin components of combined oral contraceptives, which can cause irregular bleeding (spotting), though not shown to increase pregnancy rates. Patients should be advised not to discontinue their oral contraceptive if spotting occurs.
- Interactions affecting renal handling or electrolytes:
  - Concomitant use with non–potassium-sparing diuretics may potentiate hypokalemia. Monitor potassium.
- Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors:
  - Concomitant use with other carbonic anhydrase inhibitors (e.g., acetazolamide, zonisamide) may increase the severity of metabolic acidosis and the risk of kidney stones. Avoid if possible; if unavoidable, monitor bicarbonate and urinary parameters.
- Monoamine oxidase inhibitors:
  - Contraindicated within 14 days before or after MAOI therapy due to potential interactions.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- CNS depressants and alcohol:
  - Concomitant use may potentiate CNS depression (dizziness, somnolence, cognitive impairment). Counsel patients to avoid excessive alcohol and exercise caution with other CNS depressants.
- Antidiabetic medications:
  - Patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus on antidiabetic drugs should have glucose monitored prior to and during treatment; dosing adjustments of antidiabetic medications may be necessary based on clinical status (specific pharmacokinetic interactions not detailed).

### Clinically significant interactions and management notes
- Oral contraceptives: counsel on possibility of breakthrough bleeding; continue contraception.
- Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors: avoid concomitant use; if necessary, monitor closely for metabolic acidosis and kidney stones.
- Non–potassium-sparing diuretics: monitor potassium; treat or adjust diuretic therapy as needed.
- CNS depressants/alcohol: advise avoidance of excessive alcohol and caution with other CNS depressants.
- MAOIs: do not initiate within 14 days of MAOI therapy and avoid starting MAOI within 14 days of stopping phentermine + topiramate.

- Other specific pharmacokinetic parameters (e.g., CYP-mediated interactions, enzyme induction/inhibition by phentermine or topiramate) are not specified in the available source.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- **Contraindicated in pregnancy.** Topiramate component is associated with an increased risk of major congenital malformations, including oral clefts (cleft lip and/or cleft palate), and with small for gestational age infants in epidemiologic and registry data. Animal studies showed structural malformations including craniofacial defects and reduced fetal weight.
- Pregnancy testing: obtain a negative pregnancy test prior to initiation and monthly during therapy for patients who can become pregnant.
- Contraception: advise use of effective contraception during therapy.
- Regulatory/distribution restriction for teratogenic risk: [US] product distribution is limited under a REMS/limited program (see Section 6).

- Pregnancy category/classification by jurisdiction (e.g., letter category or Australian pregnancy categories): Not specified in available sources.

### Breastfeeding
- Breastfeeding is not recommended while receiving phentermine + topiramate (per available source statements).

### Fertility
- Data on effects on human fertility are not specified in available sources.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Approved for use in patients aged 12 years and older (per available source).
- Pediatric dosing follows the same titration and evaluation algorithm as adults, with assessment based on BMI reduction.
- Monitor height velocity: documented reduction in height velocity (~−1.3 to −1.4 cm/year vs placebo in a 56-week pediatric study); consider dose reduction or discontinuation if growth is inadequate.
- Monitor for oligohidrosis, hyperthermia, kidney stones, and cognitive/psychiatric adverse events which have been reported in pediatric populations.

### Elderly use
- Specific dosing recommendations or established differences in safety/efficacy in geriatric patients are not specified in the available source.

### Renal impairment
- See Section 4 (Dosage and Administration) for dosing by renal function:
  - Mild: no change.
  - Moderate to severe: maximum recommended dose 7.5 mg / 46 mg once daily.
  - Avoid in end-stage renal disease on dialysis.

### Hepatic impairment
- See Section 4 (Dosage and Administration) for hepatic dosing recommendations:
  - Mild: no change.
  - Moderate: maximum recommended dose 7.5 mg / 46 mg once daily.
  - Avoid in severe hepatic impairment.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Phentermine: sympathomimetic amine anorectic (appetite suppressant).
- Topiramate: antiepileptic agent with multiple pharmacologic actions; in clinical context it also inhibits carbonic anhydrase activity which is implicated in some adverse effects (e.g., metabolic acidosis, nephrolithiasis). The exact mechanism by which the combination induces weight loss is not fully detailed in the available source.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Detailed pharmacokinetic parameters (absorption, distribution, metabolism, elimination) for phentermine + topiramate are not specified in the available source.
- Clinical pharmacology observations:
  - Topiramate component: carbonic anhydrase inhibitory activity is clinically relevant (metabolic acidosis, changes in urinary citrate/pH, kidney stone risk).
  - Changes in serum creatinine and reductions in estimated GFR have been observed during treatment (clinical renal effects).

### Pharmacodynamics
- Specific pharmacodynamic data (e.g., receptor affinities, dose–response curves) are not specified in the available source beyond class-level activity and clinical effect profiles (weight loss, CNS effects, carbonic anhydrase inhibition).

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Pivotal efficacy evaluation and stopping criteria implemented in clinical trial programs informed the recommended evaluation timepoints:
  - Escalation criteria: after 12 weeks on 7.5 mg / 46 mg, adults not achieving ≥3% weight loss (pediatrics: ≥3% BMI reduction) should escalate dose; after 12 weeks on the highest dose (15 mg / 92 mg), adults not achieving ≥5% weight loss (pediatrics: ≥5% BMI reduction) should discontinue.
- Pediatric data:
  - A 56-week pediatric study reported reduced height velocity in treated adolescents (mean decrease in height velocity ~1.3–1.4 cm/year versus placebo).
- Suicidality data:
  - Pooled analyses of 199 placebo-controlled clinical studies of 11 AEDs (median treatment 12 weeks) showed an increased risk of suicidal thinking/behavior with AEDs (adjusted relative risk ~1.8; estimated incidence 0.43% in AED-treated vs 0.24% in placebo). In pediatric QSYMIA clinical trial data cited, 1 of 167 QSYMIA-treated pediatric patients reported suicidal ideation/behavior requiring hospitalization; no placebo patients did.
- Trial design details, primary efficacy endpoints, and full numerical efficacy results are not fully specified in the available source text. For comprehensive trial data, consult the full clinical study reports or full prescribing information.
