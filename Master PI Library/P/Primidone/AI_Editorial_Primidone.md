# Editorial Product Information: Primidone

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not intended to replace the official PI used for prescribing in specific jurisdictions. Clinicians must refer to the current local PI for legal and prescribing compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Primidone (an anticonvulsant of the pyrimidine derivative class).
- **Brand Name(s):** Mysoline [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 250 mg tablets [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Link Medical Products Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- Management of grand mal epilepsy.
- Management of psychomotor (temporal lobe) epilepsy.
- Management of focal or Jacksonian seizures [Australian].
- Management of myoclonic jerks [Australian].
- Management of akinetic attacks [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Children >9 years)
- **Initiation:** Begin with 125 mg (1/2 tablet) once daily, administered late in the evening.
- **Titration:** Increase the daily dose by 125 mg every three days until the patient is receiving 500 mg/day (2 tablets). Subsequently, increase by 250 mg (1 tablet) every three days until seizure control is achieved or maximum tolerance is reached.
- **Maintenance:** Usually 750 mg to 1,500 mg per day (3–6 tablets).
- **Maximum Dose:** 1,500 mg/day [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing (Children <9 years)
- **Titration:** Follow the same initial schedule as adults. After reaching 500 mg/day, increases should be limited to 125 mg (1/2 tablet) every three days.
- **Maintenance (by age):**
    - Under 2 years: 250–500 mg/day.
    - 2–5 years: 500–750 mg/day.
    - 6–9 years: 750–1,000 mg/day.
- **Maximum Paediatric Dose:** 1,000 mg/day [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- The total daily dose is typically divided into two equal doses (morning and evening).
- For patients with nocturnal seizures, the majority or entirety of the daily dose may be given in the evening.
- For seizures associated with specific events (e.g., menstruation), a slight temporary increase in dose may be beneficial.

### Switching Therapy
- When adding primidone to existing therapy, introduce it gradually. Once a therapeutic effect is noted and primidone has reached at least half of the estimated requirement, the previous anticonvulsant can be gradually withdrawn over two weeks.
- **Note:** If the previous treatment was primarily phenobarbital, withdrawal should occur earlier to prevent excessive drowsiness during titration.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity or allergic reaction to primidone or any of its excipients.
- Acute intermittent porphyria [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Suicidal Behaviour and Ideation:** Antiepileptic drugs (AEDs) increase the risk of suicidal thoughts or behaviour. Patients should be monitored for the emergence or worsening of depression, suicidal ideation, or unusual changes in mood. This risk may be observed as early as one week after starting treatment.
- **Severe Skin Reactions:** Life-threatening reactions, including **Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS)** and **Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN)**, have been reported. The highest risk occurs within the first weeks of treatment. Primidone must be permanently discontinued if these signs (e.g., progressive rash with blisters) appear.
- **Abrupt Withdrawal:** Sudden cessation of treatment may precipitate status epilepticus or convulsive fits, particularly in patients with alcoholism. Withdrawal must be gradual.
- **Bone Health:** Long-term use may reduce bone mineral density, predisposing patients to osteopenia and osteoporosis. Monitoring bone density and Vitamin D supplementation should be considered.
- **Respiratory and Hepatic Function:** Use with caution in patients with impaired respiratory function. While not directly causative, there is a theoretical risk of hepatic damage due to liver enzyme induction.
- **Aggravated Convulsions:** In some cases, AEDs may cause an increase in seizure frequency or the onset of new seizure types, which may indicate toxicity, overdose, or inappropriate treatment choice.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally most frequent in the early stages of treatment.

- **Very Common (>10%):** Drowsiness, listlessness.
- **Common/Frequency Not Specified:**
    - **Neurological:** Ataxia, visual disturbances, nystagmus, dizziness, headache.
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting.
    - **Dermatological:** Skin eruptions, exfoliative dermatitis.
- **Rare (<0.1%):**
    - **Haematological:** Megaloblastic anaemia (may respond to folic acid/B12), leucopenia, thrombocytopenia.
    - **Systemic:** Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), arthralgia.
    - **Psychiatric:** Personality changes, psychotic reactions, apathy.
    - **Other:** Dupuytren’s contracture, lymphadenopathy.
- **Serious Reactions:** SJS, TEN, hypersensitivity syndrome.

## 7. Drug Interactions
Primidone and its metabolite, phenobarbital, are potent inducers of hepatic enzymes (CYP450).

### Concomitant Use Not Recommended
- **CNS Depressants:** Alcohol and barbiturates (enhanced sedative effects).
- **Reduced Efficacy of Co-administered Drugs:**
    - **Antivirals:** Cobicistat, daclatasvir, sofosbuvir, various protease inhibitors.
    - **Anticoagulants:** Apixaban, dabigatran, rivaroxaban.
    - **Antineoplastics:** Tyrosine kinase inhibitors, ifosfamide (increased neurotoxicity).
    - **Antifungals:** Voriconazole, itraconazole.
    - **Other:** St John’s wort (may decrease primidone levels).

### Precaution - Dose Adjustment May Be Required
- **Antiepileptics:** Phenytoin (increased phenobarbital levels/toxicity), carbamazepine, valproic acid, lamotrigine.
- **Hormonal Contraceptives:** Significant risk of contraceptive failure; breakthrough bleeding reported. Use of highly effective non-hormonal methods (e.g., IUD) or dual barrier methods is advised.
- **Anticoagulants:** Warfarin (requires INR monitoring).
- **Corticosteroids:** Reduced steroid efficacy.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy Category:** **Category D** [Australian].
- **Fetal Risk:** Primidone crosses the placenta and is metabolised to phenobarbital. Exposure is associated with major and minor congenital malformations (6–7% frequency), including craniofacial and cardiac defects, digital abnormalities, and cleft lip/palate.
- **Neonatal Risk:** Risk of infants born small for gestational age, neonatal haemorrhage, and addiction/withdrawal symptoms. **Vitamin K1 prophylaxis** for the mother before delivery and for the neonate is recommended [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Primidone enters breast milk. The infant must be monitored closely for sedation.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use with caution; dose adjustments required as specified in Section 3.
- **Elderly/Debilitated:** Primidone should be administered with caution; reduced dosages may be required.
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution. Elimination half-life increases significantly in uraemic patients (to approx. 13.9 hours), though it is reduced during dialysis [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution; reduced dosages may be required due to enzyme induction risks.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Primidone reduces CNS sensitivity to seizure-inducing stimuli. Its anticonvulsant activity is attributed to both the parent drug and its active metabolites: **phenobarbital (PB)** and **phenylethylmalonamide (PEMA)**.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid but variable absorption. Peak plasma levels occur approx. 3 hours post-500 mg dose.
    - **Metabolism:** Converted to PEMA (half-life 22–24 hours) and PB (slow conversion).
    - **Distribution:** Readily crosses the blood-brain barrier and placenta. Low plasma protein binding for primidone and PEMA.
    - **Excretion:** Predominantly renal; approx. 92% of a dose is recovered in urine as parent drug or metabolites.

- Protect from light and moisture.
