# Editorial Product Information: Phenelzine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of phenelzine for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s current PI for legal and prescribing compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Phenelzine sulfate (a hydrazine derivative).
- **Chemical Class:** Monoamine oxidase inhibitor (MAOI).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Nardil** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 15 mg film-coated biconvex tablets (equivalent to 15 mg phenelzine base) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Neon Healthcare Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Major Depression:** Specifically indicated for patients who have failed to respond to first-line antidepressant therapies [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:**
    - **Initial Dose:** 15 mg three times daily [Australian].
    - **Early Phase Treatment:** Dosage should be rapidly increased to at least 60 mg/day, as tolerated. Some patients may require up to 90 mg/day to achieve sufficient monoamine oxidase inhibition. Note that clinical response at 60 mg may not be evident for at least four weeks [Australian].
    - **Maintenance Dose:** Following maximum clinical benefit, the dose should be reduced slowly over several weeks. Maintenance levels may be as low as 15 mg daily or every second day [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets are administered orally.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to phenelzine or any tablet excipients [Australian].
- Phaeochromocytoma [Australian].
- Cerebrovascular disease [Australian].
- Congestive heart failure [Australian].
- History of liver disease or abnormal liver function tests [Australian].
- **Concurrent Drug Therapy:**
    - Guanethidine [Australian].
    - Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors (SSRIs) [Australian].
    - Serotonin-Noradrenaline Reuptake Inhibitors (SNRIs) [Australian].
    - Tricyclic Antidepressants (TCAs) [Australian].
    - Other MAO inhibitors or dibenzazepine derivatives [Australian].
    - Dextromethorphan [Australian].
- **Surgical Contraindications:** Not for use during elective surgery requiring general anaesthesia or local anaesthesia containing sympathomimetic vasoconstrictors. Phenelzine must be discontinued at least 10 days prior to elective surgery [Australian].
- **Clinical Phase:** Not indicated for use during the manic phase of bipolar disorder [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Clinical Worsening and Suicidality:** Antidepressants may increase the risk of suicidal ideation and behaviour, particularly in young adults (aged 18–24 years) during the first two months of therapy. Close monitoring is required, especially during dose titrations [Australian].
- **Hypertensive Crisis:** The most serious reaction involves significant blood pressure elevation. Symptoms include occipital headache, neck stiffness, palpitations, and nausea. Therapy must be discontinued immediately if palpitations or frequent headaches occur [Australian].
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Risk is elevated when combined with other serotonergic agents. Characterised by neuromuscular excitation, altered mental status, and autonomic dysfunction [Australian].
- **Postural Hypotension:** Patients must be monitored for blood pressure changes; hypotensive effects can occur in normotensive, hypertensive, and hypotensive patients [Australian].
- **Bipolar Disorder:** Screening is essential prior to treatment, as phenelzine may induce a manic or mixed episode in patients at risk for bipolar disorder [Australian].
- **Co-morbid Conditions:**
    - **Epilepsy:** Use with caution due to variable effects on the convulsive threshold [Australian].
    - **Diabetes:** MAOIs may affect glucose metabolism or potentiate hypoglycaemic agents [Australian].
    - **Schizophrenia:** May cause excessive stimulation [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Frequent blood pressure monitoring is required to detect pressor responses [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by system organ class. Frequencies are defined as common or less common based on available clinical reporting [Australian].

| System Organ Class | Common (>10% or reported as "common") | Less Common / Rare |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nervous System** | Dizziness, headache, drowsiness, insomnia, fatigue, tremors, twitching, hyperreflexia. | Jitteriness, paraesthesias, nystagmus, ataxia, convulsions, neuroleptic malignant syndrome. |
| **Psychiatric** | Sleep disturbances. | Hypomania, hallucinations, confusion, euphoria, manic reaction. |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Constipation, dry mouth, elevated transaminases. | Fatal progressive necrotising hepatocellular damage (rare), reversible jaundice. |
| **Cardiovascular** | Postural hypotension, oedema. | Arrhythmias, hypertensive crisis. |
| **Metabolic** | Weight gain. | Hypernatraemia, SIADH, metabolic acidosis. |
| **Genitourinary** | Anorgasmia, ejaculatory disturbances. | Urinary retention. |
| **Other** | Blurred vision. | Glaucoma, blood dyscrasias, pruritus, skin rash, lupus-like illness. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Hypertensive crisis (potentially fatal), intracranial bleeding, and serotonin syndrome [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Sympathomimetics:** (e.g., amphetamines, cocaine, adrenaline, dopamine, ephedrine) Risk of hypertensive crisis.
- **Serotonergic Agents:** (SSRIs, SNRIs, TCAs, tryptophan) Risk of serotonin syndrome and serious neurological symptoms. A 5-week washout is required after fluoxetine; 10 days for other SSRIs/antidepressants before starting phenelzine [Australian].
- **Narcotics:** Pethidine is strictly contraindicated (risk of excitation, seizures, circulatory collapse, and death). Phenelzine should not be used with propoxyphene [Australian].
- **Antihypertensives:** Potentiation of hypotensive effects, especially with thiazide diuretics and beta-blockers [Australian].
- **CNS Depressants:** Potentiates alcohol and barbiturates (reduce barbiturate dose) [Australian].
- **Tyramine-Rich Foods:** Ingestion of aged, fermented, or pickled foods (e.g., aged cheeses, beer, wine, pickled herring, yeast extracts) can trigger a hypertensive crisis. These restrictions must be maintained for 14 days after discontinuing phenelzine [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian: Category B3]. Safe use has not been established. Animal studies (mice) showed suppressed implantation and increased risk of congenital abnormalities with antidepressants. Use only if benefits clearly justify the risk to the foetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if phenelzine is excreted in human milk. Due to the potential for serious reactions in infants, a decision should be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug [Australian].
- **Fertility:** In mouse models, high-dose phenelzine (25 mg/kg) partially suppressed pregnancy implantation [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients <18 years are not established; phenelzine should not be used in this age group [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Use with great caution. The elderly are at higher risk for postural hypotension and drug interactions due to polypharmacy [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with a history of liver disease or abnormal liver function tests [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Irreversible inhibition of monoamine oxidase (MAO), a flavin-containing enzyme. This increases the concentration of epinephrine, norepinephrine, and serotonin in the CNS [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Maximal MAO inhibition occurs within 5–10 days; however, clinical antidepressant effects usually take 2–3 weeks. Favourable response is associated with 85% inhibition of platelet MAO [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed from the GI tract.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via acetylation. Patients may be "slow" or "rapid" acetylators, though this has not been consistently linked to adverse effect variance [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Half-life is approximately 1.2 hours. Metabolites (phenylacetic acid and parahydroxyphenyl acetic acid) are excreted in the urine (79% within 96 hours) [Australian].
