# Editorial Product Information: Pimozide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official PI. Clinicians must consult the approved PI in their specific jurisdiction before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pimozide (a diphenyl-butylpiperidine derivative).
- **Brand Names:** Pimozide Tablets, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** N05AD01 (Not explicitly stated in source, but standard classification).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Oral tablets: 1 mg (round, white/off-white) and 2 mg (oval, white/off-white) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** ANI Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (Distributed by); Esjay Pharma Private Limited (Manufactured by) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Tourette’s Disorder:** Indicated for the suppression of motor and phonic tics in patients who have failed to respond satisfactorily to standard treatment (e.g., haloperidol) [US].
- **Usage Restrictions:** 
    - Not intended as a first-line treatment.
    - Not for tics that are merely annoying or cosmetically troublesome.
    - Reserved for patients whose development or daily function is severely compromised.
    - Contraindicated for simple tics or tics not associated with Tourette’s Disorder [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Principles
Dosing requires slow titration to balance tic suppression against adverse effects. A gradual withdrawal is recommended when discontinuing therapy [US].

### Adult Dosing
- **Initial Dose:** 1 to 2 mg per day in divided doses.
- **Titration:** Increase every other day.
- **Maintenance:** Most patients are maintained at <0.2 mg/kg/day or 10 mg/day, whichever is less.
- **Maximum Dose:** Doses exceeding 10 mg/day or 0.2 mg/kg/day are not recommended [US].

### Paediatric Dosing (Ages 2–12)
- **Initial Dose:** 0.05 mg/kg, preferably at bedtime.
- **Titration:** Increase every third day.
- **Maximum Dose:** 0.2 mg/kg/day, not to exceed 10 mg/day [US].

### Pharmacogenomic-Based Dosing
- **CYP2D6 Genotyping Requirement:** 
    - **Adults:** Perform genotyping if doses exceed 4 mg/day.
    - **Children:** Perform genotyping if doses exceed 0.05 mg/kg/day.
- **Poor CYP2D6 Metabolizers:** 
    - **Adults:** Do not exceed 4 mg/day; do not increase doses earlier than every 14 days.
    - **Children:** Do not exceed 0.05 mg/kg/day; do not increase doses earlier than every 14 days [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Tics:** Treatment of simple tics or non-Tourette’s tics [US].
- **Cardiac Conditions:** 
    - Congenital long QT syndrome or history of cardiac arrhythmias.
    - Concomitant use of drugs that prolong the QT interval.
    - Known hypokalaemia or hypomagnesaemia [US].
- **Metabolic/Drug Interactions:**
    - Concomitant use of strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., clarithromycin, erythromycin, itraconazole, ketoconazole, ritonavir, nefazodone).
    - Concomitant use of strong CYP2D6 inhibitors (e.g., paroxetine).
    - Concomitant use of sertraline, citalopram, or escitalopram [US].
- **CNS Status:** Severe toxic CNS depression or comatose states.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to pimozide.
- **Tics Inducing Agents:** Patients taking stimulants (e.g., pemoline, methylphenidate, amphetamines) until they are withdrawn to determine if the stimulants are the cause of tics [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **QT Prolongation and Sudden Death:** Sudden deaths have occurred at doses above 20 mg/day. ECGs must be performed at baseline and periodically during titration [US]. 
    - **Dose adjustment criteria:** Stop dose increases if the QTc interval exceeds 0.47 seconds (children), 0.52 seconds (adults), or increases by more than 25% over baseline [US].
- **Tardive Dyskinesia:** Potentially irreversible, involuntary movements may develop. Risk increases with duration of treatment and cumulative dose, though it can occur at low doses. 
- **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** Potentially fatal complex (hyperpyrexia, muscle rigidity, altered mental status, autonomic instability). Discontinue pimozide immediately if NMS is suspected [US].
- **Blood Dyscrasias:** Leukopenia, neutropenia, and agranulocytosis reported. Discontinue if absolute neutrophil count <1000/mm³ [US].
- **Seizures:** May lower the convulsive threshold; use with caution in patients with a history of seizures [US].
- **Anticholinergic Effects:** Use with caution in conditions aggravated by anticholinergic activity.
- **Tumorigenic Potential:** Increased pituitary and mammary tumors observed in mice; clinical significance in humans is unknown but should be considered for long-term use in young patients [US].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Extrapyramidal reactions (Parkinson-like symptoms, dystonia, oculogyric crises).
- Tardive Dyskinesia.
- Sudden death/ventricular arrhythmias.
- Hemolytic anemia [Postmarketing].
- Hyponatremia [Postmarketing].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **QT-Prolonging Agents:** Contraindicated (e.g., quinidine, sotalol, thioridazine, chlorpromazine, ziprasidone, etc.) [US].
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Contraindicated. Includes macrolides, azole antifungals, and protease inhibitors. Grapefruit juice must be avoided [US].
- **CYP2D6 Inhibitors:** Contraindicated (e.g., paroxetine). Increases pimozide exposure significantly [US].
- **SSRIs:** Concomitant use with citalopram, escitalopram, or sertraline is contraindicated [US].
- **CNS Depressants:** Pimozide may potentiate the effects of analgesics, sedatives, anxiolytics, and alcohol [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not explicitly lettered, but descriptive]. Use only if the potential benefit outweighs the risk to the fetus. 
    - **Animal data:** Decreased pregnancies and retarded foetal development in rats at 8x human dose. 
    - **Neonatal risk:** Risk of extrapyramidal and/or withdrawal symptoms (agitation, tremor, respiratory distress) in neonates exposed during the third trimester [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if pimozide is excreted in human milk. Due to potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants, a decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed prolonged oestrus cycles [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Limited data for patients under 12. A 24-week study in children aged 2–12 showed a similar safety profile to adults [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Higher risk of tardive dyskinesia, especially in women. Dose selection should be cautious [US].
- **Hepatic/Renal Impairment:** Administer with caution as the drug is metabolised by the liver and excreted by the kidneys [US].
- **Pharmacogenomics:** 5–10% of the population are poor CYP2D6 metabolizers and will have higher drug concentrations and longer half-lives [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Central nervous system dopamine receptor blockade. The suppression of tics in Tourette’s is specifically attributed to dopaminergic blockade [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** >50% absorbed; significant first-pass metabolism.
    - **Peak Plasma Time:** 6 to 8 hours (range 4–12 hours).
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised via N-dealkylation in the liver, primarily by CYP3A4, and to a lesser extent by CYP2D6 and CYP1A2.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal.
    - **Half-life:** Approximately 55 hours in patients with schizophrenia [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Tourette’s Disorder:** Approval was based on two controlled clinical investigations in patients aged 8 to 53 years. 
- **Paediatric Safety:** A 24-week open-label study in 36 children (ages 2–12) demonstrated a safety profile comparable to older populations [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container with a child-resistant closure [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only. Poison schedule not specified in source [US].
