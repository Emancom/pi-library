# Editorial Product Information: Peginterferon beta-1a

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesizes Product Information (PI) from the Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA - Australia) and the Food and Drug Administration (FDA - United States).
- Purpose: To provide a harmonised clinical overview for healthcare professionals regarding the use of **peginterferon beta-1a**.
- Disclaimer: This document is an editorial synthesis for informational purposes and does not replace the official PI used for prescribing in specific jurisdictions.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Peginterferon beta-1a (rch). It is a conjugate of recombinant interferon beta-1a and a single, linear 20 kDa methoxy poly(ethylene glycol) molecule.
- **Brand Name:** Plegridy
- **Chemical Class:** Interferon; Immunomodulator.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
  - Single-dose pre-filled pen or syringe: 63 mcg/0.5 mL, 94 mcg/0.5 mL, and 125 mcg/0.5 mL.
- **Routes of Administration:** 
  - Subcutaneous (SC) injection.
  - Intramuscular (IM) injection [FDA only].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Biogen Australia Pty Ltd [TGA]; Biogen Inc. [FDA].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Relapsing forms of Multiple Sclerosis (MS):** Approved in both jurisdictions to reduce the frequency of clinical relapses and help delay the accumulation of physical disability.
- **Expanded Indication [FDA only]:** Specifically includes Clinically Isolated Syndrome (CIS), Relapsing-Remitting MS (RRMS), and active Secondary Progressive MS (SPMS) in adults.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Maintenance Dosage:** 125 micrograms every 14 days (2 weeks).
- **Administration:**
  - **Subcutaneous:** Injection sites include the abdomen, back of the upper arm, or thigh. 
  - **Intramuscular [FDA only]:** Injection sites are the left and right thighs.
- **Treatment Initiation/Titration:**
  A titration schedule is recommended to improve tolerability and reduce the incidence of flu-like symptoms.
  - **Dose 1 (Day 1):** 63 micrograms.
  - **Dose 2 (Day 15):** 94 micrograms.
  - **Dose 3 (Day 29 onwards):** 125 micrograms (full dose).
- **Missed Dose:** Administer as soon as possible.
  - If ≥7 days until next dose: Administer immediately and resume original schedule.
  - If <7 days until next dose: Administer immediately and start a new 2-week schedule. Do not administer two doses within 7 days of each other [TGA].
- **Pre-medication:** Use of analgesics and/or antipyretics on treatment days may ameliorate flu-like symptoms.

## 4. Contraindications
- History of hypersensitivity to natural or recombinant interferon beta, peginterferon, or any excipients.
- **Specific Contraindications [TGA only]:**
  - Initiation of treatment during pregnancy.
  - Patients with current severe depression and/or suicidal ideation.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatic Injury:** Elevations in serum transaminases and rare cases of severe hepatic failure have occurred. Monitor liver function tests (LFTs) at baseline and periodically. 
- **Depression and Suicidal Ideation:** Use with caution in patients with previous depressive disorders. Advise patients to report any new symptoms of depression immediately.
- **Anaphylaxis/Hypersensitivity:** Serious allergic reactions, including angioedema, have been reported. Discontinue if a serious reaction occurs.
- **Injection Site Reactions:** Common; can include erythema, pain, and pruritus. Rare cases of injection site necrosis have been reported. Rotate injection sites with every dose.
- **Decreased Peripheral Blood Counts:** Cytopenias (neutropenia, thrombocytopenia) and rare pancytopenia have been reported. Monitor complete blood counts (CBC).
- **Thrombotic Microangiopathy (TMA):** Cases of thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP) or haemolytic uraemic syndrome (HUS), some fatal, have been reported. Discontinue therapy if clinical or laboratory findings of TMA occur.
- **Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension (PAH):** Reported with interferon beta products. Symptoms (dyspnoea, fatigue) may occur years after starting treatment. If PAH is diagnosed, discontinue treatment.
- **Seizures:** Exercise caution in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders.
- **Congestive Heart Failure (CHF):** Monitor patients with significant cardiac disease for worsening symptoms, as flu-like symptoms may stress the cardiac system.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are most frequent during treatment initiation and generally decrease over time.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (≥10%)** | Injection site erythema (62%), Influenza-like illness (47%), Pyrexia (45%), Headache (44%), Myalgia (19%), Chills (17%), Injection site pain (15%), Asthenia (13%), Injection site pruritus (13%), Arthralgia (11%). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Nausea, Vomiting, Hyperthermia, Pain, Injection site oedema/warmth/haematoma, Pruritus, Increased LFTs (ALT, AST, GGT). |
| **Rare (<0.1%) / Post-marketing** | Injection site necrosis, Anaphylaxis, Thrombotic microangiopathy, Nephrotic syndrome, Pulmonary arterial hypertension, Severe neutropenia/thrombocytopenia. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Cytochrome P450:** Interferons may reduce the activity of hepatic P450-dependent enzymes. Exercise caution when co-administering drugs with a narrow therapeutic index largely dependent on the hepatic P450 system (e.g., some antiepileptics and antidepressants).
- **Corticosteroids:** Clinical trial patients received corticosteroids for relapses without identified interactions.
- No formal drug interaction studies have been conducted for **peginterferon beta-1a**.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **[TGA]: Category D.** Contraindicated for treatment initiation. Advise women of childbearing potential to use effective contraception.
  - **[FDA]:** Epidemiological data do not suggest a clear risk of major congenital malformations, though animal data in rhesus monkeys showed increased abortifacient potential at high doses.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if the drug is excreted in human milk. A decision must be made to either discontinue breastfeeding or the drug, considering the mother’s clinical need.
- **Fertility:** In animal studies, high-dose peginterferon was associated with menstrual irregularities and anovulation in female monkeys, which were reversible upon discontinuation.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients <18 years have not been established.
- **Geriatric Use:** Data are limited for patients >65 years. Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers to determine if they respond differently than younger patients.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for mild, moderate, or severe impairment. However, exposure (AUC) increases with severity (up to 53% increase in severe impairment [TGA]). Monitor for adverse reactions in severe renal impairment [FDA]. Hemodialysis removes approximately 24% of the dose.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not studied in patients with hepatic insufficiency. Close monitoring is advised in cases of severe hepatic impairment.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Binds to the Type I interferon receptor on cell surfaces, activating a cascade of intracellular events and regulating gene expression. It modulates immune responses (up-regulating anti-inflammatory cytokines) and inhibits the migration of activated T cells across the blood-brain barrier.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Pharmacodynamic responses (induction of neopterin and 2’,5’-OAS) are sustained longer compared to non-pegylated interferon beta-1a.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Peak concentration (Cmax) reached 1 to 1.5 days post-dose (SC).
  - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is approximately 481 L.
  - **Elimination:** Primarily renal. The half-life (t½) is approximately 78 hours in MS patients, which is significantly longer than non-pegylated forms.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ADVANCE Study:** A pivotal 2-year randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study (Year 1) followed by an active treatment phase (Year 2).
- **Primary Outcome:** 125 mcg every 14 days reduced the Annualized Relapse Rate (ARR) by 36% (p=0.0007) compared to placebo at one year.
- **Secondary Outcomes:** Significant reductions were observed in the proportion of patients relapsing (39%), sustained disability progression at 12 weeks (38%), and various MRI metrics including new T2 hyperintense lesions (67% reduction).
- **ATTAIN Extension:** Long-term efficacy was maintained for up to 4 years of treatment.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze.
- **Room Temperature Stability:** May be stored between 2°C to 25°C for a total of up to 30 days if protected from light. Once removed from the refrigerator, the product can be returned to it if the total cumulative time out of refrigeration does not exceed 30 days.
- **Handling:** Protect from light in the original carton. Allow to reach room temperature (approx. 30 minutes) before injection.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia [TGA]:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **United States [FDA]:** Rx Only.
