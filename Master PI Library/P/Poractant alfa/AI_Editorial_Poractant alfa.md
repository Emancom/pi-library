# Editorial Product Information: Poractant alfa

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a single, clinically focused reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI. Clinicians must consult the approved PI in their specific region before prescribing or administering this medication.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Poractant alfa (a natural surfactant derived from porcine lungs).
- **Chemical Class:** Lung surfactant; mixture of phospholipids (99%) and hydrophobic low molecular weight proteins (SP-B and SP-C, ~1%).
- **Brand Names:** **CUROSURF** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** R07AA02.
- **Dosage Form and Strengths:** 
    - Suspension for intratracheal administration.
    - 120 mg poractant alfa in 1.5 mL vials (80 mg/mL).
    - 240 mg poractant alfa in 3 mL vials (80 mg/mL).
- **Route of Administration:** Intratracheal only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Chiesi Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Respiratory Distress Syndrome (RDS):** Treatment (rescue) of RDS in premature infants.
- **Prophylaxis:** Prophylactic use in infants at risk of developing RDS.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Neonatal)
**Poractant alfa** must be administered by clinicians experienced in neonatal intensive care and ventilation management. Infants should be stabilised (acidosis, hypotension, anaemia, and temperature) prior to dosing.

### Rescue Treatment
- **Initial Dose:** 2.5 mL/kg (200 mg/kg) birth weight, initiated as soon as RDS is diagnosed.
- **Repeat Doses:** Up to two doses of 1.25 mL/kg (100 mg/kg) at approximately 12-hour intervals for infants remaining intubated with deteriorating respiratory status.
- **Maximum Total Dose:** 5 mL/kg (400 mg/kg).

### Prophylaxis
- **Initial Dose:** 100–200 mg/kg (1.25–2.5 mL/kg) as soon as possible after birth (ideally within 15 minutes).
- **Subsequent Doses:** 100 mg/kg can be administered 6–12 hours after the first dose, and again 12 hours later if the infant remains ventilator-dependent with signs of RDS.
- **Maximum Total Dose:** 300–400 mg/kg.

### Administration Instructions
1. **Preparation:** Slowly warm the vial to room temperature. Gently turn the vial upside down (do not shake) to ensure a uniform white-to-creamy-white suspension.
2. **Standard Instillation:** Administered via a 5 French end-hole catheter. The infant is briefly disconnected from the ventilator, and the dose is instilled in aliquots (often with the infant positioned on the left and then right side). Manual ventilation with 100% oxygen at 40-60 breaths/minute is performed for one minute between aliquots.
3. **Dual Lumen Tube:** Administered through the secondary lumen as a single dose over one minute without interrupting mechanical ventilation.
4. **LISA Technique:** Less Invasive Surfactant Administration using a thin catheter in spontaneously breathing infants on CPAP.
5. **Post-Administration:** Do not suction the airways for at least 1 hour (standard) to 6 hours (LISA/general caution) unless airway obstruction is suspected.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to the active substance (poractant alfa) or any of the excipients (sodium chloride, sodium bicarbonate).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
- **Rapid Physiological Changes:** Treatment can rapidly improve lung compliance and oxygenation. Ventilator settings (FiO2, inspiratory pressure) must be adjusted promptly to prevent hyperoxia or volutrauma.
- **Intracranial Haemorrhage:** Associated with early peaks in PaO2 and reductions in mean arterial blood pressure. Continuous monitoring of transcutaneous PaO2 or oxygen saturation is advised.
- **Airway Obstruction:** Administration may cause mucus plugging or reflux. If ventilation is impaired, stop administration and clear the endotracheal tube; replacement of the tube may be necessary.
- **Stabilisation:** Pre-treatment stabilisation of acidosis, hypotension, and hypoglycaemia is essential.
- **Infection Risk:** Infants should be monitored for signs of infection (pneumonia, sepsis) and treated with antibiotics immediately if suspected.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often transient or associated with the administration procedure or the underlying condition of prematurity.

| Adverse Effect | Frequency | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bradycardia** | Common | Usually transient during administration |
| **Hypotension** | Common | Related to physiological shifts |
| **Oxygen Desaturation**| Common | Frequent in LISA technique (up to 57%) |
| **ET Tube Blockage** | Rare | Due to mucus/surfactant plugging |
| **Pneumothorax** | Common | Complication of RDS/Ventilation |
| **Pulmonary Haemorrhage**| Uncommon | Associated with rapid lung changes |

- **LISA-specific effects:** Coughing (7.9%), froth at the mouth (21.8%), and sneezing (5%) were more frequent than in standard administration [Australian].

- **Paediatric Use:** Indicated exclusively for premature neonates. Prophylaxis is recommended for infants <27 weeks' gestation and considered for those 26–30 weeks if not treated with prenatal corticosteroids.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Poractant alfa** compensates for the deficiency of endogenous surfactant in premature infants. It reduces surface tension at the alveolar air-liquid interface, preventing atelectasis and improving gas exchange.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Treatment increases lung compliance, improves a/A PO2 and PaO2/FiO2 ratios, and reduces required inspiratory pressures.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** No human studies have characterised the absorption, metabolism, or excretion of **poractant alfa**. It is administered directly to the target organ (the lungs).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Rescue Studies:** Large trials (Study 1 and 2) in infants 700–2000g birth weight showed that **poractant alfa** significantly reduced 28-day mortality (from 51% in control to 31% in treated groups) and reduced the incidence of pneumothorax. Multiple dosing (Study 2) further reduced mortality compared to single dosing (13% vs 21%).
- **LISA Technique:** Trials in infants 23–27 weeks' gestation demonstrated that the LISA technique was non-inferior to standard intubation for survival without bronchopulmonary dysplasia (BPD) and significantly reduced the need for mechanical ventilation.
- **Prophylaxis Meta-analysis:** A meta-analysis of three trials (Studies 3, 4, 5) showed that prophylactic administration reduces the risk of severe RDS, overall mortality, and chronic lung disease.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate).
- **Handling:** Do not freeze. Protect from light and moisture. 
- **Thermal Stability:** Unopened vials that have been warmed to room temperature for use may be returned to the refrigerator **one time only** within 24 hours.
- **Shelf Life:** Consult the expiry date on the packaging. Single-use vials only; discard any unused portion.
