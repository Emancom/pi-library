# Editorial Product Information: Procainamide

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of procainamide hydrochloride, synthesised for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Procainamide hydrochloride (a Group 1A cardiac antiarrhythmic).
- **Brand Names:** Procainamide Hydrochloride Injection, USP (Hospira, Inc.) [US].
- **ATC Code:** C01BA02.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Injection: 100 mg/mL in a 10 mL multiple-dose vial [US].
- **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous (IV), Intramuscular (IM).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Hospira, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Ventricular Arrhythmias:** Indicated for the treatment of documented, life-threatening ventricular arrhythmias, such as sustained ventricular tachycardia [US].
- **Clinical Note:** Due to proarrhythmic potential, use in "lesser" arrhythmias is generally not recommended, and treatment of asymptomatic ventricular premature contractions should be avoided [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Intramuscular (IM):** 
    - Initial estimate: 50 mg/kg/day [US].
    - Administration: Divided into fractional doses (1/8 to 1/4 of total) every 3 to 6 hours until oral therapy is feasible [US].
    - Arrhythmias associated with surgery/anesthesia: 100 to 500 mg IM [US].
- **Intravenous (IV) Loading:** 
    - **Method A (Direct Injection):** Rate not to exceed 50 mg/minute. Doses of 100 mg may be administered every 5 minutes until the arrhythmia is suppressed or a maximum of 500 mg is reached. Wait 10 minutes before resuming [US].
    - **Method B (Infusion):** 1 g diluted to 50 mL (20 mg/mL) in 5% Dextrose Injection. Administer at 1 mL/minute (20 mg/min) for 25–30 minutes to deliver 500–600 mg [US].
    - **Maximum Loading Dose:** 1 g [US].
- **Intravenous (IV) Maintenance:**
    - Standard rate: 2 to 6 mg/minute [US].
    - Dilution: 1 g in 500 mL (2 mg/mL) or 1 g in 250 mL (4 mg/mL) of 5% Dextrose Injection [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Monitoring:** Initiation should occur in a hospital setting with continuous ECG and blood pressure monitoring [US].
- **Transition to Oral Therapy:** Allow 3 to 4 hours (one half-life) to elapse after the last IV dose before administering the first oral dose [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Complete Heart Block:** Due to the risk of suppressing nodal or ventricular pacemakers and causing asystole [US].
- **Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE):** An established diagnosis of SLE, as procainamide is highly likely to aggravate symptoms [US].
- **Torsades de Pointes:** Group 1A antiarrhythmics may aggravate this specific arrhythmia [US].
- **Idiosyncratic Hypersensitivity:** Known sensitivity to procaine or ester-type local anesthetics (though cross-sensitivity is rare, it must be considered); contraindication applies if the drug produces acute allergic dermatitis, asthma, or anaphylaxis [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
- **ANA and Lupus-like Syndrome:** Prolonged administration frequently leads to a positive anti-nuclear antibody (ANA) test, with or without a lupus erythematosus-like syndrome.
- **Mortality:** In the CAST trial, certain antiarrhythmics (flecainide, encainide) increased mortality in post-MI patients. Procainamide should be reserved for life-threatening arrhythmias.
- **Blood Dyscrasias:** Agranulocytosis, bone marrow depression, and thrombocytopenia (0.5% incidence) have been reported. Fatalities occur in ~20–25% of agranulocytosis cases.

### General Warnings
- **Digitalis Intoxication:** Use with extreme caution; procainamide may cause ventricular asystole or fibrillation if used to treat digitalis-induced arrhythmias [US].
- **First-Degree Heart Block:** Use caution and consider dosage reduction; evaluate benefit-risk if the block persists [US].
- **Congestive Heart Failure (CHF):** Myocardial depression may further reduce cardiac output in patients with damaged hearts [US].
- **Myasthenia Gravis:** Procainamide may worsen symptoms due to procaine-like effects on acetylcholine release [US].
- **Sulfite Sensitivity:** The injection contains sodium metabisulfite, which may cause anaphylaxis or asthma in susceptible individuals [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Haematology:** CBC (including white cell, differential, and platelet counts) weekly for the first 3 months, then periodically [US].
- **ECG:** Monitor for QRS widening (>25%), Q-T prolongation, or heart block. Discontinue infusion if QRS widens by 50% [US].
- **Blood Pressure:** Monitor with the patient supine during parenteral administration; discontinue if BP falls >15 mmHg [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
| System | Frequency/Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Multisystem** | **Common (~30% in long-term oral use):** Lupus-like syndrome (arthralgia, pleural/abdominal pain, arthritis, fever, chills) [US]. |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypotension (especially with rapid IV), ventricular asystole, or fibrillation [US]. |
| **Haematologic** | **Rare (0.5%):** Neutropenia, agranulocytosis, thrombocytopenia, hemolytic anemia [US]. |
| **Gastrointestinal** | **3–4%:** Anorexia, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, bitter taste [US]. |
| **Neurological** | Dizziness, psychosis with hallucinations, depression, weakness, tremors (at high concentrations) [US]. |
| **Dermatological** | Angioneurotic edema, urticaria, pruritus, maculopapular rash [US]. |
| **Hepatic** | Elevated transaminases, alkaline phosphatase, and bilirubin; rare fatal liver failure [US]. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Antiarrhythmics:** Additive cardiac effects; dosage reduction may be necessary [US].
- **Anticholinergics:** Possible additive antivagal effects on A-V conduction [US].
- **Neuromuscular Blockers (e.g., succinylcholine):** Procainamide may enhance the effects of these agents by reducing acetylcholine release [US].
- **Lab Interactions:** Suprapharmacologic lidocaine or meprobamate, and therapeutic propranolol, may interfere with fluorescence-based assays for procainamide and NAPA [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not specified by US letter category in provided source, but noted that animal studies have not been conducted [US].
    - **Recommendation:** Should be given to a pregnant woman only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Both procainamide and its metabolite, N-acetylprocainamide (NAPA), are excreted in human milk. Due to potential serious adverse reactions in infants, a decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** Long-term animal studies have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Patients over age 50 often require lower dosages due to reduced renal clearance of procainamide and NAPA. Clearance may be reduced by 25% at age 50 and 50% at age 75 [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Procainamide and NAPA accumulate in renal insufficiency. Individual dose adjustment and plasma level monitoring (if available) are required [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Patients who are "slow acetylators" may have different metabolism profiles; liver disease may necessitate closer monitoring of clinical response [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Group 1A antiarrhythmic. Increases the effective refractory period of atria and ventricles; reduces impulse conduction velocity in the atria, His-Purkinje system, and ventricles [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolised in the liver to N-acetylprocainamide (NAPA), which also has antiarrhythmic activity [US].
    - **Distribution:** Apparent volume of distribution is ~2 L/kg. 15–20% protein bound [US].
    - **Excretion:** 30–60% excreted unchanged in urine. Elimination half-life is 3–4 hours in normal renal function [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** ECG changes include slight sinus tachycardia, widened QRS, and prolonged Q-T and P-R intervals [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **CAST Trial:** Referenced as a warning regarding increased mortality with certain Class 1 antiarrhythmics in non-life-threatening arrhythmias post-myocardial infarction [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Stability:** Solution may develop a slightly yellow color over time; this does not indicate a loss of potency. Discard if the solution is darker than light amber [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
