# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- At the time of writing, official PI documents for other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian, Japanese) were not available for comparison. Consequently, the information presented reflects the US-approved labeling.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is not a substitute for the official PI and should not be used as the sole basis for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (a naturally occurring carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (specifically (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corp.) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not specified in source, provided for reference).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Sterile injection for intravenous use: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - **Administration:** Delivered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less frequently than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised based on clinical requirement [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
    - **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels (normal range 40 to 50 µmol/L). Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration before use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal oedema, and bronchospasm have occurred, particularly in ESRD patients. Reactions may manifest within minutes of administration [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known epilepsy, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral vs. IV):** While this document focuses on the IV formulation, it is noted that high-dose oral levocarnitine is not recommended in patients with severe renal impairment due to the accumulation of metabolites (TMA and TMAO) [US].
- **Monitoring:** Pre-treatment plasma carnitine levels should be obtained. Regular monitoring (weekly and monthly) should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** 
    - **General:** Injection site reaction (33–59%), pain (21–49%), headache (3–37%), fever (5–12%), abdominal pain (5–21%) [US].
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (14–21%), hypotension (3–19%).
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhoea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%).
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–33%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–19%).
- **Common (1–10%):** 
    - **Metabolic:** Hypercalcaemia (6–15%), hyperkalaemia (6%), peripheral oedema (3–6%).
    - **Nervous:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%).
- **Serious/Postmarketing:** 
    - Seizures, anaphylaxis, laryngeal oedema, and bronchospasm.
    - Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent body odour and gastritis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients on anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned a letter category in this specific source; however, reproductive studies in rats and rabbits (up to 3.8 times the human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Animal data (cows) suggest increased milk concentrations following administration. The benefits to the mother must be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders (50 mg/kg) applies to paediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD patients on dialysis. IV administration avoids certain metabolite accumulation risks associated with chronic high-dose oral therapy in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also facilitates the excretion of toxic acyl-CoA esters by forming acylcarnitine [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) and γ-butyrobetaine [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In 9-week and 24-week studies of ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg post-dialysis, trough (pre-dialysis) plasma carnitine levels increased significantly from baseline (~20 µmol/L) to levels between 190 and 243 µmol/L [US].
- **Clinical Outcome Note:** While IV levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the definitive effect on specific clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, cardiomyopathy) in this population has not been fully determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C.
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only. Poison schedule not applicable.
