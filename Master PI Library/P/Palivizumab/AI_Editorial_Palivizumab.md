# Editorial Product Information: Palivizumab

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. 
- Information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., US, European, Canadian, Japanese) was not available in the provided source material; where information is absent across all provided sources, it is noted as "Not specified in available sources."
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a synthesis of clinical and regulatory data and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Palivizumab (rmc).
- **Chemical Class:** Humanized IgG1 monoclonal antibody (composed of 95% human and 5% murine amino acid sequences).
- **Brand Name:** **Synagis** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for injection (clear or slightly opalescent).
    - 50 mg/0.5 mL single-use vial [Australian].
    - 100 mg/1 mL single-use vial [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intramuscular (IM) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AstraZeneca Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Prevention of serious lower respiratory tract disease caused by respiratory syncytial virus (RSV)** in paediatric patients at high risk of RSV disease. Safety and efficacy are established in the following populations [Australian]:
    - Infants with a history of prematurity (gestational age $\le$ 35 weeks at birth).
    - Children with bronchopulmonary dysplasia (BPD).
    - Children with haemodynamically significant congenital heart disease (CHD).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Paediatric):** 15 mg/kg of body weight administered once monthly.
- **Administration Schedule:** 
    - Doses should be administered monthly during anticipated periods of RSV risk in the community. 
    - Ideally, the first dose should be given prior to the commencement of the RSV season.
    - To avoid reinfection risk, children who become infected with RSV while receiving prophylaxis should continue to receive monthly doses for the remainder of the season [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - **Route:** Intramuscular injection only, preferably in the anterolateral aspect of the thigh.
    - **Injection Volume:** If the volume to be injected exceeds 1 mL, it should be administered as a divided dose.
    - **Site Caution:** Do not use the gluteal muscle routinely due to the risk of sciatic nerve damage.
    - **Handling:** Do not dilute. Do not shake the vial. The solution contains no preservative and must be administered immediately after drawing into the syringe [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **palivizumab** or any of its excipients.
- Known hypersensitivity to other humanised monoclonal antibodies [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity and Anaphylaxis:** Allergic reactions, including rare cases of anaphylaxis and anaphylactic shock (some fatal), have been reported. Medications for immediate treatment of severe hypersensitivity must be available. If a severe reaction occurs, palivizumab must be permanently discontinued [Australian].
- **Coagulation Disorders:** Use with caution in patients with thrombocytopenia or any coagulation disorder, as with any intramuscular injection.
- **Acute Infection/Febrile Illness:** Administration may be delayed in the presence of moderate to severe acute infection or febrile illness. Mild upper respiratory infections or mild febrile illness are generally not reasons to defer administration [Australian].
- **Laboratory Test Interference:** Palivizumab may interfere with immune-based RSV diagnostic tests (e.g., antigen detection assays) and viral culture assays, potentially leading to false-negative results. It does not interfere with reverse transcriptase polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) based assays [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
In clinical prophylaxis studies, the safety profile was similar between palivizumab and placebo groups. Most events were transient and mild to moderate.

### Frequency of Adverse Effects [Australian]
| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Upper respiratory infection (URI), otitis media, rhinitis, fever, rash, cough, diarrhoea, wheeze. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Nervousness, vomiting, bronchiolitis, conjunctivitis, pain, pneumonia, oral moniliasis, gastroenteritis, viral infection, AST increased, ALT increased, anaemia, constipation, fungal dermatitis, dyspnoea, eczema, injection site reactions, pharyngitis, apnoea. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, anaphylactic shock, and convulsions have been reported in the post-marketing setting [Australian].
- **Immunogenicity:** Low titres of anti-palivizumab antibodies have been observed in ~1% of patients but were not associated with adverse events or altered serum concentrations [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Formal Studies:** No formal drug-drug interaction studies have been conducted.
- **Clinical Observations:** No increase in adverse reactions was observed in patients receiving routine childhood vaccines, influenza vaccine, bronchodilators, or corticosteroids concurrently [Australian].
- **Vaccine Interference:** Palivizumab is specific to RSV and is not expected to interfere with the immune response to vaccines, including live viral vaccines [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - Palivizumab is not indicated for adult use. 
    - **Australian Category:** Not explicitly assigned a letter category in the provided source, but the document states it is not known if palivizumab can cause foetal harm or affect reproductive capacity. Animal reproduction studies have not been performed [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** No data available [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Reproductive toxicity studies have not been performed [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Indicated for infants and children up to 24 months of age at high risk (see Section 2).
- **Elderly Use:** No data available [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Dosing adjustments for renal or hepatic impairment are not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Palivizumab is a humanized IgG1 monoclonal antibody that binds to the A antigenic site of the RSV fusion (F) protein. It exhibits potent neutralising and fusion-inhibitory activity, preventing the virus from entering cells and inhibiting viral replication [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Serum concentrations of ~30 $\mu$g/mL have been shown to produce a 99% reduction in pulmonary RSV replication in animal models.
- **Pharmacokinetics [Australian]:**
    - **Absorption:** Following IM administration, the half-life ($t_{1/2}$) is approximately 20 days in paediatric patients and healthy adults.
    - **Distribution:** $C_{max}$ is reached approximately 3–5 days after injection.
    - **Elimination:** Metabolism has not been formally assessed, but the drug is expected to be cleared via proteolysis.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **IMpact-RSV Trial:** A randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of 1,502 high-risk children (preterm and BPD). Palivizumab (15 mg/kg monthly) reduced RSV-related hospitalisation by 55% (4.8% vs. 10.6% in placebo). 
- **CHD Study:** A study of 1,287 children with haemodynamically significant CHD. Palivizumab reduced RSV hospitalisations by 45% (5.3% vs. 9.7% in placebo).
- **Other Data:** Studies confirmed that the solution for injection is bioequivalent to the previously available lyophilised powder formulation [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate).
- Do not freeze.
- Store in the original container to protect from light.
- Do not use beyond the expiration date [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
