# Editorial Product Information: B-Complex Vitamins (B1, B2, B3, B5, B6, B12)

## Editorial Note
- The jurisdictions contributing to this editorial include: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of parenteral B-complex vitamin supplementation for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Note:** This synthesis is an editorial review and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the local PI for the specific product being administered.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Thiamine hydrochloride (B1), Riboflavine sodium phosphate (B2), Nicotinamide (B3), Dexpanthenol (B5), Pyridoxine hydrochloride (B6), and Cyanocobalamin (B12).
- **Brand Name(s):** Biological Therapies B-Dose 2 mL Injection [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injection solution in a 2 mL amber glass vial [Australian]. 
  - Each 2 mL contains:
    - **Thiamine hydrochloride (B1):** 20 mg
    - **Riboflavine sodium phosphate (B2):** 5 mg
    - **Nicotinamide (B3):** 50 mg
    - **Dexpanthenol (B5):** 5 mg
    - **Pyridoxine hydrochloride (B6):** 10 mg
    - **Cyanocobalamin (B12):** 1 mg
- **Route of Administration:** Slow intramuscular (IM) injection (preferred) or slow intravenous (IV) infusion (if diluted) [Australian].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Biological Therapies (Australia).

## 2. Approved Indications
Parenteral administration is indicated for B-vitamin deficiencies that cannot be corrected by oral intake [Australian]. 
- **Specific Clinical Settings:** Malabsorption disorders, alcoholism, gastrectomy, gastrointestinal pathologies, and in debilitated or elderly patients where oral intake is insufficient [Australian].
- **Approved Indications [Australian]:**
  - Rapid saturation of B-group vitamins.
  - When oral administration is not feasible.
  - Dietary inadequacy in alcoholic, debilitated, or elderly patients.
  - Recovery from Beriberi and Wernicke’s syndrome (Note: This specific formulation contains insufficient **thiamine** for acute Wernicke’s treatment).
  - Pellagra (Vitamin B3 deficiency).
  - Peripheral neuritis associated with B-group deficiencies.
  - Pernicious anaemia (Vitamin B12 deficiency).
  - Angular stomatitis and glossitis.
  - General energy depletion.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** Administered via slow intramuscular (deep intragluteal) injection once weekly for 2 to 6 weeks [Australian].
- **Severe Cases:** One vial daily until acute symptoms subside [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
  - For patient comfort, the solution should be warmed to body temperature before injection [Australian].
  - **IM Route:** Preferred for high-dose B-complex due to the risk of anaphylaxis [Australian].
  - **IV Route:** If used, the solution should be diluted (e.g., in TPN) and infused slowly while monitoring for signs of anaphylaxis [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to any of the active ingredients or excipients [Australian].
- Pre-existing B hyper-vitaminosis [Australian].
- **Dexpanthenol-specific:** Contraindicated in patients with haemophilia and those with mechanical intestinal obstruction (ileus) [Australian].
- **Cyanocobalamin-specific:** Must not be used to treat megaloblastic anaemia caused by folate deficiency [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Anaphylaxis:** Rare but serious cases of anaphylactic shock have occurred following parenteral **thiamine** administration; this risk is highest with intravenous use. Hypersensitivity reactions to **dexpanthenol** and **cyanocobalamin** have also been reported [Australian].
- **Anaphylaxis Management:** If shock occurs, stop administration immediately. Treatment includes IM adrenalin (1:1000), and potentially IV corticosteroids and antihistamines [Australian].
- **Sensitivity Testing:** For patients with a known allergic disposition, a supervised intradermal test dose is recommended before full administration [Australian].
- **Wernicke’s Syndrome:** This product does not contain sufficient **thiamine** for the acute management of Wernicke’s syndrome [Australian].
- **Laboratory Interference:** Large doses of **riboflavine** can cause bright yellow discolouration of the urine, which may interfere with certain lab tests [Australian].
- **Diagnostic Masking:** Indiscriminate use of **cyanocobalamin** may mask the precise diagnosis of folate deficiency [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally rare for these components at the indicated doses:
- **Thiamine hydrochloride:** Rare cases of anaphylactic shock, predominantly following IV administration [Australian].
- **Nicotinamide:** Chronic high-dose administration (3 g/day) may cause heartburn, nausea, headache, fatigue, hives, and visual disturbances [Australian].
- **Pyridoxine hydrochloride:** Long-term administration of very high doses (>1000 mg/day) is associated with severe peripheral neuropathies [Australian].
- **Riboflavine sodium phosphate:** Yellow discolouration of urine [Australian].
- **Cyanocobalamin:** Rare allergic hypersensitivity reactions; potential for haematological response in folate deficiency [Australian].
- **Dexpanthenol:** Rare unconfirmed allergic reactions [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Levodopa:** **Pyridoxine** reduces the therapeutic effects of levodopa. However, this interaction is neutralized if levodopa is administered concurrently with a dopa decarboxylase inhibitor [Australian].
- **Other Interactions:** No other specific data available in the source documents.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
- **Thiamine (B1):** Essential for carbohydrate metabolism, converting pyruvate to Acetyl Coenzyme A for aerobic respiration. Deficiency leads to pyruvate accumulation and syndromes like Beriberi and Wernicke-Korsakoff [Australian].
- **Riboflavine (B2):** Acts as an electron carrier (FMN/FAD) in oxidative/reductive reactions and is necessary for the activation of **pyridoxine** and **nicotinic acid** [Australian].
- **Nicotinamide (B3):** A component of NAD/NADP coenzymes involved in energy metabolism. Deficiency causes pellagra [Australian].
- **Dexpanthenol (B5):** A precursor to Coenzyme A, vital for the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle and aerobic respiration [Australian].
- **Pyridoxine (B6):** Essential for amino acid, carbohydrate, and fat metabolism, as well as haem production and homocysteine regulation [Australian].
- **Cyanocobalamin (B12):** Converted to active coenzymes (mecobalamin, adenosylcobalamin) required for energy production in the TCA cycle and folate recycling [Australian].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** B vitamins are synergistic in metabolism. Parenteral administration bypasses gastrointestinal absorption issues [Australian].
- **Distribution/Metabolism:** Specific pharmacokinetic studies for this combination have not been conducted; however, individual vitamins are expected to behave as they do when administered separately [Australian].
- **Elimination:** **Riboflavine** and its metabolites are excreted renally [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
