# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing in any particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism.
- **Chemical Class**: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. 
- **Brand Names**: Not specifically listed in the source, but manufactured/distributed by Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].
- **ATC Code**: A16AA01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**: Levocarnitine injection, USP: 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration**: Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism**: For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD)**: For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders**:
  - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
  - Administration: Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
  - Loading Doses: Often utilised in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
  - Frequency: Every 3 to 4 hours (q3h or q4h); frequency should never be less than every 6 hours (q6h).
  - Maximum Dose: The highest administered dose reported is 300 mg/kg [US].

- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis**:
  - Recommended Starting Dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
  - Administration: Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
  - Dose Adjustments: Should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Monitoring
- Prior to parenteral therapy, plasma carnitine concentrations should be obtained.
- Ongoing monitoring (weekly and monthly) should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions**: Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Reactions may occur within minutes of intravenous administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks/benefits before any re-challenge [US].
- **Seizures**: Neurologic reactions, including seizures, have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Formulation Precaution**: The safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. High-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects listed below were reported in clinical trials for ESRD patients at a frequency of ≥5%, regardless of causality [US].

### Very Common (>10%)
- **General**: Injection site reaction (33%–38%), pain (21%–35%), flu syndrome (15%–29%), chest pain (6%–15%), headache (3%–37%).
- **Cardiovascular**: Hypertension (18%–21%), hypotension (3%–19%).
- **Digestive**: Diarrhea (9%–35%), vomiting (9%–21%), nausea (5%–12%).
- **Respiratory**: Pharyngitis (15%–27%), cough increase (10%–18%), dyspnea (3%–11%).
- **Neurologic**: Dizziness (10%–18%).

### Common (1–10%)
- **General**: Abdominal pain, fever, accidental injury, asthenia, infection.
- **Cardiovascular**: Arrhythmia, atrial fibrillation, palpitations, tachycardia.
- **Metabolic**: Hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight changes.
- **Nervous**: Insomnia, paresthesia, anxiety, depression.
- **Other**: Anemia, leg cramps, pruritus, rash, taste perversion.

### Serious/Specific Effects
- **Seizures**: Postmarketing neurologic reaction [US].
- **Body Odor**: Reported as a less frequent adverse reaction [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin**: Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Clinical Management**: Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: [US Category: Not specifically lettered, but follows similar criteria]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. There are no adequate/well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: Not specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration. Risks to the infant of excess carnitine intake must be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility**: No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Dosing for metabolic disorders is calculated on a mg/kg basis, applicable to paediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly Use**: Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment**: ESRD patients on hemodialysis are a primary target population for the IV formulation. Dose adjustments are based on pre-dialysis trough levels [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
  - **Distribution**: Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
  - **Metabolism**: Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
  - **Elimination**: Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies**: In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma concentrations from ~20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging**: Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg three times weekly showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Stability**: Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
- **Handling**: Protect from freezing and excessive heat. Discard unused portions [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States**: Rx-only [US].
