# Editorial Product Information: Phentolamine Ophthalmic Solution

## Editorial Note
- This editorial product information has been harmonised based on official documentation from the **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised clinical overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a single reference tool.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Prescribing Information required for clinical practice in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Phentolamine mesylate (an alpha-adrenergic antagonist).
- **Chemical Class:** Substituted imidazoline.
- **Brand Names:** **RYZUMVI** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not explicitly stated in source (Categorised under S01: Ophthalmologicals).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Ophthalmic solution 0.75% (equivalent to 1% phentolamine mesylate). Each mL contains 10 mg phentolamine mesylate [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Topical ophthalmic use.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Oyster Point Pharma, Inc. (a Viatris company) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Reversal of Mydriasis:** Indicated for the treatment of pharmacologically-induced mydriasis produced by adrenergic agonists (e.g., phenylephrine) or parasympatholytic agents (e.g., tropicamide) [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Adults and Paediatric Patients (Aged 12 years and older):** Instill 1 or 2 drops in each dilated eye following the completion of the ophthalmic procedure or examination [US].
- **Paediatric Patients (Aged 3 to 11 years):** Instill 1 drop in each dilated eye following the completion of the ophthalmic procedure or examination [US].

### Administration Instructions
- If a second drop is required (for patients $\ge$ 12 years), it should be administered at least 5 minutes after the first drop [US].
- A single-patient-use vial provides sufficient volume for both eyes.
- The vial must be discarded immediately after use.
- To prevent eye injury or contamination, avoid contact between the vial tip and the eye, eyelids, or any other surfaces.

## 4. Contraindications
- None identified in available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Uveitis:** Phentolamine is not recommended for use in patients with active ocular inflammation (e.g., iritis). Its use in these conditions may lead to the formation of synechiae (adhesions) between the iris and the lens [US].
- **Potential for Eye Injury or Contamination:** Proper aseptic technique is required; do not touch the tip of the single-use vial to any surface [US].
- **Contact Lens Wearers:** Patients should be advised to remove contact lenses prior to instillation. Lenses should not be reinserted for at least 10 minutes following administration [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are primarily localized to the site of administration. Data from clinical trials (N=642) indicate the following frequencies [US]:

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Instillation site discomfort (pain, stinging, burning) (16%), Conjunctival hyperemia (12%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Dysgeusia (altered taste) (6%) |

Serious adverse effects were not reported as common in the provided source for topical ophthalmic use.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources for the ophthalmic route. 
- **Note:** While systemic phentolamine is known to interact with various cardiovascular agents, the low systemic exposure following ophthalmic administration suggests a lower risk for traditional systemic interactions.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Risk Summary:** There are no available data regarding the use of phentolamine ophthalmic solution in pregnant women. [US]
    - **Animal Data:** High-dose oral administration in rats and mice (at least 24 times the recommended clinical dose) resulted in skeletal immaturity and decreased growth in offspring. Use during pregnancy should only be considered if the potential benefit justifies the risk to the foetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There is no information regarding the presence of phentolamine in human milk, the effects on breastfed infants, or the impact on milk production. The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the mother's clinical need and any potential adverse effects on the child [US].
- **Fertility:** Based on male rat studies, no adverse effects on male fertility or reproductive parameters were observed at doses up to 648 times the human therapeutic exposure [US]. Female fertility has not been studied.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have been established in patients aged 3 to 17 years. No significant differences in safety or efficacy were observed between paediatric and adult populations [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness have been observed between geriatric and younger adult subjects [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Dosing adjustments for renal or hepatic impairment are not specified in the available source for the ophthalmic formulation [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A relatively non-selective alpha-1 and alpha-2 adrenergic antagonist. It induces miosis (pupil constriction) by reversibly binding to alpha-1 receptors on the radial iris dilator muscle, thereby antagonising the effects of mydriatics [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Onset of action typically occurs within 30 minutes. Maximal effect is observed between 60 and 90 minutes, with the effect persisting for at least 24 hours [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Systemic exposure is minimal. Following topical administration of 3 drops, peak plasma concentrations (Cmax) were achieved between 15 minutes and 1 hour with a median value of 0.45 ng/mL [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **MIRA-2 and MIRA-3 Trials:** These were randomised, double-masked, vehicle-controlled trials involving 553 subjects (aged 12–80 years). The primary endpoint was the percentage of eyes returning to within 0.2 mm of baseline pupil diameter. Both trials showed a statistically significant (p < 0.01) improvement in pupil diameter recovery in the phentolamine group compared to the placebo group across all time points from 60 minutes to 24 hours [US].
- **MIRA-4 Trial:** Evaluated paediatric subjects aged 3 to 11 years, showing similar efficacy to the adult and adolescent populations [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Refrigerated Storage:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) in the original packaging. Protect from freezing [US].
- **Room Temperature Excursion:** Once removed from refrigeration, the product may be stored at room temperature (up to 25°C/77°F) for a maximum of 35 days. If not used within this period, it must be discarded [US].
- **Vial Handling:** Once a single-patient-use vial is opened, it must be used immediately and then discarded. Unopened vials should remain in the foil pouch until use [US].
