# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data, regulatory requirements, and pharmacological profiles into a single, structured reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing or dispensing in any particular region. At the time of writing, only the United States PI was available for review; additional regional nuances (e.g., Australian, European) should be consulted as they become available.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Name:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation (USA); Manufactured by Baxter Pharmaceuticals India Private Ltd [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often utilised in patients presenting with severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; intervals should never be less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically in the range of 50 mg/kg, though titration may be required based on clinical response.
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Titration:** Adjustments may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known:** There are currently no listed contraindications for levocarnitine injection in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and initiate medical treatment [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** 
    - Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels before therapy [US].
    - Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations is recommended [US].
    - Target plasma free carnitine concentrations: 35 to 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Oral Levocarnitine in Renal Impairment:** The safety/efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events observed in clinical trials of ESRD patients (frequency ≥5% regardless of causality):

| Body System | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain (12%), Flu syndrome (25%), Headache (22%), Infection (15%), Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%), Abdominal pain (9%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%) |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%), Nausea (8%), Anorexia (5%) |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness (13%), Paresthesia (5%), Depression (5%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (23%), Cough increase (9%), Dyspnea (7%), Rhinitis (9%) |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia (9%), Hyperkalemia (6%) |

- **Serious/Neurologic Effects:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Other Notable Effects:** Transient nausea, vomiting, gastritis, and a distinct body odor have been reported [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalised Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels closely after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. However, there are no adequate/controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. While carnitine is a normal component of human milk, studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations following exogenous administration. The benefits of maternal supplementation must be weighed against potential risks to the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Refer to Dosage and Administration for metabolic disorder dosing, which is weight-based [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources; generally, dose selection should be cautious [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is specifically indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD patients on dialysis. Dose adjustments in ESRD should be guided by pre-dialysis trough levels [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with metabolic errors, it promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Efficacy:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg post-dialysis three times weekly showed that trough concentrations reached 54–180 µmol/L (normal range 40–50 µmol/L) within one week.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses in ESRD patients over 12 and 24 weeks demonstrated dose-dependent increases in trough levocarnitine concentrations [US].
- **Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the impact on specific clinical outcomes in this population remains to be determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources [Australian data absent].
