# Editorial Product Information: Pitolisant

## Editorial Note
- **Jurisdictions Contributed:** US (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of pitolisant, synthesised for healthcare professionals to facilitate clinical decision-making. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pitolisant hydrochloride (a histamine-3 [H3] receptor antagonist/inverse agonist).
- **Brand Name(s):** **WAKIX** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 4.45 mg and 17.8 mg [US].
    - Tablets are white, round, biconvex; 4.45 mg is marked with "S"; 17.8 mg is marked with "H" [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Harmony Biosciences, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Narcolepsy:** Treatment of excessive daytime sleepiness (EDS) or cataplexy in adult and pediatric patients aged 6 years and older [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** Administer once daily in the morning immediately upon awakening. It may take up to 8 weeks to observe a clinical response.
- **Adult Titration Schedule:**
    - **Week 1:** 8.9 mg (two 4.45 mg tablets) once daily.
    - **Week 2:** 17.8 mg (one 17.8 mg tablet) once daily.
    - **Week 3:** May increase to a maximum of 35.6 mg (two 17.8 mg tablets) once daily.
- **Pediatric Titration Schedule (6 years and older):**
    - **Week 1:** 4.45 mg once daily.
    - **Week 2:** 8.9 mg once daily.
    - **Week 3:** 17.8 mg once daily (This is the maximum dose for patients <40 kg).
    - **Week 4 (Patients $\ge$40 kg only):** May increase to a maximum of 35.6 mg once daily.
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, the patient should skip that dose and take the next scheduled dose the following morning upon awakening.
- **Dose Adjustments:** Reductions are required for moderate hepatic impairment, renal impairment (eGFR <60), and CYP2D6 poor metabolizers (see Section 9).

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to pitolisant or any component of the formulation; anaphylaxis has been reported [US].
- **Severe Hepatic Impairment:** Pitolisant is contraindicated in patients with Child-Pugh Class C impairment as it is extensively metabolised by the liver [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None noted in available sources.
- **QT Interval Prolongation:** Pitolisant prolongs the QT interval. 
    - **Avoid use** in patients with known QT prolongation, history of cardiac arrhythmias, or in combination with other drugs known to prolong the QT interval.
    - **Risk Factors:** Avoid in patients with symptomatic bradycardia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, or congenital long QT syndrome.
    - **Monitoring:** Increased monitoring is required in patients with renal or hepatic impairment due to higher drug concentrations.
- **Contraceptive Failure:** Pitolisant is a weak CYP3A4 inducer and may reduce the efficacy of hormonal contraceptives. Non-hormonal backup is required during treatment and for 21 days after cessation [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Adult Patients:**
    - **Very Common (>10%):** Headache (18%).
    - **Common (1–10%):** Insomnia (6%), nausea (6%), anxiety (5%), upper respiratory tract infection (5%), musculoskeletal pain (5%), heart rate increase (3%), hallucinations (3%), irritability (3%), abdominal pain (3%), sleep disturbance (3%), decreased appetite (3%), cataplexy (2%), dry mouth (2%), and rash (2%).
- **Pediatric Patients:**
    - **Very Common (>10%):** Headache (19%).
    - **Common (1–10%):** Insomnia (7%).
- **Postmarketing Experience:** Fatigue, weight increase, dizziness, epilepsy, abnormal dreams, suicidal ideation, and pruritus have been reported.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **Strong CYP2D6 Inhibitors (e.g., paroxetine):** Increase pitolisant exposure 2.2-fold. **Management:** Reduce the pitolisant dose by half.
    - **Strong CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., rifampin):** Decrease pitolisant exposure by approximately 50%. **Management:** Dose adjustment may be required; assess for loss of efficacy.
    - **CYP3A4 Substrates:** Pitolisant is a weak inducer. It reduces the exposure of sensitive substrates (e.g., midazolam, hormonal contraceptives).
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **H1 Receptor Antagonists:** Centrally acting antihistamines may reduce the effectiveness of pitolisant; avoid co-administration.
    - **QT-Prolonging Drugs:** Co-administration increases the risk of cardiac arrhythmias.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified by letter, but a Pregnancy Exposure Registry is available. Animal data show developmental toxicity (cleft palate, abnormal limb flexure) at doses significantly higher than the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD).
- **Breastfeeding:** Pitolisant is present in human milk. A lactation study estimated the relative infant dose to be less than 1% of the maternal weight-adjusted dose. Benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need for the drug.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats showed reduced sperm morphology and motility at 13 times the MRHD, though no significant effect on overall fertility indices was noted.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for ages 6 and older. Safety and efficacy not established in patients <6 years [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Limited data. Generally, dosing should start at the low end of the range, reflecting the higher frequency of decreased organ function.
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **eGFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m²:** 
        - Adults: Max dose 17.8 mg daily. 
        - Pediatrics: Max 8.9 mg (<40 kg) or 17.8 mg ($\ge$40 kg).
    - **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Not recommended.
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild (Class A):** No dose adjustment required.
    - **Moderate (Class B):** 
        - Adults: Max dose 17.8 mg daily.
        - Pediatrics: Max 8.9 mg (<40 kg) or 17.8 mg ($\ge$40 kg).
    - **Severe (Class C):** Contraindicated.
- **CYP2D6 Poor Metabolizers:** 
    - Adults: Max dose 17.8 mg daily.
    - Pediatrics: Max 8.9 mg (<40 kg) or 17.8 mg ($\ge$40 kg).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inverse agonist/antagonist at the H3 receptor. By blocking H3 autoreceptors, it increases the synthesis and release of histamine, a wake-promoting neurotransmitter.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** 90% absorbed; $T_{max}$ is approximately 3.5 hours. No clinically significant food effect.
    - **Distribution:** 91–96% protein bound. Apparent volume of distribution is ~700 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by CYP2D6 and to a lesser extent CYP3A4.
    - **Elimination:** Half-life is ~20 hours. 90% excreted in urine (mostly as metabolites).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adult Narcolepsy (EDS):** Two 8-week studies (Study 1 and 2) demonstrated a statistically significant reduction in Epworth Sleepiness Scale (ESS) scores compared to placebo.
- **Adult Narcolepsy (Cataplexy):** Study 3 (7 weeks) showed a significant reduction in the weekly rate of cataplexy attacks (rate ratio 0.51).
- **Pediatric Narcolepsy:** Study 4 (8 weeks) showed significant improvement in the Pediatric Daytime Sleepiness Scale (PDSS) and reduction in cataplexy frequency in patients aged 6–17 years.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Excursions:** Permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F).

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **US:** Classified as Rx-only. Poison schedule not specified in available documents.
