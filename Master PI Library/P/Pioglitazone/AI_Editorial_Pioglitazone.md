# Editorial Product Information: Pioglitazone

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of pioglitazone for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing or dispensing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of this writing, only the Australian PI was provided for synthesis; references to other jurisdictions are limited to those cited within the source text.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pioglitazone (as hydrochloride).
- **Chemical Class:** Thiazolidinedione antidiabetic agent.
- **Brand Names:** Actaze [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A10BG03.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Oral tablets: 15 mg, 30 mg, and 45 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrow Pharma Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Pioglitazone is indicated for the treatment of type 2 diabetes mellitus in adults to improve glycaemic control (in conjunction with diet and exercise):
- **Monotherapy:** For patients inadequately controlled by diet and exercise alone.
- **Dual Therapy:** In combination with metformin, a sulfonylurea, or insulin.
- **Triple Therapy:** In combination with metformin and a sulfonylurea.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Standard Dose:** 15 mg or 30 mg once daily.
- **Maximum Dose:** 45 mg once daily. Doses exceeding 45 mg/day have not been clinically studied.
- **Administration:** May be taken with or without food.

### Titration and Specific Recommendations
- **Initial Titration:** If a greater therapeutic effect is required, the dose may be increased to 45 mg once daily after four weeks of therapy.
- **Combination with Insulin/Sulfonylureas:** To reduce the risk of hypoglycaemia, the dose of the concomitant insulin or sulfonylurea may need to be lowered. 
- **Female Patients:** Due to a higher reported incidence of oedema in women, therapy should be initiated at 15 mg and increased with caution [Australian].
- **Missed Doses:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to pioglitazone or any tablet excipients (e.g., lactose).
- **Cardiac Failure:** Initiation is contraindicated in patients with NYHA Class II, III, or IV heart failure [Australian].
- **Diabetes Type:** Must not be used in Type 1 diabetes or for the treatment of diabetic ketoacidosis.
- **Bladder Cancer:** Active bladder cancer or a history of bladder cancer [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Cardiac Effects
- **Congestive Heart Failure (CHF):** Thiazolidinediones can cause fluid retention, which may lead to or exacerbate CHF. Patients should be monitored for rapid weight gain, dyspnoea, or oedema. If symptoms develop, discontinuation is required. 
- **NYHA Class I:** Initiation should be at the lowest approved dose with gradual escalation only after several months of monitoring.

### Malignancy
- **Bladder Cancer:** Epidemiological data suggest an increased risk of bladder cancer associated with long-term use (especially >12 months). Risk factors (smoking, age, exposure to cyclophosphamide) should be assessed before starting therapy. Patients must report macroscopic haematuria or urinary urgency immediately.

### Skeletal Effects
- **Bone Fractures:** An increased incidence of bone fractures has been observed in women. This risk should be considered in the long-term management of female patients.

### Hepatic and Other Precautions
- **Hepatic Monitoring:** Therapy should not be initiated if ALT is >2.5 times the upper limit of normal (ULN). Monitor liver function at baseline, every two months for the first year, and periodically thereafter. Discontinue if ALT remains >3x ULN or if jaundice occurs.
- **Oedema:** Use with caution in patients already prone to fluid retention.
- **Macular Oedema:** Rare post-marketing reports of new-onset or worsening diabetic macular oedema with decreased visual acuity have occurred.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Categorisation
- **Very Common (>10%):** 
    - Hypoglycaemia (when used with insulin: 30.6%) [Australian].
    - Oedema (when used with insulin: 15.8%) [Australian].
    - Weight gain (triple therapy: 26.2%) [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** 
    - Upper respiratory tract infection, headache, sinusitis, myalgia, pharyngitis.
    - Weight increase (monotherapy and dual therapy).
    - Arthralgia, back pain.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** 
    - Bladder cancer (observed in PROactive study at 0.5% vs 0.2% placebo) [Australian].
- **Rare (<0.1% / Post-marketing):**
    - Congestive heart failure (very rare: 0.9/10,000 patient-years).
    - Hepatocellular dysfunction/hepatic failure.
    - Macular oedema.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP2C8 Inhibitors (e.g., Gemfibrozil):** May result in a threefold increase in pioglitazone AUC. A dose reduction of pioglitazone may be necessary.
- **CYP2C8 Inducers (e.g., Rifampicin):** May decrease pioglitazone AUC by approximately 54%. Dose increases may be required based on clinical response.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Oral Contraceptives:** Pioglitazone may reduce the plasma concentration of ethinyloestradiol and norethindrone by approximately 30%, potentially leading to loss of contraception. Alternative methods or higher doses of contraceptives should be considered.
- **Hypoglycaemic Agents:** Increased risk of hypoglycaemia when combined with insulin or sulfonylureas.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B3.** 
    - Animal studies show embryotoxicity and foetotoxicity (reduced foetal weight and retarded development). 
    - There are no adequate/controlled studies in humans. Use only if potential benefits outweigh risks. 
- **Breastfeeding:** Pioglitazone is secreted in the milk of lactating rats; human data are unavailable. Breastfeeding should be discontinued if pioglitazone use is essential.
- **Fertility:** May result in the resumption of ovulation in premenopausal anovulatory patients with insulin resistance (e.g., PCOS), increasing pregnancy risk.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established; use is not recommended.
- **Elderly Use:** No significant differences in safety or efficacy compared to younger patients, but caution is advised due to the higher risk of heart failure in this population.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required in renal insufficiency. However, it should not be used in patients on dialysis due to lack of data [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Clearance is reduced. Start at 15 mg and increase cautiously. Do not initiate if active liver disease is present.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A potent and selective agonist for the peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma (PPAR-gamma). Activation modulates the transcription of genes involved in glucose and lipid metabolism, primarily by increasing insulin sensitivity in muscle and adipose tissue and inhibiting hepatic gluconeogenesis.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Reduces fasting and postprandial glucose, lowers HbA1c, and decreases circulating insulin levels. It also reduces triglycerides and increases HDL cholesterol [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak plasma concentration in ~2 hours. Bioavailability ~83%.
    - **Distribution:** >99% protein bound (mainly albumin).
    - **Metabolism:** Extensive hepatic metabolism via CYP2C8 and CYP3A4 into active and inactive metabolites.
    - **Excretion:** 55% in faeces, 45% in urine. Elimination half-life: 5–6 hours (parent drug); 16–23 hours (total active compounds).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Monotherapy/Combination Trials:** Studies ranging from 16 to 26 weeks demonstrated significant reductions in HbA1c (up to 1.4–1.6% relative to placebo) and Fasting Blood Glucose [Australian].
- **PROactive Study:** A cardiovascular outcome study in 5,238 patients with T2DM and macrovascular disease. While it showed an increased risk of serious heart failure (5.7% vs 4.1% placebo), it did not show an increase in all-cause mortality over 3.5 years.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging.
- **Packaging:** Typically presented in aluminium blister packs.
