# Editorial Product Information: Paricalcitol

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: US (Food and Drug Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of paricalcitol product information, synthesised for use by healthcare professionals. 
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this writing, only the United States (US) PI was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Paricalcitol (a synthetic, biologically active vitamin D$_2$ analog of calcitriol).
- **Brand Name(s)**: Zemplar [US].
- **ATC Code**: H05BX02.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s)**: 
  - Soft gelatin capsules: 1 mcg (gray) and 2 mcg (orange-brown) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: AbbVie Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Hyperparathyroidism (SHPT)**: Indicated for the prevention and treatment of SHPT associated with:
  - Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) Stages 3 and 4 in adults and paediatric patients 10 years and older [US].
  - CKD Stage 5 in adults and paediatric patients 10 years and older receiving hemodialysis (HD) or peritoneal dialysis (PD) [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Capsules may be administered with or without food [US].
- When dosed three times weekly (TIW), the medication should be taken no more frequently than every other day [US].

### Adult Dosing: CKD Stages 3 and 4
Initial dosing is determined by baseline intact parathyroid hormone (iPTH) levels:
- **Baseline iPTH $\le$ 500 pg/mL**: 1 mcg daily OR 2 mcg TIW [US].
- **Baseline iPTH > 500 pg/mL**: 2 mcg daily OR 4 mcg TIW [US].

### Adult Dosing: CKD Stage 5
- **Initial Dose**: Calculated using the formula: `Baseline iPTH (pg/mL) / 80` [US].
- **Frequency**: Administered TIW [US].
- **Threshold**: Treatment should only begin if baseline serum calcium is $\le$ 9.5 mg/dL to minimise hypercalcemia risk [US].

### Dose Titration (All Stages)
Titration is based on iPTH, serum calcium, and phosphorus levels, typically at 2- to 4-week intervals.
- **CKD 3 & 4**: Increase dose by 1 mcg daily or 2 mcg TIW if iPTH is unchanged or decreased by < 30%. Decrease dose by 1 mcg daily or 2 mcg TIW if iPTH decreases by > 60% or falls below 60 pg/mL [US].
- **CKD 5**: Use the formula `Most recent iPTH / 80` to determine the TIW dose. If serum calcium is elevated, decrease the dose by 2 to 4 mcg [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Evidence of hypercalcemia [US].
- Evidence of vitamin D toxicity [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypercalcemia**: Excessive administration can lead to significant hypercalcemia, hypercalciuria, hyperphosphatemia, and over-suppression of PTH, potentially leading to adynamic bone disease. Acute hypercalcemia may trigger cardiac arrhythmias or seizures [US].
- **Digitalis Toxicity**: Hypercalcemia of any cause increases the risk of digitalis toxicity. Caution is required when co-administering paricalcitol with digitalis compounds [US].
- **Laboratory Monitoring**: Monitor serum calcium, phosphorus, and iPTH every two weeks for the first 3 months after initiation or dose adjustment, then monthly for 3 months, and every 3 months thereafter [US].
- **Renal Function**: In pre-dialysis patients, paricalcitol may increase serum creatinine and decrease estimated GFR (eGFR) [US].
- **Aluminum Toxicity**: Avoid chronic use of aluminum-containing preparations (e.g., antacids, phosphate binders) due to the risk of aluminum bone toxicity [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (Frequency > 5%)
| Adverse Reaction | Frequency [US] |
| :--- | :--- |
| Diarrhea | 7–11% |
| Nasopharyngitis | 8% |
| Dizziness | 5–7% |
| Vomiting | 5–7% |
| Hypertension | 7% |
| Nausea | 6% |
| Edema | 6% |

### Other Adverse Effects
- **Uncommon/Rare (< 2%)**: Dry mouth, hepatic enzyme abnormalities, dysgeusia, urticaria, acne, breast tenderness, anxiety, and insomnia [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects**: Hypercalcemia, hyperphosphatemia, and angioedema (including laryngeal edema) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Strong CYP3A Inhibitors**: (e.g., ketoconazole, clarithromycin, ritonavir, grapefruit juice). These increase paricalcitol exposure. Monitor iPTH and calcium closely if these agents are started or stopped; dose adjustments may be required [US].
- **Cholestyramine and Mineral Oil**: These may reduce the intestinal absorption of paricalcitol. Paricalcitol should be taken at least 1 hour before or 4 to 6 hours after these substances [US].
- **Thiazide Diuretics/Calcium Supplements**: Increased risk of hypercalcemia [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**:
  - **US Category**: Not specified as a letter, but data are insufficient to inform drug-associated risks. Animal studies showed slightly increased embryofetal loss at doses toxic to the mother [US].
  - **Australian/European/Canadian Category**: Not specified in available sources.
- **Breastfeeding**: It is unknown if paricalcitol is excreted in human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions (e.g., hypercalcemia) in the infant, breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and efficacy established in patients 10–16 years old. Not established in children under 10 [US].
- **Elderly Use**: No overall differences in safety or efficacy were observed between patients $\ge$ 65 years and younger patients, though greater sensitivity in some older individuals cannot be ruled out [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: No dose adjustment is required for mild to moderate impairment (Child-Pugh). Use in severe hepatic impairment has not been studied [US].
- **Renal Impairment**: Dosing is specifically targeted for CKD stages 3–5. Paricalcitol is not significantly removed by dialysis [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Paricalcitol is an analog of 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D$_3$ (calcitriol) that selectively binds to and activates the Vitamin D Receptor (VDR). This results in the inhibition of PTH synthesis and secretion [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
  - **Absorption**: Absolute bioavailability is 72–86%. Food does not significantly affect AUC but may delay T$_{max}$ by 2 hours [US].
  - **Distribution**: Highly protein bound (> 99.8%) [US].
  - **Metabolism**: Extensively metabolised by multiple hepatic and non-hepatic enzymes, including CYP3A4, mitochondrial CYP24, and UGT1A4 [US].
  - **Elimination**: Primarily via hepatobiliary excretion (70% in feces). The mean elimination half-life is 14–20 hours in CKD patients [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **CKD Stages 3 and 4**: Three 24-week, double-blind trials showed that 91% of patients achieved a $\ge$ 30% reduction in iPTH compared to 13% for placebo. Results were consistent between daily and TIW dosing regimens [US].
- **CKD Stage 5**: A 12-week study in patients on dialysis showed 88% of patients achieved $\ge$ 30% iPTH reduction [US].
- **Paediatric Data**: Studies in children aged 10–16 with CKD 3, 4, and 5 confirmed reductions in iPTH levels, though the incidence of hypercalcemia was notable in the Stage 5 cohort (23%) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F–86°F) [US].
- **Handling**: Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container [US].
