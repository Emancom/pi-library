# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **Carnitine Deficiency in End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:**
    - **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose:** Often provided in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
    - **Frequency:** Administered every 3 to 4 hours; should not be administered less frequently than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Maximum Dose:** Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
    - **Administration:** Slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].

### Titration and Monitoring
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Dosing should be guided by weekly/monthly monitoring of plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L), blood chemistries, and clinical response [US].
- **ESRD:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels <40–50 µmol/L. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulates or discoloration prior to use [US].
- Discard any unused portion as the formulation lacks preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have occurred, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. Treatment should be discontinued immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine (oral or IV) has been reported to cause seizures in patients with or without a history of seizure activity. In those with pre-existing conditions, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].

### Precautions
- **Oral Levocarnitine in Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) normally cleared by the kidneys [US].
- **Clinical Outcomes in ESRD:** While IV levocarnitine increases carnitine levels in dialysis patients, the direct effect on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, arrhythmias) has not been definitively determined [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **General:** Transient nausea and vomiting. Body odor and gastritis are reported less frequently [US].
- **ESRD Population (Frequency ≥5% in Clinical Trials):**
    - **Body as a Whole:** Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (21–35%), chest pain (6–15%), fever (5–12%), flu syndrome (15–29%), headache (3–37%), infection (10–24%), abdominal pain (5–21%) [US].
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%) [US].
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%) [US].
    - **Nervous System:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%) [US].
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%) [US].
    - **Metabolic/Nutritional:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hypervolemia (3–12%) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (BSA-based) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm [US].
    - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers is not specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations after administration. Weigh the benefits of maternal supplementation against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg body weight as specified in the Metabolic Disorders section [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available source; general caution regarding organ function in older adults is standard [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis (see Dosage and Administration). Safety of oral formulations in renal impairment is not established [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, it alleviates metabolic abnormalities resulting from toxic organic acid accumulation and corrects biochemical carnitine deficiency (defined as <20 µmol/L) [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound; mean distribution half-life of 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
    - **Bioavailability (Oral):** Approximately 15–16% [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Dosing Studies:** A 9-week study of ESRD patients (n=12) receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis showed a significant increase in mean trough plasma levocarnitine concentrations from ~20 µmol/L (baseline) to 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Comparisons of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses over 24 weeks demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels, with the 40 mg/kg group reaching 456 ± 162 µmol/L [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
