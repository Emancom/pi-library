# Editorial Product Information: Peginterferon alfa-2a

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a single, clinically focused reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Peginterferon alfa-2a, a covalent conjugate of recombinant interferon alfa-2a (produced in *E. coli*) with a single branched bis-monomethoxypolyethylene glycol.
- **Brand Name:** **Pegasys** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L03AB11.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Solution for injection in pre-filled syringes. Available in 135 micrograms/0.5 mL and 180 micrograms/0.5 mL [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Echo Therapeutics Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Hepatitis C (CHC):** 
    - As monotherapy for treatment-naïve adult patients with compensated liver disease.
    - In combination with ribavirin for treatment-naïve patients and those who have failed previous interferon alfa therapy (pegylated or non-pegylated).
    - In combination with ribavirin for patients with clinically stable HIV-HCV co-infection who are treatment-naïve.
- **Chronic Hepatitis B (CHB):** 
    - Treatment of adult patients with evidence of viral replication, liver inflammation, and compensated liver disease [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Adult Dosing
- **Standard Dose:** 180 micrograms once weekly administered subcutaneously in the abdomen or thigh.
- **Duration (CHC Monotherapy):** 48 weeks.
- **Duration (CHB):** 48 weeks.

### CHC Combination Therapy with Ribavirin
Dosing and duration are individualised based on viral genotype:
- **Genotypes 1, 4:** 180 mcg **Pegasys** weekly + ribavirin (1000 mg if <75 kg; 1200 mg if ≥75 kg) for 48 weeks.
- **Genotypes 2, 3:** 180 mcg **Pegasys** weekly + 800 mg ribavirin for 24 weeks.
- **Prior Non-responders/Relapsers:** 180 mcg weekly for up to 72 weeks (Genotypes 1, 4) or 48 weeks (Genotypes 2, 3).
- **HIV-HCV Co-infection:** 180 mcg weekly (alone or with 800 mg ribavirin) for 48 weeks.

### Dose Modifications
- **General:** Initial reduction to 135 mcg is usually adequate; further reductions to 90 mcg or 45 mcg may be required for adverse reactions.
- **Haematological:** 
    - **ANC:** Reduce to 135 mcg if <750 cells/mm³; suspend if <500 cells/mm³ until ANC >1000 cells/mm³.
    - **Platelets:** Reduce to 90 mcg if <50,000 cells/mm³; discontinue if <25,000 cells/mm³.
- **Hepatic:** For HCV, reduce to 135 mcg if ALT increases progressively above baseline. For HBV, monitor flares (>10x ULN) closely; discontinue if increases are severe, progressive, or accompanied by bilirubin elevation.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to alfa interferons, *E. coli*-derived products, or polyethylene glycol.
- Autoimmune hepatitis.
- Decompensated cirrhosis.
- HIV-HCV co-infection with cirrhosis and Child-Pugh score ≥6 (unless solely due to indirect hyperbilirubinaemia from drugs like atazanavir).
- Neonates and infants up to 3 years of age (due to benzyl alcohol content).
- **Combination with Ribavirin:** Contraindicated in pregnancy (and male partners of pregnant women), breastfeeding, haemoglobinopathies (e.g., thalassaemia), and severe/unstable pre-existing cardiac disease.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Neuropsychiatric:** Severe reactions including depression, suicidal ideation, suicide, and relapse of drug dependence can occur. Monitor all patients for depression; discontinue if severe.
- **Hepatic Decompensation:** Discontinue immediately if hepatic decompensation occurs. HIV-HCV co-infected patients on HAART (specifically NRTIs like didanosine) are at increased risk.
- **Pulmonary:** Dyspnoea, pneumonia, and pulmonary infiltrates have been reported. **Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension (PAH)** is a known risk; assess patients with unexplained dyspnoea or fatigue.
- **Endocrine:** May cause or aggravate thyroid disorders (hypo/hyperthyroidism) and diabetes mellitus.
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension, supraventricular arrhythmias, and myocardial infarction have been associated with therapy.
- **Bone Marrow Suppression:** Use with caution in patients with low baseline counts (Neutrophils <1500; Platelets <90,000; Hb <120 g/L).
- **Ophthalmologic:** Retinopathy (haemorrhages, cotton wool spots), optic neuropathy, and retinal artery/vein obstruction may occur. Baseline and periodic eye exams are recommended.
- **Infections:** Serious bacterial, viral, and fungal infections reported.
- **Organ Transplant:** Liver and renal graft rejections have been reported.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally similar between monotherapy and combination therapy, though more frequent with the latter.
- **Very Common (>10%):**
    - **General:** Fatigue, pyrexia, rigors, asthenia, injection site reactions.
    - **Psychiatric:** Insomnia, depression, irritability, anxiety.
    - **Nervous System:** Headache, dizziness, concentration impairment.
    - **Musculoskeletal:** Myalgia, arthralgia.
    - **GI:** Nausea, diarrhoea, anorexia, weight loss.
    - **Skin:** Alopecia, pruritus, dermatitis, dry skin.
- **Common (1–10%):** Lethargy, influenza-like illness, palpitations, flushing, dyspnoea, cough, vomiting, dyspepsia.
- **Serious/Rare:** Interstitial pneumonitis (potentially fatal), pulmonary embolism, sarcoidosis, peripheral neuropathy, psychosis, suicidal ideation, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome/Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Theophylline:** **Pegasys** is a modest inhibitor of CYP1A2; may increase theophylline AUC by 25%. Monitor serum levels.
- **Methadone:** Levels may increase by 10–15% during therapy; monitor for toxicity.
- **Azathioprine:** Ribavirin (used in combination) may increase levels of 6-methyl-thioinosine monophosphate (6-MTIMP), increasing myelotoxicity risk.
- **Didanosine:** Co-administration with ribavirin is not recommended due to risk of fatal hepatic failure and lactic acidosis.
- **Zidovudine:** Increased risk of severe anaemia and neutropenia when used with **Pegasys** and ribavirin.
- **Telbivudine:** Combination with **Pegasys** increases the risk of peripheral neuropathy.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B3]. Not established in humans. Statistically significant increase in abortifacient activity in monkeys. Use only if benefit outweighs potential fetal risk. Effective contraception is mandatory for women of childbearing potential.
- **Breastfeeding:** Unknown if excreted in human milk. A decision should be made to either discontinue breastfeeding or the drug, considering the mother's clinical need.
- **Fertility:** In female monkeys, peginterferon alfa-2a caused prolongation of the menstrual cycle and decreased 17β-estradiol/progesterone levels; effects reversed upon discontinuation.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established in patients <18 years. Use in infants <3 years is contraindicated (benzyl alcohol risk) [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment is required based on age; however, modest increases in AUC have been noted in patients >62 years.
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Mild/Moderate:** No adjustment.
    - **Severe:** Reduce dose to 135 mcg once weekly.
    - **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Starting dose of 135 mcg once weekly; monitor closely for serious adverse events.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Safe and effective in compensated cirrhosis. Contraindicated in decompensated disease (Child-Pugh score ≥7).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Interferons bind to cell surface receptors, initiating intracellular signalling (JAK-STAT pathway) and gene transcription. This results in antiviral (inhibition of replication), antiproliferative, and immunomodulatory effects.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Sustained absorption; peak serum concentrations reached 72–96 hours post-dose. Absolute bioavailability is 84%.
    - **Distribution:** Vss of 6–14 L. Predominantly found in bloodstream and extracellular fluid.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 160 hours (range 84–353 hours) following SC administration. Steady-state is reached within 5–8 weeks.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **CHC Monotherapy (NV15495, NV15497):** Demonstrated superior Sustained Virological Response (SVR) compared to standard interferon alfa-2a in patients with and without cirrhosis. SVR rates were ~30% in cirrhotic patients and ~39% in non-cirrhotic patients [Australian].
- **CHC Combination (NV15942, NV15801):** SVR rates were significantly higher with 48 weeks vs 24 weeks of treatment for Genotype 1 (46-52% vs 29-42%). Genotypes 2/3 achieved ~80% SVR with 24 weeks of therapy.
- **CHB (WV16240, WV16241):** Demonstrated significant HBeAg seroconversion (~32%) and HBV DNA suppression in both HBeAg-positive and HBeAg-negative patients compared to lamivudine [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store in a refrigerator between 2°C and 8°C.
- **Handling:** Do not freeze or shake. Protect from light.
- **Shelf Life:** Expiry date is located on the packaging [Australian].
