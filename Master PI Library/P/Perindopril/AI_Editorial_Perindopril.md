# Editorial Product Information: Perindopril Arginine and Amlodipine Besilate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis was prepared using official Product Information (PI) documentation from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of the fixed-dose combination of perindopril arginine and amlodipine besilate for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official PI. Clinicians must refer to the current local approved Product Information in their specific jurisdiction before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Perindopril arginine (Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme [ACE] Inhibitor) and Amlodipine besilate (Dihydropyridine Calcium Channel Blocker).
- **Brand Names:** APO-Perindopril Arginine/Amlodipine [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Oral tablets containing perindopril arginine/amlodipine (as besilate) in the following ratios: 5 mg/5 mg, 5 mg/10 mg, 10 mg/5 mg, and 10 mg/10 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Substitution Therapy:** Indicated for the treatment of hypertension and/or stable coronary heart disease in patients whose blood pressure and/or stable coronary heart disease are already adequately controlled with separate doses of perindopril and amlodipine given concurrently at the same dose levels [Australian].
- **Note:** Treatment should not be initiated with this fixed-dose combination [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** One tablet daily as a single dose.
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets should be taken in the morning before a meal. Food intake may reduce the hepatic biotransformation of perindopril to its active metabolite, perindoprilat [Australian].
- **Titration:** If a dose adjustment is required, the individual components (perindopril and amlodipine) should be titrated separately. Once clinical stability is reached at the desired doses, the patient may be transitioned back to the equivalent fixed-dose combination [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **General:** Hypersensitivity to perindopril, amlodipine, other ACE inhibitors, dihydropyridine derivatives, or any excipients [Australian].
- **Pregnancy and Lactation:** Contraindicated during pregnancy and for women who are breastfeeding [Australian].
- **Perindopril-Specific:**
    - History of hereditary or idiopathic angioedema.
    - History of angioedema associated with previous ACE inhibitor therapy.
    - Bilateral or unilateral renal artery stenosis.
    - Concurrent use with aliskiren-containing products in patients with diabetes or renal impairment (GFR < 60 mL/min/1.73 m²).
    - Concurrent use with sacubitril/valsartan; therapy must not be initiated within 36 hours of the last dose of sacubitril/valsartan.
    - Extracorporeal treatments leading to contact with negatively charged surfaces (e.g., high-flux dialysis membranes like "AN69", LDL apheresis with dextran sulfate) [Australian].
- **Amlodipine-Specific:**
    - Severe hypotension.
    - Shock, including cardiogenic shock.
    - Left ventricular outflow tract obstruction (e.g., high-grade aortic stenosis).
    - Unstable angina pectoris (excluding Prinzmetal's angina).
    - Heart failure following acute myocardial infarction [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
- **Hyperkalaemia:** ACE inhibitors can cause increases in serum potassium. Risk factors include renal impairment, age >70 years, diabetes, and concurrent use of potassium-sparing diuretics, potassium supplements, or NSAIDs. Monitoring of electrolytes is required [Australian].
- **Angioedema:** May occur at any time during treatment. If involving the face, lips, or tongue (potentially obstructing the airway), therapy must be discontinued immediately and emergency management initiated.
- **Risk with Concomitant Medicines:** Increased risk of angioedema when used with mTOR inhibitors (e.g., sirolimus), gliptins (e.g., sitagliptin), or NEP inhibitors (e.g., sacubitril).
- **Hypotension:** More likely in volume-depleted patients (e.g., those on diuretics, salt restriction, or dialysis). In patients with ischaemic heart or cerebrovascular disease, excessive hypotension may lead to myocardial infarction or stroke.
- **Aortic/Mitral Valve Stenosis:** Use with caution in patients with outflow obstruction.
- **Ethnicity:** ACE inhibitors appear less effective in lowering blood pressure in patients of African origin, who also face a higher risk of angioedema [Australian].
- **Cough:** A persistent, non-productive dry cough is a class effect of ACE inhibitors and resolves upon discontinuation [Australian].
- **Surgery/Anaesthesia:** Perindopril may block angiotensin II formation secondary to compensatory renin release; treatment should be discontinued one day prior to surgery [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (>1% to <10%):** 
    - **Perindopril/Amlodipine Combo:** Dizziness (very common in combo), lethargy, vertigo, dyspnoea, cough (very common in combo), abdominal pain, nausea, diarrhoea, constipation, pruritus, muscle spasms, fatigue, and ankle swelling (joint swelling).
- **Uncommon (0.1% to 1%):** Eosinophilia, hypersensitivity, hypoglycaemia, hyperkalaemia, hyponatraemia, mood changes, sleep disorders, syncope, palpitations, tachycardia, vasculitis, peripheral coldness, dry mouth, vomiting, angioedema, rash, arthralgia, myalgia, renal failure, erectile dysfunction, and malaise.
- **Rare (<0.1%):** SIADH, increased appetite, confusional state, increased bilirubin, and jaundice.
- **Very Rare (<0.01%):** Rhinitis, leukopenia, agranulocytosis, pancytopenia, thrombocytopenia, haemolytic anaemia, myocardial infarction (secondary to hypotension), and Stevens-Johnson Syndrome.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Angioedema, anaphylactoid reactions, severe hypotension, acute renal failure, and fulminant hepatic necrosis [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Dual RAAS Blockade:** Combination of ACE inhibitors with ARBs or aliskiren increases the risk of hypotension, hyperkalaemia, and renal impairment; not recommended.
- **Potassium-Sparing Agents:** Use with potassium-sparing diuretics (e.g., spironolactone), potassium supplements, or salt substitutes is not recommended due to hyperkalaemia risk [Australian].
- **Lithium:** Increased serum lithium concentrations and toxicity; not recommended.
- **NSAIDs:** May reduce the antihypertensive effect and increase the risk of renal deterioration, especially in the elderly.
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors/Inducers:** Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., erythromycin, clarithromycin, ritonavir) may increase amlodipine exposure. CYP3A4 inducers (e.g., rifampicin, St John’s Wort) may decrease amlodipine levels.
- **Simvastatin:** Amlodipine increases exposure to simvastatin; limit simvastatin dose to 20 mg daily in patients on amlodipine [Australian].
- **Dantrolene (Infusion):** Risk of ventricular fibrillation and cardiovascular collapse; avoid combination with amlodipine [Australian].
- **Grapefruit Juice:** May increase amlodipine bioavailability in some patients, leading to increased blood pressure-lowering effects [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D [Australian].** ACE inhibitors can cause foetal and neonatal morbidity and death when administered during the second and third trimesters. If pregnancy is detected, therapy must be stopped immediately.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Perindopril is excreted in animal milk; amlodipine is excreted in human milk. Alternative treatments with established safety profiles are preferred [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in rat studies for perindopril (10 mg/kg/day) or amlodipine (18 mg/kg/day) [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established; use is not recommended in children [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Clearance of perindoprilat and amlodipine is decreased. Treatment should start at the lower end of the dosing range (equivalent to 2.5 mg perindopril arginine and 2.5 mg amlodipine) using separate components before switching to the combination [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Elimination of perindoprilat is reduced. For patients with CrCl < 60 mL/min, individual titration of components is required before using the fixed-dose combination. Amlodipine is not dialysable; perindopril is dialysable [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Dosage recommendations for amlodipine have not been established in mild-to-moderate impairment. Treatment should start at the lower end of the dosing range using separate components [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Perindopril:** A prodrug that is hydrolysed to perindoprilat, which inhibits ACE, reducing angiotensin II levels and increasing plasma renin activity. 
    - **Amlodipine:** A calcium ion influx inhibitor that inhibits the transmembrane influx of calcium into cardiac and vascular smooth muscle, resulting in peripheral vasodilation [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Perindopril (bioavailability 24%); Amlodipine (bioavailability 64-90%). Peak levels: Perindoprilat (3-4 hours); Amlodipine (6-12 hours).
    - **Distribution:** Amlodipine is 97.5% protein-bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Perindopril is extensively metabolised in the liver to perindoprilat. Amlodipine is extensively metabolised to inactive metabolites.
    - **Elimination:** Perindoprilat is excreted via urine (terminal half-life ~17 hours). Amlodipine elimination half-life is 35-50 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **EUROPA Study:** A multicentre trial of 12,218 patients showing that perindopril 10 mg (arginine) reduced the relative risk of the composite endpoint (cardiovascular mortality, non-fatal MI, cardiac arrest) by 20% over 4.2 years in patients with stable coronary artery disease without heart failure [Australian].
- **ASCOT-BPLA:** Study comparing a perindopril/amlodipine-based regimen against an atenolol/thiazide-based regimen in hypertensive patients with additional cardiovascular risk factors [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C. 
- Keep in the original container, tightly closed, to protect from moisture and light [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
