# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   **Jurisdictional Sources:** This editorial synthesis is based on Product Information (PI) documents from the **United States (FDA)**.
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview intended for healthcare professionals. 
*   **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not replace the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing decisions in any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
*   **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
*   **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
*   **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric)
*   **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion. 
    *   *Loading Dose:* In severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often administered, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours.
    *   *Maintenance:* Subsequent daily doses are generally 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilized based on clinical requirements.
*   **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    *   *Starting Dose:* 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    *   *Administration:* Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    *   *Adjustment:* Dose adjustments (e.g., reduction to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations (target range: 40 µmol/L to 50 µmol/L).

### Administration Instructions
*   Inspect parenterals visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
*   Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial.
*   **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in dialysis patients. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
*   **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
*   **Oral Formulation Caution in Renal Impairment:** The safety of **oral** levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in urine [US].
*   **Monitoring:** Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended for metabolic patients, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
*   **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not assigned/Pharmacological synthesis]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human surface area dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Animal data (dairy cows) indicate carnitine concentrations in milk increase following exogenous administration. Weigh the benefits of maternal therapy against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
*   **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was observed in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg); safety and efficacy follow adult parameters for metabolic and ESRD indications [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources; however, dose selection should generally be cautious due to the higher frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function [US].
*   **Renal Impairment:** 
    *   **Intravenous:** Used as primary therapy in ESRD patients [US].
    *   **Oral:** Not recommended in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters that are rapidly eliminated [US].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    *   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    *   **Elimination:** Following IV administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Bioavailability Study:** A study in 15 healthy volunteers demonstrated that levocarnitine tablets and oral solution are bioequivalent. Absolute bioavailability (corrected for endogenous levels) was approximately 15–16% [US].
*   **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, IV administration of 20 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis significantly increased plasma carnitine concentrations. Trough levels remained stable or increased over 8 weeks, reaching significantly higher levels compared to baseline [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
*   **Precautions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
*   **Handling:** Product does not contain preservatives; discard any unused portion after opening [US].
