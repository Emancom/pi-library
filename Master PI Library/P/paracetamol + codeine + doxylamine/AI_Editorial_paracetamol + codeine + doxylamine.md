# Editorial Product Information: paracetamol + codeine + doxylamine

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **paracetamol + codeine + doxylamine**
- Chemical class:
  - Paracetamol: analgesic/antipyretic (acetaminophen class) — chemical class not otherwise specified in available sources.
  - Codeine: opioid analgesic (phenanthrene opioid) — chemical class not otherwise specified in available sources.
  - Doxylamine: first‑generation antihistamine (ethanolamine class) — chemical class explicitly referenced as ethanolamine-class antihistamine in available sources.
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration: oral.

## 3. Approved Indications
- For the temporary relief of acute moderate pain.
- Use limited to situations where non-opioid analgesics are ineffective, not tolerated or otherwise inadequate to manage pain.

(If any jurisdictional differences in approved indications exist, they are not specified in the available sources.)

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adults and adolescents)
- Adults and children over 12 years: one to two tablets orally every 4–6 hours as required.
- Maximum: do not exceed eight tablets in any 24‑hour period.
- Do not take concurrently with other products containing paracetamol unless advised by a doctor or pharmacist.

### Dosing by indication
- Indication-specific dosing beyond the above (e.g., different regimens for particular pain types) is not specified in available sources.

### Administration instructions
- Take with water.
- Check that other concurrently taken preparations do not contain paracetamol to avoid overdose.
- Avoid alcohol while taking the product.

### Titration and discontinuation
- When initiating opioid therapy, start at the lowest appropriate dose and titrate carefully to clinical effect and tolerability.
- For patients who may be physically dependent, do not stop abruptly. Tapering is recommended; an individualized taper is required. As general guidance: reduce the dose by no more than 10–25% every 2–4 weeks, with slower reductions if withdrawal symptoms or increased pain occur.
- If opioid use disorder is suspected during tapering, consider medication-assisted treatment or specialist referral.

### Missed dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
List includes all contraindications stated in source material:
- Known hypersensitivity or idiosyncratic reaction to paracetamol, codeine phosphate, other opiates, doxylamine succinate, other ethanolamine-class antihistamines, or any excipient.
- CYP2D6 ultra-rapid metabolisers.
- Children younger than 12 years.
- Children 12–18 years in whom respiratory function might be compromised, including post‑tonsillectomy and/or adenoidectomy for obstructive sleep apnoea, obesity and pulmonary disease.
- Breastfeeding (use in lactation).
- Concomitant use of monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs) or within 14 days of stopping MAOIs.
- Impending childbirth or anticipated risk of premature birth (codeine).
- Severe respiratory disease, acute respiratory disease (e.g., acute asthma, acute exacerbations of COPD), and respiratory depression.
- Severe hepatocellular insufficiency (severe hepatic impairment).
- Glucose‑6‑phosphate‑dehydrogenase deficiency.
- Chronic constipation.
- Active alcoholism.
- Diarrhoea due to pseudomembranous colitis or poisoning until causative organism/toxin eliminated.
- Narrow‑angle glaucoma.
- Stenosing peptic ulcer.
- Symptomatic prostatic hypertrophy.
- Bladder neck obstruction.
- Pyloroduodenal obstruction.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- No formal boxed/boxed‑style warnings are specified in the available sources. The product carries several clinically significant warnings; these are presented below.

### Major/general warnings
- **Risk of life‑threatening respiratory depression**: serious, life‑threatening or fatal respiratory depression may occur, particularly on initiation and after dose increases. Increase monitoring in patients at risk (elderly, frail, renal or hepatic impairment, pre‑existing respiratory disease).
- **Hazardous and harmful use, misuse, abuse and dependence**: contains an opioid (codeine); risk of addiction, misuse, diversion, physical and psychological dependence. Assess risk prior to prescribing and monitor regularly.
- **Concomitant use with benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, other CNS depressants or alcohol**: additive CNS and respiratory depression can cause profound sedation, coma, respiratory depression and death. Reserve combined use for when alternatives are not possible; use lowest dose and shortest duration; monitor carefully.
- **Hepatotoxicity with paracetamol**: paracetamol overdose can lead to hepatic injury and hepatic failure; hepatotoxicity has occurred with large single doses and with prolonged use; risk increased with chronic alcohol use, malnutrition and pre‑existing liver disease. Check other paracetamol exposure.
- **Severe cutaneous adverse reactions**: Stevens–Johnson Syndrome and Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis have been reported with paracetamol; discontinue immediately if signs of severe skin/mucosal reaction appear.
- **CYP2D6 metabolism**: codeine is bioactivated to morphine via CYP2D6. Ultra‑rapid metabolisers may produce higher than expected morphine levels and experience opioid toxicity; poor metabolisers may have inadequate analgesia. Product is contraindicated in CYP2D6 ultra‑rapid metabolisers.
- **Paediatric risks**: contraindicated in children under 12 years; contraindicated in 12–18 years with compromised respiratory function (e.g., post adenotonsillectomy/adenoidectomy for OSA).
- **Breastfeeding**: contraindicated for use in breastfeeding women because morphine is transferred in breast milk and may cause neonatal harm.
- **QT interval prolongation**: doxylamine may potentiate QT prolongation; caution with other QT‑prolonging drugs, electrolytic disturbances (e.g., hypokalaemia) and in overdose.
- **Pyroglutamic (5‑oxoproline) acidosis / high anion gap metabolic acidosis (HAGMA)**: reported in severely ill patients and/or with predisposing factors treated with prolonged therapeutic paracetamol, particularly with concurrent flucloxacillin; monitor for unexplained metabolic acidosis.
- **Other important cautions**: use with caution in patients with impaired hepatic or renal function, decreased respiratory reserve (asthma, COPD), raised intracranial pressure or head injury, hypotension, hypothyroidism, epilepsy or convulsive disorders, benign prostatic hypertrophy, urinary retention, chronic colitis ulcerative, cholecystectomy history (sphincter of Oddi effects), and in malnourished patients.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor for signs of respiratory depression and sedation, especially on initiation and after dose increases.
- Monitor for signs of misuse, abuse or dependence, and for worsening pain or new pain patterns (possible opioid‑induced hyperalgesia).
- For prolonged use: monitor full blood count, liver function tests and renal function.
- Monitor for signs of severe skin reactions and discontinue immediately if suspected.
- In patients at risk for HAGMA, monitor blood gases and consider urinary 5‑oxoproline measurement if indicated.
- Check concomitant medications for paracetamol content prior to prescribing.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Avoid concomitant alcohol consumption.
- Avoid concomitant use with gabapentinoids unless necessary due to increased risk of respiratory depression; co‑use is not recommended.
- If concomitant CNS depressants are necessary, use lowest effective doses and monitor closely.
- Prevent accidental ingestion; store securely out of reach of children.
- When ceasing therapy in a physically dependent patient, taper slowly to avoid withdrawal; individualise tapering schedule.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Pharmacodynamic interactions / additive CNS depressant effects:
  - Concomitant use with benzodiazepines, other opioids, sedatives, hypnotics, antipsychotics, tricyclic antidepressants, antihistamines, centrally‑acting antiemetics, gabapentinoids (gabapentin, pregabalin), cannabis and alcohol may produce profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma and death. Limit dosages and duration; use the lowest effective doses and monitor closely. Concomitant use with gabapentinoids is not recommended.
  - Doxylamine may potentiate QT prolongation; caution with other QT‑prolonging drugs and in patients with risk factors for QT prolongation (e.g., hypokalaemia).
- Pharmacokinetic interactions:
  - CYP2D6 metabolism: Codeine is metabolised to morphine by CYP2D6. CYP2D6 inhibitors may reduce analgesic efficacy; CYP2D6 ultra‑rapid metabolism increases risk of morphine toxicity. Product is contraindicated in CYP2D6 ultra‑rapid metabolisers.
  - MAOIs: Concomitant use with monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs) or within 14 days of stopping MAOIs is contraindicated.
  - Paracetamol + flucloxacillin: prolonged concomitant use has been associated with pyroglutamic acidosis (HAGMA) in susceptible patients.
- Other clinically significant interactions and management notes:
  - Avoid alcohol: active alcohol consumption (three or more drinks daily) increases risk of liver damage with paracetamol.
  - When combined CNS depressants are necessary, reduce doses and monitor for signs of sedation and respiratory compromise.
  - Before prescribing, review all patient medications for paracetamol content to avoid unintentional overdose.

(Region‑specific interaction differences are not specified in available sources.)

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Use in pregnancy: Not specified in available sources (no pregnancy category/system details provided).
- Codeine: contraindicated in the event of impending childbirth or when premature birth is anticipated (risk of neonatal withdrawal and neonatal respiratory depression noted).

### Breastfeeding
- Contraindicated in breastfeeding women. Morphine (active metabolite of codeine) is excreted in breast milk and may cause life‑threatening adverse reactions in breastfed infants, particularly in mothers who are CYP2D6 ultra‑rapid metabolisers.

### Fertility
- Fertility data: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Contraindicated in children younger than 12 years.
- Contraindicated in children aged 12–18 years in whom respiratory function might be compromised (including post‑tonsillectomy/adenoidectomy for obstructive sleep apnoea), obesity and pulmonary disease due to increased risk of serious, life‑threatening adverse reactions.
- Analgesic efficacy may be reduced in CYP2D6 poor metabolisers; risk of toxicity in ultra‑rapid metabolisers affects children particularly.

### Elderly
- Elderly patients are at increased risk of respiratory depression, sedation and other opioid adverse events. Use with caution, start at low doses and monitor closely. Specific dose adjustments are not specified in available sources.

### Renal impairment
- Use with caution in patients with renal impairment. Specific dosing adjustments by eGFR/stage are not specified in available sources.

### Hepatic impairment
- Contraindicated in severe hepatocellular insufficiency. Use with caution in mild to moderate hepatic impairment; monitor liver function. Specific dose adjustments by severity are not specified in available sources.
