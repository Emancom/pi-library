# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is designed to synthesise clinical data into a coherent reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labelling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP)
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Generic) [US]
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not explicitly stated in source, but standard classification)
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Administer by intravenous injection only.
- Parenteral products should be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use [US].
- Compatible with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) and stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg given as a slow (2 to 3 minute) bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often used in severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the next 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 to 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Monitor weekly/monthly, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Slow (2 to 3 minute) bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations (normal range: 40–50 µmol/L). Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and consider risks/benefits before re-administration [US].
- **Seizures:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **TMA/TMAO Accumulation:** Chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide). The safety of the oral formulation in renal insufficiency has not been evaluated [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (ESRD Trials)
The following effects occurred at a frequency ≥5% in placebo-controlled trials of ESRD patients on hemodialysis [US]:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (21% at 10mg/kg), injection site reaction (up to 38%), pain (up to 35%), headache (up to 37%), fever, flu syndrome.
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (up to 19%), tachycardia, chest pain.
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (up to 35%), vomiting (up to 21%), nausea (up to 12%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (up to 27%), cough increase (up to 18%), dyspnea.
- **Neurological/Other:** Dizziness (up to 18%), paresthesia, anemia, hypercalcemia.

### Post-Marketing Experience
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent body odor and gastritis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating treatment or after dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category C]. Reproductive studies in rats/rabbits (3.8x human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. However, no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women exist. Use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine in milk. Weigh the benefits to the mother against potential risks to the infant; consider discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is based on mg/kg body weight (refer to Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in provided source [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. High-dose oral therapy is cautioned against due to metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg three times weekly), mean pre-dialysis (trough) levocarnitine levels increased from ~20 µmol/L at baseline to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks. Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) showed dose-proportional increases in trough concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the effect on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, cardiomyopathy) in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of opened vials as the product contains no preservative [US].

- **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription Only) [US].
