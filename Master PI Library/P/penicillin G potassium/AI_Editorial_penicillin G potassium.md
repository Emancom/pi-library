# Editorial Product Information: Penicillin G Potassium

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a single, clinically focused reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Penicillin G potassium (a natural penicillin).
- **Brand Names:** Buffered Penicillin G Potassium for Injection, USP. 
- **ATC Code:** J01CE01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Powder for reconstitution in vials containing 5,000,000 units (5 million units) [US].
    - Powder for reconstitution in vials containing 20,000,000 units (20 million units) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion (continuous or intermittent) and Intramuscular (IM) injection. High doses (20 million units) must be administered by IV infusion only [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Distributed by HF Acquisition Co LLC (dba HealthFirst); manufactured by Sagent Pharmaceuticals [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
Penicillin G potassium is indicated for the treatment of serious infections caused by susceptible organisms. [US-approved indications]:
- **Septicemia, empyema, pneumonia, pericarditis, endocarditis, and meningitis:** Caused by susceptible *Streptococcus pyogenes* (Group A), other beta-hemolytic streptococci, *S. pneumoniae*, and *Staphylococcus* species (non-penicillinase producing).
- **Anthrax:** Caused by *Bacillus anthracis*.
- **Actinomycosis:** (Cervico-facial, thoracic, and abdominal) caused by *Actinomyces israelii*.
- **Clostridial Infections:** Adjunctive therapy for botulism, gas gangrene, and tetanus.
- **Diphtheria:** Adjunctive therapy to antitoxin and for prevention of the carrier state.
- **Erysipeloid Endocarditis:** Caused by *Erysipelothrix rhusiopathiae*.
- **Fusospirochetosis:** (Vincent’s angina) infections of the oropharynx and lower respiratory tract.
- **Listeria Infections:** Including meningitis and endocarditis caused by *Listeria monocytogenes*.
- **Pasteurella Infections:** Including bacteremia and meningitis caused by *Pasteurella multocida*.
- **Haverhill Fever and Rat-bite Fever:** Caused by *Streptobacillus moniliformis* or *Spirillum minus*.
- **Gonococcal Infections:** Disseminated infections caused by penicillin-susceptible *Neisseria gonorrhoeae*.
- **Syphilis:** Including congenital, neurosyphilis, and symptomatic late syphilis.
- **Meningococcal Meningitis/Septicemia:** Caused by *Neisseria meningitidis*.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
Penicillin G is typically administered in divided doses every 4 to 6 hours due to its short half-life.
- **Serious Streptococcal Infections:** 12 to 24 million units/day in divided doses every 4 to 6 hours.
- **Anthrax:** Minimum 8 million units/day in divided doses every 6 hours; higher doses may be required.
- **Syphilis (Neurosyphilis):** 12 to 24 million units/day (administered as 2 to 4 million units every 4 hours) for 10 to 14 days.
- **Meningococcal Meningitis:** 24 million units/day administered as 2 million units every 2 hours.

### Administration Instructions
- **Intramuscular:** The volume of injection should be kept small. Concentrations up to 100,000 units/mL are recommended to minimise discomfort.
- **Intravenous:** For high doses (e.g., 10 million units or more), administration should be slow to avoid electrolyte imbalance.
- **Reconstitution:** Use Sterile Water for Injection or Isotonic Sodium Chloride. Reconstituted solutions are stable for 7 days under refrigeration (2°C to 8°C).

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** A history of a hypersensitivity (anaphylactic) reaction to any penicillin is a strict contraindication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Anaphylaxis:** Serious and occasionally fatal hypersensitivity reactions have been reported. Cross-sensitivity may occur with cephalosporins.
- **Electrolyte Imbalance:** Each million units contains 1.68 mEq of potassium and 0.3 mEq of sodium. High-dose therapy (>10 million units) may lead to fatal hyperkalemia or sodium loading, particularly in patients with renal or cardiac disease [US].
- **Clostridium difficile-Associated Diarrhea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all antibacterial agents; severity may range from mild diarrhea to fatal colitis.
- **Neurological Toxicity:** Massive IV doses can cause neurotoxicity (hyperreflexia, myoclonic twitches, seizures, coma), especially in patients with renal impairment [US].
- **Superinfection:** Prolonged use may promote the overgrowth of non-susceptible organisms, including fungi.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, urticaria, pruritus, angioneurotic edema, serum sickness-like reactions (fever, malaise, arthralgia), and various skin rashes.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Pseudomembranous colitis, nausea, vomiting, and stomatitis.
- **Hematologic:** Neutropenia (resolves on discontinuation), Coombs-positive hemolytic anemia (associated with doses >10 million units/day), and bleeding diathesis secondary to platelet dysfunction.
- **Metabolic:** Hyperkalemia (with high-dose IV potassium salt).
- **Renal:** Interstitial nephritis and renal tubular damage (associated with high doses).
- **Neurological:** Agitation, confusion, asterixis, hallucinations, and encephalopathy (with massive doses).
- **Systemic (Jarisch-Herxheimer Reaction):** Characterised by fever, chills, and headache; occurs within 1-2 hours of initiating treatment for syphilis or other spirochetal infections [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Bacteriostatic Antibiotics:** (e.g., chloramphenicol, erythromycins, tetracyclines) may antagonise the bactericidal effect of penicillin; concurrent use should generally be avoided.
- **Probenecid:** Competitively inhibits renal tubular secretion, prolonging penicillin serum levels.
- **Other Drugs:** Aspirin, sulfonamides, indomethacin, and thiazide diuretics may also compete for renal secretion, increasing the half-life of penicillin [US].
- **Laboratory Interferences:**
    - False-positive glucose reaction in urine with copper reduction tests (Benedict’s, Fehling’s, Clinitest®).
    - Pseudoproteinuria with certain test methods.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category B]. Animal studies show no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Human data has not shown positive evidence of adverse effects; however, it should be used during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Penicillins are excreted in human milk; caution is advised when administering to nursing women.
- **Fertility:** No long-term animal studies on carcinogenesis or mutagenesis are available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Neonates have incompletely developed renal function, delaying penicillin elimination. Dosage should be calculated by weight (e.g., 150,000 to 300,000 units/kg/day for serious infections) [US]. Product should not be used if the required dose is less than 1 million units per dose [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Dose selection should be cautious (starting at the low end) due to the higher frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function. Monitoring renal function is recommended.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosage adjustments are required for severe impairment:
    - **CrCl <10 mL/min/1.73m²:** Full loading dose followed by half the loading dose every 8 to 10 hours.
    - **Uremic patients (CrCl >10 mL/min/1.73m²):** Full loading dose followed by half the loading dose every 4 to 5 hours.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Additional dose modifications are required when hepatic disease accompanies renal impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal; inhibits the biosynthesis of cell-wall mucopeptides during active multiplication.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak serum concentrations are achieved immediately following IV infusion.
    - **Distribution:** Distributed to most body tissues and placenta. Penetration into CSF, eye, and prostate is poor unless inflammation (meningitis) is present.
    - **Metabolism/Elimination:** Predominantly renal (60-90% via tubular secretion). Half-life is approximately 42 minutes in healthy adults, but can extend up to 20 hours in anuric patients.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Highly active against most streptococci and *N. meningitis*. It is inactivated by penicillinase-producing bacteria (e.g., most *Staphylococci*).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Formal pivotal trial summaries are not provided in the standard PI for this well-established monograph drug [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Dry Powder:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Must be refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F). Discard any unused solution after 7 days [US].
