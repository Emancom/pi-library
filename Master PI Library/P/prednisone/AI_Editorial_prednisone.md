# Editorial Product Information: Prednisone

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on Product Information (PI) documents from the Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA, Australia) for **Panafcort** and the Food and Drug Administration (FDA, United States) for **Prednisone Tablets USP**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating global clinical perspectives.
- This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Prednisone (a synthetic glucocorticoid).
- **Brand Names:** **Panafcort** [TGA]; Generic prednisone tablets [FDA].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in provided sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Australia [TGA]:** 1 mg, 5 mg, and 25 mg tablets.
    - **United States [FDA]:** 1 mg, 2.5 mg, 5 mg, 10 mg, and 20 mg tablets.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** 
    - **Australia:** Aspen Pharmacare Australia Pty Ltd.
    - **United States:** Bryant Ranch Prepack / Endo USA.

## 2. Approved Indications
Prednisone is indicated for a wide range of endocrine, rheumatic, collagen, dermatologic, allergic, ophthalmic, respiratory, hematologic, neoplastic, and gastrointestinal disorders including:
- **Endocrine:** Primary/secondary adrenocortical insufficiency; congenital adrenal hyperplasia; nonsuppurative thyroiditis; hypercalcemia associated with cancer [FDA].
- **Rheumatic/Musculoskeletal:** Adjunctive therapy for acute episodes of rheumatoid arthritis (including juvenile), ankylosing spondylitis, psoriatic arthritis, acute gouty arthritis, bursitis, and epicondylitis. Specific Australian listings include periarthritis of the shoulder [TGA].
- **Collagen Diseases:** Systemic lupus erythematosus; acute rheumatic carditis; systemic dermatomyositis.
- **Dermatologic:** Pemphigus vulgaris; bullous dermatitis herpetiformis; severe erythema multiforme (Stevens-Johnson syndrome); exfoliative dermatitis; mycosis fungoides; severe psoriasis.
- **Allergic States:** Severe/intractable bronchial asthma; allergic rhinitis; contact/atopic dermatitis; serum sickness; drug hypersensitivity.
- **Ophthalmic:** Severe acute/chronic allergic and inflammatory processes (e.g., iritis, chorioretinitis, optic neuritis [FDA—note: TGA/FDA differ on optic neuritis recommendation, see Section 5]).
- **Respiratory:** Symptomatic sarcoidosis; Loeffler’s syndrome; fulminating/disseminated pulmonary tuberculosis (with concurrent antituberculous therapy); berylliosis [FDA].
- **Hematologic:** Idiopathic/secondary thrombocytopenia in adults; acquired hemolytic anemia; erythroblastopenia.
- **Neoplastic:** Palliative management of leukemias and lymphomas.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Ulcerative colitis; regional enteritis.
- **Other:** Multiple sclerosis (acute exacerbations) [FDA]; nephrotic syndrome; tuberculous meningitis [FDA]; trichinosis with neurologic/myocardial involvement [FDA].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
Dosage must be individualized based on disease severity and patient response.
- **Adults:** Initial dosage ranges from 5 mg to 60 mg daily [FDA]. The TGA notes a typical range of 20 to 40 mg daily, which may increase to 60–80 mg if necessary.
- **Maintenance:** Should be the lowest dose that maintains adequate clinical response. Maintenance typically ranges from 5 mg to 20 mg daily [TGA].
- **Multiple Sclerosis (Acute Exacerbation):** 200 mg daily for one week, followed by 80 mg every other day for one month [FDA].

### Administration Instructions
- Administer with food or milk to reduce gastric irritation [FDA].
- **Timing:** Single daily doses should be taken in the morning (prior to 9 am) to coincide with the natural cortisol peak and minimize HPA axis suppression [FDA/TGA].
- **Tablets:** Despite being scored, the 1 mg tablet should not be broken [TGA].

### Alternate-Day Therapy (ADT)
ADT is recommended for long-term treatment to minimize HPA axis suppression and growth retardation in children. A single dose (twice the usual daily dose) is administered every other morning.

### Titration and Withdrawal
Dosage should be reduced gradually when therapy exceeds a few days to avoid acute adrenal insufficiency.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to prednisone, prednisolone, or any excipients.
- Systemic fungal infections [FDA].
- Uncontrolled infections [TGA].
- Administration of live or live-attenuated vaccines in patients receiving immunosuppressive doses [FDA].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **HPA Axis Suppression:** Prolonged use may result in secondary adrenocortical insufficiency, which may persist for months after withdrawal. Stress during this period requires reinstitution or increased doses of corticosteroids.
- **Scleroderma Renal Crisis:** Increased incidence (possibly fatal) observed with daily doses ≥15 mg in patients with systemic sclerosis. Monitor blood pressure and renal function [TGA].
- **Infection:** Corticosteroids may mask signs of infection, activate latent amebiasis, or reactivate tuberculosis. Avoid use in cerebral malaria [FDA].
- **Thyrotoxic Periodic Paralysis (TPP):** Risk in patients with hyperthyroidism and prednisone-induced hypokalaemia; monitor potassium levels [TGA].
- **Ophthalmic Effects:** Prolonged use may cause cataracts, glaucoma, or central serous chorioretinopathy. Use in active ocular herpes simplex is discouraged due to potential corneal perforation [FDA]. Note: FDA does not recommend oral steroids for optic neuritis [FDA].
- **Cardiovascular:** Use with caution post-myocardial infarction (risk of left ventricular free wall rupture) and in patients with congestive heart failure or hypertension.
- **Musculoskeletal:** Increased risk of osteoporosis and avascular necrosis. Growth and development in children must be monitored.
- **Neuro-Psychiatric:** Potential for euphoria, insomnia, mood swings, personality changes, and frank psychosis.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally related to dose and duration of therapy.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, peptic ulcer (with possible perforation/haemorrhage), pancreatitis, ulcerative oesophagitis, abdominal distention.
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension, salt/water retention, potassium loss, bradycardia (following high doses) [TGA], cardiac arrest.
- **Endocrine/Metabolic:** Cushingoid state (moon face, buffalo hump), weight gain, glucose intolerance, steroid diabetes, growth retardation in children, menstrual irregularities.
- **Dermatologic:** Impaired wound healing, skin thinning, petechiae, ecchymoses, acne, hirsutism, striae, panniculitis (frequency unknown) [TGA].
- **Musculoskeletal:** Osteoporosis, vertebral fractures, muscle weakness, aseptic necrosis of femoral/humeral heads, tendon rupture (particularly Achilles) [FDA].
- **Neurological/Psychiatric:** Vertigo, headache, convulsions, depression, emotional instability.
- **Ophthalmic:** Posterior subcapsular cataracts, increased intraocular pressure, glaucoma, blurred vision.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Enzyme Inducers (e.g., Phenytoin, Rifampicin, Barbiturates):** May increase prednisone clearance, necessitating a dose increase.
- **Enzyme Inhibitors (e.g., Ketoconazole, Macrolide Antibiotics):** May decrease clearance; ketoconazole can decrease metabolism by up to 60% [FDA].
- **Antidiabetics:** Corticosteroids increase blood glucose; dosage adjustments of insulin or oral agents may be required.
- **NSAIDs/Aspirin:** Increased risk of gastrointestinal ulceration. Aspirin should be used cautiously in patients with hypoprothrombinemia [FDA].
- **Potassium-Depleting Agents (e.g., Diuretics, Amphotericin B):** Increased risk of hypokalaemia.
- **Anticholinesterases (e.g., Neostigmine):** May produce severe weakness in patients with myasthenia gravis.
- **Oral Anticoagulants (Warfarin):** May inhibit or enhance anticoagulant response; monitor INR/prothrombin time frequently.
- **Fluoroquinolones:** Increased risk of tendon rupture, especially in the elderly [FDA].
- **Quetiapine:** Prednisone may decrease quetiapine plasma concentrations [FDA].
- **Vaccines:** Diminished response to toxoids and live/inactivated vaccines.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australia:** Category A (Drugs taken by a large number of pregnant women without proven increase in malformations).
    - **United States:** Category C [FDA].
    - Animal studies show increased incidence of cleft palate. Use only if potential benefit outweighs risk to the fetus; observe infants for signs of hypoadrenalism.
- **Breastfeeding:** Prednisone is excreted in breast milk. High doses may suppress growth or interfere with endogenous corticosteroid production. TGA recommends against use in nursing mothers; FDA recommends a risk-benefit assessment.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric:** Requires strict monitoring for growth retardation and HPA axis suppression. Alternate-day therapy is the preferred regimen.
- **Elderly:** Start at the low end of the dosing range due to higher frequency of hepatic, renal, or cardiac dysfunction and concomitant disease.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Metabolism is decreased in patients with cirrhosis [FDA]. In chronic active liver disease, major adverse reactions occur in approximately 30% of patients [TGA].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Prednisone is a synthetic glucocorticoid that reacts with receptor proteins in the cytoplasm to form a steroid-receptor complex, which then influences protein synthesis to produce anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive effects.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Readily absorbed from the GI tract.
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly converted in the liver to the active metabolite, **prednisolone**. Preconversion biological half-life is approximately 60 minutes [TGA].
    - **Elimination:** Plasma half-life is 3–4 hours. Excreted primarily in urine [TGA].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **General:** Specific pivotal trial data is not detailed in the available sources.
- **Multiple Sclerosis:** Efficacy in speeding the resolution of acute exacerbations is noted, though it does not affect the ultimate outcome or natural history of the disease [FDA].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Australia [TGA]:** Store below 30°C.
- **United States [FDA]:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Dispense in tight, light-resistant containers [FDA].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Poison Schedule S4 (Prescription Animal Remedy/Prescription Only Medicine).
- **United States:** Rx-only.
