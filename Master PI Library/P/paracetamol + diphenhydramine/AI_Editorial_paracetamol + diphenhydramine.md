# Editorial Product Information: paracetamol + diphenhydramine

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **paracetamol + diphenhydramine**
  - Strength example from available source: paracetamol 500 mg + diphenhydramine hydrochloride 25 mg per unit (strength reported in source).
- Chemical class:
  - Paracetamol: p-aminophenol derivative with analgesic and antipyretic activity.
  - Diphenhydramine: centrally and peripherally acting H1‑receptor antagonist; lipophilic molecule with anticholinergic activity (centrally active; crosses blood–brain barrier).
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration: Oral.

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources.
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources.
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- For temporary relief of pain when associated with sleeping difficulty (examples listed in the source):
  - Headache, migraine
  - Backache
  - Arthritis, rheumatic and muscle pain
  - Neuralgia
  - Toothache
  - Period pain
- Relief of fever.
- Note: Indications as listed above are from the available source. Regional differences in approved indications were not provided in the available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Adults and children over 12 years: 1–2 units orally with water (or other fluid) only at bedtime.
- Maximum: two units in any 24‑hour period. Do not exceed the stated dose.
- Do not use in children under 12 years of age.

### Concomitant paracetamol-containing products
- **Do not use with any other paracetamol-containing products.** The total daily intake of paracetamol must not exceed 4,000 mg in any 24‑hour period when other paracetamol products are used during the day.
- Allow at least four hours between taking any other paracetamol-containing product and this product, where applicable.

### Duration of use
- For adults: paracetamol should not be taken for more than a few days at a time except on medical advice.
- For children: paracetamol should not be taken for more than 48 hours except on medical advice.
- Do not take for more than 3 days without consulting a doctor. If symptoms persist, seek medical advice.

### Method of administration
- Oral use only.

### Titration / Missed dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
List includes all contraindications reported in the available source:
- Hypersensitivity to paracetamol, diphenhydramine hydrochloride, or any excipient.
- Severe hepatocellular insufficiency.
- Not for use in children 12 years of age and younger.
- Diphenhydramine-specific contraindications:
  - Newborns or premature infants.
  - Lactating women (contraindicated).
  - Patients receiving monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs).
- Cautionary/condition-related items listed as contraindications in source (included here as explicitly stated contraindications): Not specified beyond those above. (Conditions such as narrow-angle glaucoma, stenosing peptic ulcer, symptomatic prostatic hypertrophy, bladder neck obstruction, pyloroduodenal obstruction are listed in the source under cautions/precautions rather than as absolute contraindications and therefore appear below in Warnings and Precautions.)

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources as a boxed/black‑box warning. (Serious risks such as severe hepatic injury and life‑threatening cutaneous reactions are described in warnings below.)

### General warnings and precautions
- **Contains paracetamol. Do not co-administer with other paracetamol-containing products** to avoid overdose.
- Severe cutaneous adverse reactions (SCARs): Life‑threatening skin reactions such as Stevens–Johnson syndrome (SJS) and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN) have been reported. Patients should be advised to stop treatment immediately and seek medical attention if progressive rash, blisters, or mucosal lesions occur.
- Hepatic impairment:
  - Contraindicated in severe hepatocellular insufficiency.
  - Use with caution in mild-to-moderate hepatocellular insufficiency. Underlying liver disease increases the risk of paracetamol-related liver damage.
  - Hepatotoxicity may occur with paracetamol even at therapeutic doses, after short treatment duration, and in patients without known liver disease.
- Renal impairment:
  - Use with caution in patients with impaired renal function, including severe renal insufficiency.
- High anion gap metabolic acidosis (HAGMA) due to pyroglutamic (5-oxoproline) acidosis:
  - Cases reported in patients with severe illness (e.g., severe renal impairment, sepsis), malnutrition, chronic alcoholism, or other low glutathione states treated with paracetamol at therapeutic doses for prolonged periods or with concurrent flucloxacillin. If suspected, discontinue paracetamol and monitor; urinary 5‑oxoproline measurement may assist diagnosis.
- Conditions requiring caution (may exacerbate anticholinergic or other effects):
  - Narrow-angle glaucoma
  - Stenosing peptic ulcer
  - Symptomatic prostatic hypertrophy
  - Bladder neck obstruction
  - Pyloroduodenal obstruction
  - Epilepsy or seizure disorders
  - Myasthenia gravis
  - Urinary retention
  - Asthma, bronchitis, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD)
- Chronic alcohol use, malnutrition, sepsis and other depleted glutathione states increase risk of paracetamol toxicity and metabolic complications.
- Diphenhydramine may cause drowsiness and potentiate effects of alcohol and other CNS depressants (e.g., tranquilizers, hypnotics, anxiolytics, opioids). Drowsiness may persist to the following day. Avoid driving or operating machinery if affected.
- Potential for abuse and dependence is low but cases reported, particularly in patients with psychotic disorders or prior substance abuse.
- Use with caution with other antimuscarinic/anticholinergic agents (e.g., atropine, tricyclic antidepressants) because of potentiation of anticholinergic effects.
- Avoid use with other antihistamine-containing preparations, including cough and cold medicines and topical antihistamines.
- Monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs) or within 2 weeks of stopping an MAOI: diphenhydramine may interact and cause severe anticholinergic and CNS depressive effects.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor for signs of skin reactions; discontinue immediately if SJS/TEN suspected.
- Patients on warfarin or other antivitamin K anticoagulants: monitor coagulation (INR) and for bleeding if paracetamol is used for prolonged periods.
- Consider urinary 5‑oxoproline measurement when HAGMA due to pyroglutamic acidosis is suspected.
- In suspected overdose: measure plasma paracetamol concentration at ≥4 hours after ingestion; monitor liver function tests and coagulation parameters.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Keep out of the reach of children.
- Do not take more than recommended dose; immediate medical attention required in overdose because of risk of delayed severe liver damage.
- Use during pregnancy only on medical advice; use during lactation is not recommended (see Section 9).

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions (paracetamol-related)
- Warfarin and other antivitamin K anticoagulants: paracetamol may increase bleeding risk; prolonged, regular paracetamol use may require reduction of anticoagulant dose and monitoring of coagulation (INR).
- Flucloxacillin: concomitant use associated with cases of high anion gap metabolic acidosis due to pyroglutamic acidosis, especially in patients with risk factors.
- Chelating resin (e.g., activated charcoal/resins): decrease intestinal absorption of paracetamol — separate by >2 hours if possible.
- Gastric motility modifiers:
  - Increased paracetamol absorption with agents that increase gastric emptying (e.g., metoclopramide, domperidone).
  - Decreased paracetamol absorption with agents that decrease gastric emptying (e.g., propantheline, antidepressants with anticholinergic properties, opioid analgesics).
- Chloramphenicol: paracetamol may increase chloramphenicol concentrations.
- Hepatotoxic drugs and enzyme inducers: concomitant use with potentially hepatotoxic drugs or CYP‑inducing antiepileptics (phenobarbital, phenytoin, carbamazepine, topiramate), rifampicin, and alcohol may increase risk of paracetamol toxicity by inducing metabolic activation to the hepatotoxic metabolite.
- Probenecid: may affect paracetamol excretion and plasma concentrations.
- Colestyramine: reduces paracetamol absorption if administered within 1 hour of paracetamol.

### Pharmacokinetic interactions (diphenhydramine-related)
- Diphenhydramine is an inhibitor of CYP2D6: potential interaction with drugs primarily metabolised by CYP2D6 (examples provided in source: metoprolol, venlafaxine) — may increase plasma concentrations of such drugs.
- Avoid concomitant use with other antihistamines (systemic or topical) and cough/cold medicines containing antihistamines.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- CNS depressants (alcohol, sedatives, opioid analgesics, hypnotics): additive or synergistic sedation with diphenhydramine.
- Monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs) and tricyclic antidepressants (TCAs): may potentiate anticholinergic and CNS depressive effects of diphenhydramine; combined use may prolong/intensify effects.
- Other anticholinergic agents: additive anticholinergic adverse effects (tachycardia, dry mouth, blurred vision, urinary retention, constipation, etc.).

### Clinically significant interactions and management notes
- If used with warfarin: monitor INR and bleeding if paracetamol use is prolonged.
- Avoid concurrent paracetamol-containing products to prevent overdose.
- Exercise caution and monitor when combining with agents that increase hepatic enzyme activity, other hepatotoxins, or in patients with risk factors for paracetamol toxicity.
- Avoid or use caution when combining diphenhydramine with other sedating or anticholinergic medications; counsel patient regarding increased sedation and impairment of psychomotor skills.
- When potential CYP2D6-mediated interactions are possible (e.g., metoprolol, venlafaxine), consider monitoring for increased effects or dose adjustment of the concomitant medication.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- **[Australian]** Classification reported in available source: Category A.
- Both paracetamol and diphenhydramine have been taken by large numbers of pregnant women and women of childbearing age without proven increase in malformations or other direct/indirect harmful effects observed in available data. Paracetamol can be used during pregnancy if clinically needed; use the lowest effective dose for the shortest possible time and at the lowest possible frequency.
- Use of sedating antihistamines (diphenhydramine) during the third trimester may result in reactions in the newborn or premature neonates.
- This product should not be used during pregnancy without medical advice.

### Breastfeeding
- The product is not recommended for use while breastfeeding in the available source.
- Paracetamol: excreted in small amounts (<0.2%) in breast milk; not considered clinically significant at usual analgesic doses.
- Diphenhydramine: excreted in breast milk; use during breastfeeding is not recommended unless benefits to the mother outweigh potential risk to the infant.

### Fertility
- No data available in the available source.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Children under 12 years: product not for use / not recommended.
- Children may experience paradoxical excitation with diphenhydramine.

### Elderly
- Elderly patients may be more susceptible to central nervous system depressive effects including confusion and paradoxical excitation; paradoxical excitation can also occur in elderly. Use with caution; product should not be taken by elderly patients with confusion and paradoxical excitation.

### Renal impairment
- Use with caution in patients with impaired renal function including severe renal insufficiency.
- Specific dose adjustments by eGFR/stage: Not specified in available sources.

### Hepatic impairment
- Contraindicated in severe hepatocellular insufficiency.
- Use with caution in mild-to-moderate hepatocellular insufficiency.
- Specific dose adjustments according to degree of hepatic impairment: Not specified in available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Paracetamol: a p‑aminophenol derivative with analgesic and antipyretic effects; analgesia thought to be mediated by central inhibition of prostaglandin synthesis. Lacks significant peripheral anti‑inflammatory activity.
- Diphenhydramine: H1‑receptor antagonist that competes with histamine at central and peripheral H1 receptor sites; also exerts anticholinergic effects and interacts with serotonergic (5‑HT) and α‑adrenergic receptors. Highly lipophilic and crosses the blood–brain barrier; reduces sleep onset and increases depth/quality of sleep.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption:
  - Paracetamol: readily absorbed from GI tract; peak plasma concentrations ~10–60 minutes after oral administration.
  - Diphenhydramine hydrochloride: well absorbed orally; peak plasma concentrations ~1–4 hours after administration; sedative effect maximal within ~1–3 hours.
- Distribution:
  - Paracetamol: distributed into most tissues; plasma protein binding negligible at therapeutic doses; elimination half‑life approx. 1–3 hours.
  - Diphenhydramine: widely distributed including CNS; ~80–85% plasma protein binding.
- Metabolism:
  - Paracetamol: extensively hepatic metabolism to inactive glucuronide and sulfate conjugates; minor pathway yields hepatotoxic intermediate detoxified by glutathione.
  - Diphenhydramine: extensively metabolised in liver by multiple CYP enzymes, including CYP2D6, principally to diphenylmethoxyacetic acid and dealkylated metabolites; significant first‑pass metabolism.
- Elimination:
  - Paracetamol: mainly urinary excretion of conjugated metabolites; <5% excreted unchanged.
  - Diphenhydramine: excreted mainly as metabolites in urine; little (<1%) unchanged drug in urine; elimination half‑life reported ~2.4–9.3 hours in healthy adults.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Diphenhydramine produces sedation and can impair cognitive and psychomotor performance; effect corresponds with plasma concentrations.
