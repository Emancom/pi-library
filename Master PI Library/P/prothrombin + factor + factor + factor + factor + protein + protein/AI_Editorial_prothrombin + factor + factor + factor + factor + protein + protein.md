# Editorial Product Information: prothrombin + factor + factor + factor + factor + protein + protein

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **prothrombin + factor + factor + factor + factor + protein + protein**
- Chemical class: Human plasma‑derived, purified, virus‑inactivated, non‑activated Prothrombin Complex Concentrate (PCC) containing vitamin K–dependent coagulation factors II, VII, IX and X and anticoagulant Proteins C and S.
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration: Intravenous (after reconstitution).

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Initial U.S. approval 2023.  
- Date of last revision/update of source PI: Revised: 7/2023.
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.
- If other jurisdictional registration dates or PI revision dates are required: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Urgent reversal of acquired coagulation factor deficiency induced by vitamin K antagonist (VKA, e.g., warfarin) therapy in adult patients who require an urgent surgery or invasive procedure. [US]

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General dosing (adult)
- Individualize dosing using the patient’s most recent pre‑dose International Normalized Ratio (INR) and body weight. Dosing is expressed in International Units (IU) of Factor IX potency.
- Recommended dosing by pre‑treatment INR:
  - INR 2 to <4: 25 IU of Factor IX/kg body weight
  - INR 4 to 6: 35 IU of Factor IX/kg body weight
  - INR >6: 50 IU of Factor IX/kg body weight
- Maximum total Factor IX units per dose (based on potency): do not exceed
  - INR 2–<4: 2500 IU
  - INR 4–6: 3500 IU
  - INR >6: 5000 IU
- Dosing should be based on actual potency stated on the vial. Dose is based on actual potency up to a body weight of 100 kg; for patients >100 kg, do not exceed the stated maximum total IU per dose.

### Dosing by indication
- Indication-specific dosing provided only for urgent reversal of VKA anticoagulation prior to urgent surgery/invasive procedure (see above). No other approved indications described in available sources.

### Administration instructions
- Reconstitute the lyophilized product with sterile Water for Injection (sWFI) using aseptic technique and the supplied transfer device. Reconstitution typically completes within 1–5 minutes at room temperature (20–25°C).
- The reconstituted solution should be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration; only clear solutions that may be colorless to slightly blue should be used.
- As the product contains no preservatives, administer reconstituted product immediately or within 8 hours at room temperature provided sterility is maintained.
- If more than one vial is required for a patient dose, contents of multiple vials may be pooled prior to administration provided sterility is maintained; use a separate sterile transfer device for each vial.
- Do not mix with other medicinal products. Administer through a separate infusion line. The infusion line may be flushed with normal saline before and after administration.
- Do not allow blood to enter the syringe or tubing due to risk of fibrin clot formation.
- Infusion rate: administer at 0.12 mL/kg/min (approximately 3 IU/kg/min of Factor IX), up to a maximum of 8.4 mL/min (approximately 210 IU/min of Factor IX).
- The safety and effectiveness of repeat dosing have not been established and repeat dosing is not recommended.

### Titration schedules
- Not specified in available sources beyond weight/INR‑based single‑dose recommendations above.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not applicable for single‑use urgent reversal dosing; Not specified in available sources for repeat/multiple dosing scenarios.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known anaphylactic or severe systemic hypersensitivity reactions to **prothrombin + factor + factor + factor + factor + protein + protein** or any component of the product.
- Known allergy to heparin or history of heparin‑induced thrombocytopenia (HIT).
- IgA‑deficient patients with known anti‑IgA antibodies.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed warning
- **Arterial and venous thromboembolic complications have been reported; weigh benefit of urgent VKA reversal against thromboembolic risk, and consider resumption of anticoagulation as soon as clinically appropriate.** [US]

### General warnings and precautions
- Hypersensitivity/anaphylactic reactions: discontinue infusion immediately if severe allergic or anaphylactic‑type reactions occur and initiate appropriate treatment.
- Thromboembolic risk/complications:
  - Both fatal and non‑fatal arterial and venous thromboembolic events have been reported in clinical trials and postmarketing experience.
  - There is a risk of thrombosis or disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC) when treating acquired deficiency with human prothrombin complex concentrates. Observe patients closely for signs or symptoms of thrombosis or DIC.
  - The product may not be suitable in patients with thrombotic or thromboembolic events within the prior 3 months.
  - Carefully consider timing of resumption of anticoagulation and use vitamin K to maintain vitamin K‑dependent clotting factor levels once the effect of the PCC has diminished.
- Infectious agents: as a plasma‑derived product, there remains a residual risk of transmission of infectious agents (viruses, variant Creutzfeldt‑Jakob disease agent and, theoretically, Creutzfeldt‑Jakob disease agent), including unknown or emerging pathogens, despite donor screening and validated viral reduction steps.
- Administration cautions:
  - Do not mix with other medicinal products.
  - Avoid blood contamination of syringes/lines.
  - Use aseptic technique during reconstitution and administration.

### Monitoring requirements
- Measure INR immediately prior to dosing (close to the time of dosing) and monitor INR and clinical response during and after treatment.
- Monitor for signs and symptoms of thromboembolism (e.g., limb pain/swelling, chest pain, neurological deficits), DIC, allergic reaction, and for signs of infection potentially related to plasma‑derived products.
- Observe for surgical bleeding/hemostatic efficacy; the relationship between specific INR corrections and clinical hemostasis has not been fully established.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Use with caution in patients with coronary heart disease, liver disease, and other conditions at elevated risk of thromboembolic events.
- Administer vitamin K concurrently to sustain factor levels after the PCC effect wanes.

## 7. Adverse Effects

- Serious adverse reactions described: hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis, arterial and venous thromboembolic events (including cerebral infarction, pulmonary embolism, myocardial ischemia/unstable angina), disseminated intravascular coagulation, hemorrhage, acute respiratory failure, circulatory collapse/shock, subdural hemorrhage, and deaths reported in clinical experience (not necessarily related to product).

- Frequencies derive from the pivotal randomized controlled clinical trial (N=105 subjects treated with this product for urgent VKA reversal) and postmarketing reports. Postmarketing frequencies cannot be reliably estimated.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Vitamin K: Administer vitamin K concurrently to patients receiving this product to maintain vitamin K‑dependent clotting factor levels after the effect of the PCC has waned. This is standard co‑management and is recommended.
- Heparin: Product contains heparin as an excipient; patients with known allergy to heparin or history of HIT are contraindicated.
- Mixing with other medicinal products: Do not mix with other medicinal products. Administer via a separate infusion line.
- Pharmacokinetic interactions: Not specified in available sources.
- Pharmacodynamic interactions: Not specified beyond interactions implied by coagulation status and the need to consider timing of resumption of anticoagulation.

## 11. Pharmacology

### Mechanism of action
- Replaces depleted vitamin K‑dependent coagulation factors II, VII, IX and X and the anticoagulant Proteins C and S, thereby restoring functional levels of clotting factors necessary for hemostasis and reversing anticoagulation induced by vitamin K antagonists.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Rapid correction of INR observed: in clinical studies most subjects had INR ≤1.5 within 30 minutes after infusion. The relationship between laboratory INR correction and clinical hemostasis has not been fully established.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Detailed absorption, distribution, metabolism, and elimination parameters are not specified in available sources.

## 12. Clinical Trials

### Pivotal trial summary
- Trial design: Prospective, randomized, controlled, double‑blind, multicenter non‑inferiority trial in subjects requiring urgent reversal of VKA anticoagulation for urgent surgery or invasive procedure.
- Population: 208 subjects enrolled; 105 received this prothrombin complex concentrate; age range 31–92 years; 59% ≥65 years.
- Key efficacy outcome: The product decreased INR to ≤1.5 within 30 minutes in most subjects.
- Safety outcomes: In the trial, three subjects (2.9%) receiving the product experienced four thromboembolic events (including cerebral infarction and pulmonary embolism); one serious adverse reaction (unstable angina) was assessed as related to treatment. Other serious adverse events observed in the post‑surgical setting included hemorrhage, acute respiratory failure, shock, and subdural hemorrhage. Four deaths occurred in the treatment group between 22 and 45 days post surgery (none considered related to study treatment).
- Comparator: An active comparator product was used in the trial. (Comparator trade names are not included here.)

### Other clinical data
- Postmarketing surveillance has reported additional adverse reactions including anaphylaxis, thromboembolic events, cerebrovascular events, cardiac events, respiratory failure, and hypersensitivity reactions; frequency estimates are not available from postmarketing reports.
