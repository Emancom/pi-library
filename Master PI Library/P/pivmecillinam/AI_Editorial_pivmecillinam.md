# Editorial Product Information: Pivmecillinam

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a single clinically relevant reference. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing in any specific region. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pivmecillinam (as pivmecillinam hydrochloride).
- **Chemical Class:** Penicillin-class antibacterial; prodrug of mecillinam (an amidinopenicillanic acid).
- **Brand Names:** PIVYA [US].
- **ATC Code:** J01CA08 (Mecillinam/Pivmecillinam).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 185 mg pivmecillinam (equivalent to 200 mg pivmecillinam hydrochloride) [US].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Alembic Pharmaceuticals Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Uncomplicated Urinary Tract Infections (uUTI):** Treatment of female patients 18 years of age and older with uUTI caused by susceptible isolates of *Escherichia coli*, *Proteus mirabilis*, and *Staphylococcus saprophyticus* [US].
- **Editorial Note on Usage:** To prevent the development of drug-resistant bacteria, pivmecillinam should only be used for infections proven or strongly suspected to be caused by susceptible bacteria [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Adult Dosing (Females $\ge$ 18 years):** 185 mg orally three times daily for 3 to 7 days, depending on clinical indication [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - May be administered with or without food [US].
    - Tablets should be swallowed with adequate fluid to avoid esophageal irritation.
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as possible. Patients must not double the dose to compensate for a missed one [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** History of serious hypersensitivity reactions (e.g., anaphylaxis or Stevens-Johnson syndrome) to pivmecillinam or other beta-lactam antibacterial drugs (e.g., penicillins, cephalosporins) [US].
- **Carnitine Deficiency:** Primary or secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inherited disorders of mitochondrial fatty acid oxidation, carnitine metabolism, or other inborn errors of metabolism (e.g., methylmalonic aciduria, propionic acidemia) [US].
- **Acute Porphyria:** Pivmecillinam is contraindicated in patients suffering from porphyria, as it has been associated with acute attacks [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious and occasionally fatal hypersensitivity (anaphylactic) reactions have been reported. Cross-hypersensitivity with other beta-lactams is well-documented. Discontinue immediately if an allergic reaction occurs [US].
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCAR):** AGEP, DRESS, SJS, and TEN have been reported. Treatment should be stopped at the first sign of skin rash or mucosal lesions [US].
- **Carnitine Depletion:** Pivmecillinam is a pivalate-generating prodrug. Pivalate is excreted as pivaloylcarnitine, which can lead to clinically significant hypocarnitinemia.
    - Not recommended for prolonged treatment.
    - Avoid in patients with significant renal impairment or decreased muscle mass [US].
- **Clostridioides difficile-Associated Diarrhea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all systemic antibacterials. Severity may range from mild diarrhea to fatal colitis [US].
- **Interference with Newborn Screening:** Maternal treatment prior to delivery may cause a false positive test for isovaleric acidemia in the newborn screening process [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (1–10%):**
    - Nausea (4.3% [US])
    - Diarrhea (2.1% [US])
- **Uncommon to Rare (<1%):**
    - Vulvovaginal candidiasis (1.8% [US])
    - Genital pruritus (1.8% [US])
    - Headache (1.4% [US])
    - Vomiting, rash, dyspepsia, and abdominal pain [US].
- **Post-Marketing Experience (Frequency Not Established):**
    - **Blood:** Thrombocytopenia.
    - **Ear:** Vertigo.
    - **GI:** Esophageal ulcer, esophagitis, mouth ulceration.
    - **Hepatobiliary:** Abnormal hepatic function.
    - **Immune:** Anaphylactic reaction, angioedema.
    - **Neurological:** Dizziness.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Valproic Acid/Valproate:** Avoid concurrent use. Combined pivalate-generating drugs and valproate significantly increase the risk of carnitine depletion [US].
- **Methotrexate:** Penicillins may reduce the clearance of methotrexate, potentially increasing toxicity. Monitor levels or consider alternative therapy [US].
- **Probenecid:** Inhibits the renal excretion of mecillinam. Concomitant use increases mecillinam $C_{max}$ by ~80% and AUC by ~40% [US].
- **Other Antimicrobials:** In vitro antagonism has been observed with nitrofurantoin [US].
- **Laboratory Interference:** May cause false positive results for isovaleric acidemia in newborn screening [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Classification:** Data from observational studies do not indicate an increased risk of major birth defects [US].
    - **Clinical Considerations:** No dosage adjustment is required. Note the risk of false-positive newborn screening for isovaleric acidemia if used prior to delivery [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Mecillinam is present in animal milk. Human data are insufficient to exclude presence in human milk or effects on the breastfed infant. Pharmacovigilance reports include rash and diarrhea in breastfed infants exposed to mecillinam [US].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in animal studies at doses significantly higher than the human equivalent dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US]. Symptomatic hypocarnitinemia has been reported in pediatric populations outside the US on long-term therapy [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy observed between patients $\ge$ 65 years and younger patients. However, caution is advised due to the higher likelihood of reduced renal function in this population [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Reductions in systemic elimination are expected. While specific dose adjustments are not suggested by available safety data, alternative therapies should be considered in patients with significant renal impairment due to carnitine depletion risks [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Metabolism/excretion by the liver is a minor pathway. Dosage adjustments are not required [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Pivmecillinam is a prodrug that is rapidly hydrolysed to mecillinam. Mecillinam binds with high specificity to penicillin-binding protein 2 (PBP-2) in the gram-negative cell wall, interfering with biosynthesis and leading to cell lysis [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics [US]:**
    - **Absorption:** Bioavailability of 25-35%. $T_{max}$ is approximately 90 minutes.
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is <25%.
    - **Metabolism:** Hydrolysed by non-specific esterases to mecillinam and pivalic acid.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal; 80% of a dose is excreted in urine as mecillinam. Terminal half-life is approximately 61 minutes.
- **Microbiology:** Active against *E. coli*, *P. mirabilis*, and *S. saprophyticus*. Demonstrates in vitro activity against some ESBL-producing Enterobacterales [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Trial 1 (vs. Placebo):** In a study of women with uUTI, pivmecillinam (185 mg TID for 7 days) achieved a composite response (clinical cure and microbiological gold standard) of 62% compared to 10% for placebo [US].
- **Trial 2 (vs. Cephalexin):** Pivmecillinam (185 mg TID for 3 days) showed a composite response of 72% vs. 76% for cephalexin (250 mg QID for 7 days) [US].
- **Trial 4 (vs. Ibuprofen):** Pivmecillinam (185 mg TID for 3 days) demonstrated a composite response of 66% vs. 22% for ibuprofen (600 mg TID for 3 days) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Store and dispense in the original unit-dose blisters to protect from moisture [US].
