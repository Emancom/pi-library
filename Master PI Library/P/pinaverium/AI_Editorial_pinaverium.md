# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of levocarnitine injection, synthesized for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the local approved PI for specific regulatory requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (Generic); chemical class: carrier molecule/amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Titration/Loading:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Monitoring:** Daily doses typically range from 50 mg/kg upwards as clinically required. The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow (2 to 3 minute) bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Adjustments:** Dose adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations (normal range: 40 µmol/L to 50 µmol/L). Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration before use [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate risks before re-administration [US].
- **Seizures:** Both new-onset seizures and increased seizure frequency/severity have been reported in patients receiving **levocarnitine**, regardless of pre-existing seizure history [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Form Precaution):** While this PI concerns the injection, clinicians should note that chronic high-dose oral **levocarnitine** in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic errors.

### Frequency Summary (ESRD Clinical Trials)
| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%), Hypertension (20%), Diarrhea (16%), Headache (22%), Pharyngitis (23%), Fever (7–12%), Dizziness (13%), Nausea (8–12%), Vomiting (15%), Dyspnea (7–11%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Abdominal pain (9%), Asthenia (9%), Chest pain (12%), Infection (15%), Tachycardia (6%), Hypercalcemia (9%), Anemia (6%), Paresthesia (5%) |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (reported post-marketing) [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis and laryngeal edema [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concomitantly with **levocarnitine**.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of **levocarnitine** or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified by letter]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** **Levocarnitine** supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after administration. The benefits of maternal supplementation must be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See Section 3 (Dosage and Administration) for weight-based dosing; no specific age-range restrictions provided [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety of the oral form is not established in renal failure; however, **levocarnitine** is easily removed from plasma via dialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Levocarnitine** is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with metabolic errors, **levocarnitine** promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly cleared [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and [3H]-γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous concentrations) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Trials:** Evidence is based on two double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in patients on chronic hemodialysis. While **levocarnitine** injection significantly increases carnitine concentrations, the effect on specific clinical outcomes or signs/symptoms of carnitine deficiency in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing.
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].
