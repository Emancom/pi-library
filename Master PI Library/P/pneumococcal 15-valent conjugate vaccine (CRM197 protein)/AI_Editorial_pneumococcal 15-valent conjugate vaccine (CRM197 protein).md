# Editorial Product Information: pneumococcal 15-valent conjugate vaccine (CRM197 protein)

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **pneumococcal 15-valent conjugate vaccine (CRM197 protein)**
- Chemical class: Polysaccharide–protein conjugate vaccine (capsular polysaccharides from 15 Streptococcus pneumoniae serotypes conjugated to CRM197 diphtheria-derived protein and adsorbed to aluminium phosphate adjuvant)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration: Intramuscular injection only.

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources.
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources.
- Withdrawal, suspension or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Active immunisation for the prevention of pneumococcal disease caused by Streptococcus pneumoniae serotypes 1, 3, 4, 5, 6A, 6B, 7F, 9V, 14, 18C, 19A, 19F, 22F, 23F and 33F in adults and in children from 6 weeks of age.
- The vaccine may not protect against disease caused by serotypes not included in the vaccine.
- Use should be guided by official/local immunisation recommendations.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
- General dosing (all ages): Administer 0.5 mL intramuscularly per dose.
- Paediatric dosing and schedules:
  - 3‑dose regimen (two-dose primary series followed by a toddler dose): first dose as early as 6 weeks of age; second dose 8 weeks after first; third (toddler) dose at approximately 11–15 months of age.
  - 4‑dose regimen (three-dose primary series followed by a toddler dose): first dose as early as 6–12 weeks of age; interval of 4–8 weeks between doses in the primary series; fourth (toddler) dose at approximately 11–15 months of age and at least 2 months after the third dose.
  - Preterm infants (<37 weeks gestation): should receive the 4‑dose regimen as above (first dose as early as 6–12 weeks; 4–8 week intervals; toddler dose at 11–15 months, ≥2 months after dose 3).
- Catch‑up vaccination (for pneumococcal vaccine‑naïve or incompletely vaccinated children 7 months through 17 years):
  - Infants 7 through 11 months: three doses — first two doses at least 4 weeks apart; third dose after 12 months of age and at least 2 months after the second dose.
  - Children 12 through 23 months: two doses, with an interval of 2 months between doses.
  - Children and adolescents 2 through 17 years: a single dose.
  - If a prior pneumococcal conjugate vaccine was given, wait at least 2 months before administering this vaccine.
- Adults:
  - One single dose (routine adult immunisation).
- Completing mixed schedules:
  - A vaccination series started with another pneumococcal conjugate vaccine may be completed with this vaccine.
- Administration instructions:
  - For intramuscular use only; do not inject intravascularly.
  - Preferred injection sites: anterolateral thigh in infants; deltoid muscle in children and adults.
  - Avoid injection into the gluteal area or sites where a major nerve trunk or blood vessel may be present.
  - Do not dilute or mix with other vaccines. When administering concomitant injectable vaccines, give each at a different injection site.
  - The product is an adjuvanted suspension; shake vigorously immediately before use to obtain a uniform opalescent suspension. If the vaccine cannot be resuspended, do not use.
  - Use full recommended dose; prefilled syringes are for single use only.
- Titration schedules: Not applicable.
- Missed dose instructions: Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- History of a severe allergic reaction (e.g., anaphylaxis) to any component of the vaccine, including any diphtheria toxoid–containing vaccine.
- Other contraindications: Not specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- Boxed warnings: Not specified in available sources.
- General warnings and precautions:
  - Individuals with altered or reduced immunocompetence (including those receiving immunosuppressive therapy, irradiation, antimetabolites, alkylating agents, cytotoxic drugs, high‑dose corticosteroids, therapeutic proteins or targeted immunomodulators) may mount a diminished immune response to vaccination.
  - The potential for apnoea should be considered when administering intramuscular vaccines to infants born prematurely; however, because the benefit is substantial, vaccination should generally not be withheld or delayed in preterm infants.
  - As with all vaccines, this product may not protect all recipients.
  - Safety and effectiveness have not been established in children younger than 6 weeks of age.
- Specific clinical precautions:
  - Concomitant administration with certain vaccines has not been studied in some age groups (see Section 8).
  - Exercise appropriate caution to avoid inadvertent intravascular injection and accidental needle stick injuries.
- Monitoring requirements:
  - Observe standard post‑vaccination precautions (monitor for immediate hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis). Specific monitoring intervals or laboratory monitoring requirements are not specified in available sources.
- Effects on laboratory tests: Not applicable as reported in available sources.

## 7. Adverse Effects
- The full list below includes solicited (predefined) local and systemic adverse reactions recorded in clinical studies of infants, toddlers, children/adolescents and limited summary information for adults as available in the source material. Frequencies are presented using the following categories where data permit: very common (>10%), common (1–10%), uncommon (0.1–1%), rare (<0.1%). Where dose‑ or age‑specific frequencies are reported in the sources, the highest observed frequency is used to classify the event into a frequency band for conservative presentation.
- Serious adverse effects: Not fully enumerated in the available source excerpt. Severe local or systemic reactions (defined in paediatric studies as extremely distressed or unable to do usual activities or size >7.6 cm for local reactions) occurred at low rates: severe reactions occurred in ≤1.3% of infants and toddlers following each dose, except irritability which was reported up to 5.2% in some datasets.

- Notes on frequencies from paediatric trial datasets:
  - In pooled infant primary‑series data, solicited local reaction rates after doses ranged (examples): pain ≈19–27%; erythema ≈14–20%; swelling ≈9–14%; induration ≈11–15%.
  - Solicited systemic reactions in infants/toddlers included decreased appetite (≈14–17%), irritability (≈43–55%), somnolence (≈22–41%), and fever ≥38°C (<39°C) in ≈35–43% after doses.
  - In toddler doses, solicited local and systemic reactions were similar in pattern and frequency (pain ≈21%; erythema ≈21%; irritability ≈46%; fever ≥38°C ≈34%).
  - In catch‑up cohorts (7 months–17 years), solicited reactions were variable by age: older children/adolescents (2–17 years) had higher frequencies of local pain (≈55%), fatigue (≈16%), headache (≈12%), and myalgia (≈24%) in solicited datasets.
  - Injection‑site urticaria was reported as an unsolicited event in up to 0.3% of infants/toddlers after each dose in some studies.
- Adult adverse reaction frequencies: Detailed adult solicited and unsolicited frequencies are not specified in the available source excerpt; the safety program included multiple adult studies but frequency tables for adults were not provided in the available material.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Pharmacokinetic interactions: No pharmacokinetic interaction data are specified in available sources.
- Pharmacodynamic / co‑administration interactions:
  - Infants and children <2 years: Concomitant administration with a range of routine paediatric vaccines was studied and is acceptable. Vaccines studied concomitantly (either as monovalent or combination vaccines) include antigens for diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis, poliomyelitis (types 1, 2 and 3), hepatitis A, hepatitis B, Haemophilus influenzae type b, measles, mumps, rubella, varicella and rotavirus.
  - Concomitant administration with meningococcal ACWY and meningococcal B vaccines has not been studied in the available sources for infants and children.
  - Children and adolescents 2 through 17 years: No data on concomitant administration with other vaccines were provided in the available sources.
  - Adults: Concomitant administration with inactivated seasonal influenza vaccine has been studied and is acceptable in adults; data on concomitant administration with other adult vaccines were not provided in available sources.
- Interactions with immunosuppressive therapies:
  - Immunosuppressive treatments (including irradiation, antimetabolites, alkylating agents, cytotoxic drugs, corticosteroids, therapeutic proteins and targeted immunomodulators) may reduce the immune response to this vaccine.
- Clinically significant interactions and management:
  - When administered concomitantly with other injectable vaccines, give each injection at a different anatomical site.
  - When co‑administration data are lacking for specific vaccine combinations in certain age groups, follow applicable official immunisation guidance and exercise clinical judgment.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- Pregnancy:
  - Animal data: In female rats given multiple intramuscular doses at a high dose (~200× human dose on a µg/kg basis) there were no effects on mating performance, fertility, embryofetal survival, or pre‑weaning development. Antibodies to all 15 serotypes were detected in offspring, attributed to maternal transfer during gestation and possibly via lactation.
  - Human data: Adequate and well‑controlled studies in pregnant women are not available in the source materials. The decision to vaccinate during pregnancy should consider the woman’s risk of exposure to S. pneumoniae; vaccinate only if clearly needed.
  - Australian pregnancy category reported in available sources: Category B1.
  - Other jurisdictions’ pregnancy categorisations: Not specified in available sources.
- Breastfeeding:
  - It is not known whether the vaccine is excreted in human milk. Vaccine‑specific antibodies were transferred to offspring in animal studies; evaluation of antibody levels in animal milk was not conducted.
- Fertility:
  - In animal fertility studies in female rats at high dose (≈200× human µg/kg), no effects on mating performance or fertility were observed.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
- Paediatric use:
  - Approved down to age 6 weeks of age (see Section 3 for indication).
  - Safety and effectiveness have not been established in infants younger than 6 weeks.
  - Preterm infants (<37 weeks gestation) should receive the 4‑dose regimen (three primary doses plus toddler dose) beginning as early as 6–12 weeks with 4–8 week intervals in the primary series and a toddler dose at 11–15 months, ≥2 months after dose 3.
- Elderly use:
  - Clinical development programs included adults aged ≥50 years and substantial numbers ≥65 years; no routine dose adjustments are specified for older adults. Specific age‑related dose modifications are not specified in available sources.
- Renal impairment:
  - Dosing adjustments by level of renal function (eGFR/stage) are not specified in available sources.
- Hepatic impairment:
  - Dosing adjustments by hepatic function/severity are not specified in available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology
- Mechanism of action:
  - The vaccine contains purified capsular polysaccharides from 15 S. pneumoniae serotypes, chemically conjugated to CRM197 (a nontoxic diphtheria protein). Conjugation to CRM197 and adsorption to aluminium phosphate adjuvant enhance immunogenicity, inducing serotype‑specific antibody responses that contribute to protection against pneumococcal disease caused by the contained serotypes.
- Pharmacokinetics:
  - Classical pharmacokinetic parameters (absorption, distribution, metabolism, elimination) are not applicable in the conventional sense for this vaccine and are not specified in available sources.
- Pharmacodynamics:
  - The intended pharmacodynamic effect is induction of serotype‑specific antibody responses; specific quantitative immunogenicity results in human clinical trials are not provided in the available source excerpt.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Paediatric pivotal safety program:
  - Five randomized, double‑blind, active comparator‑controlled studies in infants and toddlers (Protocols 008, 025, 027, 029, 031) enrolled 7,229 participants across Americas, Europe and Asia Pacific to evaluate safety when administered as a 3‑ or 4‑dose regimen. In the pooled paediatric program, 4,286 participants received the pneumococcal 15‑valent conjugate vaccine, 2,405 received a 13‑valent conjugate vaccine comparator, and 538 received mixed regimens.
  - Safety assessment included solicited local and systemic adverse events recorded via vaccination report card for up to 14 days post‑vaccination, with body temperature solicited for 7 days post‑vaccination. Duration of safety follow‑up after the last vaccination was 1 to 6 months depending on protocol.
  - Solicited local and systemic reactions were predominantly mild to moderate and of short duration (≤3 days). Severe reactions were uncommon (≤1.3% for most reactions; irritability up to 5.2%).
  - Protocol 027 evaluated mixed 4‑dose regimens (completing a regimen with this vaccine after one or more doses of a 13‑valent vaccine); safety profiles were generally comparable to complete series with either vaccine.
- Catch‑up study:
  - Protocol 024 was a double‑blind, active comparator‑controlled study of 606 participants aged 7 months through 17 years receiving 1–3 doses depending on age; safety follow‑up after last vaccination was 6 months. Solicited adverse reaction profiles were consistent with the routine infant/toddler studies and varied by age cohort.
- Adult clinical program:
  - Six randomized, double‑blind studies in adults (Protocols 007, 016, 017, 019, 020, 021) enrolled a total of 7,136 adults aged 18–98 years, including adults with stable underlying medical conditions and risk factors for pneumococcal disease. Of these, 5,478 adults received the vaccine; 5,101 were pneumococcal vaccine‑naïve and 377 had been previously vaccinated with a 23‑valent pneumococcal polysaccharide vaccine at least one year prior.
  - Safety of the vaccine in pneumococcal vaccine‑naïve adults ≥50 years was evaluated in active comparator‑controlled trials (Protocols 016, 019, 020) including 3,032 participants receiving the 15‑valent conjugate vaccine versus 1,154 receiving a 13‑valent conjugate comparator.
  - A study evaluated concomitant administration with inactivated seasonal influenza vaccine in adults ≥50 years (1,196 participants); detailed adverse reaction frequencies for adults are not specified in the available source excerpt.
- Efficacy endpoints and quantitative immunogenicity results: Not specified in available sources.
