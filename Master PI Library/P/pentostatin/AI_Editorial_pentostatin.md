# Editorial Product Information: Pentostatin

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial:
    - **United States (FDA)**: NIPENT (pentostatin for injection) 
- **Purpose**: This document is a harmonised editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises available data into a clinically relevant format.
- **Note**: This synthesis is an editorial resource and does not replace the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing decisions in any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Pentostatin (also known as 2'-deoxycoformycin or DCF).
- **Chemical Class**: Potent transition-state inhibitor of adenosine deaminase (ADA).
- **Brand Names**: NIPENT [US].
- **ATC Code**: Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**: 
    - **Lyophilized Powder for Injection**: 10 mg of pentostatin per single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration**: Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Hospira, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hairy Cell Leukemia (HCL)**: Indicated as a single-agent treatment for both untreated and alpha-interferon-refractory patients with active disease (defined by clinically significant anemia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, or disease-related symptoms) [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult)**: The recommended dose is 4 mg/m² administered every other week [US].
- **Hydration Requirements**: Patients should receive hydration with 500 to 1,000 mL of 5% Dextrose in 0.5% Normal Saline (or equivalent) prior to administration, with an additional 500 mL of 5% Dextrose (or equivalent) administered post-dose [US].
- **Administration Instructions**: 
    - Administer via IV bolus injection or diluted in 25 to 50 mL (0.9% Sodium Chloride or 5% Dextrose) and infused over 20 to 30 minutes [US].
- **Treatment Duration**: 
    - The optimal duration is not strictly determined. Patients should be treated until a complete response (CR) is achieved. 
    - It is recommended to administer two additional "consolidation" doses after CR is reached [US].
    - If no response (CR or PR) is observed after 6 months, treatment should be discontinued. 
    - If a partial response (PR) is achieved at 6 months, treatment continues toward CR. If only a PR is achieved after 12 months, discontinuation is recommended [US].
- **Dose Modification**: 
    - No specific reduction is recommended at the start of therapy for baseline cytopenias [US].
    - Withhold dose if the absolute neutrophil count (ANC) falls below 200 cells/mm³ in a patient with an initial ANC > 500 cells/mm³; resume when the count returns to predose levels [US].
    - Withhold dose for severe rash or evidence of nervous system toxicity [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity**: Contraindicated in patients with demonstrated hypersensitivity to pentostatin [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings [US]**:
    - **Supervision**: Must be administered under the supervision of a physician experienced in cancer chemotherapy.
    - **Dose Limitation**: Doses higher than 4 mg/m² every other week are not recommended. Severe renal, hepatic, pulmonary, and CNS toxicities occurred in Phase 1 studies at higher doses (20–50 mg/m²).
    - **Combination Therapy**: Use in combination with fludarabine phosphate is **not recommended** due to an increased risk of severe or fatal pulmonary toxicity.
- **Myelosuppression**: May occur primarily during the first few courses of treatment. Frequent monitoring of CBC is required [US].
- **Infections**: Patients with pre-existing infections may experience worsening of their condition. Treat infections before initiating or resuming therapy [US].
- **Renal Toxicity**: Dose-related elevations in serum creatinine occur. Assessment of renal function (serum creatinine and/or creatinine clearance) is mandatory before each dose [US].
- **Hepatic Toxicity**: Elevations in liver function tests (LFTs) have occurred and are generally reversible [US].
- **Neurological Toxicity**: Treatment should be withheld or discontinued if evidence of CNS toxicity occurs [US].
- **Pulmonary Toxicity**: Fatal acute pulmonary edema and hypotension have been reported when used in high-dose ablative regimens for bone marrow transplant (not an approved use) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%)**:
    - **Gastrointestinal**: Nausea/vomiting (63%), diarrhea (17%), abdominal pain (16%), anorexia (13%), stomatitis (12%) [US].
    - **Systemic**: Fever (46%), fatigue (42%), chills (19%), headache (17%), asthenia (12%) [US].
    - **Dermatologic**: Rash (43%), pruritus (21%) [US].
    - **Respiratory**: Coughing (20%), upper respiratory infection (13%), rhinitis (11%), dyspnea (11%) [US].
    - **Musculoskeletal**: Myalgia (19%) [US].
    - **Hematologic**: Leukopenia (22%), anemia (8–35%), thrombocytopenia (up to 32% in refractory patients) [US].
- **Common (1–10%)**:
    - Pharyngitis, sweating, arthralgia, elevated creatinine, confusion, dizziness, insomnia, anxiety [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects**:
    - Fatal pulmonary toxicity (especially with fludarabine), severe myelosuppression, nephrotoxicity, hepatotoxicity, and CNS toxicity [US].
    - **Postmarketing**: Febrile neutropenia, Hemolytic Uremic Syndrome (HUS), Thrombotic Thrombocytopenic Purpura (TTP), and acute respiratory failure [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Fludarabine Phosphate**: Risk of fatal pulmonary toxicity; combination is contraindicated/not recommended [US].
- **Vidarabine**: Pentostatin enhances the effect of vidarabine, potentially increasing adverse reactions. The benefit of this combination is not established [US].
- **Allopurinol**: Both drugs are associated with rashes. While studies did not show an increased incidence of rash with the combination, one case of fatal hypersensitivity vasculitis has been reported [US].
- **Carmustine, Etoposide, and Cyclophosphamide**: Reports of fatal pulmonary edema when used together in bone marrow transplant regimens [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **Risk Summary**: Pentostatin can cause fetal harm. Teratogenic effects (skeletal malformations, omphalocele, gastroschisis) were observed in animal studies (rats and mice) [US].
    - **Contraception**: Females of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for 6 months after the last dose. Males with female partners of reproductive potential should use effective contraception for 3 months after the last dose [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: It is unknown if pentostatin is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for 1 week following the final dose [US].
- **Fertility**: No human data available. Animal studies showed mild seminiferous tubular degeneration in dogs [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and effectiveness in children or adolescents have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use**: Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65+ to determine if they respond differently. Dosing should be cautious, reflecting the greater frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function [US].
- **Renal Impairment**: 
    - Pentostatin clearance is positively correlated with creatinine clearance (CLcr). 
    - In patients with CLcr < 50 mL/min, the half-life increases significantly (~18 hours vs. 6 hours) [US].
    - There is insufficient data to recommend a dose for patients with CLcr < 60 mL/min [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Dosing adjustments not specified, but caution is advised due to reports of LFT elevations and potential for toxicity [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: A potent transition-state inhibitor of adenosine deaminase (ADA). Inhibition of ADA leads to elevated intracellular levels of dATP, which blocks DNA synthesis by inhibiting ribonucleotide reductase. It may also inhibit RNA synthesis and cause direct DNA damage [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Distribution**: Low plasma protein binding (~4%). Minimal CNS penetration [US].
    - **Metabolism**: Not extensively metabolised [US].
    - **Elimination**: Approximately 90% of a dose is excreted in the urine as unchanged drug or metabolites with ADA inhibitory activity. Mean terminal half-life is 5.7 hours in patients with normal renal function [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **SWOG 8691**: A multicenter study comparing pentostatin to alpha-interferon (IFN) in untreated HCL patients. 
    - **Results**: Pentostatin achieved a significantly higher complete response (CR) rate (84% vs 18% for IFN). 
    - **Recovery**: Time to recovery of neutrophil and platelet counts was shorter with pentostatin [US].
- **NCI Phase 2 Studies**: Evaluated pentostatin in IFN-refractory patients, showing a CR rate of 58% [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials**: Store under refrigerated conditions at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) [US].
- **Reconstituted Solution**: Contains no preservatives. Reconstituted or further diluted solutions may be stored at room temperature under ambient light but must be used within 8 hours [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification**: Prescription Only Medicine (Rx-only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule**: Not specified in available sources.
