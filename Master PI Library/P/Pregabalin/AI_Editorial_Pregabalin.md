# Editorial Product Information: Pregabalin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a single clinically focused reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a supplement to, not a substitute for, the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pregabalin, a structural analogue of gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA).
- **Brand Names:** AdiraMedica Pregabalin [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Hard Gelatin Capsules:** 25 mg, 75 mg, 150 mg, and 300 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AdiraMedica Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Neuropathic Pain:** Treatment of neuropathic pain in adults [Australian].
- **Epilepsy:** Adjunctive therapy in adults with partial seizures, with or without secondary generalisation [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The effective dose range is 150 mg to 600 mg per day, administered in two divided doses. Pregabalin may be taken with or without food [Australian].
- **Neuropathic Pain:** 
    - **Initial dose:** 150 mg/day (divided).
    - **Titration:** May increase to 300 mg/day after 3–7 days based on response.
    - **Maximum dose:** 600 mg/day after an additional 7-day interval [Australian].
- **Epilepsy:**
    - **Initial dose:** 150 mg/day (divided).
    - **Titration:** May increase to 300 mg/day after 1 week.
    - **Maximum dose:** 600 mg/day after an additional week [Australian].
- **Discontinuation:** If treatment is to be terminated, it must be withdrawn gradually over a minimum of one week to reduce the risk of withdrawal symptoms [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to pregabalin or any excipients (e.g., mannitol) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Misuse, Abuse, and Dependence:** **Pregabalin is a potential drug of misuse.** Use carries a risk of overdose and death, particularly when combined with opioids or other CNS depressants. Patients should be assessed for history of substance abuse and monitored for drug-seeking behavior [Australian].
- **Suicidal Behaviour and Ideation:** Antiepileptic drugs (AEDs) increase the risk of suicidal thoughts. Monitor patients for new or worsening depression and mood changes [Australian].
- **Respiratory Depression:** Severe respiratory depression has been reported. Risk is higher in patients with compromised respiratory function, neurological disease, renal impairment, the elderly, or those using concomitant CNS depressants [Australian].
- **Dizziness and Somnolence:** Very common; may lead to accidental injury (falls), particularly in the elderly [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity/Angioedema:** Discontinue immediately if swelling of the face, perioral area, or upper airway occurs [Australian].
- **Withdrawal Symptoms:** Abrupt discontinuation may cause insomnia, headache, nausea, anxiety, hyperhidrosis, and diarrhoea [Australian].
- **Congestive Heart Failure:** Use with caution in patients with cardiac compromise due to reports of CHF [Australian].
- **Vision Changes:** Blurred vision and visual field changes have been reported; resolution often occurs with continued dosing [Australian].
- **Creatine Kinase (CK) Elevation:** Discontinue if myopathy is diagnosed or if CK levels are markedly elevated [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Dizziness, somnolence, headache.
- **Common (1–10%):** Nasopharyngitis, increased appetite, euphoric mood, confusion, irritability, libido decreased, ataxia, tremor, dysarthria, memory impairment, blurred vision, diplopia, vertigo, vomiting, constipation, dry mouth, flatulence, erectile dysfunction, peripheral oedema, fatigue, and weight gain.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Neutropenia, hypoglycaemia, hallucinations, syncope, myoclonus, tachycardia, hypotension/hypertension, dyspnoea, and urinary incontinence.
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Panic attacks, disinhibition, stupor, keratitis, arrhythmia, pulmonary oedema, pancreatitis, rhabdomyolysis, and renal failure.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Angioedema, suicidal ideation, respiratory depression, and rhabdomyolysis [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Unlikely. Pregabalin is predominantly excreted unchanged in urine (<2% metabolism) and does not inhibit drug metabolism *in vitro* or bind to plasma proteins [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **Opioids:** Increased risk of severe sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death.
    - **CNS Depressants:** May potentiate the effects of ethanol and lorazepam.
    - **Oxycodone:** Pregabalin may be additive in the impairment of cognitive and gross motor function.
    - **Gastrointestinal function:** Potential for additive constipation when used with opioid analgesics [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3 [Australian].** Not studied in pregnant women. Use only if the benefit outweighs the potential risk to the fetus. Animal studies have shown developmental toxicity at high doses.
- **Breastfeeding:** Pregabalin is excreted in human milk (approx. 7% of maternal dose on a mg/kg basis). Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal data showed decreased sperm motility and fertility at high doses; however, human trials at 600 mg/day for 3 months showed no detrimental effects on sperm motility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 18 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly (>65 years):** Increased risk of dizziness and falls. Dose adjustments may be required due to age-related decline in renal function [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dose reduction is mandatory.
    - **CLcr ≥ 60 mL/min:** 150–600 mg/day (2 doses).
    - **CLcr 30–60 mL/min:** 75–300 mg/day (1 or 2 doses).
    - **CLcr 15–30 mL/min:** 25–150 mg/day (1 or 2 doses).
    - **CLcr < 15 mL/min:** 25–75 mg/day (Single daily dose).
- **Haemodialysis:** A supplementary dose of 25–100 mg should be given immediately after each 4-hour dialysis session [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Binds to the $\alpha_2-\delta$ subunit of voltage-gated calcium channels in the CNS, reducing the release of several neurotransmitters (e.g., glutamate, noradrenaline, substance P).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid; peak plasma levels within 1 hour (fasted). Oral bioavailability ≥ 90%.
    - **Distribution:** Apparent Vd is ~0.56 L/kg; not protein bound. Crosses the blood-brain barrier.
    - **Metabolism:** Negligible (<2%).
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (98% unchanged). Mean half-life is 6.3 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Neuropathic Pain:** Efficacy demonstrated in 11 double-blind, placebo-controlled studies. Significant pain reduction was observed by Week 1 and maintained for up to 12 weeks.
- **Epilepsy:** Efficacy as adjunctive therapy demonstrated in three 12-week trials. Significant reductions in seizure frequency were observed by Week 1 [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to expiry date on packaging.
- **Disposal:** Unused medicine should be returned to a pharmacy for disposal [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
