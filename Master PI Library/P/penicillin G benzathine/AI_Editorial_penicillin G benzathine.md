# Editorial Product Information: Penicillin G Benzathine and Penicillin G Procaine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of the combination of penicillin G benzathine and penicillin G procaine for use by healthcare professionals.
- This synthesis is an editorial summary intended for informational purposes and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Penicillin G benzathine and penicillin G procaine (equimolar amounts).
- **Chemical Class:** Natural penicillins; beta-lactam antibacterials.
- **Brand Name:** Bicillin C-R [US].
- **ATC Code:** J01CE30.
- **Dosage Form and Strengths:** 
    - Injectable suspension in disposable syringes: 1,200,000 units per 2 mL (comprising 600,000 units of penicillin G benzathine and 600,000 units of penicillin G procaine) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Deep intramuscular (IM) injection only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Laboratories Div Pfizer Inc [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
The combination of penicillin G benzathine and penicillin G procaine is indicated for the treatment of moderately severe infections caused by penicillin G-susceptible microorganisms that are sensitive to the serum levels achieved by this specific formulation.

- **Streptococcal Infections (Group A):** 
    - Moderately severe to severe infections of the upper respiratory tract.
    - Scarlet fever.
    - Erysipelas.
    - Skin and soft-tissue infections.
- **Pneumococcal Infections:** 
    - Moderately severe pneumonia (excluding pneumococcal meningitis).
    - Otitis media.
- **Usage Restrictions:** 
    - This product is NOT indicated for the treatment of venereal diseases, including syphilis, gonorrhea, yaws, bejel, and pinta [US].
    - It is not intended for infections requiring high, sustained serum levels (e.g., bacteremia, meningitis, or endocarditis), for which aqueous penicillin G sodium or potassium should be used [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Pediatrics)
**Streptococcal Infections (Group A):**
- **Adults and pediatric patients over 60 lbs:** 2,400,000 units.
- **Pediatric patients 30 to 60 lbs:** 900,000 to 1,200,000 units.
- **Pediatric patients under 30 lbs:** 600,000 units.
- **Schedule:** Usually administered as a single session using multiple IM sites if indicated. An alternative schedule of half the dose on Day 1 and half on Day 3 may be used [US].

**Pneumococcal Infections (except meningitis):**
- **Adults:** 1,200,000 units.
- **Pediatric patients:** 600,000 units.
- **Schedule:** Repeat every 2 to 3 days until the patient has been afebrile for 48 hours [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Route:** For **DEEP INTRAMUSCULAR INJECTION ONLY**. 
- **Site Selection:** 
    - Adults: Upper, outer quadrant of the buttock (dorsogluteal) or the ventrogluteal site.
    - Infants and small children: Midlateral aspect of the thigh is preferred.
- **Technique:** Inject at a slow, steady rate to prevent needle blockage due to high suspension concentration. Rotate injection sites for repeated doses [US].
- **Caution:** Avoid injection into or near a nerve or blood vessel to prevent permanent neurological or neurovascular damage [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to any penicillin.
- Known hypersensitivity to procaine [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning [US]
- **NOT FOR INTRAVENOUS USE:** Do not inject intravenously or admix with other IV solutions. Inadvertent IV administration has been associated with cardiorespiratory arrest and death.

### General Warnings
- **Anaphylaxis:** Serious and occasionally fatal hypersensitivity reactions have occurred. Cross-reactivity may occur in patients with cephalosporin sensitivity.
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCAR):** SJS, TEN, DRESS, and AGEP have been reported. Discontinue immediately if suspected [US].
- **Methemoglobinemia:** Associated with the procaine component. Increased risk in patients with G6PD deficiency, infants under 6 months, or those concurrently exposed to oxidizing agents [US].
- **Clostridioides difficile-Associated Diarrhea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all antibacterial agents; can range from mild diarrhea to fatal colitis [US].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Neurovascular Damage (Nicolau Syndrome):** Inadvertent intravascular or perivascular injection can cause severe damage including gangrene, necrosis, and transverse myelitis. This is most common in infants and small children [US].
- **Quadriceps Femoris Fibrosis:** Reported following repeated IM injections into the anterolateral thigh; avoid this site where possible [US].
- **Procaine Sensitivity:** A small percentage of patients are sensitive to procaine. If suspected, a skin test (0.1 mL of 1–2% procaine) is recommended [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Frequencies are not specifically quantified in the source documentation ("Post-marketing experience") [US].

| System Organ Class | Adverse Reactions Reported |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Immune System** | Anaphylaxis, serum sickness-like reactions (fever, chills, arthralgia), Kounis syndrome (allergic myocardial ischemia). |
| **Skin/Subcutaneous** | Urticaria, maculopapular eruptions, exfoliative dermatitis, SJS, TEN, DRESS, AGEP, diaphoresis. |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Pseudomembranous colitis, nausea, vomiting, blood in stool, intestinal necrosis. |
| **Neurologic** | **Hoigne’s Syndrome** (agitation, confusion, hallucinations, fear of death) following procaine penicillin; seizures, neuropathy, transverse myelitis. |
| **Cardiovascular** | Cardiac arrest, hypotension, tachycardia, pulmonary embolism, syncope. |
| **Injection Site** | Pain, inflammation, abscess, necrosis, Nicolau syndrome (embolic cutis medicamentosa). |
| **Hematologic** | Hemolytic anemia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, lymphadenopathy. |
| **Urogenital** | Nephropathy, renal failure, hematuria, proteinuria, priapism. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Tetracyclines:** Bacteriostatic antibiotics may antagonise the bactericidal effect of penicillin; concurrent use should be avoided [US].
- **Probenecid:** Competitively inhibits renal tubular secretion, increasing and prolonging serum penicillin levels [US].
- **Oxidizing Agents:** Concurrent use with drugs like nitrates, local anesthetics, or certain antimalarials increases the risk of methemoglobinemia [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned (narrative description provided). 
    - Animal reproduction studies (mice, rats, rabbits) show no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Penicillins are generally considered safe in pregnancy, but should only be used if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Penicillin G is excreted in human milk. Caution should be exercised when administering to nursing women [US].
- **Fertility:** No long-term animal studies have been conducted to evaluate carcinogenic or mutagenic potential or effects on fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Weight-based dosing applies (see Section 3). High risk of neurovascular complications; midlateral thigh is the preferred injection site for neonates and infants [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects ≥65 years. Dose selection should be cautious, starting at the low end of the range, due to higher frequencies of decreased renal, hepatic, or cardiac function [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Excretion of penicillin G is delayed in patients with impaired kidney function. Dose adjustments may be necessary, and monitoring of renal function is advised in the elderly [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal; inhibits the biosynthesis of cell-wall peptidoglycan during active multiplication, leading to osmotic instability and bacterial death.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Both salts have low solubility, providing a slow-release depot from IM sites. Peak levels occur within 3 hours. 
    - **Levels:** 1,200,000 units produces peak levels of 2.1 to 2.6 units/mL, falling to 0.04 units/mL at seven days [US].
    - **Distribution:** Approximately 60% protein bound. Widely distributed; highest levels in kidneys, lower in liver, skin, and intestines.
    - **Elimination:** Excreted rapidly by renal tubular excretion in patients with normal renal function.
