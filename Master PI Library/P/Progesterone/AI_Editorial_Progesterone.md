# Editorial Product Information: Estradiol and Progesterone

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of the combination of estradiol and progesterone for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing or dispensing in any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Estradiol (as hemihydrate) and Progesterone.
- **Chemical Class:** Estrogen and natural progestogen combination.
- **Brand Names:** **BIJUVA** 1/100 [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** G03FA04 (Progesterone and estrogen).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Soft capsule: 1 mg estradiol (as hemihydrate) and 100 mg progesterone [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Theramex Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hormone Replacement Therapy (HRT):** Indicated for the treatment of estrogen deficiency symptoms in postmenopausal women who have an intact uterus and have experienced at least 12 months since their last menstrual period [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** One capsule should be taken orally once daily in the evening.
- **Administration Instructions:** Must be taken with food to ensure adequate absorption of the progesterone component.
- **Initiation of Therapy:** 
    - **Natural Menopause:** Treatment should commence at least 12 months after the last natural menstrual bleed.
    - **Surgically Induced Menopause:** Treatment may begin immediately.
    - **Switching from other HRT:** Patients transitioning from a continuous sequential or cyclical preparation should complete the current 28-day cycle before starting this combination. Patients switching from another continuous combined preparation may start at any time.
- **Titration:** Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration necessary to meet treatment goals.
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is forgotten, it should be taken as soon as possible. If more than 12 hours have elapsed, the missed dose should be skipped, and the next dose taken at the usual time. Missing a dose may increase the likelihood of breakthrough bleeding or spotting.

## 4. Contraindications
This combination is contraindicated in patients with:
- Known, past, or suspected breast cancer.
- Known or suspected estrogen-dependent malignant tumours (e.g., endometrial cancer).
- Undiagnosed genital bleeding.
- Untreated endometrial hyperplasia.
- Current or previous venous thromboembolism (VTE), including deep vein thrombosis (DVT) and pulmonary embolism (PE).
- Known thrombophilic disorders (e.g., protein C, protein S, or antithrombin deficiency).
- Active or recent arterial thromboembolic disease (e.g., myocardial infarction, angina).
- Acute liver disease or a history of liver disease where liver function tests have not returned to normal.
- Porphyria.
- Known hypersensitivity to the active substances or any excipients.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [Australian]:** 
    - Estrogens and progestogens should not be used for the prevention of cardiovascular disease or dementia.
    - Data from the Women’s Health Initiative (WHI) indicates increased risks of myocardial infarction, stroke, invasive breast cancer, and VTE. 
    - The Women’s Health Initiative Memory Study (WHIMS) reported an increased risk of probable dementia in women aged 65 years and older.
- **Endometrial Hyperplasia/Carcinoma:** The addition of progesterone to estrogen therapy in women with an intact uterus reduces the risk of estrogen-induced endometrial hyperplasia and carcinoma.
- **VTE Risk:** HRT is associated with a 1.3 to 3-fold increased risk of developing VTE, particularly in the first year of therapy.
- **Stroke and Coronary Artery Disease (CAD):** Combined HRT is associated with a slightly increased risk of CAD and up to a 1.5-fold increase in the risk of ischaemic stroke.
- **Breast and Ovarian Cancer:** Long-term use is associated with an increased risk of breast cancer (dependent on duration) and a slightly increased risk of ovarian cancer.
- **ALT Elevations:** Caution is advised when co-administering with certain Hepatitis C Virus (HCV) drug regimens (e.g., ombitasvir/paritaprevir/ritonavir and dasabuvir) as ALT elevations have been observed in patients using ethinylestradiol. While not seen with estradiol, vigilance is warranted.
- **Monitoring:** Annual risk-benefit appraisal is recommended. Regular breast examinations and mammography should be performed according to clinical needs.
- **Fluid Retention:** Estrogens may cause fluid retention; patients with cardiac or renal dysfunction require close observation.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequencies are based on clinical trial data (TXC12-05) [Australian].

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Breast tenderness (10.4%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Headache, nausea, pelvic pain, vaginal haemorrhage, vaginal discharge, abdominal pain/distension, fatigue, back pain, weight increase, dizziness, acne, alopecia. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Anaemia, vertigo, hirsutism, visual impairment, constipation, diarrhoea, dyspepsia, vomiting, dry mouth, pancreatitis (acute), hypersensitivity, weight decreased, fluid retention, hyperlipidaemia, musculoskeletal pain, mood swings, hypertension, superficial thrombophlebitis. |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Thromboembolic events (VTE, stroke, MI), breast cancer, and endometrial malignancy.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP450 Inducers:** Substances such as anticonvulsants (phenobarbital, phenytoin, carbamazepine), anti-infectives (rifampicin, rifabutin, nevirapine, efavirenz, griseofulvin), and St John’s Wort may increase the metabolism of estrogens and progestogens, potentially decreasing clinical efficacy.
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Ketoconazole and other inhibitors may increase the bioavailability of progesterone.
- **Ritonavir and Nelfinavir:** While known as strong inhibitors, they may exhibit inducing properties when used concomitantly with steroid hormones.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Lamotrigine:** HRT may decrease plasma concentrations of lamotrigine, potentially reducing seizure control [Australian].
- **Ciclosporin:** Progesterone may increase ciclosporin plasma concentrations.
- **HCV Regimens:** Risk of ALT elevations with specific combination regimens (see Section 5).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category D:** Estrogens and progestogens have caused or may be suspected of causing an increased incidence of human fetal malformations or irreversible damage. 
    - Not indicated for use during pregnancy. If pregnancy occurs, treatment must be discontinued immediately.
- **Breastfeeding:** Not indicated for use during lactation.
- **Fertility:** Not indicated for use in women of childbearing potential.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not indicated for use in children; safety and efficacy in patients under 18 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Limited clinical experience in women >65 years. HRT does not improve cognitive function, and there is evidence of increased dementia risk in women starting combined HRT after age 65.
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specifically studied; however, caution is required due to potential fluid retention.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with acute liver disease or where liver function tests have failed to return to normal.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
- **Estradiol:** Chemically and biologically identical to endogenous human 17β-estradiol. It replaces lost estrogen production in menopausal women, alleviating vasomotor and other deficiency symptoms.
- **Progesterone:** A natural progestogen identical to endogenous human progesterone. Its primary role in HRT is to protect the endometrium from estrogen-induced hyperplasia.

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Both components undergo extensive first-pass metabolism. Progesterone bioavailability is highly dependent on food; co-administration with food increases Cmax by 82% and AUC by 2.7-fold compared to fasting [Australian].
- **Distribution:** Estradiol is 95–98% protein-bound (primarily to albumin and SHBG). Progesterone is 97% protein-bound (primarily to albumin and transcortin).
- **Metabolism:** Estradiol is converted to estrone and estriol. Progesterone is metabolised to pregnanediols and pregnanolones. Both are metabolised via the liver.
- **Elimination:** Estradiol metabolites are excreted in the urine. Progesterone metabolites are eliminated in the urine as glucuronide and sulfate conjugates.
- **Half-life:** Estradiol ~26 hours; Progesterone ~10 hours (at steady state).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study TXC12-05:** A 12-week randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled substudy of 726 postmenopausal women (mean age 54.6). BIJUVA 1/100 demonstrated a statistically significant reduction in both the frequency and severity of moderate to severe vasomotor symptoms (hot flushes) compared to placebo at Weeks 4 and 12.
- **Endometrial Safety:** In a 52-week safety trial, the incidence of endometrial hyperplasia was ≤ 1% (1 case out of 268 women) with the 1 mg/100 mg dose.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** No special temperature storage conditions are required [Australian].
- **Handling:** Keep the blister in the outer carton to protect from light.
- **Disposal:** Unused medicine should be disposed of via a pharmacy to prevent environmental contamination.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
