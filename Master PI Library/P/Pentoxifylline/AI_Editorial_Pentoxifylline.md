## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pentoxifylline (a tri-substituted xanthine derivative).
- **Chemical Class:** Hemorrheologic agent.
- **Brand Name(s):** Pentoxifylline Extended-Release Tablets (Generic) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available source.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Extended-release tablets, 400 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Rising Pharma Holdings, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Intermittent Claudication:** Indicated for the management of patients with intermittent claudication resulting from chronic occlusive arterial disease of the limbs. Pentoxifylline is intended to improve function and symptoms but is not a substitute for definitive treatments such as surgical bypass or arterial obstruction removal [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:** The standard dose is 400 mg (one tablet) three times daily with meals [US].
- **Onset and Duration:** Therapeutic effects may be observed within 2 to 4 weeks; however, treatment should be maintained for at least 8 weeks to determine efficacy [US].
- **Dose Titration/Adjustment:** If patients experience digestive or central nervous system adverse effects, the dosage should be reduced to 400 mg twice daily (800 mg/day). If symptoms persist at this lower dose, the medication should be discontinued [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets are extended-release and must be taken with meals to minimize gastrointestinal side effects [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hemorrhage:** Patients with recent cerebral and/or retinal hemorrhage [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known intolerance to pentoxifylline or other methylxanthines (e.g., caffeine, theophylline, theobromine) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available source.
- **Anaphylaxis:** Treatment must be discontinued immediately at the first sign of an anaphylactic or anaphylactoid reaction [US].
- **Bleeding Risk:** Increased risk of bleeding has been reported, particularly in patients with risk factors such as recent surgery, peptic ulceration, or those on concomitant anticoagulants/antiplatelets. Periodic monitoring of hematocrit and hemoglobin is recommended for these patients [US].
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** While rare, occasional reports of angina, hypotension, and arrhythmia have occurred. Periodic blood pressure monitoring is advised for patients receiving concurrent antihypertensive therapy [US].
- **Theophylline Toxicity:** Concomitant use may lead to increased theophylline levels and toxicity. Monitoring of theophylline levels is required [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally dose-related and more frequent with immediate-release formulations than the 400 mg extended-release tablet.

### Common Adverse Effects (1–10%)
- **Digestive:** Dyspepsia (2.8%), Nausea (2.2%), Vomiting (1.2%), Belching/Flatus/Bloating (0.6%) [US].
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (1.9%), Headache (1.2%) [US].

### Uncommon to Rare Adverse Effects (<1%)
- **Cardiovascular:** Angina/chest pain (0.3%), arrhythmia, palpitations, hypotension, edema, dyspnea [US].
- **Nervous System:** Tremor (0.3%), agitation, nervousness, seizures, aseptic meningitis [US].
- **Digestive:** Anorexia, constipation, dry mouth, cholecystitis, hepatitis, cholestasis, increased liver enzymes [US].
- **Skin:** Brittle fingernails, pruritus, rash, urticaria, angioedema [US].
- **Hemic/Lymphatic:** Leukopenia, pancytopenia, aplastic anemia, thrombocytopenia, decreased serum fibrinogen [US].
- **Immune System:** Anaphylactic reaction, anaphylactic shock [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin/Vitamin K Antagonists:** Reports of increased prothrombin time and anticoagulant activity. More frequent monitoring of INR/prothrombin time is required upon initiation or dose change of pentoxifylline [US].
- **Theophylline:** May increase serum theophylline concentrations and the risk of toxicity [US].
- **CYP1A2 Inhibitors (e.g., Ciprofloxacin, Fluvoxamine):** May increase pentoxifylline exposure and associated adverse effects [US].
- **Cimetidine:** Increases steady-state plasma concentrations of pentoxifylline (approx. 25%) and its active Metabolite I (approx. 30%) [US].
- **Antihypertensives:** Potential for small decreases in blood pressure; dosage adjustment of the antihypertensive agent may be necessary [US].
- **NSAIDs/Antiplatelets:** Increased risk of bleeding [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Not specified by letter category in source. Use during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus. Rat studies showed increased resorption at high doses (24x MRHD), though no fetal malformations were observed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Pentoxifylline and its metabolites are excreted in human milk. Due to potential tumorigenicity observed in animal studies, a decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the drug's importance to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of mutagenic activity in Salmonella or mammalian cell tests [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in pediatric patients have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** AUC is increased and elimination rate is decreased in older populations (60–68 years). Cautious dose selection is required, typically starting at the low end of the range [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Exposure to the active Metabolite V is significantly increased in renal impairment (2.4-fold in mild/moderate; 10-12-fold in severe). In patients with **severe renal impairment (CrCl < 30 mL/min)**, the dose should be reduced to 400 mg once daily [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** AUC and Cmax of pentoxifylline and Metabolite I are increased 6.5 to 8.2-fold in mild-to-moderate impairment. Specific dosing recommendations for hepatic impairment cannot be provided based on current data [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Improves blood flow by decreasing viscosity and improving erythrocyte flexibility. It increases leukocyte deformability and inhibits neutrophil activation/adhesion, leading to enhanced tissue oxygenation in patients with peripheral arterial disease [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Almost completely absorbed; undergoes significant first-pass metabolism. Food increases the AUC of the ER tablet by approximately 1.1 fold [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolized by erythrocytes and the liver. Major metabolites are Metabolite I (active) and Metabolite V [US].
    - **Excretion:** Almost exclusively renal (Metabolite V). Half-life of parent drug is 0.4–0.8 hours; metabolites range from 1–1.6 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy:** Clinical trials demonstrating efficacy in intermittent claudication were double-blind and conducted for a duration of 6 months. Extended-release tablets were studied for up to 60 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15° and 30°C (59° and 86°F) [US].
- **Protection:** Protect from light [US].
- **Container:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container with a child-resistant closure [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx Only [US].
