# Editorial Product Information: Ponatinib

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions**: This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)** regulatory authority. 
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data into a concise reference.
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official, legally approved Product Information for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Ponatinib (as hydrochloride).
- **Chemical Class**: Antineoplastic agent; BCR::ABL1 tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI).
- **Brand Name(s)**: **ICLUSIG** [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: L01XE24.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s)**:
    - Film-coated tablets: 15 mg and 45 mg (currently marketed), and 10 mg and 30 mg (approved/not currently marketed) [Australian].
    - Identification: 10 mg (white, oval, "NZ"), 15 mg (white, round, "A5"), 30 mg (white, round, "C7"), 45 mg (white, round, "AP4").
- **Route(s) of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Takeda Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**ICLUSIG** is indicated for the treatment of adult patients with:
- **Chronic Myeloid Leukaemia (CML)**: Chronic phase (CP), accelerated phase (AP), or blast phase (BP) disease that is resistant to or intolerant of at least two prior tyrosine kinase inhibitors; or where the T315I mutation is present.
- **Philadelphia chromosome positive Acute Lymphoblastic Leukaemia (Ph+ ALL)**: Disease that is resistant to or intolerant of dasatinib and for whom subsequent treatment with imatinib is not clinically appropriate; or where the T315I mutation is present.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 General Dosing (Adult)
- **Starting Dose**: 45 mg once daily.
- **CP-CML Specific Titration**: Achievement of a molecular response ($\le$1% BCR::ABL1$^{IS}$) should prompt a dose reduction to 15 mg once daily. In cases of loss of response, the dose may be re-escalated to 30 mg or 45 mg. 
- **AP-CML, BP-CML, and Ph+ ALL**: Continue 45 mg daily. Dose reduction should be considered for AP-CML patients achieving a major cytogenetic response.
- **Discontinuation**: Consider cessation if a haematologic response has not occurred by 3 months.

### 3.2 Administration Instructions
- Tablets must be swallowed whole; do not crush, dissolve, or chew.
- May be taken with or without food.
- Take at approximately the same time each day.

### 3.3 Missed Dose
- If a dose is missed, the patient should skip the missed dose and take the next dose at the scheduled time. Do not take an additional dose to compensate.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to ponatinib hydrochloride or any excipient (including lactose monohydrate).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### 5.1 Boxed Warnings [Australian]
- **Arterial Occlusive Events (AOEs)**: Fatal myocardial infarction, stroke, stenosis of large cerebral arteries, and severe peripheral vascular disease (including those requiring amputation) have occurred. Risk increases with age and cardiovascular history.
- **Venous Thromboembolic Events (VTEs)**: Serious VTEs, including deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism, have been reported.
- **Heart Failure**: Fatal and serious cases have occurred. Monitor cardiac function.
- **Hypertension**: Includes hypertensive crisis. Monitor blood pressure at every visit.
- **Hepatotoxicity**: Liver failure and death have occurred. Monitor liver function tests (LFTs) at baseline and monthly.

### 5.2 General Warnings and Precautions
- **Pancreatitis and Lipase Elevation**: Check serum lipase every 2 weeks for the first 2 months.
- **Haemorrhage**: Serious and fatal haemorrhagic events have occurred; often associated with grade 3/4 thrombocytopenia.
- **QT Prolongation**: Measurement of baseline QT interval is recommended.
- **Myelosuppression**: Severe (Grade 3/4) thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, and anaemia occur frequently, particularly in the first 3 months.
- **Reversible Posterior Leukoencephalopathy Syndrome (RPLS)**: Monitor for neurological changes (seizure, headache, vision loss).
- **Hepatitis B Reactivation**: Test for HBV infection prior to initiation.

### 5.3 Monitoring Requirements
- **CBC**: Every 2 weeks for the first 3 months, then monthly.
- **Lipase**: Every 2 weeks for the first 2 months, then regularly.
- **LFTs**: Baseline, then at least monthly.
- **Cardiovascular Status**: Continuous assessment and management of risk factors (e.g., blood pressure, lipids).

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (>10%) | URTI, anaemia, decreased appetite, insomnia, headache, dizziness, hypertension, cough, dyspnoea, abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation, rash, dry skin, pruritus, arthralgia, fatigue, pyrexia, peripheral oedema. |
| **Common** (1-10%) | Pneumonia, sepsis, febrile neutropenia, hypothyroidism, hyperuricaemia, peripheral neuropathy, blurred vision, angina pectoris, atrial fibrillation, pleural effusion, pancreatitis, alopecia, erectile dysfunction. |
| **Uncommon** (0.1-1%) | Tumour lysis syndrome, cerebral haemorrhage, retinal artery occlusion, myocardial ischaemia, splenic infarction, hepatotoxicity, renal artery stenosis. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects**: Arterial occlusion occurred in 25% of patients in the PACE study and 14% in the OPTIC study (45mg cohort).

## 7. Drug Interactions
### 7.1 Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors**: (e.g., ketoconazole, ritonavir, clarithromycin) Increase ponatinib exposure. 
    - *Management*: Reduce ponatinib dose to 30 mg (from 45 mg) or 15 mg (from 30 mg).
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inducers**: (e.g., rifampicin, St John’s Wort) Decrease ponatinib exposure.
    - *Management*: Avoid concurrent use unless benefit outweighs risk.
- **Gastric pH**: Solubility is pH-dependent. However, clinical data suggest no dose adjustment is needed for concurrent use of proton pump inhibitors or H2 antagonists.

### 7.2 Transporter Interactions
- **P-gp and BCRP Substrates**: Ponatinib inhibits these transporters in vitro. Monitor for increased toxicity of substrates (e.g., digoxin, dabigatran, methotrexate, rosuvastatin).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: **Category D** [Australian]. Based on animal studies, ponatinib can cause fetal harm (embryo-fetal lethality and teratogenicity). Advise women and men of reproductive potential to use effective contraception.
- **Breastfeeding**: It is unknown if ponatinib is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding must be discontinued during treatment.
- **Fertility**: Animal data show potential impairment of female fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and efficacy in patients <18 years have not been established.
- **Elderly Use**: Patients $\ge$65 years are more likely to experience adverse reactions, particularly vascular occlusion and peripheral oedema.
- **Renal Impairment**: No dose adjustment for mild-to-moderate impairment (CrCl 30-89 mL/min). Caution in severe renal impairment or end-stage renal disease.
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Caution in moderate-to-severe impairment. A starting dose of 30 mg is recommended for these patients [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: A potent pan-BCR::ABL1 inhibitor. It inhibits native BCR::ABL1 and all tested mutations, including the gatekeeper T315I mutation. It also inhibits other kinases, including VEGFR, PDGFR, FGFR, EPH, and SRC families.
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Peak concentrations at ~4 hours.
    - **Distribution**: Highly protein bound (>99%).
    - **Metabolism**: Primarily via esterases/amidases and CYP3A4.
    - **Elimination**: Half-life ~22 hours. 87% excreted in faeces; 5% in urine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **PACE Study**: A phase 2, single-arm trial (N=449). Major Cytogenetic Response (MCyR) in CP-CML was 55% overall and 70% in those with T315I mutation.
- **OPTIC Study**: A dose-optimization trial for CP-CML. The 45 mg starting dose with reduction to 15 mg upon response demonstrated the most optimal benefit-risk profile, achieving a $\le$1% BCR::ABL1$^{IS}$ rate of 44% at 12 months.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **15 mg, 30 mg, 45 mg Tablets**: Store below 30°C.
- **10 mg Tablets**: Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Handling**: Store in the original container to protect from light.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule**: **Schedule 4 (S4)** (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
