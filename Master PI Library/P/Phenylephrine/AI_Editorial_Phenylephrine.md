# Editorial Product Information: Phenylephrine Hydrochloride (Ophthalmic)

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from the following official Product Information (PI) documents: 
    - **Australian (TGA)**: Minims Phenylephrine Hydrochloride Eye Drops PI.
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonized editorial overview for healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference only and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Phenylephrine hydrochloride.
- **Chemical Class**: Sympathomimetic agent; alpha-adrenergic agonist.
- **Brand Names**: Minims Phenylephrine [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: S01FB01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**: 
    - Single-use, preservative-free eye drops in 2.5% w/v (25 mg/mL) and 10% w/v (100 mg/mL) strengths [Australian].
- **Route of Administration**: Topical ophthalmic.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Bausch & Lomb (Australia) Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Mydriasis**: Indicated for the dilation of the pupil for diagnostic or therapeutic procedures [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing
- **Standard Dose**: Instil one drop topically into each eye.
- **Repeat Dosing**: If required, the dose may be repeated once only, at least one hour after the first instillation [Australian].

### Paediatric and Elderly Dosing
- **Strength Restriction**: Use is restricted to the 2.5% solution. 
- **10% Strength**: Contraindicated in children and the elderly due to heightened risks of systemic toxicity [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Local Anaesthetic**: Pre-treatment with a topical anaesthetic a few minutes prior to instillation is recommended to mitigate stinging.
- **Systemic Absorption Reduction**: To minimize systemic uptake, the lacrimal sac should be compressed at the medial canthus for one minute during and immediately following instillation. This is particularly critical in paediatric patients.
- **Single-Use**: Each unit is for single-patient use only and must be discarded immediately after use [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to phenylephrine hydrochloride or any excipients (e.g., sodium metabisulphite, disodium edetate).
- **10% Solution Specific**: Contraindicated in children and the elderly [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **General Warning**: For topical ophthalmic use only; must not be injected [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Risk**: Rare but serious cardiovascular reactions, including myocardial infarction, ventricular arrhythmias, and significant hypertension, have been reported, primarily in elderly patients with pre-existing cardiovascular disease.
- **Glaucoma**: Anterior chamber angle should be evaluated before use to avoid precipitating an attack of narrow-angle glaucoma (unless previously treated by iridectomy).
- **Corneal Damage**: Ocular hyperaemia or a damaged corneal epithelium may significantly increase systemic absorption. Instillation of the 10% strength in patients with a denuded corneal epithelium may lead to corneal clouding.
- **Specific Populations**: Exercise extreme caution in patients with:
    - Hypertension, cardiac disease, or tachycardia.
    - Aneurysms.
    - Long-standing insulin-dependent diabetes mellitus.
    - Hyperthyroidism.
    - Generalised arteriosclerosis.
    - Bronchial asthma [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Local Effects
- **Common**: Eye pain, stinging upon instillation, and transient blurred vision.
- **Other**: Photophobia, conjunctival allergy, reactive hyperaemia, transient punctate keratitis, lacrimation, corneal oedema, pigmented aqueous floaters (especially in older individuals), rebound miosis, and rebound conjunctival vasoconstriction.

### Systemic Effects
- **Cardiovascular**: Palpitations, tachycardia, extrasystoles, arrhythmias, hypertension, and reflex bradycardia.
- **Neurological/Other**: Headache, subarachnoid haemorrhage, tremors, blanching of the skin, and increased perspiration.
- **Paediatric Specific**: Periorbital pallor in preterm patients (2.5% strength); pulmonary oedema (10% strength). Frequencies are not established [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Anti-hypertensive Agents**: Topical phenylephrine may reverse the effects of many antihypertensives, potentially leading to fatal consequences.
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs)**: Increased risk of severe adrenergic reactions if used within three weeks of MAOIs.
- **Tricyclic Antidepressants (TCAs)**: Potentiation of the pressor response and risk of cardiac arrhythmias (within several days of TCA discontinuation).
- **General Anaesthetics (e.g., Halothane)**: Increased risk of ventricular fibrillation when used with agents that sensitise the myocardium to sympathomimetics.
- **Cardiac Glycosides or Quinidine**: Increased risk of arrhythmias [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **Australian Category B2**: Safety has not been established. Use only if considered essential by a physician.
- **Breastfeeding**: Safety not established. Use only if considered essential by a physician.
- **Fertility**: No data available regarding the potential to impair fertility in humans or animals [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: The 10% strength is contraindicated. Serious systemic reactions have occurred in children under 12. Clinical experience in children aged 12–18 is limited; the 2.5% strength is preferred if use is indicated.
- **Elderly Use**: The 10% strength is contraindicated due to systemic toxicity risks. Older individuals may develop transient pigment floaters in the aqueous humor 30–45 minutes post-instillation.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment**: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Direct-acting alpha-adrenergic agonist (alpha-1A and alpha-1B). It induces mydriasis via stimulation of the dilator muscle.
- **Pharmacodynamics**: Phenylephrine has almost no cycloplegic effect. Mydriasis peaks within 10–90 minutes, with recovery occurring after 5–7 hours. It does not significantly affect intraocular pressure in open-angle glaucoma.
- **Pharmacokinetics**: Ocular penetration depends on corneal integrity; a damaged cornea increases absorption. Systemic absorption occurs via the naso-lacrimal duct and the pharyngeal mucosa [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Data regarding pivotal clinical trials were not provided in the source documentation.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store between 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze).
- **Protection**: Protect from strong light.
- **Shelf Life**: Consult the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule**: 
    - **Australian**: 2.5% strength is Schedule 2 (Pharmacy Medicine); 10% strength is Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
