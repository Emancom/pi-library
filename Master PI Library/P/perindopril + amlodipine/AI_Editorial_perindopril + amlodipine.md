# Editorial Product Information: perindopril + amlodipine

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **perindopril + amlodipine**
- Chemical class:
  - Perindopril: angiotensin‑converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitor
  - Amlodipine: dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Indicated as substitution (fixed‑dose combination) therapy for the treatment of:
  - Hypertension, and/or
  - Stable coronary heart disease,
  in patients already controlled on separate concurrent perindopril and amlodipine given at the same dose level.
- Important prescribing note: Treatment should not be initiated with the fixed‑dose combination; it is intended for substitution when a patient is already controlled on the two agents given separately at equivalent doses.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Fixed tablet strengths available for substitution: perindopril 5 mg/amlodipine 5 mg; 5 mg/10 mg; 10 mg/5 mg; 10 mg/10 mg (use only to substitute equivalent separate doses of perindopril and amlodipine).
- Recommended: one tablet once daily as a single dose.

### Dose conversion guidance (substitution)
- Perindopril arginine 5 mg (or perindopril erbumine 4 mg) + amlodipine 5 mg → perindopril/amlodipine 5/5 tablet
- Perindopril arginine 5 mg (or perindopril erbumine 4 mg) + amlodipine 10 mg → perindopril/amlodipine 5/10 tablet
- Perindopril arginine 10 mg (or perindopril erbumine 8 mg) + amlodipine 5 mg → perindopril/amlodipine 10/5 tablet
- Perindopril arginine 10 mg (or perindopril erbumine 8 mg) + amlodipine 10 mg → perindopril/amlodipine 10/10 tablet

### Administration instructions
- Preferably taken in the morning and before a meal.
- Food may reduce hepatic biotransformation of perindopril to the active metabolite perindoprilat; administer consistently with regard to meals.

### Titration and dose adjustments
- Because perindopril and amlodipine may be used for different indications, individual dose adjustments should be based on clinical judgement.
- To adjust dose, use separate perindopril and/or amlodipine products until clinical stability is re‑established.
- For down‑titration when required, consider using:
  - Amlodipine 2.5 mg (separate product), or
  - Perindopril equivalent to perindopril arginine 2.5 mg (separate product).

### Missed dose
- Not specified in available sources

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to perindopril, amlodipine, ACE inhibitors, dihydropyridines or any excipient in the product.
- Pregnancy.
- Lactation (breastfeeding).
- History of hereditary and/or idiopathic angioedema or angioedema associated with previous ACE inhibitor treatment.
- Bilateral or unilateral renal artery stenosis.
- Extracorporeal treatments leading to blood contact with negatively charged surfaces (e.g. dialysis/haemofiltration with certain high‑flux membranes such as polyacrylonitrile "AN69") and low density lipoprotein apheresis with dextran sulfate because of increased risk of severe anaphylactoid reactions following ACE inhibitor treatment.
- Combined use with aliskiren‑containing products in patients with diabetes or renal impairment (GFR < 60 mL/min/1.73 m2).
- Combined use with sacubitril/valsartan fixed‑dose combinations (the combination is contraindicated). Do not initiate this product within 36 hours of the last dose of sacubitril/valsartan; conversely, if sacubitril/valsartan is started, do not initiate this product until 36 hours after the last dose of sacubitril/valsartan.
- Related to the amlodipine component:
  - Severe hypotension
  - Shock, including cardiogenic shock
  - Obstruction of left ventricular outflow tract (e.g. severe aortic stenosis)
  - Unstable angina pectoris (excluding Prinzmetal's angina)
  - Heart failure following acute myocardial infarction

(All contraindications applicable to the individual components apply to the fixed combination.)

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources

### General warnings and precautions
- Hyperkalaemia: ACE inhibitors reduce angiotensin II → decreased aldosterone → potential for increased serum potassium. Monitor serum electrolytes, particularly in patients with renal impairment, diabetes, elderly patients (>70 years), those on potassium supplements, potassium‑sparing diuretics, or other medicines that increase potassium.
- Hypotension: Symptomatic hypotension may occur, especially in volume‑depleted patients (diuretics, severe diarrhoea or vomiting), patients with impaired renal function, severe renin‑dependent hypertension, or those with heart failure. Monitor blood pressure; manage symptomatic hypotension with supine position and volume expansion (e.g. normal saline) as needed.
- Renovascular disease: In renovascular hypertension (e.g. renal artery stenosis) there is an increased risk of severe hypotension and renal insufficiency. Begin treatment under close supervision and monitor renal function.
- Renal function and electrolytes: Because elimination of perindoprilat is decreased in the elderly and in patients with renal failure, monitor creatinine and potassium regularly. Adjust dosing using separate products when required (see Section 4 Dosage).
- Hepatic impairment: Use with caution in mild‑to‑moderate hepatic impairment. Start at the lower end of the dosing range; start amlodipine at 2.5 mg once daily.
- Cough: Persistent dry, non‑productive cough may occur; incidence varies (see Adverse Effects).
- Angioedema: Life‑threatening angioedema has been reported with ACE inhibitors; may affect face, extremities, lips, tongue, glottis and larynx and may be fatal. Intestinal angioedema has also been reported. Discontinue drug if angioedema occurs; provide appropriate emergency treatment and observation for possible rebound.
- Anaphylactoid/anaphylactic reactions during extracorporeal treatments and desensitisation: Severe reactions have been reported in patients receiving ACE inhibitors during LDL apheresis with dextran sulfate, dialysis with some membranes, or during desensitisation treatments. Withhold ACE inhibitor therapy prior to these procedures where appropriate and consider alternative dialysis membranes or antihypertensives.
- Haematologic effects: Neutropenia/agranulocytosis, thrombocytopenia and anaemia have been reported with ACE inhibitors; use perindopril with extreme caution in patients with collagen vascular disease, on immunosuppressants, allopurinol, procainamide, or with pre‑existing renal impairment. Consider periodic WBC monitoring.
- Dermatologic reactions: Maculopapular pruritic rashes and photosensitivity reported; rare severe skin reactions (lichenoid eruptions, psoriasis, pemphigus‑like rash, rosacea, Stevens‑Johnson syndrome) have been reported.
- Proteinuria: Perindopril therapy has occasionally been associated with mild or transient proteinuria (< 1 g/24 h).
- Ethnicity: ACE inhibitors have a higher incidence of angioedema in patients of indigenous African origin and may be less effective in lowering blood pressure in these patients (higher prevalence of low‑renin states). It is unknown whether similar observations apply to indigenous Australian populations.
- Diabetes: In patients with diabetes treated with oral hypoglycaemic agents or insulin, monitor glycaemic control closely during the first month of ACE inhibitor therapy.
- Lithium: Combination with lithium is not recommended.
- Dantrolene: Combination with dantrolene is not recommended.

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and periodic monitoring of:
  - Serum electrolytes (sodium, potassium)
  - Renal function (creatinine, urea)
  - Blood pressure
  - Full blood count (where risk factors for neutropenia/agranulocytosis exist)
  - Hepatic enzymes and bilirubin if hepatic dysfunction suspected
- Monitor diabetic patients’ glycaemic control closely during the first month after initiation.

### Specific clinical precautions
- In patients with heart failure or significant coronary/cerebrovascular disease, monitor closely for excessive blood pressure lowering, especially during the first two weeks of treatment and after dose changes or initiation/intensification of diuretics.
- In elderly, small or frail patients start amlodipine at 2.5 mg and perindopril at low dose (equivalent to perindopril arginine 2.5 mg); titrate carefully.
- If hypotension occurs, temporarily discontinue diuretics or reduce doses of ACE inhibitor until stability is re‑established, and correct volume depletion.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Major interactions and clinical management
- Potassium‑sparing diuretics (e.g. spironolactone, eplerenone, triamterene, amiloride), potassium supplements or potassium‑containing salt substitutes:
  - Risk: Marked hyperkalaemia.
  - Management: Avoid combination where possible; if co‑administration is necessary, monitor serum potassium and renal function frequently.
- Other medicines that increase serum potassium (e.g. heparin, co‑trimoxazole/trimethoprim‑sulfamethoxazole, ARBs, other ACE inhibitors, high‑dose aspirin ≥3 g/day, COX‑2 inhibitors, non‑selective NSAIDs):
  - Risk: Hyperkalaemia and/or renal impairment.
  - Management: Use with caution; monitor electrolytes and renal function.
- Diuretics (particularly loop diuretics, aggressive diuretic therapy):
  - Risk: Volume depletion and symptomatic hypotension on initiation of ACE inhibitor.
  - Management: Consider reducing diuretic dose or withholding prior to starting perindopril; monitor blood pressure and renal function closely during initiation.
- Lithium:
  - Risk: Increased lithium concentrations and lithium toxicity.
  - Management: Combination not recommended; if combined, monitor lithium levels closely.
- Dantrolene:
  - Risk: Not recommended to combine with perindopril (specific risk not specified).
  - Management: Combination not recommended.
- Aliskiren:
  - Risk: Combination with aliskiren in patients with diabetes or renal impairment (GFR <60) is contraindicated.
- Sacubitril/valsartan and other neprilysin (NEP) inhibitors:
  - Risk: Increased risk of angioedema when combined with ACE inhibitors.
  - Management: Combination is contraindicated; do not initiate perindopril/amlodipine within 36 hours of last sacubitril/valsartan dose and vice versa (36‑hour washout required).
- Mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) inhibitors (e.g. temsirolimus, sirolimus, everolimus) and DPP‑4 inhibitors ("gliptins", e.g. linagliptin, saxagliptin, sitagliptin, vildagliptin, alogliptin):
  - Risk: Possible increased risk of angioedema when combined with ACE inhibitors.
  - Management: Use caution when commencing these medicines in patients already on an ACE inhibitor; monitor for signs of angioedema.
- LDL apheresis with dextran sulfate and dialysis with certain high‑flux membranes (e.g. polyacrylonitrile "AN69"):
  - Risk: Life‑threatening anaphylactoid reactions when ACE inhibitors are present.
  - Management: Consider temporarily withholding ACE inhibitor therapy before each apheresis; avoid ACE inhibitors or use alternative membranes (cuprophane or polysulfone) if extracorporeal treatment is necessary.
- Non‑steroidal anti‑inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) and COX‑2 inhibitors:
  - Risk: May reduce antihypertensive effect and worsen renal function; combined use with ACE inhibitors and diuretics may precipitate renal impairment and hyperkalaemia.
  - Management: Monitor renal function and electrolytes; avoid in high‑risk patients where possible.

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Food may reduce hepatic conversion of perindopril to perindoprilat (see Section 4 Dosage); administer consistently with regard to meals.
- Amlodipine is not dialysable; changes in amlodipine plasma concentrations are not correlated with the degree of renal impairment.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
- Paediatric use:
  - Not specified in available sources
- Elderly:
  - Elimination of perindoprilat is decreased in the elderly.
  - Start with lower doses; consider starting amlodipine at 2.5 mg once daily and perindopril at a dose equivalent to perindopril arginine 2.5 mg daily; monitor blood pressure, renal function and electrolytes closely during initiation and titration.
- Renal impairment:
  - Elimination of perindoprilat decreased with renal impairment; monitor creatinine and potassium frequently.
  - Where down‑titration is required for patients with CrCl < 60 mL/min, consider adjusting doses using separate perindopril and/or amlodipine products, e.g. amlodipine 2.5 mg or perindopril equivalent to perindopril arginine 2.5 mg, until stability is achieved.
  - Amlodipine plasma concentration changes are not correlated with degree of renal impairment; amlodipine is not dialysable.
- Hepatic impairment:
  - Dosage recommendations are not established for mild to moderate hepatic impairment; use with caution and start at the lower end of the dosing range.
  - Start amlodipine at 2.5 mg once daily in hepatic impairment.
  - Severe hepatic impairment: Not specified in available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Not fully specified in available sources. (The available text notes that ACE inhibition by perindopril reduces angiotensin II formation and thereby decreases aldosterone production, which can lead to increased serum potassium.)

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption/Metabolism:
  - Food may reduce hepatic biotransformation of perindopril to perindoprilat.
  - Perindopril is metabolised to perindoprilat (active metabolite).
- Distribution:
  - Not specified in available sources
- Elimination:
  - Elimination of perindoprilat is decreased in elderly patients and in those with renal failure.
  - Amlodipine is not dialysable.
  - Changes in amlodipine plasma concentrations are not correlated with degree of renal impairment.
- Other pharmacokinetic parameters (half‑life, bioavailability, protein binding, metabolites): Not specified in available sources

### Pharmacodynamics
