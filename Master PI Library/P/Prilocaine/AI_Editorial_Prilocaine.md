# Editorial Product Information: Prilocaine Hydrochloride and Felypressin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data into a concise, structured format.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Prilocaine hydrochloride (amide-type local anaesthetic) and Felypressin (vasoconstrictor; synthetic analogue of vasopressin).
- **Brand Name:** Citanest DENTAL with Octapressin [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N01BB54.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injection solution containing 30 mg/mL prilocaine hydrochloride and 0.54 micrograms/mL (0.03 IU/mL) felypressin [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Local infiltration or nerve block (dental).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Dentsply Sirona Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Dental Anaesthesia:** Indicated for the production of local anaesthesia in routine dental procedures and oral surgery using infiltration or nerve block techniques [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
The lowest effective dose required to achieve adequate anaesthesia should be utilised. Dosage must be individualised based on the area of the oral cavity, tissue vascularity, and the specific technique employed.

**Recommended dosage for a healthy 70 kg adult:**
- **Infiltration:** 1 mL
- **Nerve Block:** 1.5 – 2 mL

### Maximum Recommended Dose
- **Prilocaine:** The maximum dose should not exceed 9 mg/kg body weight [Australian].
- **Clinical Note:** Doses should be significantly reduced in patients with acute or chronic systemic disease.

### Administration Instructions
- **Aspiration:** Frequent aspiration is mandatory to avoid inadvertent intravascular injection, which can lead to systemic toxicity.
- **Rate of Injection:** Administer slowly.
- **Spacing:** Multiple injections should be administered at spaced intervals.
- **Technique:** Clinicians should refer to standard textbooks for specific nerve block and infiltration techniques.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity or allergy to amide-type local anaesthetics or any excipients in the formulation [Australian].
- Congenital or idiopathic methaemoglobinaemia [Australian].
- Presence of inflammation or sepsis at the intended site of injection [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Precautions
- **Resuscitation Facilities:** Local anaesthetics should only be administered in settings where oxygen, resuscitative equipment, and emergency medications are immediately available to manage cardiovascular, respiratory, or central nervous system (CNS) toxicity.
- **Cardiovascular Disease:** Use with extreme caution in patients with severe bradycardia, cardiac conduction disturbances, severe heart disease, or severe hypertension [Australian].
- **Systemic Disease:** Caution is required in patients with advanced diabetes, severe anaemia, or circulatory failure.
- **Malignant Hyperthermia:** Use with caution in patients with a genetic predisposition; the safety of amide anaesthetics in this population is not fully established.

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Metabolite Toxicity:** Prilocaine is metabolised to *o*-toluidine, which is responsible for the formation of methaemoglobin. Risk increases with dose (see Section 6).
- **Tissue Trauma:** Patients should be advised to avoid eating or biting the lips/tongue until normal sensation returns to prevent self-inflicted soft tissue trauma.
- **Felypressin Effects:** While generally free of side effects at dental doses, excessive amounts of felypressin may cause coronary constriction or elevated blood pressure [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Systemic Toxicity
Adverse effects are rare at standard dental doses and typically result from high plasma levels due to excessive dosage, rapid absorption, or accidental intravascular injection.

- **CNS Effects:** Initial excitation (nervousness, dizziness, blurred vision, tremors), followed by depression (drowsiness, convulsions, unconsciousness, and potential respiratory arrest).
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** Usually depressant; includes hypotension, myocardial depression, bradycardia, and potential cardiac arrest.
- **Psychogenic Reactions:** Common in dental settings; may include fainting or sweating, which can mimic systemic toxicity.

### Methaemoglobinaemia and Cyanosis
- **Mechanism:** Caused by the metabolite *o*-toluidine.
- **Incidence:** Remote at dental doses (30–150 mg); clinical cyanosis is extremely rare at doses below 400 mg. At 600 mg, methaemoglobin levels remain below 15% (usually symptomless in healthy patients) [Australian].
- **Treatment:** Persistent cyanosis may be treated with **methylene blue** (1% solution) at 1 mg/kg IV over 5 minutes.

### Neurological Reactions
- Persistent numbness, paraesthesia, and other sensory disturbances have been reported following regional nerve blocks [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Anti-arrhythmics:** Use with caution in patients taking medications such as mexiletine, as toxic effects may be additive [Australian].
- **Methaemoglobin-inducing Agents:** Sulfonamides, antimalarials, and certain nitric compounds may potentiate prilocaine-induced methaemoglobinaemia [Australian].
- **Digitalis:** Use with great caution in patients with severe digitalis intoxication [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy Category:** **Category A** [Australian].
- **Clinical Summary:** Extensively used in dental procedures during pregnancy without reports of malformations or direct foetal harm. However, large doses may cause maternal/foetal methaemoglobinaemia and subsequent hypoxia [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Prilocaine enters breast milk in negligible amounts; risk to the neonate is considered minimal at clinical doses. The excretion of felypressin into breast milk is not documented.
- **Fertility:** Data not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosage must be reduced according to body weight and the child's physical status [Australian].
- **Elderly:** While dental doses are typically small, elderly patients may require special attention and potentially reduced doses due to the risk of accumulation in the presence of reduced organ function.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Prilocaine and its metabolites may accumulate during repeated administration in patients with hepatic or renal disease, though this is unlikely at standard dental doses [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Prilocaine is a membrane-stabilising agent that reversibly prevents the initiation and conduction of nerve impulses by blocking sodium channels. Its potency and onset are similar to lignocaine, but it possesses lower systemic toxicity.

### Pharmacodynamics
- **Felypressin:** Added as a non-sympathomimetic vasoconstrictor to slow the absorption of prilocaine, thereby increasing the duration of anaesthesia and reducing peak plasma levels. Unlike adrenaline, it does not produce significant ischaemia distal to the injection site [Australian].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption/Distribution:** Onset of action is approximately 2 minutes (infiltration) to 4 minutes (nerve block). Duration varies by technique (pulp anaesthesia: 40–90 mins; soft tissue: 2.5–3.5 hours) [Australian].
- **Metabolism:** Metabolised in both the liver and kidneys. A key metabolite is *o*-toluidine.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Handling:** Protect from light. Do not autoclave cartridges.
- **Safety Note:** Avoid prolonged contact with metal (e.g., pre-loading needles) to prevent the release of metal ions that can cause local irritation. Discard cartridges that are cracked or discoloured.
