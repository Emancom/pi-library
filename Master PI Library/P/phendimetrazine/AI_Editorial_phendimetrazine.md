# Editorial Product Information: Phendimetrazine Tartrate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview intended for healthcare professionals to provide clinically relevant information in a concise format. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial resource and does not substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Phendimetrazine tartrate (a sympathomimetic amine; dextro isomer).
- **Chemical Class:** Sympathomimetic amine; Morpholine derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Phendimetrazine Tartrate (generic) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A08AA02.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
  - Oral tablets: 35 mg [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** KVK-Tech, Inc. (Manufacturer); Calvin Scott & Co., Inc. (Labeler/Registrant) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Exogenous Obesity:** Indicated as a short-term (a few weeks) adjunct in a weight reduction regimen based on caloric restriction for patients with an initial Body Mass Index (BMI) $\ge$ 30 kg/m² who have failed to respond to diet and exercise alone [US].
- **Monotherapy:** Specifically indicated for use as monotherapy only [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The usual dose is 35 mg (one tablet) administered two or three times daily [US].
- **Administration Timing:** Doses should be taken one hour before meals [US].
- **Individualisation:** Dosage should be titrated to the lowest effective dose. In some patients, 17.5 mg (half tablet) per dose may be sufficient [US].
- **Maximum Dosage:** Do not exceed 70 mg (two tablets) three times per day [US].
- **Treatment Duration:** Limited to "a few weeks" due to the development of tolerance to the anorectic effect. Treatment should be discontinued if tolerance occurs [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Cardiovascular Disease:** Advanced arteriosclerosis, symptomatic cardiovascular disease, and moderate to severe hypertension [US].
- **Endocrine/Metabolic:** Hyperthyroidism and glaucoma [US].
- **Psychiatric:** Highly nervous or agitated states [US].
- **History of Substance Use:** Patients with a history of drug abuse [US].
- **Drug Interactions:** Concurrent use of other CNS stimulants or use of Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs) within the preceding 14 days [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity or idiosyncratic reactions to sympathomimetic amines [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Combination Therapy Warning:** **Phendimetrazine** must not be used in combination with other anorectic agents, including prescription drugs, OTC products, or herbal supplements [US].
- **Pulmonary Hypertension:** Use of anorectic agents is associated with an increased risk of pulmonary hypertension, a rare but often fatal disorder. Use for >3 months increases risk 23-fold. Discontinue immediately if exertional dyspnea, angina, syncope, or lower extremity edema occur [US].
- **Valvular Heart Disease:** While associated with other anorectics (e.g., fenfluramine), clinicians should monitor for valvulopathy. Baseline cardiac evaluation is recommended in patients with known murmurs. Treatment should only continue if weight loss is $\ge$ 4 lbs in the first 4 weeks [US].
- **CNS Effects:** May impair the ability to operate heavy machinery or drive motor vehicles. Abrupt cessation after high-dose prolonged use can result in extreme fatigue and mental depression [US].
- **Tolerance:** Effectiveness is lost within a few weeks; the dose should not be increased to overcome tolerance [US].
- **Tartrazine Allergy:** Yellow 35 mg tablets contain FD&C Yellow No. 5 (tartrazine), which may cause allergic reactions (including asthma) in susceptible individuals, particularly those with aspirin hypersensitivity [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Note: Frequencies are not specified in the available source document.*

- **Cardiovascular:** Palpitations, tachycardia, hypertension, ischemic events, and potential for valvular heart disease.
- **Central Nervous System:** Overstimulation, restlessness, insomnia, agitation, flushing, tremor, sweating, dizziness, headache, psychotic states, and blurred vision.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Dry mouth (xerostomia), nausea, diarrhea, constipation, and abdominal pain.
- **Genitourinary:** Urinary frequency, dysuria, and changes in libido.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Pulmonary hypertension, valvular heart disease, and drug-induced psychosis (clinically indistinguishable from schizophrenia) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **MAOIs:** Use within 14 days of phendimetrazine may result in a hypertensive crisis [US].
- **Guanethidine:** Phendimetrazine may decrease the hypotensive efficacy of guanethidine [US].
- **Antidiabetic Agents:** Insulin requirements in patients with diabetes mellitus may be altered due to the drug and the concomitant weight-loss diet [US].
- **Other Anorectics:** Combined use may lead to serious cardiac problems; efficacy of such combinations has not been established [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **US Category C.** Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. It is unknown if phendimetrazine causes fetal harm. Use is not recommended unless the potential benefits clearly outweigh the risks [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is not known if phendimetrazine is excreted in human milk. Administration to nursing mothers is not recommended [US].
- **Fertility:** Studies have not been performed to evaluate effects on fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in paediatric patients have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources (Note: Major route of elimination is renal) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Prior Anorectic Use:** Not recommended for patients who have used any anorectic agent within the prior year [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A sympathomimetic amine with pharmacological activity similar to amphetamines. Its primary action is central nervous system stimulation and elevation of blood pressure. The exact mechanism in obesity is not fully established but may involve appetite suppression and other metabolic effects [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Absorption of the immediate-release 35 mg tablet is rapid [US].
  - **Metabolism:** Partially metabolised to phendimetrazine and phendimetrazine-N-oxide [US].
  - **Elimination:** Primarily excreted via the kidneys.
  - **Half-life:** The average elimination half-life is approximately 3.7 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Short-term Efficacy:** Clinical trials indicate that obese subjects treated with anorectic drugs and dietary management lose more weight on average than those treated with placebo and diet.
- **Weight Loss Magnitude:** Increased weight loss compared to placebo is typically only a fraction of a pound per week. The highest rate of loss occurs in the first weeks of therapy and decreases thereafter [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Excursions:** Permitted between 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as a **Schedule III (CIII)** controlled substance due to its chemical and pharmacological relationship to amphetamines. Abuse may lead to intense psychological dependence and social dysfunction [US].
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
