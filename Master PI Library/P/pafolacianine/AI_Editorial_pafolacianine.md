# Editorial Product Information: Pafolacianine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a single clinical reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pafolacianine (as pafolacianine sodium).
- **Chemical Class:** Optical imaging agent (folate receptor-targeted).
- **Brand Name:** **CYTALUX** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injection: 3.2 mg/1.6 mL (2 mg/mL) pafolacianine in a single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** On Target Laboratories, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Ovarian Cancer:** As an adjunct for the intraoperative identification of malignant lesions in adult patients [US].
- **Lung Cancer:** As an adjunct for the intraoperative identification of malignant and non-malignant pulmonary lesions in adult patients with known or suspected lung cancer [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
The recommended intravenous dosage for both indications is **0.025 mg/kg** [US].

### Indication-Specific Timing
- **Ovarian Cancer:** Administer 1 to 9 hours prior to surgery [US].
- **Lung Cancer:** Administer 1 to 24 hours prior to surgery [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Dilution:** Must be diluted in 250 mL of **5% Dextrose Injection** only. Do not use other diluents (e.g., Normal Saline) as they may cause pafolacianine aggregation [US].
- **Infusion Rate:** Administer over 60 minutes using a dedicated infusion line [US].
- **Premedication:** Consider antihistamines and/or anti-nausea medications as prophylaxis for infusion-related reactions [US].
- **Imaging:** Use only with Near-Infrared (NIR) imaging systems cleared for use with pafolacianine by relevant authorities. Surgeons should be specifically trained in the use of these systems [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None listed in available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Infusion-Related Reactions (IRRs):** IRRs (e.g., nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, flushing, hypersensitivity, hypertension) occurred in 17% of patients, typically within 15 minutes of starting the infusion. If an IRR occurs, the infusion should be interrupted and treated as clinically indicated [US].
- **Risk of Misinterpretation:** Imaging results may include false negatives and false positives. Non-fluorescing tissue does not guarantee the absence of malignancy. Conversely, fluorescence may be observed in non-cancerous tissues, including inflamed tissue, the bowel, kidneys, lymph nodes, and lungs [US].
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Based on the mechanism of action, pafolacianine may cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status in females of reproductive potential prior to administration [US].
- **Aggregation Risk:** Use only 5% Dextrose for dilution to avoid drug aggregation, which may increase the risk of infusion reactions [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions were reported in 17% of patients during administration.
- **Common (1–10%):** Nausea (13%), Vomiting (5%), Abdominal pain (2%), Flushing (2%), Hypersensitivity (2%), Infusion-related reactions (2%) [US].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Elevation in blood pressure (1%), Dyspepsia (1%), Chest discomfort (1%) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Folate/Folic Acid Supplements:** High levels of folate can compete for folate receptor binding, potentially reducing the diagnostic efficacy of pafolacianine. **Avoid** folate, folic acid, or folate-containing supplements for at least 48 hours prior to administration [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Pafolacianine is not a significant inhibitor of major CYP enzymes (1A2, 2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 3A4/5) or UGT1A1. It is a substrate for OATP1B1, OATP1B3, and OAT1 [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: No specific letter category]. Animal studies in rats and rabbits showed no adverse developmental effects at doses significantly higher than the human dose; however, fetal harm is possible based on its mechanism. Clinical use should be avoided if pregnancy is confirmed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on the presence of pafolacianine in human milk or its effects on the breastfed infant. The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need for the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies have not been performed to evaluate effects on fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established in patients under 18 years of age [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed between patients ≥65 years and younger adults [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No clinically significant pharmacokinetic differences were noted in patients with mild to moderate renal impairment (CLcr 30–89 mL/min). The effects of severe renal impairment have not been studied [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No clinically significant pharmacokinetic differences were noted in mild to moderate hepatic impairment. Severe hepatic impairment has not been studied [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Pafolacianine is a fluorescent drug that targets folate receptors (FR), specifically alpha and beta isoforms, which are overexpressed in various cancer cells. Upon binding, it is internalized via receptor-mediated endocytosis, allowing for fluorescence-guided visualization [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Peak fluorescence absorption occurs at 776 nm, with peak emission at 796 nm (near-infrared range) [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is 93.7%; Vz is 17.1 L [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Not metabolized by CYP enzymes [US].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is 0.44 hours; plasma clearance is 28.6 L/hr. Approximately 35% of the dose is recovered (19.1% in urine; 15.8% in feces) over 3-5 weeks [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Ovarian Cancer (NCT03180307):** A randomized, multicenter, open-label study (N=134) showed that pafolacianine identified at least one cancerous lesion not detected by normal light or palpation in 26.9% of patients (95% CI: 19.6%, 35.2%) [US].
- **Lung Cancer (NCT04241315):** A randomized, multicenter, open-label study (N=100) found that pafolacianine identified clinically significant events (localization of primary lesions or detection of synchronous malignant lesions) not found by standard methods in 24% of patients (95% CI: 16%, 33.6%) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store frozen between **-25°C to -15°C (-13°F to 5°F)** in the original carton to protect from light [US].
- **Thawing:** Vials can be thawed at room temperature (60 mins) or under refrigeration (6 hours). Thawed vials can be stored refrigerated for up to 30 days or at room temperature for up to 24 hours. They may be refrozen up to three times [US].
- **Diluted Solution:** If not used immediately, store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C for up to 24 hours. Infusion must be completed within 3 hours of reaching room temperature [US].
