# Editorial Product Information: Potassium Bicarbonate

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Regulatory Agencies:** Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA - Australia); Food and Drug Administration (FDA - United States).
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of official Product Information from the listed jurisdictions, designed for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial is a clinical summary and does not substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Note that the FDA-sourced document (Klor-Con/EF) includes a disclaimer stating the drug has not been formally found by the FDA to be safe and effective, and the labeling has not been approved by the FDA [FDA].

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Potassium bicarbonate. Note: Some formulations are multi-salt combinations containing potassium chloride and potassium carbonate [TGA].
- **Chemical Class:** Electrolyte/Potassium supplement.
- **Brand Names:** 
    - **Chlorvescent** (Potassium chloride, potassium bicarbonate, and potassium carbonate) [TGA].
    - **Klor-Con/EF** (Potassium bicarbonate and citric acid) [FDA].
- **ATC Code:** A12BA04 (Potassium bicarbonate); A12BA30 (Potassium combinations).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Effervescent Tablet [TGA]:** 14 mmol (548 mg) potassium and 8 mmol (283 mg) chloride.
    - **Effervescent Tablet for Oral Solution [FDA]:** 25 mEq (978 mg) potassium (as bicarbonate and citrate) per sugar-free tablet.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral (after dissolution in water).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** 
    - Aspen Pharmacare Australia Pty Ltd [TGA].
    - Upsher-Smith Laboratories, LLC [FDA].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Treatment and prevention of hypokalaemia:** Indicated for patients with or without metabolic alkalosis [TGA, FDA].
- **Digitalis intoxication:** For the treatment of associated hypokalaemia [FDA].
- **Hypokalaemic familial periodic paralysis:** [FDA-approved].
- **Prophylaxis:** For patients at particular risk if hypokalaemia were to develop, such as those with significant cardiac arrhythmias or those taking digitalis [FDA].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **TGA Recommendation:** 1 to 2 tablets dissolved in 120–240 mL of cold water, two to three times daily.
- **FDA Recommendation:** 25 to 100 mEq per day, typically 1 tablet (25 mEq) taken 1 to 4 times daily. 
- **Dose Adjustment:** Dosage must be individualised based on the patient's serum potassium levels. Prevention of hypokalaemia typically requires 25 mEq/day, while depletion may require 50–100 mEq/day [FDA].

### Administration Instructions
- **Dissolution:** Tablets must be completely dissolved in cold water (at least 120 mL or 4 ounces) before ingestion to avoid gastric irritation [TGA, FDA].
- **Timing:** Administer with or immediately after food [TGA, FDA].
- **Dose Splitting:** If daily doses exceed 25 mEq, the total dose should be divided so that no single dose exceeds 25 mEq [FDA].

## 4. Contraindications
Potassium supplements are contraindicated in the following conditions:
- **Hyperkalaemia:** From any cause, as further increases in serum potassium can lead to cardiac arrest [TGA, FDA].
- **Renal Impairment:** Chronic renal failure or impairment [TGA, FDA].
- **Potassium-Sparing Diuretics:** Concomitant use with agents such as spironolactone, triamterene, or amiloride [TGA, FDA].
- **Gastrointestinal Disease:** Acute peptic ulcer or gastritis [TGA].
- **Metabolic/Systemic Conditions:** Adrenal insufficiency (e.g., Addison’s disease), acute dehydration, or systemic acidosis (e.g., diabetic acidosis) [TGA, FDA].
- **Tissue Trauma:** Extensive tissue breakdown, such as severe burns or crush injuries [TGA, FDA].
- **Other:** Heat cramps or therapy with any drugs that may cause hyperkalaemia [TGA].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hyperkalaemia Risk:** Potentially fatal hyperkalaemia can develop rapidly and may be asymptomatic. It occurs most commonly in patients with impaired potassium excretion mechanisms [TGA, FDA].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Periodic monitoring of serum potassium, acid-base balance, and the clinical status of the patient is essential. ECG monitoring is recommended during replacement therapy in patients with cardiac disease or those receiving digitalis [TGA, FDA].
- **Cardiac Disease:** Proceed with caution in patients with cardiac disease, as the extent of potassium deficiency cannot be accurately determined [TGA, FDA].
- **Gastrointestinal Irritation:** Concentrated potassium preparations can cause gastric irritation. Proper dilution and administration with food are mandatory [TGA, FDA].
- **Laboratory Artifacts:** Artifactual elevations of plasma potassium can occur due to improper venipuncture technique or *in vitro* hemolysis [FDA].
- **Specific Clinical Precautions:** Use with caution in patients with liver cirrhosis [TGA]. Delayed GI transit (e.g., in pregnant patients or those taking anti-muscarinics) requires cautious administration [TGA].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal discomfort/pain, and flatulence [TGA, FDA]. These side effects are more frequent if the medication is not taken with food or is insufficiently diluted.
- **Serious (Hyperkalaemia):** Although rare in patients with normal renal function, hyperkalaemia is the most severe adverse effect. 
    - **Signs and Symptoms:** Cardiac arrhythmias, mental confusion, unexplained anxiety, numbness or tingling (paresthesia) in extremities or lips, shortness of breath, unusual tiredness, and weakness or heaviness of the legs [TGA, FDA].
- **Serious (GI):** Irritation of the gastrointestinal tract [FDA].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Potassium-Sparing Diuretics:** (e.g., spironolactone, triamterene, amiloride) Simultaneous administration can produce severe, potentially fatal hyperkalaemia [TGA, FDA].
- **ACE Inhibitors:** (e.g., captopril, enalapril) Can cause potassium retention; concurrent use requires close monitoring [TGA, FDA].
- **Salt Substitutes:** Many contain substantial amounts of potassium and can result in hyperkalaemia if used concomitantly [TGA].
- **NSAIDs and Beta-Blockers:** Interactions may occur, potentially increasing the risk of hyperkalaemia [TGA].
- **Digitalis:** Lowering potassium in digitalised patients increases the risk of digoxin toxicity. Conversely, rapid lowering of potassium during hyperkalaemia treatment in digitalised patients can also trigger toxicity [TGA, FDA].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **TGA:** Category not explicitly assigned in provided text. Note that GI transit may be delayed in pregnancy, requiring caution. Only use if clearly needed.
    - **FDA:** Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. It is unknown if it causes fetal harm. Only use if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Many drugs are excreted in human milk. A decision should be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug, weighing the importance of the drug to the mother against the potential for adverse reactions in the infant [TGA, FDA].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies regarding carcinogenicity, mutagenicity, or impairment of fertility have not been performed [FDA].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:**
    - **TGA:** Not for use in children under 12 years of age.
    - **FDA:** Safety and effectiveness in children have not been established.
- **Elderly Use:** Specific data not available in provided sources [TGA], though general cautions regarding renal function apply.
- **Renal Impairment:** Requires particularly careful monitoring of serum potassium concentration and appropriate dosage adjustment due to the risk of hyperkalaemia [TGA, FDA].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Potassium is the predominant intracellular cation (approx. 150–160 mEq/L). It is essential for nerve impulse transmission, contraction of cardiac, skeletal, and smooth muscle, maintenance of renal function, acid-base balance, and enzymatic reactions [TGA, FDA].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Well absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract [TGA, FDA].
    - **Distribution:** Diffuses into extracellular fluid and is actively transported into cells (intracellular to extracellular ratio ~40:1) [TGA].
    - **Metabolism:** Normal adult plasma levels are 3.5–5 mmol/L (or mEq/L) [TGA, FDA].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal via filtration, re-absorption, and secretion. Influenced by chloride concentration, acid-base balance, and adrenal mineralocorticoids [TGA].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Summaries:** No clinical trial data were specified in the available source documents for these specific formulations [TGA, FDA].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **TGA:** Store below 25°C. Shelf life is 2 years.
- **FDA:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F).
