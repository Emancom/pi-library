# Editorial Product Information: Probenecid

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data into a single coherent reference.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Probenecid (a uricosuric agent and renal tubular blocking agent).
- **Brand Names:** PRO-CID [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 500 mg capsule-shaped, yellow film-coated tablets, bisected on one side [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Phebra Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Probenecid is indicated for the following uses [Australian]:
- **Gout:** Treatment of hyperuricaemia associated with all stages of chronic gout and gouty arthritis (excluding acute attacks).
- **Prophylaxis of Hyperuricaemia:** Prevention of urate deposition and gouty attacks in asymptomatic relatives of patients with gout.
- **Diuretic-Induced Hyperuricaemia:** Management of elevated uric acid levels caused by diuretic therapy (e.g., thiazides) used for hypertension or oedema.
- **$\beta$-lactam Antibiotic Therapy:** Elevation and prolongation of plasma concentrations of various penicillins and cephalosporins.
- **Cidofovir Adjunct:** Concurrent administration with cidofovir to reduce the potential for nephrotoxicity during treatment of CMV retinitis in patients with HIV.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Gout (Adults)
- **Initial Dose:** 250 mg (half tablet) twice daily for one week.
- **Maintenance Dose:** 500 mg (one tablet) twice daily.
- **Titration:** If control is inadequate and 24-hour urate excretion does not exceed 700 mg, dosage may be increased by 500 mg increments every four weeks, typically not exceeding 2 g daily.
- **Reduction:** If the patient has been free of acute attacks for $\ge$ 6 months and uric acid levels are normal, the dose may be reduced by 500 mg every six months to the minimum effective maintenance dose.

### $\beta$-lactam Antibiotic Therapy
- **General (Adults):** 2 g daily in divided doses. This dose should be reduced in older patients where renal impairment is suspected.
- **Uncomplicated Gonorrhoea:** A single 1 g dose (two tablets) administered with the antibiotic. If using oral ampicillin, administer probenecid simultaneously; if using parenteral antibiotics, administer probenecid at least 30 minutes prior to injection.

### Concurrent Cidofovir Therapy
- **Schedule:** 2 g (four tablets) administered 3 hours before the cidofovir dose, followed by 1 g (two tablets) at 2 hours and 8 hours after the completion of the 1-hour cidofovir infusion (Total dose: 4 g).

### Paediatric Dosing (Children $\ge$ 2 years)
- **Initial Dose:** 25 mg/kg (or 0.7 g/m²) of body weight.
- **Maintenance:** 40 mg/kg (or 1.2 g/m²) daily, administered in divided doses every six hours.
- **Weight > 50 kg:** Use adult dosing.

### Administration Instructions
- Therapy for gout should not begin until an acute attack has fully subsided.
- If an acute attack is triggered during therapy, continue probenecid at the same dose and manage the attack with appropriate anti-inflammatory agents (e.g., colchicine, indomethacin).
- Gastric intolerance may be managed by dose reduction.

## 4. Contraindications
Probenecid is contraindicated in the following scenarios [Australian]:
- Known hypersensitivity to probenecid or any tablet excipients.
- Presence of blood dyscrasias.
- Existing uric acid stones (urolithiasis).
- Children under two years of age.
- Concomitant use with salicylates (as they antagonise the uricosuric effect).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Acute Gout Attacks:** Early therapy may increase the frequency of acute gouty episodes due to the mobilisation of urate deposits; prophylactic anti-inflammatories may be required.
- **Peptic Ulcer:** Use with caution in patients with a history of peptic ulcer disease.
- **Renal Stones and Haematuria:** To prevent urate stone formation, renal colic, or haematuria, patients should maintain a liberal fluid intake and alkalise the urine.
- **Alkalisation Monitoring:** When using alkalising agents (e.g., sodium bicarbonate or potassium citrate), the patient's acid-base balance must be monitored.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Therapy must be discontinued immediately if hypersensitivity reactions occur.
- **Methotrexate Toxicity:** Probenecid significantly increases methotrexate plasma levels; concurrent use requires dose reduction of methotrexate and frequent serum level monitoring.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by system organ class below. Precise frequency data (e.g., %) is not specified in the available source, though some are noted as rare [Australian].

| System Organ Class | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Central Nervous System** | Headache, dizziness |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea, vomiting, anorexia, sore gums. **Rare:** Hepatic necrosis. |
| **Genitourinary** | Urinary frequency, uric acid stones (with/without haematuria), renal colic, costovertebral pain. **Rare:** Nephrotic syndrome. |
| **Hypersensitivity** | Anaphylaxis, fever, pruritus, urticaria, Stevens-Johnson syndrome. |
| **Haematologic** | Anaemia, leucopenia, thrombocytopenia. **Rare:** Aplastic anaemia, haemolytic anaemia (potentially linked to G6PD deficiency). |
| **Integumentary** | Flushing, dermatitis, alopecia. **Rare:** Toxic epidermal necrolysis (reported with combined colchicine therapy). |
| **Musculoskeletal** | Exacerbation of acute gout. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Salicylates:** Small or large doses of aspirin antagonise the uricosuric action of probenecid. Paracetamol is the preferred alternative for analgesia [Australian].
- **Pyrazinamide:** Antagonises the uricosuric effect of probenecid [Australian].

### Pharmacokinetic Interactions (Inhibition of Renal/Hepatic Clearance)
Probenecid inhibits the tubular secretion or transport of numerous drugs, leading to increased plasma concentrations and potential toxicity [Australian]:
- **Antivirals:** Zidovudine, ganciclovir, aciclovir/valaciclovir, famciclovir/penciclovir.
- **Antibiotics:** Most $\beta$-lactams (penicillins, cephalosporins), ciprofloxacin, dapsone, rifampicin, and sulfonamides. Note: It does **not** affect streptomycin, chloramphenicol, or tetracyclines.
- **NSAIDs/Analgesics:** Naproxen, indomethacin, ketoprofen, meclofenamate, ketorolac, and paracetamol (prolongs half-life).
- **Other:** Methotrexate (critical interaction), allopurinol (both drugs may see increased levels), sulfonylureas (risk of hypoglycaemia), thiazide diuretics, and benzodiazepines (lorazepam, midazolam, nitrazepam).
- **Anaesthetics:** Thiopental and ketamine anaesthesia may be prolonged.

### Laboratory Test Interference
- **Urinary Glucose:** May produce a false-positive Benedict's test (copper reduction method). Use enzymatic glucose oxidase tests instead.
- **Theophylline:** Falsely high readings may occur when using the Schack and Waxler technique.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B2]. Data from a limited number of pregnant women show no increase in malformations or fetal harm. Animal studies are inadequate. Probenecid crosses the placental barrier. Use only when the anticipated benefit outweighs the potential hazards to the fetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if probenecid is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution when administering to nursing mothers [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies (rats) showed no adverse effects on reproductive parameters at doses up to 100 mg/kg/day [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for children $\ge$ 2 years (see Section 3 for dosing). Contraindicated in children < 2 years.
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available, but dosage reduction is recommended in older patients where renal impairment is suspected, particularly during antibiotic therapy.
- **Renal Impairment:** Probenecid may be ineffective in patients with chronic renal insufficiency, particularly when the glomerular filtration rate (GFR) is 30 mL/min or less. It is not recommended for use with $\beta$-lactams in the presence of known renal impairment.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No specific dosage adjustments provided in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Probenecid is a uricosuric and renal tubular blocking agent. It inhibits the renal tubular reabsorption of urate, thereby increasing the urinary excretion of uric acid and lowering serum urate levels. It also competitively inhibits the active transport of many weak organic acids at the proximal renal tubule, retarding their excretion.

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Completely absorbed after oral administration. Peak plasma levels are achieved in 2 to 4 hours.
- **Distribution:** 85% to 95% protein-bound to plasma albumin. Apparent volume of distribution is approximately 11 litres.
- **Metabolism:** Undergoes oxidation of side chains and glucuronide conjugation. The major metabolite is probenecid acyl glucuronide.
- **Elimination:** Plasma half-life is dose-dependent (nonlinear), ranging from 6 to 12 hours. 75% to 88% is recovered in urine, primarily as metabolites; unchanged drug excretion is pH-dependent.
