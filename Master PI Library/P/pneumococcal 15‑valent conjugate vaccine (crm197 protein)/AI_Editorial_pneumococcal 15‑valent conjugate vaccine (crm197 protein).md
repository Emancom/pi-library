# Editorial Product Information: pneumococcal 15‑valent conjugate vaccine (crm197 protein)

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **pneumococcal 15‑valent conjugate vaccine (crm197 protein)**
- Chemical class: Pneumococcal capsular polysaccharide conjugate vaccine (CRM197 protein carrier)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration: Intramuscular only.
- DO NOT include brand or trade names, dosage form physical descriptions, or schedule listings.

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 2021.
- Date of last revision/update of the source PI provided: June 2026.
- Withdrawal/suspension/restriction history: Not specified in available sources.
- Other jurisdictions' registration dates: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Active immunization for the prevention of invasive disease caused by Streptococcus pneumoniae serotypes 1, 3, 4, 5, 6A, 6B, 7F, 9V, 14, 18C, 19A, 19F, 22F, 23F and 33F in individuals 6 weeks of age and older.
- Regional note: The indication above is presented as approved in the available source documentation; jurisdictional differences in approved age ranges or indications are not specified in available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing
- Each dose: 0.5 mL administered intramuscularly.
- Adults (≥18 years): single 0.5 mL dose.
- Infants and young children (primary series): 4-dose series at 2 months, 4 months, 6 months and a booster at 12–15 months (the booster should be given at least 2 months after the third dose). The first dose may be administered as early as 6 weeks of age.
- Children/adolescents initiating catch-up vaccination (7 months through 17 years): follow catch-up schedule below.

### Catch-up vaccination schedule (for individuals initiating pneumococcal conjugate vaccination at 7 months through 17 years)
| Age at first dose | Total number of 0.5 mL doses |
|---|---|
| 7 through 11 months | 3 doses |
| 12 through 23 months | 2 doses |
| 2 years through 17 years | 1 dose |

- Children and adolescents 2 through 17 years of age who have received an incomplete series of another pneumococcal conjugate vaccine may receive a single 0.5 mL dose of this vaccine. Wait at least 2 months after the last dose of another pneumococcal conjugate vaccine before administering this vaccine.

### Administration instructions
- Inspect visually prior to use. Vaccine may appear as a clear liquid with a precipitate before shaking.
- Immediately before use, hold the prefilled syringe horizontally and shake vigorously to obtain an opalescent suspension. Multiple attempts may be required to achieve complete resuspension. Do not use if the product cannot be resuspended.
- Do not use if particulate matter is present after vigorous shaking or if discoloration is observed.
- Administer intramuscularly according to standard IM injection technique for the age group (specific injection sites by age not specified in available sources).

### Titration / missed dose instructions
- Titration schedules: Not specified in available sources.
- Missed doses: Not specified in available sources beyond the catch-up schedules provided.

## 5. Contraindications
- History of severe allergic reaction (e.g., anaphylaxis) to any component of the vaccine.  
- History of severe allergic reaction to diphtheria toxoid.  
- Additional jurisdiction-specific contraindications: Not specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- **Management of allergic reactions:** Appropriate medical treatment and supervision must be readily available in case of an acute severe allergic reaction following vaccination.
- **Apnea in premature infants:** Apnea has been observed following intramuscular vaccination in some infants born prematurely. The timing of vaccination in preterm infants should be individualized based on the infant's medical status and weighing potential benefits and risks.
- **Altered immunocompetence:** Individuals with altered or suppressed immune function (including those receiving immunosuppressive therapy) may mount a reduced immune response to the vaccine.
- Monitoring requirements: Immediate monitoring following vaccination as per usual practice for parenteral vaccines; specific additional monitoring intervals or laboratory monitoring are Not specified in available sources.
- Other clinical precautions: Not specified in available sources.

## 7. Adverse Effects
- The adverse effects below are reported from clinical trial safety data provided in the available sources. Frequency categories used:
  - Very common: >10%
  - Common: 1–10%
  - Uncommon: 0.1–1%
  - Rare: <0.1%
  - Very rare: <0.01%
- Where different frequencies were reported between population groups (infants/children/adults) the figures are shown by age group. Where only one group reported the event, other group cells indicate "Not specified in available sources."

- Infants (4-dose primary series, 2, 4, 6 and 12–15 months): most commonly reported solicited reactions (range across series): irritability 57.3–63.4% (very common), somnolence 24.2–47.5% (very common), injection-site pain 25.9–40.3% (very common), fever ≥38.0°C 13.3–20.4% (very common), decreased appetite 14.1–19.0% (very common), injection-site induration 13.2–15.4% (very common), injection-site erythema 13.7–21.4% (very common), injection-site swelling 11.3–13.4% (very common).
- Children/adolescents 2 through 17 years (single dose): injection-site pain 54.8% (very common), myalgia 23.7% (very common), injection-site swelling 20.9% (very common), injection-site erythema 19.2% (very common), fatigue 15.8% (very common), headache 11.9% (very common), injection-site induration 6.8% (common).
- Adults 18–49 years (single dose): injection-site pain 75.8% (very common), fatigue 34.3% (very common), myalgia 28.8% (very common), headache 26.5% (very common), injection-site swelling 21.7% (very common), injection-site erythema 15.1% (very common), arthralgia 12.7% (very common).
- Adults ≥50 years (single dose): injection-site pain 66.8% (very common), myalgia 26.9% (very common), fatigue 21.5% (very common), headache 18.9% (very common), injection-site swelling 15.4% (very common), injection-site erythema 10.9% (borderline very common/common), arthralgia 7.7% (common).

- Fever detailed breakdowns from studies: across trials, fever ≥38.0°C after infant doses ranged approximately 15–20% depending on dose; higher-grade fevers (≥39.0°C) occurred at lower rates (typically 0.7–2.4% depending on dose and study).

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Pharmacokinetic interactions: Not specified in available sources.
- Pharmacodynamic/clinical interactions: Concomitant administration was evaluated in clinical trials with the following routinely administered childhood vaccines:
  - DTaP‑IPV‑Hib vaccine (pentavalent DTaP-IPV-Hib) coadministered at 2, 4 and 6 months.
  - Rotavirus vaccine (oral, live pentavalent) and hepatitis B vaccine (recombinant) coadministered at 2, 4 and 6 months.
  - Measles–mumps–rubella (MMR), hepatitis A (inactivated), varicella (live) and Hib (Hib–TT) coadministered at 12–15 months.
- Clinical note: Safety and reactogenicity were assessed when coadministered with the listed routine pediatric vaccines in the described trials. Specific recommendations for coadministration, spacing, or altered immunogenicity outcomes beyond the fact that coadministration was studied are Not specified in available sources.
- Management notes: If coadministration with other injectable vaccines is performed, administer each vaccine at a different injection site when possible.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Pivotal/safety trial program (summarized):
  - Four randomized, double-blind, active comparator‑controlled clinical studies (designated Studies 8–11 in source documents) evaluated safety of a 4‑dose series in infants and children; geographic regions included the Americas, Europe and Asia‑Pacific.
  - Total participants: 3,349 participants received at least one dose of the 4‑dose series of this vaccine; comparator groups included participants who received a 13‑valent pneumococcal conjugate vaccine.
  - Study 8 (US participants included) randomized infants to receive either the 15‑valent vaccine or a 13‑valent comparator; study sizes for US subset: N≈858 vaccinated with the 15‑valent vaccine and N≈855 with comparator (overall study N values vary by dose and specific analysis).
  - Concomitant vaccines in infant studies included DTaP‑IPV‑Hib, rotavirus, hepatitis B at 2, 4 and 6 months; and MMR, hepatitis A, varicella and Hib at 12–15 months in various studies.
  - Safety monitoring: solicited local and systemic adverse events were captured for up to 14 days postvaccination (or 7 days for temperatures in some cohorts), unsolicited adverse events through 14 days, and serious adverse events monitored up to 6 months following last study vaccination in most studies.
  - Key safety observations: solicited adverse events as summarized in Section 7; median duration of solicited reactions was 1 day with most resolving within 3 days; injection-site urticaria reported in a small percentage; one febrile seizure temporally associated with vaccination and concomitant routine vaccines was reported in a 9‑week‑old.
- Catch-up study:
  - A double‑blind, multi‑regional study (Study 12) evaluated safety of 1–3 doses depending on age; included infants/children 7 months through 17 years and compared to a 13‑valent comparator in younger age cohorts.
  - Safety findings in catch-up cohorts were consistent with those observed in the primary series studies; specific immunogenicity endpoints and comparative immunogenicity results are Not specified in available sources.
- Primary efficacy/immunogenicity outcomes: Not specified in available sources (clinical study descriptions in the available source focused on safety; detailed immunogenicity and efficacy results were not included in the provided text).
