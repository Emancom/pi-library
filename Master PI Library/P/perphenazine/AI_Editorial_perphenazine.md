# Editorial Product Information: Perphenazine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: United States (FDA), as repackaged by RemedyRepack Inc. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data into a concise, professional format.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Perphenazine (a piperazinyl phenothiazine).
- **Chemical Class:** Phenothiazine antipsychotic.
- **ATC Code:** N05AB03 (Note: Not explicitly listed in source, but standard for perphenazine).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Oral film-coated tablets: 2 mg, 4 mg, 8 mg, and 16 mg [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** RemedyRepack Inc. (Repackager); Wilshire Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (Original Manufacturer) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Schizophrenia:** Treatment of schizophrenia in adults [US].
- **Nausea and Vomiting:** Control of severe nausea and vomiting in adults [US].
- **Note:** Perphenazine has not been shown effective for behavioral complications in patients with mental retardation [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- Dosage must be individualised and adjusted to the minimum effective dose based on severity and patient response. Extrapyramidal symptoms (EPS) are dose-dependent.

### Schizophrenia
- **Non-hospitalised (Moderately Disturbed):** 4 mg to 8 mg three times daily (TID) initially; reduce to the minimum effective dose as soon as possible [US].
- **Hospitalised Patients:** 8 mg to 16 mg two to four times daily (BID to QID). Do not exceed 64 mg daily [US].
- **Prolonged Administration:** Doses exceeding 24 mg daily should be reserved for hospitalised or closely observed patients [US].

### Severe Nausea and Vomiting
- **Adults:** 8 mg to 16 mg daily in divided doses; occasionally 24 mg may be required. Dosage should be reduced as soon as possible [US].

### Elderly Patients
- Initiate treatment at the lower end of the dosing range. With increasing age, plasma concentrations per daily dose tend to increase [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Comatose or greatly obtunded states [US].
- Concurrent use of large doses of CNS depressants (e.g., alcohol, barbiturates, narcotics, antihistamines) [US].
- Existing blood dyscrasias or bone marrow depression [US].
- Liver damage [US].
- Known hypersensitivity to perphenazine or other phenothiazines [US].
- Suspected or established subcortical brain damage (with or without hypothalamic involvement), due to the risk of severe hyperthermic reactions (>104°F) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning [US]
- **Increased Mortality in Elderly Patients with Dementia-Related Psychosis:** Elderly patients with dementia-related psychosis treated with antipsychotic drugs are at an increased risk of death. Perphenazine is not approved for the treatment of patients with dementia-related psychosis.

### General Warnings
- **Tardive Dyskinesia (TD):** Potentially irreversible, involuntary movements may develop. Risk increases with treatment duration and cumulative dose. If signs appear, consider discontinuation.
- **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** A potentially fatal complex (hyperpyrexia, muscle rigidity, altered mental status, autonomic instability). Requires immediate discontinuation and intensive symptomatic treatment.
- **Falls:** May cause somnolence, postural hypotension, and motor/sensory instability, leading to fractures. Fall risk assessments are recommended.
- **Hypotension:** Severe hypotension may occur, particularly in patients with mitral insufficiency or pheochromocytoma. **Contraindication:** Do not use epinephrine to treat hypotension induced by perphenazine, as the drug reverses the vasopressor effect of epinephrine. Use norepinephrine if a vasopressor is required [US].
- **Seizure Threshold:** May lower the convulsive threshold; use with caution in alcohol withdrawal or convulsive disorders.

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Hematology:** Perform periodic blood counts. Discontinue if absolute neutrophil count (ANC) <1000/mm³ [US].
- **Organ Function:** Periodic hepatic and renal function tests are required. Discontinue if liver function abnormalities or abnormal blood urea nitrogen (BUN) occur [US].
- **Ocular:** Long-term therapy requires monitoring for lenticular/corneal deposits and pigmentary retinopathy.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Categorisation
(Note: Specific percentage frequencies are not provided in the source document; categories are based on class descriptions).

- **Very Common/Common:**
    - **CNS:** Drowsiness (usually subsides after 1-2 weeks), extrapyramidal symptoms (dystonia, akathisia, parkinsonism).
    - **Autonomic:** Dry mouth, blurred vision, constipation, nasal congestion.
- **Uncommon/Rare:**
    - **CNS:** Paradoxical exacerbation of psychosis, seizures, cerebral edema.
    - **Cardiovascular:** Postural hypotension, tachycardia, cardiac arrest, ECG changes (quinidine-like effect).
    - **Hematologic:** Agranulocytosis, eosinophilia, leukopenia, hemolytic anemia, thrombocytopenic purpura.
    - **Endocrine:** Galactorrhea, gynecomastia, menstrual irregularities, hyperglycemia/hypoglycemia.
    - **Hepatic:** Jaundice (biliary stasis), typically between weeks 2 and 4.
    - **Dermatologic/Ocular:** Photosensitivity, skin pigmentation, lenticular opacities (long-term use).

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP2D6 Inhibitors:** Drugs that inhibit CYP2D6 (e.g., fluoxetine, sertraline, paroxetine, tricyclic antidepressants) can significantly increase perphenazine plasma levels. Dose reduction and close monitoring are essential [US].
- **Metabolism:** Perphenazine is a substrate of CYP2D6; "poor metabolizers" (7-10% of Caucasians) will experience higher concentrations [US].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **CNS Depressants:** Potentiates the effects of alcohol, opiates, and barbiturates.
- **Anticholinergics:** Additive effects with atropine or related compounds.
- **Epinephrine:** Perphenazine blocks the alpha-adrenergic effect of epinephrine, potentially leading to paradoxical hypotension. Use norepinephrine instead [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not specified by letter in source; however, it notes neonates exposed in the third trimester are at risk for EPS and withdrawal symptoms (agitation, hypertonia, respiratory distress). 
    - **Recommendation:** Use only if potential benefit justifies the risk to the fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been established. The benefits must be weighed against potential hazards to the child [US].
- **Fertility:** Not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for patients under 12 years of age [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased sensitivity to EPS, sedation, and orthostatic hypotension. Higher risk of falls/fractures. Initiate with lower doses and monitor closely.
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution; monitor BUN; discontinue if BUN becomes abnormal [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in cases of existing liver damage. Monitor LFTs periodically [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** While the exact mechanism of therapeutic effect is unknown, it has actions at all levels of the CNS, particularly the hypothalamus [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Exhibits antipsychotic and antiemetic properties characteristic of phenothiazines.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak plasma concentrations reached within 1–3 hours.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively hepatic via sulfoxidation, hydroxylation, dealkylation, and glucuronidation. Mediated by CYP2D6.
    - **Elimination:** Elimination half-life is 9–12 hours. Steady state is reached within 72 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Dementia-Related Psychosis:** Analyses of 17 placebo-controlled trials (modal duration 10 weeks) showed a 1.6 to 1.7 times increase in mortality risk compared to placebo [US].
- **Schizophrenia:** Specific pivotal trial data for the original approval are not detailed in the provided source.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C (59°F–86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only. Poison schedule not applicable (Legend drug).
