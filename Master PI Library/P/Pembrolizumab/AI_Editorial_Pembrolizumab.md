# Editorial Product Information: Pembrolizumab

## Editorial Note
- This document is an editorial synthesis based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of pembrolizumab for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official PI. Clinicians must consult the current approved PI in their specific jurisdiction before prescribing or administering this medication.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pembrolizumab (rch)
- **Chemical Class:** Humanised monoclonal anti-programmed cell death-1 (PD-1) antibody (IgG4 kappa isotype).
- **Brand Name:** KEYTRUDA [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** L01XC18
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 100 mg/4 mL concentrated injection for intravenous infusion. It is a sterile, preservative-free, clear to slightly opalescent, colourless to slightly yellow solution.
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Sponsor:** Merck Sharp & Dohme (Australia) Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
*Note: All indications listed below are [Australian-approved].*

### Melanoma
- Monotherapy for unresectable or metastatic melanoma in adults.
- Adjuvant treatment for Stage IIB, IIC, or III melanoma in patients (≥12 years) following complete resection.

### Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)
- First-line metastatic non-squamous NSCLC (with pemetrexed and platinum chemotherapy; no EGFR/ALK aberrations).
- First-line metastatic squamous NSCLC (with carboplatin and paclitaxel/nab-paclitaxel).
- Monotherapy for first-line Stage III (not candidates for surgery/chemoradiation) or metastatic NSCLC expressing PD-L1 (TPS ≥1%; no EGFR/ALK aberrations).
- Monotherapy for advanced NSCLC expressing PD-L1 (TPS ≥1%) following platinum chemotherapy.
- Adjuvant monotherapy for Stage IB (T2a ≥4 cm), II, or IIIA NSCLC following resection and platinum chemotherapy.
- Neoadjuvant/adjuvant combination (with platinum chemotherapy) for resectable Stage II, IIIA, or IIIB (T3-4N2) NSCLC.

### Other Malignancies
- **Malignant Pleural Mesothelioma (MPM):** First-line treatment for unresectable advanced/metastatic disease (with pemetrexed and platinum).
- **Head and Neck Squamous Cell Cancer (HNSCC):** Resectable locally advanced (neoadjuvant/adjuvant RT); metastatic/recurrent expressing PD-L1 (CPS ≥1) as monotherapy or with platinum/5-FU.
- **Classical Hodgkin Lymphoma (cHL):** Relapsed/refractory in adults/paediatrics.
- **Primary Mediastinal B-cell Lymphoma (PMBCL):** Relapsed/refractory in adults/paediatrics.
- **Urothelial Carcinoma:** First-line metastatic (with enfortumab vedotin); monotherapy for platinum-ineligible/previously treated; high-risk BCG-unresponsive NMIBC.
- **MSI-H or dMMR Cancer:** Unresectable/metastatic solid tumours or colorectal cancer.
- **Biliary Tract Carcinoma (BTC):** Locally advanced unresectable/metastatic (with gemcitabine and cisplatin).
- **Endometrial Carcinoma:** Primary advanced/recurrent (with carboplatin/paclitaxel) or advanced (not MSI-H/dMMR) with lenvatinib.
- **Cervical Cancer:** High-risk locally advanced (with CRT) or persistent/recurrent/metastatic expressing PD-L1 (CPS ≥1) with chemotherapy ± bevacizumab.
- **Merkel Cell Carcinoma (MCC):** Recurrent locally advanced or metastatic.
- **Renal Cell Carcinoma (RCC):** First-line advanced (with axitinib or lenvatinib); adjuvant clear cell RCC.
- **Cutaneous Squamous Cell Carcinoma (cSCC):** Recurrent/metastatic or locally advanced not curable by surgery/radiation.
- **Gastric/GOJ Adenocarcinoma:** First-line HER2-negative (with chemotherapy) or HER2-positive expressing PD-L1 (CPS ≥1) with trastuzumab and chemotherapy.
- **Oesophageal Cancer:** First-line locally advanced/metastatic (with chemotherapy).
- **TMB-H Cancer:** Unresectable/metastatic solid tumours (≥10 mut/Mb).
- **Triple-Negative Breast Cancer (TNBC):** High-risk early-stage (neoadjuvant/adjuvant); locally recurrent unresectable/metastatic expressing PD-L1 (CPS ≥10).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Standard Dose:** 200 mg every 3 weeks or 400 mg every 6 weeks.
- **Adjuvant Treatment:** Continue until disease recurrence or unacceptable toxicity, for up to 12 months.
- **Metastatic/Advanced Disease:** Continue until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity, generally up to 24 months.

### Dosing (Paediatric/Adolescent)
- **Dose:** 2 mg/kg every 3 weeks (up to a maximum of 200 mg).

### Administration Instructions
- Administer as an IV infusion over 30 minutes.
- Must use a sterile, non-pyrogenic, low-protein binding 0.2 to 5 µm in-line or add-on filter.
- Do not co-administer other drugs through the same infusion line.
- Dilute in 0.9% sodium chloride or 5% glucose to a final concentration of 1 to 10 mg/mL.

### Dose Modifications
- **No dose reductions are recommended.**
- Toxicity management requires withholding or permanent discontinuation based on the severity (Grade 2–4) of immune-mediated adverse reactions.
- Systemic corticosteroids (initial dose 1-2 mg/kg/day prednisone or equivalent) are generally required for Grade 2 or higher immune-mediated reactions.

## 4. Contraindications
- None listed in available sources [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Immune-Mediated Adverse Reactions:** Can occur in any organ system, including pneumonitis, colitis, hepatitis, nephritis, and endocrinopathies (adrenal insufficiency, hypophysitis, type 1 diabetes, thyroid disorders). These may occur after discontinuation.
- **Infusion-Related Reactions:** Severe reactions (hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis) require permanent discontinuation.
- **Multiple Myeloma:** Increased mortality observed when pembrolizumab was added to a thalidomide analogue plus dexamethasone (not an indicated use).
- **Allogeneic HSCT:** Complications (including fatal GVHD and hepatic VOD) can occur in patients with a history of HSCT or those who proceed to HSCT after pembrolizumab.

### Monitoring Requirements
- Monitor for clinical signs/symptoms of immune-mediated reactions.
- **Liver Function:** Monitor AST, ALT, and bilirubin at baseline and periodically.
- **Thyroid Function:** Monitor at baseline and periodically.
- **Blood Glucose:** Monitor for hyperglycaemia.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Very Common (>10%)
- Fatigue, pruritus, rash, diarrhoea, nausea, arthralgia, cough.
- Laboratory abnormalities: Anaemia, lymphopenia, hyperglycemia, hyponatremia, hypoalbuminemia.

### Immune-Mediated Reactions (Pooled Analysis)
| Adverse Reaction | All Grades (%) | Grades 3-5 (%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Hypothyroidism | 8.5 | 0.1 |
| Hyperthyroidism | 3.4 | 0.1 |
| Pneumonitis | 3.4 | 1.3 |

| Adrenal Insufficiency | 0.8 | 0.3 |
| Hepatitis | 0.7 | 0.4 |
| Nephritis | 0.3 | 0.1 |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Pneumonitis, sepsis, acute kidney injury, and severe skin reactions (SJS/TEN).
- Fatal cases have been reported for various immune-mediated toxicities.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetics:** No formal studies. Pembrolizumab is cleared via catabolism; metabolic drug-drug interactions are not expected.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Avoid systemic corticosteroids or immunosuppressants before starting pembrolizumab as they may interfere with efficacy. However, they can be used to treat immune-mediated reactions after treatment initiation.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category D]. Based on its mechanism, pembrolizumab may cause fetal harm/loss. Contraception is required during treatment and for at least 4 months after the last dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Unknown if pembrolizumab is excreted in human milk. Advise against breastfeeding during treatment and for 4 months post-treatment.
- **Fertility:** No formal fertility studies conducted.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for cHL, PMBCL, MSI-H/dMMR, and TMB-H cancers. Safety profile similar to adults.
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy in patients ≥65 years. Limited data for patients ≥75 years in combination therapies.
- **Renal Impairment:** No adjustment for mild or moderate impairment. Not studied in severe renal impairment.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for mild hepatic impairment. Not studied in moderate or severe hepatic impairment.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Blockade of the PD-1 receptor. PD-1 is an immune checkpoint that inhibits T-cell activity. Pembrolizumab prevents PD-1 from binding to its ligands (PD-L1 and PD-L2), reactivating tumour-specific cytotoxic T lymphocytes.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Distribution:** Small volume of distribution at steady state (6.0 L).
    - **Metabolism:** Catabolised through non-specific pathways.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life (t½) is approximately 22 days. Steady state is reached by 16 weeks.

## 11. Clinical Trials
The efficacy of pembrolizumab is supported by an extensive range of trials (KEYNOTE series), including:
- **KEYNOTE-006:** Demonstrated OS and PFS superiority over ipilimumab in advanced melanoma.
- **KEYNOTE-189/407:** Established first-line NSCLC combination therapy benefits.
- **KEYNOTE-A39:** Demonstrated OS/PFS benefit in urothelial carcinoma when combined with enfortumab vedotin.
- **KEYNOTE-057:** Showed 41% CR rate in BCG-unresponsive NMIBC.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store in a refrigerator at 2°C to 8°C.
- **Handling:** Protect from light. Do not freeze. Do not shake.
- **Shelf Life:** Varies; consult packaging. After dilution, the solution may be stored at room temperature for up to 6 hours or refrigerated (2°C to 8°C) for up to 96 hours (total time from dilution to end of infusion).
