# Editorial Product Information: Perampanel

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis was developed using official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the Australian PI was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Perampanel (present as hemisesquihydrate).
- **Chemical Class:** Selective, non-competitive antagonist of the ionotropic α-amino-3-hydroxy-5-methyl-4-isoxazoleproprionic acid (AMPA) glutamate receptor.
- **Brand Name:** **FYCOMPA** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available source.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Film-coated Tablets:** 2 mg, 4 mg, 6 mg, 8 mg, 10 mg, and 12 mg [Australian].
    - **Oral Suspension:** 0.5 mg/mL (provided as 170 mg perampanel in a 340 mL bottle) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Eisai Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Partial-onset Seizures (POS):** Adjunctive treatment in patients with epilepsy from 4 years of age, with or without secondarily generalised seizures [Australian-approved].
- **Primary Generalised Tonic-Clonic Seizures (PGTCS):** Adjunctive treatment in patients from 7 years of age with idiopathic generalised epilepsy [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Adolescents $\ge$ 12 years)
- **Administration:** Once daily at bedtime. May be taken with or without food.
- **Starting Dose:** 2 mg/day.
- **Titration:** Dose increases should be based on clinical response and tolerability. 
    - For patients **not** taking enzyme-inducing Anti-Epileptic Drugs (AEDs): Titrate in 2 mg/day increments no more frequently than every 2–4 weeks.
    - For patients taking enzyme-inducing AEDs (e.g., carbamazepine, phenytoin, oxcarbazepine): Titrate in 2 mg/day increments no more frequently than weekly.
- **Maintenance Dose:** 4 mg/day to 8 mg/day.
- **Maximum Dose:** 12 mg/day.

### Paediatric Dosing (Children < 12 years)
Dosage is weight-dependent [Australian]:
- **$\ge$ 30 kg:** Start at 2 mg/day; Maintenance 4–8 mg/day; Max 12 mg/day.
- **20 kg to < 30 kg:** Start at 1 mg/day; Maintenance 4–6 mg/day; Max 8 mg/day.
- **< 20 kg:** Start at 1 mg/day; Maintenance 2–4 mg/day; Max 6 mg/day.

### Administration Instructions
- **Tablets:** Swallow whole; do not chew, crush, or split.
- **Oral Suspension:** Shake vigorously for at least 5 seconds before use. Use the provided oral syringe and adaptor. Discard any remaining suspension 90 days after first opening.

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Due to the long half-life, if a single dose is missed, the patient should wait and take the next dose as scheduled.
- If more than one dose is missed for a period less than 5 half-lives (3 weeks for those not on inducing AEDs; 1 week for those on inducing AEDs), consider restarting from the last dose level. 
- If discontinued for more than 5 half-lives, follow initial titration recommendations.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to perampanel or any of the excipients (including lactose in tablets and sorbitol in the oral suspension) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Serious Psychiatric and Behavioural Reactions:** Serious and life-threatening reactions including aggression, hostility, irritability, anger, and homicidal ideation have been reported. These are more frequent during titration and at higher doses. Aggression is reported more often in adolescents and children than in adults.
- **Suicidal Ideation and Behaviour:** Patients should be monitored for emergence or worsening of depression or suicidal thoughts. Risk is observed as early as one week after initiation.
- **Nervous System Disorders:** Significant dose-related increases in dizziness and gait disturbance (ataxia, balance disorder). Elderly patients are at higher risk.
- **Falls:** Increased risk of falls, potentially leading to serious injury (head trauma, fractures), especially in the elderly.
- **Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS):** Also known as multiorgan hypersensitivity; potentially fatal. Discontinue immediately if suspected.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Cases of increased hepatic enzymes have been reported. Monitor liver function if elevations occur.
- **Abuse Potential:** Caution in patients with a history of substance abuse; perampanel has shown potential for "euphoria" and "sedation" similar to alprazolam in clinical studies.
- **Withdrawal:** Discontinue gradually to minimise rebound seizures; however, abrupt withdrawal is possible if absolutely necessary due to the long half-life.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Dizziness, somnolence, fatigue, headache [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Irritability, falls, nausea, ataxia, weight gain, vertigo, diplopia, blurred vision, dysarthria, and anxiety.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Homicidal ideation (0.1%), DRESS, severe aggression, and serious fall-related injuries.
- **Paediatric Nuance:** Aggression is observed more frequently in adolescents. In children (4–12 years), somnolence, irritability, and agitation occurred more frequently than in adults [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Enzyme Inducers (Major):** Carbamazepine (3-fold increase in clearance), phenytoin, and oxcarbazepine (2-fold increase) significantly reduce perampanel plasma levels.
- **Oral Contraceptives:** 12 mg/day perampanel decreases levonorgestrel exposure by approximately 40%. Use of an additional non-hormonal contraceptive method is recommended.
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Ketoconazole increases perampanel AUC by 20%.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Alcohol:** Additive or supra-additive effects on alertness, vigilance, and driving ability. Alcohol significantly worsens mood and increases anger.
- **CNS Depressants:** Enhanced sedative effects.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category B3. Not recommended for use during pregnancy. There are limited data (less than 300 outcomes). Animal studies showed increased stillbirths and intestinal diverticulum at doses near human exposure.
- **Breastfeeding:** Excreted in rat milk; human excretion is unknown. A decision should be made whether to discontinue nursing or the drug, weighing the benefit to the mother against the risk to the infant.
- **Fertility:** Effect on human fertility is not established. Animal studies showed irregular estrous cycles in females but no clear effect on male fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric:** Safety and efficacy not established in children < 4 years (POS) or < 7 years (PGTCS).
- **Elderly ($\ge$ 65 years):** Increased risk of dizziness (up to 55.6%) and falls (up to 57.1%). Use with extreme caution.
- **Renal Impairment:** No adjustment for mild impairment. Use is **not recommended** in moderate/severe renal impairment or patients on haemodialysis [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild/Moderate (Child-Pugh A/B):** Dosing should not exceed maintenance levels; titrate no faster than every 2 weeks.
    - **Severe:** Not recommended [Australian].
    - **Paediatric:** No data available for children < 12 years with hepatic impairment.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** First-in-class non-competitive antagonist of the AMPA glutamate receptor, reducing neuronal overexcitation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** ~100% bioavailable; peak plasma levels delayed by 2 hours when taken with food.
    - **Distribution:** ~95% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised via primary oxidation (primarily CYP3A4) and sequential glucuronidation.
    - **Elimination:** Average half-life (t1/2) is ~105 hours; reduced to ~25 hours when co-administered with carbamazepine. 30% recovered in urine, 70% in faeces.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Partial-onset Seizures:** Efficacy established in three Phase 3 trials (Studies 304, 305, 306). Responder rates (50% reduction) were significantly higher than placebo at doses of 4 mg (28.5%), 8 mg (35.3%), and 12 mg (35.0%) [Australian].
- **PGTCS:** Study 332 showed a median percent reduction in seizure frequency of 76.5% for perampanel vs 38.4% for placebo.
- **Cognition:** A 19-week study in adolescents showed no statistically significant difference in global cognition compared to placebo.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Tablets and Suspension:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Suspension:** Do not freeze. Use within 90 days of opening.
- **Container:** Store in original container to protect from light/moisture.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
