# Editorial Product Information: paracetamol + metoclopramide

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **paracetamol + metoclopramide**
- Chemical class:
  - Paracetamol: aniline derivative analgesic/antipyretic
  - Metoclopramide: substituted benzamide dopamine (D2) receptor antagonist; prokinetic agent
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first approval/registration:
  - 20 September 1991 [Australian]
- Date of last revision/update of source PI:
  - 04 June 2025 [Australian]
- Withdrawal, suspension or restriction history: Not specified in available sources
- Other jurisdictions' registration or revision dates: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Symptomatic relief of headache, nausea and vomiting associated with migraine. [Australian]
- Other indications: Not specified in available sources

## 4. Dosage and Administration
- General dosing (adult)
  - Each tablet contains 5 mg metoclopramide and 500 mg paracetamol. [Australian]
  - Recommended adult dose: 1–2 tablets initially, then 1–2 tablets every 4 hours as required.
  - Maximum: 6 tablets in any 24-hour period. [Australian]
  - Note: Total daily metoclopramide dose should normally not exceed 0.5 mg/kg body weight, particularly in adolescents and young adults. [Australian]
- Dosing by indication
  - Migraine (symptomatic relief of headache, nausea and vomiting): take at first warning of attack; repeat dosing as above if symptoms persist. [Australian]
- Administration instructions
  - Take at first warning of a migraine; adhere to recommended dosing intervals to reduce risk of dystonic reactions. [Australian]
- Titration schedules: Not specified in available sources
- Missed dose instructions: Not specified in available sources

## 5. Contraindications
List of contraindications from available sources:
- Known hypersensitivity or intolerance to paracetamol, metoclopramide or any excipients. [Australian]
- Conditions in which stimulation of gastrointestinal motility might be dangerous (e.g. gastrointestinal haemorrhage, mechanical obstruction, perforation). [Australian]
- Phaeochromocytoma (risk of hypertensive crisis). [Australian]
- Epilepsy — metoclopramide may increase frequency and severity of seizures; use is contraindicated in patients with epilepsy. [Australian]
- Patients receiving other drugs likely to cause extrapyramidal reactions (concurrent use may increase frequency/severity). [Australian]
- Children and adolescents under 18 years of age (use contraindicated). [Australian]
- Pregnancy and lactation: Insufficient safety data to support use of the combination in pregnancy or during lactation — contraindicated in these circumstances in available source. [Australian]
- Porphyria: metoclopramide should not be administered to patients with porphyria. [Australian]

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed / highlighted warnings
- **Tardive dyskinesia and extrapyramidal reactions**: risk of persistent tardive dyskinesia with long-term metoclopramide therapy; dystonic reactions can occur after a single dose, particularly in children and young adults. [Australian]

### General warnings and precautions
- Paracetamol-related: Cases of high anion gap metabolic acidosis (HAGMA) due to pyroglutamic (5-oxoprolin) acidosis have been reported in patients with risk factors (e.g. severe renal impairment, sepsis, malnutrition, chronic alcoholism, prolonged therapeutic paracetamol use, or concomitant flucloxacillin). If suspected, discontinue paracetamol and monitor. Measurement of urinary 5-oxoproline may aid diagnosis. [Australian]
- Metoclopramide-related:
  - Dystonic reactions occur in ~1% of patients; more frequent in children and young adults and may occur after a single dose. [Australian]
  - Persistent tardive dyskinesia may follow long-term therapy and may be irreversible; risk greater in elderly patients on high-dose therapy, especially females. Fine vermicular tongue movements may be an early sign. If symptoms appear, consider progressive reduction and discontinuation of antidopaminergic agents where possible. [Australian]
  - Neuroleptic malignant syndrome (NMS) has been reported with metoclopramide monotherapy and with combinations; NMS is potentially fatal and requires urgent treatment and immediate drug discontinuation. [Australian]
  - Metoclopramide elevates prolactin — consider potential implications in patients with known breast cancer; galactorrhoea, amenorrhoea, gynaecomastia and impotence have been reported with prolactin-elevating drugs. [Australian]
  - May exacerbate Parkinsonian symptoms; use with caution in Parkinsonian syndrome. [Australian]
  - Metoclopramide may mask symptoms of underlying serious conditions by suppressing nausea/vomiting; reassess if vomiting persists. [Australian]
- Use in hepatic and renal impairment: exercise caution. Plasma concentrations of paracetamol and its conjugates are increased in moderate renal failure. Clearance of metoclopramide is likely reduced in clinically significant renal or hepatic impairment; dose adjustment guidance not specified. [Australian]
- Surgery: withhold metoclopramide for 3–4 days after operations such as pyloroplasty or gut anastomosis because strong contractions may impair healing. [Australian]
- Care with other centrally active drugs: additive CNS effects and increased extrapyramidal risk when combined with neuroleptics or other antidopaminergic agents. [Australian]
- Paediatric use: not to be used in children and adolescents under 18 years due to higher incidence of adverse reactions. [Australian]

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor for early signs of extrapyramidal reactions (dystonia, oculogyric crisis), tardive dyskinesia (including fine tongue movements), neuroleptic malignant syndrome (hyperpyrexia, rigidity, autonomic instability), and signs of hepatic dysfunction if clinically warranted. [Australian]
- In patients at risk for pyroglutamic acidosis/HAGMA, monitor acid-base status and consider measurement of urinary 5-oxoproline. [Australian]
- Monitor anticoagulant effect if paracetamol is used long-term (anticoagulant dose adjustment may be required). [Australian]

Notes:
- The most frequent adverse reactions to metoclopramide are restlessness, drowsiness, fatigue and lassitude, occurring at approximately 10% incidence. Most other adverse effects are less common to rare. [Australian]
- Extrapyramidal reactions (including dystonic reactions) may appear shortly after initiation (within 36 hours) and often remit within 24 hours of withdrawal; severe reactions may require anticholinergic/antiparkinson treatment. [Australian]
- Tardive dyskinesia may be persistent and sometimes irreversible; no reliably effective treatment is known. [Australian]
- Paracetamol: reports of adverse reactions are rare; dyspepsia, nausea, allergic and haematological reactions have been reported but causal relationship is not confirmed. HAGMA due to pyroglutamic acidosis has been observed in patients with risk factors; frequency not known. [Australian]

### Serious adverse effects (highlighted)
- Tardive dyskinesia (potentially irreversible)
- Neuroleptic malignant syndrome (potentially fatal)
- Severe hepatotoxicity and acute liver failure following paracetamol overdose (see Overdose below)
- Severe dystonic reactions with potential for respiratory compromise
- High anion gap metabolic acidosis due to pyroglutamic acidosis in at-risk patients on paracetamol (often with other risk factors and/or flucloxacillin)

### Overdose (extracted from adverse/overdose information)
- Paracetamol overdose:
  - Symptoms: vomiting, abdominal pain, hypotension, sweating, CNS excitation including convulsions in children, drowsiness, respiratory depression, cyanosis, coma. Hepatotoxicity may develop after 48–72 hours and can be fatal. Adults: single ingestion of 10–15 g may produce hepatotoxicity; ≥25 g potentially fatal.
  - Management: prompt treatment essential even if asymptomatic; activated charcoal to reduce absorption; antidotes: methionine or acetylcysteine if given within a few hours depending on time since ingestion; supportive care and monitoring of liver function, coagulation, glucose, acid–base.
- Metoclopramide overdose:
  - Symptoms: extrapyramidal side effects more frequently reported; very rarely atrioventricular block.
  - Management: gastric decontamination, supportive care; antiparkinson agents or antihistamine/anticholinergic drugs (e.g. diphenhydramine) can control extrapyramidal reactions. Haemodialysis and continuous ambulatory peritoneal dialysis are not effective for metoclopramide removal. [Australian]

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Metoclopramide interactions
  - Anticholinergic drugs and opioid analgesics: anticholinergics and opioids antagonize the prokinetic effects of metoclopramide. [Australian]
  - CNS depressants: additive sedation may occur with alcohol, sedatives, hypnotics, narcotics or tranquillisers; monitor patients for increased sedation. [Australian]
  - Drugs affected by altered gastrointestinal motility: metoclopramide accelerates gastric emptying and small-bowel transit; this may increase or decrease absorption of concomitant oral drugs:
    - May accelerate absorption of drugs absorbed in small bowel (e.g. paracetamol, tetracycline, L‑dopa).
    - May reduce absorption of drugs primarily absorbed in the stomach (e.g. digoxin). [Australian]
  - Concomitant use with neuroleptics or other antidopaminergic agents increases risk of extrapyramidal symptoms and neuroleptic malignant syndrome; avoid or monitor closely. [Australian]
- Paracetamol interactions
  - Anticoagulants (e.g. warfarin): prolonged paracetamol therapy may necessitate anticoagulant dose reduction (monitor INR). [Australian]
  - Gastric motility modifiers: drugs that increase gastric emptying (e.g. metoclopramide) increase paracetamol absorption; drugs that decrease gastric emptying (e.g. anticholinergic antidepressants, propantheline, opioids) decrease paracetamol absorption. [Australian]
  - Chloramphenicol: paracetamol may increase chloramphenicol concentrations. [Australian]
  - Enzyme-inducing agents: chronic alcohol or anticonvulsant therapy may increase the likelihood of paracetamol hepatotoxicity. [Australian]
  - Concomitant use with flucloxacillin: caution — concurrent intake has been associated with high anion gap metabolic acidosis due to pyroglutamic acidosis (especially in patients with risk factors). [Australian]
- Clinical management notes
  - Monitor for increased sedation with CNS depressant co‑administration; adjust doses where necessary. [Australian]
  - Monitor INR if long-term paracetamol treatment is given to patients on anticoagulants. [Australian]
  - Consider avoiding combination with other drugs that increase risk of extrapyramidal reactions where possible. [Australian]

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- Pregnancy
  - Overall statement for combination product: Insufficient safety data to support use of the paracetamol/metoclopramide combination in pregnancy; use contraindicated in available source. [Australian]
  - Individual component categories (Australian pregnancy categories):
    - Metoclopramide: Category A [Australian]
    - Paracetamol: Category A [Australian]
  - Adequate human data for metoclopramide in pregnancy are not available in source. [Australian]
- Breastfeeding
  - Both paracetamol and metoclopramide are excreted in breast milk. [Australian]
  - Paracetamol: amounts in milk are small; maternal ingestion of usual analgesic doses does not appear to present a risk to breastfed infant. Reported milk levels: <0.1% of a 500 mg dose to 0.04–0.23% of a 650 mg dose (various reports). [Australian]
  - Metoclopramide: may increase the risk of adverse reactions in young children breastfed by mothers receiving metoclopramide; insufficient safety data to support use of the combination product during lactation — use contraindicated in available source. [Australian]
- Fertility
  - No data available in source documents.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
- Paediatric use
  - The combination product is contraindicated in children and adolescents under 18 years due to higher incidence of metoclopramide-related adverse reactions. [Australian]
  - Dosing for paediatrics: Not applicable / contraindicated. [Australian]
- Elderly
  - No specific dosing data available for elderly. Source notes an increased risk of tardive dyskinesia with long-term therapy in elderly patients, particularly females; exercise caution. [Australian]
- Renal impairment
  - Use with caution. Plasma concentrations of paracetamol and its conjugates are increased in moderate renal failure. Clearance of metoclopramide is likely reduced in renal impairment; specific dosing adjustments by eGFR/stage are not provided. [Australian]
- Hepatic impairment
  - Use with caution. Clearance of metoclopramide is likely reduced in hepatic impairment; paracetamol is hepatically metabolised and hepatotoxicity risk exists with overdose or concomitant enzyme inducers; specific dosing adjustments by severity are not provided. [Australian]

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Metoclopramide:
  - Dopamine (D2) receptor antagonist with central and peripheral antidopaminergic activity.
  - Stimulates gastrointestinal motility, accelerates gastric emptying and small bowel transit; used for symptomatic relief of nausea and vomiting.
  - Increases rate of absorption of orally administered paracetamol. [Australian]
- Paracetamol:
  - Analgesic and antipyretic; mechanism not fully clarified in source documents but provides relief of migraine-related pain. [Australian]
