# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) documents from the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements. 
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive prescribing information.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP).
*   **Chemical Class:** A naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism (facilitates long-chain fatty acid transport).
*   **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
*   **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only [US].
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric)
*   **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion. 
    *   **Severe Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. 
    *   **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    *   **Maximum Dose:** The highest recorded dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].
*   **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** 
    *   **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    *   **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    *   **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations (target range: 40 µmol/L to 50 µmol/L) [US].

### Administration Instructions
*   Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
*   **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of intravenous administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and initiate medical treatment [US].
*   **Seizures:** Levocarnitine may cause seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been reported [US].
*   **Oral Levocarnitine in Renal Impairment:** The safety of oral levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in urine [US].
*   **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Monitor weekly and monthly, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma free carnitine concentrations (target: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concurrently with levocarnitine.
*   **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine treatment or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified, but described as "Category C" equivalent]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Not specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after exogenous administration. Weigh the benefits of maternal supplementation against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].
*   **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg). See Section 3 for specific instructions [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Renal Impairment:** See Section 3 (ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis) and Section 5 regarding the accumulation of oral metabolites [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, thereby clearing toxic acyl-CoA compounds [US].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    *   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    *   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (primarily feces).
    *   **Elimination:** Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x weekly after dialysis), trough levocarnitine concentrations increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
*   **Dose-Ranging Data:** A study of ~90 ESRD patients compared 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses. Trough concentrations at 24 weeks were 148, 240, and 456 µmol/L, respectively. Note: While carnitine levels increased, the effect on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
*   **Conditions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
*   **Handling:** Vials are single-dose and contain no preservative; discard any unused portion [US].
