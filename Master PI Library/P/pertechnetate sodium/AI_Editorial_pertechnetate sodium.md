# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single, structured reference.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic management of patients with metabolic defects resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance haemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Metabolic Disorders:**
    - **Adult and Paediatric Dosing:** Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2–3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    - **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
    - **Maintenance:** Daily maintenance doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg may be required based on clinical response [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Haemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus (2–3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - **Dose Adjustment:** Should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine range: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal oedema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of intravenous administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue the drug immediately and initiate appropriate medical care [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a known seizure disorder, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures has been reported [US].
- **General Precautions:** The safety of oral levocarnitine has not been established in renal insufficiency; chronic high doses of oral formulations in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects observed in clinical trials of ESRD patients (occurring at ≥5% frequency) include:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), headache (22%), chest pain (12%), and fever (7%) [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhoea (16%), vomiting (15%), abdominal pain (9%), and nausea (8%) [US]. Transient nausea and vomiting are common. Body odour and gastritis have been reported less frequently [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), and tachycardia (6%) [US].
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough (9%), and dyspnea (7%) [US].
- **Neurological:** Dizziness (13%) and paresthesia (5%) [US].
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcaemia (9%) and hyperkalaemia (6%) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalised Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels at the initiation of therapy or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies in nursing mothers have not been conducted. Levocarnitine is naturally present in breast milk, and studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations following exogenous administration. The benefits of maternal supplementation must be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is consistent with adult weight-based recommendations (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** See "ESRD Patients on Haemodialysis" in Section 3. The accumulation of metabolites from oral formulations is a concern in severe renal impairment [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, facilitating oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in certain organic acidopathies [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, levocarnitine administration increases plasma carnitine concentrations and may alleviate metabolic abnormalities [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Distribution half-life is approximately 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in faeces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly post-dialysis increased pre-dialysis trough levels from 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While carnitine levels increased, the impact on specific clinical outcomes in this population remains undetermined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat. Discard any unused portion as the product contains no preservatives [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) [US].
