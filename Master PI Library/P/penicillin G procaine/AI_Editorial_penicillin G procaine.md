# Editorial Product Information: Penicillin G Benzathine and Penicillin G Procaine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local clinical guidelines. Prescribers should consult the specific regulatory documentation valid in their jurisdiction prior to administration.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Penicillin G benzathine and Penicillin G procaine.
- **Chemical Class:** Natural penicillins; beta-lactam antibiotics.
- **Brand Names:** Bicillin C-R [US].
- **ATC Code:** J01CE30 (Combination of penicillins).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Injectable suspension in pre-filled disposable syringes: 1,200,000 units per 2 mL (comprising 600,000 units of penicillin G benzathine and 600,000 units of penicillin G procaine) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Deep Intramuscular (IM) injection only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Laboratories Div Pfizer Inc [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Streptococcal Infections (Group A):** Treatment of moderately severe to severe infections of the upper respiratory tract, scarlet fever, erysipelas, and skin and soft-tissue infections [US].
- **Pneumococcal Infections:** Treatment of moderately severe pneumonia and otitis media (excluding pneumococcal meningitis) [US].
- **Note on Limitations:** 
    - This combination is not indicated for the treatment of venereal diseases, including syphilis, gonorrhea, yaws, bejel, or pinta [US]. 
    - Penicillin G sodium or potassium should be used when high, sustained serum levels are required (e.g., bacteremia, meningitis, or acute stages of severe pneumonia) [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Group A Streptococcal Infections:** 
    - Adults and children >60 lbs (approx. 27 kg): 2,400,000 units.
    - Children 30–60 lbs (approx. 13.5–27 kg): 900,000 to 1,200,000 units.
    - Children <30 lbs (approx. 13.5 kg): 600,000 units.
    - *Note:* Dosing is typically administered in a single session using multiple injection sites if required. Alternatively, half the dose may be given on Day 1 and the remainder on Day 3 [US].
- **Pneumococcal Infections (except meningitis):**
    - Adults: 1,200,000 units.
    - Children: 600,000 units.
    - *Frequency:* Repeat every 2 to 3 days until the patient has been afebrile for 48 hours [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Route:** Deep IM injection ONLY. 
- **Site:** Upper, outer quadrant of the buttock (dorsogluteal) or ventrogluteal site. In neonates and small children, the midlateral aspect of the thigh may be preferred [US].
- **Technique:** Use a slow, steady rate to prevent needle blockage due to the high concentration of suspended material. Aspirate before injection to ensure the needle is not in a blood vessel [US].
- **Prohibition:** Do not inject intravenously (IV), intra-arterially, or near major nerves [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to any penicillin-class antibiotic [US].
- Known hypersensitivity to procaine or local anesthetics of the ester type [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Not for Intravenous Use.** Inadvertent IV administration has been associated with cardiorespiratory arrest and death. Do not inject IV or admix with other IV solutions [US].
- **Anaphylaxis:** Serious and occasionally fatal hypersensitivity reactions may occur. Cross-reactivity with cephalosporins is possible. Emergency equipment (epinephrine, oxygen, airway management) must be available [US].
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCAR):** SJS, TEN, DRESS, and AGEP have been reported. Discontinue immediately if SCAR is suspected [US].
- **Methemoglobinemia:** Associated with the procaine component. Risk is higher in infants <6 months, patients with G6PD deficiency, or those taking other oxidizing agents. Monitor for cyanosis [US].
- **Clostridioides difficile-Associated Diarrhea (CDAD):** May range from mild diarrhea to fatal colitis. Can occur up to two months post-treatment [US].
- **Neurovascular Damage (Nicolau Syndrome):** Inadvertent injection into or near an artery/nerve can cause permanent neurological damage, transverse myelitis, gangrene, and necrosis. Infants and small children are at higher risk [US].
- **Hoigne’s Syndrome:** A pseudo-anaphylactic reaction (agitation, hallucinations, fear of death) may occur following penicillin G procaine administration, likely due to accidental intravascular entry [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Frequencies are not specifically quantified in the source document; the following are reported based on clinical experience/post-marketing [US]:
- **General:** Hypersensitivity (skin eruptions, urticaria, fever, eosinophilia), serum sickness-like reactions, anaphylaxis.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Pseudomembranous colitis, nausea, vomiting, blood in stool.
- **Hematologic:** Hemolytic anemia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia.
- **Neurologic:** Neuropathy, seizures (with overdose), Hoigne's syndrome symptoms (tremors, dizziness, confusion, psychosis).
- **Injection Site:** Pain, inflammation, lump, abscess, necrosis, and neurovascular reactions (pallor, mottling, gangrene).
- **Other:** Nephropathy, Kounis syndrome (allergic myocardial ischemia), rhabdomyolysis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Tetracyclines:** Bacteriostatic antibiotics may antagonize the bactericidal effect of penicillin; concurrent use should be avoided [US].
- **Probenecid:** Competitively inhibits renal tubular secretion, increasing and prolonging serum penicillin levels [US].
- **Oxidizing Agents/Local Anesthetics:** Increased risk of methemoglobinemia when used concurrently with procaine (e.g., nitrates, lidocaine, sulfonamides, acetaminophen) [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not explicitly lettered]. Reproduction studies in animals showed no impairment of fertility or fetal harm. Human data has not shown evidence of adverse effects, but no well-controlled studies in pregnant women exist. Use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Soluble penicillin G is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution when administering to nursing mothers [US].
- **Fertility:** No long-term animal studies have been conducted regarding carcinogenesis or mutagenesis [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Weight-based dosing applies. Increased risk of neurovascular damage; avoid injection into the anterolateral thigh due to risk of quadriceps femoris fibrosis [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Clinical studies did not identify differences in response compared to younger patients. However, because the drug is substantially excreted by the kidney, dose selection should be cautious (starting at the low end), and renal function monitoring is recommended [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Excretion is considerably delayed in patients with impaired kidney function; monitoring and potential dose adjustment are required [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal; inhibits the biosynthesis of cell-wall peptidoglycan during active multiplication, leading to bacterial cell death [US].
- **Microbiology:** Active against penicillin-susceptible *Streptococcus pneumoniae* and beta-hemolytic streptococci (Groups A, B, C, G, H, L, and M). Not active against penicillinase-producing bacteria [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Low solubility results in slow release from IM sites, providing prolonged but lower serum levels compared to other parenteral penicillins. Peak levels in adults are reached within 3 hours [US].
    - **Distribution:** Approximately 60% protein bound. Highest concentrations in kidneys; lower in liver, skin, and intestines. Poor CNS penetration unless meninges are inflamed [US].
    - **Elimination:** Rapidly excreted via renal tubular secretion in patients with normal kidney function [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store in a refrigerator between 2°C and 8°C (36°F to 46°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Keep from freezing. Protect from light where possible [US].
