# Editorial Product Information: Patisiran

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, specifically clinical pharmacists.
- This document serves as an editorial synthesis and clinical reference; it is not a substitute for the official PI and should not be used as the sole basis for prescribing or dispensing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Patisiran (as patisiran sodium)
- **Chemical Class**: Double-stranded small interfering ribonucleic acid (siRNA) formulated as lipid nanoparticles (LNPs).
- **Brand Name**: **Onpattro** [Australian]
- **ATC Code**: N07XX12
- **Dosage Form and Strength**: Concentrated injection for infusion; 10 mg/5 mL (2 mg/mL).
- **Route of Administration**: Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Medison Pharma Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- Treatment of hereditary transthyretin-mediated amyloidosis (hATTR amyloidosis) in adult patients with stage 1 or stage 2 polyneuropathy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Standard Dose**: 300 micrograms per kg body weight administered once every 3 weeks via IV infusion.
- **Maximum Dose**: For patients weighing $\ge$ 100 kg, the dose should not exceed 30 mg.

### Required Premedication
To reduce the risk of infusion-related reactions (IRRs), all patients must receive the following regimen at least 60 minutes prior to infusion:
- **IV Corticosteroid**: Dexamethasone 10 mg (or equivalent).
- **Oral Paracetamol**: 500 mg.
- **IV H1 Blocker**: Diphenhydramine 50 mg (or equivalent).
- **IV H2 Blocker**: Famotidine 20 mg (or equivalent).

*Note: If IV forms are unavailable or not tolerated, oral equivalents may be used. Corticosteroids may be tapered in 2.5 mg increments to a minimum of 5 mg dexamethasone if the patient tolerates three consecutive infusions without IRRs.*

### Preparation and Administration Instructions
- **Filtration**: The concentrate must be filtered through a sterile 0.45 micron polyethersulfone (PES) syringe filter before dilution.
- **Dilution**: Dilute the required volume into an infusion bag of 0.9% Sodium Chloride for a total volume of 200 mL. Use DEHP-free bags.
- **Infusion Setup**: Use a dedicated line with a 1.2 micron PES in-line filter. Sets/lines must be DEHP-free.
- **Infusion Rate**: Approximately 1 mL/min for the first 15 minutes, then increase to approximately 3 mL/min for the remainder (total duration ~80 minutes).
- **Home Infusion**: May be considered for patients who have well-tolerated at least three infusions in a clinical setting.

### Missed Dose
- **Within 3 days**: Administer as soon as possible and maintain the original schedule.
- **More than 3 days**: Administer as soon as possible and reset the 3-week schedule from that date.

## 4. Contraindications
- Severe hypersensitivity (e.g., anaphylaxis) to patisiran or any of its excipients (including lipid components and sodium) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Infusion-Related Reactions (IRRs)**: Frequently occur during the first two infusions. Symptoms include flushing, back pain, nausea, abdominal pain, dyspnoea, and headache. In case of IRR, slow or interrupt the infusion. Discontinue permanently for life-threatening reactions.
- **Vitamin A Deficiency**: Patisiran reduces serum transthyretin (TTR), which is the carrier for retinol-binding protein. This leads to significantly decreased serum Vitamin A levels.
    - **Supplementation**: All patients should take approximately 2500 IU Vitamin A per day.
    - **Monitoring**: Ocular symptoms (night blindness, dry eyes, corneal inflammation) require ophthalmological referral.
    - **Note**: Serum Vitamin A levels do not accurately reflect total body stores during treatment and should not guide supplementation.
- **Sodium Content**: Contains 3.99 mg sodium per mL (approx. 20 mg per vial).

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Table
| System Organ Class | Adverse Reaction | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **General/Administration** | Peripheral oedema | Very Common (29.7%) |
| **Immune System** | Infusion-related reactions | Very Common (18.9%) |
| **Infections** | Bronchitis, Sinusitis, Rhinitis | Common |
| **Musculoskeletal** | Arthralgia, Muscle spasms | Common |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Dyspepsia | Common |
| **Ear/Labyrinth** | Vertigo | Common |
| **Respiratory** | Dyspnoea | Common |
| **Skin** | Erythema | Common |
| **General** | Extravasation | Uncommon (<0.5%) |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Infusion-Related Reactions**: Although mostly mild-to-moderate, they resulted in permanent discontinuation in <1% of clinical trial patients.
- **Extravasation**: May lead to phlebitis, cellulitis, or burning sensations. Monitor the infusion site closely.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions**: No formal clinical studies performed.
- **Cytochrome P450**: Based on *in vitro* data, patisiran may cause induction and time-dependent inhibition of **CYP2B6**. The clinical relevance for substrates like bupropion or efavirenz is unknown.
- **General**: Patisiran is not a substrate, inhibitor, or inducer of other major CYP enzymes or transporters.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: **Category D** [Australian]. There are no data in pregnant women.
- **Teratogenic Risk**: Unbalanced Vitamin A levels (too high or too low) are associated with foetal malformations.
    - **Pre-conception**: Discontinue patisiran and Vitamin A; ensure serum Vitamin A levels have normalised before attempting conception.
    - **First Trimester**: Discontinue Vitamin A unless signs of clinical deficiency are present (max 2500 IU/day if deficiency occurs). Resume 2500 IU/day in 2nd and 3rd trimesters.
- **Breastfeeding**: Safety not established in humans. In animal studies (rats), small amounts of lipid components were present in milk, but patisiran was not. A benefit-risk assessment is required.
- **Fertility**: No human data. Animal studies showed no adverse effects on male or female fertility at clinically relevant exposures.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and efficacy in children/adolescents < 18 years not established.
- **Elderly Use ($\ge$ 65 years)**: No dose adjustment required.
- **Renal Impairment**: No dose adjustment for mild or moderate impairment (eGFR $\ge$ 30 to < 90 mL/min/1.73m²). Not studied in severe or end-stage renal disease.
- **Hepatic Impairment**: No dose adjustment for mild impairment. Not studied in moderate or severe hepatic impairment; use only if benefits outweigh risks.
- **Liver Transplant**: Safety and efficacy profiles in liver transplant recipients were consistent with non-transplant patients in limited studies.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Patisiran uses RNA interference (RNAi) to cause catalytic degradation of TTR mRNA in the liver. This reduces the production of both mutant and wild-type TTR protein.
- **Pharmacodynamics**: Mean serum TTR reduction of ~80% within 10–14 days of a single dose. Reductions of 83–84% maintained with chronic dosing.
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Distribution**: Low protein binding ($\le$ 2.1%).
    - **Metabolism**: Metabolised by nucleases to nucleotides of various lengths.
    - **Elimination**: Plasma clearance 3.0 mL/h/kg; terminal half-life approximately 3.2 days. < 1% excreted intact in urine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Study**: A randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled study (n=225) in hATTR patients with polyneuropathy.
- **Primary Endpoint**: Modified Neuropathy Impairment Score +7 (mNIS+7) at 18 months.
- **Results**: Onpattro-treated patients showed a significant 34.0-point mean difference vs. placebo (p < 0.001). 56% of patients showed improvement from baseline score.
- **Secondary Outcomes**: Significant improvements in Norfolk Quality of Life-Diabetic Neuropathy (QoL-DN) and gait speed (10-metre walk test).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Vials**: Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze).
- **Temperature Excursion**: May be stored at room temperature (up to 25°C) for up to 14 days if refrigeration is unavailable.
- **Diluted Solution**: Use as soon as possible. If necessary, store at room temperature (15°C to 30°C) for up to 16 hours (including infusion time). Do not freeze.
