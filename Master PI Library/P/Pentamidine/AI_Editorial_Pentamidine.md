# Editorial Product Information: Pentamidine

## Editorial Note
- The information contained in this editorial was synthesized from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a structured format.
- This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference and does not substitute for the official PI or clinical guidelines specific to the practitioner's jurisdiction. Prescribers should consult local regulatory authorities for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pentamidine isetionate (also referred to as pentamidine isethionate).
- **Chemical Class:** Diamidine antiprotozoal agent.
- **Brand Names:** DBL Pentamidine Isethionate for Injection [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** P01CX01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 300 mg of pentamidine isetionate provided as a freeze-dried powder or plug in a single-dose vial [Australian].
- **Equivalency:** 4 mg of pentamidine isetionate is equivalent to 2.3 mg of pentamidine base.
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion. Note: Injected pentamidine is not approved for inhalation in the Australian jurisdiction [Australian].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Pneumocystis jirovecii (formerly P. carinii) pneumonia (PCP):**
    - Alternative first-line treatment in patients with AIDS [Australian].
    - Second-line treatment in non-AIDS patients [Australian].
- **Leishmaniasis (Visceral and Cutaneous):**
    - Generally used as second-line treatment, except in cases of *Leishmania aethiopica*, where it may be employed as first-line therapy [Australian].
- **Trypanosomiasis:**
    - Second-line treatment for the haemolymphete stage (ineffective against *Trypanosomiasis rhodesiense*) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia:** 4 mg/kg body weight once daily for 14 days [Australian].
- **Visceral Leishmaniasis (Kala-azar):** 3 to 4 mg/kg body weight on alternate days (three times weekly) for a maximum of 10 injections [Australian].
- **Cutaneous Leishmaniasis:** 3 to 4 mg/kg body weight once or twice weekly until resolution [Australian].
- **Trypanosomiasis (haemolymphete stage):** 4 mg/kg body weight daily or on alternate days for a total of 7 to 10 injections [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Preparation:** Reconstitute the 300 mg vial with 3 mL to 5 mL of Water for Injections. Further dilute the dose in 50 mL to 250 mL of either Glucose 5% or Sodium Chloride 0.9% [Australian].
- **Infusion:** Administer strictly by slow intravenous infusion over at least 60 minutes.
- **Patient Positioning:** Patients must remain in a supine position during administration to mitigate the risk of sudden, severe hypotension [Australian].
- **Contraindicated Routes:** Direct bolus or rapid intravenous injection must not be used [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to pentamidine isetionate [Australian].
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding, unless the clinical situation is deemed essential by the treating physician [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Serious Warnings and Precautions
- **Fatalities:** Deaths have occurred due to severe hypotension, hypoglycaemia, acute pancreatitis, renal failure, and cardiac arrhythmias [Australian].
- **Low Therapeutic Index:** The ratio between therapeutic and toxic doses is very narrow; adverse effects occur frequently [Australian].
- **Hospital Setting:** Administration should only occur in a hospital environment with immediate access to resuscitation equipment and monitoring facilities [Australian].

### General Precautions
- **Cardiovascular:** Sudden, severe hypotension may occur after a single dose. Monitor blood pressure closely during and after infusion. QT interval prolongation and *torsade de pointes* have been reported [Australian].
- **Endocrine/Metabolism:** Severe, life-threatening hypoglycaemia may occur (typically after 5–7 days) and may be followed by hyperglycaemia or the development of permanent diabetes mellitus [Australian].
- **Pancreatitis:** Acute pancreatitis has been documented and requires treatment cessation [Australian].
- **Extravasation:** May lead to tissue necrosis, ulceration, and long-term sequelae [Australian].

### Monitoring Requirements
The following should be monitored before, during, and after therapy [Australian]:
- **Daily:** Blood urea nitrogen (BUN), serum creatinine, CBC, platelet counts, and fasting blood glucose.
- **Weekly:** Serum calcium and liver function tests (bilirubin, ALP, AST, ALT).
- **Regular Intervals:** Electrocardiograms (ECG), urine analysis, and serum electrolytes.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are frequent due to the drug's toxicity profile. Frequencies are not explicitly categorized by percentage in available sources but are described by severity [Australian].

| System Organ Class | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Vascular** | Severe hypotension (potentially fatal), facial flushing, venous thrombosis, phlebitis. |
| **Metabolism** | Hypoglycaemia (severe/prolonged), hypocalcaemia, hyperglycaemia, hyperkalaemia, hyponatraemia, diabetes mellitus. |
| **Cardiac** | QT prolongation, *torsade de pointes*, ventricular tachycardia, cardiac arrest, bradycardia. |
| **Renal** | Acute renal failure, azotaemia, albuminuria, glycosuria, increased creatinine. |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Acute pancreatitis, nausea, vomiting, taste disturbances. |
| **Haematologic** | Leucopenia (<1,000/mm³), thrombocytopenia (<20,000/mm³), anaemia, neutropenia. |
| **Hepatic** | Abnormal liver function tests, hepatic dysfunction. |
| **Skin/Local** | Injection site abscess, necrosis, rash, Stevens-Johnson syndrome (rare), alopecia. |
| **Neurological** | Toxic delirium, syncope, dizziness, hallucinations. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Nephrotoxic Agents:** Risk of additive nephrotoxicity when used concurrently or sequentially with aminoglycosides, amphotericin B, cisplatin, vancomycin, or methoxyflurane. Use with extreme caution [Australian].
- **Hepatotoxic Agents:** Increased risk of liver dysfunction [Australian].
- **Haematopoietic Suppressants:** Increased risk of severe bone marrow toxicity [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category B3. Animal studies show evidence of increased foetal damage (embryotoxicity and delayed ossification). Pentamidine crosses the placenta in animal models. Use in humans is contraindicated unless the benefits outweigh the unknown risks to the foetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety is not established; distribution into human milk is unknown. Contraindicated unless considered essential [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established. Pharmacokinetic data are extremely limited [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific information available regarding use in the elderly [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution. Severe renal impairment (including death) has been reported, particularly when other nephrotoxic agents or sepsis are present. Kinetics are poorly defined for ClCr <35 mL/min [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No specific dosing adjustments provided in available sources, but monitoring is required [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Antiprotozoal activity. The exact mechanism is not fully elucidated but appears to involve interference with nuclear metabolism (interference with DNA, RNA, phospholipid, and protein synthesis) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak plasma levels average 612 ng/mL after a 4 mg/kg IV infusion [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Rapidly and extensively distributed to tissues (highest in liver, followed by kidneys, adrenals, spleen, lungs, and pancreas). Minimal CNS penetration. 69% protein bound [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Biphasic decline. Initial half-life ~18 minutes; terminal half-life ~6.4 hours. Accumulates in tissues and is released very slowly.
    - **Excretion:** Approximately 6% excreted unchanged in urine over 15 days [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Not specified in available sources.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store the dry powder below 25°C [Australian].
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Chemically stable for 48 hours at 2°C to 8°C and at room temperature under fluorescent light. However, to maintain sterility, the product should ideally be used within 24 hours of preparation [Australian].
