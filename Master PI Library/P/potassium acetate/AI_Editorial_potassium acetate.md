# Editorial Product Information: Potassium Acetate

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Regulatory Agencies:** Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA - Australia), Food and Drug Administration (FDA - United States).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of potassium acetate, synthesising information from international product information (PI) sources for healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Potassium acetate (Generic name).
- **Brand Names:** 
    - **DBL Potassium Acetate Concentrated Injection** [TGA]
    - **Normosol-M and 5% Dextrose Injection** (Combination product containing potassium acetate, sodium chloride, magnesium acetate, and dextrose) [FDA]
- **ATC Code:** A12BA04
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Concentrated Injection:** 2.45 g/5 mL (490 mg/mL) in glass ampoules. Each mL contains 5 mEq (5 mmol) of potassium and acetate ions [TGA].
    - **Parenteral Maintenance Solution (Combination):** 128 mg/100 mL potassium acetate (providing 13 mEq/L of potassium) in 500 mL or 1000 mL flexible containers [FDA].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only. **Must be diluted prior to use** [TGA].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** 
    - Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [TGA]
    - ICU Medical, Inc. [FDA]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypokalaemia Management:** Prophylaxis and treatment of potassium deficiency in patients with or without metabolic acidosis [TGA].
- **Specific Clinical States:** Chronic digitalis intoxication and hypokalaemic familial periodic paralysis [TGA].
- **Parenteral Nutrition:** As an electrolyte supply in total parenteral nutrition (TPN) solutions [TGA].
- **Maintenance Therapy:** Parenteral maintenance of routine daily fluid and electrolyte requirements (when administered as part of a multi-electrolyte solution) [FDA].
- **Requirement for Administration:** Indicated only when oral therapy is contraindicated or not tolerated [TGA].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Instructions
- **DILUTION MANDATORY:** Concentrated potassium acetate must be diluted in a compatible infusion fluid before administration [TGA].
- **Visual Inspection:** Solutions should be clear and containers undamaged. Discard any unused portions.

### Adult Dosing
- **Standard Infusion Rate:** Should not exceed 10 mEq/hour [TGA].
- **Standard Concentration:** Prepared infusion should ideally be less than 40 mEq/L [TGA].
- **Maximum Daily Dose:** Generally should not exceed 150 mEq per 24 hours [TGA].
- **Maintenance (Combination Product):** 1500 mL/m² of body surface area typically meets daily requirements; approximately 3 litres for a 70 kg adult [FDA].

### Urgent Treatment (K+ < 2 mEq/L with ECG changes)
- **Rate:** Up to 40 mEq/hour [TGA].
- **Maximum Dose:** 400 mEq per 24 hours [TGA].
- **Requirements:** Must use an infusion pump, continuous ECG monitoring, and frequent serum potassium testing if rates exceed 20 mEq/hour [TGA].

### Administration Nuances
- **Fluid Selection:** In critical states, saline is preferred over glucose, as glucose may further decrease plasma potassium levels [TGA].
- **Mixing:** Avoid "layering" of potassium in flexible containers; invert and agitate containers during addition to ensure thorough mixing [TGA, FDA].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Metabolic or Respiratory Alkalosis:** Acetate is a bicarbonate precursor and may exacerbate these conditions [TGA].
- **Renal Impairment:** Severe impairment associated with oliguria, anuria, or azotaemia [TGA].
- **Hyperkalaemia:** Including the hyperkalaemic form of familial periodic paralysis [TGA].
- **Adrenal Insufficiency:** Untreated chronic adrenocortical insufficiency (Addison's disease) [TGA].
- **Cardiovascular Conditions:** Ventricular fibrillation, atrioventricular or intraventricular heart block [TGA].
- **Other:** Acute dehydration, extensive tissue breakdown (e.g., severe burns), heat cramps, uncontrolled diabetes mellitus, and hyperadrenalism associated with adrenogenital syndrome [TGA].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to potassium [TGA].
- **Note:** The FDA PI for the maintenance combination product states "none known" for that specific formulation [FDA].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Rapid Administration:** Rapid IV injection can cause fatal hyperkalaemia. Serum levels of 8–11 mEq/L may cause death via cardiac depression or arrest [TGA].
- **Monitoring:** Periodic laboratory determinations (electrolytes, acid-base balance) and clinical evaluation are essential during prolonged therapy [TGA, FDA].
- **Acetate Metabolism:** Use with extreme caution in patients with impaired acetate utilisation, such as severe hepatic insufficiency [TGA, FDA].
- **Heart Block:** Use cautiously as increased potassium can increase the degree of block [TGA].
- **Fluid Overload:** Administration can cause fluid/solute overloading, resulting in pulmonary oedema or congested states [FDA].
- **Extravasation:** Care must be taken to avoid local tissue injury [TGA].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common/Serious (Frequency not defined):**
    - **Hyperkalaemia:** Characterised by paraesthesia, listlessness, mental confusion, weakness, flaccid paralysis, and peripheral vascular collapse [TGA].
    - **Cardiac Effects:** Arrhythmias, heart block, and cardiac arrest [TGA].
    - **ECG Changes:** Tall peaked T waves, ST segment depression, disappearance of P waves, QT interval prolongation, and widening/slurring of the QRS complex [TGA].
- **Local Reactions:**
    - Pain at the injection site and phlebitis (more common at concentrations ≥ 30 mEq/L) [TGA, FDA].
    - Infection at the site of injection and extravasation [FDA].
- **Systemic Reactions:** Febrile response, venous thrombosis, and hypervolemia [FDA].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Potassium-Sparing Diuretics:** (e.g., amiloride, spironolactone, triamterene). Simultaneous use may result in severe, potentially fatal hyperkalaemia [TGA].
- **ACE Inhibitors:** (e.g., enalapril, captopril). Concurrent use may induce hyperkalaemia [TGA].
- **Digoxin:** Potassium protects cardiac muscle from digoxin toxicity; however, rapid lowering of potassium in digitalised patients can precipitate toxicity [TGA].
- **Bicarbonate:** May prevent the reversal of hypokalaemia if administered concurrently with potassium [TGA].
- **Corticosteroids/Corticotropin:** Exercise caution when administering electrolyte solutions to patients on these agents [FDA].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **FDA Category C:** Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. Use only if clearly needed [FDA].
    - **General:** Potassium is a natural tissue constituent. Serum levels must be closely monitored to avoid maternal or foetal cardiac dysfunction [TGA].
- **Breastfeeding:** Potassium is excreted into breast milk (normal content approx. 13 mEq/L). No specific adverse data provided [TGA].
- **Fertility:** No data available [TGA].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - **Dosing:** Maximum 3 mmol/kg per day [TGA].
    - **Cautions:** Neonates and low birth weight infants are at higher risk of fluid/electrolyte balance shifts and hyperglycemia/hypoglycemia [FDA].
    - **Safety:** In very low birth weight infants, rapid dextrose administration (in combo products) may cause increased serum osmolality and intracerebral haemorrhage [FDA].
- **Elderly Use:** Start at the low end of the dosing range due to the higher prevalence of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function [TGA]. Monitoring of renal function is recommended [TGA].
- **Renal Impairment:** Requires careful monitoring and dose adjustment; contraindicated in severe impairment [TGA].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with great care in severe hepatic insufficiency due to impaired acetate metabolism [TGA].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Potassium is the primary intracellular cation (approx. 150–160 mEq/L). It is vital for nerve impulse conduction, muscle contraction (cardiac, skeletal, smooth), and maintaining renal function and acid-base balance [TGA, FDA].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Acetate is metabolised in the liver to bicarbonate, serving as a source of hydrogen ion acceptors and providing a mild systemic antiacidotic effect [TGA, FDA].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Distribution:** Actively transported into cells via Na+/K+-ATPase. Glucose and insulin facilitate this movement [TGA].
    - **Excretion:** Primarily renal (80–90%); the remainder is excreted in faeces and sweat. The kidneys have a poor capacity to conserve potassium even during severe depletion [TGA, FDA].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Concentrated Injection:** Store below 25°C [TGA].
- **Combination Product:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [FDA].
- **Special Handling:** Do not use flexible containers in series connections to avoid air embolism [FDA].
