# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a concise, structured format.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not explicitly specified in source, but standard for levocarnitine).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection solution: 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients where these errors result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often utilised in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Administered every 3 to 4 hours (q3h or q4h), and never less than every 6 hours (q6h) [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported administered dose is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if concentrations are adequate [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use [US].
- Discard any unused portion as the formulation lacks preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, particularly in ESRD patients on dialysis. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **TMA/TMAO Accumulation:** While primarily a concern with high-dose oral administration in renal impairment, chronic use in ESRD should be monitored as potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) are normally excreted in urine [US].
- **Monitoring:** 
    - **Metabolic Disorders:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Monitor weekly/monthly including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
    - **ESRD:** Monitor trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations (normal range: 40–50 µmol/L) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (>5% in ESRD clinical trials):**
    - **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (9%), headache (22%), infection (15%), injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), fever (7%) [US].
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%) [US].
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%) [US].
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%) [US].
- **Neurologic:** Dizziness (13%), paresthesia (5%) [US].
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hypervolemia (5%) [US].
- **Postmarketing/Other:** 
    - Body odor, gastritis, and transient nausea/vomiting [US].
    - **Serious:** Anaphylaxis and seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. **Management:** Monitor INR levels at initiation and following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned a lettered category in the provided source; however, reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation has not been specifically studied in nursing mothers. While carnitine is a normal component of human milk, the risks of excess intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impairment found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Recommended dosing for metabolic disorders is based on mg/kg, applicable to paediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific geriatric data was not provided in the source; caution is generally advised in dosing due to potential decreased organ function [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety of oral levocarnitine is not established in renal insufficiency. For injection, dose adjustments in ESRD are based on trough plasma carnitine levels [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, facilitating energy production via oxidation [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Facilitates the excretion of excess organic acids or fatty acids in patients with specific metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine compounds, which are rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life: 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and [³H]-γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life: 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study (n=12), patients receiving 20 mg/kg post-dialysis reached mean trough concentrations of 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks, compared to a baseline of 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L.
- **Dose-Ranging:** A study of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses (n~30 per group) showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) [US].

- **Legal Classification:** 
    - **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription only) [US].
