# Editorial Product Information: Prochlorperazine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) documents from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or clinical guidelines required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority for the most current and regionally specific information.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Prochlorperazine maleate (a phenothiazine derivative with a piperazine side chain).
- **Brand Names:** Ipca-Prochlorperazine [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N05AB06 (Antipsychotics; Phenothiazines with piperazine structure).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Oral Tablet:** 5 mg prochlorperazine maleate [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral. (Note: Injection and other forms are referenced in safety warnings but details are not specified in the provided oral tablet PI).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Ipca Pharma (Australia) Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Nausea and Vomiting:** Management of nausea and vomiting resulting from various etiologies, including migraine [Australian].
- **Vertigo:** Treatment of vertigo associated with Menière's syndrome, labyrinthitis, and other causes [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Nausea and Vomiting:** 5 mg to 10 mg administered two or three times daily [Australian].
- **Acute Episode:** An initial dose of 20 mg may be given, followed by 10 mg two hours later if required [Australian].
- **Vertigo and Menière's Disease:** Initially 5 mg to 10 mg three or four times daily. This may be gradually reduced to a maintenance dose of 5 mg to 10 mg daily after several weeks of treatment [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing
- **Restrictions:** Not recommended for children under 2 years of age or weighing less than 10 kg [Australian].
- **General Dosing:** If use is deemed unavoidable, the dosage is 250 micrograms/kg bodyweight, administered two or three times daily. The use of 5 mg tablets is recommended for children [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Dosage should be individualised, beginning at the lowest recommended dose.
- In cases of elderly patients or those with impaired liver function, dosage reduction and gradual titration are recommended [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to prochlorperazine, other phenothiazines, or any excipients [Australian].
- **Systemic State:** Circulatory collapse or central nervous system (CNS) depression (e.g., coma or acute intoxication with alcohol, barbiturates, or narcotics) [Australian].
- **Haematological/Hepatobiliary:** Previous history of blood dyscrasia or jaundice related to phenothiazines; bone marrow depression [Australian].
- **Paediatric Specifics:**
    - Children under 2 years (due to potential association with Sudden Infant Death Syndrome [SIDS]) [Australian].
    - Use in paediatric surgery [Australian].
    - Paediatric conditions where dosage is not established [Australian].
    - **Injection use:** Contraindicated in children due to high risk of dystonic reactions at cumulative doses of 0.5 mg/kg [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** Potentially fatal; characterised by hyperthermia, muscle rigidity, and autonomic instability. Immediate discontinuation is mandatory [Australian].
- **Tardive Dyskinesia:** Risk increases with treatment duration and cumulative dose; higher risk in elderly females. Symptoms may be masked by higher doses [Australian].
- **Extrapyramidal Symptoms:** Acute dystonic reactions may occur, particularly in children and young adults or in patients with hypocalcaemia/hypoparathyroidism [Australian].
- **Agranulocytosis:** Regular blood count monitoring is recommended. Treatment must cease if unexplained fever or infection occurs alongside blood count changes [Australian].
- **QT Prolongation:** Rare reports of QT interval prolongation; increased risk of torsade de pointes. Caution in patients with bradycardia, hypokalaemia, or congenital QT prolongation [Australian].
- **Cerebrovascular Events/Thromboembolism:** Increased risk of stroke in elderly patients with dementia. All patients should be screened for venous thromboembolism (VTE) risk factors [Australian].
- **Withdrawal:** Abrupt cessation of high doses may cause acute symptoms (nausea, insomnia, movement disorders); gradual tapering is advised [Australian].
- **General Precautions:** Avoid use in patients with Parkinson’s disease, hypothyroidism, phaeochromocytoma, myasthenia gravis, prostate hypertrophy, or narrow-angle glaucoma [Australian].
- **Photosensitisation:** Advise patients to avoid direct sunlight [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **More Common** | Drowsiness, akathisia, parkinsonism (tremor, rigidity), orthostatic hypotension, blurred vision, constipation, dry mouth [Australian]. |
| **Less Common** | Dystonia, tardive dyskinesia, seizures, agranulocytosis, leucopenia, cholestatic jaundice, elevated liver enzymes, peripheral oedema, ECG changes (QT prolongation, ST depression), galactorrhoea, gynaecomastia, amenorrhoea, erectile dysfunction, urinary retention, photosensitivity [Australian]. |
| **Not Known** | Torsade de pointes, eosinophilia, venous thromboembolism (VTE), neuroleptic malignant syndrome, SIADH, hyponatraemia, neonatal drug withdrawal syndrome [Australian]. |

**Serious Reactions:** Prochlorperazine can cause severe acute dystonic reactions in children, potentially leading to laryngospasm, cyanosis, and death [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Contraindicated:** Dopaminergics (e.g., levodopa) due to mutual antagonism (except in specific Parkinsonian management) [Australian].
- **Adrenaline:** Contraindicated in prochlorperazine overdose; may cause paradoxical hypotension [Australian].
- **CNS Depressants:** Potentiates effects of alcohol, barbiturates, sedatives, and opioids [Australian].
- **Anticholinergics:** May reduce antipsychotic efficacy and increase side effects like dry mouth and constipation [Australian].
- **QT Prolonging Agents:** Increased risk of arrhythmias with Class Ia/III antiarrhythmics, certain antibiotics, and other antipsychotics [Australian].
- **Metabolic Interactions:** 
    - **Desferrioxamine:** Simultaneous use may induce transient metabolic encephalopathy (loss of consciousness for 48-72 hours) [Australian].
    - **CYP2D6 Inhibitors:** Prochlorperazine may increase plasma levels of CYP2D6 substrates (e.g., amitriptyline) [Australian].
    - **MAOIs:** Avoid use; cease MAOIs at least 14 days before starting prochlorperazine [Australian].
- **Antacids:** Gastrointestinal agents (Mg, Al, Ca salts) may reduce phenothiazine absorption [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Not recommended unless benefits outweigh risks. Exposure in the third trimester carries a risk of neonatal extrapyramidal symptoms and withdrawal (e.g., respiratory distress, feeding difficulties, agitation). Neuroleptics may prolong labour and should be withheld until the cervix is dilated 3–4 cm [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. Phenothiazines may be excreted in breast milk; specific data for prochlorperazine are absent, but risks to the newborn cannot be excluded [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Data not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Higher susceptibility to neuromuscular reactions (dystonias), especially during dehydration or febrile illness. Use under close supervision at low doses [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Higher risk of hypotension, sedation, and drug-induced Parkinsonism. Lower initial doses and gradual titration are required. Increased mortality risk in elderly patients with dementia-related psychosis [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Extensively metabolised by the liver; dosage reduction may be necessary. Use with caution in existing liver disease [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use should generally be avoided; limited data available [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - Strong antidopaminergic activity (D2 receptor antagonism).
    - Adrenoreceptor antagonism (alpha-adrenergic blockade).
    - Blocks noradrenaline reuptake.
    - Weak anticholinergic, antihistamine, and serotonin-antagonist properties [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Well absorbed from the GI tract (animal data) [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Widely distributed to brain, fat, and organs; stored in reticuloendothelial tissues [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic; undergoes enterohepatic circulation [Australian].
    - **Excretion:** Primarily via faeces. Approximately 0.1% excreted in urine in the first 24 hours. Half-life is approximately 24 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Not specified in available sources.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Stability:** Protect from light; tablets may discolour upon exposure [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Prescription Only Medicine (**Schedule 4**) [Australian].
