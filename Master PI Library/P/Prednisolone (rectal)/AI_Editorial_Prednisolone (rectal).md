# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (US FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a concise reference format. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to their local regulatory guidelines for specific legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule for fatty acid transport).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Alimentary tract and metabolism).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Clear, colorless sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
- **Acute Management:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours (and never less frequently than every 6 hours).
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered.
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma free carnitine concentrations (target range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
- **Titration:** Adjustments may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels below 40–50 µmol/L. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and do not re-administer without careful risk-benefit assessment and close monitoring [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** Safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral use in ESRD patients may lead to accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (ESRD Clinical Trials, ≥5%)
| Body System | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%), Nausea (8%), Abdominal pain (9%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%) |
| **Neurological** | Headache (22%), Dizziness (13%), Paresthesia (5%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (23%), Cough increase (9%), Dyspnea (7%) |
| **Injection Site** | Reaction (33%) |
| **General** | Chest pain (12%), Flu syndrome (25%), Infection (15%), Asthenia (9%) |

### Postmarketing Experience
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (regardless of prior history).
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Body odor, nausea, and gastritis (incidence difficult to estimate due to underlying pathology) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Clinical Management:** Monitor INR levels at the initiation of levocarnitine treatment or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not formally categorised by letter, but reproductive data available]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after exogenous administration. Weigh the benefits of maternal supplementation against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant. Discontinuation of nursing or treatment may be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg); see Section 3 for specific instructions [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. *Intravenous* levocarnitine is indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis; monitor trough levels for dose adjustments [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, carnitine deficiency may be caused by reduced meat intake, reduced renal synthesis, and dialytic losses. IV administration increases plasma carnitine levels [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and [3H]-γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following a 20 mg/kg IV bolus, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Total body clearance is approximately 4.00 L/h [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis (trough) levels from ~20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses over 24 weeks showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations.
- **Clinical Outcomes Note:** While IV levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the effect on specific signs, symptoms, or clinical outcomes in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature (25°C) when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
