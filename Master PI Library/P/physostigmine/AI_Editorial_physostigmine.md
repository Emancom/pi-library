# Editorial Product Information: Physostigmine Salicylate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on regulatory information provided by the **United States (FDA)** and the **German (BfArM)** standards (via the temporarily imported Anticholium® product information).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating information regarding the US-marketed physostigmine salicylate and the German-origin Anticholium® product.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to local regulatory labels for legal compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Physostigmine salicylate (a tertiary amine anticholinesterase).
- **Brand Names:** Anticholium® [German; US-Imported], Physostigmine Salicylate Injection [US-generic].
- **ATC Code:** N07AA01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Injection/Infusion:** 0.4 mg/mL (2 mg in 5 mL ampules) [German].
    - **Injection:** 1 mg/mL (2 mg in 2 mL ampules) [US].
- **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) [German; US], Intramuscular (IM) [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Provepharm Inc. (US Distributor), Dr. Franz Köhler Chemie GmbH (German Manufacturer).

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Central Anticholinergic Syndrome (CAS):** To reverse toxic effects upon the central nervous system caused by drugs or plant alkaloids capable of producing anticholinergic syndrome.
- **Antidote for Specific Intoxications:** Indicated as an antagonist in cases of overdose with [German]:
    - Alcohol
    - Tropane alkaloids (e.g., Atropine, Scopolamine, Hyoscyamine found in *Atropa belladonna*, *Datura*, *Brugmansia*)
    - *Amanita pantherina* and *Amanita muscaria* mushrooms
    - Tricyclic antidepressants (e.g., Amitriptyline, Imipramine, Clomipramine)
    - Antiemetics/Antihistamines (e.g., Phenothiazines, Diphenhydramine, Promethazine)
    - Neuroleptic drugs (especially butyrophenones)
    - Benzodiazepines
    - Baclofen, GHB (4-hydroxybutyric acid), Ketamine
    - Parkinson’s disease medications (e.g., Amantadine)
    - Antispasmodics (e.g., Tolterodine, Oxybutynin)
- **Postoperative Care [German]:** 
    - Delayed postoperative awakening
    - Postoperative shivering

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing
- **Postoperative Awakening Disorders:** 0.04 mg/kg body weight (approximately 1 mg/min) via slow IV injection. Maximum single dose: 2 mg. If the effect is insufficient, subsequent injections may be given 5–20 minutes after the initial dose evaluation [German].
- **Intoxication/Antidote:** 
    - **Standard Dose:** 0.5 mg to 2.0 mg IV or IM [US].
    - **Weight-based Dose:** 0.04 mg/kg (2 mg) via slow IV injection, followed by 1–4 mg every 20 minutes as required [German]. 
    - **Continuous Infusion:** May be used if symptoms recur [German].

### Pediatric Dosing
- **Intoxication:** 
    - **General:** 0.02 mg/kg IM or by slow IV injection (max rate 0.5 mg/min). Dose may be repeated at 5–10 minute intervals if toxic effects persist and no cholinergic signs are present, up to a maximum of 2 mg [US].
    - **Infants:** Start with 0.5 mg IV; repeat every 5 minutes until symptoms resolve or a maximum total dose of 2 mg is reached, provided no cholinergic symptoms occur [German].

### Administration Instructions
- **IV Rate:** Must be administered slowly, not exceeding **1 mg per minute** in adults or **0.5 mg per minute** in children [US; German]. Rapid administration may induce bradycardia, hypersalivation, respiratory distress, or convulsions.
- **Infusion:** Anticholium® may be administered as a brief infusion in 50 mL physiological saline over 10–15 minutes [German].
- **Clinical Monitoring:** Dosing adequacy is determined by the recovery of mental status and responsiveness (e.g., orientation to name, date, and place).

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to physostigmine salicylate.
- **Mechanical Obstructions:** Mechanical obstruction of the intestine (constipation/obstipation) or the urogenital tract (urinary retention/ischuria).
- **Respiratory/Cardiac Conditions:** Bronchial asthma, coronary heart disease.
- **Vascular/Metabolic:** Gangrene (ulcers with tissue destruction), diabetes mellitus [US], cardiovascular disease [US].
- **Neuromuscular:** Myotonic dystrophy (hereditary muscle atrophy), closed traumatic brain injuries [German].
- **Drug-Specific Contraindications:** 
    - Concurrent use of choline esters or depolarizing neuromuscular blocking agents (e.g., Suxamethonium, Decamethonium, Succinylcholine).
    - Intoxication due to "irreversibly acting" cholinesterase inhibitors (e.g., phosphoric acid esters) or barbiturates [German].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Sulfite Sensitivity:** The US-marketed product contains **sodium bisulfite**, which may cause anaphylactic or life-threatening asthmatic episodes in susceptible individuals [US].
- **Cardiovascular Risk:** Acute cardiac arrest may occur during treatment for tricyclic antidepressant overdose; continuous ECG monitoring is mandatory in these cases [German].
- **Cholinergic Crisis:** Overdosage may lead to a cholinergic crisis (excessive salivation, emesis, urination, defecation). Treatment must be terminated immediately if these symptoms occur.
- **Atropine Availability:** Atropine sulfate injection should always be available as a direct antagonist and antidote to physostigmine [US; German].
- **Special Clinical Precautions:** Use with caution in patients with bradycardia, AV conduction disturbances, Parkinson's disease, or ulcerative colitis [German].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally dose-related and secondary to cholinergic stimulation.

| System Organ Class | Frequency: Unknown [German] |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Immune System** | Hypersensitivity reactions (may be life-threatening) |
| **Endocrine** | Hypersalivation |
| **Cardiac** | Bradycardia, tachycardia, sinoatrial block, cardiac arrest |
| **Vascular** | Hypotension |
| **Respiratory** | Bronchospasm, respiratory difficulties |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea, vomiting |
| **Skin/Subcutaneous** | Sweating |
| **Nervous System** | Convulsions (if administered too rapidly) |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Antagonism:** 
    - **Atropine:** Antagonises the action of physostigmine.
    - **Neuromuscular Blockers:** Physostigmine antagonizes the action of non-depolarizing muscle relaxants but **potentiates** and is contraindicated with depolarizing agents like suxamethonium [German].
- **Pharmacodynamic Synergism:** 
    - **Cholinesterase Inhibitors:** Caution is required when using other cholinesterase inhibitors (e.g., dementia medications) due to potentiation of effects [German].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Classification:** Not explicitly stated in available source, but requires benefit-risk assessment.
    - **German Assessment:** No experience in pregnant women. Physostigmine passes the placenta. Animal studies are insufficient regarding reproductive toxicity. Use only if deemed absolutely necessary by the attending physician.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been established. It is unknown if physostigmine is excreted in human milk. Use only if benefits clearly outweigh risks [German].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for use in children and infants; see Section 3 for specific weight-based dosing and maximum rates.
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources, though caution is advised due to higher prevalence of cardiovascular contraindications.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Physostigmine is a reversible cholinesterase inhibitor. It increases the concentration of acetylcholine at cholinergic transmission sites by inhibiting the enzyme acetylcholinesterase. As a tertiary amine, it crosses the blood-brain barrier, allowing for the reversal of both central and peripheral anticholinergic effects.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Distribution:** Crosses the blood-brain barrier and the placenta [German].
    - **Elimination:** Rapidly metabolised.

## 11. Clinical Trials

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **US Product:** Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F).
- **German/Imported Product:** Store below 25°C. Protect from light by keeping ampules in the outer packaging. 
- **Stability:** Once prepared as an infusion, it must be used immediately [German]. Shelf life of Anticholium® is 3 years in an undamaged container.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only. Note: The US-marketed product is currently categorized by the FDA as an unapproved drug. The Anticholium® presentation is temporarily imported under a specific license to address supply shortages [US].
- **Germany:** Prescription only.
