# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, legally approved PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the US PI was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance haemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Severe Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Administration should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and no less frequently than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been used [US].
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to establish a baseline plasma carnitine level. Ideal plasma free carnitine concentrations should be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Titration:** Adjustments may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels (normal range 40–50 µmol/L). Doses may be decreased to 5 mg/kg if required [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal oedema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued and appropriate medical management initiated [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In patients with known seizure activity, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may result in the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Monitoring:** Clinical status, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations should be monitored regularly.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events reported in clinical trials for ESRD patients (frequency ≥5%) include:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), flu syndrome (25%), headache (22%), chest pain (12%), fever (7%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhoea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), abdominal pain (9%).
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), paresthesia (5%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcaemia (9%), hyperkalaemia (6%), hypervolemia (5%).

**Serious/Postmarketing Effects:**
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels upon initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine in patients on anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category C]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits using up to 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. However, there are no adequate clinical trials in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly required [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies in lactating women have not been conducted. Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. In dairy cows, carnitine concentrations in milk increase following administration. The benefits of maternal supplementation must be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing in metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg); see Section 3 [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. Note the specific warning against high-dose oral formulations in this population due to metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This provides substrate for beta-oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also assists in clearing accumulated acyl-CoA compounds by forming acylcarnitines, which are then excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. 
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in faeces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x/week post-dialysis), trough levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. 
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg in dialysis patients showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks.
- **Clinical Outcomes:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the impact on specific signs, symptoms, or clinical outcomes in this population has not been definitively determined in the provided source [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing.
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL. Stable for 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
- **Vial Usage:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the formulation lacks preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only. Poison Schedule: Not applicable.
