# Editorial Product Information: Paracetamol and Codeine Phosphate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical data into a concise, scannable format.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing within a particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Paracetamol (analgesic/antipyretic) and Codeine Phosphate hemihydrate (opioid analgesic).
- **Chemical Class:** Paracetamol is an aniline derivative; Codeine is an alkaloid of opium (phenanthrene derivative).
- **Brand Names:** AMCAL STRONG PAIN RELIEF EXTRA [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources (N02AJ06).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Uncoated, capsule-shaped tablets containing Paracetamol 500 mg and Codeine Phosphate hemihydrate 15 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sigma Company Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Moderate Pain:** Indicated for the temporary relief of acute moderate pain in patients older than 12 years of age [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Adults and Children (>12 years):** Two tablets administered up to four times daily as required. 
- **Maximum Dose:** 8 tablets (4000 mg paracetamol / 120 mg codeine phosphate) within a 24-hour period [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets feature a break line for flexible dosing if required.
- **Titration:** Treatment should be initiated at the lowest effective dose for the shortest possible duration.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to paracetamol, codeine, or any excipients (e.g., lactose monohydrate, povidone) [Australian].
- **Respiratory Conditions:** Severe or acute respiratory disease, and respiratory depression [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Chronic constipation; diarrhoea caused by pseudomembranous colitis or poisoning (until the toxin is eliminated) [Australian].
- **Metabolic Status:** Patients known to be CYP2D6 ultra-rapid metabolisers [Australian].
- **Paediatric Population:** 
    - Children under 12 years of age [Australian].
    - Children aged 12–18 years following tonsillectomy or adenoidectomy for obstructive sleep apnoea, or those with compromised respiratory function [Australian].
- **Lactation:** Use is contraindicated in breastfeeding women [Australian].
- **Other:** Active alcoholism; during labour when delivery of a premature infant is anticipated [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [Australian]:** 
    - **Limitations of Use:** Should only be used when non-opioid analgesics have failed or are not tolerated.
    - **Hazardous and Harmful Use:** Risks include addiction, abuse, and misuse, potentially leading to fatal overdose.
    - **Respiratory Depression:** Serious or fatal respiratory depression may occur; monitoring is required, especially during initiation.
    - **Concomitant CNS Depressants:** Use with benzodiazepines, alcohol, or other CNS depressants may cause profound sedation, coma, or death.
- **CYP2D6 Metabolism:** Variations in the CYP2D6 enzyme can lead to toxic levels of morphine (in ultra-rapid metabolisers) or inadequate analgesia (in poor metabolisers). Prevalence of ultra-rapid metabolism varies by ethnicity (highest in North African and Ethiopian populations) [Australian].
- **Hyperalgesia:** Opioid-induced hyperalgesia may occur, particularly at high doses; this requires dose reduction or opioid rotation rather than dose escalation [Australian].
- **Chronic Non-Cancer Pain (CNCP):** Long-term use for CNCP is generally not recommended and should only be initiated as a trial within a multidisciplinary framework [Australian].
- **Other Precautions:** Use with caution in patients with hepatic or renal dysfunction, head injury (raised intracranial pressure), hypotension, prostatic hypertrophy, or hypothyroidism [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Paracetamol:** Adverse reactions are rare. Reported effects include nausea, dyspepsia, and hematological or allergic reactions [Australian].
- **Codeine:** 
    - **Common:** Nausea, vomiting, constipation, dizziness, and drowsiness [Australian].
    - **Other:** Euphoria, dysphoria, respiratory depression, cough suppression, and histamine release (hypotension, flushing, tachycardia) [Australian].
- **Serious Effects:** Dose-dependent fatal hepatic necrosis (overdose), serious skin rashes (rare), and physiological dependence with prolonged use [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CNS Depressants:** Alcohol, benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, and cannabis increase the risk of respiratory depression and coma [Australian].
- **Anticholinergics:** Increased risk of severe constipation and urinary retention [Australian].
- **MAOIs:** Concomitant use or use within 14 days of MAOIs may enhance respiratory depression [Australian].
- **Warfarin:** Prolonged use of paracetamol may require a reduction in anticoagulant dosage [Australian].
- **Metabolism/Absorption Modifiers:** 
    - Metoclopramide increases paracetamol absorption; cholestyramine and propantheline decrease it [Australian].
    - CYP2D6 inhibitors (e.g., quinidine) may reduce codeine efficacy [Australian].
- **Antihypertensives:** Potential for potentiated hypotensive effects [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category A [Australian]**. Taken by a large number of pregnant women without proven increases in malformations. However, high doses near term may cause neonatal respiratory depression or withdrawal [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** **Contraindicated [Australian]**. Codeine is excreted into breast milk. Mothers who are CYP2D6 ultra-rapid metabolisers can achieve high morphine levels in milk, leading to life-threatening toxicity or death in the infant [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric:** Contraindicated in children under 12. Significant risks in adolescents (12–18) with respiratory issues [Australian].
- **Elderly:** Increased susceptibility to respiratory depression and age-related renal impairment [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution; codeine and paracetamol metabolites are cleared renally, and paracetamol carries hepatotoxicity risks [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Paracetamol:** Analgesic mechanism may involve blocking impulse generation at bradykinin-sensitive receptors; antipyretic effect via prostaglandin synthetase inhibition in the hypothalamus [Australian].
    - **Codeine:** Centrally acting opioid that alters the perception of and affective response to pain [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; paracetamol peaks at 30–120 mins [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Paracetamol via hepatic conjugation; Codeine via CYP2D6 to morphine (active) and norcodeine [Australian].
    - **Excretion:** Primarily renal; elimination half-life for paracetamol is 1–4 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Not specified in available sources. The document notes that combination therapy is superior to single-agent administration but does not detail specific pivotal trial data [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Packaging:** PVC/PVDC/Al blister packs [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian]**.
