# Editorial Product Information: Pimecrolimus

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (TGA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of pimecrolimus clinical data and regulatory requirements for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. The information provided is based solely on the source documents available at the time of writing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pimecrolimus (an ascomycin macrolactam derivative).
- **Chemical Class:** Topical Calcineurin Inhibitor (TCI).
- **Brand Names:** **Elidel** [Australian].
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 1% w/w cream (1 gram of cream contains 10 mg of pimecrolimus).
- **Route of Administration:** Topical.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Viatris Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Atopic Dermatitis (Eczema):** Indicated for patients aged 3 months and older for:
    - Short-term treatment of signs and symptoms.
    - Intermittent long-term treatment of emerging and resolving lesions where the use of topical corticosteroids (TCS) is not yet warranted, is no longer required, or is clinically inadvisable.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** Apply a thin layer to affected skin twice daily; rub in gently and completely.
- **Application Sites:** Suitable for all skin areas including the face, head, neck, and intertriginous areas.
- **Fingertip Dosing Unit:** One fingertip unit (from the distal skin crease to the tip of the adult index finger) is sufficient to cover an area equivalent to two adult hand surfaces.
- **Treatment Strategy:** 
    - Initiate at the first sign of symptoms (pruritus, erythema, or infiltration) to prevent progression to flares.
    - Continue until signs/symptoms resolve, then discontinue.
    - If no improvement occurs after 6 weeks, or if the condition worsens, the diagnosis and treatment plan should be re-evaluated.
- **Intermittent Use:** Long-term continuous use is not recommended.
- **Emollients:** May be applied after the pimecrolimus cream.
- **Hygiene:** Carers should wash their hands immediately after applying the cream to children.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to pimecrolimus, other macrolactams, or any excipient in the formulation (e.g., benzyl alcohol, cetyl alcohol, propylene glycol, stearyl alcohol).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Malignancy Risk:** While a causal relationship is not established, rare cases of malignancy (skin cancers and lymphoma) have been reported in patients using TCIs. Prolonged systemic exposure to calcineurin inhibitors in transplant patients is a known risk factor. Use should be limited to affected areas.
- **Skin Conditions to Avoid:** Do not apply to pre-malignant or malignant skin lesions (e.g., actinic keratoses). Not recommended for use in patients with Netherton's syndrome or generalized erythroderma due to the potential for increased systemic absorption.
- **Immunocompromised Patients:** Safety and efficacy have not been studied; use is not recommended in these populations.
- **Infections:** 
    - **Viral:** Do not apply to areas of active cutaneous viral infection (e.g., herpes simplex, chickenpox).
    - **Bacterial/Fungal:** Treat clinical infections with appropriate antimicrobials. If the infection does not resolve, pimecrolimus should be withheld until the infection is controlled.
- **Lymphadenopathy:** If lymphadenopathy occurs, investigate the etiology. In the absence of a clear cause or in cases of infectious mononucleosis, pimecrolimus should be discontinued.
- **Sun Exposure:** Minimize or avoid exposure to natural or artificial sunlight. The cream base may enhance UV-induced skin tumor development (based on animal data). Advise patients on sun protection (clothing, hats, sunscreens).
- **Occlusion:** Use under occlusive dressings is not recommended as it has not been studied.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most frequently reported adverse events are application site reactions, occurring in approximately 19% of patients.

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Application site burning |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Skin infections (folliculitis), application site reactions (irritation, pruritus, erythema) |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Furuncle, impetigo, herpes simplex, herpes zoster, eczema herpeticum, molluscum contagiosum, skin papilloma, condition aggravation, application site disorders (rash, paraesthesia, desquamation, dryness, pain, oedema) |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Alcohol intolerance (flushing, rash, burning shortly after alcohol intake), allergic reactions (urticaria, angioedema), skin discoloration |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Anaphylactic reactions |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Systemic Interactions:** Unlikely due to minimal pimecrolimus absorption following topical application.
- **Immunosuppressives:** No clinical data exists regarding concomitant use with systemic immunosuppressive agents (e.g., ciclosporin, azathioprine).
- **Vaccinations:** 
    - In a 5-year study of infants, pimecrolimus did not interfere with immune response maturation or vaccination efficacy.
    - Avoid applying the cream to vaccination sites while local reactions persist.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. There are no adequate data in pregnant women. Animal studies showed embryofoetal toxicity at high oral doses. **Pimecrolimus should not be used during pregnancy.**
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if pimecrolimus is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution and do not apply the cream to the breasts to avoid direct infant ingestion.
- **Fertility:** No human data available. Animal studies at high systemic exposures showed reduced fertility and changes in reproductive organs.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - **$\geq$ 3 months:** Approved for use.
    - **3 to 23 months:** Application should be limited to the smallest practicable surface area, and treatment for each episode should generally not exceed 3 weeks [Australian].
    - **< 3 months:** Safety and efficacy have not been evaluated.
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include sufficient numbers of patients aged 65 and over to determine if they respond differently; however, atopic dermatitis is rare in this population.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustments are required based on available data.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Pimecrolimus binds with high affinity to macrophilin-12 and inhibits the calcium-dependent phosphatase, calcineurin. This inhibits T-cell proliferation and prevents the release of inflammatory cytokines (IL-2, IFN-$\gamma$, IL-4, IL-10, TNF-$\alpha$).
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Exhibits high anti-inflammatory activity with low potential for affecting systemic immune responses compared to systemic calcineurin inhibitors. Unlike corticosteroids, pimecrolimus does not cause skin atrophy or deplete Langerhans cells.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Systemic exposure is very low in both adults and children, regardless of the surface area treated or duration of therapy.
    - **Distribution:** 99.5% plasma protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** No metabolism observed in human skin. Systemic metabolism (following oral administration) involves O-demethylation and oxygenation.
    - **Elimination:** Following oral administration, excreted primarily in faeces (78.4%).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Short-term Efficacy:** In 6-week trials, pimecrolimus showed significantly higher rates of "Clear" or "Almost Clear" IGA scores compared to vehicle (34.8% vs 18.4% in children/adolescents; 54.5% vs 23.8% in infants). Significant pruritus relief was noted within the first week.
- **Long-term Management:** Two 12-month studies in 964 paediatric patients demonstrated that pimecrolimus significantly reduced the incidence of flares and the requirement for topical corticosteroids compared to vehicle.
- **Skin Atrophy:** Studies in healthy volunteers showed that pimecrolimus 1% cream did not induce a reduction in skin thickness, whereas potent corticosteroids (betamethasone and triamcinolone) caused significant atrophy.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C. Do not freeze.
- **Shelf Life:** Once the tube is opened, the contents must be used within 12 weeks.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
