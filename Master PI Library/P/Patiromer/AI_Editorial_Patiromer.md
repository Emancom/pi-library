# Editorial Product Information: Patiromer

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of **patiromer** for healthcare professionals.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the Australian PI was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Patiromer sorbitex calcium (a non-absorbed, cation exchange polymer).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Veltassa** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Powder for oral suspension in single-use sachets containing 8.4 g, 16.8 g, or 25.2 g of patiromer (as sorbitex calcium) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Seqirus Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hyperkalaemia:** Treatment of hyperkalaemia in adults [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:**
  - **Starting Dose:** 8.4 g once daily [Australian].
  - **Titration:** Dose may be adjusted based on serum potassium levels at intervals of one week or longer. Increments or decrements should be in 8.4 g steps [Australian].
  - **Maximum Dose:** 25.2 g once daily [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
  - **Preparation:** Mix the powder with approximately 80 mL of water. Stir half the water with the powder, then add the remainder and stir thoroughly. The powder will not dissolve; the suspension will appear cloudy [Australian].
  - **Alternative Vehicles:** May be mixed with apple juice, cranberry juice, pineapple juice, orange juice, grape juice, pear juice, apricot/peach nectar, yoghurt, milk, thickener, apple sauce, or vanilla/chocolate pudding [Australian].
  - **Timing:** Take with or without food, preferably at the same time each day. 
  - **Drug Separation:** Administer **patiromer** at least 3 hours before or 3 hours after other oral medications, unless specific data confirms no interaction [Australian].
  - **Restrictions:** Do not take in dry form. Do not heat (e.g., microwave) or add to heated foods/liquids [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Take the missed dose as soon as possible on the same day. Do not double the dose to catch up [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to patiromer sorbitex calcium or any excipients (including xanthan gum) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Emergency Use:** **Patiromer** should **not** be used as a replacement for emergency treatment of life-threatening hyperkalaemia. Due to its onset of action (4–7 hours), it is not suitable for the immediate reversal of ECG changes [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal Disorders:** Use with caution in patients with a history of bowel obstruction, major GI surgery, or severe swallowing disorders, as these populations were excluded from clinical trials. Ischaemia, necrosis, and perforation have been reported with other potassium binders [Australian].
- **Serum Electrolyte Monitoring:**
    - **Potassium:** Monitor when clinically indicated and after dose titration or changes to RAAS inhibitors/diuretics [Australian].
    - **Magnesium:** Monitor for at least one month after initiation; supplement if low levels occur [Australian].
    - **Calcium:** Monitor in patients at risk of hypercalcaemia, as calcium is released from the polymer complex during the exchange process [Australian].
- **Discontinuation:** Serum potassium may rise within 2 days of stopping treatment, especially if RAAS inhibitors are continued [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (>1% to <10%):**
    - Hypomagnesaemia (serum Mg <1.4 mg/dL reported in 7.1% of trial participants) [Australian].
    - Constipation.
    - Diarrhoea.
    - Abdominal pain/discomfort.
    - Nausea.
    - Flatulence [Australian].
- **Uncommon (>0.1% to <1%):**
    - Vomiting [Australian].
- **Other Laboratory Abnormalities:** Hypokalaemia (serum K <3.5 mEq/L) occurred in approximately 4.7% of patients [Australian].
- **Post-marketing Experience:** Hypersensitivity reactions (rash, urticaria, swelling of the lips/oral cavity) have been reported; frequency is not known [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
**Patiromer** may bind to co-administered oral medications, potentially reducing their absorption. 

### Recommended 3-Hour Separation Required:
- **Antibiotics:** Ciprofloxacin.
- **Thyroid Preparations:** Levothyroxine.
- **Diabetes Agents:** Metformin.
- **Antihypertensives:** Telmisartan, bisoprolol, carvedilol, nebivolol.
- **Others:** Mycophenolate mofetil, quinidine, thiamine [Australian].

### No Separation Required (No significant interaction observed):
- **RAAS Blockers:** ACE inhibitors (e.g., ramipril, lisinopril), ARBs (e.g., losartan, valsartan).
- **Diuretics:** Furosemide, bumetanide, torasemide, spironolactone, eplerenone.
- **Cardiovascular:** Amlodipine, verapamil, metoprolol, digoxin, warfarin, apixaban, clopidogrel, aspirin, atorvastatin.
- **Others:** Lithium, tacrolimus, metformin, glipizide, allopurinol, sevelamer [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B1.**
    - No data in pregnant women. Since **patiromer** is not systemically absorbed, fetal risk is not anticipated [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** No data available. However, systemic exposure to the infant is expected to be negligible as the drug remains within the maternal GI tract [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No human data; no impairment observed in animal studies [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients under 18 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No special dose adjustments are required for patients ≥65 years [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No specific dose adjustment required. It has been used in patients with CKD. Limited data (n=6) available for patients on haemodialysis; no data for peritoneal dialysis [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources (drug is not systemically absorbed).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Patiromer** is a non-absorbed, calcium-sorbitol-based cation exchange polymer. In the gastrointestinal tract (primarily the colon), it binds potassium ions in exchange for calcium ions. This increases faecal potassium excretion and lowers serum potassium levels [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Onset of action occurs within 4–7 hours. Dose-dependent increases in faecal potassium and decreases in urinary potassium have been demonstrated [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption/Distribution:** Not systemically absorbed.
    - **Elimination:** Excreted entirely in the faeces [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Study (Two-part randomised withdrawal):** 243 hyperkalaemic patients with CKD on RAAS inhibitors were treated.
    - **Part A:** Significant mean reduction in serum potassium (-1.01 mEq/L) was achieved at 4 weeks. 
    - **Part B:** In patients randomised to placebo, potassium rose by 0.72 mEq/L compared to no change in the **patiromer** group. 
    - **Long-term Safety:** Efficacy and safety were maintained over 52 weeks in open-label studies [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Standard Storage:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze [Australian].
- **Excursion Limits:** May be stored at room temperature (below 25°C) for up to 6 months. Protect from heat [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to expiry date on individual sachets [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
