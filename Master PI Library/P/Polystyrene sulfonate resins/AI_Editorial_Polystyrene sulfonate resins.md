# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA) via Baxter Healthcare Corporation.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of the Product Information for Levocarnitine Injection. It is intended as a clinical reference for healthcare professionals and provides a consolidated overview of the drug's regulatory and clinical profile.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for full prescribing details and legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP)
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Name:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Amino acids and derivatives).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **Carnitine Deficiency in End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg, administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion. 
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. 
    - Frequency: Every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
    - Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been used [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - Administration: Slow bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - Dose adjustments: Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

### Monitoring
- **Plasma Carnitine Levels:** It is recommended to obtain levels prior to treatment. Weekly and monthly monitoring should follow, including blood chemistries and vital signs. 
- **Target Range:** Free carnitine plasma concentrations should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known:** There are currently no known contraindications for levocarnitine injection [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, treatment must be discontinued and appropriate medical management initiated [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine may increase the frequency or severity of seizures in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a prior history of seizure activity [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** While not specific to the injection, chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in the urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events are often confounded by the underlying pathology (ESRD or metabolic errors). 

### Very Common (>10%) and Common (1–10%)
Based on clinical trials in ESRD patients, the following were reported at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) [US]:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), headache (22%), flu syndrome (25%), infection (15%), abdominal pain (9%), chest pain (12%), fever (7%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), palpitations (5%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), dyspepsia (5%), anorexia (5%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%), hypervolemia (5%), weight decrease (5%).
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), paresthesia (5%), depression (5%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%).
- **Other:** Body odor (less frequent), gastritis.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Anaphylaxis/Hypersensitivity:** [US]
- **Seizures:** Increased frequency/severity in known cases or new-onset [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended that INR levels be monitored upon initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specifically assigned in current text, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm.
    - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake to the infant must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal reproductive studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing guidelines for metabolic disorders apply to paediatric patients. Specific age ranges for ESRD approvals are not delineated, but metabolic indications cover the acute/chronic treatment of inborn errors [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources regarding dose adjustments [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - Intravenous levocarnitine is indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. 
    - The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following a 20 mg/kg IV bolus, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; the mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV doses three times weekly after dialysis significantly increased plasma levocarnitine. Trough levels increased from a baseline of 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed a dose-proportional increase in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL at room temperature [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources [Australian].
