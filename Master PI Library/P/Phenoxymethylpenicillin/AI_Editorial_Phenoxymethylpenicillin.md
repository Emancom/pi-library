# Editorial Product Information: Phenoxymethylpenicillin

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information from the Australian (TGA) region.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical and regulatory data.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Phenoxymethylpenicillin (as potassium), also known as Penicillin V.
- **Chemical Class:** Beta-lactam antibiotic; acid-stable phenoxymethyl analog of penicillin G.
- **Brand Names:** Aspecillin VK [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 250 mg and 500 mg [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Viatris Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Phenoxymethylpenicillin is indicated for the following [Australian-approved]:
- **Infections:** Treatment of mild to moderately severe infections caused by penicillin-sensitive organisms, specifically:
    - Staphylococci (non-penicillinase producing)
    - Pneumococci
    - Gonococci
    - Haemolytic streptococci
- **Prophylaxis:**
    - Prevention of recurrence following rheumatic fever and/or Sydenham’s chorea.
    - Prevention of bacterial endocarditis in patients with rheumatic fever or congenital heart disease undergoing dental or upper respiratory surgery/instrumentation.
- **Note:** Oral penicillin is not recommended for adjunctive prophylaxis in genitourinary surgery, lower intestinal tract surgery, sigmoidoscopy, or childbirth [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing
- **General Infections:** 250 mg to 500 mg every four to six hours.
- **Rheumatic Fever Prophylaxis (Recurrence):** 250 mg twice daily, administered continuously.

### Paediatric Dosing [Australian]
Dosing is ideally calculated by body weight at **15 mg/kg to 50 mg/kg** daily, divided into three to six doses. If weight-based dosing is not used, the following schedule applies:
- **Up to 1 year:** 60 mg every six hours.
- **1 to 5 years:** 120 mg every six hours.
- **6 to 12 years:** 120 mg to 270 mg every six hours.

### Administration Instructions
- **Timing:** Preferably administered one hour before food, as food may delay absorption.
- **Duration:** For streptococcal infections, therapy must continue for a minimum of 10 days to ensure eradication.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to penicillins and/or cephalosporins [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity:** Serious and occasionally fatal anaphylactoid reactions have occurred. Risk is higher in patients with a history of penicillin or cephalosporin sensitivity or multiple allergens. Immediate emergency treatment with adrenaline and airway management is required for severe reactions.
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCAR):** SJS, TEN, DRESS, and AGEP have been reported. Discontinue immediately if SCAR is suspected.
- **Clostridioides difficile-Associated Disease:** Pseudomembranous colitis ranging from mild to life-threatening has been reported. Avoid drugs that delay peristalsis (e.g., opiates) if colitis is suspected.
- **Infection Limitation:** Not recommended for chronic, severe, or deep-seated infections as therapeutic tissue levels may not be achieved.
- **Bleeding Disorders:** Some penicillins may cause platelet dysfunction and haemorrhage.
- **Prolonged Use:** May result in overgrowth of non-susceptible organisms, including oral candidiasis.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Reactions
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, epigastric distress, diarrhoea, pruritus ani, and "black hairy tongue."
- **Allergic/Skin:** Urticaria, maculopapular to exfoliative dermatitis, and serum sickness-like reactions.
- **General:** Fever and eosinophilia may be the sole presenting symptoms of a reaction.

### Uncommon and Serious Reactions
- **Haematologic:** Haemolytic anaemia, leukopenia, and thrombocytopenia (usually associated with high doses).
- **Neurologic/Renal:** Neuropathy and nephropathy (usually associated with high parenteral doses).
- **Severe Allergic:** Laryngeal oedema and anaphylaxis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Bacteriostatic Antibiotics:** May antagonise the bactericidal effect of penicillin.
- **Probenecid:** Reduces renal tubular excretion, increasing serum concentrations of phenoxymethylpenicillin.
- **Antacids:** May reduce the absorption of the drug.
- **Oral Contraceptives (Estrogen-containing):** May decrease contraceptive effectiveness and increase the risk of breakthrough bleeding or unplanned pregnancy. Alternative contraception is recommended during therapy.
- **Methotrexate:** Penicillins decrease methotrexate clearance, increasing the risk of toxicity; close monitoring is required.
- **Aminoglycosides:** Substantial mutual inactivation occurs if mixed *in vitro*.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category A]. No evidence of adverse effects on the fetus has been observed in human experience; however, it should be used during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** The drug is excreted in breast milk at concentrations lower than plasma. Safety in newborns is not established; use is not recommended unless the benefits outweigh the potential risks.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies (mouse, rat, rabbit) showed no evidence of impaired fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Half-life is prolonged in premature infants and neonates (<3 months). TID dosing may be sufficient in this group [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No age-specific issues documented, but caution is advised due to the higher likelihood of age-related renal impairment.
- **Renal Impairment:** Half-life is greatly extended. Dose reduction or increased dosing interval is recommended in severe impairment. Consider the potassium load (28 mg per 250 mg tablet) in patients with renal failure.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Half-life is greatly extended. Leukopenia/neutropenia is more likely in patients with severe hepatic impairment on prolonged therapy.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal; inhibits the biosynthesis of cell wall mucopeptides during active multiplication.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Active against penicillin-sensitive Gram-positive cocci (Streptococci, non-penicillinase Staph), Gram-positive bacilli (*C. tetani, C. diphtheriae*), and some Gram-negative bacteria (*N. meningitidis*). It is **not** active against penicillinase-producing bacteria.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Acid-stable; up to 60% absorbed; peak serum levels within 30 minutes.
    - **Distribution:** Highest levels in kidneys; 80% protein-bound.
    - **Metabolism:** ~56% of a dose is metabolised to inactive metabolites.
    - **Elimination:** Plasma half-life is ~30 minutes in healthy adults. 23% to 36% is excreted unchanged in urine.
