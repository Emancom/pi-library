# Editorial Product Information: Penicillamine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)** regulatory authority. 
- **Note:** At the time of this editorial, only the Australian jurisdiction's documentation was provided for review. Consequently, this document reflects Australian clinical guidelines and regulatory status.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. It serves as a clinical reference and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Penicillamine (D-penicillamine).
- **Chemical Class:** Chelating agent; D-isomer of 3-mercaptovaline (a degradation product of penicillin).
- **Brand Names:** D-PENAMINE [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** M01CC01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - 125 mg film-coated tablets (round, white to off-white, embossed 'DS'/'125') [Australian].
    - 250 mg film-coated tablets (round, white to off-white, embossed 'DM'/'250') [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Alphapharm Pty Ltd (trading as Viatris) [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis:** Treatment of severe, active rheumatoid arthritis [Australian].
- **Wilson’s Disease:** Used as a chelating agent to promote the excretion of copper [Australian].
- **Cystinuria:** Treatment of cystinuria, particularly when high-fluid regimens are inadequate or as an adjunctive therapy [Australian].
- **Heavy Metal Poisoning:** Treatment of lead poisoning; also used to enhance the urinary excretion of gold, mercury, iron, antimony, and zinc [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration Instructions
- **Timing:** Must be taken on an empty stomach, either one hour before or two hours after meals.
- **Separation:** Administer at least one hour apart from any other medication, food, or milk [Australian].

### Rheumatoid Arthritis (Adults)
- **Initial Dose:** 250 mg daily for the first month.
- **Titration:** May increase by 250 mg at intervals of no less than one month.
- **Maintenance:** The dose should be kept at the lowest effective level. While many respond to 750 mg daily, the dose may be increased up to a maximum of 1500 mg daily.
- **Duration of Trial:** If no response is observed after six months of full maintenance dosing, therapy should be discontinued [Australian].

### Wilson’s Disease (Adults)
- **Standard Dose:** 1500 mg to 2000 mg daily.
- **Note:** Improvement may be slow despite immediate cupruresis. Clinical deterioration may occur initially [Australian].

### Lead Poisoning
- **Oral Dose:** 250 mg to 1000 mg daily in divided doses. 
- **Alternative:** Patients unable to swallow or those who are vomiting should receive parenteral E.D.T.A. [Australian].

### Cystinuria
- **Dissolution of Stones:** A single 500 mg dose at bedtime (following high fluid intake during the day) may be effective.
- **Maintenance:** 750 mg to 1000 mg daily in divided doses is generally adequate. Doses should not exceed 2000 mg daily [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to penicillamine or any excipients (including sulfites) [Australian].
- **Concomitant Therapy:** Must not be used in patients currently receiving gold therapy or antimalarial drugs [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Fatalities:** Penicillamine use has been associated with fatalities due to aplastic anaemia, agranulocytosis, thrombocytopenia, Goodpasture's Syndrome, and myasthenia gravis [Australian].
- **Haematological/Renal Monitoring:** Mandatory monitoring is required. Haemoglobin, WBC (total and differential), platelet count, and urinalysis must be performed:
    - Weekly for the first four weeks.
    - Every two weeks for the next five months.
    - Monthly thereafter for the duration of therapy [Australian].
- **Withdrawal Criteria:** Treatment must be withdrawn if:
    - WBC < 3000/mm³.
    - Neutrophils < 2000/mm³.
    - Platelets < 120,000/mm³.
    - A steady decline is observed over three successive tests (even within normal ranges) [Australian].
- **Proteinuria:** Withdraw treatment if urinary albumin increases progressively to exceed 2 g/day. Discontinue immediately if haematuria occurs [Australian].
- **Cross-Allergy:** Caution is advised in patients with penicillin hypersensitivity due to potential cross-reactivity [Australian].
- **Surgical Procedures:** Penicillamine interferes with collagen cross-linking. If possible, discontinue therapy at least six weeks prior to elective surgery (especially vascular surgery) [Australian].
- **Neurological Effects:** A neurological exam is recommended prior to therapy. If new abnormalities occur (excluding loss of taste), the drug should be withdrawn [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Allergic Reactions (Common):** Maculopapular or erythematous rash early in therapy; may be accompanied by fever, arthralgia, or lymphadenopathy. Urticaria has been reported [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, anorexia, vomiting, and occasional diarrhoea. Rare reports of pancreatitis, cheilosis, glossitis, and gingivostomatitis [Australian].
- **Haematological:** Bone marrow hypoplasia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, and eosinophilia.
- **Renal:** Nephrotic syndrome, proteinuria, and rare cases of fatal glomerulonephritis/intra-alveolar haemorrhage (Goodpasture’s syndrome) [Australian].
- **Dermatological:** Increased skin friability and extravasation (purpura/blood-filled vesicles) at pressure points (knees, elbows); elastosis perforans serpiginosa (rare); falling hair [Australian].
- **Other:** 
    - Reversible impairment of taste.
    - Reversible optic neuritis (possibly Vitamin B6 related).
    - Drug fever (typically in the 2nd or 3rd week).
    - Myasthenia gravis-like symptoms or pemphigus-like disorders.
    - Breast enlargement (rarely reported in both men and women) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Isoniazid:** Penicillamine potentiates the effects of isoniazid [Australian].
- **Pyridoxine (Vitamin B6):** Penicillamine increases the requirement for Vitamin B6. Prophylactic administration of 25 mg daily is recommended by some authorities to prevent deficiency-related nervous symptoms or optic neuritis [Australian].
- **Heavy Metals:** Potentiates excretion of gold, iron, zinc, and mercury [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. 
    - Penicillamine can cause cutis laxa and generalised connective tissue defects in the human foetus. 
    - It should not be used in pregnancy if possible. In cases of Wilson's disease or severe cystinuria, the benefits must be weighed against the risk to the foetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Concentration in breast milk is unknown. It should only be used in breastfeeding patients when considered essential by a physician [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available in source documentation [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No data provided in available sources [Australian]. Supplemental iron may be required in children due to potential deficiency [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data provided in available sources [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources, though intensive monitoring for proteinuria/haematuria is required for all patients [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources; however, liver function tests are advised every six months for the first 18 months of therapy [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Chelation:** The stable thiol group chelates heavy metals (especially copper), forming soluble complexes for excretion.
    - **Cystinuria:** Conducts disulphide interchange with cystine to form a more soluble penicillamine-cysteine disulphide.
    - **Rheumatoid Arthritis:** Mechanism remains unknown; theories involve immune response interference, copper chelation, or effects on collagen [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Readily absorbed from the GI tract.
    - **Distribution:** Distributed through body water space. High plasma protein and tissue binding (especially skin) delays final clearance.
    - **Elimination:** Up to 80% is excreted in the urine as disulphides.
    - **Half-life:** Initial blood half-life is 20 minutes (lasting < 1 hour); the half-life of stored penicillamine is approximately 90 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No clinical trial data were described in the provided source document [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Conditions:** Protect from moisture. Keep the bottle tightly closed [Australian].
- **Container:** HDPE bottle with child-resistant closure [Australian].
