# Editorial Product Information: Netupitant and Palonosetron

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) documentation from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of the fixed-dose combination of netupitant and palonosetron for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for educational and reference purposes. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory approved PI for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Netupitant and Palonosetron (as hydrochloride).
- **Chemical Class:** Netupitant is a selective substance P/neurokinin 1 (NK1) receptor antagonist. Palonosetron is a selective serotonin subtype 3 (5-HT3) receptor antagonist.
- **Brand Name:** **AKYNZEO** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A04AA55 (Palonosetron, combinations).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Hard gelatin capsule containing 300 mg netupitant (as three 100 mg immediate-release tablets) and 500 micrograms palonosetron (as one soft capsule) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Juniper Biologics Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chemotherapy-Induced Nausea and Vomiting (CINV) - HEC:** Prevention of acute and delayed nausea and vomiting associated with initial and repeat courses of highly emetogenic cancer chemotherapy (HEC) [Australian-approved].
- **CINV - MEC:** Prevention of acute and delayed nausea and vomiting associated with initial and repeat courses of moderately emetogenic cancer chemotherapy (MEC) [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** One capsule (300 mg/500 mcg) administered approximately one hour prior to the start of each chemotherapy cycle.
- **Administration Instructions:** To be taken orally. The capsule should be swallowed whole and can be taken with or without food.
- **Concomitant Dexamethasone:** Co-administration with dexamethasone is recommended. The oral dexamethasone dose should be reduced by approximately 50% when used with this combination due to the CYP3A4 inhibitory effects of netupitant [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources (Product is intended for single administration prior to chemotherapy).

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to netupitant, palonosetron, or any excipients in the formulation.
- **Cross-Hypersensitivity:** Caution in patients who have exhibited hypersensitivity to other selective 5-HT3 receptor antagonists [Australian].
- **Pregnancy:** This combination is contraindicated during pregnancy [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** There have been reports of serotonin syndrome with 5-HT3 antagonists used alone or in combination with other serotonergic drugs (e.g., SSRIs, SNRIs).
- **QT Prolongation:** While clinical studies showed no clinically important effects on ECG parameters, caution is advised in patients with a history of or predisposition to QT prolongation, electrolyte abnormalities (hypokalaemia or hypomagnesemia should be corrected prior to administration), or those taking anti-arrhythmic agents.
- **Constipation:** Palonosetron may increase large bowel transit time; monitor patients with a history of constipation or signs of subacute intestinal obstruction.
- **CYP3A4 Substrates:** Netupitant is a moderate inhibitor of CYP3A4 with a long half-life (88 hours). Its inhibitory effect can last for over 4 days. Systemic exposure to chemotherapy agents or other drugs metabolised by CYP3A4 (e.g., docetaxel, dexamethasone) may increase.
- **Excipients:**
    - Contains **sorbitol** and **sucrose**: Avoid in patients with rare hereditary fructose intolerance or glucose-galactose malabsorption.
    - Contains **lecithin** (derived from soya): Monitor patients with known peanut or soya hypersensitivity for allergic reactions [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions were reported in approximately 13% of patients. The most common reactions (≥1%) reported across clinical trials include:

| Adverse Reaction | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Headache** | 3.6% |
| **Constipation** | 3.0% |
| **Fatigue** | 1.2% |

- **Uncommon (0.1% to 1%):** Neutropenia, leucocytosis, insomnia, dizziness, hiccups, hypertension, abdominal pain, diarrhoea, dyspepsia, and increased liver transaminases.
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Arrhythmia, atrioventricular block (second degree), myocardial ischaemia, psychosis, blurred vision, hypokalaemia, and QT prolongation on ECG.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors/Inducers:** Netupitant is primarily metabolised by CYP3A4. Concomitant use with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole) increases netupitant exposure. Strong inducers (e.g., rifampicin) significantly decrease exposure and may reduce efficacy.
- **CYP3A4 Substrates:** Netupitant increases the AUC of dexamethasone (2.4-fold), midazolam (2.4-fold), and docetaxel (37%). Dexamethasone doses MUST be reduced by 50%.
- **P-glycoprotein (P-gp) Interactions:** Netupitant is an inhibitor of P-gp. Caution is advised when combined with P-gp substrates such as digoxin, dabigatran, or colchicine, particularly in patients with renal impairment.
- **BCRP Transporters:** Netupitant is an inhibitor of BCRP; caution is advised with substrates like zidovudine or morphine.
- **Oral Contraceptives:** Exposure to levonorgestrel may increase 1.4-fold, but clinical effects on contraceptive efficacy are unlikely [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Contraindicated. Animal studies show potential for fetal abnormalities (positional abnormalities, cleft palate, microphthalmia) at high doses. Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during and for one month after treatment.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if netupitant or palonosetron are excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding should be discontinued during treatment and for one month after the last dose [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies for palonosetron showed no effect at human-equivalent doses, though high doses showed testicular degeneration in rats. Netupitant showed no effect on rat fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children have not been established. No data available [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment is necessary for patients ≥65 years.
- **Renal Impairment:** No adjustment required for mild to moderate impairment. Use should be **avoided** in patients with severe renal impairment or end-stage renal disease [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment required for mild to moderate impairment (Child-Pugh 5-8). Use with **caution** in patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh ≥9) due to increased netupitant exposure.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:**
    - **Netupitant:** Competitive antagonist of human substance P/neurokinin 1 (NK1) receptors, primarily targeting the delayed phase of emesis.
    - **Palonosetron:** 5-HT3 receptor antagonist with high binding affinity, targeting the acute phase of emesis.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Netupitant bioavailability >60%. Palonosetron bioavailability ~97%.
    - **Distribution:** Both are highly protein bound (>97-99% for netupitant; 62% for palonosetron).
    - **Metabolism:** Netupitant is extensively metabolised by CYP3A4 into three active metabolites (M1, M2, M3). Palonosetron is metabolised via CYP2D6, CYP3A4, and CYP1A2.
    - **Elimination:** Netupitant half-life is approximately 88 hours. Palonosetron terminal half-life is approximately 37–48 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **HEC Study:** A multicenter, double-blind study (N=694) compared netupitant/palonosetron plus dexamethasone against oral palonosetron. The primary endpoint (Complete Response/CR in the overall phase 0-120h) was significantly higher for the combination group (89.6%) vs. palonosetron alone (76.5%) [Australian].
- **MEC Study:** A study (N=1450) in patients receiving anthracycline and cyclophosphamide showed the combination was superior to palonosetron alone in achieving CR in the delayed phase (76.9% vs 69.5%) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C.
- **Handling:** Store in the original container to protect from moisture.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
