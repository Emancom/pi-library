# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from official Product Information (PI) documents from the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing. Clinicians should consult the local approved PI for their specific region. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism which result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dose:** 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Administration:** Given as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the next 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours (q3h or q4h); frequency should never be less than every 6 hours (q6h) [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are recommended in the range of 50 mg/kg or as therapy requires. The maximum recorded dose is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Titration:** Dose adjustments may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be necessary if levels are high [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [US].
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. These may occur within minutes of intravenous administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate appropriate medical management [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed following levocarnitine administration (oral or IV) [US].
- **Thrombosis/INR Monitoring:** Increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) has been reported when used concomitantly with warfarin. Monitor INR levels after initiating treatment or following dose adjustments [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** In metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Anaphylaxis/Hypersensitivity:** [US].
- **Seizures:** [US].

### Common Adverse Effects (Clinical Trials in ESRD)
The following events occurred at a frequency of ≥5% in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials, regardless of causality [US]:

| Body System | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain, flu syndrome, headache, injection site reaction, pain, fever, abdominal pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, dyspepsia |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia, depression |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis |

- **Other Noted Reactions:** Transient nausea and vomiting, gastritis, and body odor have been observed [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased INR when co-administered. Management: Monitor INR levels regularly [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine is not bound to plasma proteins or albumin; therefore, displacement interactions are unlikely [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category C]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation. Consider discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was observed in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing guidelines for metabolic disorders are applicable to pediatric patients (see Section 3).
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **ESRD:** Levocarnitine is indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD patients on dialysis.
    - **Oral Formulation Note:** Chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with metabolic defects or organic acidopathies, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Dialysis Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x weekly post-dialysis), mean trough levocarnitine concentrations increased from ~20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed a dose-proportional increase in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Clinical Outcomes Note:** While IV levocarnitine effectively increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the effects on clinical outcomes in this population have not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL [US].
