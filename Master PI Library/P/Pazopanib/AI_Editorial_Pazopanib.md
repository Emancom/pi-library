# Editorial Product Information: Pazopanib

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically accurate editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information approved in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved PI for prescribing purposes.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pazopanib (as hydrochloride).
- **Chemical Class:** Multitargeted tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI).
- **Brand Name(s):** Pazopanib ADVZ [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01EX03.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Film-coated tablets in 200 mg and 400 mg strengths [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Advanz Pharma (Boucher & Muir Pty Ltd) [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Advanced and/or Metastatic Renal Cell Carcinoma (RCC):** First-line treatment or in patients who have received prior cytokine-based therapy [Australian].
- **Advanced Soft Tissue Sarcoma (STS):** Indicated for unresectable or metastatic disease in patients who have received prior chemotherapy (specifically including an anthracycline), unless contraindicated [Australian]. 
    - *Note:* Clinical trials excluded patients with adipocytic STS or gastrointestinal stromal tumours (GIST) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The standard recommended dose is **800 mg orally once daily** [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Must be taken without food (at least one hour before or two hours after a meal) [Australian].
    - Tablets must be swallowed whole with water; they **must not be broken or crushed** due to the potential for increased absorption rate and exposure [Australian].
- **Dose Modifications:** 
    - Adjustments should be made in 200 mg increments based on individual tolerability [Australian].
    - The maximum daily dose should not exceed 800 mg [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, it should not be taken if the next scheduled dose is due in less than 12 hours [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to pazopanib hydrochloride or any of the excipients (e.g., magnesium stearate, microcrystalline cellulose, povidone, sodium starch glycollate, hypromellose, macrogol 400, titanium dioxide, polysorbate 80, and iron oxide red) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning (Hepatotoxicity):** Severe and fatal hepatotoxicity has been observed. Serum liver tests (ALT, AST, bilirubin) must be monitored prior to initiation and at weeks 3, 5, 7, and 9; then at months 3 and 4, and periodically thereafter [Australian]. 
- **Hypertension:** Blood pressure must be well-controlled before starting therapy. Monitoring should occur within one week of initiation and frequently thereafter [Australian].
- **Cardiac Dysfunction:** Events such as congestive heart failure and decreased Left Ventricular Ejection Fraction (LVEF) have occurred. Baseline and periodic LVEF evaluation is recommended, especially in patients at risk [Australian].
- **QT Prolongation and Torsade de Pointes:** Use with caution in patients with a history of QT prolongation or those taking antiarrhythmics. Electrolytes (Ca, Mg, K) must be maintained within normal range [Australian].
- **Arterial/Venous Thrombotic Events:** Myocardial infarction, stroke, and pulmonary embolism have been reported. Use with caution in patients at increased risk [Australian].
- **Haemorrhagic Events:** Fatal events have occurred. Pazopanib has not been studied in patients with recent history of haemoptysis, cerebral haemorrhage, or significant GI bleed [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal Perforation and Fistula:** Fatal cases reported; use with caution in at-risk populations [Australian].
- **Wound Healing:** VEGF inhibitors may impair healing. Discontinue pazopanib at least 7 days prior to scheduled surgery [Australian].
- **Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES):** Permanently discontinue if PRES/RPLS develops [Australian].
- **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD)/Pneumonitis:** Monitor for pulmonary symptoms; discontinue if ILD develops [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequencies are categorised as: Very common (≥10%), Common (1–10%), and Uncommon (0.1–1%) [Australian].

| System Organ Class | Very Common (≥10%) | Common (1–10%) |

| **Gastrointestinal** | Diarrhoea, Nausea, Vomiting, Abdominal pain, Stomatitis | Dyspepsia, Flatulence, Haemorrhage |
| **Vascular** | Hypertension | Venous embolism |
| **Metabolism** | Decreased appetite | Hypomagnesaemia |
| **Nervous System** | Headache, Dysgeusia | Dizziness |

*\*Palmar-plantar erythrodysaesthesia syndrome.*

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Hepatotoxicity, arterial thromboembolism, GI perforation, and Torsade de Pointes [Australian].
- **Regional Variation:** Neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, and PPE syndrome were observed more frequently in patients of East Asian descent [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Strong inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, clarithromycin, ritonavir) increase pazopanib exposure and should be avoided. If essential, reduce pazopanib dose to 400 mg [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Strong inducers (e.g., rifampin) may decrease pazopanib concentrations; avoid concomitant use [Australian].
- **Gastric pH Elevating Agents:** Co-administration with esomeprazole or other agents that increase gastric pH decreases pazopanib bioavailability; avoid if possible [Australian].
- **Simvastatin:** Concomitant use increases the risk of ALT elevations; monitor liver function closely or consider discontinuation of simvastatin [Australian].
- **Transporters:** Pazopanib is a substrate for P-gp and BCRP. It also inhibits UGT1A1 and OATP1B1, potentially increasing concentrations of drugs like irinotecan or rosuvastatin [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D [Australian]**. Pazopanib can cause foetal harm (teratogenic in animals). It should not be used unless the benefit to the mother outweighs risks to the foetus. 
- **Contraception:** Females of childbearing potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for **2 weeks** after the last dose. Male patients (even those with vasectomies) should use condoms during treatment and for **2 weeks** post-dose [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety is not established; breastfeeding is not recommended during therapy [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies indicate pazopanib may impair male and female fertility (e.g., reduced sperm production, ovarian atrophy) [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for use in patients <18 years. **Contraindicated in children <2 years** due to severe risks to organ growth and maturation [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required for patients >65 years [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment needed for CrCl ≥30 mL/min. No experience in severe impairment or dialysis; use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild:** No dose adjustment required [Australian].
    - **Moderate:** Reduce dose to **200 mg once daily** [Australian].
    - **Severe:** Not recommended (total bilirubin >3x ULN regardless of ALT) [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Multi-tyrosine kinase inhibitor targeting VEGFR-1, -2, and -3, PDGFR-$\alpha$ and -$\beta$, and c-KIT. This results in inhibition of angiogenesis and tumour growth [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Bioavailability is low (13.5–38.9%) and increased significantly (approx. 2-fold) by food [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein bound (>99%) [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily mediated by CYP3A4, with minor contributions from CYP1A2 and CYP2C8 [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Mean half-life is 30.9 hours. Excretion is primarily via faeces; renal elimination accounts for <4% [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Renal Cell Carcinoma (VEG105192):** A Phase III trial (N=435) showed a significant improvement in progression-free survival (PFS) for pazopanib (9.2 months) vs placebo (4.2 months) [Australian].
- **Soft Tissue Sarcoma (VEG110727):** A Phase III trial (N=369) demonstrated improved PFS for pazopanib (20 weeks) vs placebo (7 weeks) [Australian].
- **Non-inferiority (COMPARZ):** Pazopanib demonstrated non-inferiority to sunitinib regarding PFS in treatment-naïve RCC patients, with different safety profiles (pazopanib associated with more liver toxicity; sunitinib with more hand-foot syndrome and fatigue) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C [Australian].
- Keep in the original container [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
