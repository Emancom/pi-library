# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical and regulatory data into a single, structured reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Always consult the current local regulatory documentation before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (naturally occurring carrier molecule).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
  - **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) sterile solution in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Metabolic Disorders:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **Carnitine Deficiency in ESRD:** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease (ESRD) who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours (whether by infusion or injection) [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically in the range of 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered if clinically required [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow (2 to 3 minute) bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be implemented as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration before use [US].
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Hypersensitivity Reactions
- **Serious reactions:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported, particularly in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate medical management [US].

### Neurologic Reactions (Seizures)
- Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a known seizure history, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].

### General Precautions
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation):** The safety of *oral* levocarnitine has not been established in patients with renal impairment. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) normally cleared by the kidneys [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Plasma Concentrations:** It is recommended to obtain a baseline plasma carnitine concentration before starting parenteral therapy.
- **Ongoing Monitoring:** Weekly and monthly monitoring should include plasma carnitine levels (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L), blood chemistries, and vital signs [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis and respiratory distress [US].
- **Neurological:** Seizures (new onset or exacerbated) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Clinical Management:** Monitor INR levels at the initiation of treatment or following any dose adjustment [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not formally categorised under the old letter system in the provided PI]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. However, no adequate, well-controlled studies exist in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. While studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after administration, the risk to the infant of excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation for the mother. Consideration may be given to discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders apply to paediatric patients (see Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous administration is indicated for ESRD patients; however, high-dose *oral* therapy is cautioned against due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
- Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US]. 
- In certain organic acidopathies, it promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis), mean trough plasma levocarnitine concentrations increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. While levocarnitine effectively increases carnitine concentrations, the direct effect on clinical outcomes (signs and symptoms) in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
