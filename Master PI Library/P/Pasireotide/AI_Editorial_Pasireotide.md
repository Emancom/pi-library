# Editorial Product Information: Pasireotide

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (TGA). [US (FDA), European (EMA), UK (MHRA), Canadian (Health Canada), and Japanese (PMDA) documents were not provided for this synthesis.]
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of pasireotide for healthcare professionals, synthesising available regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pasireotide (as diaspartate).
- **Chemical Class:** Cyclohexapeptide; somatostatin analogue.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Signifor** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** H01CB05.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Solution for injection in 1 mL ampoules containing 300 mcg, 600 mcg, or 900 mcg [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Recordati Rare Diseases [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Cushing’s Disease:** Treatment of adult patients for whom pituitary surgery is not an option or has proved unsuccessful [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** The standard dosage range is 300 mcg to 900 mcg administered via subcutaneous injection twice daily [Australian].
- **Initial Dose:** Recommended starting dose is either 600 mcg or 900 mcg twice daily [Australian].
- **Titration:** Dose adjustments should be based on clinical response (reduction in urinary free cortisol [UFC] and symptom improvement) and patient tolerability. If 600 mcg bid is well-tolerated but UFC remains elevated, an increase to 900 mcg bid may be considered [Australian].
- **Dose Reduction:** For management of adverse reactions, dose decrements of 300 mcg twice daily are suggested [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** Administer by self-injection into the top of the thighs or the abdomen. Sites should be rotated; avoid injecting into inflamed or irritated areas. The solution should reach room temperature before use [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, the next dose should be taken at the scheduled time. Doses must not be doubled [Australian].
- **Discontinuation:** Therapy should be evaluated after two months; if no clinical benefit is observed, discontinuation should be considered [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Severe Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with Child-Pugh Category C hepatic dysfunction [Australian].
- **Other Jurisdictions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypocortisolism:** Rapid suppression of ACTH may lead to low circulating cortisol levels. Patients should be monitored for signs such as weakness, fatigue, nausea, and hypotension. Temporary steroid replacement or dose reduction may be required [Australian].
- **Hyperglycaemia and Diabetes:** Very common. Glycaemic status must be assessed prior to initiation. Worsening glycaemia typically occurs within the first two weeks. Monitoring (FPG/HbA1c) should occur weekly for the first 2–3 months and periodically thereafter. Significant risk exists for patients with baseline HbA1c >8% [Australian].
- **Ketoacidosis:** Post-marketing cases have been reported, even in patients without a history of diabetes [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Events:**
    - **Bradycardia:** Caution is advised in patients with pre-existing cardiac disease or those taking bradycardic agents (e.g., beta-blockers, calcium channel blockers) [Australian].
    - **QTc Prolongation:** Pasireotide can prolong the QT interval. Baseline ECG is recommended. Use with caution in patients with congenital long QT, uncontrolled heart disease, or those taking QTc-prolonging medications [Australian].
- **Liver Test Elevations:** Monitor liver function tests (LFTs) before initiation, 1–2 weeks after starting, and then monthly for the first three months. Discontinue if jaundice or significant sustained enzyme elevations (e.g., ALT >5x ULN) occur [Australian].
- **Cholelithiasis:** Gallstones are a known class effect. Ultrasonic gallbladder examinations are recommended at 6–12 month intervals [Australian].
- **Pituitary Hormones:** May inhibit hormones other than ACTH (e.g., TSH, GH). Periodic monitoring of thyroid and pituitary function is advised [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally consistent with the somatostatin analogue class, with a notably higher incidence of glycaemic disturbances.

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Diarrhoea (58%), Nausea (52%), Hyperglycaemia (40%), Cholelithiasis (30%), Headache (28%), Abdominal pain (24%), Fatigue (19%), Diabetes mellitus (18%), Injection site reactions (17%), Nasopharyngitis (13%), Alopecia (12%), Asthenia (11%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Hypoglycaemia, Hypertension, Dizziness, Anxiety, Insomnia, Myalgia, Arthralgia, Lipase/Amylase increased, Constipation, Hypotension, Sinus bradycardia, Adrenal insufficiency, QT prolongation |
| **Rare/Unkown** | Diabetic ketoacidosis, Cholangitis (secondary to gallstones), Steatorrhoea [Australian]. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **P-glycoprotein (P-gp):** Pasireotide is a P-gp substrate. Strong P-gp inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, clarithromycin, verapamil) may increase pasireotide levels [Australian].
    - **Cyclosporin:** Pasireotide may decrease cyclosporin absorption; dose adjustments of cyclosporin may be necessary [Australian].
    - **Bromocriptine:** Co-administration may increase the bioavailability of bromocriptine [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **QTc Prolonging Drugs:** Use with extreme caution with Class Ia (e.g., quinidine) or Class III (e.g., amiodarone) antiarrhythmics and other QTc-prolonging agents [Australian].
    - **Bradycardic Agents:** Increased monitoring of heart rate required when used with beta-blockers or diltiazem [Australian].
    - **Anti-diabetic Medications:** Adjustments to insulin or oral hypoglycaemics (e.g., metformin, liraglutide) are frequently required [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. Animal studies show evidence of increased foetal damage. Use only if potential benefit outweighs risk. Effective contraception is recommended for women of childbearing potential [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Excreted in rat milk; human data unavailable. Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment [Australian].
- **Fertility:** May affect fertility in females due to cortisol reduction and IGF-1 inhibition [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for patients under 18 years due to a lack of clinical data [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Limited data for patients >65 years. No specific dose adjustment is suggested, though age-related clearance reduction has been noted [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required for patients with impaired renal function [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild (Child-Pugh A):** No adjustment.
    - **Moderate (Child-Pugh B):** Starting dose of 300 mcg twice daily; maximum dose 600 mcg twice daily [Australian].
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Contraindicated [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Pasireotide binds to multiple somatostatin receptor subtypes (hsst 1, 2, 3, and 5). It has a particularly high affinity for hsst5, which is overexpressed in the corticotroph tumour cells of Cushing’s disease patients, leading to inhibition of ACTH secretion [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Rapid (Tmax 0.25–0.5 h). 
    - **Distribution:** Widely distributed (Vz/F >100 L); 88% plasma protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Highly stable; minimal metabolism by CYP450.
    - **Elimination:** Mainly via biliary/faecal excretion (48.3%); minimal renal clearance (7.6%) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Phase III Study:** A multicentre, randomised trial (n=162) compared 600 mcg and 900 mcg bid. 
- **Primary Outcome:** Normalisation of UFC at 6 months was achieved in 15% of the 600 mcg group and 26% of the 900 mcg group. 
- **Observations:** Significant reductions in UFC were typically seen within the first two months. Clinical symptoms such as facial rubor and fat pad thickness also showed improvement in a subset of patients [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C. Keep in the original package to protect from light [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the outer packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Prescription Only Medicine (**Schedule 4**).
- **Other Jurisdictions:** Not specified in available sources.
