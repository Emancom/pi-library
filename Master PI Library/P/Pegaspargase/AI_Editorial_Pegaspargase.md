# Editorial Product Information: Pegaspargase

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, intended to consolidate clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information mandated for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should always refer to the current approved PI in their own region before prescribing or administering **pegaspargase**.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** **Pegaspargase** (a covalent conjugate of *Escherichia coli*-derived L-asparaginase with monomethoxypolyethylene glycol [mPEG]).
- **Brand Name(s):** **ONCASPAR** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01XX24.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):**
    - Lyophilised powder for solution for injection/infusion.
    - 3,750 U per vial.
    - Following reconstitution (5.2 mL water for injections), the concentration is 750 U/mL [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intramuscular (IM) or Intravenous (IV). For small volumes, IM is the preferred route [Australian].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Servier Laboratories [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Lymphoblastic Leukaemia (ALL):** Indicated as a component of antineoplastic combination therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Patients ≤ 21 years:**
    - Body Surface Area (BSA) < 0.6 m² or infants < 1 year: 82.5 U/kg body weight every 14 days.
    - BSA ≥ 0.6 m²: 2,500 U/m² BSA every 14 days.
- **Patients > 21 years:** 2,000 U/m² BSA every 14 days.
- **Elderly (≥ 65 years):** Recommended dose has not been established.

### Administration Instructions
- **Premedication:** Administer paracetamol, an H1-receptor blocker (e.g., diphenhydramine), and an H2-receptor blocker (e.g., famotidine) 30–60 minutes prior to administration to reduce the risk and severity of hypersensitivity reactions.
- **Preparation:**
    - Reconstitute with 5.2 mL water for injections.
    - **Do not shake.** Gently swirl until the powder is dissolved.
    - For IV infusion: Dilute in 100 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride or 5% dextrose. Administer over 1–2 hours into a running infusion.
    - Do not infuse other drugs through the same line.
- **Monitoring:** Patients must be observed for at least one hour post-infusion in a setting with resuscitation equipment.

## 4. Contraindications
**Pegaspargase** is contraindicated in patients with [Australian]:
- History of anaphylactic or severe hypersensitivity to the active substance or excipients.
- History of serious thrombosis associated with previous asparaginase therapy.
- History of pancreatitis (including that related to previous asparaginase therapy).
- History of serious haemorrhagic events during previous asparaginase therapy.
- Severe hepatic impairment.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in provided source.
- **Anaphylaxis and Hypersensitivity:** Life-threatening reactions can occur, especially in those with known sensitivity to *E. coli* asparaginase. Discontinue permanently if a serious reaction occurs.
- **Pancreatic Toxicity:** Pancreatitis (haemorrhagic or necrotising) may be fatal. Monitor serum amylase/lipase. **If pancreatitis is confirmed, the drug must not be restarted.**
- **Hepatotoxicity and Veno-Occlusive Disease (VOD):** Severe hepatotoxicity and fatal VOD/Sinusoidal Obstruction Syndrome have been reported. Risk is higher in Philadelphia chromosome-positive patients and those > 18 years. Monitor bilirubin and liver function at baseline and before each dose.
- **Coagulopathy:** Risk of serious thrombotic events (e.g., sagittal sinus thrombosis) and haemorrhage due to decreased fibrinogen and antithrombin III. Monitor coagulation profiles.
- **Osteonecrosis:** Increased risk when used with glucocorticoids, particularly in children and adolescents > 10 years of age (highest incidence in girls).
- **Central Nervous System (CNS) Toxicity:** Encephalopathy (including reversible posterior leukoencephalopathy syndrome [RPLS]), seizures, and somnolence may occur.
- **Hyperammonaemia:** Rapid conversion of asparagine can lead to sharp rises in ammonia, potentially causing encephalopathy.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequencies are based on clinical trial data (Studies DFCI 11-001 and AALL07P4) [Australian].

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Febrile neutropenia, pancreatitis, diarrhoea, abdominal pain, nausea, hypersensitivity/rash/urticaria, hypoalbuminaemia, increased ALT/AST, hypertriglyceridaemia, decreased fibrinogen, increased amylase/lipase, prolonged aPTT, increased bilirubin, decreased antithrombin III, hyperglycaemia, embolism. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Anaemia, coagulopathy, thrombosis, vomiting, stomatitis, ascites, hepatotoxicity, fatty liver, sepsis/infections, prolonged PT, increased INR, hypokalaemia, hyponatremia, decreased platelets, seizure, peripheral motor neuropathy, hypoxia. |
| **Rare (0.1–1%)** | Necrotising/haemorrhagic pancreatitis, hepatic cell necrosis, jaundice, hepatic failure, RPLS. |
| **Not Known** | Bone marrow failure, parotitis, anaphylactic shock, hyperammonaemia, diabetic ketoacidosis, osteonecrosis. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Vincristine:** Concomitant or immediate preceding use can increase **pegaspargase** toxicity and the risk of anaphylaxis. Administer vincristine in a timely manner *before* **pegaspargase**.
- **Methotrexate/Cytarabine:** **Pegaspargase** may synergistically or antagonistically affect agents requiring cell division; adherence to the treatment schedule is critical.
- **Protein-bound drugs:** Depletion of serum proteins by **pegaspargase** may increase the toxicity of highly protein-bound medications.
- **Anticoagulants/Antiplatelets:** Increased risk of bleeding/thrombosis due to fluctuating coagulation factors.
- **Oral Contraceptives:** Potential for impaired hepatic clearance of contraceptives; a non-hormonal method of contraception is recommended.
- **Live Vaccines:** Risk of severe infection; avoid vaccination during and for 3–6 months after completing all antileukaemic therapy.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D [Australian].** Based on animal studies showing teratogenicity with native L-asparaginase (congenital malformations and embryolethal effects). **Pegaspargase** should not be used during pregnancy unless clearly necessary.
- **Contraception:** Effective contraception is required during treatment and for at least 6 months after discontinuation.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if the drug is excreted in breast milk. Breastfeeding must be discontinued during treatment and not resumed until therapy has ceased.
- **Fertility:** Data on human fertility are not established.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Well-established as part of ALL protocols. Specific dosing for infants < 1 year and patients ≤ 21 years is provided (see Section 3).
- **Elderly:** Limited data available for patients > 65 years.
- **Renal Impairment:** As a high molecular weight protein, it is not renally excreted. No dose adjustment is expected, though it has not been formally evaluated.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe hepatic impairment. Monitor liver function closely in all patients.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Pegaspargase** catalyses the conversion of L-asparagine into aspartic acid and ammonia. Leukaemic cells, lacking asparagine synthetase, cannot synthesise asparagine de novo and die due to depletion of the extracellular supply.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Mean half-life of absorption from IM injection is 1.7 days [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Elimination half-life is approximately 5.3 to 5.5 days.
    - **Distribution:** Vss is approximately 1.86–1.95 L/m².
    - **Activity:** Asparaginase activity ≥ 0.1 U/mL is generally sustained for 14–18 days after a 2,500 U/m² dose.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study CCG-1962:** A randomised study in 118 paediatric patients (1–9 years) showed that a single IM dose of **pegaspargase** (2,500 U/m²) provided similar asparagine depletion to 9 doses of native *E. coli* asparaginase. 3-year Event-Free Survival (EFS) was 83% for **pegaspargase**.
- **Study AALL07P4:** A pilot study in 166 patients (1–31 years) evaluating IV administration. Asparagine depletion to < 0.1 U/mL was sustained for approximately two weeks.
- **Study DFCI 11-001:** An ongoing study in 239 patients (1–22 years) confirming that **pegaspargase** 2,500 U/m² administered every two weeks maintains clinically relevant activity throughout the dosing interval.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store under refrigerated conditions (2°C to 8°C). **Do not freeze.**
- **Shelf Life:** 3 years [Australian].
- **Reconstituted/Diluted Solution:**
    - Use as soon as possible.
    - Stable for 6 hours at room temperature (up to 25°C).
    - Stable for 24 hours when refrigerated (2°C to 8°C).

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Prescription Only Medicine (S4) [Australian].
