# Originality Audit Report: polyethylene glycol

## Audit Summary
- **Originality Score:** 78/100
- **Verbatim Copied Passages Found:** 4
- **Overall Assessment:** Concern

## Verbatim Copied Passages
The following passages are identical or nearly identical to the source text:

1.  **Mircera Pharmacology (Section 10):**
    - **Editorial Text:** "...slower association with the receptor and faster dissociation, leading to lower affinity but a significantly longer half-life..."
    - **Source (Doc 1, TGA, p. 10):** "...characterised by slower association to the receptor and slightly faster dissociation, resulting in a lower affinity for the receptor... Mircera thus has a longer half-life..."
2.  **Mircera PRCA Warning (Section 5):**
    - **Editorial Text:** "...therapy must be discontinued immediately and the patient should not be switched to another ESA."
    - **Source (Doc 1, TGA, p. 4):** "...therapy with Mircera must be discontinued and patients should not be switched to another ESA."
3.  **Mircera CV Risks (Section 5):**
    - **Editorial Text:** "...myocardial infarction, stroke, and congestive heart failure..."
    - **Source (Doc 1, TGA, p. 3):** "...myocardial infarction, stroke, congestive heart failure..."
4.  **PEG 3350 Interaction (Section 5):**
    - **Editorial Text:** "Do not combine PEG 3350 with starch-based thickeners used for dysphagia."
    - **Source (Doc 2, FDA, p. 2):** "do not combine with starch-based thickeners used for difficulty swallowing" (Note: "dysphagia" is the clinical synonym used by the editor).

## Close Paraphrases
1.  **Mircera Target Hb (Section 3):**
    - **Editorial:** "Use the lowest dose necessary to gradually increase haemoglobin (Hb) to a target of 100–120 g/L."
    - **Source (Doc 1, TGA, p. 1):** "Use the lowest dose of Mircera that will gradually increase the haemoglobin concentration... target of 100 and 120 g/L."
2.  **Mircera Dose Adjustment (Section 3):**
    - **Editorial:** "Adjust dose by ~25% no more than once monthly. Reduce dose if Hb rise exceeds 10 g/L in two weeks or approaches 120 g/L."
    - **Source (Doc 1, TGA, p. 2):** "...the monthly dose may be adjusted by approximately 25%. Dose adjustments should not be made more frequently than once a month... If the haemoglobin is increasing and approaching 120 g/L, the dose should be reduced by approximately 25%."
3.  **PEG 3350 Preparation (Section 3):**
    - **Editorial:** "Dissolve in 4 to 8 ounces (120–240 mL) of beverage (cold, hot, or room temperature)."
    - **Source (Doc 2, FDA, p. 2):** "stir and dissolve in any 4 to 8 ounces of beverage (cold, hot or room temperature) then drink."

## Originality Strengths
- **Conceptual Synthesis:** The editorial performs a high-level synthesis by grouping two very different drugs (a high-tech biologic injectable and an OTC laxative) under their common chemical name "polyethylene glycol." This structural choice is entirely original and does not exist in the source documents.
- **Section 1 (Product Identification):** The editorial successfully creates a comparative table-like structure that clarifies the massive differences in chemical class and ATC codes, which is original work.
- **Section 2 (Approved Indications):** The editor successfully condensed disparate paragraphs into two clear, concise bullet points.
- **Section 6 (Adverse Effects):** The editor converted a complex raw data table from the TGA document into a readable summary list with parenthetical percentages, which is an effective editorial distillation.

## Differences from Source PIs
- **Harmonisation:** The editorial adds an "Editorial Note" and "Purpose" section that explains *why* these two products are being presented together.
- **Jurisdictional Comparison:** Section 13 explicitly compares the regulatory status (Schedule 4 vs OTC), providing a cross-border perspective not found in either individual document.
- **Metric Conversion:** The editor added "120–240 mL" as a conversion for "4 to 8 ounces" in the PEG 3350 directions.
- **Omissions:** The editor removed extensive clinical trial data and detailed excipient lists, focusing only on high-level Phase III trial names (Section 11).

## Recommendation
**PASS WITH MINOR REVISION**

The editorial is a strong synthesis of two unrelated documents. It provides a unique "dual-use" perspective that is not present in the sources. However, because medical/regulatory writing requires high precision, the editor has defaulted to verbatim phrasing for "Mechanism of Action" and "PRCA Warnings." 

**Required Revisions:**
1.  **Section 10 (Pharmacology):** Rewrite the Mircera mechanism of action to avoid the "slower association... faster dissociation" verbatim sequence. *Example: "Mircera interacts with the erythropoietin receptor differently than recombinant versions; it binds more slowly but detaches less quickly, resulting in lower affinity paired with a prolonged half-life."*
2.  **Section 5 (Warnings):** Slightly rephrase the PRCA and CV risk sections to ensure they do not mirror the TGA's exact sentence structure while maintaining medical accuracy.
