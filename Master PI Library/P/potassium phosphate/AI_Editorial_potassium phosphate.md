# Editorial Product Information: Potassium Phosphate

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Regulatory Agencies:** Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA - Australia), Food and Drug Administration (FDA - United States).
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of official Product Information from the aforementioned agencies, designed for use by healthcare professionals as a comprehensive clinical reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is not a substitute for the official Product Information approved in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians must consult local regulations and the specific manufacturer’s PI before prescribing or administering this medication.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Potassium phosphate (Monobasic or a mixture of Monobasic and Dibasic).
- **Chemical Class:** Electrolyte/Mineral supplement.
- **Brand Names:** 
    - DBL Potassium Dihydrogen Phosphate Concentrated Injection [TGA].
    - Potassium Phosphates Injection, USP [FDA].
- **ATC Code:** A12BA03 (Potassium phosphate) / B05X (IV solution additives).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Australia [TGA]:** Monobasic potassium phosphate ($KH_2PO_4$) 1.361 g/10 mL. Each mL provides 1 mmol potassium, 1 mmol phosphate, and 2 mmol hydrogen ions. pH is approximately 4.5.
    - **United States [FDA]:** A mixture of Monobasic potassium phosphate ($KH_2PO_4$ 224 mg/mL) and Dibasic potassium phosphate ($K_2HPO_4$ 236 mg/mL). Each mL provides 3 mmol phosphorus (equivalent to 3 mmol phosphate) and 4.4 mEq potassium. pH is 6.0 to 7.0.
- **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only (must be diluted).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** 
    - Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [TGA].
    - Civica, Inc. [FDA].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypophosphatemia:** Treatment of severe hypophosphatemia (serum levels <0.3 mmol/L [TGA]) and other degrees of hypophosphatemia when oral/enteral replacement is not possible, insufficient, or contraindicated.
- **Parenteral Nutrition:** As a source of phosphorus in parenteral nutrition for adult and paediatric patients when oral/enteral intake is not possible [FDA-approved; not explicitly listed in TGA summary indications].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- **CRITICAL:** Must be diluted prior to administration. Never administer as an undiluted bolus or "IV push." 
- **Diluents:** 0.9% Sodium Chloride or 5% Glucose/Dextrose.
- **Pre-administration:** Check serum potassium and calcium. Normalize calcium prior to administration [FDA].
- **Route Specifics [FDA]:**
    - **Peripheral:** Maximum phosphorus concentration 6.8 mmol/100 mL; max potassium 10 mEq/100 mL.
    - **Central:** Maximum phosphorus concentration 18 mmol/100 mL; max potassium 26.4 mEq/100 mL.

### Adults (Hypophosphatemia)
- **TGA suggested dose:** Up to 10 mmol phosphate administered over 12 hours. May repeat every 12 hours until serum phosphate >0.3 mmol/L.
- **FDA suggested dose (by serum phosphorus concentration):**
    - 1.8 mg/dL to lower end of reference range: 0.16 to 0.31 mmol/kg.
    - 1.0 mg/dL to 1.7 mg/dL: 0.32 to 0.43 mmol/kg.
    - <1.0 mg/dL: 0.44 to 0.64 mmol/kg (Max single dose: 45 mmol phosphorus).
- **Max Infusion Rate [FDA]:** 6.8 mmol/hour (peripheral) or 15 mmol/hour (central).

### Paediatrics (Hypophosphatemia)
- **TGA suggested dose:** 0.15–0.33 mmol/kg administered over 6 hours. May repeat every 6 hours until serum phosphate >0.6 mmol/L. Rate must not exceed 0.2 mmol/kg/hour.
- **FDA suggested dose:** Same weight-based ranges as adults (see above) based on serum levels.

### Parenteral Nutrition [FDA]
- **Preterm and Term Infants (<12 months):** 2 mmol/kg/day.
- **Pediatric (1–12 years):** 1 mmol/kg/day (up to 40 mmol/day).
- **Adults and Pediatrics (>12 years):** 20 to 40 mmol/day.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hyperkalemia** 
- **Hyperphosphatemia**
- **Severe Renal Impairment:** eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73m² or end-stage renal disease [FDA]; <30% of normal function [TGA].
- **Calcium Imbalances:** Hypercalcemia [FDA/TGA] or significant hypocalcemia [FDA/TGA].
- **Adrenal Insufficiency:** Addison’s disease (risk of hyperkalemia) [TGA].
- **Urolithiasis:** Infected magnesium ammonium phosphate type [TGA].
- **Cardiac Disorders:** Ventricular fibrillation [TGA].
- **Tissue Trauma:** Extensive tissue breakdown (e.g., severe burns) [TGA].
- **Other [TGA]:** Acute dehydration, heat cramps, and hyperadrenalism associated with adrenogenital syndrome.
- **Sensitivity:** Increased sensitivity to potassium (e.g., adynamia episodica hereditaria or congenital paramyotonia) [TGA].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- **None reported** in provided sources; however, both agencies highlight life-threatening risks associated with rapid or undiluted administration.

### General Warnings
- **Cardiac Adverse Reactions:** Rapid infusion or bolus can cause death, cardiac arrest, arrhythmias (QT prolongation), and seizures. Continuous ECG monitoring is recommended for higher infusion rates [FDA: >10 mEq potassium/hour].
- **Pulmonary Embolism:** Vascular precipitates (especially calcium-phosphate) can cause pulmonary distress or death. If signs of pulmonary distress occur, stop infusion immediately [FDA].
- **Hyperkalemia:** Potentially fatal, rapid-onset, and often asymptomatic. Risk is increased in renal impairment, adrenal insufficiency, or concurrent use of potassium-sparing drugs.
- **Aluminum Toxicity:** The product contains aluminum, which can reach toxic levels in patients with renal impairment, particularly preterm infants [FDA].
- **Hypomagnesemia:** Infusion may cause a decrease in serum magnesium, particularly in patients with hypercalcemia or diabetic ketoacidosis [FDA].
- **Vein Damage:** Hypertonic solutions may cause thrombophlebitis. Use a central catheter for solutions >900 mOsmol/L [FDA].

### Monitoring Requirements
- Monitor serum phosphorus, potassium, calcium, magnesium, and renal function every 12 to 24 hours during therapy [TGA/FDA].

## 6. Adverse Effects
| System Organ Class | Adverse Effect | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Metabolism & Nutrition** | Fluid retention, hyperkalemia, hyperphosphatemia, hypocalcemia, hypomagnesemia, hypernatremia, hypovolemia, osmotic diuresis | Uncommon [TGA]; SOC [FDA] |
| **Cardiac** | Hypotension, arrhythmia, heart block, cardiac arrest, bradycardia, chest pain, ECG changes, myocardial infarction | Rare [TGA]; SOC [FDA] |
| **Respiratory** | Dyspnea, shortness of breath, pulmonary embolism (precipitates) | SOC [FDA] |
| **Renal** | Acute renal failure, nephrocalcinosis, decreased urine output | Rare [TGA]; SOC [FDA] |
| **Nervous System** | Confusion, tiredness, paraesthesia, paralysis, convulsions, headache, dizziness | SOC [FDA] |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal discomfort, stomach pain | SOC [FDA] |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **ACE Inhibitors, Angiotensin II Receptor Antagonists, and NSAIDs:** Increased risk of hyperkalemia, especially in renal impairment.
- **Potassium-sparing Diuretics (e.g., spironolactone):** Significant risk of severe hyperkalemia; avoid concurrent use if possible.
- **Digitalis Glycosides:** Hyperkalemia in digitalised patients with heart block may be dangerous; conversely, rapid lowering of potassium can cause digitalis toxicity [TGA/FDA].
- **Calcium-containing medicines:** Increased risk of soft tissue calcification/precipitation.
- **Salicylates:** Phosphate may decrease salicylate excretion in acidified urine, potentially leading to toxic salicylate levels [TGA].
- **Phosphate binders (Al, Ca, Mg salts):** May impair absorption if administered concurrently via gastrointestinal tract [TGA].
- **Immunosuppressants (Tacrolimus/Cyclosporine):** Increased risk of hyperkalemia [FDA].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australia [TGA]:** Not recommended. Animal studies not conducted.
    - **United States [FDA]:** Not expected to cause major birth defects. Supplementation should be considered if maternal requirements cannot be met by oral/enteral intake, as phosphorus is an essential mineral.
- **Breastfeeding:** 
    - **Australia [TGA]:** Not recommended as excretion into breast milk is unknown.
    - **United States [FDA]:** Phosphorus and potassium are present in human milk; administration at recommended doses is not expected to harm the infant.
- **Fertility:** No data available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Preterm infants are at higher risk for aluminum toxicity due to immature renal function [FDA]. Dosing must be weight-based and rate-limited.
- **Elderly Use:** Start at the low end of the dosing range. Monitor renal function closely due to the increased prevalence of hepatic, renal, or cardiac dysfunction.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Moderate (eGFR 30–60 mL/min):** Start at the low end of the dose range [FDA].
    - **Severe (eGFR <30 mL/min):** Contraindicated due to high risk of hyperkalemia and hyperphosphatemia.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Phosphate is the principal intracellular anion, essential for bone structure (80% found in skeleton), energy storage (ATP), B-vitamin utilization, and acid-base buffering. Potassium is the primary intracellular cation, vital for nerve conduction and muscle contraction.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Normal Plasma Phosphate:** 0.8 to 1.5 mmol/L.
    - **Distribution:** ~85% of serum phosphate is free/ultra-filterable; 15% is protein-bound [FDA].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (>90%). Filtered at the glomerulus and reabsorbed in the proximal tubule. Parathyroid hormone (PTH) increases urinary excretion by decreasing reabsorption.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No key pivotal trial data provided in the source documents. Approval is based on the established clinical role of potassium and phosphate in human physiology.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **TGA:** Store below 25°C.
- **FDA:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (USP Controlled Room Temperature).
- **Stability after dilution:**
    - Room Temperature: 4 hours [FDA].
    - Refrigerated (2°C to 8°C): 14 days [FDA] or 24 hours [TGA/FDA - specific to PN].
- **Incompatibilities:** Highly incompatible with calcium or magnesium salts (precipitation). Also incompatible with iron and aluminum [TGA].
