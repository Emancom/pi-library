# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data into a single, structured reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available source.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors):** For the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:**
    - **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the next 24 hours [US].
    - **Frequency:** Every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported administered dose is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Administration:** Slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - **Adjustment:** Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough levocarnitine levels [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].

### Monitoring
- **Plasma Carnitine Levels:** It is recommended to obtain levels before starting therapy. Trough (pre-dialysis) levels should be monitored weekly or monthly. Normal levels are typically between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Periodic monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations is essential [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulation):** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common and Very Common (>1%)
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting are common. Gastritis occurs less frequently [US].
- **General:** Body odor (fishy smell) [US].
- **ESRD Specific (Incidence ≥5%):**
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), chest pain (12%) [US].
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (16%), abdominal pain (9%), vomiting (15%) [US].
    - **General:** Headache (22%), infection (15%), injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), fever (7%) [US].
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%) [US].
    - **Neurological:** Dizziness (13%), paresthesia (5%) [US].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity [US].
- Seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not explicitly assigned, but follows former Category B criteria]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies on levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers have not been conducted. While studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine levels, the risk to a human infant must be weighed against the benefit of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg for metabolic disorders); see Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources, though general clinical monitoring applies.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for ESRD patients on hemodialysis. Use caution with oral formulations in this population due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Bioavailability:** Oral formulations (tablets and solution) have a low absolute bioavailability of approximately 15% [US].

### Pharmacodynamics
In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine clears accumulated acyl-CoA compounds by forming acylcarnitine, which is rapidly excreted [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg administered three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed dose-dependent increases in trough levocarnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources for other jurisdictions.
