# Editorial Product Information: Pentosan Polysulfate Sodium

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of pentosan polysulfate sodium for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single, cohesive reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial is a professional synthesis and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive prescribing guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pentosan polysulfate sodium (a semi-synthetic heparin-like macromolecular carbohydrate derivative).
- **Chemical Class:** Glycosaminoglycan-like macromolecule.
- **Brand Name(s):** Elmiron [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available source documents.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 100 mg hard gelatin capsules (white opaque) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arthropharm Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Interstitial Cystitis:** Indicated for the treatment of interstitial cystitis. Relief of symptoms typically requires 6 to 8 weeks of therapy. Long-term treatment is generally necessary for sustained symptom relief; symptoms may gradually return if treatment is discontinued [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:** 300 mg per day, administered as one 100 mg capsule three times daily [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** Capsules should be taken with water at least 1 hour before meals or 2 hours after meals [Australian].
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Note on Treatment Response:** Clinical effect may be delayed; if no improvement is seen after 6 months, the clinical benefit of continued therapy should be re-evaluated.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to pentosan polysulfate sodium or related compounds.
- Haemophilia.
- Active bleeding or a recent history of clinical bleeding.
- Concurrent therapy with heparin or oral anticoagulants [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Pigmentary Maculopathy:** Rare cases have been reported, particularly with long-term use. Symptoms may include difficulty reading or slow adjustment to reduced light environments. Regular ophthalmic examinations are recommended for all patients on long-term therapy. Discontinuation should be considered if maculopathy is detected [Australian].
- **Anticoagulant Effects:** Due to a weak anticoagulant action (approximately 1/15th the potency of heparin), caution is advised in patients with bleeding disorders, those at increased risk of bleeding (e.g., elderly, chronic alcohol use), or those undergoing surgery [Australian].
- **Empirical Use:** Should not be used for undiagnosed urogenital bleeding; a diagnosis of interstitial cystitis must be confirmed before initiating therapy [Australian].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Periodic monitoring is recommended for patients on long-term therapy, including:
    - Complete Blood Count (CBC)
    - Prothrombin Time (PT) and Activated Partial Thromboplastin Time (APTT)
    - Liver Function Tests (LFTs)
    - Renal Function Tests
    - Serum Calcium [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common to Very Common (>1%):**
    - Gastrointestinal: Nausea, indigestion, diarrhoea.
    - Central Nervous System: Headache, dizziness.
    - Other: Peripheral oedema, elevation of liver function tests [Australian].
- **Rare (<0.1%):**
    - Psychiatric: Mood swings, including suicidal ideation [Australian].
- **Post-Marketing/Frequency Unknown:**
    - Ocular: Pigmentary changes in the retina (pigmentary maculopathy) [Australian].
- **Note:** Unlike heparin, osteoporosis has not been reported in association with pentosan polysulfate sodium [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **Anticoagulants/Antiplatelets:** Contraindicated with concurrent heparin or oral anticoagulants due to increased risk of bleeding [Australian]. Use with caution in any patient on other medications that may affect coagulation.
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B1:** Animal reproduction studies (mice, rats, rabbits) using parenteral doses showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Oral data suggest minimal risk at doses of 600 mg/day. Studies in patients undergoing abortion indicate the drug does not cross the placental barrier. 
    - **Recommendation:** Despite animal data, there are no adequate controlled studies in pregnant women; therefore, use is generally not recommended during pregnancy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if the drug is excreted in human or animal milk. Use is not recommended in nursing women [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical data are limited. Use with caution due to a potentially higher risk of bleeding in this population [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Monitoring of renal function is recommended for long-term therapy, but specific dose adjustment protocols are not provided in available sources [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Elevation of liver function tests has been reported; periodic LFT monitoring is required [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Bladder Protection:** In interstitial cystitis, the drug is thought to bind to the transitional epithelium of the bladder, coating denuded areas of the protective glycosaminoglycan (GAG) layer. This restores the barrier between the urine and the epithelium.
    - **Haematological:** Inhibits Factor Xa (weak anticoagulant effect) and mobilises plasminogen activator (fibrinolytic activity).
    - **Other:** Demonstrates anti-inflammatory and lipolytic effects in animal and/or liver models [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** 40% to 60% of an oral dose is absorbed.
    - **Distribution:** Following IV administration, it localises primarily in the kidneys, liver, lungs, and periosteum. It does not appear to cross the placental barrier.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily desulfated in the liver and spleen; depolymerisation occurs in the kidneys.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 11% of an oral dose is excreted in the urine (mostly as metabolites) and 78% in the faeces. Only 1% to 3% appears in urine as unchanged compound [Australian].
    - **Half-life:** Following IV administration, 22 minutes (10 mg dose) to 55 minutes (100 mg dose) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **General Findings:** No specific pivotal trial data summaries were provided in the available Australian source document beyond the statement that relief of symptoms may take 6 to 8 weeks [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Special Precautions:** Protect from light [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4) [Australian].
