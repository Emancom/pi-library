# Editorial Product Information: Pegcetacoplan

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (TGA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of pegcetacoplan, synthesised for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pegcetacoplan (a complement inhibitor).
- **Chemical Class:** A symmetrical molecule comprised of two identical pentadecapeptides covalently bound to a linear 40-kDa polyethylene glycol (PEG) molecule.
- **Brand Name(s):** **EMPAVELI** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L04AJ03.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Solution for injection; 1,080 mg in a 20 mL single-use vial (54 mg/mL).
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Swedish Orphan Biovitrum Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Paroxysmal Nocturnal Haemoglobinuria (PNH):** Treatment of adult patients [Australian].
- **C3 Glomerulopathy (C3G):** Treatment of adults and adolescents aged 12 to 17 years [Australian].
- **Immune-Complex Membranoproliferative Glomerulonephritis (IC-MPGN):** Treatment of adults and adolescents aged 12 to 17 years with primary IC-MPGN [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Standard Dose:** 1,080 mg administered twice weekly as a subcutaneous infusion.
- **Schedule:** Doses should ideally be administered on Day 1 and Day 4 of each treatment week.

### Dosing by Indication
- **PNH:** 1,080 mg twice weekly. If Lactate Dehydrogenase (LDH) levels rise above 2 × the upper limit of normal (ULN), the frequency may be increased to 1,080 mg every third day. If increased, LDH should be monitored twice weekly for at least 4 weeks [Australian].
- **C3G and IC-MPGN (Adults and Adolescents ≥50 kg):** 1,080 mg twice weekly.
- **C3G and IC-MPGN (Adolescents <50 kg):** Weight-based titration is required:
    - **35 to <50 kg:** First dose 648 mg; second dose 810 mg; maintenance 810 mg twice weekly.
    - **30 to <35 kg:** First dose 540 mg; second dose 540 mg; maintenance 648 mg twice weekly.

### Switching from C5 Inhibitors (Eculizumab/Ravulizumab)
- To minimise haemolysis risk upon discontinuation of C5 therapy, pegcetacoplan 1,080 mg twice weekly should be added to the current C5 inhibitor dose for a 4-week lead-in period. After 4 weeks, the C5 inhibitor is discontinued, and pegcetacoplan continues as monotherapy.

### Administration Instructions
- Administered via a commercially available syringe system infusion pump.
- **Infusion Sites:** Abdomen, thighs, hips, or upper arms. Sites must be at least 7.5 cm apart.
- **Duration:** Approximately 30 minutes (if using two sites) or 60 minutes (if using one site).
- **Preparation:** Use within 2 hours of drawing into a syringe.

### Missed Doses
- Administer the missed dose as soon as possible. Resume the regular schedule even if the interval is less than 3 days. Do not administer two doses on the same day; however, doses on consecutive days are permissible.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to pegcetacoplan or any excipients (including sorbitol).
- Unresolved serious infections caused by encapsulated bacteria, including *Neisseria meningitidis*, *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, and *Haemophilus influenzae*.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning: Serious Infections Caused by Encapsulated Bacteria [Australian]
**Pegcetacoplan may predispose patients to life-threatening or fatal infections by encapsulated bacteria (e.g., *N. meningitidis*, *S. pneumoniae*, *H. influenzae* type B).**
- **Vaccination:** Patients must be vaccinated against these pathogens at least 2 weeks prior to therapy. 
- **Prophylaxis:** If urgent therapy is required in an unvaccinated patient, provide immediate vaccination and appropriate prophylactic antibiotics for 2 weeks.
- **Monitoring:** Vaccination reduces but does not eliminate risk. Monitor closely for early signs of sepsis.

### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis has been reported in postmarketing settings. Discontinue immediately if severe reactions occur.
- **Hemolysis Monitoring Post-Discontinuation:** PNH patients discontinuing treatment must be monitored for at least 8 weeks for signs of severe intravascular haemolysis (elevated LDH, fatigue, dyspnoea, or thrombosis).
- **Laboratory Interference:** Pegcetacoplan may interfere with silica-based reagents in coagulation panels, leading to artificially prolonged activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT). Avoid silica reagents for these tests.
- **Sorbitol:** Contains sorbitol; use caution in patients with hereditary fructose intolerance (HFI).

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Summary
| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site reactions (erythema, swelling, pruritus), nasopharyngitis, URTI, diarrhoea, abdominal pain, headache, fatigue, pyrexia, cough, arthralgia, hypokalaemia [Study APL2-308], viral infection. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | UTI, oral herpes, gastroenteritis, sinusitis, thrombocytopenia, anxiety, hypertension, epistaxis, oropharyngeal pain, myalgia, acute kidney injury, peripheral oedema. |
| **Postmarketing** | Anaphylaxis, anaphylactic shock, urticaria. |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Infections:** Reported in approximately 5-17% of PNH patients in trials. One death due to septic shock was reported in the APL2-308 study.
- **Haemolysis:** While pegcetacoplan reduces haemolysis, breakthrough events may occur, sometimes requiring dose adjustment.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No clinical studies performed. Nonclinical data suggests low potential for interaction; it does not inhibit/induce CYP450 enzymes and is not a substrate/inhibitor of major drug transporters.
- **Laboratory Tests:** Interference with silica-based aPTT assays (see Section 5).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. Data are insufficient to inform drug-associated risks. Animal studies (cynomolgus monkeys) showed increased stillbirths and abortions at 2.9 times clinical exposure. Use only if benefits outweigh risks. 
- **Contraception:** Recommended for females of reproductive potential during treatment and for at least 8 weeks after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Excretion in human milk is unknown. Excretion in monkey milk was minimal (<1%). Discontinuation of breastfeeding during treatment is recommended.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no microscopic abnormalities in reproductive organs, though specific fertility studies have not been conducted.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in children <18 years with PNH, or <12 years with C3G/IC-MPGN. No non-clinical safety data is available for children under 12.
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy observed, though clinical trial numbers for patients ≥65 years were limited.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for renal impairment, including severe impairment. No data available for end-stage renal disease requiring dialysis.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specifically studied; however, population PK data suggest no dose adjustment is necessary.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Pegcetacoplan is a complement inhibitor that binds to complement protein C3 and its activation fragment C3b. This regulates C3 cleavage and inhibits downstream effectors, acting proximal to both C3b (preventing extravascular haemolysis) and Membrane Attack Complex (MAC) formation (preventing intravascular haemolysis).
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In PNH, it increases C3 concentration and the percentage of PNH Type II + III RBCs while decreasing C3 deposition on RBCs. In C3G/IC-MPGN, it reduces C3c staining intensity on renal biopsy.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Median Tmax is 4.5 to 6.0 days. Steady-state is reached in 4–8 weeks.
    - **Distribution:** Mean central volume of distribution is ~3.98 L (PNH).
    - **Metabolism:** Expected to be degraded into small peptides and amino acids via catabolic pathways.
    - **Elimination:** Effective half-life (t1/2) is approximately 8.6 days (PNH) to 10.1 days (C3G/IC-MPGN). Primary route is likely urinary excretion.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **PNH (Study APL2-302):** In complement inhibitor-experienced patients, pegcetacoplan was superior to eculizumab in haemoglobin change from baseline at Week 16 (adjusted mean increase 3.8 g/dL vs eculizumab; p<0.0001). 85% achieved transfusion avoidance.
- **PNH (Study APL2-308):** In inhibitor-naïve patients, 85.7% achieved haemoglobin stabilisation vs 0% in the standard of care (SOC) group at Week 26.
- **C3G and IC-MPGN (Study APL2-C3G-310):** A Phase 3 study showed a 68.1% reduction in proteinuria (uPCR) at Week 26 compared to placebo (p<0.0001). 74.3% of patients showed a reduction of at least two orders of magnitude in C3c staining intensity on renal biopsy.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate).
- **Handling:** Do not freeze. Keep the vial in the original carton to protect from light. 
- **Administration:** Should be used within 2 hours of being drawn into a syringe.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction

- **Monitoring:** Subject to additional monitoring in Australia due to new indications.
