# Editorial Product Information: Pemetrexed

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of pemetrexed for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Clinicians should consult the current PI approved in their specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pemetrexed (as disodium heptahydrate).
- **Chemical Class:** Antifolate antineoplastic agent.
- **Brand Name(s):** ALIMTA [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01BA04.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile lyophilised powder for intravenous infusion in single-dose vials containing 100 mg or 500 mg of pemetrexed [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Eli Lilly Australia Pty. Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Malignant Pleural Mesothelioma:** Indicated in combination with cisplatin for the treatment of patients with malignant pleural mesothelioma [Australian-approved].
- **Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC):**
    - **Initial Treatment:** In combination with cisplatin for the first-line treatment of locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC (other than predominantly squamous cell histology) [Australian-approved].
    - **Maintenance Treatment:** Monotherapy for locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC (other than predominantly squamous cell histology) in patients whose disease has not progressed immediately following platinum-based chemotherapy [Australian-approved].
    - **Second-line Treatment:** Monotherapy for the treatment of locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC (other than predominantly squamous cell histology) after prior chemotherapy [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Recommended Dose:** 500 mg/m² body surface area (BSA) administered as an IV infusion over 10 minutes on Day 1 of each 21-day cycle [Australian].
- **Combination with Cisplatin:** Cisplatin 75 mg/m² BSA should be infused over 2 hours, starting approximately 30 minutes after the completion of the pemetrexed infusion. Patients must receive adequate anti-emetic treatment and hydration as per cisplatin protocols [Australian].

### Mandatory Premedication and Supplementation
To reduce the incidence and severity of haematologic and gastrointestinal toxicities, the following regimen is mandatory [Australian]:
- **Corticosteroids:** Dexamethasone 4 mg (or equivalent) orally twice daily on the day before, the day of, and the day after pemetrexed administration.
- **Folic Acid:** 350 to 1000 µg (commonly 400 µg) orally daily. At least five doses must be taken during the seven days preceding the first pemetrexed dose, continuing through the course of therapy and for 21 days after the final dose.
- **Vitamin B12:** 1000 µg intramuscularly (IM) during the week preceding the first dose and every three cycles thereafter. Subsequent injections may be given on the same day as pemetrexed.

### Monitoring and Dose Adjustments
- **Pre-treatment Requirements:** Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) $\ge$ 1500 cells/mm³ and platelets $\ge$ 100,000 cells/mm³ [Australian].
- **Dose Reductions:** Based on nadir haematologic counts or maximum non-haematologic toxicity in the preceding cycle. Reductions typically involve 75% or 50% of the previous dose for both pemetrexed and cisplatin. Therapy should be discontinued if Grade 3 or 4 neurotoxicity occurs [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Severe hypersensitivity to pemetrexed or any of the excipients (e.g., mannitol) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Myelosuppression:** Pemetrexed can suppress bone marrow function (anaemia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, and pancytopenia). This is usually the dose-limiting toxicity [Australian].
- **Renal Function:** Serious renal events, including acute renal failure, have been reported. Pemetrexed should not be administered if CrCl is < 45 mL/min [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal Toxicity:** Severe dehydration can occur due to GI toxicity, especially when combined with cisplatin. Adequate anti-emetic and hydration prophylaxis is essential [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Events:** Myocardial infarction and cerebrovascular accidents have been uncommonly reported, typically in patients with pre-existing risk factors [Australian].
- **Radiation Effects:** Radiation pneumonitis and radiation recall have been reported in patients receiving concurrent or prior radiotherapy [Australian].
- **Third Space Fluid:** The effect of pleural effusion or ascites on pemetrexed is unknown; consideration should be given to draining significant effusions prior to administration [Australian].
- **Vaccinations:** Use of live attenuated vaccines is not recommended in immunodepressed patients [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Frequency data is derived from clinical trials for NSCLC and mesothelioma [Australian].

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Neutropenia, anaemia, leukopenia, nausea, vomiting, fatigue, anorexia, stomatitis/pharyngitis, diarrhoea, rash/desquamation, sensory neuropathy, increased creatinine. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Thrombocytopenia, constipation, fever, ALT/AST elevation, pruritus, alopecia, conjunctivitis, dyspepsia, dehydration, motor neuropathy. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Supraventricular arrhythmias, sepsis (sometimes fatal), oesophagitis. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Interstitial pneumonitis, colitis, bullous conditions (Stevens-Johnson syndrome, Toxic epidermal necrolysis), immune-mediated haemolytic anaemia, radiation recall, hepatitis. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Renal Clearance:** Pemetrexed is primarily eliminated unchanged in the urine via glomerular filtration and tubular secretion.
- **Nephrotoxic Drugs:** Concomitant use of nephrotoxic drugs or substances that are tubularly secreted (e.g., probenecid, penicillin) may delay pemetrexed clearance [Australian].
- **OAT3 Inhibitors:** Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) may result in delayed pemetrexed clearance. Caution is advised when combining these agents [Australian].
- **NSAIDs:**
    - **Normal Renal Function (CrCl $\ge$ 80 mL/min):** Ibuprofen (moderate doses) may reduce pemetrexed clearance by 16%.
    - **Mild to Moderate Renal Impairment (CrCl 45-79 mL/min):** Avoid NSAIDs with short elimination half-lives for 2 days before and after pemetrexed. Avoid NSAIDs with long half-lives for 5 days before and 2 days after pemetrexed [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category D**. Pemetrexed is a suspected teratogen and should be avoided in pregnant women due to potential foetal hazard (e.g., cleft palate, embryofetal death) [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if pemetrexed is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding must be discontinued during therapy [Australian].
- **Fertility:** In animal models, pemetrexed caused reduced fertility, hypospermia, and testicular atrophy. Male patients are advised to seek advice on sperm storage [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients under 18 years; use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No evidence of increased risk in patients $\ge$ 65 years compared to younger patients. No specific dose adjustments are required beyond standard protocols [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No adjustments needed for CrCl $\ge$ 45 mL/min. Use is not recommended for patients with CrCl < 45 mL/min [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Pemetrexed is not extensively metabolised by the liver. Specific studies in patients with bilirubin > 1.5x ULN or significant transaminase elevation have not been conducted [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A multitargeted antifolate that inhibits three key enzymes in the de novo biosynthesis of thymidine and purine nucleotides: thymidylate synthase (TS), dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR), and glycinamide ribonucleotide formyltransferase (GARFT). This results in the inhibition of cell replication.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Steady-state volume of distribution is 16.1 L; 81% bound to plasma proteins.
    - **Metabolism:** Limited hepatic metabolism.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (70%–90% recovered unchanged in urine within 24 hours). Elimination half-life is approximately 3.5 hours in normal renal function [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **EMPHACIS (Mesothelioma):** A Phase 3 study of pemetrexed/cisplatin vs cisplatin alone showed a median survival of 13.3 months vs 10.0 months in fully supplemented patients [Australian].
- **JMEN (Maintenance NSCLC):** Showed superior overall survival (13.4 vs 10.6 months) and progression-free survival for pemetrexed vs placebo in non-squamous NSCLC [Australian].
- **PARAMOUNT (Continuation Maintenance):** Confirmed survival benefit of continuing pemetrexed after induction with pemetrexed/cisplatin [Australian].
- **Second-line NSCLC:** A study vs docetaxel showed comparable efficacy (median survival 8.3 vs 7.9 months) but a more favourable toxicity profile for pemetrexed [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store below 25°C. The product is not light-sensitive.
- **Reconstituted/Infusion Solution:** Contains no antimicrobial preservatives. Chemical and physical stability demonstrated for 24 hours when refrigerated (2°C to 8°C). Discard any unused portion [Australian].
- **Incompatibilities:** Physically incompatible with diluents containing calcium (e.g., Lactated Ringer’s). Use only 0.9% Sodium Chloride for reconstitution and dilution [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
