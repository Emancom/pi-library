# Editorial Product Information: Paliperidone

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data, regulatory requirements, and pharmacological profiles into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory authorities for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Paliperidone (INN); a benzisoxazole-derivative atypical antipsychotic.
- **Brand Name(s):** **INVEGA** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N05AX13.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Modified-release tablets: 3 mg, 6 mg, 9 mg, and 12 mg [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Delivery Technology:** The tablets utilise an osmotic drug-release technology (OROS) consisting of a tri-layer core and a semipermeable membrane. The non-absorbable shell is eliminated in the stool [Australian].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Janssen-Cilag Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Schizophrenia:** Indicated for acute treatment and the prevention of recurrence [Australian].
- **Schizoaffective Disorder:** Indicated for the treatment of acute exacerbations as monotherapy or in combination with antidepressants and/or mood stabilisers (specifically lithium or valproate) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** 
    - **Standardisation:** Administration should be standardised in relation to food. Patients must either always take the dose while fasting or always take it with breakfast; alternating between fed and fasting states is not recommended [Australian].
    - **Administration:** Tablets must be swallowed whole with liquid. They must not be chewed, crushed, or divided [Australian].
- **Schizophrenia:**
    - **Recommended Dose:** 6 mg once daily, administered in the morning.
    - **Titration:** Initial titration is not required. Adjustments (range: 3–12 mg/day) should occur after clinical reassessment at intervals greater than 5 days, in 3 mg/day increments [Australian].
- **Schizoaffective Disorder:**
    - **Recommended Dose:** 6 mg once daily, administered in the morning.
    - **Titration:** Range: 3–12 mg/day. Adjustments should occur after clinical reassessment at intervals greater than 4 days, in 3 mg/day increments [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available source.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to paliperidone, risperidone (paliperidone is the major active metabolite of risperidone), or any excipient in the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in provided source.
- **QT Prolongation:** Caution is required in patients with known cardiovascular disease, family history of QT prolongation, or when used with other QTc-prolonging drugs.
- **Tardive Dyskinesia & Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** As with all antipsychotics, there is a risk of potentially irreversible tardive dyskinesia or fatal NMS. Discontinue treatment if suspected.
- **Extrapyramidal Symptoms (EPS):** Monitor for akathisia, dystonia, and parkinsonism. Caution when co-administered with psychostimulants (e.g., methylphenidate), as EPS may emerge during dose adjustments.
- **Metabolic Risks:** Hyperglycaemia and diabetes mellitus (rarely including ketoacidosis or coma) and significant weight gain have been reported. Regular monitoring of glucose and weight is recommended.
- **Haematological:** Leukopenia, neutropoenia, and agranulocytosis. Discontinue if absolute neutrophil count is < 1 x 10⁹/L.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Because the tablet is non-deformable, it should not be administered to patients with severe pre-existing GI narrowing (e.g., oesophageal motility disorders, "short gut" syndrome, or strictures) [Australian].
- **Orthostatic Hypotension:** Alpha-blocking activity may cause syncope or hypotension; use with caution in patients with known cardiovascular disease.
- **Hyperprolactinaemia:** Paliperidone elevates prolactin levels; use with caution in patients with previously detected breast cancer or pituitary tumours.
- **Intraoperative Floppy Iris Syndrome (IFIS):** Observed during cataract surgery in patients treated with alpha1a-adrenergic antagonists, including paliperidone.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Headache [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Tachycardia, sinus tachycardia, akathisia, dizziness, extrapyramidal disorder, somnolence, tremor, dystonia, hypertonia, sedation, orthostatic hypotension, vomiting, upper abdominal pain, dry mouth, salivary hypersecretion, asthenia, and fatigue [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Syncope, bundle branch block, sinus arrhythmia, and parkinsonism [Australian].
- **Rare (<0.1%) / Post-marketing:** Agranulocytosis, anaphylactic reaction, priapism, venous thromboembolism (deep vein thrombosis/pulmonary embolism), and angioedema [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **Centrally Acting Drugs/Alcohol:** Potential for additive CNS effects.
    - **Levodopa/Dopamine Agonists:** Paliperidone may antagonise their effects.
    - **QTc-Prolonging Drugs:** Caution with Class IA and Class III antiarrhythmics.
    - **Risperidone:** Concomitant use is not recommended due to additive exposure (paliperidone is the active metabolite of risperidone) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **Carbamazepine:** Co-administration (200 mg BID) decreases paliperidone Cmax and AUC by ~37%. Dosing of paliperidone may need re-evaluation upon starting or stopping carbamazepine.
    - **Divalproex Sodium:** Increases paliperidone Cmax and AUC by ~50%. Consider dose reduction of paliperidone if co-administered.
    - **CYP450:** Paliperidone is not a significant inhibitor or inducer of major CYP enzymes. It is metabolised to a limited extent by CYP2D6 and CYP3A4.
    - **P-gp:** Paliperidone is a substrate of P-glycoprotein.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category C]. Antipsychotic exposure during the third trimester carries a risk of extrapyramidal and/or withdrawal symptoms in neonates (e.g., agitation, respiratory distress, somnolence). Use only if benefits outweigh risks.
- **Breastfeeding:** Paliperidone is excreted in breast milk. Due to potential adverse effects on the infant, breastfeeding is not recommended [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed decreased testosterone and sperm parameters (risperidone data) and increased pre-implantation loss (paliperidone data).

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients < 18 years have not been established; use is not recommended in this group [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Generally same as adults; however, dosing must be adjusted based on renal function. There is an increased risk of death and cerebrovascular events in elderly patients with dementia-related psychosis (not an approved indication) [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Mild (CrCl 50 to <80 mL/min):** Initial 3 mg once daily; may increase based on response.
    - **Moderate (CrCl 30 to <50 mL/min):** Recommended dose is 3 mg once daily.
    - **Severe (CrCl 10 to <30 mL/min):** Initial 3 mg **every other day**; may increase to 3 mg once daily after reassessment.
    - **CrCl <10 mL/min:** Use not recommended [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild-to-Moderate:** No adjustment required.
    - **Severe:** Not studied; caution recommended.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Central dopamine D2 and serotonin 5-HT2A receptor antagonism. It also acts as an antagonist at α1 and α2 adrenergic and H1 histaminergic receptors, with no affinity for cholinergic or β-receptors.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Absolute oral bioavailability is 28%. Cmax is reached ~24 hours post-dose.
    - **Distribution:** 74% protein binding; primarily to α1-acid glycoprotein and albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Limited hepatic metabolism.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (59% excreted unchanged in urine). Terminal half-life is ~23 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Schizophrenia:** Three 6-week, placebo-controlled trials established efficacy using the Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale (PANSS). Doses from 3–15 mg/day were superior to placebo. A long-term maintenance trial showed significantly longer time to recurrence compared to placebo [Australian].
- **Schizoaffective Disorder:** Two 6-week trials (monotherapy and combination therapy) showed that doses of 3–12 mg/day improved PANSS scores significantly better than placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C. Keep the bottle/blister in the outer carton to protect from moisture [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].
