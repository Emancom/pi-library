# Editorial Product Information: Parecoxib

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. 
- **Note:** At the time of this editorial preparation, documents from other major regulatory authorities (e.g., US FDA, European EMA, Health Canada) were not provided for comparison. Therefore, this document reflects the harmonised position based on the available Australian data.
- **Purpose:** This document is intended as a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Parecoxib (as parecoxib sodium).
- **Chemical Class:** Sulfonamide; selective cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2) inhibitor; prodrug of valdecoxib.
- **Brand Name(s):** Dynastat [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** M01AH04.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Powder for injection; 40 mg vial (as 42.36 mg parecoxib sodium).
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) or Intramuscular (IM).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Post-operative Pain:** Management of acute post-operative pain as a single peri-operative dose [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The standard recommended dose is a single 40 mg administration.
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - **IV Bolus:** May be administered rapidly and directly into a vein or existing IV line.
    - **IM Injection:** Should be administered slowly and deeply into the muscle.
    - **Reconstitution:** Dissolve the 40 mg vial in 2 mL of sodium chloride (0.9% w/v).
    - **Compatible Diluents:** 0.9% Sodium Chloride, 5% Glucose, or 0.45% Sodium Chloride with 5% Glucose.
    - **Incompatibilities:** Do not use Sterile Water for Injections (not isotonic). Do not reconstitute with Lactated Ringer’s or 5% Glucose in Lactated Ringer's as precipitation will occur.
- **Titration:** Not applicable for single-dose indication.
- **Missed Dose:** Not applicable for single-dose peri-operative use.

## 4. Contraindications
Parecoxib is contraindicated in patients:
- Undergoing coronary artery bypass graft (CABG) surgery or other major vascular surgery [Australian].
- With established ischemic heart disease, peripheral arterial disease, or cerebrovascular disease [Australian].
- With known hypersensitivity to parecoxib, valdecoxib, or any excipients.
- Who have experienced asthma, urticaria, or allergic-type reactions after taking aspirin or other NSAIDs (including COX-2 inhibitors).
- With a history of allergic-type reactions to sulfonamides.
- With severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh score ≥10).
- In the third trimester of pregnancy or who are breastfeeding.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** Like all NSAIDs, parecoxib may increase the risk of serious cardiovascular thrombotic events, myocardial infarction, and stroke. Risk is highest in patients undergoing CABG surgery (contraindicated) and those with significant CV risk factors.
- **Serious Skin Reactions:** Severe cutaneous adverse reactions, including Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), and Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS), have been reported. Discontinue at the first sign of rash or mucosal lesions.
- **Gastrointestinal Effects:** Risk of upper GI ulceration, perforation, and bleeding. Caution is required in patients with a history of GI disease or those taking concomitant anticoagulants, corticosteroids, or SSRIs.
- **Anaphylactoid Reactions:** May occur regardless of prior exposure; contraindicated in the "aspirin triad."
- **Hypertension:** Can lead to new-onset or worsening of pre-existing hypertension; monitor blood pressure closely.
- **Fluid Retention and Edema:** Caution in patients with compromised cardiac function or conditions predisposing to fluid retention.
- **Masking of Fever:** May hide signs of infection at the surgical site.
- **Severe Hypotension:** Reported shortly after administration in post-marketing settings.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Category: Very Common (>10%)
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea.

### Frequency Category: Common (1–10%)
- **Gastrointestinal:** Abdominal pain, constipation, dyspepsia, vomiting.
- **General:** Peripheral edema.
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness, hypoaesthesia.
- **Psychiatric:** Insomnia.
- **Respiratory:** Respiratory insufficiency.
- **Infections:** Alveolar osteitis (dry socket).
- **Vascular:** Hypotension.
- **Blood:** Post-operative anaemia.
- **Metabolic:** Hypokalaemia.
- **Renal:** Oliguria.
- **Skin:** Increased sweating, pruritus.

### Frequency Category: Uncommon (0.1–1%)
- **Cardiac:** Bradycardia, myocardial infarction.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastroduodenal ulceration, dry mouth, flatulence, gastroesophageal reflux.
- **Infections:** Wound infection, pharyngitis.
- **Nervous System:** Cerebrovascular disorder.
- **General:** Injection site pain/reaction, asthenia.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Acute renal failure.
- SJS/TEN/DRESS.
- Anaphylaxis and angioedema.
- Pulmonary embolism.
- Congestive heart failure and circulatory collapse.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Fluconazole/Ketoconazole:** Fluconazole increases valdecoxib exposure by 62%; reduce parecoxib dose in patients on fluconazole [Australian].
- **Anticoagulants (Warfarin):** Increased risk of bleeding; monitor INR closely, especially in the first few days.
- **Lithium:** NSAIDs decrease lithium renal clearance; monitor serum lithium levels.
- **ACE Inhibitors, ARBs, and Diuretics:** Co-administration increases risk of acute renal failure, particularly in elderly or volume-depleted patients ("triple whammy").
- **Methotrexate:** NSAIDs may increase methotrexate plasma levels; monitor for toxicity.
- **Ciclosporin:** Increased risk of nephrotoxicity.
- **Aspirin:** Can be used with low-dose aspirin, but risk of GI ulceration increases; parecoxib is not a substitute for aspirin in CV prophylaxis.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category C [Australian].** 
    - Contraindicated in the **third trimester** due to risk of premature closure of the ductus arteriosus and uterine inertia. 
    - Use in the first and second trimesters should be avoided unless benefits outweigh risks; use the lowest dose for the shortest duration.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Parecoxib and its active metabolite are excreted in human milk.
- **Fertility:** NSAIDs may delay or prevent ovulation, potentially causing reversible infertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not studied in patients under 18 years; use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Generally no dose adjustment needed. However, in elderly females weighing **<50 kg**, the dose should be reduced to **20 mg**.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild-to-severe impairment based on pharmacokinetics, but initiate with caution and monitor renal function closely.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild (Child-Pugh 5-6):** No adjustment.
    - **Moderate (Child-Pugh 7-9):** Reduce dose by half (20 mg).
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh ≥10):** Contraindicated.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Parecoxib is a prodrug of valdecoxib. Valdecoxib is a selective COX-2 inhibitor that blocks prostaglandin synthesis. It does not inhibit COX-1 at therapeutic concentrations.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly converted to valdecoxib; peak levels of valdecoxib reached in ~30 min (IV) or ~1 hour (IM).
    - **Distribution:** Valdecoxib is 98% protein bound; Volume of distribution ~55 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Hepatic via CYP3A4, CYP2C9, and glucuronidation.
    - **Elimination:** Valdecoxib half-life is ~8 hours; excreted primarily as inactive metabolites in urine (70%) and faeces.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Onset of analgesia occurs within 7–14 minutes, peaking within 2 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Surgical Pain:** Efficacy established in oral, gynaecologic (hysterectomy), and orthopaedic (hip/knee replacement) models. 
- **Comparative Efficacy:** A 40 mg dose provided analgesia comparable to ketorolac 60 mg IM or 30 mg IV.
- **Pre-emptive Dosing:** Administration 30–45 minutes prior to surgery significantly delayed the need for rescue medication post-operatively.
- **CABG Safety:** Two studies showed a significantly higher incidence of CV/thromboembolic events and surgical wound complications in parecoxib-treated patients compared to placebo.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store below 25°C. Protect from light.
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Use as soon as practicable; do not store in a refrigerator or freezer.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
