# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory jurisdiction:
    - **United States (FDA)**: Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation)
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It synthesises available data into a clinically concise format.
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved PI for legal prescribing requirements. 
- *Note: Only the US (FDA) document was provided for this synthesis. Information from other regions is marked as "Not specified in available sources" where applicable.*

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Levocarnitine (also known as L-carnitine).
- **Chemical Class**: A naturally occurring amino acid derivative; a carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s)**: Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code**: A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s)**:
    - **Intravenous Injection**: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration**: Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism**: For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD)**: For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders**:
    - **Recommended Dose**: 50 mg/kg, administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose**: Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
    - **Frequency**: Administer every 3 or 4 hours; should not be administered less frequently than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Maximum Dose**: Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis**:
    - **Recommended Starting Dose**: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
    - **Administration**: Administer as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
    - **Dose Adjustment**: Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) can be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

### Monitoring
- **Metabolic Disorders**: Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- **ESRD**: Monitor trough (pre-dialysis) concentrations (normal range: 40–50 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions**: Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Activity**: Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions**: The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been established in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- Monitor INR in patients concurrently receiving warfarin [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects observed in clinical trials of ESRD patients (frequency ≥5% regardless of causality) include [US]:

| Body System | Adverse Event (Frequency ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, back pain, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, palpitations |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, anorexia, dyspepsia |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight changes |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia, depression |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis |
| **Skin** | Pruritus, rash |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Anaphylaxis/Hypersensitivity**: Potential for life-threatening respiratory distress or systemic collapse [US].
- **Neurologic**: Seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin**: Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used with levocarnitine. **Management**: Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions**: Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **Category**: US Category C (Not explicitly lettered in text, but describes risk-benefit assessment). 
    - **Data**: Animal studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate/well-controlled human studies exist. Use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentration after exogenous administration. Weigh the risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefit of supplementation for the mother [US].
- **Fertility**: No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Dosing for metabolic disorders follows the mg/kg weight-based protocol (see Section 3) [US].
- **Geriatric Use**: Not specifically detailed in the provided source.
- **Renal Impairment**: Specifically indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis (see Section 3). Caution is advised regarding the accumulation of metabolites if the oral formulation is used in renal impairment [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution**: Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Initial distribution half-life is approximately 0.585 hours [US].
- **Metabolism**: Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination**: Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours [US].

### Pharmacodynamics
- In patients with inborn errors of metabolism, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly cleared [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies**: In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine levels from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. While increased concentrations are proven, the direct effect on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, malaise) in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling**: Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat. Discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].
- **Compatibility**: Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
