# Editorial Product Information: Protamine Sulfate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Protamine Sulfate (Salmine); a basic protein derived from fish sperm.
- **Brand Names:** Fisons Protamine Sulfate [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** V03AB14.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 1% solution for injection (50 mg in 5 mL glass ampoules) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Slow intravenous (IV) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** sanofi-aventis australia pty ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Protamine Sulfate** is indicated for the neutralisation of the anticoagulant effect of heparin in the following clinical scenarios [Australian]:
- Prior to surgery.
- Following renal dialysis.
- Following open-heart surgery.
- Management of heparin overdose or excessive bleeding.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
The dose is determined by the quantity of heparin requiring neutralisation.
- **Neutralisation Ratio:** 1 mg of protamine sulfate typically neutralises at least 100 international units (IU) of mucous heparin or 80 units of lung heparin [Australian].
- **Time-Dependent Adjustments:** Because heparin is continuously excreted, the required dose of protamine should be reduced if more than 15 minutes have elapsed since the initial heparin administration [Australian].
- **Maximum Dose:** No more than 50 mg (one 5 mL ampoule) should be administered at any single time [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Rate of Administration:** Must be given by slow intravenous injection over approximately 10 minutes [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Ideally, dosing should be guided by blood clotting time determinations to ensure an acceptable level of neutralisation.
- **Caution:** In gross excess, protamine itself exerts an anticoagulant effect [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to protamine sulfate or any excipients [Australian].
- Known hypersensitivity to **Neutral Protamine Hagedorn (NPH) insulin**, as it contains protamine [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Rapid Administration Risk:** Administering the drug too quickly may lead to severe hypotension and anaphylactoid reactions. Resuscitation and shock treatment facilities must be readily available [Australian].
- **Anaphylaxis:** Fatal cases of anaphylactic reactions have occurred. 
- **Ineffectiveness:** Protamine is not suitable for the reversal of oral anticoagulants [Australian].

### Risk Groups for Allergic Reactions
Caution is advised in patients at increased risk of protamine allergy, including [Australian]:
- Patients who have previously received protamine (e.g., during coronary angioplasty or cardiopulmonary bypass).
- Diabetics treated with protamine-containing insulin (NPH).
- Individuals with a known allergy to fish.
- Men who have undergone a vasectomy or are infertile (potential presence of protamine antibodies).

### Clinical Precautions
- **Rebound Bleeding:** A rebound anticoagulant effect may occur up to 18 hours post-operatively. This requires careful monitoring of clotting parameters and may respond to further protamine doses [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Continuous monitoring of clotting parameters is necessary for patients undergoing prolonged procedures involving repeated dosing [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following adverse effects have been reported following the administration of protamine sulfate [Australian]:

| System Organ Class | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Immune System** | Hypersensitivity reactions (including shock), fatal anaphylaxis. |
| **Vascular** | Hypotension (sudden fall in BP), acute pulmonary arterial hypertension, systemic hypertension, transitory flushing, sensation of warmth. |
| **Respiratory** | Non-cardiogenic pulmonary oedema (sometimes fatal), dyspnoea. |
| **Cardiac** | Bradycardia. |
| **Blood & Lymphatic** | Thrombocytopenia (sometimes with antibody formation). |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea, vomiting. |
| **Musculoskeletal** | Back pain. |
| **General Disorders** | Lassitude. |
| **Procedural** | Post-procedural haemorrhage (rebound effect). |

**Note:** If used in excess of the amount required to neutralise heparin, protamine sulfate exerts its own anticoagulant effect [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** No specific drug interactions are known [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category B2. There are limited data on use in pregnant women; use is not recommended unless the clinical benefit clearly outweighs the potential risks [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if protamine or its metabolites are excreted in human milk. Due to potential risks to the infant, use is not recommended during breastfeeding [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children have not been established; use in this population is not recommended [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No specific data provided in the available source [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Protamine is a basic protein that combines with heparin (an acidic substance) to form a stable, inactive complex (salt), thereby neutralising heparin’s anticoagulant activity [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** When administered alone, protamine possesses its own anticoagulant properties.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Not specified in available source.
