# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided for the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- Information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian, Japanese) was not provided in the source material; therefore, clinical recommendations and regulatory classifications are presented primarily from the US perspective.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP); chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for mitochondrial fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the next 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
- **Dose Range:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses may be required. The maximum recorded dose is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Adjustments:** Dosage adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations [US].

### Administration Notes
- Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None Known:** There are no listed contraindications in the available US source material [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Serious Clinical Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including **anaphylaxis**, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These often occur within minutes of intravenous administration, primarily in ESRD patients. Treatment must be discontinued immediately if a reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].

### General Precautions
- **Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, plasma carnitine levels should be obtained prior to therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring (including blood chemistry and vital signs) is recommended. Target free carnitine levels should be 35–60 µmol/L [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulations):** While this PI concerns the injectable form, clinicians should note that chronic high-dose *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported at a frequency of ≥5% regardless of causality in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials include:

| Body System | Adverse Event (Incidence ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain. |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia. |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, dyspepsia, nausea, vomiting. |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia. |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, depression. |
| **Respiratory** | Cough increase, dyspnea, pharyngitis, rhinitis. |

### Other Adverse Effects
- **Common:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent reports of body odor and gastritis.
- **Serious Post-marketing Reports:** Seizures, anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Clinical Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine is not plasma protein bound [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category B equivalent]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. However, there are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal constituent of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. In dairy cows, exogenous carnitine increases milk concentrations. Clinicians must weigh the benefits to the mother against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for children is included in the metabolic disorder recommendations (50 mg/kg). Specific age-related secondary deficiency in neonates is defined by plasma levels below age-related norms [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** In ESRD patients on dialysis, I.V. administration is indicated. Oral formulations should be avoided in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, it alleviates metabolic abnormalities resulting from the accumulation of toxic organic acids (acyl-CoA compounds).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound; mean distribution half-life of 0.585 hours.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an I.V. dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg I.V. post-dialysis increased mean plasma carnitine concentrations from ~20 µmol/L to trough levels between 54 and 180 µmol/L after one week of therapy. While I.V. levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations, clinical outcomes regarding signs and symptoms of deficiency in the ESRD population have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C.
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].
