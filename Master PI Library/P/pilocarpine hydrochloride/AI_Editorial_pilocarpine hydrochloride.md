# Editorial Product Information: pilocarpine hydrochloride

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **pilocarpine hydrochloride**
- Chemical class: Cholinergic muscarinic agonist
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Ophthalmic (topical)

## 2. Regulatory History
- First marketing/approval date (as stated in available source material): 10 October 2024 / 15 October 2024 (marketing dates listed in source documents).

- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.  
- If additional jurisdictional registration dates or regulatory events are required: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of presbyopia in adults. [US]

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General adult dosing
- Instill one drop in each eye. A second instillation may be given 2 to 3 hours after the first dose to extend effect up to approximately 8 hours. Dosing may be performed once daily or as needed, up to twice daily.

### Dosing by indication
- Presbyopia (adults): as above.

### Administration instructions
- If more than one topical ophthalmic medication is to be used, administer each medication at least 5 minutes apart.
- Avoid touching the tip of the applicator/vial to the eye or any surface to decrease risk of eye injury or contamination.
- For single-use vial presentations: discard vial after one use. (Storage/handling specifics beyond clinical administration are not provided here.)

### Titration schedules

### Missed dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to pilocarpine hydrochloride or to any excipients contained in the product.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- **Blurred vision / accommodative spasm**: Pilocarpine may cause accommodative spasm and temporary blurred vision. Advise patients not to drive or operate machinery while vision is unclear. Exercise caution with night driving or performing hazardous tasks in low illumination.
- **Risk of retinal detachment**: Rare cases of retinal detachment have been reported with miotic agents in susceptible individuals and in patients with pre-existing retinal disease. A retinal examination is recommended prior to initiating therapy. Patients should be instructed to seek immediate medical evaluation for sudden onset of flashes, floaters, or vision loss.
- **Iritis / anterior uveitis**: Use is not recommended when iritis is present because anterior synechiae (adhesions between iris and lens) may form.
- **Contact lens wear**: Remove contact lenses prior to instillation; wait at least 10 minutes after dosing before reinsertion.
- **Potential for eye injury or contamination**: Avoid contact between vial tip and ocular surface or other surfaces to reduce risk of injury or contamination.
- **Systemic cholinergic effects (sensitivity/overdose)**: Systemic exposure after ocular use is generally low, but susceptible individuals may develop systemic parasympathetic effects (e.g., sweating, gastrointestinal overactivity). Severe poisoning from accidental ingestion or high systemic exposure requires appropriate medical management (see Overdosage and Drug Interactions).
- Monitoring recommendations:
  - Retinal examination prior to initiation in patients at risk for retinal disease.
  - Monitor visual function; counsel patients to report new visual symptoms (flashes, floaters, sudden vision loss).

- Most commonly reported treatment-related adverse events: instillation site pain and headache (reported in approximately 5–8% of patients).

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Pharmacokinetic interactions: Not specified in available sources.
- Pharmacodynamic and other interactions:
  - Atropine is the pharmacologic antagonist to pilocarpine and should be used in cases of severe systemic pilocarpine poisoning or overdose.
  - When administering multiple topical ophthalmic agents, separate instillations by at least 5 minutes to prevent washout and reduce interaction by physical displacement.
- Region-specific interaction guidance: Not specified in available sources beyond the above.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Formal pregnancy category: Not specified in available sources.
- Risk summary: There are no adequate and well-controlled studies of topical ocular pilocarpine in pregnant women. Oral administration of pilocarpine in animal studies produced maternal toxicity and fetal effects at doses substantially higher than the maximum recommended human ophthalmic dose (MRHOD) on a mg/m² basis.
- Animal data: In rats, oral pilocarpine during organogenesis produced maternal toxicity, skeletal anomalies and decreased fetal weight at 90 mg/kg/day (≈1200-fold higher than MRHOD on mg/m² basis). Peri-/postnatal rat studies showed increased stillbirths and decreased neonatal survival at doses substantially higher than MRHOD.

### Breastfeeding
- Risk summary: Data on the presence of pilocarpine in human milk are lacking. Pilocarpine and/or metabolites are excreted in the milk of lactating rats following oral dosing; systemic exposure with topical ocular administration is low, and it is unknown whether measurable levels occur in human milk after ocular use. Consider the benefits of breastfeeding, the mother's clinical need for treatment, and potential risks to the infant.

### Fertility
- Animal data indicate impaired reproductive function at high oral doses in rats (e.g., reduced fertility, decreased sperm motility, abnormal sperm at 18 mg/kg/day, ≈240-fold MRHOD on mg/m² basis). Dogs showed impaired spermatogenesis after prolonged exposure at doses substantially greater than the MRHOD.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Presbyopia is an age-related condition not present in pediatric populations. Safety and effectiveness in pediatric patients have not been established. [Information in sources: presbyopia does not occur in pediatric population.]

### Elderly use
- Clinical studies did not include subjects aged 65 years and older to determine differential response. Other reported clinical experience with ophthalmic pilocarpine solutions has not identified overall differences in safety between elderly and younger patients. Safety and effectiveness have not been established in patients over 65 years.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **Pilocarpine hydrochloride** is a muscarinic cholinergic agonist that stimulates muscarinic receptors on the iris sphincter and ciliary muscle. Contraction of the iris sphincter constricts the pupil, increasing depth of focus and thereby improving near visual acuity while preserving some pupillary light response.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Systemic absorption after topical ocular administration is low but measurable. In one evaluation (12 healthy subjects), following twice-daily ocular dosing (2 doses separated by 2 hours, for 8 days):
  - Median time to detectable plasma concentration (Tmax) after Day 8: ~2.20 hours.
  - Mean (SD) plasma Cmax after Day 8: approximately 897.2 (287.2) pg/mL.
  - Mean AUC0–t after Day 8: approximately 2699 (741.4) hr·pg/mL.
  - Mean elimination half-life (t1/2) after Day 8: approximately 3.96 hours.
- Distribution, detailed metabolism, and routes of elimination after topical ocular dosing: Not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Causes pupillary constriction and enhanced accommodation via muscarinic receptor activation on iris sphincter and ciliary muscle, producing improved near vision.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Efficacy was demonstrated in two randomized, double-masked, vehicle-controlled phase 3 studies (NEAR-1 and NEAR-2).
  - Total randomized across studies: 613 participants aged 45 to 64 years; 309 received pilocarpine solution.
  - Treatment regimen in trials: one drop in each eye once in the morning with a repeat instillation 2 to 3 hours later; treatment duration in studies was two weeks.
  - Primary responder definition: improvement from baseline of ≥3 lines in near best distance-corrected visual acuity (BDCVA) at 40 cm without loss of ≥1 line (≥5 letters) in distance BDCVA at 4 meters.
  - Key efficacy results (Day 8; percentages of responders):
    - NEAR-1 (N=155 pilocarpine; N=154 vehicle):
      - 1 hour post Dose 1: 39% vs 17% (p < 0.01)
      - 2 hours post Dose 1: 39% vs 17% (p < 0.01)
      - 1 hour post Dose 2: 48% vs 16% (p < 0.01)
      - 2 hours post Dose 2: 39% vs 15% (p < 0.01)
    - NEAR-2 (N=154 pilocarpine; N=150 vehicle):
      - 1 hour post Dose 1: 42% vs 21% (p < 0.01)
      - 2 hours post Dose 1: 40% vs 21% (p < 0.01)
      - 1 hour post Dose 2: 52% vs 17% (p < 0.01)
      - 2 hours post Dose 2: 46% vs 19% (p < 0.01)
  - Time to onset and duration: Effect onset observed from approximately 20 minutes after dose 1, with effect duration up to 8 hours (approximately 5 hours after the second dose).
  - Safety in trials: Most reported adverse events were mild and transient; most common were instillation site pain and headache (5–8%), and blurred vision (2–5%).

## 13. Overdosage
- Systemic toxicity following ocular use is uncommon but can occur in sensitive individuals. Signs of systemic cholinergic overexposure include sweating, increased salivation, nausea, tremor, bradycardia, and hypotension.
- Accidental oral ingestion can produce sweating, salivation, nausea, tremors, bradycardia and hypotension. Moderate overdosage: spontaneous recovery is possible with supportive care (e.g., intravenous fluids for dehydration). Severe poisoning: atropine is the pharmacologic antagonist and should be used as indicated.
- Specific antidote dosing: Not specified in available sources.

## 14. Nonclinical Toxicology (selected findings)
- Carcinogenesis: Lifetime oral carcinogenicity studies in mice and rats did not demonstrate tumorigenesis in mice up to 30 mg/kg/day (≈200-fold MRHOD on mg/m² basis). In rats, an oral dose of 18 mg/kg/day (≈240-fold MRHOD on mg/m² basis) produced statistically significant increases in benign pheochromocytomas in both sexes and hepatocellular adenomas in females.
- Mutagenesis: Pilocarpine was negative in a battery of genotoxicity assays, including bacterial reverse mutation tests, in vitro chromosome aberration assay, an in vivo micronucleus test in mice, and an unscheduled DNA synthesis assay in rat hepatocytes.
- Impairment of fertility: Oral administration in rats at 18 mg/kg/day (≈240-fold MRHOD on mg/m² basis) produced impaired reproductive function (reduced fertility, decreased sperm motility, abnormal sperm). Dogs exposed to 3 mg/kg/day for 6 months showed impaired spermatogenesis at exposures substantially exceeding the MRHOD.

## 15. Patient Counseling Information (summary for clinicians to convey)
- Counsel patients that the medication may cause temporary blurred, dim, or dark vision and advise against driving or operating machinery while vision is unclear; exercise caution with night driving.
- Advise patients to remove contact lenses prior to instillation and to wait at least 10 minutes after dosing before reinsertion.
- Instruct patients to avoid touching the vial tip to the eye or other surfaces to reduce risk of contamination or eye injury.
- Advise patients to seek immediate medical attention for sudden onset of flashes, floaters, or vision loss.
- Inform patients about the possibility of mild, transient ocular discomfort and headache.
