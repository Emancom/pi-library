# Editorial Product Information: Pentobarbital Sodium

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: US (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of available product information for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial reference and does not replace the official PI or summary of product characteristics required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pentobarbital sodium (a substituted pyrimidine derivative; short-acting barbiturate).
- **Brand Names:** Pentobarbital Sodium Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** N05CA01 (not explicitly stated in source, but standard for the class).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 50 mg/mL [US].
    - Available in 20 mL (1000 mg) and 50 mL (2500 mg) multiple-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV), Intramuscular (IM). 
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Hikma Pharmaceuticals USA Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Sedation:** Utilized as a sedative agent [US].
- **Hypnosis:** Short-term treatment of insomnia (efficacy typically lost after 2 weeks of use) [US].
- **Preanesthetic Medication:** Administered prior to surgical procedures [US].
- **Anticonvulsant:** Emergency control of acute convulsive episodes (e.g., status epilepticus, eclampsia, meningitis, tetanus, or toxic reactions to strychnine or local anesthetics) [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
Dosage must be strictly individualized based on age, weight, and clinical condition. Parenteral administration is reserved for cases where oral administration is impractical [US].

### Intramuscular (IM) Administration
- **Adults:** 150 mg to 200 mg as a single injection [US].
- **Pediatrics:** 2 mg/kg to 6 mg/kg as a single injection (maximum dose not to exceed 100 mg) [US].
- **Instructions:** Inject deeply into a large muscle. Do not exceed 5 mL at a single injection site to minimize tissue irritation [US].

### Intravenous (IV) Administration
- **General Instruction:** Slow injection is mandatory. Do not exceed a rate of 50 mg/minute [US].
- **Adults (70 kg):** Common initial dose is 100 mg. At least one minute should pass to assess the full effect. If required, small increments may be given up to a total of 200 mg to 500 mg [US].
- **Anticonvulsant Use:** Dosage should be kept to the minimum required to avoid compounding post-convulsive depression [US].

### Administration Notes
- **Inspection:** Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration. Do not use if the material has precipitated [US].
- **Incompatibility:** Pentobarbital should not be admixed with other medications or solutions [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity or sensitivity to barbiturates [US].
- History of manifest or latent porphyria [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Habit-Forming Potential:** Pentobarbital may be habit-forming. Tolerance, psychological dependence, and physical dependence may occur with prolonged use. Abrupt cessation in dependent patients can result in delirium, convulsions, or death [US].
- **Rapid IV Administration:** Excessively rapid injection may cause severe respiratory depression, apnea, laryngospasm, or significant vasodilation with a drop in blood pressure [US].
- **Pediatric Neurotoxicity:** Exposure to sedative/anesthetic drugs for >3 hours or repeated exposures in children <3 years old may negatively impact brain development and lead to long-term cognitive or behavioral deficits [US].
- **Alkalinity and Tissue Damage:** Solutions are highly alkaline (pH ~9.5). Care must be taken to avoid extravasation or intra-arterial injection, which can lead to tissue necrosis or gangrene [US].
- **Acute or Chronic Pain:** Paradoxical excitement may occur; barbiturates may mask important symptoms in patients with pain [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are listed by organ system and estimated frequency based on surveillance of hospitalized patients.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (>1%)** | Somnolence (1% to 3%) |
| **Uncommon/Rare (<1%)** | **Nervous:** Agitation, confusion, hyperkinesia, ataxia, nightmares, hallucinations, psychiatric disturbance, anxiety, dizziness. |
| | **Respiratory:** Hypoventilation, apnea. |
| | **Cardiovascular:** Bradycardia, hypotension, syncope. |
| | **Digestive:** Nausea, vomiting, constipation. |
| | **General:** Headache, injection site reactions, hypersensitivity (angioedema, rash, exfoliative dermatitis), fever, liver damage. |

- **Chronic Use Note:** Megaloblastic anemia has been reported following chronic use of phenobarbital [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
Pentobarbital is a potent inducer of hepatic microsomal enzymes, which can significantly alter the metabolism of other drugs.

- **Anticoagulants:** Decreases plasma levels and activity of warfarin and other coumarins; dosage adjustment of the anticoagulant may be required [US].
- **Corticosteroids:** Enhanced metabolism of exogenous corticosteroids [US].
- **Phenytoin:** Effect on phenytoin metabolism is variable and unpredictable; monitor blood levels of both drugs [US].
- **Sodium Valproate/Valproic Acid:** May decrease barbiturate metabolism, increasing pentobarbital blood levels [US].
- **CNS Depressants:** Concomitant use with alcohol, narcotics, or antihistamines produces additive depressant effects [US].
- **MAOIs:** May prolong barbiturate effects by inhibiting metabolism [US].
- **Griseofulvin/Doxycycline:** May decrease absorption or shorten the half-life of these agents [US].
- **Steroidal Hormones:** May decrease the effectiveness of estradiol and oral contraceptives [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not explicitly lettered in PI, but noted as causing "fetal damage"]. Barbiturates readily cross the placenta. Retrospective studies suggest an increased incidence of fetal abnormalities. Use in the third trimester may cause neonatal withdrawal (seizures/hyperirritability) [US].
- **Labor and Delivery:** Hypnotic doses do not significantly impair uterine activity, but anesthetic doses can decrease contraction force. May cause respiratory depression in the newborn, especially in premature infants [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Caution should be exercised; small amounts of barbiturates are excreted in breast milk [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness are supported by case reports and studies. Note the specific "Pediatric Neurotoxicity" warning regarding use in children under 3 years [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Geriatric patients may be more sensitive and react with marked excitement, confusion, or depression. Initial dosing should be at the low end of the range [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Administer with caution and initially in reduced doses. Do not use if premonitory signs of hepatic coma are present [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosage reduction is required as these patients may be more sensitive to the drug [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Pentobarbital is a nonselective CNS depressant. It depresses the sensory cortex, decreases motor activity, and alters cerebellar function. It produces levels of alteration from mild sedation to deep coma [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Barbiturates are respiratory depressants (dose-dependent). They reduce time spent in REM sleep [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Onset:** IV: almost immediate; IM: slightly faster than 20–60 minutes [US].
    - **Distribution:** Rapidly distributed to all tissues; high concentrations in brain, liver, and kidneys. Lipid solubility is the dominant factor in distribution [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily by the hepatic microsomal enzyme system [US].
    - **Elimination:** Metabolites excreted in urine and occasionally feces. Plasma half-life in adults is 15 to 50 hours (dose-dependent) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F and 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat. Do not use if the solution is precipitated [US].
- **Latex Safety:** Container closure is not made with natural rubber latex [US].
