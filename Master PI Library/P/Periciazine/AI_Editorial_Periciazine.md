# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Levocarnitine).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Metabolic Disorders:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It may be administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion. In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours. Daily maintenance doses typically remain at 50 mg/kg or as clinically required. Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). This should be administered as a slow (2 to 3 minute) bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Titration and Monitoring
- Dosing in ESRD should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Normal levels are between 40 µmol/L and 50 µmol/L.
- Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are supratherapeutic [US].
- Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and do not re-administer without a thorough risk-benefit assessment [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine (both oral and IV) has been associated with reports of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a known seizure history, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Carnitine Level Monitoring:** Efficacy in ESRD should be monitored via plasma carnitine concentrations rather than just clinical symptoms, as the direct effect on clinical outcomes in this population has not been fully determined [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are common in the ESRD population due to underlying pathology; however, the following have been reported in clinical trials (≥5% frequency):

| Body System | Adverse Event (reported at ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain, abdominal pain, asthenia |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, arrhythmia, palpitations |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, anorexia, constipation, dyspepsia |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight changes |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia, depression, anxiety |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, dyspnea, rhinitis, cough increase, sinusitis |
| **Skin** | Pruritus, rash |

**Serious/Specific Reactions:**
- **Transient Nausea/Vomiting:** Common during initiation.
- **Body Odor:** Less frequent but noted in clinical reports.
- **Gastritis:** Observed infrequently.
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (see Section 5).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. It is recommended to monitor INR levels frequently after starting levocarnitine or after dose adjustments [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not assigned/narrative]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate/controlled studies in pregnant women exist. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk; however, supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine after exogenous administration. Weigh the risks to the infant of potential excess intake against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders (50 mg/kg) applies to paediatric patients. In premature infants and newborns, secondary deficiency is defined by concentrations below age-related normals [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65+ to determine if they respond differently than younger subjects [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency; chronic high-dose oral use in ESRD may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO). *Intravenous* levocarnitine is specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also facilitates the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, it alleviates the metabolic abnormalities associated with carnitine loss during dialysis and reduced renal synthesis.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) and γ-butyrobetaine.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% is excreted in the urine within 24 hours of an IV bolus. Mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, IV levocarnitine (20 mg/kg post-dialysis) increased mean trough plasma levels from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L by week 8.
- **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses confirmed a dose-dependent increase in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].
