# Editorial Product Information: Phentermine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of Product Information (PI) for phentermine, intended for use by healthcare professionals as a clinically accurate, consolidated reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial overview and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this publication, only the Australian PI was provided for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Phentermine hydrochloride (as an ion-exchange resin complex)
- **Chemical Class:** Sympathomimetic amine; anorectic agent
- **Brand Name(s):** ALENAMI [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** A08AA01
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Modified-release capsules: 15 mg, 30 mg, and 40 mg [Australian]
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aspen Pharmacare Australia Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Obesity Management:** Indicated as a short-term (up to three months) adjunct in a medically supervised weight reduction program (including exercise, caloric restriction, and behavior modification) for patients with a Body Mass Index (BMI) of 30 kg/m² or greater [Australian].
- **Overweight Management:** May be initiated in patients with a BMI between 25 and 29.9 kg/m² if there is an increased risk of morbidity from associated disorders [Australian].
- **Note:** Secondary organic causes of obesity must be excluded prior to prescription.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Adolescents >12 years):**
    - **Starting Dose:** 30 mg once daily [Australian].
    - **Maintenance Dose:** 15 mg to 40 mg once daily, depending on patient responsiveness and body frame size (e.g., 40 mg may be required for larger-framed individuals) [Australian].
- **Administration:** One capsule daily at breakfast. Capsules must be swallowed whole. 
- **Instructional Note:** Evening dosing should be avoided due to the high risk of inducing insomnia.
- **Titration and Duration:** Use the lowest effective dose. Treatment duration should not exceed three months [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Pulmonary artery hypertension [Australian].
- Existing heart valve abnormalities or murmurs [Australian].
- Moderate to severe arterial hypertension [Australian].
- Cerebrovascular disease [Australian].
- Severe cardiac disease, including arrhythmias and advanced arteriosclerosis [Australian].
- Known hypersensitivity to sympathomimetic drugs [Australian].
- Hyperthyroidism [Australian].
- Glaucoma [Australian].
- History of drug or alcohol abuse/dependence [Australian].
- Agitated states or history of psychiatric illness, specifically anorexia nervosa and depression [Australian].
- Concomitant use of Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs) or use within 14 days of MAOI administration [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Valvular Heart Disease:** While valvulopathy (mitral, aortic, or tricuspid) has been reported with phentermine used in combination with fenfluramine or dexfenfluramine, the risk with phentermine monotherapy is not established. Use as monotherapy only [Australian].
- **Primary Pulmonary Hypertension (PPH):** Rare and potentially fatal cases of PPH have been reported with anorectics. Patients should be monitored for dyspnoea, angina pectoris, syncope, or lower extremity oedema. If these occur, discontinue treatment immediately [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** Use with caution in patients with mild hypertension or established coronary artery disease. Monitor blood pressure closely during the initial days of treatment [Australian].
- **Diabetes:** Weight loss and dietary changes may alter requirements for insulin or oral hypoglycaemic agents [Australian].
- **Neurological/Psychiatric:** Use with caution in patients with epilepsy or those receiving psychotropic drugs/sedatives. Phentermine may cause CNS overstimulation [Australian].
- **Weight Loss Rate:** Weight loss should be gradual; rapid weight loss has been rarely associated with cardiovascular or cerebrovascular events [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Note: Precise frequency data (e.g., %) was not provided in the source document.*

- **Cardiovascular:** Common reactions include palpitations, tachycardia, increased blood pressure, and precordial pain. Rare events include myocardial infarction, angina, cardiac failure, cardiac arrest, and stroke [Australian].
- **Central Nervous System:** Restlessness, nervousness, insomnia, tremor, dizziness, headache, and overstimulation. Euphoria followed by fatigue/depression and rare psychotic episodes or hallucinations have been reported [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Dry mouth, nausea, vomiting, abdominal cramps, unpleasant taste, constipation, or diarrhoea [Australian].
- **Other:** Rash, micturition disturbances, impotence, changes in libido, and facial oedema [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **MAOIs:** Contraindicated; risk of hypertensive crisis [Australian].
- **Antihypertensives:** Phentermine may antagonise the effects of adrenergic neurone blocking drugs such as clonidine, methyldopa, and guanethidine [Australian].
- **Sympathomimetics:** Potentiation of effects; use with extreme caution [Australian].
- **Thyroid Hormones:** May increase the risk of CNS stimulation [Australian].
- **Alcohol:** Concurrent use should be avoided as it may exacerbate CNS side effects like dizziness and confusion [Australian].
- **Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors (SSRIs):** Theoretical risk of valvular disease when used in combination (e.g., fluoxetine, sertraline, paroxetine); coadministration is not recommended [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category B2 [Australian]:** Inadequate evidence of safety in humans. Animal studies showed abolished oestrous cycling at high doses. Use is not recommended in pregnant women [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Use should be avoided as no safety data is available for lactating women [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No human data available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for children under 12 years of age [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Not recommended for use in the elderly [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources; however, elimination is assisted by urine acidification if renal function is adequate [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A sympathomimetic amine with anorectic activity. It primarily suppresses appetite via the hypothalamus and impacts dopaminergic and noradrenergic neurotransmission [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Produces a pressor response and increases heart rate and cardiac contractility [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Nearly complete absorption from the GI tract. Ion-exchange resin provides slower absorption compared to HCl salt, with controlled release over 10–14 hours [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Partially metabolised in the liver [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (62.7% to 84.8% excreted unchanged in urine).
    - **Half-life:** Approximately 25 hours; reduced to 7–8 hours if urine is acidified [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No clinical trial data was provided in the source documentation.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Protection:** Protect from light [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
