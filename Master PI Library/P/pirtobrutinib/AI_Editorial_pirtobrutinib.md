# Editorial Product Information: Pirtobrutinib

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Regulatory Agencies:** Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA - Australia); Food and Drug Administration (FDA - United States).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of pirtobrutinib, synthesising product information from multiple global jurisdictions for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and clinical resource; it does not substitute for the official Product Information or Prescribing Information required for clinical practice within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pirtobrutinib (a reversible, non-covalent inhibitor of Bruton’s tyrosine kinase [BTK]).
- **Chemical Class:** Kinase inhibitor; pyrazole-4-carboxamide derivative.
- **Brand Name:** **Jaypirca**.
- **ATC Code:** L01EL05.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **50 mg film-coated tablets:** Blue, arc-triangle shaped, debossed with “Lilly 50” and “6902”.
    - **100 mg film-coated tablets:** Blue, round, debossed with “Lilly 100” and “7026”.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Eli Lilly and Company (Global); Eli Lilly Australia Pty Ltd [TGA].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia (CLL) / Small Lymphocytic Lymphoma (SLL):** Treatment of adult patients with relapsed or refractory CLL/SLL who have been previously treated with a covalent BTK inhibitor (e.g., ibrutinib, acalabrutinib, or zanubrutinib).
- **Mantle Cell Lymphoma (MCL):** Treatment of adult patients with relapsed or refractory MCL after at least two lines of systemic therapy, including a BTK inhibitor [FDA-approved under accelerated approval; not specified as a primary indication in the provided TGA document].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Standard Dose:** 200 mg orally once daily.
- **Duration:** Continue until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.

### Administration Instructions
- Swallow tablets whole with water; do not cut, crush, or chew.
- May be taken with or without food.
- Administer at approximately the same time each day.
- If vomiting occurs, do not take an additional dose; resume at the next scheduled time.

### Missed Dose Instructions
- If a dose is missed by **less than 12 hours**, take the dose immediately.
- If **more than 12 hours** have passed, skip the missed dose and take the next dose at the regular time. Do not double the dose to make up for a missed one.

### Dosage Modifications for Adverse Reactions
Dosing should be interrupted for Grade 3 or higher toxicities (neutropenia with fever, prolonged Grade 4 neutropenia, Grade 3 thrombocytopenia with bleeding, Grade 4 thrombocytopenia, or Grade 3/4 non-haematologic toxicity).

| Occurrence | Dose Modification |
| :--- | :--- |
| **First** | Interrupt until Grade 1 or baseline; resume at 200 mg daily. |
| **Second** | Interrupt until Grade 1 or baseline; resume at 100 mg daily. |
| **Third** | Interrupt until Grade 1 or baseline; resume at 50 mg daily. |
| **Fourth** | Permanently discontinue. |
*Note: Dose modification is not recommended for asymptomatic lymphocytosis or asymptomatic lipase increase.*

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to pirtobrutinib or any excipients (including lactose) [TGA].
- **FDA Listing:** The FDA PI states "None."

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Infections:** Serious and fatal infections (including bacterial, viral, fungal, and opportunistic infections like PJP) have occurred. Grade ≥3 infections occurred in 25–32% of patients. Monitor for fever and symptoms; consider prophylaxis in high-risk patients.
- **Haemorrhage:** Fatal bleeding events have been reported. Major haemorrhage (Grade ≥3 or CNS) occurred in approximately 2-3% of patients. Risk is increased with concomitant antiplatelet or anticoagulant therapy.
- **Cytopenias:** Grade 3 or 4 neutropenia (up to 27%), anaemia, and thrombocytopenia are common. Monitor CBCs monthly or as clinically indicated.
- **Cardiac Arrhythmias:** Atrial fibrillation and flutter occurred in 1.6%–3.4% of patients. Monitor for palpitations, dizziness, and dyspnea, particularly in patients with cardiovascular risk factors.
- **Second Primary Malignancies:** Reported in 9% of patients, most commonly non-melanoma skin cancers. Advise sun protection and routine skin monitoring.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Drug-induced liver injury (DILI) has been reported with BTK inhibitors. Monitor liver function tests (LFTs) at baseline and throughout treatment.
- **Tumour Lysis Syndrome (TLS):** Reported rarely; monitor patients with high tumour burden closely.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Profile (Integrated Data)
- **Very Common (>10%):** Fatigue (26–36%), bruising/contusion (16–36%), diarrhoea (16–26%), musculoskeletal pain (27–32%), cough (14–33%), COVID-19 (13–28%), pneumonia (12–28%), nausea (11–21%), abdominal pain (10–25%), dyspnea (17–22%), rash (14–19%), and oedema (11–21%).
- **Common (1-10%):** Pyrexia, dizziness, peripheral neuropathy, urinary tract infection, headache, and hypertension.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Pneumonia (up to 21%) and febrile neutropenia (up to 7%) were the most frequent serious reactions.

### Laboratory Abnormalities
- **Haematology:** Decreased neutrophil count (up to 63%), decreased haemoglobin (up to 48%), and decreased platelets (up to 39%).
- **Chemistry:** Increased creatinine (up to 30%), decreased calcium (up to 40%), and increased AST/ALT.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Substrate:** Pirtobrutinib is primarily metabolised by CYP3A4.
    - **Strong CYP3A Inhibitors:** Increase pirtobrutinib exposure. Avoid concomitant use; if unavoidable, reduce pirtobrutinib dose by 50 mg [FDA]. *Note: [TGA] notes a 48% AUC increase with itraconazole but does not mandate dose adjustment.*
    - **Strong/Moderate CYP3A Inducers:** Decrease pirtobrutinib exposure (e.g., rifampin reduces AUC by 71%). Avoid concomitant use. If unavoidable, increase pirtobrutinib dose to 300 mg daily [TGA, FDA].
- **Pirtobrutinib as an Inhibitor:**
    - **CYP2C8 Substrates:** Pirtobrutinib is a moderate inhibitor. Use caution with substrates like repaglinide.
    - **BCRP Substrates:** Moderate inhibitor. Monitor closely when used with narrow therapeutic index substrates (e.g., methotrexate).
    - **P-gp and CYP2C19 Substrates:** Weak inhibitor. Monitor sensitive substrates (e.g., digoxin, dabigatran).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **TGA Category D:** Pirtobrutinib can cause fetal harm based on animal studies (skeletal malformations and embryo-fetal mortality).
    - **FDA:** Advise pregnant women of the risk to the fetus. Verify pregnancy status in females of reproductive potential before initiation.
- **Contraception:**
    - **Females:** Use effective contraception during treatment and for **5 weeks** [TGA] or **1 week** [FDA] after the last dose. 
    - **Males:** Use effective contraception and do not father a child during treatment and for **3 months** after the last dose [TGA].
- **Breastfeeding:** Avoid breastfeeding during treatment and for **1 week** after the last dose.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in patients <18 years.
- **Geriatric Use:** No overall differences in efficacy between younger and older patients, though patients ≥65 years may experience higher rates of Grade 3+ adverse reactions [FDA].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Mild/Moderate (eGFR 30–89 mL/min):** No dose adjustment required.
    - **Severe (eGFR 15–29 mL/min):** Reduce dose to 100 mg daily (if previously 200 mg) or 50 mg daily (if previously 100 mg). If on 50 mg, discontinue.
    - **End-Stage/Dialysis:** No data available.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild, moderate, or severe hepatic impairment [TGA, FDA].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Highly selective, reversible, non-covalent inhibitor of BTK. Unlike covalent inhibitors, it retains activity against BTK C481-mutation-bearing cells.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability 85.5%; Tmax ~2 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding 96%; Volume of distribution (Vd) 34.2 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP3A4 and UGT1A8/UGT1A9.
    - **Elimination:** Half-life ~20 hours. Excretion is 57% via urine (10% unchanged) and 37% via faeces (18% unchanged).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **BRUIN (CLL/SLL Cohort):** A Phase 1/2 study showing an ORR of 72% in patients previously treated with both a covalent BTK inhibitor and a BCL-2 inhibitor.
- **BRUIN CLL-321:** A randomized Phase 3 trial in patients with CLL/SLL previously treated with a covalent BTK inhibitor. Pirtobrutinib monotherapy demonstrated a median Progression-Free Survival (PFS) of 14.0 months [TGA updated data] or 11.2 months [FDA primary analysis] vs 8.7 months for investigator's choice (idelalisib/rituximab or bendamustine/rituximab).
- **BRUIN (MCL Cohort):** Demonstrated an ORR of 50% with a median Duration of Response (DOR) of 8.3 months [FDA].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **TGA:** Store below 30°C in the original container.
- **FDA:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F and 86°F).

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia [TGA]:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine). Subject to additional monitoring (black triangle symbol).
- **United States [FDA]:** Rx Only.
