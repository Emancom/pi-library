# Editorial Product Information: Promethazine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) document provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian** (Therapeutic Goods Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, intended to facilitate clinical decision-making. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing within a specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Promethazine hydrochloride.
- **Chemical Class:** Phenothiazine derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** AdiraMedica Promethazine [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** R06AD02.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - 10 mg film-coated tablets [Australian].
    - 25 mg film-coated tablets [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AdiraMedica Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Allergic Conditions:** Treatment of urticaria, allergic contact dermatitis, and reactions to drugs, insect bites, and stings [Australian].
- **Upper Respiratory Tract:** Relief of excessive secretions associated with hay fever and allergic rhinitis [Australian].
- **Nausea and Vomiting:** Management of emesis from various causes, including motion sickness, irradiation sickness, drug-induced nausea, and postoperative vomiting [Australian].
- **Sedation:** Short-term sedative use (not to exceed 7 to 10 consecutive days) [Australian].
- **Other:** Symptomatic management of measles and chickenpox; pre-anaesthetic medication for the prevention/control of postoperative vomiting [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
*Note: This product is contraindicated in children under 6 years of age [Australian].*

### 3.1 Allergic Disorders
- **Adults and children >12 years:** 25 mg to 75 mg as a single dose at night, or 10 mg to 20 mg two to three times daily [Australian].
- **Children 6–12 years:** 10 mg to 25 mg as a single dose at night, or 10 mg two to three times daily [Australian].

### 3.2 Sedation
- **Adults and children >12 years:** 25 mg to 75 mg as a single dose at night [Australian].
- **Children 6–12 years:** 10 mg to 25 mg as a single dose at night [Australian].

### 3.3 Travel Sickness (Prevention and Treatment)
- **Adults and children >12 years:** 10 mg to 25 mg. Prevention doses should be taken the night before travel and may be repeated after 6 to 8 hours the following day if necessary [Australian].
- **Children 6–12 years:** 10 mg [Australian].

### 3.4 Nausea and Vomiting
- **Adults and children >12 years:** 25 mg, 3 to 4 times daily (Maximum: 100 mg/day) [Australian].
- **Children 6–12 years:** 10 mg, 2 times daily (Maximum: 20 mg/day) [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
Promethazine is contraindicated in:
- Patients with known hypersensitivity to promethazine, other phenothiazines, or any excipients (e.g., lactose) [Australian].
- Newborns or premature infants [Australian].
- Children under 6 years of age (due to risk of fatal respiratory depression) [Australian].
- Women who are lactating or breastfeeding [Australian].
- Concurrent use of Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs) or use within 14 days of MAOI discontinuation [Australian].
- Patients with jaundice induced by other phenothiazines [Australian].
- Patients in a comatose state or who have received high doses of CNS depressants [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Respiratory Depression:** Promethazine may cause fatal respiratory depression. Use is strictly contraindicated in children <6 years. Caution is advised in patients with acute or chronic respiratory impairment as the drug may thicken lung secretions and impair expectoration [Australian].
- **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** Potentially fatal NMS (hyperthermia, muscle rigidity, autonomic instability) may occur. Discontinue immediately if suspected [Australian].
- **QT Prolongation:** Phenothiazines may prolong the QT interval, increasing the risk of torsade de pointes. Risk is higher in patients with bradycardia, hypokalaemia, or concurrent use of QT-prolonging drugs [Australian].
- **CNS Effects:** Significant sedation may occur; patients should be cautioned against operating machinery. Paradoxical excitation may occur, particularly in children and the elderly [Australian].
- **Agranulocytosis:** Cases of blood dyscrasias have been reported. Regular monitoring of complete blood counts is recommended; patients should report any fever or sore throat immediately [Australian].
- **Photosensitivity:** Risk of solar dermatitis. Patients should avoid sun or UV light exposure during and shortly after treatment [Australian].
- **Other Precautions:** Use with caution in patients with cardiovascular disease, epilepsy (may lower seizure threshold), narrow-angle glaucoma, prostatic hypertrophy, or stenosing peptic ulcers [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### 6.1 Common Reactions
- **Gastrointestinal:** Dry mouth, nausea, vomiting, constipation, epigastric distress.
- **Nervous System:** Sedation, somnolence, dizziness, fatigue, restlessness, incoordination.
- **Ocular:** Blurred vision.

### 6.2 Less Common and Rare Reactions
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, bradycardia, faintness, hypotension.
- **Hematological:** Leucopenia, agranulocytosis, aplastic anemia, thrombocytopenic purpura.
- **Hepatobiliary:** Jaundice (cholestatic).
- **Neurological:** Extrapyramidal symptoms, tardive dyskinesia (prolonged use), oculogyric crises, tinnitus.
- **Psychiatric:** Hallucinations, anxiety, agitation, confusion (especially in elderly).

### 6.3 Life-threatening Reactions
- **Anaphylaxis** and **Agranulocytosis** [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CNS Depressants:** Enhanced sedative effects with alcohol, opioids, barbiturates, anxiolytics, and neuroleptics. Alcohol must be avoided [Australian].
- **Anticholinergic Agents:** Additive effects when used with tricyclic antidepressants (TCAs), atropine, or other muscarinic antagonists [Australian].
- **MAOIs:** Concurrent use is contraindicated; may prolong and intensify anticholinergic and CNS depressive effects [Australian].
- **CYP2D6 Metabolism:** Promethazine is a moderate inhibitor of CYP2D6. Concurrent use with CYP2D6 substrates (e.g., amitriptyline) may increase substrate plasma levels [Australian].
- **QT-Prolonging Drugs:** Increased risk of arrhythmias when used with certain antiarrhythmics, antimicrobials, and antipsychotics [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal Agents:** Non-absorbable agents (magnesium, aluminium, and calcium salts) may reduce phenothiazine absorption. Space doses by at least 2 hours [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category C. Not recommended during pregnancy or for women of childbearing potential not using contraception. High doses in late pregnancy have caused prolonged neurological disturbances in neonates. Use only if benefits outweigh potential risks to the fetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Promethazine is excreted in breast milk and poses a risk of neonatal irritability and excitement [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Contraindicated under 6 years. Potential for fatal respiratory depression, central/obstructive apnoea, and sudden death. Avoid in children with signs suggestive of Reye’s Syndrome [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased susceptibility to anticholinergic effects, confusion, and hypotension. Paradoxical excitation is more common [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution; should be avoided in patients with renal dysfunction [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution; should be avoided in patients with liver dysfunction [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A long-acting H1-receptor antagonist with mild anticholinergic (atropine-like) and antiserotonin effects. It acts as an antiemetic, sedative, and potentiator of analgesics [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Well absorbed from the GI tract; peak plasma concentrations occur 2 to 3 hours post-oral administration. Significant first-pass metabolism results in low systemic bioavailability [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Widely distributed; 76–93% protein bound. Crosses the blood-brain barrier and placenta [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Extensive hepatic metabolism, primarily to promethazine sulfoxide and N-desmethylpromethazine [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Excreted via urine and bile. Elimination half-life ranges from 5 to 14 hours. Clinical antihistamine action persists for 4 to 12 hours [Australian].
