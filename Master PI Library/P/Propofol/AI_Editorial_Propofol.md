# Editorial Product Information: Propofol

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Propofol (2, 6-diisopropylphenol)
- **Chemical Class:** Alkylphenol derivative; intravenous general anaesthetic.
- **Brand Name(s):** **DIPRIVAN** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** N01AX10
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Injectable emulsion 10 mg/mL (1%) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (injection or infusion).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aspen Pharmacare Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Induction of General Anaesthesia:** Approved for use in adults and children aged 1 month and older [Australian].
- **Maintenance of General Anaesthesia:** 
    - Adults [Australian].
    - Children aged 3 years and older [Australian].
    - Children aged 1 month to 3 years for procedures not exceeding 60 minutes (unless alternative agents must be avoided) [Australian].
- **ICU Sedation:** Sedation of ventilated adult patients (aged over 16 years) receiving intensive care [Australian].
- **Conscious Sedation:** Monitored conscious sedation for surgical and diagnostic procedures in adults [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Principles
**Propofol** should be titrated against the patient's clinical response until the onset of anaesthesia or the desired level of sedation is achieved. It lacks analgesic properties; supplementary analgesia is typically required.

### Adult Dosing
- **Induction of General Anaesthesia:**
    - **Healthy Adults (<55 years):** Titrate approximately 40 mg (4 mL) every 10 seconds. Typical requirement: 2.0 to 2.5 mg/kg.
    - **Elderly/Debilitated/ASA III or IV:** Slower titration (20 mg every 10 seconds) is required.
- **Maintenance of General Anaesthesia:**
    - **Continuous Infusion:** 4 to 12 mg/kg/h (0.067 to 0.2 mg/kg/min).
    - **Repeat Bolus:** Increments of 25 mg to 50 mg as needed.
- **ICU Sedation:** Continuous infusion only. Adjust to required depth; 1.0 to 3.0 mg/kg/h usually provides satisfactory sedation. Rates >4.0 mg/kg/h are not recommended [Australian].
- **Conscious Sedation:**
    - **Onset:** 0.5 to 1 mg/kg over 1 to 5 minutes.
    - **Maintenance:** 1.5 to 3.0 mg/kg/h; may supplement with 10–20 mg boluses for rapid depth increase.

### Administration Instructions
- **Aseptic Technique:** Must be strictly maintained. **Propofol** contains 0.005% disodium edetate to retard microbial growth but is not an antimicrobially preserved product.
- **Preparation:** Shake before use. May be used undiluted or diluted with 5% Glucose (minimum concentration 2 mg/mL). Dilutions must be prepared immediately before use and used within 6 hours.
- **Equipment:** Use of burettes, drop counters, or volumetric pumps is recommended to prevent uncontrolled infusion.
- **Target Controlled Infusion (TCI):** Use is restricted to induction and maintenance of general anaesthesia in adults using the DIPRIFUSOR system [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **propofol** or any excipients (including egg lecithin, soya oil, glycerol, and disodium edetate) [Australian].
- Sedation in children aged 16 years or younger for intensive care or monitored conscious sedation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Aseptic Technique:** Failure to use aseptic technique has been linked to microbial contamination resulting in fever, infection/sepsis, and death.
- **Propofol Infusion Syndrome (PRIS):** Very rare reports of metabolic acidosis, rhabdomyolysis, hyperkalaemia, and rapidly progressive cardiac failure (sometimes fatal) in adults treated for >48 hours with doses >5 mg/kg/h. Doses for ICU sedation should not exceed 4 mg/kg/h [Australian].
- **Cardiorespiratory Effects:** Hypotension and transient apnoea are common. Caution in patients with cardiac, respiratory, renal, or hepatic impairment, or those who are hypovolaemic or debilitated.
- **Zinc Loss:** Disodium edetate (EDTA) is a chelator. Consider supplemental zinc during prolonged administration (>5 days), especially in patients predisposed to deficiency (burns, sepsis) [Australian].
- **Paediatric Neurotoxicity:** Preclinical studies indicate potential neurotoxicity; the risk/benefit of prolonged use (>3 hours) in children <3 years should be considered [Australian].
- **Involuntary Movements:** May occur during sedation; caution in procedures requiring immobility.

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Pain during injection (burning, tingling/stinging) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Hypotension, hypertension, bradycardia, transient apnoea, cough, headache, nausea, vomiting, shivering, flushing |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Tachycardia, extrasystole, thrombosis, phlebitis |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Convulsions/epileptiform seizures, fever, anaphylactoid reactions (including angioedema, bronchospasm) |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Rhabdomyolysis (at ICU doses >4 mg/kg/h), pancreatitis, pulmonary oedema, acute hepatic failure, priapism, urine discolouration |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CNS Depressants:** Potent analgesics, benzodiazepines, and inhalational anaesthetics increase the sedative and cardiorespiratory effects of **propofol**.
- **Alfentanil/Midazolam:** Co-administration results in enhanced sedation and respiratory depression; dose reductions of **propofol** are recommended.
- **Valproate:** May necessitate lower doses of **propofol**.
- **Dexmedetomidine:** Likely to lead to increased body temperature (pyrexia).
- **Rifampicin:** Profound hypotension has been reported following induction in patients treated with rifampicin [Australian].
- **Neuromuscular Blockers:** No clinically significant change in the onset or duration of action of succinylcholine or non-depolarizing muscle relaxants.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category C:** General anaesthetics cross the placenta and may cause neonatal depression. Not recommended for obstetric anaesthesia. 
    - Animal studies show evidence of delayed ossification and adverse effects on perinatal survival [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. **Propofol** is excreted in human milk; the effects of oral absorption on the infant are unknown [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility observed in rat studies at doses up to 15 mg/kg/day [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:**
    - Induction: $\ge$1 month. Maintenance: $\ge$3 years (or 1 month to 3 years for short procedures).
    - Higher clearance in infants compared to older children/adults [Australian].
    - Contraindicated for ICU/conscious sedation in patients $\le$16 years.
- **Elderly Use:** Requirement for induction is reduced. Slower rates of administration are essential to maintain haemodynamic stability.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Caution is advised. Moderate impairment does not significantly alter pharmacokinetics, but severe impairment has not been adequately studied [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Thought to produce sedative/anaesthetic effects via positive modulation of the inhibitory neurotransmitter GABA at $\text{GABA}_A$ receptors.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Rapid onset (approx. 30 seconds). Decreases intracranial pressure, cerebral blood flow, and intraocular pressure. Does not suppress the adrenal response to ACTH [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Distribution:** Follows a three-compartment open model. Rapid equilibration between plasma and brain. Highly protein-bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic via glucuronidation to inactive metabolites.
    - **Elimination:** Excreted renally. Adult clearance: 1.5 to 2 L/min. Initial distribution half-life: 2–4 minutes; elimination half-life: 30–60 minutes.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store between 2°C and 25°C. 
- Do not freeze [Australian].
- Single-use only; discard any unused portions. Containers and infusion lines must be replaced after 12 hours.
