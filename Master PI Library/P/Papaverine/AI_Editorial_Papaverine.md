# Editorial Product Information: Papaverine Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **papaverine hydrochloride**, synthesised from available official Product Information for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Papaverine hydrochloride (a benzylisoquinoline alkaloid).
- **Brand Names:** DBL Papaverine Hydrochloride Injection [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Sterile injection: 120 mg/10 mL ampoules [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intracavernosal injection (ICI); parenteral (intravenous) mentioned for high-dose management [Australian].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Erectile Dysfunction:** Treatment of erectile dysfunction (ED) [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Titration:** The lowest effective dose must be determined for each patient. Doses typically range from 2.5 mg to 60 mg.
- **Starting Doses:**
    - **Standard:** 15 mg is recommended for most aetiologies.
    - **Neurogenic (Spinal Cord Injury):** 5 mg is recommended.
    - **Psychogenic:** Patients typically respond to lower doses.
    - **Vasculogenic:** Higher doses are often required.
- **Maximum Dose:** Patients failing to respond to 60 mg should be transitioned to alternative therapies. 
- **Frequency:** Tolerance may develop during long-term use, necessitating dosage increases.

### Administration Instructions
- **Specialist Supervision:** Treatment must be initiated and supervised by prescribers expert in ED management. The first injection must be performed by medically trained personnel.
- **Self-Administration:** Patients may self-inject only after thorough training.
- **Technique:**
    - Use a 13 mm, 27 to 30-gauge needle.
    - Inject into the corpora cavernosa at a 90-degree angle over 1–2 minutes.
    - Avoid the midline, underside of the penis, and visible superficial veins.
    - Alternate injection sites between the left and right cavernosa and vary the site along the penile shaft.
    - Apply pressure to the site following injection until bleeding ceases.
- **Incompatibilities:** A precipitate forms if mixed with lactated Ringer’s Injection.

## 4. Contraindications
- Complete atrioventricular (AV) block.
- Conditions predisposing to priapism (e.g., sickle cell anaemia, multiple myeloma, leukaemia).
- Penile implants.
- Anatomical deformation of the penis (e.g., angulation, cavernosal fibrosis, Peyronie’s disease).
- Pre-existing penile fibrosis.
- Men for whom sexual activity is inadvisable or contraindicated.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Priapism:** Intracavernosal injection can cause prolonged erections. Patients must seek urgent medical attention for erections lasting more than four hours to prevent tissue damage. 
- **Penile Fibrosis:** Risk increases with injection volumes exceeding 1 mL. Regular clinical examination of the penis is mandatory; treatment must stop if fibrotic changes (nodules, plaques) appear.
- **Cardiovascular Risk:** Use with extreme caution in patients with cardiac conduction disorders or unstable cardiovascular disease. High doses may induce cardiac arrhythmias or fatal apnoea.
- **Postural Hypotension:** Patients should be advised of the risk of dizziness when rising from a seated or lying position.
- **Injection Site Safety:** Misplaced injections may cause urethral injury or haematoma. Needle breakage has occurred, sometimes requiring surgical removal.
- **Disease Transmission:** Site bleeding may increase the risk of transmitting blood-borne diseases to partners.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Local Adverse Events
- **Common:** Mild penile discomfort during injection, ecchymosis/bruising.
- **Serious:** Priapism, penile fibrosis (nodules, Peyronie’s disease), cavernositis, penile fracture, and intracavernosal needle breakage.
- **Other:** Urethral bleeding, glans paraesthesia, injection site blisters, and oedema.

### Systemic Adverse Events
- **Vasodilatory Effects:** Headache, flushing, dizziness, tachycardia, hypotension, and syncope.
- **Hepatic:** Elevations in serum ALT, AST, and bilirubin.
- **Other:** Allergic reactions (urticaria), impaired ejaculation, vasovagal reactions, and rare reports of death.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Levodopa:** Papaverine may interfere with the therapeutic effect of levodopa and worsen Parkinsonian symptoms. Concomitant use should be avoided [Australian].
- **CNS Depressants:** Effects of papaverine may be potentiated by CNS depressants.
- **Morphine:** Synergistic effects may occur.
- **Anticoagulants (e.g., Warfarin, Heparin):** Increased risk of bleeding/haematoma at the injection site.
- **Ioxaglate:** Can form a paste-like precipitate with papaverine, posing a severe risk if formed within the penis.
- **Combination ED Therapy:** Safety and efficacy with alprostadil or phentolamine have not been established; co-administration increases the risk of dizziness and syncope.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. Not intended for use by women. Animal studies (mice) showed increased fetal mortality and neural tube defects at high doses (140 mg/kg).
- **Breastfeeding:** Not intended for use by women. It is unknown if papaverine is excreted in human milk; caution is advised.
- **Fertility:** No adequate human or animal data available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No data available.
- **Elderly Use:** An ECG is required before prescribing to rule out pre-existing cardiac conduction disorders [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution. If signs of exacerbation occur, discontinue the drug. Liver function tests (LFTs) should be obtained at baseline and every 6 months for patients with a history of liver disease or alcohol abuse.
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A direct-acting smooth muscle relaxant. In ED, it induces erection by relaxing the arteriolar smooth muscle and the sinusoidal wall of the corpora cavernosa, increasing blood flow and compressing venous outflow.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Spasmolytic effects are most pronounced on the vascular system (coronary, cerebral, pulmonary, and peripheral). It may depress myocardial conduction and prolong the refractory period.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Protein Binding:** Approximately 90%.
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly metabolised by the liver.
    - **Half-life:** 1–2 hours.
    - **Elimination:** Excreted in the urine as inactive glucuronide conjugates of phenolic metabolites.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No clinical trial data provided in available sources.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Conditions:** Protect from light.
- **Single Use:** Ampoules are for single use only; discard any remaining portion.
