# Editorial Product Information: Peramivir

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- Please note that at the time of this writing, official PI documents for other major jurisdictions (e.g., US, European, UK, Canadian, Japanese) were not provided for comparison. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, legally approved PI in any specific jurisdiction. Prescribers must consult the local approved PI for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Peramivir (anhydrous).
- **Chemical Class:** Neuraminidase inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** **Rapivab** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Concentrate solution for infusion. 200 mg peramivir in 20 mL (10 mg/mL) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Biocelect Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Influenza Infection:** Treatment of acute influenza in adults and children aged 2 years and older who have been symptomatic for no longer than two days [Australian].
- **Limitations of Use:** 
    - Efficacy has not been established for repeated doses in patients with serious influenza requiring hospitalisation [Australian].
    - Data regarding the treatment of Influenza B are limited [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult and Adolescent (13 years and older)
- **Standard Dose:** A single 600 mg intravenous dose administered over 15 to 30 minutes [Australian].

### Paediatric Patients (2 to 12 years)
- **Standard Dose:** A single 12 mg/kg intravenous dose (up to a maximum of 600 mg) administered over 15 to 30 minutes [Australian].

### Preparation and Administration
- **Dilution:** Must be diluted prior to use in 0.9% or 0.45% sodium chloride, 5% dextrose, or Ringer’s lactate to a total volume of 100 mL [Australian].
- **Inspection:** Visually inspect for particulate matter or discolouration before administration [Australian].
- **Storage of Diluted Solution:** Administer immediately or store refrigerated (2°C to 8°C) for up to 24 hours. Allow to reach room temperature before administration if refrigerated [Australian].

### Missed Dose
- Not applicable as this is intended as a single-dose treatment [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known serious hypersensitivity or anaphylaxis to peramivir or any excipient. Reported reactions include anaphylaxis, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), and erythema multiforme [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Serious Skin Reactions:** Rare reports of SJS, erythema multiforme, and exfoliative dermatitis. Discontinue treatment immediately if a serious skin reaction is suspected [Australian].
- **Neuropsychiatric Events:** Influenza can be associated with delirium, hallucinations, and abnormal behaviour, primarily in paediatric patients. Monitor patients for signs of abnormal behaviour [Australian].
- **Secondary Bacterial Infections:** Peramivir does not treat bacterial infections. Prescribers should remain alert for secondary bacterial complications and treat with antibiotics as appropriate [Australian].
- **Not a Vaccination Substitute:** Treatment does not replace annual influenza vaccination [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosage adjustment is required for patients with creatinine clearance below 50 mL/min [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common (1–10%)
- **Diarrhoea:** Reported at 8% (vs 7% for placebo) in adult trials [Australian].
- **Laboratory Abnormalities:**
    - Neutrophils < 1.000 x 10⁹/L (8%) [Australian].
    - Serum glucose > 160 mg/dL (5%) [Australian].
    - Creatine phosphokinase ≥ 6.0 x ULN (4%) [Australian].
    - Alanine aminotransferase > 2.5 x ULN (3%) [Australian].

### Paediatric-Specific Effects (≥ 2%)
- Vomiting (3%), fever (3%), proteinuria (3%), and tympanic membrane erythema (2%) [Australian].

### Serious and Post-Marketing Experience
- **Dermatologic:** Stevens-Johnson Syndrome, erythema multiforme, exfoliative dermatitis, rash [Australian].
- **General:** Anaphylactic/anaphylactoid reactions [Australian].
- **Psychiatric:** Hallucinations, delirium, abnormal behaviour (most common in children) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Cytochrome P450:** Low potential for interactions; peramivir does not induce or inhibit CYP enzymes [Australian].
- **Vaccines:**
    - **Inactivated Influenza Vaccine:** Can be administered at any time relative to peramivir [Australian].
    - **Live Attenuated Influenza Vaccine (LAIV):** Antiviral drugs may inhibit LAIV replication. LAIV is not recommended for use until 48 hours after peramivir administration [Australian].
- **Probenecid:** Co-administration with probenecid did not significantly alter peramivir's pharmacokinetic profile [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B3:** Limited data in pregnant women. Animal studies showed foetal abnormalities (reduced renal papilla/dilated ureters) in rats and developmental toxicity in rabbits only at maternally toxic doses or exposures exceeding human recommendations [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on peramivir in human milk. The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need for the drug [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No effects on mating or fertility were observed in rats at exposures 6 times the human dose [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for children 2 years and older. Safety and efficacy in children under 2 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Pharmacokinetics were similar to younger subjects. No dose adjustment is required unless significant renal impairment is present [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Significant increase in drug exposure occurs. Adjust dose as follows [Australian]:

| Creatinine Clearance (mL/min) | Adult/Adolescent Dose | Paediatric (2–12y) Dose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **≥ 50** | 600 mg (single dose) | 12 mg/kg (max 600 mg) |
| **30–49** | 200 mg (single dose) | 4 mg/kg |
| **10–29** | 100 mg (single dose) | 2 mg/kg |

- **Haemodialysis:** Peramivir is cleared by haemodialysis. Administer dose after the dialysis session [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specifically studied, but no clinically relevant changes are expected as the drug is primarily renally excreted [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibitor of influenza virus neuraminidase, preventing the release of viral particles from infected cells [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is < 30%.
    - **Metabolism:** Not significantly metabolised in humans.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily via the kidneys (90% as unchanged drug). Elimination half-life is approximately 20 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adult Acute Influenza:** A study in Japan (N=297) showed that a single 600 mg dose reduced the duration of symptoms by a median of 21 hours compared to placebo [Australian].
- **Paediatric Acute Influenza:** An active-controlled study (N=110) in children aged 2–17 showed safety and efficacy comparable to a 5-day course of oseltamivir [Australian].
- **Hospitalised Patients:** A trial of 398 hospitalised subjects failed to show a significant improvement in clinical resolution time when adding peramivir to standard of care [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store below 25°C in original cartons. Short excursions between 15°C and 30°C are permitted [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4) [Australian].
