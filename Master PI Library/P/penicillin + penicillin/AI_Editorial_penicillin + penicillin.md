# Editorial Product Information: penicillin + penicillin

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **penicillin + penicillin**
- Chemical class: Beta-lactam antibiotic combination (penicillin G salts: benzathine and procaine)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intramuscular (deep IM) injection only

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of moderately severe to severe infections due to penicillin‑G‑susceptible microorganisms where serum levels achieved with this formulation are appropriate, including:
  - Moderately severe to severe upper respiratory tract infections due to susceptible streptococci
  - Scarlet fever due to susceptible streptococci
  - Erysipelas due to susceptible streptococci
  - Skin and soft tissue infections due to susceptible streptococci
  - Moderately severe pneumonia and otitis media due to susceptible Streptococcus pneumoniae
    - Note: Severe pneumonia, empyema, bacteremia, pericarditis, meningitis, peritonitis, and arthritis of pneumococcal etiology are better treated with aqueous penicillin G (sodium or potassium) when high, sustained serum levels are required.
- Not indicated for treatment of venereal diseases (e.g., syphilis, gonorrhea, yaws, bejel, pinta).
- Indication age range: Indicated for adults and pediatric patients (specific pediatric dosing not specified in available sources).

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Units per dose examples present in available sources:
  - 600,000 units (examples of resulting serum levels provided)
  - 1,200,000 units (combination dose example: 600,000 units as benzathine salt + 600,000 units as procaine salt)
- Each supplied syringe example (disposable syringe formulation in available sources): contains the equivalent of 1,200,000 units of penicillin G comprising ~600,000 units as benzathine salt and ~600,000 units as procaine salt. (Formulation details beyond active content not to be interpreted as product presentation across jurisdictions.)

### Pharmacokinetic serum-level examples (adults)
- After IM administration of 600,000 units:
  - Peak blood levels approximately 1.0–1.3 units/mL within ~3 hours
  - Average ~0.32 units/mL at 12 hours; ~0.19 units/mL at 24 hours; ~0.03 units/mL at 7 days
- After IM administration of 1,200,000 units:
  - Peak blood levels approximately 2.1–2.6 units/mL within ~3 hours
  - Average ~0.75 units/mL at 12 hours; ~0.28 units/mL at 24 hours; ~0.04 units/mL at 7 days

### Dosing by indication
- Specific dosage regimens by indication (duration, frequency, pediatric dosing schedules) are not specified in available sources.

### Administration instructions
- For DEEP INTRAMUSCULAR injection only.
- Prefer upper outer quadrant of the buttock (dorsogluteal) or the ventrogluteal site for IM injection.
- Do NOT inject intravenously, intra-arterially, into or near major peripheral nerves or blood vessels, or admix with intravenous solutions.
- Do NOT inject into or near an artery or nerve (risk of permanent neurological damage and neurovascular injury).
- Avoid repeated injections into the anterolateral thigh (quadriceps femoris fibrosis and atrophy have been reported); anterolateral thigh administration is not recommended.
- If procaine sensitivity is suspected, perform intradermal test: inject 0.1 mL of a 1–2% procaine solution intradermally; erythema, wheal, flare, or eruption indicates sensitivity.
- If any signs of vascular compromise occur at or distal/proximal to injection site, obtain prompt specialist consultation.

### Missed dose instructions

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity (allergy) to any penicillin (including penicillin G) — absolute contraindication.
- Known hypersensitivity to procaine — absolute contraindication.
- Additional jurisdiction‑specific contraindications: Not specified in available sources beyond the above.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed / prominent warning
- **[US] WARNING: NOT FOR INTRAVENOUS USE. DO NOT INJECT INTRAVENOUSLY OR ADMIX WITH OTHER INTRAVENOUS SOLUTIONS. Inadvertent intravenous administration of penicillin G benzathine has been associated with cardiorespiratory arrest and death.** (This boxed warning is present in the available source.)

### General warnings and precautions
- Serious and occasionally fatal hypersensitivity (anaphylactic) reactions have been reported with penicillins; greater risk in individuals with prior penicillin hypersensitivity or sensitivity to multiple allergens. Discontinue therapy if allergic reaction occurs and institute appropriate treatment (epinephrine, oxygen, IV steroids, airway management including intubation as indicated).
- Severe cutaneous adverse reactions (SCAR) — Stevens‑Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), drug reaction with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms (DRESS), and acute generalized exanthematous pustulosis (AGEP) — have been reported. If SCAR is suspected, discontinue immediately and consider alternative therapy.
- Methemoglobinemia has been reported in association with local anesthetic use (e.g., procaine). Patients at higher risk include those with G6PD deficiency, congenital/idiopathic methemoglobinemia, cardiac or pulmonary compromise, infants <6 months, and those concurrently exposed to oxidizing agents. Monitor for cyanosis, abnormal blood coloration, headache, tachycardia, dyspnea, lightheadedness, and fatigue. Discontinue the drug and any oxidizing agents; treat supportively and consider methylene blue, exchange transfusion, or hyperbaric oxygen for severe cases.
- Clostridioides difficile–associated diarrhea (CDAD) can occur with nearly all antibacterial agents, including this combination; consider CDAD in any patient with diarrhea during or after antibiotic therapy (onset may be up to two months after antibiotic exposure). Discontinue inciting antibiotic if appropriate and manage per standard of care.
- Avoid intravascular administration; inadvertent intra‑arterial or perivascular injection has been associated with severe neurovascular damage, transverse myelitis with paralysis, gangrene requiring amputation, necrosis and sloughing (Nicolau syndrome), pallor/mottling/cyanosis of extremities, bleb formation, severe edema requiring fasciotomy; infants and small children most often affected.
- Use with caution in individuals with significant allergic history and/or asthma.
- Procaine sensitivity: a small percentage of patients are sensitive to procaine; antihistamines may help manage procaine reactions; if procaine sensitivity is confirmed, procaine‑containing penicillin preparations should not be used.
- Use of antibiotics may lead to overgrowth of nonsusceptible organisms (bacterial or fungal); discontinue therapy if new infection appears.
- In prolonged or high‑dose therapy, periodically evaluate renal and hematopoietic systems.
- Inform patients about signs of methemoglobinemia and CDAD and advise to seek immediate care if such signs develop.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor for signs of hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis during and after injection.
- Monitor renal function in patients with renal impairment and in elderly patients; consider baseline and periodic hematologic monitoring in prolonged therapy.
- Monitor for signs/symptoms of CDAD.
- Monitor injection site for neurovascular compromise, necrosis, infection, or other local reactions.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Do not use for infections where higher, sustained serum penicillin concentrations are required (e.g., severe pneumococcal infections, meningitis) — use penicillin G sodium or potassium formulations as clinically indicated.
- If concurrent local anesthetics or other oxidizing drugs are used, monitor for methemoglobinemia; review concomitant medications for agents associated with methemoglobinemia.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Probenecid: Concurrent administration increases and prolongs serum penicillin levels by decreasing apparent volume of distribution and slowing renal tubular secretion (competitive inhibition). Management: expect increased/prolonged penicillin levels when given with probenecid; adjust therapy as clinically indicated.
- Tetracyclines: Bacteriostatic tetracyclines may antagonize the bactericidal effect of penicillin; concurrent use should be avoided.

### Pharmacodynamic and other interactions
- Local anesthetics and oxidizing agents: Co‑exposure increases risk of methemoglobinemia. Examples of drugs associated with methemoglobinemia (not exhaustive):
  - Nitrates / nitrites: nitroglycerin, nitroprusside, nitric oxide, nitrous oxide
  - Local anesthetics: articaine, benzocaine, bupivacaine, lidocaine, mepivacaine, prilocaine, procaine, ropivacaine, tetracaine
  - Antineoplastic agents: cyclophosphamide, flutamide, hydroxyurea, ifosfamide, rasburicase
  - Antibiotics: dapsone, nitrofurantoin, para‑aminosalicylic acid, sulfonamides
  - Antimalarials: chloroquine, primaquine
  - Anticonvulsants: phenobarbital, sodium valproate
  - Other: acetaminophen, metoclopramide, quinine, sulfasalazine
- Management: review concomitant medications for oxidizing potential; monitor for methemoglobinemia signs; discontinue offending agents and treat as clinically indicated.

### Clinically significant interactions — management notes
- Avoid combining with tetracycline-class antibiotics when possible due to potential antagonism.
- Anticipate increased penicillin exposure with probenecid; adjust dosing if necessary based on clinical context and renal function.
- Exercise caution and monitor for methemoglobinemia when patients receive local anesthetics or other oxidizing drugs.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Reproduction studies in mouse, rat, and rabbit revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm due to penicillin G in available sources.
- Human experience with penicillins during pregnancy has not shown positive evidence of adverse fetal effect, but adequate and well‑controlled studies in pregnant women are not available.
- Recommendation: Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.

- Pregnancy category systems:
  - Specific jurisdictional pregnancy categories (e.g., Australian categories, US lettered categories): Not specified in available sources.

### Breastfeeding
- Soluble penicillin G (the hydrolysis product) is excreted in human milk. Caution is recommended when administering penicillin G benzathine and penicillin G procaine to nursing women.

### Fertility
- No impairment of fertility reported in animal reproduction studies cited in available sources.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Indication: the product is indicated for use in pediatric patients (specific dosing by age/weight not provided in available sources).
- Pharmacokinetic caution: Excretion is considerably delayed in neonates and young infants; use caution and consider altered elimination in these age groups.
- Specific pediatric dosing regimens and age‑specific administration guidance: Not specified in available sources.

### Elderly use
- Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects ≥65 years to determine whether they respond differently from younger subjects.
- Because penicillin is substantially renally excreted, and elderly patients are more likely to have decreased renal function, dose selection should be cautious and renal function monitoring may be useful.

### Renal impairment
- Penicillin G is excreted primarily by renal tubular excretion; excretion is considerably delayed in individuals with impaired renal function. The risk of toxic reactions may be greater in patients with renal impairment.
- Specific dose adjustments by eGFR or renal stage are not specified in available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Penicillin G (the active moiety after hydrolysis of benzathine and procaine salts) exerts bactericidal activity by inhibiting biosynthesis of bacterial cell wall peptidoglycan, rendering the cell wall osmotically unstable and resulting in bacterial death. Activity is greatest against organisms in active multiplication.

### Microbiology / Spectrum
- Active in vitro and in clinical infections against most isolates of:
  - Beta‑hemolytic streptococci (groups A, B, C, G, H, L, and M)
  - Streptococcus pneumoniae (penicillin‑susceptible isolates only)
- Not active against penicillinase‑producing bacteria or organisms resistant to beta‑lactams due to altered penicillin‑binding proteins.
- Resistance to penicillin G has not been reported in Streptococcus pyogenes in the available sources.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: Penicillin G benzathine and penicillin G procaine salts are of low solubility, resulting in slow release from IM injection sites; the salts are hydrolyzed to penicillin G, producing prolonged but lower serum concentrations compared with other parenteral penicillins.
- Distribution: Approximately 60% bound to serum protein. Distributed throughout body tissues with highest levels in kidney and lesser amounts in liver, skin, and intestines. Penetration into spinal fluid and other tissues is less than plasma levels.
- Metabolism: The salts are hydrolyzed to penicillin G (the active moiety).
- Elimination: Rapid renal tubular excretion with normal renal function; excretion delayed in neonates, young infants, and individuals with renal impairment.
- Pharmacokinetic serum‑level data: See Section 4 for measured serum concentrations after IM administration of 600,000 and 1,200,000 unit doses.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Time‑dependent bactericidal activity; efficacy depends on time above minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) for susceptible organisms.
