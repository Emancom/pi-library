# Editorial Product Information: Porfimer Sodium

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a single clinical reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or the clinical judgement required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians must consult the current approved PI in their region before initiating therapy.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Porfimer sodium.
- **Chemical Class:** Photoactivated radical generator (mixture of oligomers formed by ether and ester linkages of up to eight porphyrin units).
- **Brand Name:** **Photofrin** [US].
- **ATC Code:** L01XD01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Lyophilized powder for injection, 75 mg per single-dose vial [US]. 
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pinnacle Biologics, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Esophageal Cancer:** Palliation of patients with completely or partially obstructing esophageal cancer, specifically those who cannot be satisfactorily treated with Nd:YAG laser therapy [US].
- **Endobronchial Cancer:** 
    - Treatment of microinvasive non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) in patients where surgery and radiotherapy are not indicated [US].
    - Reduction of obstruction and palliation of symptoms in patients with completely or partially obstructing endobronchial NSCLC [US].
- **Barrett’s Esophagus:** Ablation of high-grade dysplasia (HGD) in Barrett’s esophagus (BE) patients who do not undergo esophagectomy [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Dose:** 2 mg/kg of body weight administered as a single slow intravenous injection over 3 to 5 minutes [US].
- **Process:** Photodynamic therapy (PDT) is a two-stage process. Stage 1 is the drug injection. Stage 2 is the illumination with laser light (630 nm) 40–50 hours later [US].

### Dosing by Indication (Photoactivation)
- **Esophageal Cancer:** 300 J/cm of fiber optic diffuser length. A second light application (without additional drug) may be given 96–120 hours after injection [US].
- **Endobronchial Cancer:** 200 J/cm of fiber optic diffuser length. A second light application (after debridement) may be given 96–120 hours after injection [US].
- **High-Grade Dysplasia (Barrett’s):** 130 J/cm of diffuser length using a centering balloon. A second light application of 50 J/cm (for "skip" areas) may be given 96–120 hours after injection [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Reconstitution:** Reconstitute each 75 mg vial with 31.8 mL of 5% Dextrose Injection or 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection to reach a concentration of 2.5 mg/mL [US].
- **Light Protection:** Reconstituted product is sensitive to bright light and must be used immediately [US].
- **Debridement:** Mandatory for endobronchial cancer 2–3 days after light treatment to remove necrotic debris and prevent obstruction [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Porphyria:** Known hypersensitivity to porphyrins or porphyria [US].
- **Fistulae:** Existing tracheoesophageal or bronchoesophageal fistula [US].
- **Vascular Erosion:** Tumors eroding into major blood vessels [US].
- **Emergency Use:** Not for emergency treatment of severe acute respiratory distress due to the 40–50 hour delay required for drug uptake [US].
- **Varices/Ulcers:** Esophageal or gastric varices; esophageal ulcers >1 cm in diameter [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Photosensitivity:** Patients remain photosensitive for at least 30 days (sometimes >90 days). Avoid direct sunlight and bright indoor light. Test skin for sensitivity before resuming outdoor activities. UV sunscreens are ineffective; protection must be against visible light [US].
- **Ocular Sensitivity:** Wear dark sunglasses with <4% white light transmittance for at least 30 days [US].
- **Fistula and Perforation:** High risk in patients with esophageal tumors eroding into the trachea or bronchial tree [US].
- **Hemorrhage:** Risk of fatal massive hemoptysis, particularly in endobronchial patients with large, central, or cavitating tumors [US].
- **Airway Obstruction:** Treatment-induced inflammation can cause acute airway obstruction. Monitor closely between light therapy and mandatory debridement [US].
- **Esophageal Strictures:** Occur in approximately 38% of patients treated for esophageal indications; multiple dilations may be required [US].
- **Radiotherapy Timing:** Allow 2–4 weeks between PDT and radiotherapy [US].
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** May cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status before initiation [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (>10%)
| Indication | Reactions |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Esophageal Cancer** | Anemia, pleural effusion, pyrexia, constipation, nausea, chest pain, abdominal pain, dyspnea, photosensitivity, pneumonia, vomiting, insomnia, back pain, pharyngitis [US]. |
| **Endobronchial Cancer** | Dyspnea, photosensitivity, hemoptysis, pyrexia, cough, pneumonia, bronchitis [US]. |
| **Barrett’s Esophagus** | Photosensitivity, esophageal stenosis/stricture, vomiting, chest pain, nausea, pyrexia, constipation, dysphagia, abdominal pain, pleural effusion, dehydration [US]. |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Respiratory:** Fatal massive hemoptysis, respiratory insufficiency, bronchial obstruction, pulmonary embolism [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Esophageal perforation, gastric ulcer, mediastinal inflammation [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Atrial fibrillation, cardiac failure, myocardial infarction [US].
- **Infusion Reactions:** Urticaria, bradycardia, hypotension [US postmarketing].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Photosensitizing Agents:** Concomitant use with tetracyclines, sulfonamides, phenothiazines, sulfonylureas, thiazide diuretics, and fluoroquinolones may increase the risk of severe photosensitivity [US].
- **PDT Antagonists:** Compounds that quench active oxygen or scavenge free radicals (e.g., glucocorticosteroids, allopurinol, calcium channel blockers) may decrease efficacy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: No specific letter category]. Based on animal data, porfimer sodium can cause embryo-fetal toxicity (increased resorptions and reduced fetal weight). Use is not recommended unless the benefit outweighs the risk to the fetus [US].
- **Contraception:** Females of reproductive potential and males with female partners of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for 5 months after the final dose [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on the presence of the drug in human milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for 5 months after the last dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in children have not been established [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness observed in patients over 60 years old [US].
- **Hepatic and Renal Impairment:** Elimination may be prolonged, potentially extending the period of photosensitivity beyond 90 days [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A two-stage process. Porfimer is selectively retained by tumors. Upon activation by 630 nm laser light, it generates singlet oxygen and superoxide/hydroxyl radicals, leading to cellular damage, ischemic necrosis (via thromboxane A2 release), and inflammatory responses [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** 90% protein bound. Clearance from most tissues occurs in 40–72 hours, but it is retained longer in tumors and the reticuloendothelial system [US].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 410 hours (17 days) after the first dose, increasing to 725 hours (30 days) with repeated dosing [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Esophageal Cancer:** In a study of 17 patients with complete obstruction, 94% achieved an objective tumor response, and 76% experienced improved dysphagia. Median duration of benefit was 69 days [US].
- **Endobronchial Cancer:** In randomized trials vs. Nd:YAG laser, PDT showed comparable response rates at 1 week (59% vs 58%) but superior response at 1 month or later (60% vs 41%) [US].
- **Barrett’s Esophagus:** In a trial of PDT + omeprazole vs. omeprazole alone, HGD was eliminated in 77% of the PDT group vs. 39% in the control group (p<0.0001) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store at controlled room temperature 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Protect from light [US].
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Use immediately. Detection of particulate matter is difficult due to the opaque nature of the solution; however, visual inspection for discoloration or large particles is required [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only (Rx-only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
