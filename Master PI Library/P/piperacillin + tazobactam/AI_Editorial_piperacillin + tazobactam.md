# Editorial Product Information: piperacillin + tazobactam

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **piperacillin + tazobactam**
- Chemical class:
  - Piperacillin: extended‑spectrum ureidopenicillin antibacterial (beta‑lactam)
  - Tazobactam: beta‑lactamase inhibitor
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intravenous (IV) infusion

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 1993.
- Most recent revision/update of source PI: Revised 5/2026 [US].
- Withdrawals, suspensions, or restrictions: Not specified in available sources.
- Regulatory dates for other jurisdictions: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Intra‑abdominal infections: treatment of appendicitis complicated by rupture/abscess and peritonitis caused by beta‑lactamase‑producing isolates of Escherichia coli and members of the Bacteroides fragilis group (B. fragilis, B. ovatus, B. thetaiotaomicron, B. vulgatus).
- Nosocomial (hospital‑acquired) pneumonia (moderate to severe) caused by beta‑lactamase‑producing isolates of Staphylococcus aureus and by piperacillin‑/tazobactam‑susceptible Acinetobacter baumannii, Haemophilus influenzae, Klebsiella pneumoniae, and Pseudomonas aeruginosa. (When P. aeruginosa is suspected/isolated, combination with an aminoglycoside is recommended.)
- Skin and skin structure infections (uncomplicated and complicated) in adults, including cellulitis, cutaneous abscesses, and ischemic/diabetic foot infections caused by beta‑lactamase‑producing strains of Staphylococcus aureus.
- Female pelvic infections in adults: postpartum endometritis and pelvic inflammatory disease caused by beta‑lactamase‑producing Escherichia coli.
- Community‑acquired pneumonia (moderate severity only) in adults caused by beta‑lactamase‑producing Haemophilus influenzae.
- Use only to treat or prevent infections proven or strongly suspected to be bacterial; use culture/susceptibility data and local epidemiology to guide therapy.

(Where approvals in jurisdictions other than the U.S. differ: Not specified in available sources.)

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adults)
- Typical total daily dose for most adult indications (non‑nosocomial pneumonia): 3.375 g every 6 hours (total daily dose 13.5 g; piperacillin 12.0 g / tazobactam 1.5 g) administered IV by infusion over 30 minutes.
- Initial presumptive treatment for adult nosocomial pneumonia: 4.5 g every 6 hours (total daily dose 18 g; piperacillin 16 g / tazobactam 2 g) administered IV by infusion over 30 minutes. Add an aminoglycoside when Pseudomonas aeruginosa coverage is required; continue aminoglycoside if P. aeruginosa is isolated.

### Dosing by renal function (adults)
Recommended IV dosing (administer each infusion over 30 minutes) by creatinine clearance and indication:
- Creatinine clearance > 40 mL/min:
  - All indications except nosocomial pneumonia: 3.375 g every 6 hours.
  - Nosocomial pneumonia: 4.5 g every 6 hours.
- Creatinine clearance 20–40 mL/min:
  - All indications except nosocomial pneumonia: 2.25 g every 6 hours.
  - Nosocomial pneumonia: 3.375 g every 6 hours.
- Creatinine clearance < 20 mL/min:
  - All indications except nosocomial pneumonia: 2.25 g every 8 hours.
  - Nosocomial pneumonia: 2.25 g every 6 hours.
- Hemodialysis (on dialysis days):
  - Maximum dose on hemodialysis days: 2.25 g every 12 hours for indications other than nosocomial pneumonia; 2.25 g every 8 hours for nosocomial pneumonia.
  - Because hemodialysis removes approximately 30%–40% of an administered dose, administer an additional supplemental dose of 0.75 g (0.67 g piperacillin and 0.08 g tazobactam) following each hemodialysis session on dialysis days.
- Continuous ambulatory peritoneal dialysis (CAPD): 2.25 g every 12 hours for indications other than nosocomial pneumonia; 2.25 g every 8 hours for nosocomial pneumonia. No additional dose necessary after CAPD exchange.

### Pediatric dosing
- Pediatric patients ≥ 2 months of age and weighing up to 40 kg with normal renal function:
  - Age 2 months to 9 months:
    - Appendicitis/peritonitis: 90 mg/kg (80 mg piperacillin + 10 mg tazobactam) every 8 hours.
    - Nosocomial pneumonia: 90 mg/kg every 6 hours.
  - Older than 9 months:
    - Appendicitis/peritonitis: 112.5 mg/kg (100 mg piperacillin + 12.5 mg tazobactam) every 8 hours.
    - Nosocomial pneumonia: 112.5 mg/kg every 6 hours.
- Pediatric patients > 40 kg and with normal renal function: use adult dosing.
- Dosage for pediatric patients with renal impairment: Not specified in available sources.

### Administration instructions
- Administer each dose by intravenous infusion over at least 30 minutes for adults and pediatric patients.
- Reconstitute/dilute and administer piperacillin + tazobactam separately from aminoglycosides due to in‑vitro inactivation of aminoglycosides by piperacillin; if simultaneous Y‑site co‑administration is necessary, follow compatibility guidance (see Section 8).
- Do not add supplementary medications to prepared solution. Inspect parenteral products visually before administration when solution and container permit.

- Missed dose instructions: Not specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed/Highlighted warnings
- **Serious hypersensitivity (anaphylactic/anaphylactoid) reactions** have occurred; discontinue immediately if anaphylaxis or severe allergic reaction occurs and institute appropriate therapy.
- **Severe cutaneous adverse reactions** including Stevens–Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), drug reaction with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms (DRESS), and acute generalized exanthematous pustulosis (AGEP) have been reported; discontinue if rash progresses.

(If any jurisdiction has a boxed warning that differs materially from the above: Not specified in available sources.)

### General warnings and precautions
- Hypersensitivity: Obtain careful history of prior hypersensitivity to beta‑lactams or other allergens prior to initiation; discontinue drug if allergic reaction occurs.
- Hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH): Cases reported in adults and pediatrics; signs may include fever, rash, lymphadenopathy, hepatosplenomegaly, and cytopenias. Discontinue if HLH is suspected and institute appropriate management.
- Rhabdomyolysis: Cases reported; discontinue if signs/symptoms (muscle pain/weakness, dark urine, elevated CK) occur and initiate appropriate therapy.
- Hematologic effects: Bleeding manifestations, leukopenia, neutropenia and other hematologic abnormalities have been reported. Leukopenia/neutropenia more commonly associated with prolonged therapy (≥ 21 days). Periodic monitoring of hematopoietic function is recommended during prolonged therapy.
- Central nervous system: Neuromuscular excitability and seizures have occurred with penicillins. Higher doses and renal impairment increase risk. Monitor patients at risk (renal impairment, seizure disorder) closely.
- Nephrotoxicity in critically ill patients: In a randomized multicenter controlled trial in critically ill patients, use of piperacillin + tazobactam was an independent risk factor for renal failure and associated with delayed recovery of renal function compared to other beta‑lactams. Consider alternative agents in critically ill patients when appropriate; monitor renal function if used.
- Electrolyte effects: The product contains a sodium load (sodium content per gram of piperacillin noted in sources); consider in patients requiring restricted sodium intake. Monitor electrolytes; hypokalemia is a possible consideration in susceptible patients.
- Clostridioides difficile–associated diarrhea: Evaluate if diarrhea occurs; C. difficile infection has been associated with broad‑spectrum antibacterial agents.
- Development of drug‑resistant bacteria: Use only when infection is proven or strongly suspected to be bacterial to limit resistance development.

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and periodic renal function monitoring, especially in critically ill patients and those with renal impairment.
- Monitor hematologic parameters during prolonged therapy (e.g., CBC with differential).
- Monitor for signs of hypersensitivity and severe cutaneous reactions.
- Monitor for signs/symptoms of HLH, rhabdomyolysis, neuromuscular excitability/seizures.
- Monitor coagulation parameters when co‑administered with heparin or oral anticoagulants.
- Monitor kidney function when co‑administered with vancomycin (increased risk of acute kidney injury).
- In hemodialysis patients receiving concomitant tobramycin, monitor tobramycin concentrations (piperacillin + tazobactam administration may significantly reduce tobramycin concentrations).

### Specific clinical precautions
- Co‑administration with probenecid: Probenecid prolongs half‑lives of both components; co‑administration is not recommended unless benefit outweighs risk.
- Neuromuscular blockers: May prolong neuromuscular blockade from vecuronium and other non‑depolarizing neuromuscular blockers; monitor for adverse effects.
- Use in patients with low potassium reserves: Monitor electrolytes and consider potassium supplementation if needed.

## 7. Adverse Effects
- The most common adverse reactions reported in clinical trials were: diarrhea, constipation, nausea, headache, and insomnia (incidence >5%).
- Additional adverse reactions and postmarketing reports include serious hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis, severe cutaneous adverse reactions (SJS/TEN/DRESS/AGEP), hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH), rhabdomyolysis, hematologic abnormalities (bleeding, leukopenia, neutropenia), central nervous system adverse events (seizures, neuromuscular excitability), nephrotoxicity/acute kidney injury (noted particularly in critically ill patients and with concomitant vancomycin), electrolyte disturbances, and Clostridioides difficile–associated diarrhea.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic interactions (clinically significant)
- Aminoglycosides:
  - In vitro inactivation of aminoglycosides by piperacillin has been observed. Reconstitute, dilute and administer piperacillin + tazobactam and aminoglycosides separately when possible.
  - If Y‑site co‑administration is necessary, limited compatibility data exist for amikacin and gentamicin under specific concentration and diluent conditions (see compatibility table below). Tobramycin is not compatible for simultaneous Y‑site co‑administration.
  - In hemodialysis patients, piperacillin + tazobactam administration can significantly reduce tobramycin concentrations; monitor tobramycin levels in these patients.
- Probenecid:
  - Probenecid prolongs the half‑lives of piperacillin and tazobactam; co‑administration should be avoided unless benefits outweigh risks.
- Vancomycin:
  - Concomitant use with vancomycin may increase risk of acute kidney injury; monitor renal function.
- Anticoagulants (heparin, oral anticoagulants):
  - Monitor coagulation parameters when piperacillin + tazobactam is administered concomitantly with anticoagulant therapy (alterations in clotting tests/bleeding have been reported).
- Neuromuscular blockers:
  - May potentiate or prolong neuromuscular blockade from vecuronium and other non‑depolarizing neuromuscular blocking agents; monitor neuromuscular function.
- Methotrexate:
  - Interaction heading present in source PI, but specific details not provided in available sources.
- Effects on laboratory tests:
  - Interference with certain laboratory tests has been reported (source lists an effects section but specifics not supplied in available sources).

### Aminoglycoside Y‑site compatibility (when co‑administration is necessary)
- Compatible aminoglycosides and conditions (concentrations and diluents established in available sources):
  - Amikacin:
    - Z‑dose (2.25 g, 3.375 g, 4.5 g): amikacin concentration 1.75 mg/mL to 7.5 mg/mL; acceptable diluents: 0.9% sodium chloride or 5% dextrose.
  - Gentamicin:
    - Z‑dose (2.25 g, 3.375 g, 4.5 g): gentamicin concentration 0.7 mg/mL to 3.32 mg/mL; acceptable diluents: 0.9% sodium chloride or 5% dextrose.
- Note: Compatibility was established only for the concentrations and diluents listed above. Simultaneous co‑administration in other manners or concentrations may result in inactivation of the aminoglycoside. Some higher‑concentration preparations (e.g., certain intermediate concentrations) may not be compatible with gentamicin for co‑administration.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Piperacillin: bactericidal antibacterial that inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis by binding penicillin‑binding proteins.
- Tazobactam: a beta‑lactamase inhibitor that extends the spectrum of piperacillin by inhibiting many plasmid and chromosomal beta‑lactamases.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Hemodialysis removes approximately 30%–40% of an administered dose of piperacillin + tazobactam.
- Probenecid prolongs the half‑life of both piperacillin and tazobactam.
- Additional PK parameters (absorption, distribution, metabolism, elimination quantitative values): Not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics / Microbiology
- Active against a range of gram‑negative and gram‑positive organisms when isolates are piperacillin + tazobactam susceptible; specific organisms cited in indications include E. coli, members of the Bacteroides fragilis group, Staphylococcus aureus, Acinetobacter baumannii, Haemophilus influenzae, Klebsiella pneumoniae, and Pseudomonas aeruginosa (the latter usually requires combination therapy).
- Detailed in vitro susceptibility breakpoints and pharmacodynamic targets: Not specified in available sources.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- A randomized, multicenter, controlled trial in critically ill patients identified piperacillin + tazobactam as an independent risk factor for renal failure and associated with delayed recovery of renal function compared to other beta‑lactam antibacterial agents.
- Clinical trial data summary for common adverse reactions: Diarrhea, constipation, nausea, headache and insomnia were the most frequently reported adverse reactions (>5% incidence in clinical trials).
