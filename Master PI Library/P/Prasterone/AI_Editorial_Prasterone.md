# Editorial Product Information: Prasterone

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local clinical guidelines required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this writing, **prasterone** is subject to additional monitoring in Australia to allow for rapid identification of new safety information.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Prasterone (also known as dehydroepiandrosterone or DHEA).
- **Chemical Class:** Adrenal steroid/precursor steroid.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Intrarosa** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** G03XX01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 6.5 mg intravaginal pessary.
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravaginal.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Theramex Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- Treatment of moderate to severe symptoms of vulvar and vaginal atrophy (VVA) in postmenopausal women [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** One 6.5 mg pessary administered once daily at bedtime.
- **Review and Duration:** Therapy should only be initiated for symptoms that adversely affect quality of life. Clinicians should reassess the risks and benefits at least every 6 months. Treatment should continue only as long as the benefit outweighs the risk [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** Administered using the provided applicator or fingers.
  - If using an applicator: Activate by pulling the plunger, place the flat end of the pessary into the applicator, and insert into the vagina as far as comfortable without force.
  - Applicators should be cleaned with water for 30 seconds after use and discarded after one week (three applicators are typically provided for a 28-day supply) [Australian].
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as remembered. If the next dose is due in less than 8 hours, the missed dose should be skipped. Do not double the dose [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
**Prasterone** is contraindicated in women with:
- Hypersensitivity to prasterone or the hard fat excipient.
- Undiagnosed genital bleeding.
- Known, past, or suspected breast cancer.
- Known or suspected oestrogen-dependent malignant tumours (e.g., endometrial cancer).
- Untreated endometrial hyperplasia.
- Known or suspected pregnancy [Australian].
- Acute liver disease or a history of liver disease where liver function tests have failed to return to normal.
- Previous or current venous thromboembolism (VTE), including deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism.
- Known thrombophilic disorders (e.g., protein C, protein S, or antithrombin deficiency).
- Active or recent arterial thromboembolic disease (e.g., angina, myocardial infarction).
- Porphyria [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **General HRT Risks:** Although systemic exposure to oestrogen remains within the normal postmenopausal range with local prasterone, the risks associated with systemic Hormone Replacement Therapy (HRT)—such as breast cancer, ovarian cancer, VTE, and stroke—should be considered for long-term or repeated use [Australian].
- **Endometrial Safety:** Endometrial safety has not been studied for more than one year. Any bleeding or spotting must be investigated, potentially including endometrial biopsy.
- **Medical Examination:** Prior to initiation and during treatment, periodic check-ups (including pelvic/breast exams, blood pressure, and Pap smears) should be performed according to current clinical needs and screening practices.
- **Conditions Requiring Supervision:** Close supervision is required if the patient has or had: leiomyoma (uterine fibroids), endometriosis, hypertension, liver disorders, diabetes, cholelithiasis, migraine, systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), epilepsy, asthma, or otosclerosis [Australian].
- **Withdrawal of Therapy:** Discontinue immediately if jaundice, significant increase in blood pressure, new onset migraine-type headache, or pregnancy occurs.
- **Latex Products:** **Intrarosa** may weaken condoms, diaphragms, or cervical caps made of latex. These should be avoided during treatment [Australian].
- **Vaginal Infection:** Treat any existing vaginal infection with appropriate antimicrobials before starting prasterone.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most common adverse reaction reported is application site discharge, attributed to the melting of the hard fat vehicle and increased vaginal secretions.

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Not specified (Application site discharge reached ~8% in pooled trials) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Application site discharge, urinary tract infection (UTI), abnormal Pap smear (mostly ASCUS or LSIL), weight fluctuation |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Cervical/uterine polyps, benign breast mass, hypertension |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** While rare in clinical trials for local prasterone, systemic HRT risks (VTE, stroke, breast cancer) are noted as precautions. One case of breast cancer and one case of ovarian cancer were reported in 1196 women during a 52-week clinical study [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Systemic and Vaginal Hormones:** Concomitant use with systemic HRT (oestrogen, progestogen, or androgens) or other vaginal oestrogens has not been investigated and is not recommended [Australian].
- **In vitro Data:** Prasterone did not inhibit major CYP enzymes (CYP1A2, 2B7, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 2E1, or 3A4) at clinically relevant concentrations.
- **Latex:** Physical interaction with latex-based contraceptives (weakening of the material) is a significant non-pharmacokinetic interaction [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category D.** Contraindicated in pregnancy. Prasterone is converted into oestradiol and testosterone, which could potentially affect fetal development. If pregnancy occurs, treatment must be stopped immediately [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not indicated for use in breastfeeding women. No data are available regarding the presence of prasterone in human milk or its effects on milk production/the breastfed infant.
- **Fertility:** Not indicated in fertile women. No animal fertility studies have been conducted.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No data available; not indicated for use in children [Australian].
- **Elderly:** No dose adjustment is required in the elderly. Approximately 17% of participants in 12-week trials were over 65 years of age.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required; however, pharmacokinetics have not been specifically studied in this population.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for mild/moderate cases, though pharmacokinetics have not been studied. Use is contraindicated in acute or severe liver disease where LFTs remain abnormal [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Prasterone is biochemically identical to endogenous human DHEA. It is a precursor steroid that is converted intracellularly within the vaginal tissues into oestrogens and androgens. These metabolites activate local oestrogen and androgen receptors. It increases superficial/intermediate cells and decreases parabasal cells in the vaginal mucosa while lowering vaginal pH [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Local administration results in intracellular conversion. Systemic Cmax and AUC of prasterone and its metabolites (testosterone, oestradiol) increase slightly but remain within normal postmenopausal ranges.
  - **Metabolism:** Metabolised in the same manner as endogenous DHEA.
  - **Excretion:** Not specifically studied for this application [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy:** Data from two pivotal Phase III trials (ERC-231 and ERC-238) in the US and Canada involved postmenopausal women (ages 40–80).
- **Outcomes:** After 12 weeks of daily treatment, prasterone 6.5 mg showed significant improvement over placebo in the three co-primary endpoints: increase in superficial cells, decrease in parabasal cells, and decrease in vaginal pH (p<0.0001 for all).
- **Symptom Relief:** A significant reduction in the severity of dyspareunia (the "most bothersome symptom") was observed compared to placebo (p=0.0132 in Study 1; p=0.0002 in Study 2) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C.
- **Handling:** Store in the original container to protect from light. Do not freeze [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4) [Australian].
- **Legal Status:** Registration as a new medicinal product (2023) with a requirement for additional monitoring [Australian].
