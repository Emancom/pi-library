# Editorial Product Information: Perhexiline

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)**. No documents from the US (FDA), European (EMA), UK (MHRA), Canadian (Health Canada), or Japanese (PMDA) were provided for this synthesis.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial resource and does not substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Perhexiline maleate.
- **Chemical Class:** Piperidine derivative.
- **Brand Names:** **Pexsig** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** C01EB06.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 100 mg scored tablets [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aspen Pharmacare Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Angina Pectoris:** Indicated to reduce the frequency of moderate to severe attacks of angina pectoris resulting from coronary artery disease in patients who are refractory to or intolerant of other conventional therapies, or in whom such therapies are contraindicated [Australian].
- **Editorial Note:** Due to the risk of severe toxicity, perhexiline is strictly reserved as second- or third-line therapy for patients with intractable angina who are not candidates for coronary bypass surgery [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** Therapy should be initiated at 100 mg once daily.
- **Titration:** Dosage must be adjusted progressively (increased or decreased) at intervals of 2 to 4 weeks based on therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) of plasma levels.
- **Maintenance Dose:** The objective is to maintain the minimum effective dose. While the typical maximum is 300 mg daily in divided doses, certain cases may require up to 400 mg daily [Australian].
- **Therapeutic Drug Monitoring (TDM):**
    - Regular monitoring is mandatory, starting at the end of the first week of treatment.
    - **Target Range:** 0.15 to 0.60 µg/mL (0.6 mg/L) [Australian].
    - Dose increases should only occur if levels are subtherapeutic and at least 2–4 weeks have passed since the previous adjustment.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Impaired Hepatic or Renal Function:** Perhexiline is contraindicated in patients with any degree of liver or kidney impairment [Australian].
- **Porphyria:** A history of porphyria is a contraindication [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to perhexiline maleate or any excipients (including lactose and sucrose).
- **Monitoring Limitation:** Perhexiline must **not** be prescribed if facilities for determining plasma concentrations are unavailable [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources; however, the PI contains high-level warnings regarding monitoring.
- **Neurological Toxicity:** Long-term therapy is associated with sensory, motor, and autonomic neuropathy. Cases include demyelination and denervation atrophy. Bilateral papilloedema and vision loss (reversible or permanent) have been reported [Australian].
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Elevation of liver enzymes is common. Severe cases, including subacute alcoholic-type hepatitis, cirrhosis, and fatal hepatic damage, have occurred [Australian].
- **Hypoglycaemia:** Perhexiline can significantly lower blood sugar, particularly in the first three days of therapy. Caution is required in patients with diabetes mellitus, especially those on insulin or sulphonylureas [Australian].
- **Weight Loss:** Significant weight loss (>10% of body weight) may occur and can lead to cachexia [Australian].
- **Cardiac Effects:** May affect ventricular conduction systems; use caution in patients with conduction disturbances. Safety post-myocardial infarction has not been established [Australian].
- **Mandatory Discontinuation:** Treatment must be stopped immediately upon the appearance of peripheral neuropathy, clinical signs of liver disease, persistent liver enzyme elevations, marked hypoglycaemia, or excessive weight loss [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Approximately 65% of patients experience adverse effects; 7–8% require discontinuation due to severity [Australian].

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Dizziness, "drunken" sensation, gait disorders, unsteadiness, nausea, vomiting, headache, anorexia, moderate weight loss (2–4 kg), elevated liver enzymes (AST, ALT, ALP, LDH) and bilirubin. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Hypoglycaemia (especially in diabetics), ECG changes (T-wave depression, QT prolongation), increased lipids/triglycerides. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Profound weakness, nervousness, insomnia, tremors, paraesthesias, syncope, genitourinary disorders, libido changes, flushing, rash, urticaria. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Diplopia, abdominal pain, extrapyramidal syndromes, seizures, torsade de pointes. |
| **Serious/Severe** | Polyradiculoneuritis (demyelinating), cachexia, cirrhosis, subacute hepatitis, fatal hepatotoxicity. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP2D6 Inhibitors and Substrates:** Perhexiline is metabolised by CYP2D6. Concomitant use with inhibitors (e.g., SSRIs like fluoxetine, paroxetine; quinidine; haloperidol) or other substrates can cause toxic plasma levels. Frequent TDM is required [Australian].
- **Antidiabetic Agents:** Increased risk of severe hypoglycaemia when used with insulin or sulphonylureas.
- **Adriamycin:** May increase cellular concentrations of adriamycin, leading to toxicity [Australian].
- **Anticoagulants:** Potential for increased liver enzyme elevations when combined with warfarin or phenindione [Australian].
- **Beta-blockers:** Increased risk of hypoglycaemia [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B2:** Limited human data. Animal studies show reduced fertility and increased foetal resorptions/decreased weight. Potential benefits must outweigh risks [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if perhexiline is excreted in breast milk. Use is not recommended for nursing mothers [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal data suggests a reduction in fertility in both male and female rats [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended; safety and efficacy have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Standard adult dosing applies, but use is subject to the strict contraindication regarding renal and hepatic impairment, which are more prevalent in this population [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** An anti-anginal agent that inhibits carnitine palmitoyltransferase-1 (CPT-1), shifting myocardial metabolism from fatty acid catabolism to glucose utilisation. This results in an oxygen-sparing effect and increased myocardial efficiency [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Exhibits cardiac membrane effects similar to quinidine; reduces exercise-induced tachycardia without affecting resting heart rate; possesses mild diuretic effects [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Well absorbed (>80%) post-oral administration.
    - **Distribution:** Large volume of distribution; highly protein-bound (>90%); crosses the blood-brain barrier.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by CYP2D6. Metabolism is saturable and non-linear. Populations include fast, intermediate, and slow hydroxylators (approx. 7% of Caucasians are slow metabolisers) [Australian].
    - **Excretion:** Cleared via hepatic metabolism; metabolites excreted in bile and urine (ratio 1:2).
    - **Half-life:** Non-linear; typically 2 to 6 days in most patients, but can exceed 30 days in slow metabolisers [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Not specified in available sources. The PI notes that while perhexiline has been prescribed with various cardiovascular drugs, controlled clinical experiments only exist for its use with nitroglycerin [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store in a cool, dry place below 30°C [Australian].
- **Container:** Glass bottles of 100 tablets with child-resistant closures [Australian].

- **Poison Schedule:**
