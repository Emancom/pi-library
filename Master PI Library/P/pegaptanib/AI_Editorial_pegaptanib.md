# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: US (FDA). [Note: Only the United States Product Information was provided for this synthesis.]
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP); a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (trimethylammonium).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically range from 50 mg/kg, adjusted as clinically required. The maximum recorded dose is 300 mg/kg.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Titration:** Dose adjustments should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine levels (target range: 40–50 µmol/L). Adjustments may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion as the vial contains no preservatives.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred—predominantly in ESRD patients on dialysis. Onset may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and initiate medical management [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a known seizure disorder, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** In metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology (e.g., ESRD or metabolic errors).

### Frequency Data (ESRD Clinical Trials)
| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (30–35%), headache (22–37%), pharyngitis (15–27%), diarrhea (10–35%), nausea/vomiting (8–21%), hypertension (18–21%), fever (5–12%), asthenia (8–12%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, chest pain, infection, flu syndrome, cough increase, dyspnea, tachycardia, hypercalcemia, weight decrease/increase |

### Serious and Notable Effects
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (see Warnings and Precautions).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis, transient nausea, and vomiting.
- **Other:** A distinct "fishy" body odor has been reported [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not explicitly lettered, but labeled as "use only if clearly needed"]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. There are no adequate studies in pregnant women.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. While supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied, dairy cow studies show increased milk concentrations after administration. The risks of excess intake to the infant must be weighed against maternal benefit [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg) and follows the same principles as adult dosing for metabolic disorders.
- **Elderly Use:** Specific data not provided; generally requires cautious dosing as for other populations.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Intravenous:** The primary route of elimination for levocarnitine and its metabolites (TMA and TMAO) is renal. 
    - **Oral Caution:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO), which are normally cleared by the kidneys [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for beta-oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, it corrects low plasma carnitine levels and addresses the increased acylcarnitine/carnitine ratio caused by dialysis losses and reduced dietary intake.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) and γ-butyrobetaine.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis carnitine levels from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. Trough concentrations remained stable or increased over 12–24 weeks in longer-term studies [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's (concentration range 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) [US].
