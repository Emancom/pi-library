# Editorial Product Information: Pravastatin

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided from the Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA) jurisdiction. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Pravastatin for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical guide; it is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pravastatin sodium.
- **Chemical Class:** HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor (Statin).
- **Brand Names:** APX-Pravastatin [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Tablets:** 10 mg, 20 mg, 40 mg, and 80 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypercholesterolaemia:** As an adjunct to diet for the treatment of primary hypercholesterolaemia, after secondary causes have been investigated and treated [Australian].
- **Prevention of Coronary Heart Disease:** To reduce the risk of cardiovascular mortality and morbidity in patients with hypercholesterolaemia without previous myocardial infarction (MI) [Australian].
- **Secondary Prevention of Cardiovascular Events:** 
    - Patients with previous MI and normal or elevated cholesterol levels [Australian].
    - Patients with unstable angina pectoris [Australian].
- **Paediatric Population:** Adjunct to diet and lifestyle modification for the treatment of Heterozygous Familial Hypercholesterolaemia (HeFH) in children and adolescents aged 8 years and older [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Therapeutic Range:** 10 mg to 80 mg once daily.
- **Administration:** Should be taken once daily, preferably at bedtime. For maximum effect, it should be taken on an empty stomach [Australian].
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg to 20 mg once daily.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Lipid levels should be monitored every four weeks, and the dose adjusted according to patient response.

### Dosing by Indication
- **Prevention of Coronary Heart Disease/Secondary Prevention of MI:** 40 mg once daily [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing
- **Children (8–13 years):** 20 mg once daily. Doses >20 mg have not been studied in this group [Australian].
- **Adolescents (14–18 years):** 40 mg once daily. Doses >40 mg have not been studied in this group [Australian].

### Concomitant Therapy
- **Ciclosporin:** Initiate at 10 mg once daily; titrate with extreme caution [Australian].
- **Bile Acid Sequestrants:** Administer pravastatin either 1 hour before or 4 hours after cholestyramine/colestipol to avoid absorption interference [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to pravastatin sodium or any tablet excipients (e.g., lactose) [Australian].
- **Active Hepatic Disease:** Including unexplained, persistent elevations of serum transaminases exceeding 3 times the upper limit of normal (ULN) [Australian].
- **Pregnancy:** Pravastatin is contraindicated during pregnancy and in women of childbearing potential unless using effective contraception [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Skeletal Muscle Effects:** Myalgia, myopathy, and rare, potentially fatal rhabdomyolysis have been reported. Risk is increased in patients with predisposing factors: 
    - Age >65 years.
    - Renal impairment.
    - Uncontrolled hypothyroidism.
- **Creatine Kinase (CK) Monitoring:** Routine monitoring is not recommended for asymptomatic adults, but CK should be measured before treatment in those with predisposing factors. Therapy must be interrupted if CK >5 x ULN or if severe muscular symptoms occur [Australian].
- **Hepatic Effects:** Elevations in transaminases may occur. Liver function tests (LFTs) should be performed prior to initiation and periodically thereafter. Therapy should be discontinued if ALT/AST exceed 3 x ULN persistently [Australian].
- **Diabetes Mellitus:** Statins are associated with a risk of new-onset type 2 diabetes, particularly in patients with pre-existing risk factors (raised fasting glucose, BMI >30 kg/m², hypertension) [Australian].
- **Interstitial Lung Disease:** Exceptional cases have been reported with long-term statin use. Discontinue if suspected [Australian].
- **Endocrine Function:** Caution is advised when co-administered with drugs that may diminish steroid hormone levels (e.g., ketoconazole, spironolactone) [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Pravastatin is generally well-tolerated. Adverse events are typically mild and transient.

| **Common (>1%)** | Musculoskeletal | Musculoskeletal pain (5.9%), muscle cramp (2.0%), myalgia (1.4%) |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Fusidic Acid:** **Contraindicated.** Increased risk of rhabdomyolysis. Pravastatin must be withheld during systemic fusidic acid treatment and for 7 days after [Australian].
- **Fibrates (e.g., Gemfibrozil):** Generally avoided due to increased risk of myopathy and rhabdomyolysis [Australian].
- **Ciclosporin:** Increases pravastatin plasma concentrations significantly (approx. 5-10 fold) [Australian].
- **Macrolides (Erythromycin/Clarithromycin):** Potential to increase statin exposure; use with caution [Australian].
- **Colchicine:** Increased risk of myopathy/rhabdomyolysis [Australian].
- **Vitamin K Antagonists (e.g., Warfarin):** May increase INR; monitoring is required during initiation or dose changes of pravastatin [Australian].
- **Lenalidomide:** Increased risk of rhabdomyolysis; reinforced monitoring is warranted [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D [Australian].** Potential for foetal harm due to inhibition of cholesterol synthesis, which is essential for foetal development. Discontinue immediately if pregnancy occurs.
- **Breastfeeding:** A negligible amount is excreted in human milk. Due to potential serious adverse reactions in infants, breastfeeding is not recommended while taking pravastatin [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in rat studies at doses up to 500 mg/kg [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for HeFH in patients $\ge$8 years. Safety in children <8 years has not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly (>65 years):** Increased AUC observed. Start at 10 mg daily and monitor closely for myopathy [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Start with 10 mg once daily. Close clinical monitoring is required [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Start with 10 mg once daily. Caution is required in patients with a history of liver disease or heavy alcohol use [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Competitive inhibition of HMG-CoA reductase, the rate-limiting enzyme in cholesterol biosynthesis. This leads to a reduction in intracellular cholesterol, upregulation of LDL receptors, and increased clearance of circulating LDL-C. It also inhibits the hepatic synthesis of VLDL [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid; peak plasma levels in 1–1.5 hours. Bioavailability is 17%.
    - **Distribution:** 50% bound to plasma proteins. Hydrophilic in nature.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensive first-pass extraction in the liver. The major metabolite (3 $\alpha$-hydroxy isomer) has significantly less inhibitory activity.
    - **Elimination:** Dual routes; 20% in urine, 70% in faeces. Plasma half-life is 1.5–2 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **WOS (West of Scotland Study):** Demonstrated a 31% reduction in the risk of coronary heart disease death or non-fatal MI in hypercholesterolaemic males without prior MI [Australian].
- **CARE (Cholesterol and Recurrent Events):** Showed a 24% reduction in recurrent coronary events in post-MI patients with average cholesterol levels [Australian].
- **LIPID (Long-Term Intervention with Pravastatin in Ischaemic Disease):** Demonstrated a 24% reduction in CHD death and a 23% reduction in total mortality in patients with prior MI or unstable angina [Australian].
- **PLAC I & II:** Angiographic and ultrasound trials showing pravastatin significantly slows the progression of atherosclerosis [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Conditions:** Protect from light [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
