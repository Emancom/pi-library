# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a structured format for quick reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Naturally occurring amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources (A16AA01).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn metabolic errors [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** Administered intravenously.
- **Metabolic Disorders:**
    - **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg given as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
    - **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours (and not less than every 6 hours) [US].
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg may be required based on clinical response [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - **Titration:** Dose adjustments should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are adequate [US].
- **Monitoring:** For parenteral therapy, it is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known:** There are no known contraindications listed in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued and appropriate treatment initiated [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** In ESRD patients, chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine (not specific to the IV formulation) may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].
- **General Precautions:** Parenteral products should be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration before administration [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (≥5% in ESRD clinical trials):**
    - **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, back pain, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain.
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia.
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting.
    - **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia.
    - **Nervous System:** Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia.
    - **Respiratory:** Cough increase, pharyngitis, rhinitis.
- **Less Frequent/Post-marketing:**
    - Transient nausea and vomiting.
    - **Body Odor:** A notable but less frequent adverse effect.
    - Gastritis.
    - Seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Management:** Monitor INR levels at the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine is not bound to plasma proteins, reducing the likelihood of protein-binding displacement interactions [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Risk Summary:** No adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Animal reproductive studies (rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is based on mg/kg body weight, applicable to paediatric patients as specified in dosage instructions [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the available source; however, dosing in ESRD is based on dialysis requirements and plasma monitoring [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. For IV use in ESRD, monitor for metabolite accumulation and adjust doses based on trough carnitine levels [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into the mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound; distribution half-life is approximately 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, IV administration increases plasma carnitine concentrations and may alleviate metabolic abnormalities associated with carnitine deficiency [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis trough levels from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels at 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservative [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available source.
