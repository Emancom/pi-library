# Editorial Product Information: Pomalidomide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical data into a structured reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and must not be used as a substitute for the official, current Product Information in any specific jurisdiction where the drug is prescribed.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pomalidomide (a thalidomide analogue).
- **Chemical Class:** Immunomodulating agent.
- **Brand Names:** Pomalidomide Sandoz [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L04AX06.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Hard gelatin capsules in 1 mg, 2 mg, 3 mg, and 4 mg strengths.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sandoz Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Pomalidomide is indicated for the treatment of relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma (RRMM) in the following settings [Australian-approved]:
- **Combination therapy with bortezomib and dexamethasone:** For patients who have received at least one prior treatment regimen, which must have included lenalidomide.
- **Combination therapy with dexamethasone:** For patients who have received at least two prior treatment regimens, including both lenalidomide and bortezomib, and who have demonstrated disease progression on their last therapy.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **With Bortezomib and Dexamethasone:** 4 mg orally once daily on Days 1–14 of repeated 21-day cycles.
- **With Dexamethasone alone:** 4 mg orally once daily on Days 1–21 of repeated 28-day cycles.

### Administration Instructions
- Capsules must be swallowed whole, preferably with water.
- May be taken with or without food.
- **Handling:** Do not open, break, or crush capsules. If powder contacts the skin, wash immediately with soap and water; for mucous membranes, flush with water.

### Missed Dose Instructions
- If <12 hours have elapsed since the missed dose, the patient may take the dose. 
- If >12 hours have elapsed, the patient should skip the dose and resume the next dose at the normal time the following day.

### Initiation Criteria
To start a new cycle, the absolute neutrophil count (ANC) must be $\geq 1.0 \times 10^9$/L and the platelet count must be $\geq 50 \times 10^9$/L.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy:** Pomalidomide is a known human teratogen [Australian].
- **Females of childbearing potential:** Unless all conditions of the Pregnancy Prevention Program (PPP) are met.
- **Male patients:** Unless all conditions of the PPP are met.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to pomalidomide or any excipient.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning [Australian]
**Teratogenic Effects:** Pomalidomide is a thalidomide analogue. Thalidomide is a known human teratogen causing severe, life-threatening birth defects. Pomalidomide must not be taken during pregnancy. Pregnancy must be avoided for 4 weeks before, during, and for 4 weeks after treatment.

### General Warnings
- **Hematologic Events:** Neutropenia is the most common Grade 3/4 reaction. Monitor complete blood counts (CBC) weekly for the first 8 weeks, then monthly.
- **Venous Thromboembolism (VTE):** Significant risk of deep vein thrombosis (DVT) and pulmonary embolism (PE). Prophylactic anticoagulation (e.g., aspirin, warfarin, heparin) is recommended based on individual risk assessment.
- **Serious Skin Reactions:** Cases of Angioedema, SJS, TEN, and DRESS have been reported. Discontinue permanently for Grade 4 rash, exfoliative/bullous rash, or suspected SJS/TEN/DRESS.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Reactivation of Hepatitis B has occurred. Monitor liver function tests regularly.
- **Other:** Risk of Peripheral Neuropathy, Tumour Lysis Syndrome (TLS), and Second Primary Malignancies.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequencies are based on clinical trial data (CC-4047-MM-007 and CC-4047-MM-003).

| Frequency | Adverse Reactions |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Anaemia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, pneumonia, bronchitis, upper respiratory tract infection, fatigue, pyrexia, peripheral oedema, constipation, diarrhoea, nausea, dyspnoea, cough, bone pain, muscle spasms, insomnia. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Sepsis, febrile neutropenia, basal cell carcinoma, hypokalaemia, hyperglycaemia, dizziness, tremor, peripheral sensory neuropathy, deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism, vomiting, rash, renal failure. |
| **Uncommon (<1%)** | Hyperbilirubinaemia, angioedema, urticaria, pneumonitis, gastrointestinal haemorrhage. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP1A2 Inhibitors:** Co-administration with strong CYP1A2 inhibitors (e.g., fluvoxamine) increases pomalidomide exposure. If co-administered, reduce pomalidomide dose by 50% [Australian].
- **Smoking:** Induces CYP1A2 and reduces pomalidomide AUC by approximately 32%. Advise patients that smoking may reduce efficacy.
- **Dexamethasone:** Co-administration does not significantly affect pomalidomide pharmacokinetics.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Use caution and monitor INR closely when pomalidomide is used with dexamethasone, as the latter is a weak-to-moderate inducer of metabolism.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category X [Australian]**. Pomalidomide is contraindicated in pregnancy. All patients (male and female) must comply with a restricted distribution Pregnancy Prevention Program.
- **Contraception:** Females of childbearing potential must use at least one highly effective method for 4 weeks prior to, during, and 4 weeks after treatment. Males must use condoms during treatment and for 1 week after discontinuation if the partner is pregnant or of childbearing potential.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if pomalidomide is excreted in human milk. Because of the potential for serious adverse reactions in infants, breastfeeding should be discontinued.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed increased postimplantation loss; effects were attributed to female treatment.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Elderly:** No dose adjustment of pomalidomide is required. However, for patients >75 years, the dose of dexamethasone should be reduced (e.g., 20 mg/day instead of 40 mg/day in the 28-day cycle) [Australian].
- **Paediatric:** No experience in children/adolescents (0–18 years); use is not recommended.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required. On dialysis days, the dose should be taken *after* the dialysis session.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Patients with bilirubin >1.5x ULN were excluded from trials. Monitor closely; consider dose reduction/interruption if liver function deteriorates.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Pomalidomide binds to cereblon (CRBN), part of an E3 ubiquitin ligase complex. This leads to the ubiquitination and degradation of transcription factors Ikaros and Aiolos, resulting in direct tumouricidal and immunomodulatory effects, including inhibition of TNF-$\alpha$ and IL-6.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** $C_{max}$ reached in 2–3 hours; >70% absorbed.
    - **Distribution:** Distributed in semen at ~67% of plasma levels. Plasma protein binding is 12–44%.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP1A2 and CYP3A4, with minor contributions from CYP2C19 and CYP2D6.
    - **Elimination:** Median half-life is ~7.5 hours (patients) to 9.5 hours (healthy subjects). Approximately 73% is excreted in urine and 15% in faeces.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **MM-007 (Pom+Btz+LD-dex vs Btz+LD-dex):** Demonstrated improved median Progression-Free Survival (PFS) of 11.2 months vs 7.1 months (HR 0.61; p < 0.0001) [Australian].
- **MM-003 (Pom+LD-dex vs HD-dex):** Demonstrated improved median PFS of 16 weeks vs 8.1 weeks (p < 0.001) and improved Overall Survival (OS) of 55.4 weeks vs 35.1 weeks (p = 0.028).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Packaging:** Store in the original package to protect from light/moisture.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **S4 - Prescription Only Medicine [Australian]**.
- **Distribution:** Available only through a restricted distribution program (Pregnancy Prevention Program).
