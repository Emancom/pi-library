# Editorial Product Information: Paclitaxel (Nanoparticle Albumin-Bound)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of **ABRAXANE** (nanoparticle albumin-bound paclitaxel) for healthcare professionals. 
- This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference; it is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Paclitaxel (nanoparticle albumin-bound)
- **Chemical Class:** Antimicrotubule agent (Taxane)
- **Brand Names:** **ABRAXANE** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** L01CD01 (Taxanes)
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strengths:** 
    - Sterile, white to yellow, lyophilised powder for injectable suspension. 
    - 100 mg paclitaxel in a 50 mL glass vial [Australian].
    - 250 mg paclitaxel in a 100 mL glass vial (Note: 250 mg presentation is not currently marketed in [Australian]).
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Specialised Therapeutics Australia Pty Ltd (Distributed for Abraxis BioScience Australia/Bristol-Myers Squibb) [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metastatic Breast Cancer:** Treatment of metastatic carcinoma of the breast following the failure of anthracycline therapy [Australian-approved].
- **Non-small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC):** First-line treatment of NSCLC in combination with carboplatin for patients who are not candidates for potentially curative surgery and/or radiation [Australian-approved].
- **Metastatic Adenocarcinoma of the Pancreas:** First-line treatment in combination with gemcitabine [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metastatic Breast Cancer:** 260 mg/m² administered IV over 30 minutes every 3 weeks.
- **Non-small Cell Lung Cancer:** 100 mg/m² administered IV over 30 minutes on Days 1, 8, and 15 of each 21-day cycle. Carboplatin (AUC = 6 mg•min/mL) should be administered on Day 1 only, immediately after **ABRAXANE**.
- **Metastatic Adenocarcinoma of the Pancreas:** 125 mg/m² administered IV over 30 minutes on Days 1, 8, and 15 of each 28-day cycle. Gemcitabine (1000 mg/m²) is administered immediately after **ABRAXANE**.

### Preparation and Administration
- **Reconstitution:** Each vial must be reconstituted with 0.9% Sodium Chloride to a final concentration of **5 mg/mL**. 
- **Instruction:** Inject the diluent slowly over 1 minute onto the inside wall of the vial to avoid foaming. Let the vial stand for 5 minutes, then gently swirl/invert for 2 minutes.
- **Filtration:** If proteinaceous strands are visible in the IV bag, use a 15 µm filter. **Do not use filters with a pore size less than 15 µm.**
- **Premedication:** No premedication to prevent hypersensitivity is required prior to administration [Australian].

### Missed Dose
- For metastatic breast cancer, if a dose is missed, it should be administered as soon as possible, consistent with good medical practice.

## 4. Contraindications
- Baseline neutrophil counts < 1.5 x 10⁹/L.
- Hypersensitivity to paclitaxel or human albumin.
- Pregnancy and lactation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Interchangeability:** **ABRAXANE** is an albumin-bound form of paclitaxel and is **not clinically interchangeable** with other paclitaxel formulations (e.g., solvent-based paclitaxel). Do not substitute for or with other paclitaxel products.
- **Haematology:** Bone marrow suppression (primarily neutropenia) is dose-dependent and the primary dose-limiting toxicity. Monitoring of frequent peripheral blood cell counts is required. 
- **Neuropathy:** Peripheral neuropathy occurs frequently and is dose-dependent. Treatment should be withheld for Grade 3 symptoms until resolution to Grade 1 or 2, followed by dose reduction.
- **Sepsis:** Reported in 5% of pancreatic cancer patients. High risk in patients with biliary obstruction or stents.
- **Pneumonitis:** Monitor for signs and symptoms; permanently discontinue if diagnosed.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Rare reports of severe anaphylaxis. If a severe reaction occurs, the patient should not be re-challenged. Cross-hypersensitivity with other taxanes may occur.
- **Cardiotoxicity:** Uncommon events of CHF and left ventricular dysfunction. Monitoring is required, especially in patients with prior anthracycline exposure or cardiac history.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Increased risk of toxicity, particularly myelosuppression.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency by System Organ Class
| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Neutropenia, anaemia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, lymphopenia, neuropathy (sensory/peripheral), alopecia, rash, arthralgia, myalgia, fatigue, asthenia, pyrexia, nausea, diarrhoea, vomiting, constipation, stomatitis, anorexia. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Febrile neutropenia, infection, UTI, oral candidiasis, dehydration, hypokalaemia, insomnia, depression, dizziness, blurred vision, tachycardia, hypotension, dyspnoea, epistaxis, cough, dyspepsia, pruritus, peripheral oedema. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Sepsis, neutropenic sepsis, CHF, left ventricular dysfunction, hyperbilirubinaemia, injection site reaction, cystoid macular oedema (post-marketing). |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Neutropenic sepsis and pancytopenia.
- Severe peripheral neuropathy.
- Pneumonitis and interstitial lung disease.
- Severe hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Paclitaxel metabolism is catalysed by **CYP2C8** and **CYP3A4**. 
- **Clinically Significant Interactions:** Use caution when co-administering with known inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, erythromycin, fluoxetine, ritonavir) or inducers (e.g., rifampicin, carbamazepine, phenytoin) of CYP2C8 or CYP3A4.
- **Specific Combinations:** No clinically relevant PK interaction was found between **ABRAXANE** and carboplatin. Interaction studies between **ABRAXANE** and gemcitabine have not been evaluated in humans.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. **ABRAXANE** is a cytotoxic agent that can cause foetal harm, including birth defects and foetal loss. Females of reproductive potential must have a pregnancy test before starting treatment and use effective contraception during and for at least 6 months after the last dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Breastfeeding must be discontinued during therapy.
- **Fertility:** May impair fertility in both males and females. Males should avoid fathering a child for 6 months after the final dose.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established.
- **Elderly Use:** 
    - **NSCLC:** Higher frequency of myelosuppression, neuropathy, and arthralgia in those ≥ 65 years.
    - **Pancreas:** Higher incidence of diarrhoea, dehydration, and epistaxis in those ≥ 65 years. Patients ≥ 75 years showed higher rates of serious adverse reactions leading to discontinuation.
- **Renal Impairment:** No starting dose adjustment is required for mild to moderate impairment (CrCl 30 to < 90 mL/min). Insufficient data for CrCl < 30 mL/min.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild:** No dose adjustment.
    - **Moderate to Severe:** 20% dose reduction recommended for MBC and NSCLC. 
    - **Total Bilirubin > 5 x ULN:** Not recommended regardless of indication.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Paclitaxel is an antimicrotubule agent that promotes the assembly of microtubules from tubulin dimers and prevents depolymerisation. This stabilizes the microtubule network, inhibiting normal dynamic reorganisation and resulting in mitotic arrest and cell death.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (94%). **ABRAXANE** provides a higher fraction of unbound paclitaxel (6.2%) compared to solvent-based paclitaxel.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic via CYP2C8 and CYP3A4.
    - **Excretion:** Primarily faecal (20%); only 4% is excreted unchanged in urine. Terminal half-life is approximately 13–27 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Metastatic Breast Cancer:** A randomised trial (n=454) showed **ABRAXANE** 260 mg/m² every 3 weeks achieved a significantly higher response rate (33% vs 19%) and longer progression-free survival (5.2 vs 3.8 months) compared to solvent-based paclitaxel.
- **NSCLC:** A study of 1052 patients showed a significantly higher overall response rate for **ABRAXANE**/carboplatin (33%) compared to solvent-based paclitaxel/carboplatin (25%).
- **Pancreatic Cancer:** A study of 861 patients showed **ABRAXANE**/gemcitabine significantly improved median overall survival (8.5 vs 6.7 months) compared to gemcitabine monotherapy.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Vials:** Store below 25°C in the original carton to protect from light [Australian].
- **Reconstituted Suspension (in vial):** Stable for up to 8 hours if refrigerated (2°C to 8°C).
- **Diluted Suspension (in IV bag):** Use immediately; if necessary, can be held at 2°C to 8°C for no more than 8 hours.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
