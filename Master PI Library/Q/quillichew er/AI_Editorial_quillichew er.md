# Editorial Product Information: Methylphenidate Hydrochloride (QuilliChew ER)

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **QuilliChew ER** for use by healthcare professionals. It is designed to consolidate clinical data into a concise, structured format.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory guidance before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Methylphenidate hydrochloride (a central nervous system stimulant).
- **Chemical Class:** Phenethylamine derivative; benzylpiperidine.
- **Brand Name(s):** **QuilliChew ER** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Extended-release chewable tablets: 20 mg (functionally scored), 30 mg (functionally scored), and 40 mg (non-scored) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** NextWave Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (a subsidiary of Tris Pharma, Inc.) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD):** Indicated for the treatment of ADHD in patients aged 6 years and older [US].
- **Limitations of Use:** Not recommended for use in pediatric patients under 6 years of age due to higher plasma exposure and increased incidence of adverse reactions (e.g., weight loss) compared to older children [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Pediatrics ≥6 years):** 
    - **Initial Dose:** 20 mg administered orally once daily in the morning [US].
    - **Titration:** Dosage may be adjusted weekly in increments of 10 mg, 15 mg, or 20 mg per day to achieve a well-tolerated therapeutic effect [US].
    - **Maximum Dose:** 60 mg per day [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - May be taken with or without food [US].
    - Tablets must be chewed thoroughly before swallowing [US].
    - The 20 mg and 30 mg tablets are functionally scored and may be broken in half to achieve 10 mg or 15 mg doses, respectively [US].
- **Switching from Other Methylphenidate Products:** 
    - Discontinue the previous methylphenidate treatment and titrate with **QuilliChew ER** using the standard schedule [US].
    - Do not substitute on a milligram-per-milligram basis due to differing pharmacokinetic profiles and compositions [US].
- **Dosage Reduction and Discontinuation:** If symptoms are paradoxically aggravated or other adverse reactions occur, reduce the dose or discontinue. If no improvement is observed after one month of appropriate adjustment, the drug should be discontinued [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to methylphenidate or any excipients (e.g., angioedema and anaphylactic reactions have been reported) [US].
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Concurrent use, or use within 14 days of discontinuing an MAOI, due to the risk of hypertensive crisis [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning (Abuse, Misuse, and Addiction):** **QuilliChew ER** has a high potential for abuse and misuse, which can lead to substance use disorders and addiction. Misuse can result in overdose and death. Risk is higher with unapproved administration routes (e.g., snorting, injection). Patients must be assessed for risk prior to prescribing and monitored throughout treatment [US].
- **Serious Cardiac Disease:** Avoid use in patients with known structural cardiac abnormalities, cardiomyopathy, serious arrhythmias, or coronary artery disease. Sudden death has been reported in such patients taking CNS stimulants [US].
- **Blood Pressure and Heart Rate:** May cause increases in blood pressure (mean 2–4 mmHg) and heart rate (mean 3–6 bpm). Regular monitoring of all patients is required [US].
- **Psychiatric Adverse Reactions:** 
    - May exacerbate pre-existing psychotic or thought disorders [US].
    - May induce manic or mixed episodes in patients with bipolar disorder; screen for risk factors before initiation [US].
    - New onset psychotic or manic symptoms (e.g., hallucinations, mania) can occur in patients without a prior history at recommended doses [US].
- **Priapism:** Prolonged and painful erections, sometimes requiring surgery, have been reported. Seek immediate medical attention if this occurs [US].
- **Peripheral Vasculopathy (Raynaud’s Phenomenon):** Associated with digital changes. Monitor for finger/toe numbness, coldness, or color changes [US].
- **Long-Term Growth Suppression:** CNS stimulants may cause weight loss and slowing of growth rate in pediatric patients. Monitor height and weight closely; treatment interruption may be necessary [US].
- **Phenylketonurics (PKU):** Tablets contain aspartame (a source of phenylalanine). Content per tablet: 20 mg (3 mg), 30 mg (4.5 mg), and 40 mg (6 mg) [US].
- **Ocular Effects:** 
    - **Acute Angle Closure Glaucoma:** Evaluation by an ophthalmologist is recommended for patients at risk [US].
    - **Increased Intraocular Pressure (IOP):** Use in patients with open-angle glaucoma only if benefits outweigh risks; monitor IOP closely [US].
- **Motor and Verbal Tics:** May onset or exacerbate tics and Tourette’s syndrome. Evaluate family history prior to initiation [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common Adverse Effects (≥5% and twice placebo):** Appetite decreased, insomnia, nausea, vomiting, dyspepsia, abdominal pain, weight decreased, anxiety, dizziness, irritability, affect lability, tachycardia, and increased blood pressure [US].
- **Other Reported Effects (≥2% and >placebo):** Aggression, emotional poverty, headache [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:**
    - Cardiovascular: Sudden death, myocardial infarction, stroke [US].
    - Psychiatric: Psychosis, mania, suicidal ideation [US].
    - Dermatologic/Immune: Erythema multiforme, exfoliative dermatitis, anaphylactic reactions [US].
    - Hematologic: Pancytopenia, thrombocytopenia [US].
    - Hepatic: Severe hepatocellular injury [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **MAOIs:** Contraindicated; risk of hypertensive crisis [US].
- **Antihypertensive Drugs:** Methylphenidate may decrease the effectiveness of these agents; monitor blood pressure and adjust therapy as needed [US].
- **Halogenated Anesthetics:** Risk of sudden blood pressure and heart rate increase during surgery; avoid use on the day of surgery [US].
- **Risperidone:** Increased risk of extrapyramidal symptoms (EPS) when the dose of one or both drugs is changed [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Risk Summary:** Insufficient data in humans to inform drug-associated risks. Animal studies showed spina bifida in rabbits at 40 times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) [US].
    - **Clinical Considerations:** CNS stimulants can cause vasoconstriction and decrease placental perfusion [US].
    - **Pregnancy Registry:** Healthcare providers are encouraged to register patients in the National Pregnancy Registry for Psychostimulants [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Methylphenidate is present in human milk (infant dose 0.16% to 0.7% of maternal weight-adjusted dose). Monitor breastfed infants for agitation, insomnia, anorexia, and reduced weight gain [US].
- **Fertility:** No impairment observed in animal studies at doses up to 8-fold the MRHD [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established for ages 6–17. Not recommended in those <6 years due to risk of higher exposure and adverse effects [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not studied in patients over 65 years of age [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not expected to significantly impact pharmacokinetics as renal clearance is not a major route for methylphenidate [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No specific experience available in this population [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Blocks the reuptake of norepinephrine and dopamine into the presynaptic neuron and increases their release into the extraneuronal space [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** A racemic mixture where the *d*-isomer is more pharmacologically active than the *l*-isomer [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Reaches maximal concentration ($C_{max}$) at a median of 5 hours post-dose. Comprised of approximately 30% immediate-release and 70% extended-release methylphenidate [US].
    - **Food Effect:** High-fat meals increase $C_{max}$ and AUC by approximately 20% and 4%, respectively [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via de-esterification to alpha-phenyl-piperidine acetic acid (PPAA), which is inactive [US].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 5.2 hours. ~90% of radioactivity recovered in urine after oral dosing [US].
    - **Alcohol Effect:** Alcohol concentrations of 40% can result in 90% release of the drug within 30 minutes; avoid alcohol during treatment [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Study:** A laboratory classroom study of 90 pediatric subjects (ages 6–12) with ADHD. After a 6-week open-label optimization, patients were randomized to their optimized dose (20–60 mg) or placebo for 1 week [US].
- **Primary Outcome:** **QuilliChew ER** was statistically significantly superior to placebo based on the SKAMP-Combined score (average of treatment effects across 0.75 to 13 hours post-dose) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Store in a safe, preferably locked place to prevent theft or misuse [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as a Schedule II (CII) controlled substance due to high potential for abuse and dependence. Available by prescription only (Rx-only) [US].
