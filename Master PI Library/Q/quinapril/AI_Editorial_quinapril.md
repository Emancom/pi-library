# Editorial Product Information: Quinapril

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Regulatory Agencies:** Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA - Australia); Food and Drug Administration (FDA - United States).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of quinapril product information, synthesised from multiple regulatory sources for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Quinapril hydrochloride.
- **Chemical Class:** Nonsulfhydryl angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitor; ethyl ester prodrug of quinaprilat.
- **Brand Names:** ACQUIN [TGA].
- **ATC Code:** C09AA06.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 5 mg, 10 mg, 20 mg [TGA, FDA].
    - Film-coated tablets: 40 mg [FDA].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** 
    - Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [TGA].
    - Northwind Health Company, LLC; Lupin Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [FDA].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypertension:** Indicated for the treatment of hypertension, either as monotherapy or in combination with thiazide diuretics.
- **Congestive Heart Failure:** Indicated as adjunctive therapy in the management of heart failure when added to conventional therapy (e.g., diuretics and/or cardiac glycosides/digitalis) [TGA, FDA].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Instructions
Quinapril should preferably be taken before meals, as high-fat food may moderately diminish the rate and extent of absorption [TGA, FDA].

### Hypertension
- **Monotherapy (Initial Dose):** 5 mg to 10 mg once daily [TGA]; 10 mg or 20 mg once daily [FDA].
- **Titration:** Adjust dosage according to blood pressure response at peak (2–6 hours post-dose) and trough (pre-dose). Adjustments should generally occur at intervals of 2 weeks [FDA] to 4 weeks [TGA].
- **Maintenance Dose:** 10 mg to 40 mg daily, administered as a single dose or in two equally divided doses [TGA]. Dosages up to 80 mg daily have been used [FDA].
- **Concomitant Diuretics:** If possible, discontinue the diuretic 2 to 3 days prior to initiating quinapril. If the diuretic cannot be discontinued, initiate quinapril at 2.5 mg [TGA] to 5 mg [FDA] under close medical supervision for at least 2 hours.

### Heart Failure
- **Initial Dose:** 5 mg once daily [TGA]; 5 mg twice daily [FDA].
- **Titration:** If well tolerated, titrate at weekly intervals to an effective dose.
- **Maintenance Dose:** Usually 20 mg to 40 mg daily in two equally divided doses [TGA, FDA].

### Administration Nuances
- **Switching from Sacubitril/Valsartan:** Do not initiate quinapril until 36 hours after the last dose of sacubitril/valsartan [FDA].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to quinapril or any excipients.
- History of angioedema (hereditary, idiopathic, or related to previous ACE inhibitor therapy).
- Concomitant use with a neprilysin inhibitor (e.g., sacubitril/valsartan) [FDA, TGA].
- Concomitant use with aliskiren in patients with diabetes [FDA, TGA].
- **[TGA-specific]:** Severe renal artery stenosis; patients on high-flux polyacrylonitrile ('AN69') dialysis membranes; pregnancy or women intending to become pregnant.
- **[TGA-specific]:** Do not use aliskiren combination in patients with moderate to severe renal insufficiency (GFR <60 mL/min), hyperkalaemia (>5 mmol/L), or hypotensive CHF.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning (FDA)
- **Fetal Toxicity:** Drugs that act directly on the renin-angiotensin system can cause injury and death to the developing fetus. Discontinue quinapril as soon as pregnancy is detected.

### General Warnings
- **Angioedema:** May involve the face, extremities, lips, tongue, glottis, or larynx. Laryngeal oedema can be fatal. Black patients have a higher reported incidence of angioedema compared to non-black patients [FDA, TGA].
- **Intestinal Angioedema:** Should be considered in patients presenting with abdominal pain.
- **Hypotension:** Excessive hypotension is rare in uncomplicated hypertension but may occur in volume/salt-depleted patients, those on high-dose diuretics, or those with heart failure.
- **Neutropenia/Agranulocytosis:** Reported rarely. Monitoring of white blood cell counts should be considered in patients with collagen vascular disease or renal impairment.
- **Hepatic Failure:** Rarely, ACE inhibitors are associated with a syndrome starting with cholestatic jaundice progressing to fulminant hepatic necrosis. Discontinue if jaundice or marked liver enzyme elevations occur.
- **Psoriasis:** Quinapril should be used with caution in patients with a history of psoriasis, as aggravation may occur [TGA].

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Renal Function:** Periodic assessment of serum creatinine and BUN, especially in patients with pre-existing renal disease or renal artery stenosis.
- **Electrolytes:** Monitor for hyperkalaemia, particularly in patients with renal impairment, diabetes, or those using potassium-sparing agents.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse experiences are usually mild and transient. Discontinuation due to adverse events occurred in approximately 4.7% of hypertension patients and 6.8% of heart failure patients.

| Body System | Common (1–10%) | Uncommon/Rare (<1%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness (3.9–7.7%), Headache (1.7–5.6%), Fatigue (2.6%) | Somnolence, vertigo, syncope, depression |
| **Respiratory** | Cough (2.0–4.3%) | Dyspnoea, pharyngitis, eosinophilic pneumonitis |
| **Gastrointestinal**| Nausea/Vomiting (1.4–2.4%), Abdominal pain (1.0%) | Pancreatitis, GI haemorrhage, constipation, dry mouth |
| **Skin** | Rash (1.4%) | Angioedema (0.1%), alopecia, photosensitivity, psoriasis |
| **Musculoskeletal** | Myalgia (1.5%) | Arthralgia, back pain |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Agranulocytosis, haemolytic anaemia, acute renal failure, myocardial infarction, and cerebrovascular accident.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Diuretics:** Risk of excessive hypotension.
- **Potassium-sparing Diuretics/Supplements:** Increased risk of hyperkalaemia.
- **Lithium:** Increased serum lithium levels and toxicity risk.
- **Tetracycline:** Absorption reduced by 28–37% due to the magnesium carbonate excipient in quinapril tablets.
- **NSAIDs (including COX-2 inhibitors):** May attenuate antihypertensive effect and increase risk of renal functional deterioration.
- **Gold:** Nitritoid reactions (flushing, nausea, hypotension) reported rarely with injectable sodium aurothiomalate.
- **mTOR/DPP-IV/Neprilysin Inhibitors:** Increased risk of angioedema.
- **Dual Blockade (ACE-I + ARB or Aliskiren):** Increased risk of hypotension, hyperkalaemia, and renal impairment.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **[TGA]:** Category D. Contraindicated.
    - **[FDA]:** Warning regarding fetal toxicity (2nd/3rd trimester). Discontinue immediately upon pregnancy detection.
- **Breastfeeding:** Quinapril is secreted in human milk to a limited extent. Due to the potential for serious reactions in nursing infants, use is not recommended [TGA] or caution is advised [FDA].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatrics:** Safety and effectiveness not established.
- **Elderly (≥65 years):** Initial dose of 10 mg recommended [TGA]. While no overall differences in safety/efficacy were observed, greater sensitivity in some individuals cannot be ruled out.
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - CrCl >60 mL/min: 10 mg initial dose.
    - CrCl 30–60 mL/min: 5 mg initial dose.
    - CrCl 10–30 mL/min: 2.5 mg initial dose.
    - CrCl <10 mL/min: Insufficient data for recommendation.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution. Conversion of quinapril to quinaprilat may be reduced in patients with alcoholic cirrhosis.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Quinapril is converted to quinaprilat, which inhibits ACE, preventing the conversion of angiotensin I to the vasoconstrictor angiotensin II. This leads to decreased aldosterone secretion and increased plasma renin activity.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak plasma levels in 1 hour; ≥60% absorbed.
    - **Metabolism:** Deesterified to quinaprilat (approx. 38% of oral dose).
    - **Distribution:** 97% protein bound. Does not cross the blood-brain barrier (rats).
    - **Excretion:** Primarily renal (96%). Effective accumulation half-life of quinaprilat is ~3 hours; terminal phase half-life is ~25 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Endothelial Function:** The TREND study (n=105) and BANFF study (n=80) demonstrated that quinapril may improve endothelium-dependent vasomotor function/vasodilation [TGA].
- **Hypertension/Heart Failure:** Controlled trials showed blood pressure reduction within 1 hour, peaking at 2–4 hours. In heart failure, quinapril improved NYHA functional class and exercise tolerance [TGA, FDA].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Australia [TGA]:** Store below 25°C.
- **United States [FDA]:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Protect from light. Keep container well-closed.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia [TGA]:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **United States [FDA]:** Rx Only.
