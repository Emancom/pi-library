# Editorial Product Information: Quinapril

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Regulatory Agencies:** Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA - Australia); Food and Drug Administration (FDA - United States).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised clinical overview of quinapril hydrochloride, synthesising data from Australian and U.S. official product information for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Quinapril hydrochloride, a non-sulfhydryl angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitor.
- **Brand Names:** **ACQUIN** [TGA]; Quinapril Tablets USP [FDA].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Film-coated tablets:** 5 mg, 10 mg, and 20 mg [TGA, FDA].
    - **Film-coated tablets:** 40 mg [FDA only; TGA lists 40 mg as a dose but does not list a 40 mg tablet strength in the qualitative composition section].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [TGA]; Northwind Health Company, LLC / Lupin Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [FDA].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypertension:** Indicated for the treatment of hypertension to lower blood pressure and reduce the risk of fatal/nonfatal cardiovascular events, primarily strokes and myocardial infarctions [FDA]. It may be used as monotherapy or in combination with thiazide diuretics. 
    - *Note:* Sufficient data have not been provided to support use in renovascular hypertension [TGA].
- **Congestive Heart Failure (CHF):** Indicated as adjunctive therapy in the management of heart failure when added to conventional therapy including diuretics and/or cardiac glycosides/digitalis. 
    - *Note:* Specified for mild to moderate CHF [TGA].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Instructions
- **Administration:** Should preferably be taken before meals, as high-fat food may diminish the rate and extent of absorption [TGA, FDA].
- **Titration:** Adjust dosage according to blood pressure response at peak (2–6 hours post-dose) and trough (pre-dosing).

### Hypertension (Monotherapy)
- **Initial Dose:** 
    - 5–10 mg once daily [TGA].
    - 10 or 20 mg once daily [FDA].
- **Maintenance/Titration:** Adjustments should generally occur at intervals of at least 2 weeks [FDA] or 4 weeks [TGA].
- **Maintenance Range:** 10–40 mg/day given as a single dose or two equally divided doses [TGA]; 20, 40, or 80 mg/day [FDA].
- **Maximum Dose:** 40 mg/day [TGA]; 80 mg/day [FDA].

### Hypertension (Concomitant Diuretics)
- If possible, discontinue the diuretic 2 to 3 days prior to initiating quinapril. If the diuretic cannot be discontinued, initiate quinapril at 2.5 mg [TGA] or 5 mg [FDA] under medical supervision until blood pressure stabilises.

### Congestive Heart Failure
- **Initial Dose:** 5 mg once daily [TGA]; 5 mg twice daily [FDA].
- **Titration:** If the initial dose is well tolerated, titrate at weekly intervals up to an effective dose.
- **Maintenance Range:** 10–20 mg/day in one or two doses [TGA]; 20–40 mg/day in two equally divided doses [FDA].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to quinapril or any component of the formulation.
- History of angioedema (hereditary, idiopathic, or related to previous ACE inhibitor therapy).
- **Concomitant use with neprilysin inhibitors:** Do not use with sacubitril/valsartan. Quinapril must not be administered within 36 hours of switching to or from sacubitril/valsartan [FDA, TGA].
- **Dual Blockade with Aliskiren:** Contraindicated in patients with diabetes [TGA, FDA] or moderate to severe renal impairment (GFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m²) [TGA].
- **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated in pregnancy and in women intending to become pregnant [TGA].
- **Dialysis:** Use of high-flux polyacrylonitrile ('AN69') membranes [TGA].
- **Renal/Vascular:** Severe renal artery stenosis [TGA].
- **Additional TGA Contraindications:** Hyperkalaemia (>5 mmol/L) or hypotensive CHF patients when combined with aliskiren, ARBs, or other ACE inhibitors.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning (Fetal Toxicity):** Drugs acting on the renin-angiotensin system can cause injury and death to the developing fetus. Discontinue quinapril as soon as pregnancy is detected [FDA].
- **Angioedema:** 
    - **Head and Neck:** May occur in the face, extremities, lips, tongue, glottis, and larynx. Laryngeal oedema can be fatal. Higher incidence reported in Black patients compared to non-Black patients [FDA, TGA].
    - **Intestinal:** Reported in patients presenting with abdominal pain (with or without nausea/vomiting).
- **Hypotension:** Excessive hypotension may occur, particularly in volume- or salt-depleted patients, those on high-dose diuretics, or those with heart failure.
- **Neutropenia/Agranulocytosis:** Reported rarely with ACE inhibitors; risk is higher in patients with renal impairment or collagen vascular disease. Periodic monitoring of WBC counts is recommended in these populations [TGA, FDA].
- **Hepatic Failure:** Rarely associated with a syndrome starting with cholestatic jaundice progressing to fulminant hepatic necrosis and death. Discontinue if jaundice or marked liver enzyme elevations occur.
- **Hyperkalaemia:** Risk factors include renal insufficiency, diabetes, and use of potassium-sparing diuretics or potassium supplements. Monitor serum potassium.
- **Cough:** Characteristically persistent, dry, and non-productive; resolves after discontinuation.
- **Surgery/Anaesthesia:** May enhance the hypotensive effect of anaesthetics.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse experiences are usually mild and transient. Discontinuation rates in trials were approximately 4.7% for hypertension and 6.8% for CHF [TGA, FDA].

| Adverse Event | Hypertension (n=1563) | Heart Failure (n=585) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dizziness** | 3.9% | 7.7% |
| **Headache** | 5.6% | 1.7% |
| **Fatigue** | 2.6% | 2.6% |
| **Coughing** | 2.0% | 4.3% |
| **Nausea/Vomiting** | 1.4% | 2.4% |
| **Hypotension** | <1.0% | 2.9% |

**Serious/Other Clinically Significant Effects:**
- **Dermatological:** Stevens-Johnson syndrome, pemphigus, photosensitivity, and aggravation of psoriasis [TGA].
- **Cardiovascular:** Myocardial infarction, angina pectoris, palpitations, and syncope.
- **Renal:** Acute renal failure, worsening renal function.
- **Immune:** Anaphylactoid reactions (especially during desensitisation, dialysis with high-flux membranes, or LDL apheresis).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Diuretics:** Additive hypotensive effect.
- **Potassium-Sparing Diuretics/Supplements:** Increased risk of hyperkalaemia.
- **Tetracycline:** Absorption of tetracycline reduced by 28–37% due to the magnesium carbonate excipient in quinapril tablets [FDA, TGA].
- **Lithium:** Increased serum lithium levels and toxicity.
- **NSAIDs (including COX-2 inhibitors):** May attenuate antihypertensive effect and increase risk of renal deterioration, including acute renal failure.
- **Gold (Sodium Aurothiomalate):** Nitritoid reactions reported rarely with injectable gold and concomitant ACE inhibitors [FDA].
- **mTOR/DPP-IV/Neprilysin Inhibitors:** Increased risk of angioedema (e.g., temsirolimus, vildagliptin, sacubitril).
- **Agents Affecting Sympathetic Activity:** Use with caution; may increase antihypertensive effect.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **FDA Category:** Not assigned (Boxed Warning for Fetal Toxicity).
    - **TGA Category:** D.
    - **Clinical Data:** ACE inhibitors cause fetal/neonatal morbidity (hypotension, skull hypoplasia, renal failure) and death when used in the 2nd and 3rd trimesters.
- **Breastfeeding:** Secreted in human milk to a limited extent. **ACQUIN** should not be given to nursing mothers [TGA]; use caution if administered [FDA].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric:** Safety and effectiveness not established.
- **Elderly:** Start at the low end of the dosing range (e.g., 10 mg initial [TGA]). While no overall differences in safety/efficacy were seen, greater sensitivity cannot be ruled out.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosage must be adjusted based on Creatinine Clearance (CrCl):
    - CrCl >60 mL/min: 10 mg initial dose.
    - CrCl 30–60 mL/min: 5 mg initial dose.
    - CrCl 10–30 mL/min: 2.5 mg initial dose.
    - CrCl <10 mL/min: Insufficient data for recommendation.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Conversion of quinapril to quinaprilat may be reduced in patients with alcoholic cirrhosis. Use with caution.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Quinapril is a prodrug deesterified to quinaprilat. Quinaprilat inhibits ACE, reducing angiotensin II levels, which leads to decreased vasopressor activity and reduced aldosterone secretion.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak plasma levels in 1 hour; absorption is ≥60%.
    - **Distribution:** 97% protein bound. Does not cross the blood-brain barrier in rats.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily converted to quinaprilat (approx. 38% of oral dose).
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (96% of IV dose). Terminal elimination half-life of quinaprilat is ~25 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Hypertension:** Doses of 10–80 mg/day effectively lowered blood pressure. Trough effects represent approximately 50% of peak effects [FDA].
- **Heart Failure:** Quinapril has shown modest improvements in exercise tolerance and NYHA functional class [TGA, FDA]. 
- **Endothelial Function:** The **TREND** and **BANFF** studies showed improvements in endothelium-dependent vasomotor function with quinapril [TGA].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** 
    - Store below 25°C [TGA].
    - Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [FDA].
- **Conditions:** Protect from light; dispense in well-closed containers [FDA].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Poison Schedule S4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **United States:** Rx Only.
