# Editorial Product Information: Topiramate (Extended-Release)

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **Qudexy XR** (topiramate extended-release capsules) for healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, regulatory warnings, and dosing strategies into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this writing, documents from other major jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian, Japanese) were not provided for comparison.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Topiramate (a sulfamate-substituted monosaccharide).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Qudexy XR** [US].
- **ATC Code:** N03AX11 (Topiramate).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Extended-release capsules available in 25 mg, 50 mg, 100 mg, 150 mg, and 200 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Upsher-Smith Laboratories, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Epilepsy (Monotherapy):** Initial treatment of partial-onset or primary generalised tonic-clonic seizures in patients 2 years of age and older [US].
- **Epilepsy (Adjunctive Therapy):** Treatment of partial-onset seizures, primary generalised tonic-clonic seizures, or seizures associated with Lennox-Gastaut Syndrome (LGS) in patients 2 years of age and older [US].
- **Migraine Prophylaxis:** Preventive treatment of migraine in patients 12 years of age and older [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Monotherapy (Epilepsy):** Recommended dose is 400 mg once daily. Titration begins at 50 mg daily for the first week, increasing by 50 mg weekly until reaching 200 mg, then increasing by 100 mg weekly to the target dose [US].
- **Adjunctive Therapy (Epilepsy):** 200 mg to 400 mg once daily. For primary generalised tonic-clonic seizures, 400 mg once daily is recommended [US].
- **Migraine Prophylaxis:** 100 mg once daily, titrated over four weeks (starting at 25 mg/day) [US].

### Titration Schedules
Titration should be gradual to minimise adverse effects, particularly cognitive dysfunction and somnolence. If a patient is unable to tolerate a specific increment, longer intervals between adjustments may be utilised.

### Administration Instructions
- **Qudexy XR** should be taken once daily.
- Capsules may be swallowed whole or opened and the entire contents sprinkled onto a small amount (teaspoon) of soft food (e.g., applesauce). The mixture must be swallowed immediately without chewing or crushing [US].
- May be administered without regard to meals [US].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- If a single dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as possible. If more than one dose is missed, the patient should contact their healthcare provider [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** History of hypersensitivity to topiramate or any inactive ingredient (e.g., microcrystalline cellulose, ethylcellulose, diethyl phthalate) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Ocular Effects:** Acute myopia and secondary angle-closure glaucoma have been reported. Symptoms typically occur within one month of initiation and include acute onset of decreased visual acuity and/or ocular pain. Discontinuation is required to prevent permanent vision loss [US].
- **Visual Field Defects:** Reported independently of elevated intraocular pressure; consideration should be given to discontinuation if these occur [US].
- **Metabolic Acidosis:** Topiramate is a carbonic anhydrase inhibitor and can cause hyperchloremic, non-anion gap metabolic acidosis. Baseline and periodic serum bicarbonate monitoring is recommended. Chronic acidosis in pediatrics may result in decreased growth rates and bone mineral density [US].
- **Neuropsychiatric Effects:** Significant risk of suicidal behavior and ideation. Patients should be monitored for depression or unusual mood changes. Cognitive-related dysfunction (confusion, psychomotor slowing, difficulty with concentration/memory) is common and dose-related [US].
- **Hyperammonemia and Encephalopathy:** Risk is increased when used concomitantly with valproic acid. Ammonia levels should be measured in patients presenting with unexplained lethargy or mental status changes [US].
- **Kidney Stones:** Increased risk due to carbonic anhydrase inhibition. Adequate hydration is recommended [US].
- **Serious Skin Reactions:** Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS) and Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN) have been reported. Discontinue at the first sign of a rash [US].
- **Withdrawal of Therapy:** Antiepileptic drugs (AEDs) must be withdrawn gradually to minimise the risk of increased seizure frequency [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency and Severity
| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Paresthesia, anorexia, weight loss, somnolence, dizziness, fatigue, nervousness, psychomotor slowing, abnormal vision, fever (pediatrics) [US]. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Difficulty with memory, taste perversion, diarrhea, hypoesthesia, nausea, abdominal pain, upper respiratory tract infection [US]. |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Metabolic acidosis and associated osteomalacia/osteoporosis.
- Acute myopia and secondary angle-closure glaucoma.
- Hyperammonemic encephalopathy.
- Major congenital malformations (Fetal Toxicity).
- SJS/TEN and Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Phenytoin/Carbamazepine:** Concomitant use may decrease topiramate plasma concentrations by 40-48%. Dosage adjustment of topiramate may be necessary [US].
- **Oral Contraceptives:** Efficacy may be decreased, especially at doses >200 mg/day. Patients should report changes in bleeding patterns [US].
- **Hydrochlorothiazide (HCTZ):** May increase topiramate $C_{max}$ and AUC by 27% and 29%, respectively [US].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Valproic Acid:** Increased risk of hyperammonemia (with or without encephalopathy) and hypothermia [US].
- **CNS Depressants and Alcohol:** Potentially increased risk of CNS depression and cognitive adverse reactions; use with extreme caution [US].
- **Other Carbonic Anhydrase Inhibitors:** Concomitant use with drugs like zonisamide or acetazolamide may increase the severity of metabolic acidosis and the risk of kidney stones [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not specified in current labeling, but Fetal Toxicity is warned]. Use during pregnancy is associated with an increased risk of major congenital malformations (cleft lip/palate) and being Small for Gestational Age (SGA). It should be used only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Topiramate is excreted in human milk. Diarrhea and somnolence have been reported in breastfed infants. A decision should be made whether to discontinue nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on male or female fertility were observed in animal studies at clinically relevant doses [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for epilepsy monotherapy/adjunctive therapy in ages $\ge$ 2 years and migraine prophylaxis in ages $\ge$ 12 years. Safety/efficacy not established for epilepsy in children < 2 years or migraine in children < 12 years [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Dosage adjustment may be necessary due to age-related declines in renal function (CrCl < 70 mL/min) [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** In patients with moderate (CrCl 30–69 mL/min) or severe (CrCl < 30 mL/min) impairment, one-half of the usual adult dose is recommended [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Plasma clearance may be decreased by 26% in patients with moderate to severe impairment; caution is advised [US].
- **Hemodialysis:** Topiramate is cleared by hemodialysis at a rate 4 to 6 times higher than normal. A supplemental dose may be required to avoid rapid drops in plasma concentration [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** The exact mechanisms are unknown. Preclinical properties include:
    1. Blocking voltage-dependent sodium channels.
    2. Augmenting GABA-A receptor activity.
    3. Antagonising AMPA/kainate glutamate receptors.
    4. Inhibiting carbonic anhydrase (isozymes II and IV) [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Bioavailability is not affected by food. $T_{max}$ is approximately 20 hours [US].
    - **Distribution:** 15% to 41% plasma protein bound [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Not extensively metabolised [US].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily eliminated unchanged in the urine (~70%). Mean effective half-life is approximately 56 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Bridging Study:** Approval of **Qudexy XR** was based on a bridging study demonstrating pharmacokinetic equivalence to immediate-release topiramate formulations [US].
- **Epilepsy Monotherapy (Study 1):** In a trial of 470 patients, time to first seizure was significantly longer in the 400 mg/day group compared to the 50 mg/day group [US].
- **Migraine Prophylaxis (Studies 11, 12, 13):** Significant reductions in mean 4-week migraine headache frequency were observed at doses of 100 mg/day compared to placebo in both adult and adolescent populations [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C [US].
- Keep in a tightly closed container and protect from moisture [US].
