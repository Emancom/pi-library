# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a concise format.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or the local prescribing requirements of a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/generic name).
- **Chemical Class:** A naturally occurring carrier molecule (amino acid derivative) involved in fatty acid metabolism.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn metabolic defects that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Adult Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Doses should be given every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    - **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Administration:** Administer as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - **Titration:** Adjustments may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma carnitine concentrations (target range: 40–50 µmol/L).

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine may trigger seizures. In patients with pre-existing seizure disorders, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures has been reported [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** The safety of oral levocarnitine has not been established in patients with renal impairment. Chronic high doses of oral formulations in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in the urine [US].
- **Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trials Experience (ESRD Population)
Adverse events reported with a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials:

| Body System | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain (12%), Flu syndrome (25%), Headache (22%), Infection (15%), Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%) |
| **Gastrointestinal**| Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%), Abdominal pain (9%), Nausea (8%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (23%), Cough increase (9%), Dyspnea (7%), Rhinitis (9%) |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness (13%), Paresthesia (5%) |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia (9%), Hyperkalemia (6%) |

### Serious/Other Adverse Effects
- **Neurologic:** Seizures.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Body odor, gastritis, and transient nausea/vomiting.
- **Postmarketing Reports:** Reports of drug dependence and various sensory disturbances [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels frequently after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not assigned/Not specified]. Animal reproductive studies (rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies in nursing mothers have not been conducted. While studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine in milk, the risks to the infant must be weighed against the benefits to the mother. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is based on mg/kg body weight (see Section 3).
- **Elderly Use:** Specific data not provided in the source; general caution is typically advised regarding the higher frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function in this population.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. In this population, monitoring of trough carnitine levels is essential to guide dosing (see Section 3) [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with metabolic errors, carnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following a 20 mg/kg IV bolus, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis), trough carnitine levels increased from a baseline of 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed a dose-proportional increase in trough levocarnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only [US].
