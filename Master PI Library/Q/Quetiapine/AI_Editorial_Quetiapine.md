# Editorial Product Information: Quetiapine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (TGA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **quetiapine** for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current approved PI in their respective region for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Quetiapine (as fumarate); a dibenzothiazepine derivative.
- **Chemical Class**: Atypical antipsychotic agent.
- **Brand Names**: ALKEM QUETIAPINE [Australian].
- **Dosage Forms**: Film-coated, immediate-release tablets.
- **Strengths**: 25 mg, 100 mg, 150 mg, 200 mg, and 300 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Pharmacor Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Bipolar Disorder (Adults)**
- Maintenance treatment of Bipolar I Disorder (monotherapy or adjunctive to lithium or sodium valproate) to prevent manic, depressive, or mixed episodes [Australian].
- Treatment of depressive episodes associated with bipolar disorder [Australian].
- Treatment of acute mania associated with Bipolar I Disorder (monotherapy or adjunctive to lithium or sodium valproate) [Australian].

**Bipolar Disorder (Pediatric)**
- Monotherapy for acute mania in Bipolar I Disorder (children/adolescents aged 10–17 years) [Australian].

**Schizophrenia**
- Treatment of schizophrenia in adults and adolescents (aged 13–17 years) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Administration**: May be taken with or without food [Australian].
- **Withdrawal**: Abrupt cessation should be avoided; gradual tapering over 1–2 weeks is recommended to prevent acute withdrawal symptoms (nausea, vomiting, insomnia) [Australian].

### Adult Dosing by Indication
- **Bipolar Depression**: Once daily at bedtime. Titrate: 50 mg (Day 1), 100 mg (Day 2), 200 mg (Day 3), and 300 mg (Day 4). Maximum recommended dose is 600 mg/day [Australian].
- **Bipolar Maintenance**: Twice daily. Effective range 300–800 mg/day [Australian].
- **Acute Mania**: Twice daily. Titrate: 100 mg (Day 1) to 400 mg (Day 4). Further adjustments up to 800 mg/day by Day 6 (increments ≤200 mg/day). Usual effective range 400–800 mg/day [Australian].
- **Schizophrenia**: Twice daily. Titrate: 50 mg (Day 1) to 300 mg (Day 4). Usual effective range 300–450 mg/day. Range 150–750 mg/day [Australian].

### Pediatric Dosing (10–17 years)
- **Acute Mania/Schizophrenia**: Twice or three times daily. Titrate: 50 mg (Day 1) to 400 mg (Day 5). 
- **Effective range**: 400–600 mg/day (Mania) or 400–800 mg/day (Schizophrenia) [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **quetiapine** or any excipients (e.g., lactose monohydrate) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Mortality in Elderly with Dementia**: Increased risk of death; **quetiapine** is NOT approved for dementia-related psychosis [Australian].
- **Suicidality**: High risk in the early stages of treatment for depression and schizophrenia. Close supervision and small prescription quantities are advised [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular**: Caution in patients with known cardiovascular or cerebrovascular disease. Risk of **orthostatic hypotension** (especially during titration) and **QT prolongation**. Avoid use with Class Ia/III antiarrhythmics or other QT-prolonging drugs [Australian].
- **Metabolic Factors**: Monitor for weight gain, hyperglycaemia (including risk of ketoacidosis/coma), and lipid changes (triglycerides/cholesterol) at baseline and periodically [Australian].
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs)**: Discontinue immediately if SJS, TEN, or DRESS is suspected [Australian].
- **Extrapyramidal Symptoms (EPS) and Tardive Dyskinesia**: While risk is lower than typical antipsychotics, monitor for involuntary movements. Akathisia may also occur [Australian].
- **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS)**: Discontinue if hyperthermia, muscle rigidity, or altered mental status occurs [Australian].
- **Hematologic**: Risk of neutropenia and agranulocytosis (often in first 2 months). Discontinue if neutrophil count <1.0 x 10⁹/L [Australian].
- **Anticholinergic Effects**: Norquetiapine (metabolite) may cause urinary retention, prostatic hypertrophy, or narrow-angle glaucoma [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%)**: Somnolence, dizziness, dry mouth, headache, withdrawal symptoms, elevations in serum triglycerides/total cholesterol, weight gain, and EPS [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%)**: Tachycardia, palpitations, vision blurred, constipation, dyspepsia, vomiting, asthenia, peripheral oedema, irritability, pyrexia, elevations in ALT/GGT, and increased appetite [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%)**: Seizures, syncope, restless legs syndrome, tardive dyskinesia, and hypersensitivity [Australian].
- **Rare (<0.1%)**: Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome, agranulocytosis, pancreatitis, intestinal obstruction/ileus, and priapism [Australian].
- **Serious ADRs**: SJS/TEN, cardiomyopathy, myocarditis, and rhabdomyolysis (very rare) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors**: (e.g., ketoconazole, macrolides, protease inhibitors) Significantly increase **quetiapine** levels; dose reduction of **quetiapine** is required [Australian].
- **Hepatic Enzyme Inducers**: (e.g., carbamazepine, phenytoin) Significantly decrease **quetiapine** levels; dose increases may be necessary [Australian].
- **Serotonergic Agents**: Risk of potentially life-threatening **serotonin syndrome** [Australian].
- **Thioridazine**: Increases **quetiapine** clearance by 65% [Australian].
- **Levodopa**: **Quetiapine** may antagonize the effects of dopamine agonists [Australian].
- **Grapefruit Juice**: Not recommended due to CYP3A4 inhibition [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: **Category C** [Australian]. Neonates exposed in the third trimester are at risk for EPS and withdrawal symptoms (agitation, tremor, respiratory distress). Use only if benefits outweigh risks.
- **Breastfeeding**: **Quetiapine** is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding should be avoided during treatment [Australian].
- **Fertility**: Rats showed effects from hyperprolactinaemia; human relevance is uncertain [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Elderly**: Slower titration and lower starting doses are required; clearance is reduced by 30–50% [Australian].
- **Paediatric**: Approved for Bipolar Mania (10–17y) and Schizophrenia (13–17y). Long-term safety (>26 weeks) is not established [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment**: No dosage adjustment required [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Start at 25 mg/day; increase by 25–50 mg/day to effective dose [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Antagonism at serotonin 5HT₂ and dopamine D₁/D₂ receptors. The active metabolite, **norquetiapine**, additionally inhibits the norepinephrine transporter (NET) and acts as a partial 5HT₁ₐ agonist [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Well absorbed; not affected by food.
    - **Distribution**: ~83% plasma protein bound.
    - **Metabolism**: Extensively hepatic, primarily via CYP3A4.
    - **Elimination**: Half-life ~7 hours (quetiapine) and ~12 hours (norquetiapine). Excreted in urine (73%) and faeces (21%) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Bipolar Maintenance**: Combined therapy with lithium or valproate reduced recurrence risk by 70% compared to mood stabilisers alone [Australian].
- **Bipolar Depression**: All four pivotal 8-week trials showed 300 mg and 600 mg doses were superior to placebo in improving MADRS scores [Australian].
- **Schizophrenia**: Effectiveness demonstrated in both positive and negative symptoms across multiple 6-week trials [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Packaging**: PVC/aluminium foil blister packs [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule**: **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
