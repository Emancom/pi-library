# Editorial Product Information: Qamzova (Meloxicam Injection)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **United States (FDA)**. No other jurisdictional documents (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian) were available for comparison at the time of writing.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for final prescribing decisions.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Meloxicam, a non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) of the oxicam class.
- **Brand Name:** **QAMZOVA** [United States].
- **ATC Code:** M01AC06 (Meloxicam).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile, clear, greenish-yellow, non-pyrogenic aqueous solution for intravenous injection; available in single-dose vials containing 30 mg/mL (1 mL fill) [United States].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Nanjing Delova Biotech Co., Ltd. [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Management of Moderate-to-Severe Pain:** Indicated for use in adults, either as monotherapy or in combination with non-NSAID analgesics [United States].
- **Limitation of Use:** Due to a delayed onset of analgesia, **QAMZOVA** is not recommended when rapid pain relief is required [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration necessary to achieve patient treatment goals.
- **Adult Dosage:** 30 mg administered once daily [United States].
- **Administration Instructions:** Deliver as an intravenous bolus injection over approximately 15 seconds.
- **Patient Monitoring:** 
    - Assess analgesic response following administration. 
    - If the response is inadequate, a short-acting, non-NSAID, immediate-release analgesic may be administered.
    - The median time to meaningful pain relief is approximately 2 to 3 hours [United States].
- **Hydration:** Patients must be adequately hydrated prior to administration to mitigate renal toxicity risks.
- **Preparation:** Visually inspect the vial for particulate matter or discoloration before use. Discard if the solution is discoloured or contains particles [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity (e.g., anaphylaxis or serious skin reactions) to meloxicam or any excipients.
- History of asthma, urticaria, or other allergic-type reactions following the use of aspirin or other NSAIDs.
- Use in the setting of coronary artery bypass graft (CABG) surgery [United States].
- Moderate-to-severe renal insufficiency in patients identified as being at risk for renal failure due to volume depletion [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Cardiovascular and Gastrointestinal Risk [United States]**
    - **Cardiovascular (CV) Thrombotic Events:** NSAIDs cause an increased risk of serious, potentially fatal CV thrombotic events, including myocardial infarction and stroke. This risk can occur early in treatment and increases with duration of use.
    - **Gastrointestinal (GI) Events:** NSAIDs cause an increased risk of serious, potentially fatal GI adverse events, including bleeding, ulceration, and perforation. The elderly and those with a history of peptic ulcer disease or GI bleeding are at highest risk.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Elevations in ALT or AST (≥3x ULN) occur in approximately 1% of patients. Rare cases of severe hepatic injury (e.g., fulminant hepatitis, liver failure) have been reported. Discontinue immediately if clinical signs of liver disease develop or abnormal liver tests persist [United States].
- **Hypertension:** NSAIDs can lead to new-onset hypertension or worsening of pre-existing hypertension. Monitor blood pressure throughout therapy.
- **Heart Failure and Edema:** NSAIDs increase the risk of heart failure hospitalisation. Avoid use in patients with severe heart failure unless the benefits outweigh the risks [United States].
- **Renal Toxicity:** Long-term use can lead to renal papillary necrosis. Acute renal toxicity is more likely in patients with impaired renal function, heart failure, liver dysfunction, or those taking ACE inhibitors, ARBs, or diuretics.
- **Anaphylactic Reactions:** Meloxicam may cause anaphylaxis in patients with or without known sensitivity.
- **Serious Skin Reactions:** NSAIDs can cause fatal Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), and Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS). Discontinue at the first sign of skin rash [United States].
- **Masking of Inflammation/Fever:** The pharmacological activity of meloxicam may diminish the utility of diagnostic signs for detecting infections.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common Adverse Reactions (≥2% and > placebo):** Constipation, increased gamma-glutamyl transferase (GGT), and anemia [United States].

| Adverse Reaction | QAMZOVA (n=748) | Placebo (n=393) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Constipation | 7.6% | 6.1% |
| GGT Increased | 2.8% | 1.5% |
| Anemia | 2.4% | 1.0% |

- **Other Adverse Reactions (<2%):** 
    - **General:** Fatigue, pyrexia, infusion site reactions (pain, pruritus, phlebitis), edema.
    - **Nervous System:** Somnolence, syncope, migraine, dizziness.
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Abdominal pain, gastritis, vomiting, rectal hemorrhage.
    - **Psychiatric:** Insomnia, confusion, hallucinations.
- **Serious Postmarketing Events:** Acute urinary retention, agranulocytosis, liver failure, interstitial nephritis, and exfoliative dermatitis [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Drugs Interfering with Hemostasis (e.g., Warfarin, Aspirin, SSRIs/SNRIs):** Increased risk of serious GI bleeding. Monitor patients closely.
- **ACE Inhibitors, ARBs, and Beta-Blockers:** NSAIDs may diminish the antihypertensive effect. In elderly or volume-depleted patients, co-administration may result in reversible renal function deterioration, including acute renal failure.
- **Diuretics:** Meloxicam may reduce the natriuretic effect of loop and thiazide diuretics.
- **Lithium:** NSAIDs increase plasma lithium levels and reduce renal lithium clearance (approx. 20% decrease). Monitor for lithium toxicity [United States].
- **Methotrexate:** Concomitant use increases the risk of methotrexate toxicity (e.g., neutropenia, renal dysfunction).
- **Pemetrexed:** Meloxicam should be interrupted for 5 days before, the day of, and 2 days after pemetrexed administration in patients with renal impairment (CrCl 45–79 mL/min). Concomitant use is not recommended if CrCl is <45 mL/min [United States].
- **CYP2C9 Inhibitors (e.g., Amiodarone, Fluconazole):** May increase meloxicam plasma levels. Consider dose reduction.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:**
    - **General [United States]:** Avoid NSAIDs at ~30 weeks gestation and later due to the risk of premature closure of the fetal ductus arteriosus.
    - **20 to 30 Weeks Gestation:** Limit use to the lowest dose and shortest duration. If treatment exceeds 48 hours, monitor for oligohydramnios via ultrasound. Discontinue if oligohydramnios occurs.
- **Labor and Delivery:** No human data; animal studies show delayed parturition and increased stillbirth.
- **Breastfeeding:** No human data on presence in milk. Meloxicam is present in the milk of lactating rats. Consider the developmental benefits of breastfeeding alongside the mother's clinical need [United States].
- **Fertility:** NSAIDs may cause reversible infertility in females by delaying ovulation. Male fertility may also be compromised based on animal data (testicular degeneration) [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients under 18 years [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk for CV, GI, and renal adverse reactions. Exercise caution and monitor renal function.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Mild:** No dose adjustment required. 
    - **Moderate-to-Severe:** Not recommended; contraindicated in those at risk for renal failure due to volume depletion [United States].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild-to-Moderate:** No dose adjustment necessary. 
    - **Severe:** Not studied; use with caution and monitor for adverse events [United States].
- **CYP2C9 Poor Metabolizers:** Consider dose reduction as plasma levels may be abnormally high due to reduced clearance [United States].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibition of cyclooxygenase (COX-1 and COX-2), leading to reduced prostaglandin synthesis.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Meloxicam does not prolong the QTc interval at doses up to 180 mg (6 times the recommended dose) [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ is approximately 0.1 hours (6 minutes) following IV bolus. $C_{max}$ is 6667 ng/mL for a 30 mg dose.
    - **Distribution:** ~99.4% protein bound (primarily albumin). Volume of distribution is 9.63 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively hepatically metabolized, primarily by CYP2C9 (major) and CYP3A4 (minor).
    - **Elimination:** Excreted equally in urine and feces, mainly as metabolites. The mean elimination half-life is approximately 21 hours [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Bunionectomy and Abdominoplasty (Phase 3):** Two randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in adults with postoperative pain. 
- **Efficacy:** **QAMZOVA** 30 mg IV once daily demonstrated a statistically significant difference in the summed pain intensity difference over 48 hours (SPID48) compared to placebo. 
- **Rescue Medication:** Median time to first rescue analgesic use was 1 hour in the abdominoplasty study and 2 hours in the bunionectomy study [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [United States].
- **Handling:** Do not freeze. Protect from light.
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [United States].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription only (Rx Only). Poison schedule not applicable.
