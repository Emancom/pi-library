# Editorial Product Information: Quazepam

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of quazepam, synthesised for use by healthcare professionals. 
- This editorial synthesis is intended for informational purposes and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Quazepam (a trifluoroethyl benzodiazepine hypnotic agent).
- **Brand Name(s):** Doral [US].
- **ATC Code:** N05CD10.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 15 mg functionally scored, capsule-shaped, light orange tablets [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Atland Pharmaceuticals, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Insomnia:** Treatment of insomnia characterised by difficulty falling asleep, frequent nocturnal awakenings, and/or early morning awakenings [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The recommended initial dose is 7.5 mg. This may be increased to 15 mg if necessary for clinical efficacy [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets are functionally scored and may be split to achieve the 7.5 mg dose. Use the lowest effective dose to minimise dose-related adverse effects [US].
- **Duration of Therapy:** Effectiveness has been established for up to 5 nights in acute/chronic insomnia and up to 28 nights in sleep laboratory studies. Prolonged administration is generally not recommended as insomnia is often transient [US].
- **Discontinuation:** To reduce the risk of withdrawal, a gradual taper is required. If withdrawal symptoms occur, consider pausing the taper or returning to the previous dose before tapering more slowly [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to quazepam or other benzodiazepines (including rare cases of angioedema) [US].
- Established or suspected sleep apnea [US].
- Chronic pulmonary insufficiency [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning (United States):**
    - **Risks from Concomitant Use with Opioids:** May result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death.
    - **Abuse, Misuse, and Addiction:** Use exposes users to risks of addiction, which can lead to overdose or death.
    - **Dependence and Withdrawal Reactions:** Continued use may lead to physical dependence. Abrupt discontinuation or rapid dose reduction can precipitate life-threatening withdrawal reactions.
- **CNS Depression and Daytime Impairment:** May impair daytime function even when used as prescribed. Risk of next-day psychomotor impairment is increased if taken with less than 7–8 hours of sleep remaining.
- **Evaluation of Comorbidities:** Failure of insomnia to remit after 7–10 days of treatment requires evaluation for primary psychiatric or medical illnesses.
- **Severe Anaphylactic/Anaphylactoid Reactions:** Rare cases of angioedema involving the tongue, glottis, or larynx have been reported. Do not rechallenge if such reactions occur.
- **Complex Sleep Behaviours:** Risk of "sleep-driving," preparing/eating food, or making phone calls while not fully awake. Discontinue immediately if these occur.
- **Worsening of Depression:** Benzodiazepines may worsen depression; monitor for suicidal ideation.
- **Fall Risk:** Increased risk of falls and diminished consciousness, particularly in the elderly.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions reported at an incidence of ≥1% in clinical trials (15 mg dose) [US]:

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Daytime Drowsiness** | 12 | 3 |
| **Headache** | 5 | 2 |
| **Fatigue** | 2 | 0 |
| **Dizziness** | 2 | <1 |
| **Dry Mouth** | 2 | <1 |
| **Dyspepsia** | 1 | <1 |

**Serious Adverse Effects:**
- Respiratory depression [US].
- Acute withdrawal reactions (e.g., seizures) [US].
- Severe anaphylaxis and angioedema [US].
- Protracted withdrawal syndrome (lasting >12 months) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Opioids:** Increased risk of fatal respiratory depression and profound sedation. Limit dosage and duration to the minimum required [US].
- **CNS Depressants (e.g., alcohol, psychotropics, anticonvulsants):** Additive CNS depressant effects. Downward dose adjustment of quazepam may be necessary. Alcohol should generally be avoided [US].
- **Bupropion:** Co-administration of quazepam does not significantly affect the pharmacokinetics of bupropion or its metabolites [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified in current labeling; however, warnings exist for neonatal sedation and withdrawal syndrome (respiratory depression, lethargy, hypotonia, and irritability) when used late in pregnancy [US].
    - **Registry:** Healthcare providers are encouraged to register pregnant patients in the National Pregnancy Registry for Psychiatric Medications [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Quazepam and its metabolites are excreted in breast milk. Infants should be monitored for sedation, poor feeding, and poor weight gain [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in mice showed slight reductions in fertility at doses 60–180 times the human dose, likely due to excessive sedation [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased sensitivity to benzodiazepines. Start with 7.5 mg and monitor closely for confusion, over-sedation, and falls. Elimination half-life of active metabolites is approximately twice as long in the elderly as in younger adults [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Specific dosing adjustments not provided; however, elderly/debilitated patients with decreased renal function may show increased sensitivity [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Specific dosing adjustments not provided; use caution as the drug is extensively metabolised in the liver [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** GABAA receptor agonist; binds to stereo-specific receptors at several sites within the CNS [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Sedative-hypnotic effects; decreases sleep latency and total wake time while increasing total sleep time [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; peak plasma concentrations reached in approximately 2 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively hepatic; converted to active metabolites: 2-oxoquazepam and N-desalkyl-2-oxoquazepam [US].
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding >95% [US].
    - **Elimination:** Mean elimination half-life is 39 hours for quazepam/2-oxoquazepam and 73 hours for N-desalkyl-2-oxoquazepam. Excreted via urine (31%) and feces (23%) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Insomnia Studies:** Placebo-controlled trials (5 nights) demonstrated efficacy in acute and chronic insomnia.
- **Sleep Laboratory (Polysomnography):** A 28-night study in chronic insomnia showed significant decreases in sleep latency and wake time. Effects persisted for several nights after discontinuation. No "rebound insomnia" was observed in a small study of 12 patients [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at controlled room temperature, 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Handling:** Dispense in a tight container [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Schedule IV (C-IV) controlled substance; Rx-only [US].
