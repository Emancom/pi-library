# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (Food and Drug Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises the clinical data and regulatory instructions provided in the available Product Information (PI).
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to local regulatory guidelines for final prescribing authority.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (Generic name); chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. 
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection solution: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in a single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It may be administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). Administration should occur as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Loading Doses:** In acute metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often administered, followed by equivalent doses over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and not less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Monitoring:** Plasma carnitine concentrations should be obtained prior to initiating parenteral therapy. Monitoring (weekly and monthly) should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels (target free carnitine concentration: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** In ESRD, downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) levocarnitine concentrations [US].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These reactions have occurred within minutes of administration, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. If a reaction occurs, treatment must be discontinued immediately and appropriate medical intervention initiated [US].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures has been observed following levocarnitine administration [US].
- **Oral Formulation Limitation:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of the oral formulation in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are frequently confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disorders.

### Frequency Data (ESRD Population)
| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%), Flu syndrome (25%), Pharyngitis (23%), Headache (22%), Hypertension (20%), Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%), Infection (15%), Hypotension (14%), Dizziness (13%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Abdominal pain, Accidental injury, Asthenia, Chest pain, Fever, Back pain, Tachycardia, Palpitations, Nausea, Hypercalcemia, Hyperkalemia, Peripheral edema, Depression, Insomnia, Cough increase, Dyspnea, Rhinitis, Pruritus, Rash |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Anaphylaxis and Severe Hypersensitivity:** Characterized by bronchospasm and laryngeal edema [US].
- **Neurologic:** Emergence or exacerbation of seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended that INR levels be monitored upon initiation of levocarnitine or following any dosage adjustment in patients receiving warfarin anticoagulation [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Pregnancy Category not specified under current labeling, but studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm]. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration. The benefits to the mother should be weighed against the potential risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal reproductive studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is based on mg/kg body weight, similar to adults (see Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific data not provided in the source; clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65 and over to determine if they respond differently than younger subjects.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily used in ESRD patients. However, the oral formulation should be used with caution due to metabolite accumulation (see Section 5) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It acts as a carrier molecule facilitating the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Excretion:** Approximately 76% of a single IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis, 20 mg/kg levocarnitine IV three times weekly increased mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine concentrations from 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks of therapy [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg showed dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].
