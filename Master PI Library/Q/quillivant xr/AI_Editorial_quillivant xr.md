# Editorial Product Information: Methylphenidate Hydrochloride (Quillivant XR)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) documents from the following jurisdictions:
    - United States (Food and Drug Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI. Clinicians must refer to the current local PI when prescribing in their specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Methylphenidate hydrochloride (CNS stimulant)
- **Brand Name(s):** **Quillivant XR** [United States]
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Extended-release oral suspension: 25 mg per 5 mL (5 mg/mL) after reconstitution [United States].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** NextWave Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (a subsidiary of Tris Pharma, Inc.) [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD):** Indicated for the treatment of ADHD in patients 6 years and older [United States].
- **Limitations of Use:** Use is not recommended in pediatric patients under 6 years of age due to higher plasma exposure and increased incidence of adverse reactions compared to older patients [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Pediatric 6 years and older/Adult):**
    - The recommended starting dose is 20 mg orally once daily in the morning [United States].
    - **Titration:** Dosage may be adjusted weekly in increments of 10 mg to 20 mg per day until a therapeutic, well-tolerated dose is reached [United States].
    - **Maximum Dose:** Daily dosages exceeding 60 mg are not recommended and have not been studied [United States].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Must be taken orally once daily in the morning with or without food [United States]. 
    - **Vigorous shaking:** The bottle must be shaken for at least 10 seconds before every dose to ensure proper drug distribution [United States].
    - **Reconstitution (Pharmacist Only):** Reconstitute with the specified amount of water. Tap the bottle to loosen powder, add water, insert the bottle adapter, and shake vigorously for at least 10 seconds [United States].
- **Switching from other Methylphenidate Products:**
    - Discontinue current treatment and titrate using the **Quillivant XR** schedule. Do not substitute on a milligram-per-milligram basis due to differing pharmacokinetic profiles [United States].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources; however, patients are generally advised to consult their healthcare provider [United States].
- **Discontinuation:** If symptoms do not improve after one month of appropriate dose adjustment, or if paradoxical aggravation occurs, the drug should be discontinued [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to methylphenidate or any excipients in the formulation (e.g., angioedema or anaphylactic reactions) [United States].
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Concurrent use with MAOIs, or use within 14 days following discontinuation of an MAOI, due to the risk of hypertensive crisis [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Abuse, Misuse, and Addiction [United States]:**
    - CNS stimulants, including **Quillivant XR**, have a high potential for abuse and misuse, which can lead to substance use disorders and addiction. 
    - Misuse and abuse can result in overdose and death.
    - Assess risk before prescribing; monitor patients frequently for signs of abuse and addiction during treatment.
- **Serious Cardiac Disease:** Avoid use in patients with known structural cardiac abnormalities, cardiomyopathy, serious arrhythmias, or coronary artery disease. Sudden death has been reported in these populations at recommended dosages [United States].
- **Blood Pressure and Heart Rate:** Can cause clinically significant increases in heart rate and blood pressure. Periodic monitoring of all patients is required [United States].
- **Psychiatric Adverse Reactions:**
    - May exacerbate symptoms in patients with pre-existing psychosis.
    - May induce manic or mixed episodes in patients with bipolar disorder (screen for risk factors prior to initiation).
    - New psychotic or manic symptoms (e.g., hallucinations, delusional thinking) can occur in patients without a prior history [United States].
- **Priapism:** Prolonged and painful erections, sometimes requiring surgical intervention, have been reported in both pediatric and adult patients [United States].
- **Peripheral Vasculopathy (including Raynaud’s Phenomenon):** Stimulants are associated with digital changes and ulceration. Monitor for new numbness, pain, or skin colour changes in fingers or toes [United States].
- **Growth Suppression:** Long-term use in pediatric patients is associated with temporary slowing in growth rate (height and weight). Monitor growth closely; treatment interruption may be necessary [United States].
- **Ocular Effects:** 
    - Risk of acute angle-closure glaucoma.
    - Elevation of intraocular pressure (IOP); use with caution in patients with open-angle glaucoma or increased IOP [United States].
- **Tics and Tourette’s Syndrome:** May onset or exacerbate motor and verbal tics. Evaluate family history and monitor patients regularly [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are based on accumulated data from methylphenidate products.

**Common Adverse Reactions (≥5% and twice the rate of placebo):**
- Decreased appetite, insomnia, nausea, vomiting, dyspepsia, abdominal pain, weight decreased, anxiety, dizziness, irritability, affect lability, tachycardia, and increased blood pressure [United States].

**Pediatric-Specific Data (Phase 3 Trial, ages 6–12):**

**Serious Adverse Effects:**
- Sudden cardiac death, stroke, myocardial infarction, seizure, and serotonin syndrome (when used with serotonergic drugs) [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **MAOIs:** Risk of hypertensive crisis. Avoid concurrent use or use within 14 days of MAOI cessation [United States].
- **Antihypertensive Drugs:** Methylphenidate may decrease the effectiveness of these agents; monitor blood pressure [United States].
- **Halogenated Anesthetics:** Increased risk of sudden blood pressure/heart rate increases during surgery. Avoid **Quillivant XR** on the day of surgery [United States].
- **Risperidone:** Combined use may increase the risk of extrapyramidal symptoms (EPS) during dosage changes [United States].
- **Alcohol:** Consumption should be avoided as it may cause a more rapid release of the methylphenidate dose [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not specified by letter [United States].
    - **Risk Summary:** Limited data in humans are insufficient to inform drug-associated risks. Animal studies (rabbits) showed spina bifida at 40 times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD).
    - **Clinical Considerations:** Stimulants can cause vasoconstriction, potentially decreasing placental perfusion [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** Methylphenidate is present in human milk (milk/plasma ratio 1.1 to 2.7). Monitor infants for agitation, insomnia, anorexia, and reduced weight gain [United States].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in mice studies at doses up to 8 times the MRHD [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established for ages 6–17. Not recommended for children under 6 years [United States].
- **Geriatric Use:** Not studied in patients over 65 years of age [United States].
- **Renal Impairment:** Renal clearance is not a significant route of methylphenidate elimination; no dosage adjustment is expected to be necessary [United States].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources (no experience in this population) [United States].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A CNS stimulant. It is thought to block the reuptake of norepinephrine and dopamine into the presynaptic neuron and increase their release into the extraneuronal space [United States].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** A racemic mixture of $d$- and $l$-isomers. The $d$-isomer is the more pharmacologically active component [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Contains approximately 20% immediate-release and 80% extended-release methylphenidate. Fasted median Tmax for $d$-methylphenidate is 5 hours.
    - **Food Effect:** High-fat meals increase Cmax by 28% and AUC by 19%, but these changes are not considered clinically significant.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via deesterification to alpha-phenyl-piperidine acetic acid (PPAA), which is inactive.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 90% is recovered in urine. Mean terminal half-life is approximately 5.6 hours [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Study (Ages 6–12):** A laboratory classroom study (N=45) using a randomized, double-blind, crossover design. 
- **Outcome:** **Quillivant XR** demonstrated statistically significant improvement in ADHD symptoms compared to placebo based on SKAMP-Combined scores.
- **Duration of Effect:** Significant improvement was observed at all tested time points from 0.75 hours to 12 hours post-dosing [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [United States].
- **Reconstituted Stability:** The suspension is stable for up to 4 months after reconstitution [United States].
- **Handling:** Dispense in original packaging with the bottle adapter and oral dosing dispenser. Store upright [United States].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule/Legal Classification:** 
    - **United States:** Schedule II controlled substance (CII); Rx-only.
