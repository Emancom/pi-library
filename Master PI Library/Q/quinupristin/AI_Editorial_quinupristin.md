# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial is derived from the following jurisdictional Product Information (PI) source: United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the provided regulatory information for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available source (Standard: A16AA01).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for both acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Administer via the intravenous route only. 
- Parenteral products should be visually inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use [US].

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by equivalent dosing over the next 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours, and not less frequently than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
- **Method:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments (e.g., reduction to 5 mg/kg) should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels and may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of treatment [US].

### Monitoring
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels before initiating parenteral therapy.
- Conduct weekly and monthly monitoring, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma free carnitine concentrations (target range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, particularly in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizures:** Seizures have occurred in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with known seizure disorders, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures has been reported following levocarnitine administration [US].

### Clinical Precautions
- **Oral Formulation Note:** The safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of the oral form in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO) [US].
- **Monitoring:** In ESRD patients, monitor trough levels closely to avoid excessive accumulation [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common and Very Common (>1%)
Based on clinical trials in ESRD patients on dialysis, the following events occurred at a frequency of ≥5% (regardless of causality):
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), flu syndrome (25%), headache (22%), infection (15%), abdominal pain (9%), fever (7%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), chest pain (12%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), anorexia (5%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%).
- **Neurologic:** Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), paresthesia (5%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%), hypervolemia (5%).

### Other Reported Reactions
- **Body Odor:** Transient nausea, vomiting, and body odor have been observed [US].
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (postmarketing) [US].
- **Gastritis:** Reported as less frequent [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. 
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels in patients on warfarin therapy following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not assigned/Not specified]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits using doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. There are no adequate studies in pregnant women; use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefit to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no impairment of fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See Dosage and Administration; dosing is weight-based (mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available source; general caution is typically advised in dosing for elderly patients due to potential renal or hepatic decline.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosing is specifically provided for ESRD patients on hemodialysis (see Section 3).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available source.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) and γ-butyrobetaine [US].
- **Excretion:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg showed dose-proportional increases in trough concentrations at 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the impact on clinical outcomes in this specific population has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing.
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservative [US].
