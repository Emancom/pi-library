# Editorial Product Information: Colestyramine (Questran)

## Editorial Note
- Regulatory agencies' PI documents contributing to this editorial: Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA - Australia) and the Food and Drug Administration (FDA - United States).
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals by synthesising information from international regulatory sources.
- This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active ingredient:** Colestyramine (also referred to as Cholestyramine [FDA]).
- **Chemical class:** Basic anion-exchange resin; chloride salt.
- **Brand name(s):** **Questran**, **Questran Lite**.
- **ATC code:** C10AC01.
- **Dosage form(s) and strength(s):**
    - **Questran (Regular):** Powder for oral suspension containing 4 g of anhydrous colestyramine resin in 9 g of powder [FDA].
    - **Questran Lite:** 
        - [TGA]: 4 g anhydrous resin in a 4.7 g sachet; 8 g anhydrous resin in a 9.4 g sachet.
        - [FDA]: 4 g anhydrous resin in a 5 g sachet or scoop.
- **Route(s) of administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/sponsor:** Viatris Pty Ltd [TGA]; Endo USA, Inc. [FDA].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Primary Hypercholesterolaemia:** Adjunctive therapy to diet for the reduction of elevated serum cholesterol in patients with primary hypercholesterolaemia (elevated LDL cholesterol). It may be used in patients with combined hypercholesterolaemia and hypertriglyceridaemia, though it is not indicated as primary therapy for isolated hypertriglyceridaemia.
- **Prevention of Coronary Heart Disease:** Indicated to reduce the risk of CHD and related mortality in patients with primary hypercholesterolaemia.
- **Pruritus:** Relief of pruritus associated with partial biliary obstruction, including patients with primary biliary cirrhosis.
- **Diarrhoea:** Relief of diarrhoea resulting from ileal resection or ileal disease (cholerrhoeic enteropathy) [TGA-approved; not explicitly listed in provided FDA PI].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Starting Dose:** 4 g (one sachet/scoop) once or twice daily.
- **Maintenance Dose:** 8 g to 16 g of anhydrous resin daily, typically divided into two doses [FDA]. The TGA notes a usual maintenance range of 12 g to 16 g.
- **Maximum Dose:** 24 g of anhydrous colestyramine resin per day.

### Administration Instructions
- **Warning:** **Colestyramine must never be taken in its dry form.** Ingesting the dry powder carries a risk of inhalation or oesophageal obstruction.
- **Mixing:** The powder should be mixed with 60 mL to 150 mL (2 to 6 ounces) of water, fruit juice, or other non-carbonated beverages. It may also be mixed with highly fluid soups or pulpy fruits (e.g., apple puree, crushed pineapple).
- **Timing:** Ideally administered at mealtime, though timing may be adjusted to avoid interference with the absorption of other medications.

### Titration and Monitoring
- Dosage increases should be gradual, with assessment of lipid levels at intervals of at least 4 weeks.
- If response for bile acid-induced diarrhoea is not observed within 3 days, alternative therapy should be considered [TGA].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to colestyramine or any excipients in the formulation.
- Complete biliary obstruction (where bile is not secreted into the intestine).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Phenylketonuria (PKU):** **Questran Lite** contains aspartame. 
    - [TGA]: Contains 16.8 mg phenylalanine per 4 g dose.
    - [FDA]: Contains 14.0 mg phenylalanine per 5 g dose.
- **Hyperchloraemic Acidosis:** Prolonged use, especially in children and smaller patients, may lead to hyperchloraemic acidosis as colestyramine is a chloride form of resin. Caution is advised in patients with renal insufficiency or volume depletion, and those taking spironolactone [FDA].
- **Constipation:** May produce or aggravate pre-existing constipation. Dosage should be reduced if impaction is a risk, particularly in patients with symptomatic coronary artery disease.
- **Vitamin Deficiencies:** Chronic use may interfere with the absorption of fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K). Supplementation with water-miscible or parenteral forms of vitamins A and D is recommended for long-term therapy.
- **Bleeding Tendencies:** Vitamin K deficiency-induced hypoprothrombinaemia may occur, increasing bleeding risk. This is reversible with parenteral Vitamin K1.
- **Dental Health:** Prolonged contact of the resin suspension in the mouth may cause tooth discoloration, enamel erosion, or decay. Maintain good oral hygiene.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### More Common (>10%)
- **Gastrointestinal:** Constipation (the most frequent complaint, especially in patients >60 years or on high doses). It may be associated with faecal impaction and aggravation of haemorrhoids.

### Less Common (0.1–10%)
- **Gastrointestinal:** Abdominal pain/distention, flatulence, nausea, vomiting, heartburn, diarrhoea, anorexia, dyspepsia, and steatorrhoea.
- **Haematological:** Bleeding tendencies (hypoprothrombinaemia) due to Vitamin K deficiency; Vitamin A and D deficiencies.
- **Dermatological:** Rash and irritation of the skin, tongue, or perianal area.

### Serious Adverse Effects (Frequency Rare/Not Specified)
- **Intestinal Obstruction:** Rare post-marketing reports, including paediatric fatalities.
- **Biliary/Hepatic:** Calcified material in the biliary tree/gallbladder; biliary colic.
- **Hyperchloraemic Acidosis:** Reported in children and elderly patients [TGA].

### Other Reported Events (Relationship to drug not necessarily established)
- Rectal bleeding, black stools, peptic ulcer activation, pancreatitis, headache, dizziness, tinnitus, muscle/joint pain, uveitis, and haematuria.

## 7. Drug Interactions
Colestyramine is a strong anion-exchange resin and may delay or reduce the absorption of almost any concomitant oral medication.
- **General Management:** Administer other drugs at least **1 hour before** or **4 to 6 hours after** colestyramine.
- **Acidic Drugs:** High affinity for acidic drugs, including warfarin, phenylbutazone, and thiazide diuretics (e.g., chlorothiazide).
- **Other Affected Medications:** Thyroid and levothyroxine preparations, digitalis/digoxin, tetracycline, phenobarbital, progestins, estrogens, and iron.
- **Enterohepatic Recirculation:** May interfere with drugs that undergo enterohepatic circulation (e.g., estrogens).
- **Bile Acid Sequestrants:** May interfere with oral phosphate supplements.
- **Toxicity Risk:** Discontinuing colestyramine in a patient stabilised on a toxic drug (e.g., digitalis) may lead to a sudden increase in that drug's plasma levels as the "buffering" effect of the resin is removed.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - [TGA Category]: **B2**. 
    - [FDA Category]: **C**.
    - **Clinical Note:** Colestyramine is not absorbed systemically; however, it interferes with fat-soluble vitamin absorption. This may result in maternal and foetal vitamin deficiencies even with supplementation. Benefit must be weighed against potential hazards.
- **Breastfeeding:** Caution is advised. Potential lack of vitamin absorption in the mother may impact the vitamin content of breast milk and affect the nursing infant.
- **Fertility:** Not specified in available sources; no data available [TGA].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - [FDA]: Usual dose is 240 mg/kg/day of anhydrous resin in 2–3 divided doses, not to exceed 8 g/day. 
    - [TGA]: Experience is limited and a practical schedule is not established; start with small doses. 
    - **Risk:** Higher risk of hyperchloraemic acidosis.
- **Elderly Use:** Patients over 60 years are at increased risk of gastrointestinal side effects, particularly severe constipation.
- **Renal Impairment:** Exercise caution due to the risk of hyperchloraemic acidosis [FDA].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution in obstructive liver disease; however, it is specifically indicated for pruritus in partial biliary obstruction.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Colestyramine is a non-absorbable resin that binds bile acids in the intestine, forming an insoluble complex excreted in the faeces. This removes bile acids from the enterohepatic circulation, prompting the liver to convert more cholesterol into bile acids. This results in increased LDL-receptor activity and a decrease in serum LDL-cholesterol levels.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Not absorbed from the digestive tract.
    - **Metabolism/Elimination:** Excreted entirely in the faeces as an insoluble complex with bile acids.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Serum triglyceride levels may increase or remain unchanged during therapy.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **LRC-CPPT (Lipid Research Clinics Coronary Primary Prevention Trial):** A 7-year study involving 3,806 men showed that colestyramine significantly reduced total cholesterol and LDL-C. The treated group experienced a 19% reduction in the combined rate of CHD death plus non-fatal myocardial infarction [FDA].
- **Atherosclerosis Regression:** Arteriographic studies (NHLBI Type II and STARS) demonstrated that colestyramine monotherapy can slow the progression and promote the regression of coronary atherosclerotic lesions [FDA].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **TGA:** Store below 30°C.
- **FDA:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Poison Schedule **S4** (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **United States:** **Rx-only** (Prescription Only).
