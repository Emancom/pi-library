# Editorial Product Information: Colestyramine (Questran)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) documents provided by the Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA, Australia) and the Food and Drug Administration (FDA, United States).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical data, regulatory variations, and administration guidelines from two major jurisdictions.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI used for prescribing. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current documentation for legal and prescribing compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Colestyramine (also spelled Cholestyramine [FDA]); a basic anion exchange resin (chloride salt).
- **Brand Names:** Questran, Questran Lite.
- **ATC Code:** C10AC01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Questran (Standard):** 4 g anhydrous colestyramine resin in 9 g of powder [FDA].
    - **Questran Lite (Sugar-Free):** 4 g anhydrous colestyramine resin in 4.7 g [TGA] or 5 g [FDA] of powder. Also available as 8 g resin in 9.4 g powder [TGA].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral (suspension).
- **Manufacturers/Sponsors:** Viatris Pty Ltd [TGA]; Endo USA, Inc. [FDA].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Primary Hypercholesterolaemia:** Adjunctive therapy to diet for the reduction of elevated serum cholesterol in patients with primary hypercholesterolaemia (elevated LDL cholesterol).
- **Coronary Heart Disease (CHD) Prevention:** Indicated to reduce the risks of CHD mortality and non-fatal myocardial infarction [FDA] and for the prevention of coronary heart disease [TGA].
- **Pruritus:** Relief of pruritus associated with partial biliary obstruction (e.g., primary biliary cirrhosis).
- **Bile Acid Diarrhoea:** Relief of diarrhoea following ileal resection or ileal disease (cholerrhoeic enteropathy) [TGA-approved; not listed as a primary indication in FDA source].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Starting Dose:** 4 g (one sachet/scoop) once or twice daily [FDA]. The TGA recommends beginning with 4 g in the morning or evening, increasing over 2 to 4 weeks.
- **Maintenance Dose:** 12 g to 16 g of resin per day [TGA]; 8 g to 16 g per day divided into two doses [FDA].
- **Maximum Dose:** 24 g of resin per day.

### Administration Instructions
- **Preparation:** **Never ingest in dry form.** The powder must be mixed with 60 mL to 150 mL [FDA/TGA] of water, fruit juice, highly fluid soups, or pulpy fruits (e.g., apple puree, crushed pineapple).
- **Mixing:** Sprinkle the powder on the surface of the liquid and allow it to stand for 1–2 minutes before stirring or shaking to avoid clumps.
- **Timing:** Preferably taken at mealtime, but timing may be adjusted to avoid interference with other medications.

### Titration and Monitoring
- Dose increases should be gradual (at intervals of not less than 4 weeks) with periodic assessment of lipid levels. 
- In cases of bile acid-induced diarrhoea, a clinical response should typically be observed within 3 days; if no response occurs, alternative therapy is indicated [TGA].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to colestyramine or any of the excipients (e.g., aspartame in the "Lite" formulation).
- Complete biliary obstruction where no bile is secreted into the intestine.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Dry Ingestion:** To prevent accidental inhalation or oesophageal obstruction, colestyramine must always be mixed with fluids.
- **Phenylketonuria (PKU):** **Questran Lite** contains aspartame, a source of phenylalanine. 
    - [TGA]: 16.8 mg phenylalanine per 4 g resin dose.
    - [FDA]: 14.0 mg phenylalanine per 5 g "Lite" dose.
- **Dental Health:** Prolonged contact of the resin suspension with teeth may cause surface changes (discoloration, erosion, or decay). Maintain good oral hygiene.

### Clinical Precautions
- **Chronic Use/Bleeding Risk:** Long-term use may interfere with the absorption of fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K). Vitamin K deficiency can lead to hypoprothrombinaemia and increased bleeding tendencies. Folic acid deficiency has also been reported.
- **Hyperchloraemic Acidosis:** Because colestyramine is a chloride-form resin, its use may lead to hyperchloraemic acidosis, particularly in children and smaller patients [TGA/FDA]. Caution is advised in patients with renal insufficiency, volume depletion, or those taking spironolactone [FDA].
- **Gastrointestinal Effects:** May produce or aggravate pre-existing constipation. Use with extreme caution in patients with symptomatic coronary artery disease to avoid the strain of impaction.

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (>10%) | Constipation (may be severe, occasionally leading to faecal impaction) |
| **Common/Uncommon** | Abdominal pain/distension, flatulence, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, heartburn, anorexia, dyspepsia, steatorrhoea. |
| **Rare** (<0.1%) | Intestinal obstruction (including paediatric fatalities), biliary colic, calcified material in the biliary tree/gallbladder (may be disease-related). |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** 
- Hypoprothrombinaemia (bleeding) due to Vitamin K deficiency.
- Hyperchloraemic acidosis.
- Osteoporosis (associated with Vitamin D deficiency).

## 7. Drug Interactions
Colestyramine may delay or reduce the absorption of concomitantly administered oral medications. 
- **Management:** Other drugs should be taken **at least 1 hour before** or **4 to 6 hours after** colestyramine.
- **Specifically Affected Drugs:** 
    - Warfarin and other anticoagulants.
    - Phenylbutazone.
    - Thiazide diuretics (acidic).
    - Propranolol (basic) [FDA].
    - Tetracycline, Penicillin G [FDA].
    - Phenobarbital.
    - Thyroid and levothyroxine preparations.
    - Digitalis (digoxin).
    - Oestrogens (interference with enterohepatic recirculation).
    - Oral phosphate supplements [FDA].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **TGA Category B2:** Safety in pregnant women has not been established.
    - **FDA Category C:** No adequate, well-controlled studies. 
    - **Note:** Colestyramine is not absorbed systemically; however, interference with maternal vitamin absorption may be detrimental to the fetus, even with supplementation.
- **Breastfeeding:** Use with caution. The potential lack of maternal vitamin absorption may affect the nursing infant.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - [FDA]: Standard texts suggest 240 mg/kg/day of anhydrous resin in 2–3 divided doses, typically not exceeding 8 g/day.
    - [TGA]: Specific dosage schedules for infants and children have not been established; therapy should begin with small doses.
    - *Risk:* Higher susceptibility to hyperchloraemic acidosis and intestinal obstruction.
- **Elderly Use:** Patients over 60 years are at increased risk of gastrointestinal side effects and constipation.
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution; potential for hyperchloraemic acidosis.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Colestyramine is a non-absorbable anion exchange resin. It adsorbs and combines with bile acids in the intestine to form an insoluble complex, which is excreted in the faeces. This prevents the reabsorption of bile acids (enterohepatic circulation), leading the liver to increase the oxidation of cholesterol into bile acids, thereby lowering serum LDL cholesterol.

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Not absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract.
- **Distribution/Metabolism:** Not applicable (not absorbed).
- **Elimination:** Excreted entirely in the faeces as an insoluble complex with bile acids.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **LRC-CPPT Study:** A 7-year, placebo-controlled study of 3,806 men showed that colestyramine produced a 19% reduction in the combined rate of CHD death and non-fatal myocardial infarction.
- **Regression Studies:** Arteriographic trials (NHLBI Type II, STARS) demonstrated that colestyramine monotherapy can retard the progression and promote the regression of atherosclerotic lesions in coronary arteries [FDA].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Australia [TGA]:** Store below 30°C.
- **USA [FDA]:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F).

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Schedule 4 (S4) – Prescription Only Medicine.
- **United States:** Rx Only.
