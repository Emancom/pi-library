# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection, synthesised for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of preparation, only the United States (FDA) source documentation was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours (q3h or q4h), and never less frequently than every 6 hours (q6h) [US].
- **Monitoring:** Clinical response and plasma carnitine levels (target range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) should guide subsequent daily doses, which may reach up to 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow (2–3 minute) bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be implemented as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are adequate [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use [US].
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, particularly in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued and appropriate medical treatment initiated [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine (oral or IV) has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations is recommended [US].
- **Oral Formulation Caution:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine in patients with renal insufficiency have not been evaluated. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD or severely impaired renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common and Very Common (>1%)
Based on clinical trials in patients with ESRD on hemodialysis, the following events occurred at a frequency of ≥5% (regardless of causality):
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), flu syndrome (25%), headache (22%), chest pain (12%), fever (7%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), abdominal pain (9%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), rhinitis (9%), dyspnea (7%).
- **Neurological:** Dizziness (13%), paresthesia (5%), insomnia (4%).

### Serious and Other Adverse Effects
- **Neurological:** Seizures (reported in post-marketing experience) [US].
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%), hypervolemia (5%) [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis (less frequent) [US].
- **Other:** Body odor [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Clinical Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not assigned a letter category in the current provided source]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after exogenous administration. Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal reproductive studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Refer to the "Dosage and Administration" section for metabolic disorder dosing, which applies to paediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in the available source.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosage adjustments are required for ESRD patients on dialysis (see Section 3). The safety of oral formulations in renal impairment is not established [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, levocarnitine corrects the deficiency and the high ratio of acylcarnitine/carnitine caused by dialysis losses and reduced dietary intake [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg three times weekly), mean trough levocarnitine levels increased from 21.3 µmol/L at baseline to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) demonstrated dose-dependent increases in trough concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Clinical Outcomes:** While IV levocarnitine effectively increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the impact on specific clinical outcomes and signs/symptoms of carnitine deficiency in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Not specified; refer to expiry date on packaging.
- **Handling:** Solution is compatible with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL concentrations) for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature [US].
