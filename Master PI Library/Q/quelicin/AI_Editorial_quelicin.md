# Editorial Product Information: Succinylcholine Chloride (Quelicin)

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of succinylcholine chloride for healthcare professionals, synthesising data from available regulatory sources.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing in any particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Succinylcholine chloride (a depolarizing neuromuscular blocker).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Quelicin** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources (standard code: M03AB01).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Injection: 200 mg/10 mL (20 mg/mL) in multiple-dose fliptop vials [US].
    - Injection: 1,000 mg/10 mL (100 mg/mL) in single-dose fliptop vials (requires dilution) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) or Intramuscular (IM).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Hospira, Inc. (a Pfizer company) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Quelicin** is indicated for use in both adult and pediatric patients [US]:
- As an adjunct to general anesthesia.
- To facilitate tracheal intubation.
- To provide skeletal muscle relaxation during surgical procedures or mechanical ventilation.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Short Surgical Procedures:** The average dose for neuromuscular blockade and intubation is 0.6 mg/kg IV. The optimal dose ranges from 0.3 mg/kg to 1.1 mg/kg.
- **Test Dose:** A 5 mg to 10 mg IV test dose may be used to assess patient sensitivity and recovery time.
- **Long Surgical Procedures (Continuous Infusion):** Average rate is 2.5 mg to 4.3 mg per minute. Solutions are typically diluted to 1 mg/mL or 2 mg/mL. 
- **Intermittent Injection:** Initial IV dose of 0.3 mg/kg to 1.1 mg/kg, followed by 0.04 mg/kg to 0.07 mg/kg at appropriate intervals as needed for relaxation.

### Administration Instructions
- **Monitoring:** Neuromuscular function should be monitored with a peripheral nerve stimulator, especially during infusions, to avoid overdose and detect Phase II block.
- **Pretreatment:** Prophylactic administration of anticholinergics (e.g., atropine) may reduce the risk of bradyarrhythmias.
- **Unconsciousness:** Do not administer until unconsciousness has been induced to avoid patient distress.
- **Incompatibility:** Succinylcholine is acidic (pH 3.0–4.5) and is incompatible with alkaline solutions (e.g., barbiturates); do not mix in the same syringe.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to succinylcholine [US].
- Skeletal muscle myopathies (e.g., Duchenne muscular dystrophy) [US].
- Genetic susceptibility to malignant hyperthermia (e.g., RYR1 or CACNA1S variants) [US].
- Acute phase of injury following major burns, multiple trauma, extensive skeletal muscle denervation, or upper motor neuron injury (due to risk of severe hyperkalemia) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning [US]
- **Ventricular Dysrhythmias and Cardiac Arrest in Pediatrics:** Acute rhabdomyolysis followed by hyperkalemia and death has occurred in seemingly healthy children later found to have undiagnosed skeletal muscle myopathy. Reserve use in children for emergency intubation or when immediate airway security is mandatory.

### General Warnings
- **Anaphylaxis:** Severe, potentially fatal reactions have been reported. Cross-reactivity with other neuromuscular blockers is possible.
- **Hyperkalemia:** May induce cardiac arrest in patients with electrolyte abnormalities, digitalis toxicity, chronic abdominal infections, or subarachnoid hemorrhage.
- **Malignant Hyperthermia (MH):** Can trigger a hypermetabolic state. Treatment requires immediate discontinuation of succinylcholine and administration of IV dantrolene.
- **Bradycardia:** Common in pediatric patients after the first dose; in adults, it usually occurs after repeated doses.
- **Increased Pressures:** May transiently increase intraocular, intracranial, and intragastric pressure. Use with caution in patients with narrow-angle glaucoma or penetrating eye injuries.
- **Phase II Block:** Prolonged use may lead to a transition from a depolarizing (Phase I) block to a block resembling non-depolarizing agents (Phase II), resulting in prolonged respiratory depression.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Frequencies are not specifically quantified in the source PI (postmarketing report basis).
- **Cardiovascular:** Cardiac arrest, arrhythmias, bradycardia, tachycardia, hypertension, hypotension.
- **Respiratory:** Prolonged respiratory depression or apnea.
- **Musculoskeletal:** Rhabdomyolysis (with potential acute renal failure), muscle fasciculation, jaw rigidity, postoperative muscle pain.
- **Other:** Hyperkalemia, increased intraocular pressure, excessive salivation, rash, and anaphylactic reactions [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Enhancement of Neuromuscular Block:** Blocking action may be intensified by:
    - Certain antibiotics (non-penicillin types), promazine, oxytocin, aprotinin, quinidine, β-blockers, procainamide, lidocaine, lithium carbonate, magnesium salts, quinine, and chloroquine.
    - Volatile anaesthetics (e.g., isoflurane, desflurane).
- **Plasma Cholinesterase Inhibitors:** Drugs that reduce plasma cholinesterase activity (e.g., oral contraceptives, glucocorticoids, MAOIs, organophosphate insecticides, metoclopramide) will prolong the effect of succinylcholine [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not specified; standard letter categories phased out]. Human data from decades of use have not identified a clear risk of major birth defects or miscarriage. However, plasma cholinesterase levels decrease by ~24% during pregnancy, potentially prolonging apnea. It crosses the placental barrier in small amounts.
- **Breastfeeding:** No data available on presence in human milk or effects on the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No animal studies have been conducted to evaluate impacts on fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Established from neonates to adolescents. Effective doses may be higher than predicted by weight due to a larger volume of distribution. IV dose: 2 mg/kg for infants/small children; 1 mg/kg for older children [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Generally, start at the low end of the dosing range due to the higher frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Reduced plasma cholinesterase activity associated with severe liver or kidney disease may result in prolonged neuromuscular blockade [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A short-acting depolarizing agent. It competes with acetylcholine for cholinergic receptors at the motor end plate, causing persistent depolarization (often seen as fasciculations) and subsequent skeletal muscle paralysis.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Onset is <1 minute via IV; recovery from a single dose occurs within 4–6 minutes.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly hydrolysed by plasma cholinesterase to succinylmonocholine (minimal activity) and then to succinic acid and choline.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 10% is excreted unchanged in the urine [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Pivotal trial data for the initial approval (1952) are not detailed in the current PI [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store in a refrigerator at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) [US].
- **Stability:** Multiple-dose vials are stable at room temperature for up to 14 days without significant loss of potency [US].
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials contain no preservative; discard unused portions. Diluted solutions (1–2 mg/mL) should be used within 24 hours of preparation [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only (Prescription only) [US].
- **Special Handling:** Must be stored in a manner that prevents accidental administration, as it is a potent paralyzing agent that can cause fatal respiratory arrest.
