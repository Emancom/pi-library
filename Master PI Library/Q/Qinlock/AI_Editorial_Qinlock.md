# Editorial Product Information: Ripretinib

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Regulatory Agencies:** Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA), Australia; Food and Drug Administration (FDA), United States.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the approved Product Information for healthcare professionals. It serves as a clinically accurate reference by consolidating data from multiple major jurisdictions.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to their local regulatory authority's current approved documentation.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ripretinib (as a white to off-white crystalline solid; lipophilic weak base).
- **Chemical Class:** Kinase inhibitor (switch-control tyrosine kinase inhibitor).
- **Brand Name:** **QINLOCK**.
- **ATC Code:** L01EX19.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 50 mg oral tablets. Tablets are white to off-white, oval-shaped, and debossed with "DC1" on one side.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Specialised Therapeutics PM Pty Ltd [TGA]; Deciphera Pharmaceuticals, LLC [FDA].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Gastrointestinal Stromal Tumours (GIST):** Treatment of adult patients with advanced GIST who have received prior treatment with three or more kinase inhibitors, including imatinib.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dosage is 150 mg (three 50 mg tablets) administered orally once daily.
- **Administration Instructions:** 
  - Tablets should be swallowed whole. 
  - May be taken with or without food.
  - Dose should be taken at the same time each day.
- **Dose Modifications for Adverse Reactions:**
  - First dose reduction: 100 mg once daily.
  - If 100 mg once daily is not tolerated, treatment should be permanently discontinued.
  - Specific modification protocols (withhold, reduce, or discontinue) are required for Grade 2–4 Palmar-plantar erythrodysaesthesia syndrome (PPES), Grade 3–4 Hypertension, Grade 3–4 Left Ventricular Systolic Dysfunction, and other Grade 3–4 toxicities (see Section 5 for details).
- **Dose Modifications for CYP3A Inducers:**
  - **Strong CYP3A Inducers:** Avoid concomitant use.
  - **Moderate CYP3A Inducers:** If co-administration cannot be avoided, increase the **QINLOCK** dose frequency to 150 mg twice daily. If the inducer is discontinued, resume the 150 mg once-daily dose 14 days later [TGA, FDA].
- **Missed Dose:** 
  - If a dose is missed by less than 8 hours, it should be taken as soon as remembered. 
  - If more than 8 hours have passed, the dose should be skipped, and the regular schedule resumed the next day.
  - For patients on twice-daily dosing (due to moderate CYP3A inducer use), take the missed dose if less than 4 hours have passed since the scheduled time [TGA, FDA].
- **Vomiting:** If vomiting occurs after administration, do not take an additional dose; continue with the next scheduled dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Australia [TGA]:** Hypersensitivity to ripretinib or any of the excipients (including lactose).
- **United States [FDA]:** None listed in the official PI.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Palmar-Plantar Erythrodysaesthesia Syndrome (PPES):** Very common. Based on severity, withhold treatment and resume at the same or reduced dose.
- **New Primary Cutaneous Malignancies:** Cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma (cuSCC), keratoacanthoma, and melanoma have occurred. Perform dermatologic evaluations at initiation and routinely during treatment.
- **Hypertension:** Do not initiate in patients with uncontrolled hypertension. Monitor blood pressure as clinically indicated (e.g., weekly for the first 8 weeks).
- **Cardiac Dysfunction:** Includes cardiac failure and decreased left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF). Assess LVEF by echocardiogram or MUGA scan prior to initiation and during treatment as indicated. Permanently discontinue for Grade 3 or 4 left ventricular systolic dysfunction.
- **Risk of Impaired Wound Healing:** **QINLOCK** has the potential to adversely affect wound healing. Withhold for at least 1 week prior to elective surgery. Do not resume for at least 2 weeks following major surgery and until adequate wound healing is confirmed.
- **Photosensitivity:** Advise patients to limit direct ultraviolet exposure (sunlight, sunlamps) and use protective clothing and high-SPF sunscreen during treatment and for one week after discontinuation.
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Can cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status before initiation.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequencies are based on the **INVICTUS** trial.

### Very Common (>10%)
- **Skin/Subcutaneous:** Alopecia (52%), PPES (21%), dry skin (13%), pruritus (11%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea (39%), abdominal pain (36%), constipation (34%), diarrhoea (28%), vomiting (21%), stomatitis (11%).
- **Musculoskeletal:** Myalgia (32%), arthralgia (18%), muscle spasms (15%).
- **General:** Fatigue (42%), peripheral oedema (17%), asthenia (13%), decreased appetite (27%), decreased weight (19%), headache (19%), dyspnoea (13%).
- **Vascular:** Hypertension (14%).

### Common (1–10%)
- Peripheral sensory neuropathy (7.1%), dermatitis acneiform (5.9%), seborrheic keratosis (4.7%), rash (4.7%).
- **Serious Events:** Cardiac ischemic events (1.1%) including myocardial infarction and acute coronary syndrome.

### Laboratory Abnormalities (Grade 3–4)
- Increased lipase (7%), decreased phosphate (4.9%), increased INR (3.8%), increased triglycerides (2.4%), increased ALT (1.2%), increased amylase (1.2%).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Effect of CYP3A Inhibitors:** Co-administration with strong CYP3A inhibitors (e.g., itraconazole) increases ripretinib exposure (AUC increased by 99%). Monitor more frequently for adverse reactions.
- **Effect of CYP3A Inducers:** 
  - **Strong Inducers (e.g., rifampin):** Decreases ripretinib AUC by 61%. Avoid concomitant use.
  - **Moderate Inducers (e.g., efavirenz):** Decreases ripretinib AUC by 56%. Increase **QINLOCK** frequency to twice daily (see Section 3).
- **Effect of Ripretinib on Other Drugs:**
  - Ripretinib is a weak inhibitor of CYP2C8 (e.g., may slightly increase repaglinide exposure).
  - Ripretinib is an inhibitor of P-gp and BCRP in vitro.
  - Active metabolite DP-5439 is an inhibitor of BCRP and MATE1 in vitro.
- **Acid-reducing Agents:** Co-administration with pantoprazole (PPI) has no clinically significant effect on ripretinib exposure.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **Australia:** Category D (Drugs which have caused, are suspected to have caused or may be expected to cause, an increased incidence of human fetal malformations or irreversible damage).
  - **United States:** No human data; animal studies show embryo-fetal death and malformations at 0.4–0.5 times human exposure.
  - **Contraception:** Females of reproductive potential must use effective contraception during treatment and for 1 week after the final dose. Males with female partners of reproductive potential must also use effective contraception for 1 week after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Discontinue breastfeeding during treatment and for 1 week after the final dose due to potential serious adverse reactions in the infant.
- **Fertility:** Based on animal findings, **QINLOCK** may impair male fertility [TGA, FDA].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients <18 years have not been established.
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include enough patients $\ge$ 65 years to determine if they respond differently; however, no overall differences in safety were observed.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
  - **Mild to Moderate (CrCl 30–89 mL/min):** No dose adjustment recommended.
  - **Severe (CrCl 15–29 mL/min):** Not studied; no dosing recommendation can be made.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is recommended for patients with mild (Child-Pugh A), moderate (Child-Pugh B), or severe (Child-Pugh C) hepatic impairment. Exposure increases in moderate/severe impairment are not considered clinically relevant based on the safety profile [FDA].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Ripretinib inhibits KIT and PDGFRA kinases, including wild-type and various primary and secondary mutations. It specifically locks the kinase in the inactive conformation via a dual mechanism (switch pocket and activation loop binding).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ is approximately 4 hours. Steady state reached by Day 15. A high-fat meal increases ripretinib AUC by 30%.
  - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding $\ge$ 99%. Volume of distribution ($V_{ss}/F$) is ~307 L.
  - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by CYP3A4/5. Minor metabolism via CYP2C8 and CYP2D6.
  - **Elimination:** Mean half-life is 14.8 hours. Excreted mainly in faeces (34% as parent; total radioactivity ~87%).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **INVICTUS Trial:** A Phase 3, randomised (2:1), double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in patients with advanced GIST previously treated with imatinib, sunitinib, and regorafenib ($N=129$).
- **Primary Outcome:** Median Progression-Free Survival (PFS) was 6.3 months for **QINLOCK** versus 1.0 month for placebo (HR 0.15; $p < 0.0001$).
- **Overall Survival (OS):** Median OS was 15.1 months for **QINLOCK** versus 6.6 months for placebo (HR 0.36).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Australia [TGA]:** Store below 25°C. Protect from light.
- **United States [FDA]:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C).
- **General Handling:** Store in the original container. Keep the bottle tightly closed and do not remove the desiccant (silica gel) packet.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Poison Schedule S4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **United States:** Legal Status: Rx-only.
