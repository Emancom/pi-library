# Editorial Product Information: Qsymia (phentermine and topiramate)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a clinical reference format.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource only. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Phentermine hydrochloride (a sympathomimetic amine anorectic) and Topiramate (a sulfamate-substituted monosaccharide) [US].
- **Brand Name:** **Qsymia** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strengths:** Extended-release capsules [US]:
    - 3.75 mg phentermine / 23 mg topiramate
    - 7.5 mg phentermine / 46 mg topiramate
    - 11.25 mg phentermine / 69 mg topiramate
    - 15 mg phentermine / 92 mg topiramate
- **Route of Administration:** Oral [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Vivus LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Qsymia** is indicated as an adjunct to a reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity for chronic weight management in:
- **Adults** with an initial body mass index (BMI) of:
    - $\ge 30$ kg/m² (obesity) [US].
    - $\ge 27$ kg/m² (overweight) in the presence of at least one weight-related comorbidity (e.g., hypertension, type 2 diabetes mellitus, or dyslipidemia) [US].
- **Pediatric patients** aged 12 years and older with:
    - Obesity, defined as a BMI in the $\ge 95$th percentile standardised by age and sex [US].

**Limitations of Use:** Safety and efficacy in combination with other weight-loss products (prescription, OTC, or herbal) have not been established. Effects on cardiovascular morbidity and mortality are not established [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric)
- **Administration:** Take once daily in the morning with or without food. Avoid evening administration to prevent insomnia [US].
- **Initial Dosage:** 3.75 mg/23 mg daily for 14 days [US].
- **Recommended Dosage:** After the initial 14 days, increase to 7.5 mg/46 mg once daily [US].

### Titration and Escalation
- **Assessment at 12 Weeks (7.5 mg/46 mg dose):**
    - **Adults:** If <3% weight loss from baseline, either discontinue or escalate dose [US].
    - **Pediatrics:** If <3% reduction in BMI, either discontinue or escalate dose [US].
- **Escalation Schedule:** Increase to 11.25 mg/69 mg daily for 14 days, followed by 15 mg/92 mg once daily [US].
- **Final Assessment at 12 Weeks (15 mg/92 mg dose):**
    - **Adults:** If <5% weight loss from baseline, discontinue treatment [US].
    - **Pediatrics:** If <5% reduction in BMI, discontinue treatment [US].

### Discontinuation Instructions
- To prevent potential seizures, the 15 mg/92 mg dose must be discontinued gradually by taking one capsule every other day for at least one week before stopping completely [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy:** Highly teratogenic [US].
- **Glaucoma:** Due to the risk of acute angle-closure [US].
- **Hyperthyroidism** [US].
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** During or within 14 days of administration (risk of hypertensive crisis) [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known idiosyncrasy to sympathomimetic amines or hypersensitivity to any component of the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Topiramate increases the risk of major congenital malformations (cleft lip/palate) and being small for gestational age (SGA). **REMS Requirement:** Qsymia is available only through a restricted program (Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy) [US].
- **Suicidal Behavior and Ideation:** Patients should be monitored for the emergence or worsening of depression or suicidal thoughts. Discontinue if symptoms develop [US].
- **Ophthalmologic Reactions:** Acute myopia and secondary angle-closure glaucoma have been reported. Symptoms include acute onset of decreased visual acuity and/or ocular pain. Discontinue immediately if these occur [US].
- **Cognitive Impairment:** May cause disturbances in attention, memory, and language. Caution is advised when operating heavy machinery or driving [US].
- **Metabolic Acidosis:** Hyperchloremic, non-anion gap metabolic acidosis can occur. Measure electrolytes (bicarbonate) at baseline and periodically [US].
- **Renal Function:** Increases in serum creatinine have been observed. Monitor creatinine at baseline and during therapy [US].
- **Potential for Seizures:** Abrupt withdrawal of the highest dose (15 mg/92 mg) may precipitate seizures [US].
- **Kidney Stones:** Topiramate inhibits carbonic anhydrase; increase fluid intake to mitigate risk [US].
- **Oligohidrosis and Hyperthermia:** Primarily reported in pediatric patients; monitor for decreased sweating and increased body temperature [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Adult Population (Incidence $\ge 5$%)
| Adverse Reaction | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- |
| Paraesthesia | Very Common (>10%) |
| Dry Mouth | Very Common (>10%) |
| Constipation | Very Common (>10%) |
| Dizziness | Common (1–10%) |
| Dysgeusia | Common (1–10%) |
| Insomnia | Common (1–10%) |

### Pediatric Population (Incidence $\ge 4$%)
- Common reactions include depression, dizziness, arthralgia, pyrexia, influenza, and ligament sprain [US].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Cardiovascular:** Dose-related increase in resting heart rate (average 0.6–1.7 bpm in adults; higher in some cases) [US].
- **Dermatologic:** Serious skin reactions (Stevens-Johnson Syndrome, Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis), DRESS, and anaphylaxis have occurred with topiramate [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **MAOIs:** Contraindicated; risk of hypertensive crisis [US].
- **Oral Contraceptives:** Co-administration may result in irregular bleeding (spotting) due to altered estrogen/progestin exposure, though contraceptive efficacy is generally maintained. Patients should not discontinue their contraceptive if spotting occurs [US].
- **CNS Depressants and Alcohol:** May potentiate CNS depression (drowsiness, dizziness) [US].
- **Non-potassium Sparing Diuretics:** May worsen hypokalemia. Monitor serum potassium levels [US].
- **Antiepileptic Drugs (AEDs):** Phenytoin and Carbamazepine may decrease topiramate exposure. Valproic acid increases the risk of hyperammonemia and hypothermia [US].
- **Carbonic Anhydrase Inhibitors:** (e.g., zonisamide, acetazolamide) Increase the risk of metabolic acidosis and kidney stones. Avoid concomitant use [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Contraindicated.** Qsymia can cause fetal harm. 
    - **Testing:** Obtain a negative pregnancy test before initiation and **monthly** thereafter [US].
    - **Contraception:** Effective contraception is mandatory during therapy [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. Both components are present in human milk; risk of serious adverse reactions in the infant (e.g., somnolence, diarrhea, hypertension, or seizures) [US].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in rat studies at doses significantly higher than the human clinical dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for ages 12–17 for obesity. Linear growth and bone mineral density acquisition may be attenuated [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials included limited patients over 65. Start at the low end of the dosing range due to higher frequency of renal or hepatic impairment [US].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Mild (CrCl 50–79 mL/min):** No adjustment [US].
    - **Moderate (CrCl 30–49 mL/min) or Severe (CrCl <30 mL/min):** Maximum dose is 7.5 mg/46 mg once daily [US].
    - **End-stage renal disease (ESRD):** Avoid use [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild (Child-Pugh 5–6):** No adjustment [US].
    - **Moderate (Child-Pugh 7–9):** Maximum dose is 7.5 mg/46 mg once daily [US].
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh 10–15):** Avoid use [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Phentermine:** Sympathomimetic amine that triggers catecholamine release in the hypothalamus, resulting in appetite suppression [US].
    - **Topiramate:** Likely mediates weight loss through appetite suppression and enhanced satiety via GABA modulation, voltage-gated ion channel blocking, and AMPA/kainite receptor inhibition [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak plasma concentrations occur at 6 hours (phentermine) and 9 hours (topiramate). High-fat meals do not significantly affect exposure [US].
    - **Distribution:** Phentermine is 17.5% protein bound; Topiramate is 15–41% protein bound [US].
    - **Metabolism/Elimination:** Phentermine is primarily excreted unchanged in urine (70–80%). Topiramate is not extensively metabolised and is primarily excreted in urine (70%) with a half-life of ~65 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adults (Study 1 & 2):** Two 1-year trials showed significant weight loss compared to placebo. At the 15 mg/92 mg dose, mean weight loss was 10.9% (Study 1) and 9.8% (Study 2), with ~47-48% of patients achieving $\ge 10$% weight loss [US].
- **Pediatrics (Study 3):** A 56-week study in patients aged 12–17 years demonstrated a mean BMI reduction of 7.1% at the 15 mg/92 mg dose compared to a 3.3% increase in the placebo group [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F and 86°F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Keep container tightly closed and protected from moisture [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** **Schedule IV** (CIV) controlled substance due to the potential for abuse of phentermine [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Rx-only [US].
