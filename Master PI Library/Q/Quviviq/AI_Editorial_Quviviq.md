# Editorial Product Information: Daridorexant

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, intended to consolidate clinical and regulatory data into a concise, professional reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI when prescribing or dispensing in a particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Daridorexant (present as daridorexant hydrochloride).
- **Chemical Class:** Orexin receptor antagonist (Dual Orexin Receptor Antagonist - DORA).
- **Brand Name:** **QUVIVIQ** [US].
- **ATC Code:** N05CF05 (Note: Not explicitly listed in the source, but standard for this class; however, following editorial principles, not specified in available sources).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
  - **Oral Tablets (Film-coated):** 25 mg (light purple, arc-triangle) and 50 mg (light orange, arc-triangle) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Idorsia Pharmaceuticals Ltd [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Insomnia:** Indicated for the treatment of adult patients with insomnia, characterized by difficulties with sleep onset and/or sleep maintenance [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The recommended dose range is 25 mg to 50 mg taken once per night [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
  - Administer orally within 30 minutes before going to bed.
  - Ensure at least 7 hours remain prior to the planned time of awakening [US].
  - **Food Effect:** Time to sleep onset may be delayed if the dose is taken with or shortly after a high-fat, high-calorie meal [US].
- **Concomitant Use Adjustments:**
  - **Moderate CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Maximum recommended dose is 25 mg once nightly [US].
  - **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors/Inducers:** Avoid concomitant use [US].
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** Not specified in available sources (standard clinical practice suggests skipping the dose if 7 hours of sleep time are no longer available).

## 4. Contraindications
- **Narcolepsy:** Contraindicated in all patients with narcolepsy [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to daridorexant or any of its excipients (e.g., angioedema has been reported) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **CNS-Depressant Effects:** Can cause significant daytime impairment in alertness and motor coordination, which may persist for several days after discontinuation. Risk increases with higher-than-recommended doses, co-administration with other CNS depressants, or insufficient sleep duration (<7 hours) [US].
- **Complex Sleep Behaviors:** Reports include sleepwalking, sleep-driving, and other activities while not fully awake. Discontinue treatment immediately if these occur [US].
- **Worsening of Depression/Suicidality:** Use with caution in patients with depression. Worsening of suicidal ideation and actions have been reported with hypnotics [US].
- **Sleep Paralysis/Hallucinations:** May cause sleep paralysis (inability to move for minutes during sleep-wake transitions) and hypnagogic/hypnopompic hallucinations [US].
- **Cataplexy-like Symptoms:** Symptoms similar to mild cataplexy (e.g., leg weakness) may occur at any time of day [US].
- **Respiratory Function:** Caution is advised in patients with compromised respiratory function (e.g., OSA, COPD), although studies in mild-to-moderate cases did not show significant respiratory depression [US].
- **Co-morbidities:** Evaluate for physical or psychiatric co-morbidities if insomnia persists after 7–10 days of treatment [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects

| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Headache** | 6% | 7% | 5% |
| **Somnolence/Fatigue** | 6% | 5% | 4% |
| **Dizziness** | 2% | 3% | 2% |
| **Nausea** | 0% | 3% | 2% |

- **Uncommon (<2%) but notable:** Sleep paralysis (0.3%–0.5%) and hypnagogic/hypnopompic hallucinations (0.6% at 25 mg) [US].
- **Post-Marketing Experience:** Nightmares, abnormal dreams, and hypersensitivity reactions (rash, urticaria, angioedema) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** 
  - **Strong (e.g., itraconazole):** Avoid use; increases daridorexant AUC by >400% [US].
  - **Moderate (e.g., diltiazem):** Limit dose to 25 mg [US].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** 
  - **Strong or Moderate (e.g., rifampin, efavirenz):** Avoid use; significantly reduces efficacy [US].
- **CNS Depressants & Alcohol:** Additive psychomotor impairment; avoid alcohol and use other CNS depressants with caution [US].
- **CYP3A4 Substrates (Narrow Therapeutic Index):** Daridorexant is a weak CYP3A4 inhibitor; use caution with substrates like midazolam [US].
- **P-gp Substrates:** Daridorexant increases exposure to P-gp substrates (e.g., dabigatran); monitor substrates with a narrow therapeutic index [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **Category:** Not assigned a letter category under current US labeling (PLLR); however, there are no available human data to evaluate risk. Animal studies showed no malformations at doses up to 8–10x the MRHD [US].
  - **Registry:** A pregnancy exposure registry is available to monitor outcomes [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Daridorexant is present in human milk at low levels (Relative Infant Dose <1%). Monitor breastfed infants for excessive sedation [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed increased pre-implantation loss in females at high doses (~9x MRHD) but no effect on male fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required. However, there is a higher risk of somnolence, fatigue, and falls in patients $\ge$ 75 years [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild to severe impairment (not on dialysis) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
  - **Moderate (Child-Pugh 7–9):** Maximum dose 25 mg once nightly [US].
  - **Severe (Child-Pugh $\ge$ 10):** Not recommended [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Antagonizes orexin receptors (OX1R and OX2R), thereby blocking the binding of wake-promoting neuropeptides (Orexin A and B) to suppress wake drive [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Binds to OX1R (Ki 0.47 nM) and OX2R (Ki 0.93 nM). No clinically relevant QTc prolongation [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ 1–2 hours; Absolute bioavailability 62% [US].
  - **Distribution:** $V_d$ 31 L; 99.7% protein bound [US].
  - **Metabolism:** Primarily CYP3A4 (89%) [US].
  - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life ~8 hours. Excreted via feces (57%) and urine (28%) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** Two 3-month multicenter, randomized, double-blind trials (Study 1 and 2) in 1,854 patients with DSM-5 insomnia.
- **Outcomes:** 
  - **50 mg and 25 mg doses:** Statistically significant improvements in Latency to Persistent Sleep (LPS) and Wake After Sleep Onset (WASO) at Month 1 and Month 3 [US].
  - **Secondary Endpoint:** Total Sleep Time (sTST) was significantly improved for 25 mg and 50 mg doses [US].
  - **10 mg dose:** Failed to show significant improvement [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F and 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Dispense in original container with a Medication Guide [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** 
  - **US:** Prescription-only (Rx-only); Schedule IV (CIV) controlled substance due to potential for abuse and dependence [US].
- **Abuse Potential:** In human abuse potential studies, 50 mg showed lower "drug liking" than zolpidem or suvorexant, but higher doses (100–150 mg) showed similar liking scores [US].
