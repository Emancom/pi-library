# Editorial Product Information: Qtern (Saxagliptin and Dapagliflozin)

## Editorial Note
- Regulatory agencies contributing to this editorial: TGA (Australia).
- **Important Note:** Two documents were provided for synthesis; however, Document 1 (TGA) pertains to **Qtern** (Saxagliptin/Dapagliflozin), while Document 2 (FDA) pertains to **Levocarnitine Injection**. To maintain clinical accuracy and patient safety, this editorial is based exclusively on the clinical data for the fixed-dose combination of **saxagliptin** and **dapagliflozin**. Information regarding FDA-specific Qtern labeling was not specified in the provided sources.
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Saxagliptin (as hydrochloride) and Dapagliflozin (as propanediol monohydrate).
- **Chemical Class:** Dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP-4) inhibitor and Sodium-glucose co-transporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** **Qtern** [TGA].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Film-coated tablet containing 5 mg saxagliptin and 10 mg dapagliflozin [TGA].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AstraZeneca Pty Ltd [TGA].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus:** Indicated as an adjunct to diet and exercise, in combination with metformin, to improve glycaemic control in adults when treatment with both **saxagliptin** and **dapagliflozin** is appropriate [TGA-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** One tablet (5 mg/10 mg) taken once daily at any time of the day.
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets must be swallowed whole. They may be taken with or without food.
- **Missed Dose:** Not explicitly detailed in provided sources; standard clinical practice applies.
- **Renal Considerations:** Renal function must be assessed prior to initiation. No dose adjustment is required if eGFR is ≥45 mL/min/1.73 m².
- **Maintenance:** Regular assessment of compliance and benefit/risk is recommended.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** History of any serious hypersensitivity reaction (e.g., anaphylaxis, angioedema) to the active substances or any excipients (including lactose), or to any other DPP-4 inhibitor or SGLT2 inhibitor.
- **Renal Impairment:** Patients with an eGFR persistently <45 mL/min/1.73 m², end-stage renal disease (ESRD), or those on dialysis [TGA].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Renal Function:** SGLT2 inhibitors increase serum creatinine and decrease eGFR. Monitoring is recommended:
    - Prior to initiation and at least yearly thereafter.
    - More frequently (2–4 times per year) if eGFR is between 45 and 60 mL/min/1.73 m².
    - Treatment must be discontinued if eGFR falls persistently below 45 mL/min/1.73 m².
- **Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA):** Rare but life-threatening cases reported, including euglycaemic DKA (blood glucose <14 mmol/L). Treatment should be suspended immediately if DKA is suspected.
- **Fournier’s Gangrene:** Necrotising fasciitis of the perineum has been reported. Monitor for pain, tenderness, erythema, or swelling in the genital area with fever/malaise.
- **Heart Failure:** A small increase in hospitalisation for heart failure was observed with **saxagliptin** use in the SAVOR trial. Use caution in patients with known cardiovascular risk factors.
- **Pancreatitis:** Acute pancreatitis has been reported with **saxagliptin**. Patients should be informed of characteristic symptoms (persistent, severe abdominal pain).
- **Volume Depletion/Hypotension:** Osmotic diuresis may lead to symptomatic hypotension. Correct volume status prior to initiation, especially in the elderly or those on antihypertensives/diuretics.
- **Urinary Tract Infections (UTI):** Increased risk of serious UTIs, including urosepsis and pyelonephritis.
- **Bullous Pemphigoid:** Reported with DPP-4 inhibitors. Discontinue if blisters or erosions develop.
- **Surgery:** Cease treatment prior to major surgery or procedures associated with prolonged fasting to reduce DKA risk.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects observed in pooled clinical trials (up to 52 weeks) for the triple therapy (Saxagliptin + Dapagliflozin + Metformin):

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Upper respiratory tract infection (13.6%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Urinary tract infection (5.7%), Dyslipidaemia (5.1%), Headache (4.3%), Diarrhoea (3.7%), Back pain (3.3%), Genital infection (3.0%), Arthralgia (2.4%) |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Nausea (1.6%), Cough (1.6%), Dyspepsia (0.8%), Muscle spasms (0.6%) |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Acute Kidney Injury, Fournier's Gangrene, Acute Pancreatitis, and Heart Failure hospitalisation (saxagliptin component).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic (PK) Interaction:** No clinically meaningful PK interaction exists between **saxagliptin** and **dapagliflozin**.
- **Lithium:** Dapagliflozin may increase the renal clearance of lithium. Serum lithium levels should be monitored closely during initiation or dose changes of **Qtern**.
- **Saxagliptin Interactions:** Primarily metabolised by CYP3A4/5. While CYP3A4/5 inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole) or inducers (e.g., rifampicin) alter saxagliptin levels, the total active exposure (parent + active metabolite) is not meaningfully changed.
- **Dapagliflozin Interactions:** Metabolism is mediated by UGT1A9. Rifampicin may decrease exposure by 22%, and mefenamic acid may increase it by 51%; neither requires dose adjustment.
- **Hypoglycaemia Risk:** Risk increases when combined with insulin or insulin secretagogues (e.g., sulfonylureas). A lower dose of the secretagogue/insulin may be required.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australia Category D.** 
    - **Qtern** should not be used during pregnancy. If pregnancy is detected, treatment must be discontinued. 
    - **Dapagliflozin** has shown adverse effects on renal development in animal studies (corresponding to the 2nd and 3rd trimesters).
- **Breastfeeding:** Use is not recommended. It is unknown if components are excreted in human milk. Animal data shows **dapagliflozin** excretion in milk and potential risks to juvenile renal maturation.
- **Fertility:** No human studies available. Animal studies with individual components at high doses showed some effects on oestrous cycling and sperm (at >700x human exposure).

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients <18 years of age have not been established [TGA].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment based on age. Increased risk of volume depletion and renal impairment requires clinical caution.
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with eGFR <45 mL/min/1.73 m². Use is not recommended in ESRD or patients on dialysis.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** May be used in mild or moderate impairment. Not recommended for use in patients with severe hepatic impairment [TGA].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Saxagliptin:** Selective DPP-4 inhibitor that slows the inactivation of incretin hormones (GLP-1 and GIP), increasing insulin secretion and decreasing glucagon levels in a glucose-dependent manner.
    - **Dapagliflozin:** SGLT2 inhibitor that reduces the reabsorption of filtered glucose in the proximal renal tubule, leading to urinary glucose excretion (glucuresis).
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The combination improves both fasting and post-prandial glucose levels. Dapagliflozin-induced glucuresis is associated with caloric loss and modest reductions in weight and blood pressure.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Bioavailability is ~50% (saxagliptin) and 78% (dapagliflozin). Food has no clinically meaningful effect on AUC.
    - **Distribution:** Dapagliflozin is ~91% protein bound; saxagliptin protein binding is negligible.
    - **Elimination:** Saxagliptin is eliminated via renal and hepatic pathways (half-life ~2.5h). Dapagliflozin is primarily eliminated via the urine (half-life ~12.9h).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Triple Therapy:** In a 24-week study of patients inadequate on metformin, the addition of **saxagliptin** and **dapagliflozin** resulted in a significantly greater reduction in HbA1c (-1.47%) compared to the addition of either component alone (-0.88% to -1.20%).
- **SAVOR Trial:** Demonstrated cardiovascular safety for **saxagliptin** but noted an increased risk of heart failure hospitalisation (3.5% vs 2.8% for placebo).
- **DECLARE Study:** Demonstrated that **dapagliflozin** reduced the risk of hospitalisation for heart failure in a broad population of T2DM patients.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C.
- **Handling:** Keep in the original container to protect from light and moisture.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [TGA].
