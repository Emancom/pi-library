# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis of the provided regulatory source and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/amino acid derivative ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium).
- **Brand Names:** Generic (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - Administration: Slow intravenous bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or infusion.
    - Acute Crisis: A loading dose is often given, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - Frequency: Administer every 3 or 4 hours; frequency should never be less than every 6 hours.
    - Maximum Dose: Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - Administration: Slow intravenous bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - Dose Adjustments: Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### Monitoring
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations prior to therapy.
- Conduct weekly and monthly monitoring, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine level: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue the drug immediately and initiate appropriate medical treatment [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with pre-existing conditions, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral formulation precaution):** While this document concerns the injection, clinicians should note that chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events are often difficult to distinguish from symptoms of the underlying pathology.

| System Organ Class | Common (1–10%) to Very Common (>10%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain, abdominal pain, asthenia, accidental injury. |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, arrhythmia. |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, dyspepsia, anorexia. |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia, hyperkalemia, peripheral edema, weight changes. |
| **Nervous System** | Dizziness, paresthesia, insomnia, depression. |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm, and seizures [US].
- **Other Noted Effects:** Gastritis and transient body odor [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. 
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not formally assigned in provided text, but consistent with former Category B]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations after exogenous administration. Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of maternal supplementation. Consideration may be given to discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg (see Section 3). 
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety and efficacy of the *oral* form have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. The *injection* is specifically indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD patients on hemodialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Corrects carnitine deficiency, which is biochemically defined as plasma concentrations <20 µmol/L at one week post-term [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Mean distribution half-life: 0.585 hours.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean apparent terminal elimination half-life: 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis (trough) levels from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks.
- **Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the effect on clinical outcomes in this specific population has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability (Mixed):** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion; the formulation contains no preservative.

- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
