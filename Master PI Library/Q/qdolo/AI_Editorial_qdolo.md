# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
*   **Purpose:** This document is a harmonized editorial synthesis of the Product Information for Levocarnitine Injection, intended for use by healthcare professionals. 
*   **Disclaimer:** This editorial is a clinical summary for reference purposes and does not replace the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing decisions in any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP). It is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane.
*   **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [United States]
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    *   **Intravenous Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [United States].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [United States].
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing hemodialysis [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
*   **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    *   **Administration:** Delivered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
    *   **Loading Dose:** In cases of severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose may be given, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and no less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours.
    *   **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported administered dose is 300 mg/kg [United States].
*   **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** 
    *   **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    *   **Administration:** Delivered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    *   **Adjustment:** Dosage adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations [United States].

### Administration Instructions
*   Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration before use.
*   **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [United States].

### Monitoring
*   A baseline plasma carnitine concentration is recommended.
*   Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine level: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
*   None known [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
*   None [United States].

### General Warnings and Precautions
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These often occur within minutes of administration, particularly in ESRD patients. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate the risks before considering re-administration [United States].
*   **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In patients with known seizure history, an increase in frequency or severity has been reported [United States].

### Monitoring Requirements
*   **INR Monitoring:** Reports indicate that levocarnitine may increase the International Normalized Ratio (INR) in patients concurrently using warfarin. Prothrombin time/INR should be monitored after initiation or dose adjustment [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common and Serious Adverse Effects
*   **General/Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting are common. Less frequent effects include body odor, gastritis, and abdominal pain.
*   **ESRD Clinical Trial Data (Frequency ≥5%):**
    *   **Body as a Whole:** Chest pain (12%), Flu syndrome (25%), Headache (22%), Infection (15%), Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%).
    *   **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%).
    *   **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%), Nausea (8%).
    *   **Metabolic/Nutritional:** Hypercalcemia (9%), Hypervolemia (5%).
    *   **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), Cough increase (9%), Dyspnea (7%), Rhinitis (9%).
    *   **Nervous System:** Dizziness (13%), Paresthesia (5%).

### Post-marketing Experience
*   Seizures and serious hypersensitivity reactions (anaphylaxis) have been identified during post-approval use [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Co-administration may result in an increased INR. Close monitoring of coagulation parameters is required [United States].
*   **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
*   **US Category:** Not assigned a letter category in provided text, but states use only if "clearly needed."
*   **Data:** Animal reproduction studies (rats and rabbits) using 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate, well-controlled studies exist for pregnant women [United States].

### Breastfeeding
*   **Safety Information:** Not specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration.
*   **Recommendation:** Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant should be weighed against the clinical benefits to the mother. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [United States].

### Fertility
*   No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Refer to Dosage and Administration for weight-based dosing (50 mg/kg) [United States].
*   **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]), which are normally excreted in urine [United States].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
*   Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated.

### Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
*   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
*   **Excretion:** Following IV administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours.
*   **Half-life:** Distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ESRD Study:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly showed mean pre-dialysis trough levels increased from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
*   **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg in ESRD patients demonstrated dose-proportional increases in carnitine concentrations, though the clinical impact on symptoms in this specific population was not determined during the trials [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
*   **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
*   **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [United States].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **United States:** Rx-only.
