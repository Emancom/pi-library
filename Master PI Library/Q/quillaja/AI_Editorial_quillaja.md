# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** US (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection, synthesised for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and clinical resource. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information/Prescribing Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

*Editorial Observation: While the provided source index referred to "quillaja," the official documentation provided pertains exclusively to **levocarnitine**. This editorial reflects the levocarnitine data.*

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule for fatty acid transport).
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
- **Method:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
- **Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; intervals should never be less than every 6 hours.
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose is 300 mg/kg.
- **Monitoring:** Obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is recommended.

### 3.2 ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Method:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations (normal range: 40 to 50 µmol/L). Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. Some reactions occur within minutes of IV administration, primarily in ESRD patients. Treatment must be discontinued immediately upon onset of a severe reaction.
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with established seizure disorders, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been reported.
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** Note that the safety/efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral formulations in ESRD patients may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]).

### 6.2 Other Adverse Effects
- **Common:** Transient nausea and vomiting; body odor; gastritis.
- **Neurologic:** Seizures.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. 
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustment in patients receiving warfarin anticoagulation.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US]: Not formally categorised by letter in provided source. Reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Clinical data in pregnant women are lacking; use only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been specifically studied in nursing mothers. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother.
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Refer to the weight-based dosing in Section 3.
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources. 
- **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is specifically indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD. However, chronic high-dose *oral* levocarnitine is cautioned against due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours.
    - **Bioavailability:** Oral bioavailability is low (~15%).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** Efficacy was demonstrated in 9-week and dose-ranging studies (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) involving patients on maintenance hemodialysis for ≥6 months. Treatment resulted in significant increases in plasma levocarnitine concentrations (trough levels increased from ~20 µmol/L at baseline to 190–243 µmol/L after 8–24 weeks of 20 mg/kg dosing).
- **Note:** While levocarnitine increases plasma concentrations in ESRD, the impact on specific clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, cardiomyopathy) in this population has not been definitively determined.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservative.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL).

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
