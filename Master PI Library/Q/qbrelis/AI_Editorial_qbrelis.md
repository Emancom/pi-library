# Editorial Product Information: Lisinopril Oral Solution

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **lisinopril** oral solution, synthesised for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. The information provided is based solely on the documentation available at the time of writing (US FDA PI for **Qbrelis**).

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Lisinopril (as lisinopril dihydrate).
- **Chemical Class:** Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitor.
- **Brand Names:** **Qbrelis** [US].
- **ATC Code:** C09AA03.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Oral solution, 1 mg/mL [US].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Azurity Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypertension:** For the treatment of hypertension in adults and pediatric patients aged 6 years and older to reduce blood pressure and lower the risk of fatal and nonfatal cardiovascular events (primarily strokes and myocardial infarctions) [US].
- **Heart Failure:** As adjunct therapy for the reduction of signs and symptoms of systolic heart failure [US].
- **Acute Myocardial Infarction:** For the reduction of mortality in hemodynamically stable patients within 24 hours of acute myocardial infarction (MI). Patients should receive standard care including thrombolytics, aspirin, and beta-blockers as appropriate [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Hypertension:** Initial dose is 10 mg once daily. The usual maintenance range is 20 to 40 mg per day in a single dose. Doses up to 80 mg have been used but do not provide additional efficacy [US]. 
- **Concurrent Diuretic Use:** If a diuretic cannot be discontinued, initiate lisinopril at 5 mg once daily [US].
- **Heart Failure:** Initiate at 5 mg once daily as adjunct to diuretics and digitalis. The target dose is 40 mg once daily as tolerated. In patients with hyponatremia (serum sodium < 130 mEq/L), initiate at 2.5 mg once daily [US].
- **Acute Myocardial Infarction:** 5 mg orally within 24 hours of symptoms, followed by 5 mg after 24 hours, then 10 mg once daily. Continue for at least six weeks. If systolic blood pressure is ≤ 120 mmHg and > 100 mmHg during the first 3 days post-infarct, initiate at 2.5 mg [US].

### Pediatric Dosing (Hypertension)
- **Pediatric patients ≥ 6 years (GFR > 30 mL/min/1.73m²):** Initial dose is 0.07 mg/kg (up to 5 mg total) once daily. Titrate based on blood pressure response up to a maximum of 0.61 mg/kg (or 40 mg) once daily [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Qbrelis** is a ready-to-use solution. It can be administered without regard to food [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** History of hypersensitivity to lisinopril or any component of the formulation [US].
- **Angioedema:** History of angioedema related to previous ACE inhibitor treatment, or hereditary/idiopathic angioedema [US].
- **Neprilysin Inhibitors:** Concomitant use with or within 36 hours of switching to/from a neprilysin inhibitor (e.g., sacubitril/valsartan) [US].
- **Diabetes Mellitus:** Concomitant use with aliskiren in patients with diabetes [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning: Fetal Toxicity [US]
- **Lisinopril** can cause injury and death to the developing fetus when used during the second and third trimesters of pregnancy.
- Discontinue **Qbrelis** immediately upon detection of pregnancy.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Angioedema:** Can occur at any time during treatment. If the tongue, glottis, or larynx are involved, airway obstruction may occur; discontinue immediately and provide emergency management. Black patients have a higher reported rate of angioedema compared to non-Black patients [US].
- **Hypotension:** Most common in volume-depleted patients, those on high-dose diuretics, or those with heart failure. Monitor closely for two weeks after initiation or dose increases [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** May cause acute renal failure, particularly in patients whose renal function depends on the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (e.g., renal artery stenosis, severe heart failure). Monitor renal function periodically [US].
- **Hyperkalemia:** Risk factors include renal insufficiency, diabetes, and use of potassium-sparing diuretics or potassium supplements. Monitor serum potassium regularly [US].
- **Hepatic Failure:** Rarely associated with a syndrome starting with cholestatic jaundice progressing to fulminant hepatic necrosis. Discontinue if jaundice or significant elevation in hepatic enzymes occurs [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (Incidence > 2% vs placebo)
| Indication | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Hypertension** | Headache (3.8%), Dizziness (3.5%), Cough (2.5%) |
| **Heart Failure** | Hypotension (3.8%), Chest pain (2.1%) |
| **Acute MI** | Hypotension (5.3%), Renal dysfunction (1.3%) |

### Serious and Other Adverse Effects
- **General:** Fatigue, asthenia, orthostatic effects [US].
- **Digestive:** Pancreatitis, constipation, diarrhea, dry mouth [US].
- **Hematologic:** Rare bone marrow depression, hemolytic anemia, thrombocytopenia [US].
- **Dermatologic:** Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, psoriasis, alopecia [US].
- **Laboratory Findings:** Increases in BUN/Creatinine (especially in HF patients), hyperkalemia, and small decreases in hemoglobin/hematocrit [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Diuretics:** Risk of excessive hypotension; adjust dose or increase salt intake prior to initiation [US].
- **Antidiabetics:** May increase blood-glucose-lowering effects and risk of hypoglycemia [US].
- **NSAIDs (including COX-2 inhibitors):** May result in deterioration of renal function (including acute renal failure) and attenuation of lisinopril’s antihypertensive effect [US].
- **Dual RAS Blockade:** Increased risk of hypotension, hyperkalemia, and renal impairment when combining ACE inhibitors with ARBs or aliskiren [US].
- **Lithium:** Increased serum lithium levels and risk of toxicity [US].
- **Gold:** Nitritoid reactions (flushing, nausea, hypotension) reported with injectable gold [US].
- **mTOR Inhibitors (e.g., sirolimus) & Neprilysin Inhibitors:** Increased risk of angioedema [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified, but includes Boxed Warning]. Use of RAS-acting drugs in the 2nd/3rd trimester is associated with oligohydramnios, fetal lung hypoplasia, skeletal deformations, neonatal skull hypoplasia, anuria, hypotension, renal failure, and death [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Lisinopril is present in rat milk; human data are unavailable. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants, advise women not to breastfeed during treatment [US].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on reproductive performance were observed in rat studies at doses up to 300 mg/kg/day [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Indicated only for hypertension in children ≥ 6 years. Safety and efficacy not established in children < 6 years or those with GFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m² [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No general dosage adjustment required; however, caution is advised as some older individuals may exhibit greater sensitivity. In the GISSI-3 trial, renal dysfunction was more frequent in patients ≥ 75 years [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
  - CrCl > 30 mL/min: No initial adjustment [US].
  - CrCl 10 to 30 mL/min: Reduce initial dose to half the usual dose (e.g., 5 mg for HTN, 2.5 mg for HF/MI) [US].
  - CrCl < 10 mL/min or Hemodialysis: Initial dose 2.5 mg once daily [US].
- **Race:** Monotherapy is less effective in Black patients; however, this difference is eliminated when combined with a thiazide diuretic [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Competitive inhibition of ACE, preventing the conversion of angiotensin I to angiotensin II. This reduces vasopressor activity and decreases aldosterone secretion [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Blood pressure reduction occurs within 1 hour, peaking at 6 hours. Effects are maintained for 24 hours. Does not cause compensatory tachycardia [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Mean extent of absorption is ~25%. Peak serum concentrations occur at ~7 hours. Bioavailability is reduced to 16% in heart failure patients [US].
  - **Distribution:** Does not bind to other serum proteins; binds primarily to ACE [US].
  - **Metabolism:** None [US].
  - **Elimination:** Excreted unchanged in the urine. Effective half-life is 12 hours. Removed by hemodialysis [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ATLAS Trial (Heart Failure):** Compared low (2.5–5 mg) vs. high (32.5–35 mg) doses of lisinopril. The high-dose group showed a 12% lower risk of the combined endpoint of mortality or hospitalization for any cause [US].
- **GISSI-3 (Acute MI):** Evaluated 19,394 patients. Lisinopril (started within 24 hours for 6 weeks) provided an 11% reduction in mortality risk compared to control at six weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at controlled room temperature, 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat. Keep the container tightly closed [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription-only (Rx Only).
