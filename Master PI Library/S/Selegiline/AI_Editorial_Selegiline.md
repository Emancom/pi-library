# Editorial Product Information: Selegiline

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (TGA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of selegiline hydrochloride, synthesised for use by healthcare professionals. It integrates clinical data, regulatory requirements, and safety information.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved PI for definitive legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Selegiline hydrochloride (also known as L-deprenyl).
- **Chemical Class:** Irreversible inhibitor of monoamine oxidase (MAO); a laevorotatory acetylenic derivative of phenethylamine.
- **Brand Names:** Eldepryl [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 5 mg scored tablets [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Orion Pharma (Aus) Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Parkinson's Disease:** Indicated for the treatment of Parkinson's disease. It may be employed as monotherapy during the early stages of the condition or as adjunctive therapy alongside levodopa (with or without a peripheral decarboxylase inhibitor) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** The standard initial dose is 10 mg daily.
- **Administration Schedule:** 5 mg taken twice daily, specifically at breakfast and lunch [Australian]. 
- **Dose Limitation:** Clinical evidence suggests no additional therapeutic benefit is gained from doses exceeding 10 mg per day [Australian].
- **Adjunctive Therapy Titration:** When initiating selegiline as an adjunct to levodopa, an attempt to reduce the levodopa dose by 10–30% should be made after two to three days, particularly if levodopa-related adverse reactions occur [Australian].
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to selegiline or any excipients.
- **Pethidine:** Concomitant use with pethidine is strictly contraindicated due to risks of serious or fatal reactions [Australian].
- **Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors (SSRIs):** Use in combination with SSRIs (e.g., fluoxetine, sertraline, paroxetine) is contraindicated [Australian].
- **Other Antidepressants:** Contraindicated with Serotonin Noradrenaline Reuptake Inhibitors (SNRIs; e.g., venlafaxine), Tricyclic Antidepressants (TCAs), and other Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs) [Australian].
- **Sympathomimetics:** Avoid concurrent use [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal Disease:** Contraindicated in patients with active duodenal or gastric ulcers [Australian].
- **Combination Therapy Note:** When used with levodopa, the contraindications for levodopa must also be observed.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
- **Loss of Selectivity:** Selectivity for MAO-B is dose-dependent. At doses exceeding 20 mg/day, selegiline begins to inhibit MAO-A, increasing the risk of "cheese effect" hypertensive reactions [Australian].
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Concomitant use with serotonergic agents (including MDMA/ecstasy or clozapine) can lead to potentially fatal serotonin syndrome.
- **Impulse Control Disorders:** Patients may exhibit pathological gambling, increased libido, hypersexuality, and binge eating [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular/Psychiatric Sensitivity:** Use with caution in patients with labile hypertension, cardiac arrhythmias, severe angina pectoris, or psychosis, as selegiline may exacerbate these conditions [Australian].
- **Mortality Risk:** While an early UK study suggested increased mortality when combined with levodopa, subsequent meta-analyses and large cohort studies have not confirmed this risk [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often dose-dependent or related to the potentiation of levodopa.

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Dizziness, nausea, hallucinations, confusion, dry mouth, sleep disorders (when used as adjunctive therapy) [Australian]. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Sleeping disorders, headache, vertigo, bradycardia, postural hypotension, constipation, increased hepatic enzymes [Australian]. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Anxiety, aggressive reaction, depression, supraventricular tachycardia, mild transient sleep disorders [Australian]. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Agitation, syncope, convulsions, angina pectoris, myocardial infarction, skin reactions (alopecia, photosensitivity), micturition difficulties [Australian]. |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Neuroleptic malignant syndrome, leucopenia, thrombocytopenia, sexual dysfunction [Australian]. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Postural hypotension and dyskinesia are common when selegiline is added to maximum-tolerated levodopa doses; these may require a reduction in levodopa dosage [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pethidine and Opioids:** Risk of agitation, hyperpyrexia, and death. A 2-week washout period is required after stopping selegiline before starting pethidine [Australian].
- **SSRIs/SNRIs:** Risk of "serotonergic reaction" (shivers, ataxia, mania). Fluoxetine requires a 5-week washout before starting selegiline; other SSRIs require 2 weeks [Australian].
- **Tricyclic Antidepressants:** Severe CNS toxicity (hyperpyrexia, tremor, death) reported; avoid combination [Australian].
- **MAOIs:** Combined use with non-selective MAOIs (including linezolid) or moclobemide may cause severe hypertensive or hypotensive crises [Australian].
- **Oral Contraceptives:** Concomitant use of gestodene/ethinyl estradiol or levonorgestrel/ethinyl estradiol may increase selegiline bioavailability [Australian].
- **Alcohol:** Concomitant intake should be avoided [Australian].
- **General Anaesthesia:** MAO inhibitors may potentiate CNS depressants; caution is required during surgery [Australian].
- **Digitalis/Anticoagulants:** Caution and monitoring required for drugs with a narrow therapeutic index [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **Australian Category B2:** Insufficient human data. Animal studies in rats at 30 mg/kg/day showed no teratogenicity. Use only if benefits clearly outweigh risks [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety is not established. Physio-chemical properties suggest excretion into breast milk; selegiline should not be used during breastfeeding [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Rat studies showed no impairment at oral doses up to 100 mg/kg/day [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children have not been evaluated [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available; however, clinical use suggests standard adult dosing is generally applied [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution in patients with severe renal dysfunction; specific pharmacokinetic data are absent for this population [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution in severe hepatic dysfunction. Transient increases in liver transaminases have been reported during treatment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selegiline is a "suicide" substrate that binds irreversibly to MAO-B in the brain. This inhibits the catabolism of dopamine, increasing dopaminergic activity at the synapse. It may also inhibit dopamine reuptake [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** At recommended doses (10 mg), it is a selective MAO-B inhibitor. Intestinal MAO-A remains largely unaffected, preventing the "cheese reaction" (hypertensive crisis) from dietary tyramine [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; peak plasma concentration in 0.5 hours. Bioavailability is low (~9.4%) but increases up to threefold with high-fat meals [Australian].
  - **Distribution:** Lipophilic; readily crosses the blood-brain barrier. Strongly bound to plasma macroglobulins [Australian].
  - **Metabolism:** Extensive hepatic metabolism via CYP2B6, CYP3A4, and CYP2A6 into L-amphetamine, L-methamphetamine, and N-desmethylselegiline [Australian].
  - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (45% as metabolites within 48 hours); 15% excreted in faeces. Half-life is approximately 1.2–1.8 hours (oral) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Monotherapy (DATATOP Study):** A trial of 800 patients showed that selegiline (10 mg daily) significantly delayed the need for levodopa therapy by approximately 5 to 9 months compared to placebo [Australian].
- **Adjunctive Therapy:** Studies confirmed that adding selegiline to levodopa allows for an 50–80% lower dose of levodopa while maintaining optimal clinical control and reducing "end-of-dose" fluctuations [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C. Protect from light and moisture [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to expiry date on packaging; shelf life is registered on the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG) [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Poison Schedule S4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
