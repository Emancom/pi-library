# Editorial Product Information: Sulthiame

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial information is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for clinical reference and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in specific jurisdictions. As only the Australian source was available for this synthesis, all data and classifications (e.g., pregnancy categories and poison schedules) reflect Australian regulatory standards.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Sulthiame (a cyclic sulfonamide derivative)
- **Brand Name(s):** Ospolot [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** N03AX02 (not explicitly in source, but standard classification for sulthiame)
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 50 mg (round, debossed "50") [Australian]
    - Film-coated tablets: 200 mg (round, debossed "200", scored) [Australian]
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Phebra Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Anticonvulsant Therapy:** Indicated for the management of:
    - Temporal lobe epilepsy
    - Myoclonic seizures
    - Grand mal attacks
    - Jacksonian seizures
- **Behavioural Management:** Indicated for behavioural disorders associated with epilepsy and hyperkinetic behaviour [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Initial Dose:** 100 mg twice daily OR 50 mg three times daily.
- **Titration:** Dosage should be increased gradually over approximately four weeks until a satisfactory clinical response is achieved.
- **Optimum Maintenance Dose:** 200 mg three times daily.

### Paediatric Dosing
- **Initial Dose:** 3–5 mg/kg daily in equal divided doses.
- **Optimum Maintenance Dose:** 10–15 mg/kg daily in equal divided doses.

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets should preferably be swallowed whole with a small amount of fluid.
- Administration should occur after meals.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to sulthiame, other sulfonamides, or any excipients (including lactose) [Australian].
- Known acute porphyria [Australian].
- Hyperthyroidism [Australian].
- Arterial hypertension [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Suicidal Behaviour and Ideation:** Antiepileptic drugs (AEDs) increase the risk of suicidal thoughts or behaviour. Patients should be monitored for the emergence or worsening of depression or unusual mood changes.
- **Psychiatric Disorders:** Use with caution and adequate monitoring in patients with a pre-existing history of psychiatric disorders.
- **Organ Impairment:** Sulthiame is predominantly renally excreted; use only with adequate monitoring in patients with impaired renal or hepatic function.
- **Hypersensitivity and Haematological Reactions:** 
    - Treatment must be discontinued immediately in cases of severe allergic reactions.
    - Monitor for progressive thrombocytopenia or leukopenia; treatment interruption is required if clinical symptoms (e.g., fever, sore throat) accompany blood count changes.
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Regular checks of blood counts, liver enzymes, and urine are recommended. Treatment should be interrupted if a persistent increase in creatinine occurs.
- **Carbonic Anhydrase Inhibition:** As a carbonic anhydrase inhibitor, sulthiame may predispose patients to renal stone formation, metabolic acidosis, and electrolyte imbalances.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Distribution
| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Anorexia, hyperpnoea, dyspnoea, gastric complaints (~10%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Weight loss, ataxia, dose-dependent paraesthesia (face and extremities), dizziness (giddiness), double vision, stenocardia, tachycardia, tachypnoea, singultus (hiccups) |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Hallucinations, anxiety, apathy (lack of drive), depression, behavioural anomalies, headache, myasthenic phenomena, grand-mal status, increased seizure activity, drooling, insomnia, abdominal pain, nausea, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, joint pain, leucopenia |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Not specifically categorised in available source. |
| **Not Known** | Polyneuritis, hepatotoxic reactions, increased liver enzymes, rash, Lyell's syndrome (Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis), acute renal failure |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Neurological:** One case reported progressive limb weakness, hypersalivation, and slurred speech leading to coma; symptoms resolved upon discontinuation.
- **Metabolic:** Long-term therapy may lead to disturbances in calcium and Vitamin D metabolism.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Alcohol:** Must be avoided. Sulthiame may induce a disulfiram-like reaction (headache, respiratory depression, tachycardia, hypotension, seizures).
- **Primidone:** Concomitant use may lead to severe side effects, particularly in children (dizziness, gait instability, psychotic reactions).
- **Phenytoin:** Sulthiame significantly increases phenytoin serum levels. Dose reduction of phenytoin and frequent plasma level monitoring are required.
- **Phenobarbitone:** Sulthiame may increase serum levels of phenobarbitone.
- **Lamotrigine:** Elevated lamotrigine levels have been reported; monitor levels closely at the start of therapy.
- **Carbamazepine:** Sulthiame serum levels may decrease when taken with carbamazepine.
- **Other Carbonic Anhydrase Inhibitors (e.g., Topiramate, Acetazolamide):** Increased risk of adverse effects due to additive inhibition.
- **Laboratory Interferences:** May interfere with the estimation of barbiturates in blood tests.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category D:** Drugs known or suspected to cause human fetal malformations or irreversible damage. 
    - Risk of abnormalities is approximately three times higher than the general population in mothers with epilepsy. 
    - If used, the dose must be kept as low as possible, particularly between days 20 and 40 of gestation.
- **Breastfeeding:** Excretion into breast milk is unknown. Use is not recommended unless the clinical benefit clearly outweighs the potential risk.
- **Fertility:** No data available in source.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for children; dosing is weight-based (see Section 3). Caution is advised regarding interactions with primidone in this population.
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available in source.
- **Renal Impairment:** Requires cautious dosing and strict monitoring as the drug is primarily renally excreted.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Requires caution when establishing dosage.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A cyclic sulfonamide derivative that acts as a carbonic anhydrase inhibitor. Its structure is distinct from other anticonvulsants and it lacks antimicrobial activity.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Exhibits anticonvulsant properties; may cause metabolic changes (acidosis, haemodilution) typical of its class.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption/Distribution/Metabolism/Excretion:** Not specified in available source.
