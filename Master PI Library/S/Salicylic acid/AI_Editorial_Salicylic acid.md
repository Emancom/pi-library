# Editorial Product Information: Fluorouracil and Salicylic Acid

## Editorial Note
- The PI documents contributing to this editorial include: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration). 
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview of **fluorouracil** and **salicylic acid** topical solution for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis serves as a clinical reference and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Fluorouracil (a pyrimidine antimetabolite/cytostatic agent) and Salicylic acid (a phenolic aromatic acid/keratolytic agent).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Actikerall** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** D06BB51 (Fluorouracil, combinations).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Topical solution containing 5 mg/g fluorouracil and 100 mg/g salicylic acid [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Topical application only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Mayne Pharma International Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Actinic (Solar) Keratosis:** Indicated for the topical treatment of mild to moderate (Grade I and II) solar keratosis on the face, forehead, or bald scalp [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** Apply once daily to the affected lesions or as a field treatment. 
- **Maximum Treatment Area:** Total treatment area must not exceed 25 cm² (e.g., 5 cm x 5 cm) at one time [Australian].
- **Duration of Therapy:** Continue application until lesions have cleared or for a maximum of 12 weeks.
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Use the provided brush applicator.
    - WIPE the brush on the bottle neck before application to avoid overloading.
    - Apply to the lesion and a maximum 0.5 cm rim of healthy surrounding skin.
    - Allow the solution to dry into a white film. Do not cover with a dressing.
    - **Film Removal:** Before each new application, remove the existing film by peeling it off (warm water may assist).
    - If crystals form in the bottle or on the brush, the product should not be used.
- **Special Dosing Considerations:** For areas with thin epidermis (e.g., around eyes or temples), reduce application frequency and monitor more closely [Australian].
- **Dose Adjustment:** If severe local adverse reactions occur, frequency may be reduced to three times per week until symptoms improve.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to fluorouracil, salicylic acid, acetylsalicylic acid (aspirin), or any excipients [Australian].
- **Pregnancy and Lactation:** Contraindicated during pregnancy, in women where pregnancy cannot be excluded, and during breastfeeding [Australian].
- **Renal Insufficiency:** Must not be used in patients with renal impairment [Australian].
- **DPD Status:** 
    - Must not be used in patients with known dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase (DPD) deficiency.
    - Must not be used concomitantly with potent DPD inhibitors (e.g., nucleoside analogues) [Australian].
- **Site Restrictions:** Must not come into contact with eyes or mucous membranes.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [Australian]:** **Actikerall** should only be used on a total area no greater than 25 cm² at a time. Use is limited to specific body sites and skin types.
- **Local Skin Responses:** Erythema, inflammation, burning, pain, and crusting are expected pharmacological effects. Treatment assessment may be difficult until these responses resolve.
- **UV Exposure:** Solar keratosis results from chronic UV damage. Patients must protect treated areas from direct sunlight using barriers (hats/clothing) and broad-spectrum sunscreen (SPF 30+) on unbroken skin [Australian].
- **Thin Skin/Absorption:** Application to thin skin increases the risk of systemic absorption and adverse reactions.
- **Excipient Warnings:** Contains **dimethyl sulfoxide** (skin irritant) and **alcohol** (19% v/v ethanol).
- **Secondary Skin Diseases:** No experience exists for treating areas affected by other skin diseases; clinical outcomes may vary.
- **Exclusions:** Not recommended for Basal Cell Carcinoma or Bowen’s disease [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are primarily localized to the administration site and are related to the drug's pharmacological activity.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Application site irritation (burning), inflammation, pruritus, pain, erythema. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Application site erosion, scab/crusting, headache, skin exfoliation. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Dermatitis, oedema, ulcer, dry eye, eye pruritus, increased lacrimation, gastroenteritis. |

- **Serious Reactions:** While no serious drug-related events were reported in pivotal trials, severe local reactions (ulceration, erosion) may occur, requiring treatment cessation or frequency reduction.
- **Field Treatment:** Frequency of dermatitis, scabbing, and oedema may increase when used as field therapy (up to 25 cm²) compared to focal lesion therapy [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **DPD Inhibitors:** Concomitant use with potent DPD inhibitors (e.g., brivudine, sorivudine) can drastically increase fluorouracil plasma levels and toxicity. A minimum 4-week washout period is required between these agents and fluorouracil [Australian].
- **Phenytoin:** Systemic fluorouracil may increase phenytoin plasma levels. While topical absorption is low, regular monitoring of phenytoin levels is advised [Australian].
- **Methotrexate & Sulphonylureas:** Absorbed salicylic acid may interact with these agents; however, systemic absorption of salicylic acid from this formulation is generally negligible [Australian].
- **Topical Drying Agents:** Concurrent use with abrasive cleansers, benzoyl peroxide, retinoids (tretinoin/isotretinoin), or alcohol-based products may cause cumulative irritation [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category D.** Fluorouracil is teratogenic in animals. Topical use is contraindicated due to potential fetal risk.
- **Breastfeeding:** Excretion into human milk is unknown. Use is contraindicated in breastfeeding women [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Systemic fluorouracil can cause transient male infertility and reduced female pregnancy rates in rodents; however, this is unlikely at the low systemic levels achieved via topical application [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established; solar keratosis is not typically a paediatric condition [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** The standard adult dose may be used [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with renal insufficiency [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specifically addressed in source documentation, though fluorouracil is primarily metabolised in the liver.
- **Sensory Disturbances:** Patients with diabetes mellitus or other sensory disturbances require close monitoring of the treatment site [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:**
    - **Fluorouracil:** A cytostatic antimetabolite that inhibits DNA and RNA synthesis by mimicking thymine. It targets cells with accelerated growth, such as actinic keratosis lesions.
    - **Salicylic Acid:** Acts as a keratolytic and corneolytic agent, facilitating the penetration of fluorouracil into hyperkeratotic lesions.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Extremely low. Percutaneous absorption of fluorouracil was measured at 0.0085% (single dose) and 0.035% (repeated dose) [Australian]. 
    - **Metabolism:** Fluorouracil is metabolised in the liver by DPD. Salicylic acid is metabolised to salicyluric acid and glucuronides.
    - **Excretion:** Fluorouracil is partly excreted as CO2 via the lungs; salicylic acid is excreted via the kidneys.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study 0702 (Focal):** Compared **Actikerall** to 3% diclofenac gel and placebo. **Actikerall** showed superior histological clearance (70.1%) vs. diclofenac (54.1%) and placebo (42.7%) at 8 weeks post-treatment [Australian].
- **Study 1401 (Field):** In a 25 cm² field treatment area, **Actikerall** achieved complete clinical clearance in 49.5% of patients compared to 18.2% in the vehicle group.
- **Study 1007 (vs. Cryotherapy):** **Actikerall** demonstrated a higher histological clearance rate (62.1%) compared to cryotherapy (41.9%) for Grade II/III lesions [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Restrictions:** Do not freeze or refrigerate. Keep the bottle tightly closed to prevent drying [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Discard 3 months after the first opening [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
