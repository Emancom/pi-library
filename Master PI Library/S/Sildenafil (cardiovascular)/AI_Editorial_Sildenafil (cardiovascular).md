# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data to support safe and effective prescribing.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Always consult the current local regulatory documentation for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** A naturally occurring carrier molecule (amino acid derivative) required for mammalian energy metabolism; specifically, the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane.
- **Brand Names:** Not specified in available sources (Generic name: Levocarnitine Injection, USP).
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn errors of metabolism [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg, administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
    - Maintenance doses are typically 50 mg/kg/day, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg/day have been utilized [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:**
    - The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
    - Administer as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].

### Administration and Titration
- **Monitoring:** Clinical monitoring and plasma carnitine concentrations should guide therapy. Target plasma free carnitine concentrations should be between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Dose Adjustment in ESRD:** Adjustments may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, potentially down-titrating to 5 mg/kg based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma carnitine levels (normal range 40–50 µmol/L) [US].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in patients with ESRD on dialysis. Reactions may occur within minutes of intravenous administration [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Levocarnitine (both oral and IV) has been associated with reports of seizures. In patients with pre-existing seizure disorders, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels [US].
- **Administration Precaution:** Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common Effects:** Transient nausea and vomiting have been observed. Less frequent effects include body odor and gastritis [US].

### Adverse Events in ESRD Dialysis Trials (Frequency ≥5%)
Based on two double-blind, placebo-controlled trials [US]:

| Body System | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain (9%), Asthenia (9%), Chest pain (12%), Fever (7%), Headache (22%), Infection (15%), Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%) |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%), Nausea (8%) |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia (9%), Hyperkalemia (6%), Hypervolemia (5%) |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness (13%), Insomnia (4%), Paresthesia (5%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (23%), Cough increase (9%), Dyspnea (7%), Rhinitis (9%) |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Clinical Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly categorised by letter, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm [US].
    - **Recommendation:** Should be used during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** 
    - Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied [US].
    - Studies in dairy cows indicate increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration [US].
    - **Recommendation:** Weigh the risk of excess carnitine intake in the child against the benefit of supplementation to the mother. Discontinuation of nursing or treatment may be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No mutagenic effects were observed in standard tests (Salmonella, yeast) [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy are established for inborn errors of metabolism and ESRD carnitine deficiency; follow weight-based dosing (50 mg/kg for metabolic disorders; 10–20 mg/kg for ESRD) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency [US].
    - Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (found in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (found in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Pharmacokinetic Study:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x/week post-dialysis), mean pre-dialysis (trough) levels increased from ~21 µmol/L at baseline to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** ESRD patients receiving 10, 20, or 40 mg/kg doses 3x/week showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations at 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL concentrations) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
- **Vial Usage:** Single-dose vial; discard any unused portion as the formulation is preservative-free [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** Prescription Only (Rx Only) [US].
