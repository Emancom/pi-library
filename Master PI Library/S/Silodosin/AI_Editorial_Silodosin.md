# Editorial Product Information: Silodosin

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from the following jurisdiction-specific Product Information (PI):
    - Australian (TGA): "Product Information — UROREC (silodosin) Capsules"
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is intended as a clinical reference and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Silodosin (an $\alpha_{1}$-adrenoceptor antagonist).
- **Brand Names:** Urorec [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** G04CA04.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Hard gelatin capsules: 4 mg (yellow, opaque) and 8 mg (white, opaque) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Mayne Pharma International Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (BPH):** Indicated for the relief of lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS) associated with BPH in adult men [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dose:** 8 mg once daily [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Must be taken with food, preferably at the same time each day. 
    - Capsules should be swallowed whole with a glass of water; they must not be broken or chewed [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment Titration:** In moderate renal impairment, a starting dose of 4 mg once daily is recommended. After at least one week, the dose may be increased to 8 mg once daily based on individual clinical response [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources (standard clinical practice applies).

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to silodosin or any of the excipients in the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Orthostatic Effects:** While the incidence is low, postural hypotension and, rarely, syncope can occur. Treatment is not recommended in patients with pre-existing orthostatic hypotension [Australian].
- **Carcinoma of the Prostate:** As BPH and prostate carcinoma symptoms overlap and can co-exist, malignancy must be ruled out via digital rectal examination and PSA testing before initiating therapy and at regular intervals thereafter [Australian].
- **Intraoperative Floppy Iris Syndrome (IFIS):** Observed during cataract surgery in patients currently or previously treated with $\alpha_{1}$-blockers. Initiation of silodosin is not recommended in patients scheduled for cataract surgery [Australian].
- **Retrograde Ejaculation:** Silodosin causes a reduction or absence of semen during orgasm, which may temporarily affect male fertility. This effect is reversible upon discontinuation [Australian].
- **Concomitant Alpha-Blockers:** Use with other $\alpha$-adrenoreceptor antagonists is not recommended due to insufficient safety data [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Patients should be monitored for adverse reactions when starting concomitant antihypertensives or PDE-5 inhibitors [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorized by frequency based on clinical study data.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Ejaculatory disorders (including retrograde ejaculation and anejaculation) (23%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Dizziness, headache, diarrhea, nasal congestion, orthostatic hypotension |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Libido decreased, tachycardia, hypotension, nausea, dry mouth, abnormal liver function tests, skin rash, pruritus, urticaria, drug eruption, erectile dysfunction |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Syncope, loss of consciousness, palpitations |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Allergic-type reactions (facial swelling, swollen tongue, pharyngeal oedema) |
| **Unknown** | Intraoperative Floppy Iris Syndrome (IFIS) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Syncope and severe allergic reactions are rare but require immediate clinical attention.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** 
    - **Potent Inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, ritonavir, itraconazole, cyclosporine):** Concomitant use is not recommended; ketoconazole has been shown to increase silodosin exposure (AUC) by 3.1-fold.
    - **Moderate Inhibitors (e.g., diltiazem):** AUC increases by approximately 30%; no dose adjustment is required [Australian].
- **P-Glycoprotein (P-gp) Inhibitors:** Silodosin is a P-gp substrate. Strong P-gp inhibitors may increase silodosin concentrations and are not recommended for co-administration [Australian].
- **PDE-5 Inhibitors (e.g., sildenafil, tadalafil):** Minimal pharmacodynamic interactions observed, but patients should be monitored for potential additive hypotensive effects [Australian].
- **Antihypertensives:** Use caution when initiating co-administration and monitor for orthostasis [Australian].
- **Digoxin:** No significant interaction; no dose adjustment required [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. Silodosin is not indicated for use in women. Animal studies showed no teratogenicity at clinical doses, though fetal losses occurred at maternally toxic doses in rabbits [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not indicated for use in women. No data available regarding passage into human milk [Australian].
- **Fertility:** May temporarily impair male fertility due to reduced semen volume; reversible upon cessation [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not indicated; safety and efficacy have not been evaluated in patients under 18 years of age [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required. Orthostatic hypotension may be more common in patients >75 years [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Mild (CLcr 60–89 mL/min):** No dose adjustment required.
    - **Moderate (CLcr 30–59 mL/min):** Starting dose of 4 mg daily; increase to 8 mg only with caution.
    - **Severe (CLcr < 30 mL/min):** Use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild to Moderate (Child-Pugh 7–9):** No dose adjustment required.
    - **Severe:** Use is not recommended as data are unavailable [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Highly selective antagonist of $\alpha_{1A}$-adrenoreceptors located in the prostate, bladder neck, and prostatic urethra. Blockade causes smooth muscle relaxation, reducing bladder outlet resistance without affecting detrusor contractility [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Improves both storage (irritative) and voiding (obstructive) symptoms. No significant effect on ECG intervals or cardiac repolarization [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Bioavailability ~32%; $C_{max}$ is reached in ~2.5 hours. Food reduces $C_{max}$ by 30% [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** 95% protein bound; volume of distribution is 0.81 L/kg.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensive metabolism via glucuronidation (UGT2B7), alcohol dehydrogenase, and CYP3A4. The main active metabolite is silodosin glucuronide (KMD-3213G).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 33.5% in urine and 54.9% in feces. Terminal half-life of parent drug is ~11 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** Three Phase III placebo-controlled trials (two in the US, one in Europe) involving 837 patients with IPSS $\ge$ 13.
- **Outcomes:** Silodosin 8 mg daily significantly improved total IPSS scores compared to placebo. In the European study, silodosin was demonstrated to be non-inferior to tamsulosin 0.4 mg.
- **Onset:** Significant improvements in maximum urinary flow rate ($Q_{max}$) were observed within 2 to 6 hours of the first dose [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Conditions:** Keep in the original package to protect from light and moisture [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
