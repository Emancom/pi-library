# Editorial Product Information: Selumetinib

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from the following jurisdictional Product Information (PI) source:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: KOSELUGO (selumetinib sulfate) capsules.
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of selumetinib for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesizes clinical data, regulatory requirements, and safety protocols into a single, structured reference.
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and is not intended to replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for final prescribing authority.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Selumetinib (as sulfate).
- **Chemical Class**: An orally available, non-ATP competitive inhibitor of mitogen-activated protein kinase kinases 1 and 2 (MEK1/2).
- **Brand Name(s)**: **KOSELUGO** [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: L01EE04.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**:
    - Hard capsules: 10 mg (white to off-white, marked "SEL 10") and 25 mg (blue, marked "SEL 25") [Australian].
- **Route of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Alexion Pharmaceuticals Australasia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Neurofibromatosis Type 1 (NF1)**: Indicated for the treatment of adult and paediatric patients aged 2 years and older with NF1 who have symptomatic, inoperable plexiform neurofibromas (PN).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)**: The recommended dose is **25 mg/m²** of body surface area (BSA) administered orally twice daily (approximately every 12 hours).
- **Maximal Dose**: The maximum single dose is 50 mg.
- **Dosing Table**: Doses are rounded to the nearest achievable 5 mg or 10 mg increment.
    - *Example (0.55–0.69 m²)*: 20 mg AM, 10 mg PM.
    - *Example (≥1.90 m²)*: 50 mg twice daily.
- **Administration Instructions**:
    - Capsules must be swallowed whole with water. Do not chew, dissolve, or open.
    - Can be taken with or without food [Australian].
    - For patients with swallowing difficulties, referral to a speech/language therapist is recommended.
- **Missed Dose**: If a dose is missed, it should only be taken if the next scheduled dose is more than 6 hours away.
- **Vomiting**: If vomiting occurs after administration, do not take an additional dose; resume with the next scheduled dose.
- **Dose Adjustments for Toxicity**: Interruption or reduction (by approximately 25%) may be required. A maximum of two dose reductions is permitted before permanent discontinuation is required.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity**: Known hypersensitivity to selumetinib or any excipients.
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Left Ventricular Ejection Fraction (LVEF) Reduction**: Reported in both paediatric and adult patients. LVEF should be assessed before treatment and monitored every 3 months. Treatment should be withheld if LVEF decreases by ≥10% from baseline and is below the lower limit of normal (LLN).
- **Ocular Toxicity**:
    - **Retinal Vein Occlusion (RVO)**: Requires permanent discontinuation.
    - **Retinal Pigment Epithelial Detachment (RPED) / Central Serous Retinopathy (CSR)**: Requires withholding treatment and dose reduction upon resolution.
- **Liver Laboratory Abnormalities**: AST and ALT elevations are common. Monitor liver function tests before initiation, monthly for the first 6 months, and then as clinically indicated.
- **Gastrointestinal Toxicity**: Diarrhoea is highly prevalent. Patients should be advised to start anti-diarrhoeal agents (e.g., loperamide) at the first episode of loose stools.
- **Skin Toxicity**: Dermatitis acneiform and other rashes are common. Severe cases may require dose modification or discontinuation.
- **Increased Creatine Phosphokinase (CPK)**: Monitor CPK levels periodically. Withhold dose if CPK is significantly elevated; monitor for rhabdomyolysis.
- **Vitamin E Supplementation**: **KOSELUGO** capsules contain significant amounts of vitamin E as the excipient tocofersolan (32 mg per 10 mg capsule; 36 mg per 25 mg capsule). Patients should be advised **not** to take supplemental vitamin E. High doses may increase bleeding risk, particularly in patients on anticoagulants/antiplatelets.
- **Risk of Choking**: Use with caution in children <6 years old due to the risk of choking on the capsule formulation.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Very Common (>10%)
- **Gastrointestinal**: Vomiting, abdominal pain, diarrhoea, nausea, stomatitis, constipation.
- **Skin**: Dry skin, acneiform dermatitis, rash (non-acneiform), paronychia, pruritus.
- **Musculoskeletal**: Musculoskeletal pain.
- **General**: Pyrexia, fatigue, oedema.
- **Nervous System**: Headache.
- **Cardiovascular**: Decreased ejection fraction.
- **Laboratory**: Increased CPK, increased AST/ALT, decreased haemoglobin, increased creatinine.

### Common (1–10%)
- **Gastrointestinal**: Dry mouth.
- **Infections**: Skin infections (cellulitis, abscess).
- **Vascular**: Hypertension.
- **Eye**: Blurred vision.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Cellulitis**: Noted as a serious event in 2 or more patients in adult studies.
- **LVEF Reduction**: Serious cases reported in expanded access programs.
- **Gastrointestinal Perforation**: Observed in unapproved adult populations with multiple tumour types.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 and CYP2C19 Inhibitors**: Strong or moderate inhibitors (e.g., itraconazole, fluconazole, clarithromycin, grapefruit juice) increase selumetinib exposure. If unavoidable, the dose should be reduced to **20 mg/m²** or **15 mg/m²** BID depending on the current regimen.
- **CYP3A4 Inducers**: Strong inducers (e.g., rifampicin, phenytoin, St. John’s Wort) significantly decrease exposure and should be avoided.
- **OAT3 Substrates**: Selumetinib is an *in vitro* inhibitor of OAT3; clinical relevance cannot be excluded.
- **Anticoagulants**: Bleeding risk may be increased due to vitamin E content in the capsule. Monitor INR/PT more frequently.
- **Oral Contraceptives**: Effect on hormonal contraceptives is unknown; an additional barrier method is recommended.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: **Category D** [Australian]. Based on animal data, selumetinib can cause fetal harm (reduced weight, skeletal defects, and cleft palate). 
- **Contraception**: Females of reproductive potential and males with female partners of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for 1 week after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding**: It is unknown if selumetinib is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment.
- **Fertility**: No human data available. In mice, a slight reduction in live fetuses was observed at high doses, but overall fertility was not affected.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Approved for patients aged **2 years and older**. Efficacy and safety are similar across the 2–18 year age range.
- **Elderly Use**: Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients >65 years.
- **Renal Impairment**: No dose adjustment is recommended for patients with mild, moderate, severe, or End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD).
- **Hepatic Impairment**:
    - **Mild**: No adjustment required.
    - **Moderate**: Reduce starting dose to **20 mg/m²** BID.
    - **Severe**: Contraindicated.
- **Ethnicity**: Increased exposure (AUC/Cmax) has been seen in Asian subjects, though overlap with Western populations exists. No specific dose adjustment is recommended, but close monitoring for toxicity is advised.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Selumetinib inhibits MEK 1 and 2. These kinases are critical components of the RAF-MEK-ERK pathway. By blocking MEK, selumetinib inhibits the growth of tumors where this pathway is constitutively active, as seen in NF1-related plexiform neurofibromas.
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Bioavailability is ~62%. $T_{max}$ is 1–1.5 hours. High-fat meals reduce $C_{max}$ and AUC.
    - **Distribution**: Protein binding is ~98.4%.
    - **Metabolism**: Primarily via CYP3A4-mediated oxidation and CYP2C19-mediated N-demethylation.
    - **Elimination**: Mean half-life is ~6.2 hours in children and ~9 hours in adults. Primarily excreted in faeces (59%).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **SPRINT Phase II (Paediatric)**: Single-arm study (n=50). Objective Response Rate (ORR) was **68%** (95% CI: 53.3–80.5). Responses were durable, with 86.3% remaining in response at 36 months.
- **KOMET Phase III (Adult)**: Double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (n=145). Selumetinib showed an ORR of **19.7%** compared to 5.4% for placebo (p=0.0112). 

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store below 30°C.
- **Handling**: Keep in the original bottle to protect from moisture. Do not remove the desiccant. Keep bottle tightly closed.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia**: **Schedule 4** (Prescription Only Medicine). Subject to additional monitoring (Black Triangle).
