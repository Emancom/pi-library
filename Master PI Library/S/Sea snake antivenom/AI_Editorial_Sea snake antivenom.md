# Editorial Product Information: Sea Snake Antivenom (Equine)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. No other jurisdictional documents were provided for comparison at the time of this publication.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically focused overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official PI approved for use in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory guidelines and institution-specific protocols before prescribing or administering this product.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Sea snake antivenom (equine).
- **Chemical Class:** Purified equine immunoglobulins (IgG) derived from the plasma of horses immunised with the venom of the sea snake *Enhydrina schistosa*.
- **Brand Names:** **Sea Snake Antivenom** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J06AA (Antivenoms) [Not explicitly specified in source; inferred from class].
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Concentrated injection for intravenous use. Each vial contains 1,000 units of antivenom in an aqueous solution. 
- **Composition:** Each 1 mL contains 2.2 mg phenol, 8 mg sodium chloride, and $\le$ 170 mg per mL of equine plasma protein. The total volume per vial varies by batch according to potency.
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Seqirus Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Systemic Envenoming:** Indicated for the treatment of patients who demonstrate clinical manifestations of systemic envenoming following a bite by a sea snake [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Standard Dose:** The initial dose is 1,000 units (one vial).
- **Severe Envenoming:** In cases of profound systemic toxicity (e.g., muscle weakness, myalgia, trismus, ptosis, or ophthalmoplegia), an initial dose of 3,000 to 4,000 units is recommended.
- **Total Dose Requirements:** Most patients are controlled with 3,000 units, though severe cases may require up to 10,000 units in total.
- **Paediatric Dosing:** The dose for children is identical to the adult dose, as the amount of venom to be neutralised is independent of patient weight.

### Administration Instructions
- **Dilution:** Must be administered as a slow intravenous infusion. Dilute 1 in 10 (e.g., 1 vial in 100 mL) using Hartmann’s Solution or 0.9% Sodium Chloride. 
- **Fluid Restriction:** A dilution of 1 in 5 may be used if the patient is at risk of fluid overload (e.g., small children).
- **Stability:** Use immediately after dilution. Do not store diluted solution.
- **Pre-administration:** Adrenaline must be drawn up and ready for immediate use before starting the infusion to manage potential anaphylaxis.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Absolute Contraindications:** There are no absolute contraindications to the use of antivenom for serious systemic envenoming [Australian].
- **Clinical Threshold:** The product should not be administered unless there is clear evidence of systemic envenoming with potential for serious toxicity.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Anaphylaxis:** As an equine-derived product, there is a significant risk of severe allergic reactions, including fatal anaphylaxis. Risk is increased in patients with known horse or horse dander allergies or those who have previously received equine-derived products (e.g., equine tetanus antitoxin).
- **Observation Period:** Patients bitten by a sea snake must be monitored for at least 12 hours before discharge. If a pressure immobilisation bandage was applied, the 12-hour observation period begins only after the bandage is removed.
- **Bandage Removal:** Pressure bandages and splints must not be removed until the patient is in a facility with full resuscitation equipment and antivenom available, as removal may precipitate sudden systemic envenoming.
- **Serum Sickness:** Delayed serum sickness (fever, rash, arthralgia) is common following the use of large volumes of foreign protein. 
- **Transmission of Infectious Agents:** Because the product is derived from animal plasma, the risk of transmitting infectious agents (including unknown pathogens) cannot be entirely excluded.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
The antivenom contains specific antibodies that neutralise the toxins present in sea snake venom, including potent neurotoxins and myolytics. It is effective against the venom of *Enhydrina schistosa* and various other sea snakes found in northern Australian waters.

### Pharmacodynamics
- **Venom Effects:** Sea snake venom causes muscle paralysis and respiratory failure (neurotoxicity) and muscle destruction (myolysis). Myolysis can lead to myalgia, renal failure, hyperkalaemia, and elevation of creatine kinase (CK) and aspartate transaminase (AST).
- **Cross-Reactivity:** Due to similarities between sea snake toxins and certain Australian elapids, **Tiger Snake Antivenom** is often effective and may be used if Sea Snake Antivenom is unavailable [Australian].
