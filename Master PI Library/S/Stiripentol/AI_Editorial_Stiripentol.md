# Editorial Product Information: Stiripentol

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial synthesis: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of stiripentol for use by healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a single clinical reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Stiripentol (a racemic mixture).
- **Chemical Class:** Aromatic allylic alcohol.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Diacomit** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Capsules:** 250 mg and 500 mg.
    - **Powder for oral suspension (sachets):** 250 mg and 500 mg.
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Chiesi Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Dravet Syndrome (SMEI):** Indicated as adjunctive treatment for generalized tonic-clonic and clonic seizures associated with severe myoclonic epilepsy in infancy (SMEI/Dravet syndrome). Approval is specific to patients whose seizures are not adequately managed with a combination of a benzodiazepine (typically clobazam) and valproate [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Target Dose:** 50 mg/kg/day, administered in two or three divided doses [Australian].
- **Maximum Dose:** Safety data for doses exceeding 50 mg/kg/day are not available [Australian].

### Titration Schedule
Initiation should be gradual, typically reaching the target dose over 3 to 4 weeks:
- **Week 1:** 20 mg/kg/day.
- **Week 2:** 30 mg/kg/day.
- **Subsequent Weeks (Age-dependent):**
    - **Children <6 years:** Increase by 20 mg/kg/day in Week 3 to reach 50 mg/kg/day.
    - **Children 6 to <12 years:** Increase by 10 mg/kg/day each week until 50 mg/kg/day is reached (Week 4).
    - **Adolescents ≥12 years:** Increase by 5 mg/kg/day each week based on clinical judgment until the optimum dose is reached [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Food Requirement:** Must be taken with food. Stiripentol degrades rapidly in an acidic environment (e.g., an empty stomach).
- **Capsules:** Swallow whole with a glass of water during a meal.
- **Powder:** Mix in a glass of water and consume immediately during a meal.
- **Dietary Restrictions:** Do **not** take with milk, dairy products (yogurt, cream cheese), carbonated drinks, fruit juice, or products containing caffeine or theophylline (e.g., cola, chocolate) [Australian].
- **Formulation Switch:** Capsules and sachets are **not bioequivalent** (sachets have a higher Cmax). Switching between formulations requires clinical supervision [Australian].

### Drug Discontinuation
Withdrawal must be gradual (over at least one month) to minimize the risk of increased seizure frequency or status epilepticus [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to stiripentol or any excipients.
- A past medical history of psychosis manifesting as episodes of delirium [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None noted in the provided source. However, the product is subject to additional monitoring [Australian].
- **Hematologic Monitoring:** Neutropenia and thrombocytopenia are associated with stiripentol (often in combination with valproate and clobazam). Blood counts should be assessed at baseline and every 6 months thereafter [Australian].
- **Hepatic Monitoring:** Elevated liver enzymes (AST, ALT, and γGT) are reported, particularly when combined with valproate. Liver function tests (LFTs) should be performed at baseline and every 6 months [Australian].
- **Neurologic/Psychiatric Effects:** 
    - Somnolence and drowsiness are very common; monitor patients closely, especially if using other CNS depressants.
    - Movement disorders including ataxia, hypotonia, tremor, and dysarthria have been reported.
    - Suicidal ideation and behavior have been reported with antiepileptic agents; monitor for changes in mood or behavior [Australian].
- **Growth Monitoring:** Due to the high frequency of gastrointestinal side effects (anorexia, weight loss), the growth rate in children should be carefully monitored [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are frequently related to the elevation of co-administered anticonvulsant levels (clobazam/valproate).

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Drowsiness, ataxia, hypotonia, dystonia, insomnia, anorexia, weight loss (especially with sodium valproate) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Neutropenia (often resolves upon discontinuation), hyperkinesia, nausea, vomiting, aggressiveness, irritability, behavior disorders, sleep disorders, raised γGT |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Diplopia (when used with carbamazepine), photosensitivity, rash, urticaria, fatigue |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Thrombocytopenia, abnormal liver function tests |
| **Frequency Unknown** | Pneumonia, aspiration pneumonia |

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
Stiripentol is a potent inhibitor of multiple CYP450 enzymes: **CYP1A2, CYP2C8, CYP2C19, CYP3A4, CYP2C9, and CYP2D6**.

- **Clobazam:** Stiripentol increases clobazam levels 2- to 3-fold and norclobazam (active metabolite) levels 5-fold. **Management:** Reduce clobazam dose by 25% weekly if clinical signs of toxicity (somnolence, hypotonia) occur [Australian].
- **Valproate:** Modest metabolic interaction; however, valproate dose may need reduction by 30% weekly if GI side effects (weight loss, anorexia) occur [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Substrates:** Avoid co-administration with narrow therapeutic index drugs (e.g., ergots, cisapride, halofantrine, quinidine, immunosuppressants like tacrolimus).
- **CYP2C19/CYP2D6 Substrates:** Use caution and consider dose adjustments for drugs like citalopram, omeprazole, beta-blockers, and certain antidepressants [Australian].
- **Inhibitors/Inducers:** Phenytoin, phenobarbital, and carbamazepine significantly decrease stiripentol concentrations and should generally not be used in Dravet syndrome [Australian].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Caffeine/Theophylline:** Co-administration is **not recommended** as stiripentol may inhibit their metabolism, leading to toxicity [Australian].
- **GABA Modulators:** Potential synergy with other GABAA receptor modulators (e.g., benzodiazepines, barbiturates).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category B1 [Australian]:** Stiripentol crosses the placenta in animal models. There is no human data on exposed pregnancies. Clinical decision-making should weigh benefits against risks.
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. While no human data exist, stiripentol passes into breast milk in animal models (goats). If maintained, the infant must be closely monitored [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies (up to 3-fold the MRHD) [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Pivotal trials were conducted in children ≥3 years. Safety and efficacy in children <3 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly:** No data available [Australian].
- **Hepatic/Renal Impairment:** Stiripentol is **not recommended** in patients with impaired hepatic or renal function due to lack of specific clinical data and its primary metabolism by the liver and excretion by the kidney [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    1. **GABAA Modulation:** Acts as a positive allosteric modulator of the GABAA receptor, increasing the duration of chloride channel opening. It also increases GABA release from nerve terminals and inhibits GABA uptake/metabolism.
    2. **Metabolic Inhibition:** Potentiates other anticonvulsants (clobazam) by inhibiting CYP450 3A4 and 2C19 [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed (Tmax 2-3 hours). Bioavailability is unknown.
    - **Distribution:** ~99% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensive first-pass metabolism via CYP1A2, CYP2C19, and CYP3A4. Exhibits non-linear (Michaelis-Menten) kinetics.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (73% as metabolites). Half-life ranges from 4.5 to 13 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** STICLO France (n=41) and STICLO Italy (n=23).
- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled add-on trials in patients (age 3–20 years) with Dravet syndrome already on valproate and clobazam.
- **Outcomes:** The responder rate (≥50% reduction in seizures) was significantly higher for stiripentol (69.7% pooled) compared to placebo (6.5% pooled, p < 0.0001) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Conditions:** Keep in the original container to protect from light [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian]**.
