# Editorial Product Information: Stonefish Antivenom (Equine)

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. No documents from other jurisdictions (e.g., US, Europe, Canada, Japan) were available for comparison at the time of writing.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the available product information to assist healthcare professionals. It is designed for clinical reference and should not replace the official PI used for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.
- **Scope:** As only the Australian source was provided, jurisdictional variations are limited to this region. If other jurisdictions were to be added, differences in dosing, indications, and scheduling would be highlighted accordingly.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Stonefish Antivenom (equine).
- **Chemical Class:** Purified immunoglobulin (equine origin) prepared from the plasma of horses immunised against the venom of stonefish (*Synanceia verrucosa* and/or *Synanceia horrida*).
- **Brand Names:** **Stonefish Antivenom** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J06AA (Antivenoms - proposed).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Solution for injection. Each vial contains 2,000 units of antivenom in a volume that varies by batch (potency-dependent). Excipients include 2.2 mg phenol, 8 mg sodium chloride, and plasma protein of equine origin (≤ 170 mg/mL) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intramuscular (IM) injection (preferred) or Intravenous (IV) infusion (extreme cases).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Seqirus Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Stonefish Envenomation:** Indicated for the treatment of patients who, following a stonefish sting, exhibit systemic manifestations or experience severe local oedema and excruciating pain that does not respond to standard first aid measures [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
The dose is identical for adults and children and is determined by the number of visible puncture sites:
- **1 – 2 punctures:** 1 vial (2,000 units)
- **3 – 4 punctures:** 2 vials (4,000 units)
- **5 or more punctures:** 3 vials (6,000 units)

### Administration Instructions
- **Primary Route:** Intramuscular (IM) injection.
- **Alternative Route:** In extreme clinical scenarios, the antivenom may be given via intravenous infusion. The antivenom must be diluted 1:10 (e.g., in 0.9% sodium chloride or Hartmann’s Solution). 
- **Monitoring:** Patients must be monitored for at least 6 hours following administration. If initial dosing fails to control venom effects and the identity of the stonefish is confirmed, the dose should be repeated [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Absolute Contraindications:** There are no absolute contraindications to the use of stonefish antivenom when clear evidence of severe envenoming exists [Australian].
- **Relative Considerations:** The product should not be used in the absence of clear clinical evidence of severe envenoming.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Anaphylaxis and Hypersensitivity:** This product is derived from horse plasma. Severe, potentially fatal allergic reactions (anaphylaxis) may occur. Individuals with known allergies to horses or horse dander are at significantly increased risk.
- **Preparation for Emergency:** Adrenaline must be drawn up and ready for immediate use before any antivenom is administered. Resuscitation facilities must be available.
- **Atopy and Cross-Reactivity:** Patients with a history of atopy (e.g., allergies to cats, dogs, or sheep) or those who previously received equine-derived products (e.g., equine Tetanus Antitoxin) may exhibit cross-reactivity [Australian].
- **Serum Sickness:** Delayed serum sickness is common following the administration of foreign proteins. Symptoms include fever, rash, joint pain, and lymphadenopathy. 
- **Infectious Disease Transmission:** As a plasma-derived product, the risk of transmitting infectious agents cannot be entirely excluded, though there are no recorded cases of viral transmission for this product [Australian].
- **Skin Testing:** Pre-administration skin testing is not reliable and should not be performed.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions identified during post-approval use of equine antivenoms are categorized by frequency.

| System Organ Class | Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Immune System** | Common (≥1/100 to <1/10) | Allergic reactions, anaphylactic shock, delayed serum sickness |
| **Skin and Subcutaneous** | Common (≥1/100 to <1/10) | Urticaria, rash |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and delayed serum sickness (which may involve nephritis, neuropathy, or vasculitis in rare cases) are the most significant risks [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** No specific interaction data are available [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** There is no clinical data on the safety of stonefish antivenom in pregnant women. Prescribers should weigh the high risk of untreated envenoming against the potential risks of antivenom administration [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** No information is available regarding the excretion of the antivenom in human milk. A risk-benefit analysis is required [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is the same as for adults based on the number of puncture sites [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** The antivenom contains specific antibodies that neutralise the toxins produced by *Synanceia* species. Stonefish venom contains a permeability-increasing enzyme responsible for severe local oedema and potentially pulmonary oedema.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Stonefish venom is heat-labile and can cause excruciating local pain, hypotension, bradycardia, arrhythmias, muscle weakness, and paralysis [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Absorption, distribution, metabolism, and elimination profiles are not established in available sources.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Trials:** No specific clinical trial data were provided in the source documentation. Registration is based on historical use and the biological activity of the immunoglobulins.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store between 2°C and 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze).
- **Light:** Protect from light [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date printed on the packaging.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
