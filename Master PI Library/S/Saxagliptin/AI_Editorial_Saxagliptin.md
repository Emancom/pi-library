# Editorial Product Information: Saxagliptin and Metformin Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **Kombiglyze XR** (saxagliptin/metformin hydrochloride) for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official PI used for prescribing in specific jurisdictions. Clinicians should consult local regulations and the most current version of the approved PI for their region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Saxagliptin (as hydrochloride) and Metformin hydrochloride.
- **Chemical Class:** Saxagliptin is a dipeptidyl peptidase 4 (DPP-4) inhibitor; Metformin is a biguanide.
- **Brand Names:** **KOMBIGLYZE XR** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A10BD10.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Modified-release (XR) tablets [Australian]:
    - 5 mg saxagliptin / 500 mg metformin hydrochloride
    - 5 mg saxagliptin / 1000 mg metformin hydrochloride
    - 2.5 mg saxagliptin / 1000 mg metformin hydrochloride
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AstraZeneca Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus:** Indicated as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycaemic control in adults when treatment with both saxagliptin and metformin is appropriate [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** Dosage must be individualised based on the patient's current treatment, efficacy, and tolerability.
- **Maximum Daily Dose:** 5 mg saxagliptin and 2000 mg metformin XR [Australian].
- **Administration:**
    - Administer once daily with the evening meal.
    - Tablets must be swallowed whole; do not crush, cut, or chew.
    - Gradually titrate the metformin component to reduce gastrointestinal side effects.
- **Initial Combination Therapy:** Recommended starting dose is 5 mg/500 mg once daily. Inadequate control warrants titration of the metformin component [Australian].
- **Add-on Therapy:** If used as a fixed-dose combination, the recommended saxagliptin dose is 5 mg once daily, with metformin titrated up to 2000 mg daily [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not explicitly detailed in the source, but general clinical practice suggests avoiding double doses.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to active substances, excipients, or a history of serious hypersensitivity (e.g., anaphylaxis, angioedema) to any DPP-4 inhibitor [Australian].
- **Metabolic Acidosis:** Acute or chronic metabolic acidosis, including **lactic acidosis** or diabetic ketoacidosis (with or without coma) [Australian].
- **Renal Function:** Severe renal impairment (eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m²) [Australian].
- **Acute Conditions:** Conditions with the potential to alter renal function (e.g., dehydration, severe infection, shock, intravascular iodinated contrast) [Australian].
- **Tissue Hypoxia:** Acute or chronic diseases causing hypoxia (e.g., cardiac or respiratory failure, recent myocardial infarction, sepsis, pancreatitis) [Australian].
- **Other:** Hepatic impairment, acute alcohol intoxication/alcoholism, and lactation [Australian].
- **Surgery:** During or immediately following major surgery where insulin is required [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Lactic Acidosis:** A very rare but potentially fatal metabolic complication due to metformin accumulation. Risk factors include renal impairment, advanced age, poorly controlled diabetes, and excessive alcohol intake. Discontinue immediately if suspected [Australian].
- **Heart Failure:** A small increase in hospitalisation for heart failure was observed in the SAVOR clinical trial. Exercise caution in patients with known risk factors (e.g., history of heart failure, renal impairment) [Australian].
- **Pancreatitis:** Acute pancreatitis has been reported post-marketing. Patients should be advised of characteristic symptoms (persistent, severe abdominal pain). Discontinue if pancreatitis is suspected [Australian].
- **Renal Function Monitoring:** Assess eGFR prior to initiation and at least annually thereafter (more frequently in the elderly or those approaching eGFR 45 mL/min/1.73 m²) [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Serious reactions (anaphylaxis, angioedema) have occurred with saxagliptin. Discontinue use if suspected [Australian].
- **Vitamin B12 Deficiency:** Metformin may reduce B12 levels. Monitoring is recommended for patients with risk factors or signs of deficiency (e.g., anaemia, neuropathy) [Australian].
- **Bullous Pemphigoid:** Post-marketing cases requiring hospitalisation have been reported with DPP-4 inhibitors. Refer to a dermatologist if blisters/erosions occur [Australian].
- **Arthralgia:** Severe and disabling joint pain has been reported. Symptoms typically resolve upon discontinuation [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Saxagliptin
- **Common (1–10%):** Upper respiratory tract infection, urinary tract infection, headache, nasopharyngitis [Australian].
- **Uncertain Frequency:** Hypertension, abdominal pain, rash, increased creatine phosphokinase, anaemia, depression, and anxiety [Australian].
- **Serious:** Heart failure, pancreatitis, serious hypersensitivity, bullous pemphigoid.

### Metformin
- **Very Common (>10%):** Gastrointestinal symptoms (diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, loss of appetite). These are most common during treatment initiation [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Taste disturbance [Australian].
- **Very Rare (<0.01%):** **Lactic acidosis**, skin reactions (erythema, pruritus), decreased Vitamin B12 absorption [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Saxagliptin
- **CYP3A4/5 Inhibitors/Inducers:** Saxagliptin is primarily metabolised by CYP3A4/5. While inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole) increase saxagliptin levels and inducers (e.g., rifampicin) decrease them, the total active components (parent + metabolite) remain stable; thus, no dose adjustment is generally required [Australian].
### Metformin
- **Cationic Drugs:** (e.g., cimetidine, digoxin, ranitidine) may compete for renal transport systems, increasing metformin exposure. Close monitoring is advised [Australian].
- **Iodinated Contrast Media:** Intravascular administration can lead to renal failure and lactic acidosis. Discontinue **Kombiglyze XR** prior to or at the time of the procedure and withhold for 48 hours [Australian].
- **Alcohol:** Potentiates the effect of metformin on lactate metabolism [Australian].
- **Hyperglycaemic Agents:** (e.g., corticosteroids, thiazides, estrogens) may interfere with glycaemic control [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. There are no adequate studies in pregnant women. Use only if clearly needed. Saxagliptin and its metabolites cross the placenta in animal models.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Metformin is excreted into human milk; it is unknown if saxagliptin is excreted in human milk [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in animal studies at clinically relevant doses [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established in patients under 18 years [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Caution is warranted due to the increased likelihood of decreased renal function. Dose selection should be conservative and based on renal monitoring [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **eGFR 45–89 mL/min/1.73 m²:** No dose adjustment for the combination is required, though metformin titration should be cautious [Australian].
    - **eGFR 30–44 mL/min/1.73 m²:** Not recommended for initiation. If eGFR falls to this range during treatment, the maximum dose is 2.5 mg saxagliptin / 1000 mg metformin daily [Australian].
    - **eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m²:** Contraindicated [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated due to the risk of lactic acidosis associated with metformin [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:**
    - **Saxagliptin:** A competitive DPP-4 inhibitor that slows the inactivation of incretin hormones (GLP-1 and GIP), increasing insulin release and decreasing glucagon levels in a glucose-dependent manner.
    - **Metformin:** A biguanide that reduces hepatic glucose production, decreases intestinal absorption of glucose, and improves insulin sensitivity by increasing peripheral glucose uptake.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Saxagliptin:** Rapidly absorbed (Tmax ~2 hours). Metabolised via CYP3A4/5 to an active metabolite. Eliminated by both renal and hepatic pathways.
    - **Metformin XR:** Tmax is achieved at a median of 7 hours. Absorption is increased by ~50% when taken with food. Excreted unchanged in the urine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **SAVOR Trial:** A large cardiovascular outcomes trial (n=16,492) demonstrated that saxagliptin did not increase the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) compared to placebo, but was associated with an increased risk of hospitalisation for heart failure (3.5% vs 2.8%) [Australian].
- **Initial/Add-on Combination:** Multiple 24-week trials showed significant reductions in HbA1c, FPG, and PPG when saxagliptin was added to metformin or used as initial combination therapy compared to monotherapy or placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Handling:** Keep in original packaging to protect from moisture.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
