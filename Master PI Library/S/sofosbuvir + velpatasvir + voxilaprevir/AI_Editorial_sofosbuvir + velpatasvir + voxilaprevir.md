- Chemical class: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of chronic hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection in adult patients without cirrhosis or with compensated cirrhosis (Child‑Pugh A), who meet either of the following:
  - Genotype 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, or 6 infection and previously treated with an HCV regimen containing an NS5A inhibitor.
  - Genotype 1a or 3 infection and previously treated with a sofosbuvir-containing regimen that did not include an NS5A inhibitor.
- Note: In clinical data, additional benefit of this combination over sofosbuvir/velpatasvir was not shown for adults with genotype 1b, 2, 4, 5, or 6 previously treated with sofosbuvir without an NS5A inhibitor.
- Use in children and adolescents (< 18 years): Safety and effectiveness have not been established.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Recommended dose: one oral tablet once daily with food.
- Each tablet contains 400 mg sofosbuvir, 100 mg velpatasvir and 100 mg voxilaprevir. (Clinical dosing strength specified in source.)

### Dosing by indication
- Adults without cirrhosis or with compensated cirrhosis (Child‑Pugh A):
  - Prior NS5A inhibitor experience (any of genotypes 1–6): 12 weeks.
  - Prior sofosbuvir-containing regimen without an NS5A inhibitor: 12 weeks for genotype 1a or 3. (No demonstrated additional benefit versus sofosbuvir/velpatasvir for certain genotypes as noted in Section 3.)

### Administration instructions
- Take with food.
- Separate administration from antacids by 4 hours.
- H2‑receptor antagonists: may be given simultaneously or staggered at doses not exceeding those comparable to famotidine 40 mg twice daily.
- Proton‑pump inhibitors: doses comparable to omeprazole 20 mg may be administered with the regimen.

### Titration schedules
- Not applicable / Not specified in available sources.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to any of the active substances (sofosbuvir, velpatasvir, voxilaprevir) or to any excipient.
- Concomitant use with rifampicin (contraindicated).
- Concomitant use with rosuvastatin (contraindicated).
- Other coadministrations are explicitly not recommended in the source (see Section 8) but are not labelled as formal contraindications (e.g., concomitant cyclosporine is not recommended).

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources as a boxed warning; see serious risks below.

### Serious symptomatic bradycardia with amiodarone
- Coadministration of amiodarone with a sofosbuvir‑containing regimen has been associated with reports of symptomatic bradycardia, some requiring pacemaker intervention and including fatal cardiac arrest in isolated cases.
- Coadministration of amiodarone with this combination is **not recommended**.
- If no alternative and coadministration is deemed necessary:
  - Counsel patients regarding bradycardia risk.
  - Perform cardiac monitoring in an inpatient setting for the first 48 hours of coadministration; thereafter, daily outpatient or self‑monitoring of heart rate through at least the first 2 weeks of treatment.
  - Similar monitoring is recommended for patients already taking amiodarone who are to start this HCV regimen, and for patients discontinuing amiodarone shortly before starting therapy (due to amiodarone's long half‑life).
- Symptoms of bradycardia: near‑syncope/syncope, dizziness/lightheadedness, malaise, weakness, extreme tiredness, shortness of breath, chest pain, confusion, memory problems—seek immediate medical evaluation.

### Hepatitis B virus (HBV) reactivation
- Cases of HBV reactivation, including fulminant and fatal cases, have been reported during or after treatment with direct‑acting antivirals for HCV.
- Screen all patients for current or past HBV infection prior to initiating therapy (HBsAg and anti‑HBc).
- Patients with evidence of current or past HBV infection should be monitored and managed according to clinical guidelines; consider initiating HBV antiviral therapy when indicated.

### Hepatic decompensation and hepatic failure
- Not recommended in patients with moderate or severe hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh B or C).
- Post‑marketing cases of hepatic decompensation and hepatic failure, including fatalities, have been reported in cirrhotic patients treated with NS3/4A protease inhibitor‑containing regimens (including this combination).
- In patients with compensated cirrhosis or advanced liver disease, perform hepatic laboratory testing as clinically indicated and monitor for signs of decompensation (jaundice, ascites, encephalopathy, variceal haemorrhage).
- Discontinue therapy if hepatic decompensation develops.

### Dysglycaemia
- Diabetic patients may experience changes in glycaemic control, including symptomatic hypoglycaemia, during and after initiation of direct‑acting antiviral therapy.
- Monitor blood glucose closely, particularly during the first 3 months; adjust diabetic medications as needed and inform the clinician managing diabetes.

### Use with potent enzyme/transporter inducers
- Potent inducers of P‑gp and moderate to potent inducers of CYP2B6, CYP2C8, or CYP3A4 (e.g., St. John’s wort, carbamazepine) can substantially decrease plasma concentrations of one or more components, reducing efficacy. Concomitant use is **not recommended**.

### Other precautions
- Elderly: No dose adjustment necessary; response rates observed similar to younger patients in clinical trials.
- Paediatric use: Safety and efficacy not established in children < 18 years.
- Changes in hepatic function during treatment may affect drugs metabolised by the liver (e.g., vitamin K antagonists, calcineurin inhibitors); monitor and adjust concomitant medications as clinically indicated.

### Monitoring requirements
- HBV serology (HBsAg and anti‑HBc) before initiating therapy.
- Hepatic function tests before and during therapy in patients with cirrhosis or advanced liver disease.
- Blood glucose monitoring in diabetic patients, especially during the first 3 months.
- Cardiac monitoring if coadministered with amiodarone or when amiodarone cannot be avoided.
- Therapeutic drug monitoring of concomitant narrow therapeutic index drugs when clinically appropriate (e.g., digoxin, dabigatran, tacrolimus as indicated by concomitant therapy).

## 8. Drug Interactions
Note: Interactions described are based on studies with the combination or its components. The regimen components are substrates and inhibitors of multiple transporters and are variably metabolised by CYP enzymes; interactions can be pharmacokinetic and may lead to reduced efficacy or increased toxicity of concomitant drugs.

### Mechanistic pharmacokinetic notes
- Sofosbuvir: rapidly absorbed; undergoes extensive first‑pass hepatic extraction; metabolised to the active uridine nucleoside triphosphate; predominant circulating metabolite GS‑331007.
- GS‑331007: predominant circulating metabolite; not a substrate of P‑gp or BCRP.
- Velpatasvir: substrate of P‑gp and BCRP; inhibitor of P‑gp, BCRP, OATP1B1, OATP1B3 and OATP2B1; slow metabolic turnover primarily by CYP2B6, CYP2C8, and CYP3A4.
- Voxilaprevir: substrate of P‑gp, BCRP, OATP1B1 and OATP1B3; inhibitor of P‑gp, BCRP, OATP1B1 and OATP1B3; metabolised primarily by CYP3A4.
- Sofosbuvir and GS‑331007 are not inhibitors or inducers of CYP enzymes or UGT1A1; GS‑331007 is not an inhibitor of renal transporters OAT1, OAT3, OCT2 or MATE1.

### Clinically significant interactions and management
- Acid‑reducing agents:
  - Antacids (aluminium/magnesium hydroxide): separate administration by 4 hours.
  - H2‑receptor antagonists (e.g., famotidine): may be given simultaneously or staggered at doses not exceeding famotidine 40 mg twice daily.
  - Proton‑pump inhibitors (e.g., omeprazole): doses comparable to omeprazole 20 mg may be administered with the regimen.
  - Rationale: velpatasvir solubility and exposure decrease as gastric pH increases.

- Antiarrhythmics:
  - Amiodarone: coadministration is **not recommended** due to risk of serious symptomatic bradycardia. If unavoidable, implement inpatient cardiac monitoring for first 48 hours then daily heart‑rate checks through at least 2 weeks.

- Cardiac glycosides:
  - Digoxin: plasma concentrations may increase; use caution and monitor digoxin levels when coadministered.

- Anticoagulants:
  - Dabigatran etexilate: concentrations may be increased; use caution and clinically monitor dabigatran‑related effects.

- Anticonvulsants:
  - Carbamazepine, phenytoin, phenobarbital, oxcarbazepine: decrease exposures of sofosbuvir, velpatasvir and voxilaprevir — **coadministration not recommended**.

- Antimycobacterials:
  - Rifampicin: **contraindicated** (substantial reductions in sofosbuvir/velpatasvir exposures; complex effects on voxilaprevir). 
  - Rifabutin, rifapentine: coadministration **not recommended**.

- Antiretrovirals:
  - Atazanavir‑ or lopinavir‑containing regimens: **not recommended**.
  - Efavirenz‑containing regimens: **not recommended**.
  - Tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (tenofovir DF): coadministration may increase tenofovir exposure; monitor renal function and tenofovir‑associated adverse effects per tenofovir DF product information.

- Herbal supplements:
  - St. John’s wort: **coadministration not recommended** (potent inducer of P‑gp and CYP pathways; decreased antiviral exposure).

- HMG‑CoA reductase inhibitors (statins):
  - Atorvastatin: concentrations may increase; limit dose to no more than 20 mg when coadministered.
  - Pravastatin: concentrations may increase; limit dose to no more than 40 mg.
  - Rosuvastatin: **coadministration contraindicated**.
  - Fluvastatin, lovastatin, simvastatin: concentrations may increase; use lowest approved statin dose. If higher doses required, weigh risk vs benefit due to myopathy/rhabdomyolysis risk.

- Immunosuppressants:
  - Cyclosporine: substantially increases voxilaprevir exposure; **coadministration not recommended**.

- Oral contraceptives:
  - Ethinyl estradiol–containing products: may increase risk of ALT elevations; consider monitoring ALT. (Note: some clinical pharmacology data indicated no clinically significant PK interaction with certain contraceptives but clinical signal for ALT elevation has been reported; monitor and exercise clinical judgment.)

### Drugs reported without clinically significant interactions in studies
- Based on available pharmacology studies, no clinically significant interactions observed or expected with: cobicistat, darunavir, dolutegravir, elvitegravir, emtricitabine, gemfibrozil, ketoconazole, methadone, raltegravir, rilpivirine, ritonavir, tacrolimus, tenofovir alafenamide, voriconazole. (Clinical monitoring still advised when combining therapies that may be affected by changes in hepatic function.)

### Assessment and special considerations
- Coadministered drugs that are CYP or transporter inhibitors may increase exposures of velpatasvir or voxilaprevir; potent OATP inhibitors are **not recommended** with this regimen.
- Drugs that inhibit P‑gp and/or BCRP may increase concentrations of the regimen components; clinical monitoring is advised.
- Changes in hepatic function during HCV treatment can alter exposures of concomitant drugs metabolised by the liver (e.g., vitamin K antagonists, calcineurin inhibitors) — monitor therapeutic effect and laboratory parameters.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Children and adolescents < 18 years: Safety and effectiveness have not been established. No dosing recommendation available.

### Elderly
- Clinical studies included elderly patients (≥ 65 years; 189 patients, ~17% of phase 2/3 population). Observed response rates were similar to younger patients. No dose adjustment warranted based on age.

### Renal impairment
- Mild to moderate renal impairment: No dose adjustment required.
- Severe renal impairment (eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m2) and end‑stage renal disease requiring haemodialysis: Safety and efficacy have not been established; use not recommended / not specified — data lacking.

### Hepatic impairment
- Mild hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh A): No dose adjustment required.
- Moderate to severe hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh B or C): **Not recommended**. Post‑marketing reports of hepatic decompensation/failure primarily in cirrhotic patients treated with NS3/4A protease inhibitor–containing regimens.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Not specified in available sources (mechanistic classes of individual components not described in provided text).

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: Sofosbuvir is rapidly absorbed after oral administration. Administration with food is recommended.
- Metabolism and major circulating moieties:
  - Sofosbuvir is metabolised intracellularly to the active uridine nucleoside analogue triphosphate; dephosphorylation yields the predominant circulating metabolite GS‑331007.
  - Velpatasvir undergoes slow metabolic turnover primarily via CYP2B6, CYP2C8, and CYP3A4.
  - Voxilaprevir undergoes metabolism primarily via CYP3A4.
- Transporters:
  - Sofosbuvir, velpatasvir, and voxilaprevir are substrates of P‑gp and BCRP.
  - Voxilaprevir and velpatasvir are substrates of OATP1B1 and OATP1B3 (voxilaprevir more so).
  - Velpatasvir inhibits OATP2B1 in vitro.
  - Velpatasvir and voxilaprevir inhibit P‑gp, BCRP, OATP1B1 and OATP1B3; velpatasvir additionally inhibits OATP2B1.
- GS‑331007:
  - Not a substrate of P‑gp or BCRP; not an inhibitor of OAT1, OAT3, OCT2 or MATE1; not an inhibitor/inducer of CYP or UGT1A1 enzymes.
- Elimination half‑lives, absolute bioavailability, and other numerical PK parameters: Not specified in available sources.
