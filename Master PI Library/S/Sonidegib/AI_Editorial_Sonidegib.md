# Editorial Product Information: Sonidegib

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of sonidegib for healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single, structured reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved PI for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Sonidegib (as diphosphate).
- **Chemical Class:** Hedgehog pathway inhibitor; Smoothened (Smo) antagonist.
- **Brand Name(s):** Odomzo [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01XJ03.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Hard gelatin capsules containing 200 mg of sonidegib (as diphosphate). The capsule is opaque pink and contains white to off-white powder and granules [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sun Pharma ANZ Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Basal Cell Carcinoma (BCC):** Treatment of adult patients with locally advanced basal cell carcinoma (laBCC) who are not suitable candidates for curative surgery or radiation therapy [Australian].
- **Metastatic BCC:** Treatment of adult patients with metastatic basal cell carcinoma (mBCC) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Standard Dose:** 200 mg taken orally once daily.
- **Administration:** Must be taken on an empty stomach, at least one hour before or two hours after a meal. Capsules should be swallowed whole and must not be crushed or chewed.

### Missed Dose Instructions
- If a dose is missed, it may be taken if within 6 hours of the scheduled time. 
- If more than 6 hours have elapsed, the dose should be skipped, and the next dose taken at the regular time.

### Dose Modifications for Toxicity
Therapy may require temporary interruption or dose reduction (to 200 mg every other day) based on the severity of muscle-related adverse events and Creatine Phosphokinase (CK) elevations:
- **Grade 1 CK elevation:** Continue at the same dose; monitor CK weekly.
- **Grade 2 (without renal impairment):** Interrupt treatment until resolution to baseline. Resume at 200 mg daily. If symptoms recur, resume at 200 mg every other day.
- **Grade 3/4 (without renal impairment):** Interrupt treatment until resolution to baseline. Resume at 200 mg every other day.
- **Renal Impairment (Serum Cr > ULN) with CK elevation:** Interrupt treatment. If CK and creatinine return to baseline, consider resuming at 200 mg every other day; otherwise, discontinue permanently [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy:** Sonidegib is contraindicated in women who are pregnant or planning to become pregnant due to the risk of severe birth defects or fetal death [Australian].
- **Lactation:** Use is contraindicated during breastfeeding [Australian].
- **Childbearing Potential:** Contraindicated in women of childbearing potential unless two reliable methods of contraception are used during treatment and for 20 months following the final dose [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Sonidegib is highly teratogenic. It can cause embryo-fetal death or severe midline defects, limb malformations, and craniofacial anomalies. Pregnancy must be excluded within 7 days before starting treatment and monitored monthly [Australian].
- **Muscle-Related Adverse Events:** Cases of muscle spasms, myalgia, and myopathy (including rare reports of rhabdomyolysis) have occurred. CK levels must be checked at baseline and as clinically indicated (e.g., if symptoms occur).
- **Blood Donation:** Patients must not donate blood during therapy and for at least 20 months after the last dose to prevent potential exposure to a pregnant transfusion recipient [Australian].
- **Neuromuscular Disorders:** Use with caution in patients with conditions such as muscular dystrophy or spinal muscular atrophy due to increased risk of muscle toxicity.
- **Premature Epiphyseal Fusion:** Reported in paediatric patients exposed to Hedgehog pathway inhibitors; sonidegib is not indicated for paediatric use [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are frequent and often lead to dose modification or discontinuation.
- **Very Common (>10%):** Muscle spasms (54%), alopecia (53%), dysgeusia (46%), fatigue (42%), nausea (39%), musculoskeletal pain (33%), diarrhoea (32%), weight decrease (30%), decreased appetite (23%), myalgia (19%), abdominal pain (18%), headache (15%), vomiting (11%), and pruritus (10%) [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Dyspepsia, constipation, gastroesophageal reflux, rash, abnormal hair growth, dehydration, and myopathy.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** 
    - **Rhabdomyolysis:** Although rare at the 200 mg dose, it is a significant clinical risk.
    - **Grade 3/4 Laboratory Abnormalities:** Increased lipase (12%) and increased blood CK (8%) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** (e.g., ketoconazole, ritonavir) Increase sonidegib exposure. Avoid concomitant use; if necessary, monitor closely for muscle toxicity and consider dose reduction.
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inducers:** (e.g., rifampicin, St John’s Wort, carbamazepine) Decrease sonidegib exposure. Avoid concomitant use. If required, the sonidegib dose may be increased by 200 mg increments up to a maximum of 800 mg [Australian].
- **Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs):** (e.g., esomeprazole) May decrease sonidegib AUC by approximately 32%. The clinical impact is not fully determined.
- **BCRP Substrates:** Sonidegib is a BCRP inhibitor. Monitor for adverse reactions when using substrates with a narrow therapeutic index (e.g., methotrexate, rosuvastatin).
- **Muscle-Toxic Agents:** Increased risk of muscle events when used with HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors, fibric acid derivatives, or hydroxychloroquine [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category X. Must not be used. Fetal exposure through maternal ingestion or seminal fluid is a high risk.
- **Contraception (Females):** Two methods of contraception (one highly effective and one barrier) required during and for 20 months after treatment.
- **Contraception (Males):** Must use condoms (with spermicide) during intercourse and for 6 months after treatment, even if vasectomised. Men must not father a child or donate semen during this period [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety not established; potential for serious adverse reactions in infants. Breastfeeding is prohibited during and for 20 months after therapy.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients under 18 years have not been established. There is a risk of premature epiphyseal fusion [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific dose adjustment is required for patients aged 65 and older.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is recommended for patients with any degree of renal impairment, as renal excretion of sonidegib is negligible [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for mild, moderate, or severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh A, B, or C) [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Sonidegib binds to and inhibits the Smoothened (Smo) receptor, a G protein-coupled-like molecule. This prevents the activation of the Hedgehog signaling pathway and subsequent nuclear translocation of GLI transcription factors, thereby inhibiting the growth of BCC cells.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Poorly absorbed (6–7% at 800 mg). Bioavailability increases significantly (7.4 to 7.8-fold) with high-fat meals.
    - **Distribution:** Highly bound to plasma proteins (>97%). Volume of distribution is large (9170 L), with skin concentrations 6-fold higher than plasma.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by CYP3A4.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily hepatic/fecal (93.4%). The elimination half-life is approximately 28 days [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study A2201:** A Phase II, randomised, double-blind trial compared 200 mg and 800 mg daily doses. In the 200 mg cohort (n=79), the Objective Response Rate (ORR) by central review was 56.1% for laBCC and 7.7% for mBCC. Long-term follow-up (30 months) showed a median duration of response of 26.1 months for laBCC [Australian]. The 200 mg dose demonstrated a better safety profile than the 800 mg dose with similar efficacy.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Conditions:** Protect from moisture. Store in the original package.
- **Handling:** Capsules must not be opened due to the risk of teratogenicity to the handler [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4) [Australian].
