# Editorial Product Information: Sevelamer Carbonate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of sevelamer carbonate for healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, dosing protocols, and safety profiles into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory guidelines and specific product labelling before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Sevelamer carbonate (anion exchange resin).
- **Brand Name(s):** ARX-Sevelamer [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** V03AE02.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 800 mg film-coated tablets. (Note: Powder for oral suspension may be available via other brands) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hyperphosphataemia:** Management of elevated serum phosphorus levels in adult patients with stage 4 and 5 chronic kidney disease (CKD) [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Sevelamer carbonate must be administered **three times daily with meals**. The dosage is individualised based on serum phosphorus levels.

### Starting Dose
For patients not currently taking a phosphate binder, the starting dose is determined by the baseline serum phosphorus level:
- **Serum Phosphorus > 1.78 and < 2.42 mmol/L:** 2.4 g daily (one 800 mg tablet TID).
- **Serum Phosphorus ≥ 2.42 mmol/L:** 4.8 g daily (two 800 mg tablets TID).

### Switching from Calcium-Based Binders
When transitioning from calcium carbonate, the dose should be matched on a tablet-per-meal basis (e.g., 1 tablet of 500 mg calcium carbonate per meal corresponds to 1 tablet of 800 mg sevelamer carbonate per meal) [Australian].

### Titration and Maintenance
- **Frequency:** Titrate every 2–4 weeks until target phosphorus levels are achieved.
- **Adjustment:** Increase or decrease by one tablet (800 mg) per meal at two-week intervals as necessary.
- **Target Range:** 1.13 – 1.78 mmol/L [Australian].
- **Average Maintenance Dose:** Approximately 6 g per day.

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets must be **swallowed whole** with water.
- **Do not crush, chew, or break** tablets, as the contents expand in water.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to sevelamer carbonate or any tablet excipients.
- Hypophosphataemia.
- Bowel obstruction.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Gastrointestinal Disorders:** Safety has not been established in patients with dysphagia, swallowing disorders, severe GI motility disorders (e.g., severe constipation), or major GI surgery. Use with caution in these populations.
- **GI Inflammatory Disorders:** Serious cases of bleeding, perforation, ulceration, necrosis, and colitis have been reported, sometimes associated with the presence of sevelamer crystals. Re-evaluate treatment if severe GI symptoms develop.
- **Intestinal Obstruction/Ileus:** Rare cases of obstruction and ileus/subileus have been reported. Constipation may be a precursor; monitor constipated patients closely.
- **Vitamin Deficiency:** Sevelamer may bind fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K). Monitoring of vitamin levels and supplementation is recommended, particularly for patients not taking vitamin supplements or those on peritoneal dialysis. 
- **Monitoring Requirements:**
    - Serum phosphorus (regularly).
    - Serum calcium and bicarbonate levels.
    - Vitamin A, D, E, and K status (including thromboplastin time for Vitamin K).
    - TSH levels (in patients on levothyroxine).

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are primarily gastrointestinal in nature and are generally mild to moderate.

### Serious/Post-Marketing Adverse Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Intestinal perforation, ileus, intestinal obstruction.
- **Skin:** Pruritus, rash, hypersensitivity reactions.
- **Vascular:** Arteriovenous fistula site complications (haemorrhage, thrombosis) have been observed in dialysis populations.

## 7. Drug Interactions
Sevelamer carbonate is not absorbed systemically but may bind other medications in the GI tract, reducing their bioavailability.

- **General Rule:** Administer other medicinal products at least **one hour before** or **three hours after** sevelamer carbonate. Alternatively, monitor blood levels of the co-administered drug.
- **Ciprofloxacin:** Bioavailability reduced by approximately 50%. Do not take simultaneously.
- **Immunosuppressants:** Reduced levels of cyclosporin, mycophenolate mofetil, and tacrolimus have been reported. Monitor concentrations closely during use and after discontinuation.
- **Levothyroxine:** Rare cases of increased TSH have been reported. Monitor TSH levels closely.
- **Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs):** Very rare reports of increased phosphate levels when co-administered.
- **No Clinically Significant Interaction:** Digoxin, warfarin, enalapril, and metoprolol (based on healthy volunteer studies).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B3]. Safety has not been established. Use only if clearly needed and after a careful risk/benefit analysis. Animal studies at high doses showed reduced foetal bone ossification, likely secondary to maternal vitamin D depletion.
- **Breastfeeding:** Excretion into human milk is unlikely due to the non-absorbed nature of the drug. Use only if the benefit outweighs the potential risk.
- **Fertility:** No human data; animal studies showed no impairment of fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients under 18 years of age have not been established.
- **Elderly Use:** Specific data not specified in available sources, though trials included elderly patients.
- **Renal Impairment:** Indicated for Stage 4 and 5 CKD; no specific dose adjustment required other than titration to phosphorus levels.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No specific data or dose adjustments provided in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A non-absorbed, calcium-free, metal-free anion exchange resin. It contains multiple amines that become protonated in the intestine, binding dietary phosphorus through ionic and hydrogen bonding. This reduces phosphate absorption and lowers serum phosphorus levels.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Reduces the incidence of hypercalcaemic episodes compared to calcium-based binders. Also binds bile acids, which may lower LDL and total serum cholesterol (observed declines of 15–39% after 2 weeks).
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Not systemically absorbed. A mass balance study showed no absorption in healthy volunteers. In animal models, >94% was excreted in faeces.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Cross-Over Studies:** Trials (e.g., GTC-36-301) demonstrated that sevelamer is as effective as calcium acetate in reducing serum phosphorus (average reduction of ~0.65 mmol/L). 
- **Safety Profile:** Sevelamer treated patients had a significantly lower risk of hypercalcaemia (5–7%) compared to those on calcium-based binders (22–34%).
- **Dosing Regimen:** A study (GD3-199-301) comparing once-daily powder to TID tablets found that phosphorus control was superior with the TID regimen.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C.
- **Handling:** Protect from moisture and keep the container tightly closed. Do not refrigerate.
