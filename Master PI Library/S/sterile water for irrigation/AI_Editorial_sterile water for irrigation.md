# Editorial Product Information: sterile water for irrigation

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **sterile water for irrigation**
- Chemical class: Water; sterile, non‑pyrogenic, hypotonic, solute‑free aqueous solution
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Irrigation only — **Not for injection**
- Additional identification: Intended for veterinary use only (for use by or on the order of a licensed veterinarian)

## 3. Approved Indications
- Indicated as a sterile, non‑pyrogenic irrigating fluid for washing, rinsing and dilution procedures in animals where a sterile, solute‑free solution is appropriate.
- Intended uses include cleansing of body cavities, tissues, wounds, and surgical sites during diagnostic and operative procedures.
- May be used as a diluent or vehicle for compatible veterinary drugs intended for irrigation.
- For veterinary use only. For irrigation only. Not for injection.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult animals)
- Typical irrigation volumes: range from 50 mL to 1000 mL depending on the size of the cavity or wound and the nature of the procedure.
- Dose is dependent upon the capacity or surface area of the structure to be irrigated and the nature of the procedure.

### Dosing by indication
- Specific dose by indication: Not specified in available sources beyond the typical volume range (50–1000 mL).

### Administration instructions
- Use aseptic technique for preparation and handling.
- Inspect the solution visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- Administer slowly and avoid excessive pressure to minimize the risk of systemic absorption.
- When additives are introduced, mix thoroughly using aseptic technique and inspect the final solution for cloudiness or precipitation immediately after mixing and periodically during use.
- Do not store any solution containing additives; discard any unused portion after opening (single‑use container).
- If incompatibility (e.g., cloudiness, precipitation) is suspected after mixing, do not use the mixture.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- None specified as a boxed warning in the available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- **Hypotonic solution — risk of hemolysis:** The solution is hypotonic and may cause hemolysis if absorbed into the vascular compartment.
- **Risk of electrolyte disturbance and water intoxication:** Because the solution is solute‑free and hypotonic, systemic absorption of large volumes—particularly under pressure—can cause hyponatremia, hypoosmolality, water intoxication and fluid overload.
- **Not for injection:** Do not administer intravenously or by any parenteral route.
- **Single‑use container:** Discard any unused portion promptly after opening to minimize contamination or pyrogen formation.
- **Inspection prior to use:** Inspect solution and container for leaks, particulate matter, discoloration, or compromised seals before use.
- **Use of additives:** Some additives may be incompatible. Known incompatible additives include strong oxidizing agents, alcohol‑containing solutions, and solutions with high electrolyte concentrations. Verify compatibility before mixing; if incompatibility is suspected, do not use.
- **Surgical use caution:** Although the product is described for cleansing during diagnostic and operative procedures, the product is also noted as not recommended for use during surgical procedures (see Warnings). Clinician judgement and institutional practice should guide use in operative settings.
- **For veterinary use only:** Federal/regulatory restrictions limit use to veterinary practice.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor for signs of excessive systemic absorption: check serum electrolytes (particularly sodium), fluid balance, respiratory status, and neurologic status when clinically indicated.
- If clinically significant absorption is suspected, discontinue irrigation and institute corrective measures (see Overdosage).

### Specific clinical precautions
- Administer slowly and avoid excessive pressure to reduce absorption risk.
- Maintain aseptic conditions throughout handling and administration.
- When additives are introduced, inspect immediately and periodically for cloudiness or precipitation.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- The primary action is mechanical: acts as an irrigating fluid to mechanically cleanse body cavities, tissues, wounds, and surgical sites by removal of debris, blood and contaminants.
- As a hypotonic, solute‑free solution, systemic absorption can produce osmotic and electrolyte effects (e.g., hyponatremia, hemolysis).

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: Systemic absorption can occur if irrigation fluid enters the vascular compartment (risk increased with use under pressure or large volumes). Extent and rate of absorption are not quantified in the available sources.
- Distribution, metabolism, elimination: Not specified in available sources beyond clinical sequelae of systemic absorption (electrolyte disturbances, hypoosmolality).

### Pharmacodynamics
- Mechanical cleansing with potential osmotic effects if systemically absorbed. Specific PD parameters (e.g., concentration‑effect curves) are not provided in available sources.
