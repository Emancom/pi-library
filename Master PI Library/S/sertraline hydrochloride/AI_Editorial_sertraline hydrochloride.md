# Editorial Product Information: sertraline hydrochloride

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **sertraline hydrochloride**
- Chemical class: Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 1991 [US]
- Date of last revision/update of provided source PI: August 2023 [US]
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources
- Other jurisdictions' registration or revision dates: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Major depressive disorder (MDD)
- Obsessive‑compulsive disorder (OCD)
- Panic disorder (PD)
- Posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD)
- Social anxiety disorder (SAD)
- Premenstrual dysphoric disorder (PMDD)

- Pediatric approvals / limitations:
  - OCD: efficacy and safety established in pediatric patients (specific ages and dosing below).
  - For pediatric patients outside OCD, safety and effectiveness have not been established. (Statement from source PI)

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General adult dosing
- Typical starting therapeutic dose: 25 mg or 50 mg once daily depending on indication and patient factors.
- Usual dose range (adults): up to 200 mg once daily for most indications.
- Maximum daily doses by indication (as specified in source):
  - MDD, OCD, PD, PTSD, SAD: up to 200 mg/day.
  - PMDD (continuous dosing): recommended starting 50 mg/day; therapeutic doses up to 150 mg/day.
  - PMDD (intermittent luteal-phase dosing): 50 mg/day during luteal phase (starting 14 days prior to menses and continuing through onset); may increase up to 100 mg/day during luteal phase if needed.

### Initial and indication-specific starting doses (adults)
- MDD: start 50 mg/day; titrate as needed (see below).
- OCD (adult): start 50 mg/day.
- Panic disorder, PTSD, social anxiety disorder: start 25 mg/day (adults); may increase.
- PMDD: start 50 mg/day (continuous or intermittent as above).

### Titration
- If an inadequate response to starting dose, increase in 25–50 mg increments once weekly for MDD, OCD, PD, PTSD, and SAD (based on tolerability and 24‑hour elimination half‑life).
- For PMDD continuous dosing: increases of 50 mg per menstrual cycle may be considered up to 150 mg/day.
- For PMDD intermittent (luteal-phase) dosing: escalation strategy described in PMDD section — increasing to 100 mg/day during luteal phase if 50 mg inadequate (specific schedule: 50 mg/day for first 3 days of dosing then 100 mg/day for remaining days of dosing cycle when used intermittently).

### Pediatric dosing (OCD)
- Ages 6–12 years: start 25 mg/day; therapeutic range up to 50–200 mg/day (titration as tolerated).
- Ages 13–17 years: start 50 mg/day; therapeutic range up to 200 mg/day (titration as tolerated).
- Safety and effectiveness in pediatric patients have been established for OCD only; other pediatric indications: not established.

### Hepatic impairment
- Mild hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh 5–6): recommended starting dose and usual therapeutic dose are one‑half the recommended adult dose.
- Moderate (Child‑Pugh 7–9) or severe (Child‑Pugh 10–15) hepatic impairment: use not recommended.

### Renal impairment
- Dose adjustment instructions: Not specified in available sources.

### Switching to/from MAOI-type agents
- At least 14 days must elapse between discontinuation of an MAOI antidepressant and initiation of sertraline.
- At least 14 days must elapse after stopping sertraline before starting an MAOI antidepressant.

### Discontinuation
- Gradually reduce dose rather than abrupt cessation whenever possible to reduce risk of discontinuation symptoms (see Warnings and Adverse Effects).

### Oral solution administration (when using concentrated solution)
- Oral solution concentration reported in source: sertraline hydrochloride equivalent to 20 mg/mL (contains alcohol).
- Oral solution must be diluted prior to administration.
- Use supplied calibrated measuring device (dropper with 25 mg and 50 mg graduation marks in the source PI).
- Dilute with 4 ounces (½ cup) of water, ginger ale, lemon/lime soda, lemonade or orange juice only; mix immediately and instruct patient to take immediately after mixing.
- Oral solution contains alcohol; contraindicated with disulfiram (see Contraindications).

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources

## 5. Contraindications
- Concomitant use with a monoamine oxidase inhibitor (MAOI), or use within 14 days of stopping an MAOI (including MAOI activity of linezolid or intravenous methylene blue): contraindicated.
- Initiation of an MAOI (including linezolid or intravenous methylene blue) in a patient currently taking sertraline: contraindicated unless appropriate washout (14 days) has occurred.
- Concomitant use with pimozide: contraindicated (risk of QT prolongation/ventricular arrhythmias).
- Known hypersensitivity to sertraline or to any of the product excipients (e.g., anaphylaxis, angioedema).
- Oral solution only: concomitant use with disulfiram (oral solution contains alcohol and may precipitate disulfiram‑alcohol reaction).

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed warning
- **Suicidal thoughts and behaviors**: Antidepressants, including sertraline, have been associated with an increased risk of suicidal ideation and behavior in pediatric and young adult patients in short‑term studies. Close monitoring for clinical worsening and emergence of suicidal thoughts and behaviors is required, particularly during the initial months of therapy and following dose changes. Counsel caregivers to monitor for behavioral changes and to contact prescriber promptly. [US]

### General warnings and precautions
- **Serotonin syndrome**: Risk increased with concomitant use of other serotonergic drugs (e.g., triptans, other SSRIs/SNRIs, tricyclic antidepressants, fentanyl, lithium, tramadol, meperidine, methadone, tryptophan, buspirone, amphetamines, St. John’s Wort) and with drugs that impair serotonin metabolism (e.g., MAOIs). Serotonin syndrome can be life‑threatening. If suspected, discontinue sertraline and any concomitant serotonergic agents and provide supportive treatment.
- **Increased risk of bleeding**: SSRIs including sertraline can increase bleeding risk. Concomitant use of aspirin, NSAIDs, other antiplatelet agents, warfarin, or other anticoagulants may further increase bleeding risk. Monitor INR closely in patients on warfarin.
- **Activation of mania/hypomania**: Screen for personal or family history of bipolar disorder prior to initiating; antidepressants may precipitate mania/hypomania.
- **Discontinuation symptoms**: Abrupt discontinuation can cause adverse events (see Adverse Effects: discontinuation syndrome). Advise gradual taper when stopping.
- **Seizures**: Use with caution in patients with a seizure disorder. Patients with history of seizures were generally excluded from clinical trials.
- **Angle‑closure glaucoma**: Antidepressant‑induced pupillary dilation can precipitate an angle‑closure attack in patients with anatomically narrow angles. Avoid use in patients with untreated anatomically narrow angles.
- **Hyponatremia / SIADH**: Cases, including severe (serum sodium <110 mmol/L), have been reported. Elderly patients, patients on diuretics, and those volume‑depleted may be at higher risk.
- **False‑positive benzodiazepine urine immunoassay**: Sertraline may cause false‑positive benzodiazepine urine screens; confirmatory testing (e.g., GC/MS) recommended.
- **QTc prolongation / Torsades de Pointes**: Post‑marketing reports exist; a positive relationship between QTc and serum sertraline concentration was observed in a thorough QT study. Use caution in patients with risk factors for QT prolongation.
- **Sexual dysfunction**: SSRIs including sertraline may cause decreased libido, delayed or absent orgasm, ejaculatory delay or failure, erectile dysfunction. Assess sexual function prior to and during treatment.

### Monitoring recommendations
- Monitor for emergence of suicidal ideation, worsening depression, or unusual changes in behavior, especially in children, adolescents, and young adults.
- Monitor serum sodium in patients at risk for hyponatremia.
- Monitor coagulation parameters (e.g., INR) when coadministered with warfarin.
- Monitor for signs of serotonin syndrome when given with other serotonergic agents.
- Monitor ECG and electrolytes as clinically indicated in patients at risk for QT prolongation.

## 7. Adverse Effects

- The following adverse reactions are reported in the provided source PI text. Frequencies are reported where specified in available sources. For many reactions, only occurrence or relative incidence vs placebo is provided; where numerical frequency is not provided, frequency is listed as "Not specified in available sources."

- Overview from pooled randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trials (adults; mostly 50–200 mg/day): N=3,066 sertraline‑exposed patients (8–12 weeks; 568 patient‑years). Most common adverse reactions (≥5% and at least twice placebo) across pooled trials: nausea, diarrhea/loose stools, tremor, dyspepsia, decreased appetite, hyperhidrosis, ejaculation failure, decreased libido.

## 8. Drug Interactions

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Concomitant serotonergic agents (e.g., other SSRIs/SNRIs, triptans, tricyclic antidepressants, fentanyl, lithium, tramadol, meperidine, methadone, buspirone, tryptophan, amphetamines, St. John’s Wort): increased risk of serotonin syndrome; monitor closely and avoid where clinically appropriate.
- Monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs), including linezolid and intravenous methylene blue: concomitant use contraindicated because of risk of serotonin syndrome; appropriate washout periods required (14 days).
- Pimozide: concomitant use contraindicated due to risk of QT prolongation and ventricular arrhythmias.
- Antiplatelet agents / NSAIDs / aspirin / warfarin / other anticoagulants: increased bleeding risk when coadministered with sertraline; monitor for bleeding and INR when applicable.

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Sertraline may affect CYP2D6 substrates: dosage reduction of coadministered CYP2D6 substrate drugs may be required (source PI advises to reduce dosage of drugs metabolized by CYP2D6).
- Protein‑bound drugs: sertraline may affect exposure to other highly protein‑bound drugs; monitor for adverse effects and consider dose adjustments of sertraline or concomitant drug (e.g., warfarin) as warranted.
- Specific interaction with disulfiram: oral solution contains alcohol — concomitant use contraindicated (disulfiram‑alcohol reaction).

### Laboratory interactions
- False‑positive urine benzodiazepine immunoassay results have been reported with sertraline; use confirmatory testing (e.g., GC/MS) when needed.

### Management notes
- When combining sertraline with drugs that increase bleeding risk, weigh risks/benefits and monitor for bleeding.
- When combining with CYP2D6 substrates that have narrow therapeutic indices, consider dose reduction and monitor for toxicity.
- If serotonin syndrome is suspected, discontinue sertraline and other serotonergic agents and initiate supportive and symptomatic treatment.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

### Pregnancy
- Use in the third trimester: use of sertraline in late pregnancy may increase the risk of persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn (PPHN) and may be associated with neonatal withdrawal symptoms after birth.
- Exposure to SSRIs in the month before delivery has been associated with a less‑than‑twofold increase in risk of postpartum hemorrhage in observational studies.
- Formal pregnancy risk category (regulatory category systems) is not specified in the provided source PI text for other jurisdictions.
- Detailed embryofetal safety data and specific recommendations: Not specified in available sources beyond the third‑trimester PPHN / neonatal withdrawal and postpartum hemorrhage statements.

## 10. Use in Special Populations

## 11. Pharmacology

### Mechanism of action
- **sertraline hydrochloride** is a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI). (Mechanism described at a high level in source PI.)

### Pharmacokinetics
- Elimination half‑life: approximately 24 hours (used to inform weekly titration intervals).
- Relationship between serum concentration and QTc interval: a positive relationship between sertraline serum concentration and QTc interval was observed in a thorough QT study.
- Sertraline can interact with CYP2D6‑metabolized drugs (inhibitory effect observed); protein‑binding interactions may be clinically relevant.
- Detailed ADME (absorption, full distribution parameters, metabolic pathways, specific enzyme isoforms and clearance values): Not specified in available sources beyond the points above.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Primary pharmacodynamic action is inhibition of serotonin reuptake at the presynaptic neuronal membrane, increasing serotonin availability in the synaptic cleft. (High‑level statement from source PI.)
- Pharmacodynamic adverse effects include potential for serotonin syndrome when combined with other serotonergic agents.

## 12. Clinical Trials

### Pivotal and pooled safety data summaries (from provided PI text)
- Pooled randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled clinical trials across MDD, OCD, PD, PTSD, SAD, and PMDD included 3,066 sertraline‑treated adults (exposure mostly 50–200 mg/day, 8–12 weeks), representing approximately 568 patient‑years of exposure. Mean age ≈ 40 years; 57% female.
- Most common adverse reactions in pooled adult trials (≥5% and at least twice placebo): nausea, diarrhea/loose stools, tremor, dyspepsia, decreased appetite, hyperhidrosis, ejaculation failure, decreased libido.
- Pediatric suicidality data: pooled analyses of placebo‑controlled trials of antidepressant drugs (multiple agents) including ~77,000 adult patients and >4,400 pediatric patients showed higher incidence of suicidal thoughts and behaviors in pediatric and young adult antidepressant‑treated patients vs placebo. (No suicides occurred in pediatric trials; number of suicides in adult trials insufficient to determine effect.)
- Trial design notes: trials were randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled; duration varied by indication (typically 8–12 weeks for acute treatment trials).
- Indication‑specific efficacy trial details, primary endpoints, and individual trial efficacy results: Not specified in the available excerpt beyond indication listings and pooled safety/AE summaries.
