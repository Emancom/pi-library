# Editorial Product Information: Ezetimibe and Simvastatin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following jurisdictional Product Information (PI) source: **Australian (TGA)**: "Product Information — AKM Ezetimibe Simvastatin".
- **Purpose**: To provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals (HCPs) based on available regulatory documentation. 
- **Disclaimer**: This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Not all possible global variations may be represented if source documents from other regions (e.g., US, Europe) were not provided.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients**: **Ezetimibe** (cholesterol absorption inhibitor) and **Simvastatin** (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor).
- **Chemical Class**: Lipid-modifying combination; HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor and a selective cholesterol absorption inhibitor.
- **Brand Names**: **AKM Ezetimibe Simvastatin** [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: C10BA02.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**: Oral tablets in four fixed-dose combinations: 10 mg/10 mg, 10 mg/20 mg, 10 mg/40 mg, and 10 mg/80 mg (ezetimibe/simvastatin).
- **Route of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Pharmacor Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
### Adults (≥18 years)
- **Primary Hypercholesterolaemia**: Adjunctive therapy to diet in patients with primary (heterozygous familial and non-familial) hypercholesterolaemia or mixed hyperlipidaemia when use of a combination product is appropriate (e.g., patients not controlled on monotherapy or those already receiving both agents).
- **Homozygous Familial Hypercholesterolaemia (HoFH)**: Adjunctive therapy to other lipid-lowering treatments (e.g., LDL apheresis) or if such treatments are unavailable.
- **Prevention of Cardiovascular Disease**: Indicated in patients with coronary heart disease (CHD) and a history of acute coronary syndrome (ACS) who are on their maximum tolerated dose of **simvastatin** and require additional LDL-C lowering to reduce the risk of cardiovascular events [Australian-approved].

### Children and Adolescents (10–17 years)
- **Heterozygous Familial Hypercholesterolaemia (HeFH)**: Adjunctive therapy to diet for adolescents (boys Tanner Stage II and above; girls ≥1 year post-menarche).
- **Homozygous Familial Hypercholesterolaemia (HoFH)**: Indicated as adjunctive therapy in adolescents (10–17 years).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing**: The dose range is 10/10 mg/day to 10/80 mg/day.
- **Administration**: Must be taken as a single daily dose in the **evening**, with or without food.
- **Initial Dosing**: Typically 10/10 mg or 10/40 mg daily. After initiation or titration, lipid levels should be analysed after ≥2 weeks and the dose adjusted if necessary.
- **High-Dose Restriction**: The 10/80 mg dose is restricted to patients at high risk for cardiovascular complications who have failed to reach goals on lower doses and where benefits outweigh the increased risk of myopathy.
- **Specific Indication Dosing**:
    - **HoFH**: Recommended dose is 10/40 mg or 10/80 mg daily in the evening.
    - **ACS history**: In the IMPROVE-IT study, the starting dose was 10/40 mg once daily.
- **Missed Dose**: Not specified in available sources (refer to local clinical guidelines).

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to **ezetimibe**, **simvastatin**, or any excipients.
- Active liver disease or unexplained persistent elevations of serum transaminases.
- **Pregnancy and Lactation**: Strongly contraindicated due to potential fetal harm and risk to the infant.
- Myopathy secondary to other lipid-lowering agents.
- **Concomitant Administration with**:
    - Potent CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., itraconazole, ketoconazole, HIV protease inhibitors, erythromycin, clarithromycin, telithromycin, nefazodone, cobicistat).
    - **Gemfibrozil**, **ciclosporin**, or **danazol**.
    - **Fusidic acid** (systemic use).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Myopathy/Rhabdomyolysis**: **Simvastatin** causes dose-related myopathy (CK >10x ULN). Risk is significantly higher at the 80 mg dose.
    - *Predisposing factors*: Age ≥65 years, female gender, uncontrolled hypothyroidism, and renal impairment.
    - *Management*: Discontinue immediately if myopathy is suspected or CK >10x ULN.
- **Liver Enzymes**: Consecutive elevations in transaminases (≥3x ULN) have occurred. Perform Liver Function Tests (LFTs) before treatment and periodically thereafter.
- **Myasthenia Gravis/Ocular Myasthenia**: Statins may induce new-onset or aggravate pre-existing myasthenia gravis. Discontinue if symptoms occur.
- **Immune-Mediated Necrotizing Myopathy (IMNM)**: Rare reports of persistent proximal muscle weakness and elevated CK despite statin discontinuation.
- **Interstitial Lung Disease**: Reported with long-term statin use; symptoms include dyspnoea, non-productive cough, and fatigue.
- **Diabetes Mellitus**: Statins may increase HbA1c and fasting serum glucose levels.

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (≥1/100, <1/10)** | ALT/AST increased, blood CK increased, myalgia. |
| **Uncommon (≥1/1000, <1/100)** | Dizziness, headache, paraesthesia, abdominal pain, dyspepsia, flatulence, nausea, vomiting, pruritus, rash, arthralgia, muscle spasms, asthenia, fatigue, sleep disorders/insomnia. |
| **Rare (<1/1000)** | Anaemia, hepatitis/jaundice, blurred vision, erectile dysfunction. |
| **Very Rare (<1/10,000)** | Anaphylaxis, angioedema, Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), hepatic failure (fatal/non-fatal), muscle rupture, gynaecomastia. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects**: Rhabdomyolysis with acute renal failure, IMNM, and Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs).

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Contraindicated Combinations
- **Potent CYP3A4 Inhibitors**: Increase simvastatin exposure; suspend **ezetimibe/simvastatin** during treatment.
- **Gemfibrozil/Ciclosporin/Danazol**: Significantly increase the risk of myopathy.
- **Fusidic Acid**: Discontinue statin during systemic fusidic acid therapy; may reintroduce 7 days after the last dose.

### Dose-Limiting Interactions
- **Amiodarone, Verapamil, Diltiazem, or Elbasvir/Grazoprevir**: Dose of **ezetimibe/simvastatin** should not exceed **10/20 mg daily**.
- **Amlodipine or Lomitapide**: Dose should not exceed **10/40 mg daily**.

### Other Significant Interactions
- **Bile Acid Sequestrants**: Administer **ezetimibe/simvastatin** ≥2 hours before or ≥4 hours after.
- **Grapefruit Juice**: Avoid large quantities (>1 litre/day) as it increases simvastatin levels.
- **Coumarin Anticoagulants (e.g., Warfarin)**: Statins modestly potentiate anticoagulant effect. Monitor INR before starting and frequently during early therapy.
- **Ticagrelor**: Concomitant use with simvastatin doses >40 mg is not recommended.
- **Daptomycin**: Increased risk of myopathy; consider temporary suspension of the statin.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: **Category D [Australian]**. Contraindicated. Cholesterol is essential for fetal development; the risk of inhibition outweighs any potential benefit. Treatment should be suspended for the duration of pregnancy.
- **Breastfeeding**: Contraindicated. It is unknown if **ezetimibe** is excreted in human milk; animal data shows excretion. Simvastatin should not be used by nursing mothers.
- **Fertility**: No human data available. Animal studies showed no effect on pregnancy rates but noted some testicular changes in dogs at high doses of simvastatin.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Approved for HeFH and HoFH (10–17 years). Maximum recommended dose is 10/40 mg daily. Not recommended for children <10 years.
- **Elderly Use**: No dose adjustment usually required, but use caution as age ≥65 is a risk factor for myopathy.
- **Renal Impairment**:
    - Mild (eGFR ≥60 mL/min/1.73 m²): No adjustment.
    - Chronic Kidney Disease (eGFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m²): Dose is **10/20 mg daily** [Australian]. Higher doses have not been evaluated in this population.
- **Hepatic Impairment**:
    - Mild (Child-Pugh 5–6): No adjustment.
    - Moderate to Severe (Child-Pugh >7): Not recommended due to increased ezetimibe exposure.
- **Asian Patients**: Increased risk of myopathy; use the lowest necessary dose. Co-administration with niacin (≥1 g/day) is not recommended in Asian patients.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**:
    - **Ezetimibe**: Inhibits the sterol transporter NPC1L1 at the brush border of the small intestine, reducing the absorption of biliary and dietary cholesterol.
    - **Simvastatin**: An inactive lactone prodrug converted to a $\beta$-hydroxyacid metabolite that inhibits HMG-CoA reductase, the rate-limiting enzyme in hepatic cholesterol synthesis.
- **Pharmacodynamics**: Dual inhibition leads to significant reductions in Total-C, LDL-C, Apo B, and TG, and increases in HDL-C.
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Ezetimibe is rapidly absorbed and conjugated to ezetimibe-glucuronide. Simvastatin has low systemic availability (<5%) due to extensive first-pass metabolism.
    - **Metabolism**: Simvastatin is a CYP3A4 substrate. Ezetimibe undergoes enterohepatic recycling.
    - **Elimination**: Ezetimibe is primarily excreted in faeces (78%); simvastatin is excreted in faeces (60%) and urine (13%).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **IMPROVE-IT**: A 6.0-year study of 18,144 patients with ACS. **Ezetimibe/simvastatin** 10/40 mg significantly reduced the risk of the composite endpoint (CV death, Major Coronary Events, non-fatal stroke) by 6.4% compared to simvastatin 40 mg alone.
- **SHARP**: Conducted in 9,438 patients with chronic kidney disease (CKD). **Ezetimibe/simvastatin** 10/20 mg reduced the risk of major vascular events by 16% over 4.9 years compared to placebo.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store below 25°C.
- **Conditions**: Protect from light. Store in the original packaging.
- **Shelf Life**: 3 years [Australian].
