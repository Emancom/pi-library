# Editorial Product Information: Sodium Phenylbutyrate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of sodium phenylbutyrate for healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Sodium phenylbutyrate
- **Chemical Class:** Nitrogen scavenger; pro-drug of phenylacetate.
- **Brand Name(s):** Pheburane [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** A16AX03
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Granules:** 483 mg of sodium phenylbutyrate per gram of granules [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral; may be administered via nasogastric or gastrostomy tube if prepared as a solution [Australian].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Orpharma Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Urea Cycle Disorders (UCD):** Indicated for the chronic management of hyperammonaemia in patients with urea cycle disorders. It must be used in conjunction with dietary protein restriction and, where necessary, dietary supplements such as essential amino acids, arginine, citrulline, and protein-free calorie supplements [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
Treatment must be initiated and overseen by healthcare professionals experienced in managing urea cycle disorders. Doses should be individualised based on the patient's protein tolerance and the dietary intake required for growth and development.

- **Patients < 20 kg:** Up to 600 mg/kg/day.
- **Patients > 20 kg, Adolescents, and Adults:** Up to 13.0 g/m²/day.
- **Maximum Dose:** The safety and efficacy of doses exceeding 20 g/day have not been established [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Oral Use:** The total daily dose should be divided into equal amounts and administered with each meal or feeding (e.g., 4–6 times daily in infants). 
- **Granules:** Should be swallowed immediately with a drink (water, fruit juices, or protein-free formula) or sprinkled onto a spoonful of soft food (e.g., apple sauce, mashed potatoes) and consumed immediately to maintain taste-masking [Australian].
- **Nasogastric/Gastrostomy Tube:** Granules must **not** be administered directly through a tube. A 50 mg/mL solution must be prepared by a pharmacist or hospital personnel. Once prepared, the solution is stable for 7 days under refrigeration (2°C–8°C) [Australian].

### Missed Dose
Doses should be taken as soon as possible with the next meal. A minimum of 3 hours should elapse between doses. Doses must not be doubled to compensate for a missed dose [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to sodium phenylbutyrate or any excipients.
- **Pregnancy** [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding** [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Acute Hyperammonaemia:** Sodium phenylbutyrate is not recommended for managing acute hyperammonaemic encephalopathy, which requires rapid intervention (e.g., haemodialysis) [Australian].
- **Sodium Content:** Contains 124 mg (5.4 mmol) of sodium per gram of active drug (up to 2.5 g sodium/day at max dose). Use with extreme caution in patients with congestive heart failure, severe renal insufficiency, or clinical conditions involving sodium retention and oedema [Australian].
- **Sucrose Content:** Contains 768 mg of sucrose per gram of drug. This must be considered in patients with diabetes mellitus. Contraindicated in patients with fructose intolerance, glucose-galactose malabsorption, or sucrase-isomaltase insufficiency [Australian].
- **Neurotoxicity:** Phenylacetate (the major metabolite) is associated with reversible neurotoxicity (e.g., somnolence, fatigue, light-headedness) at plasma levels ≥ 3.5 mmol/L. Dosing may need adjustment if these symptoms occur [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Periodic monitoring of plasma ammonia, arginine, essential amino acids, carnitine, and serum proteins is required. Plasma glutamine should be maintained < 1,000 µmol/L. Serum potassium must be monitored as renal excretion of metabolites may induce urinary potassium loss [Australian].

**Post-Marketing Experience:** Septic shock, loss of consciousness, urinary incontinence, and choking have been reported [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Probenecid:** May inhibit the renal excretion of sodium phenylbutyrate and its metabolite, phenylacetylglutamine [Australian].
- **Hyperammonaemia-Inducing Agents:** Haloperidol, valproate, carbamazepine, phenobarbital, and topiramate may induce hyperammonaemia and should be used with caution [Australian].
- **Corticosteroids:** May increase plasma ammonia levels via the breakdown of body protein [Australian].
- **Rifampicin:** Potential for toxic accumulation of phenylacetate due to CYP3A4 induction/interactions [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Contraindicated.** Australian Category B3. Animal studies have shown reduced foetal brain weight and neuroanatomical deficits. Effective contraception is required for women of childbearing potential [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** **Contraindicated.** It is unknown if phenylacetate is excreted in human milk [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Dedicated human studies are unavailable. In animal models using a related substance, high doses led to maternal toxicity and increased nonviable embryos [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Recommended for infants (> 1 month), children, and adolescents [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been specifically studied in the geriatric population [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution (CrCl or eGFR < 60 mL/min) as the drug is primarily excreted via the kidneys [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution, as metabolism occurs in the liver. Cirrhotic patients have shown significantly higher plasma levels of phenylacetate [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Sodium phenylbutyrate is a pro-drug. It is rapidly metabolised to phenylacetate, which conjugates with glutamine via acetylation to form phenylacetylglutamine. This compound is excreted by the kidneys, providing an alternative pathway for waste nitrogen excretion (comparable to urea) [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Reduces elevated plasma ammonia and glutamine levels. Each gram of sodium phenylbutyrate produces 0.12 to 0.15 g of phenylacetylglutamine nitrogen [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed (fasting); peak plasma levels (Cmax) of parent drug reached in ~1 hour.
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is 0.2 L/kg.
    - **Metabolism:** Oxidised to phenylacetate in the liver and kidneys.
    - **Elimination:** 80–100% excreted by the kidneys within 24 hours as phenylacetylglutamine. Half-life is ~0.8 hours for the parent drug and 1.3 hours for phenylacetate [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Study:** An open-label, multicentre study of 183 patients with various UCD enzyme deficiencies (CPS, OTC, ASS) showed a 98% survival rate in late-onset patients. However, 65% of patients demonstrated average to borderline mental impairment, and cognitive performance remained stable but did not reverse pre-existing neurological damage. Acute hyperammonaemic episodes recurred in the majority of patients despite therapy [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Granules:** Store below 30°C. Protect from light. Shelf life is 3 years; once opened, use within 45 days [Australian].
- **Prepared Solution:** Store between 2°C and 8°C. Protect from light. Use within 7 days of preparation [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
