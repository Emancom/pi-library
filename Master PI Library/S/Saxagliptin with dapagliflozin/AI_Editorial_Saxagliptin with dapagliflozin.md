# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory guidance into a single, cohesive reference. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
  - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) on Hemodialysis:** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Acute/Severe Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically in the range of 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2–3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if required [US].

### General Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** No specific contraindications are listed in the available US source, though hypersensitivity to the active ingredient would preclude use [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue the drug and initiate appropriate medical management [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:**
  - Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels.
  - Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- **Oral Formulation Caution:** The safety/efficacy of oral levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common and Very Common (>1%–10%+)
Based on trials in ESRD patients, the following events occurred at ≥5% regardless of causality:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%), Flu syndrome (25%), Headache (22%), Chest pain (12%), Fever (7%), Abdominal pain (9%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%), Palpitations (5%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%), Nausea (8%), Anorexia (5%).
- **Neurological/Psychiatric:** Dizziness (13%), Paresthesia (5%), Insomnia (4%), Depression (5%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), Cough increase (9%), Rhinitis (9%), Dyspnea (7%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), Hyperkalemia (6%), Hypervolemia (5%).

### Serious and Post-marketing Effects
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, laryngeal edema.
- **Neurological:** Seizures.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis (uncommon), body odor (uncommon).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not formally assigned a letter category in this PI]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use in pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies in nursing mothers have not been conducted. Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk, and studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations after exogenous administration. Weigh the benefits to the mother against the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant; consider discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing guidelines are included in the general dosage section; specific age-related restrictions are not specified in the US source [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Highly relevant for ESRD patients; dose adjustments are based on pre-dialysis trough levels. Accumulation of metabolites is a concern with oral formulations in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
  - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and [3H]-γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
  - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Efficacy:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis trough concentrations from ~20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L by week 8. While it increases carnitine levels, the specific effect on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, malaise) in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL [US].
