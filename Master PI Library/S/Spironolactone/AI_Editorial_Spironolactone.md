# Editorial Product Information: Spironolactone

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data, dosing strategies, and safety profiles.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Spironolactone (a steroid nucleus with a lactone ring).
- **Chemical Class:** Aldosterone antagonist (potassium-sparing diuretic).
- **Brand Names:** Aldactone [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** C03DA01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - 25 mg film-coated tablets (buff-coloured, peppermint-flavoured, stamped SEARLE 39) [Australian].
    - 100 mg film-coated tablets (buff-coloured, peppermint-flavoured, stamped SEARLE 134) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Essential Hypertension:** Effective as monotherapy or as adjunctive therapy to enhance other antihypertensive agents.
- **Malignant Hypertension:** Adjunctive therapy [Australian].
- **Oedematous Disorders:** Management of oedema and ascites in:
    - Congestive cardiac failure (CCF).
    - Hepatic cirrhosis.
    - Nephrotic syndrome.
- **Primary Hyperaldosteronism:** 
    - Diagnostic therapeutic trial.
    - Short-term pre-operative treatment.
    - Long-term maintenance for patients with adrenal adenomas (unsuitable for surgery) or bilateral adrenal hyperplasia.
- **Hypokalaemia:** Treatment of diuretic-induced hypokalaemia or prophylaxis in patients taking digitalis when other measures are inadequate [Australian].
- **Female Hirsutism:** Treatment of androgen-related increases in facial and body hair [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
Maximum clinical effect typically occurs approximately 2 weeks after initiating therapy.

- **Essential Hypertension:** 50 mg to 100 mg daily, as a single dose or divided. When adding to other antihypertensives, reduce the initial dose of the concurrent agent by at least 50% [Australian].
- **Congestive Cardiac Failure:** Initially 100 mg/day. Range: 25 mg to 200 mg/day.
- **Hepatic Cirrhosis:** 
    - Urinary Na+/K+ ratio > 1: 100 mg/day.
    - Urinary Na+/K+ ratio < 1: 200 mg to 400 mg/day.
- **Nephrotic Syndrome:** Usually 100 mg to 200 mg/day.
- **Primary Hyperaldosteronism:**
    - **Long Test:** 400 mg/day for 3 to 4 weeks.
    - **Short Test:** 400 mg/day for 4 days.
    - **Pre-operative/Maintenance:** 100 mg to 400 mg/day.
- **Malignant Hypertension:** Initially 100 mg/day, increased at two-week intervals up to 400 mg/day.
- **Hypokalaemia:** 25 mg to 100 mg daily.
- **Female Hirsutism:** 100 mg to 200 mg/day (usually in divided doses), though 50 mg/day may be effective. May be administered continuously or cyclically (Day 5 to Day 21 of the menstrual cycle) [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Acute renal insufficiency or significant impairment of renal function.
- Anuria.
- Addison’s disease.
- Hyperkalaemia.
- Pregnancy [Australian].
- Hypersensitivity to spironolactone.
- Concomitant use of eplerenone [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hyperkalaemia:** May be fatal, particularly in patients with severe heart failure (NYHA Class III-IV).
    - **Monitoring:** Monitor potassium and creatinine 1 week after initiation/dose increase, then monthly for 3 months, quarterly for a year, and then every 6 months [Australian].
    - **Thresholds:** Discontinue if serum potassium > 5 mEq/L or serum creatinine > 4 mg/dL [Australian].
- **Electrolyte Imbalance:** Periodic estimation of serum electrolytes is required due to risks of hyponatraemia and transient BUN elevation, especially in the elderly.
- **Metabolic Acidosis:** Reversible hyperchloraemic metabolic acidosis has been reported in patients with decompensated hepatic cirrhosis.
- **Surgical Caution:** Spironolactone reduces vascular responsiveness to noradrenaline; caution is advised during regional or general anaesthesia.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Endocrine/Reproductive:** Gynaecomastia (common, usually reversible; related to dose and duration), breast pain, breast neoplasm (benign), irregular menses/amenorrhoea, post-menopausal bleeding, changes in libido, inability to achieve/maintain erection.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Cramping, diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting, gastric bleeding, gastritis, ulceration.
- **Central Nervous System:** Drowsiness, lethargy, headache, mental confusion, ataxia, dizziness.
- **Dermatologic:** Maculopapular or erythematous eruptions, urticaria, alopecia, hypertrichosis, pruritus, Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), Drug Rash with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS).
- **Haematologic:** Leucopenia (including agranulocytosis), thrombocytopenia.
- **General:** Drug fever, leg cramps, malaise, abnormal hepatic function, acute renal failure.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Hyperkalaemia Risk:** Concomitant use with ACE inhibitors, angiotensin II antagonists, NSAIDs, heparin, low molecular weight heparin, potassium supplements, or other potassium-sparing agents is not recommended [Australian].
- **Digoxin:** Increases the half-life of digoxin, potentially leading to digitalis toxicity. Spironolactone also interferes with some digoxin assays.
- **Lithium:** Reduces renal clearance of lithium, increasing the risk of toxicity.
- **NSAIDs (e.g., aspirin, indomethacin):** May attenuate the natriuretic and diuretic efficacy of spironolactone.
- **Carbenoxolone:** May cause sodium retention, decreasing spironolactone effectiveness.
- **Abiraterone:** Spironolactone may increase PSA levels in patients treated for prostate cancer.
- **Ammonium Chloride/Cholestyramine:** Reported cases of hyperkalaemic metabolic acidosis.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B3.**
    - Contraindicated in pregnancy [Australian].
    - Potential for demasculinisation of male fetuses (risk exists from 6 weeks post-conception).
    - Women of child-bearing potential must use adequate contraception.
- **Breastfeeding:** Canrenone (active metabolite) is excreted in breast milk. If use is essential, an alternative method of infant feeding should be instituted.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies show increased resorption and stillbirths at high doses. Endocrine dysfunction observed in rat offspring.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Initial daily dose of approximately 3.3 mg/kg. For small children, tablets may be pulverised into a suspension (stable for 1 month if refrigerated) [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of electrolyte imbalances and renal/hepatic impairment; requires careful monitoring of the risk/benefit ratio.
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in acute renal insufficiency or significant impairment.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution in decompensated cirrhosis due to the risk of hyperchloraemic metabolic acidosis.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Competitive antagonist of aldosterone. It binds to receptors at the aldosterone-dependent sodium-potassium exchange site in the distal convoluted renal tubule, increasing sodium/water excretion while retaining potassium.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Acts as both a diuretic and antihypertensive. It also possesses moderate anti-androgenic activity by inhibiting dihydrotestosterone-receptor binding and interfering with ovarian steroidogenesis.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Bioavailability > 90%; food may increase bioavailability.
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly and extensively metabolised. Primary active metabolites include sulfur-containing products and canrenone (25-30% of dose).
    - **Distribution:** > 90% plasma protein bound.
    - **Elimination:** Metabolites excreted primarily in urine, and also in bile. Half-life of canrenone is 13–24 hours.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging.
- **Stability (Suspension):** Pulverised tablets in cherry syrup are stable for 1 month when refrigerated [Australian].
