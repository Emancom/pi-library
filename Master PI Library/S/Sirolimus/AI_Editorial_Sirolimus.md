# Editorial Product Information: Sirolimus

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the official Product Information (PI) for **sirolimus**. It is designed as a clinical reference for healthcare professionals to support therapeutic decision-making. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial compilation and does not replace the official PI approved in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for prescribing purposes.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Sirolimus (also known as rapamycin), a macrocyclic lactone produced by *Streptomyces hygroscopicus*.
- **Chemical Class:** Selective immunosuppressant; mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) inhibitor.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Rapamune** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):**
    - **Oral Solution:** 1 mg/mL [Australian].
    - **Tablets:** 0.5 mg, 1 mg, and 2 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Renal Transplantation:** Prophylaxis of organ rejection in patients at mild to moderate immunological risk. This indication requires therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Initial Loading Dose:** A single 6 mg oral dose administered as soon as possible post-transplant [Australian].
- **Maintenance Dose:** 2 mg once daily. Doses should be individualised based on whole blood trough concentrations [Australian].

### Therapeutic Drug Monitoring (TDM)
- **Target Trough Concentrations (Chromatographic Assay):**
    - **With Ciclosporin (Initial 2–4 months):** 4–12 ng/mL.
    - **After Ciclosporin Withdrawal:** 12–20 ng/mL.
- **Monitoring Frequency:** TDM is mandatory. Steady-state should be reached (usually 7–14 days after dose adjustment) before further changes, except when a loading dose is used [Australian].
- **Dose Adjustment Formula:** New Dose = Current Dose × (Target Concentration / Current Concentration).

### Administration Instructions
- **Consistency:** Must be taken consistently either with or without food.
- **Timing with Ciclosporin:** Sirolimus must be taken 4 hours after ciclosporin microemulsion [Australian].
- **Grapefruit Juice:** Strictly contraindicated for administration or dilution.
- **Oral Solution Preparation:** Use the provided syringe to mix with at least 60 mL of water or orange juice in a glass or plastic container. Stir and drink immediately; rinse with 120 mL and drink again [Australian].
- **Tablet Handling:** Tablets must not be crushed, chewed, or split. Use the oral solution if the patient cannot swallow tablets [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to sirolimus, its derivatives, or any excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Wound Healing and Fluid Accumulation:** mTOR inhibitors may impair wound healing (e.g., lymphocele, dehiscence). Risk increases with BMI >30 kg/m². Reports include peripheral oedema, lymphoedema, pleural effusion, and pericardial effusions [Australian].
- **Malignancies:** Increased risk of lymphoma and skin malignancies. Patients should limit UV exposure and use high-SPF sunscreen [Australian].
- **Infections:** Increased susceptibility to opportunistic infections (e.g., *Pneumocystis carinii*), sepsis, and latent viral activation (BK virus-associated nephropathy or JC virus-associated PML) [Australian].
- **Hyperlipidaemia:** Significant increases in cholesterol and triglycerides are common. Monitor and treat as required.
- **Renal Function:** Long-term combination with ciclosporin is associated with increased serum creatinine and reduced GFR; ciclosporin withdrawal is recommended after 2–4 months [Australian].
- **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD):** Cases of pneumonitis, BOOP, and pulmonary fibrosis have occurred; some were fatal. Risk may correlate with high trough levels [Australian].
- **CNI Conversion:** Conversion from calcineurin inhibitors (CNI) to sirolimus in patients with GFR <40 mL/min is associated with higher rates of serious adverse events, including graft loss and death [Australian].
- **Angioedema:** Concomitant use with ACE inhibitors increases the risk of angioneurotic oedema [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP3A4 and P-gp Inhibitors:** Strong inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, voriconazole, clarithromycin) significantly increase sirolimus levels. Concomitant use is not recommended [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 and P-gp Inducers:** Strong inducers (e.g., rifampicin, rifabutin, St. John’s Wort) significantly decrease sirolimus levels. Concomitant use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Ciclosporin:** Increases sirolimus AUC and Cmax. Sirolimus must be taken 4 hours after ciclosporin.
- **Cannabidiol:** Reports of increased sirolimus blood levels; monitor for toxicity [Australian].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **HMG-CoA Reductase Inhibitors/Fibrates:** Monitor for rhabdomyolysis when used with sirolimus and ciclosporin.
- **ACE Inhibitors:** Increased risk of angioedema.
- **Vaccinations:** Live vaccines (e.g., measles, mumps, rubella, oral polio, BCG, yellow fever, varicella) should be avoided [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Sirolimus is embryo/fetal toxic in rats. It should only be used if the potential benefit outweighs the risk.
- **Contraception:** Effective contraception must be initiated before therapy and continued for 12 weeks after discontinuation [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if sirolimus is excreted in human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in infants, a decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Reductions in sperm counts and testicular lesions have been observed in animal studies; azoospermia in humans is typically reversible upon discontinuation [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients <18 years have not been established. Limited data in high-risk renal transplant children showed increased risk of renal function deterioration and infections [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical data are limited. Trough levels in patients >65 years appear similar to younger adults [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild to Moderate:** Reduce maintenance dose by approximately one-third.
    - **Severe:** Reduce maintenance dose by approximately one-half. 
    - **Loading Dose:** No adjustment required. TDM is critical [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Sirolimus binds to the cytosolic protein FKBP-12. The resulting complex inhibits the activation of the mammalian Target of Rapamycin (mTOR), a key kinase for cell cycle progression. This blocks T-cell activation and cytokine-driven lymphocyte proliferation [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; Tmax of ~1 hour (solution) or ~2 hours (tablets). Bioavailability is ~14% when taken with ciclosporin.
    - **Distribution:** Extensively partitioned into formed blood elements (blood-to-plasma ratio of 36). Protein binding is ~92%.
    - **Metabolism:** Substrate for CYP3A4 and P-gp.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is ~62 hours in stable renal transplant patients. Excreted primarily in faeces (91%) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Ciclosporin Withdrawal Study:** A trial of 525 patients demonstrated that withdrawing ciclosporin 3–4 months post-transplant and increasing sirolimus to higher target troughs (12–20 ng/mL) resulted in significantly better graft and patient survival at 48 and 60 months, as well as improved renal function (higher GFR) compared to triple therapy [Australian].
- **CNI Conversion Study:** Conversion from CNIs to sirolimus was studied in 830 patients. Results indicated no benefit in renal function for those with a baseline GFR <40 mL/min, and this subgroup experienced higher rates of pneumonia, acute rejection, and death [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Tablets:** Store below 25°C. Protect from light [Australian].
- **Oral Solution:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate). Do not freeze. Protect from light. Once opened, the bottle is stable for one month. In a dosing syringe, it is stable for 24 hours at room temperature or refrigerated [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **S4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
