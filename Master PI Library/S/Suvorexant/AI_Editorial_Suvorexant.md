# Editorial Product Information: Suvorexant

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory guidance before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Suvorexant (orexin receptor antagonist).
- **Brand Name:** **BELSOMRA** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
  - Film-coated tablets: 15 mg (white, oval, biconvex, marked with "325") and 20 mg (white, round, biconvex, marked with "335") [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Merck Sharp & Dohme (Australia) Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Insomnia:** Treatment of insomnia characterized by difficulties with sleep onset and/or sleep maintenance [Australian].
- **Note:** Treatment continuation should be re-evaluated after three months [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:**
  - **Adults (<65 years):** 20 mg once nightly [Australian].
  - **Elderly (≥65 years):** 15 mg once nightly [Australian].
- **Maximum Dose:** 20 mg per night (non-elderly); 15 mg per night (elderly). Higher doses (30 mg, 40 mg) have shown similar efficacy but increased adverse effects [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
  - Take no more than once per night within 30 minutes of going to bed.
  - Ensure at least 7 hours remain before the planned time of awakening.
  - May be taken with or without food; however, food may delay sleep onset.
- **Missed Dose:** Not explicitly detailed; however, the requirement for 7 hours of remaining sleep time must be met.
- **CYP3A Inhibitors:** Not recommended for use with strong or moderate CYP3A inhibitors [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Narcolepsy:** Use is strictly contraindicated [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to suvorexant or any of its inactive ingredients (e.g., lactose monohydrate) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **CNS Depressant Effects:** Suvorexant can impair daytime wakefulness even when used as prescribed. Effects may persist for several days after discontinuation [Australian].
- **Next-Day Driving Impairment:** Risk is increased with the 20 mg dose, if taken with <7 hours sleep remaining, or if co-administered with other CNS depressants. Patients should be cautioned against next-day driving and activities requiring full mental alertness [Australian].
- **Complex Sleep Behaviours:** Reports of "sleep-driving," sleep-walking, and preparing/eating food while not fully awake (with amnesia for the event). Discontinuation is strongly recommended if these occur [Australian].
- **Worsening of Depression/Suicidality:** A dose-dependent increase in suicidal ideation has been observed. Prescribe the lowest feasible number of tablets for depressed patients [Australian].
- **Respiratory Function:** Caution is advised in patients with compromised respiratory function. It has not been studied in severe Obstructive Sleep Apnoea (OSA) or severe COPD [Australian].
- **Sleep Phenomena:** Sleep paralysis, hypnagogic/hypnopompic hallucinations (including vivid/disturbing perceptions), and cataplexy-like symptoms (e.g., leg weakness) may occur [Australian].
- **Co-morbidities:** Evaluate for underlying physical or psychiatric disorders if insomnia fails to remit after 7–10 days of treatment [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following frequencies are based on pooled Phase 3 clinical trial data (first 3 months):

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Somnolence (6.7%), Headache (7.3%), Dizziness (3.0%), Fatigue (2.2%), Diarrhoea (2.4%), Medication administration error (3.2%) |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Abnormal dreams, Dry mouth, Nausea, Upper respiratory tract infection, Cough |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Sleep paralysis, Hypnagogic/hypnopompic hallucinations |

- **Dose-Dependency:** Adverse effects such as somnolence and fatigue were more frequent at higher, non-recommended doses (30 mg and 40 mg) [Australian].
- **Post-Marketing Reports:** Palpitations, tachycardia, vomiting, psychomotor hyperactivity, anxiety, nightmare, and pruritus [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions (CYP3A):**
  - **Strong CYP3A Inhibitors (e.g., Ketoconazole, Itraconazole, Ritonavir):** Significantly increase exposure (AUC increased ~2.8-fold). Concomitant use is **not recommended** [Australian].
  - **Moderate CYP3A Inhibitors (e.g., Diltiazem, Erythromycin, Grapefruit juice):** Increase exposure (AUC increased ~2-fold). Concomitant use is **not recommended** [Australian].
  - **Strong CYP3A Inducers (e.g., Rifampin, Phenytoin, Carbamazepine):** Significantly decrease suvorexant exposure; efficacy may be reduced [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
  - **Alcohol/CNS Depressants:** Additive psychomotor impairment. Alcohol should not be consumed with suvorexant [Australian].
- **Effects on Other Drugs:**
  - **Digoxin:** Suvorexant slightly increases digoxin levels via P-gp inhibition. Monitor digoxin concentrations as clinically indicated [Australian].
  - **Midazolam, Warfarin, Oral Contraceptives:** No clinically significant interactions observed [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **Australian Category B3.** No clinical studies in pregnant women. Use only if potential benefit justifies potential risk to the fetus. Animal studies showed no teratogenicity but noted decreased fetal weight at maternotoxic doses [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Suvorexant and its metabolite are excreted in human milk (relative infant dose ~0.7%). Caution should be exercised; consider the developmental benefits of breastfeeding and the mother's clinical need for the drug [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed decreased corpora lutea and implantations in females at 25x human exposure; no effects on male fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established; use not recommended in children [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Recommended dose is 15 mg. No overall differences in safety/efficacy compared to younger adults, but greater sensitivity in some individuals cannot be ruled out [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Pharmacokinetics are similar in severe renal impairment; no dose adjustment required [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
  - **Moderate:** Similar pharmacokinetics to healthy subjects, but half-life is prolonged (~19 hours).
  - **Severe:** Not studied; use is **not recommended** [Australian].
- **Body Mass Index (BMI):** Exposure is higher in obese patients (especially females); BMI >30 kg/m² results in a ~31% increase in AUC [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Highly selective, reversible, high-affinity antagonist of orexin receptors (OX1R and OX2R). It suppresses the "wake drive" by blocking the binding of wake-promoting neuropeptides orexin A and orexin B [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Does not bind to GABA, serotonin, dopamine, or melatonin receptors [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability ~62%. Median $t_{max}$ of 2 hours. High-fat meals delay $t_{max}$ by ~1.5 hours [Australian].
  - **Distribution:** >99% protein bound (albumin and $\alpha$1-acid glycoprotein). $V_d$ ~49 L [Australian].
  - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP3A; minor contribution from CYP2C19 [Australian].
  - **Elimination:** Primarily faecal (~66%); ~23% renal. Mean elimination half-life is ~12 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies (PN028 and PN029):** Two 3-month, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in patients with primary insomnia. 
- **Outcomes:** Suvorexant (15/20 mg) was superior to placebo for both sleep latency (objective PSG and subjective patient estimation) and sleep maintenance (Total Sleep Time and Wake After Sleep Onset) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Handling:** Store in original container (aluminium/aluminium blister packs) [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (S4)** — Prescription Only Medicine [Australian].
