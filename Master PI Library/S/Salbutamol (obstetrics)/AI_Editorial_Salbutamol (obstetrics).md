# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial synthesis: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Metabolic Disorders)
- **Standard Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in patients with severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Doses should be administered every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been used based on clinical requirement [US].

### Dosing in ESRD (Hemodialysis)
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if concentrations are supra-normal [US].

### Administration and Monitoring
- **Administration:** Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
- **Monitoring:** In metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** Not specified as a formal contraindication in the source, but warnings are provided regarding serious reactions (see Section 5) [US].
- **Other:** None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occurred within minutes of administration [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known seizure disorder, an increase in frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation):** Note that the safety/efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal impairment. High oral doses in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].
- **Monitoring:** Periodic monitoring of INR is required for patients on concomitant warfarin therapy [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events observed in clinical trials of ESRD patients (occurring at ≥5% frequency, regardless of causality) include:

| System Organ Class | Adverse Events (Frequency ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, back pain, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, palpitations, vascular disorder |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, anorexia, dyspepsia |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight changes |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia, depression |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis |
| **Skin** | Pruritus, rash |

- **Serious/Notable Reactions:** Seizures and serious hypersensitivity (anaphylaxis) [US].
- **Other reported effects:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent body odor and gastritis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Management:** Monitor INR levels at initiation and following dose adjustments of levocarnitine [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine is not protein-bound, reducing the likelihood of displacement-based interactions [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified in the current format, but clinical data noted]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies in nursing mothers have not been conducted. Levocarnitine concentrations increase in dairy cow milk following administration. Risk-benefit assessment is required; consideration may be given to discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the available source [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Used as a primary treatment for carnitine deficiency in ESRD patients on dialysis. However, accumulation of metabolites from *oral* formulations is a concern in severe renal impairment [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (faeces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, a 20 mg/kg IV dose significantly increases plasma levocarnitine concentrations above the baseline (normal range: 40–50 µmol/L) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, IV levocarnitine (20 mg/kg post-dialysis) increased mean pre-dialysis trough concentrations from ~20 µmol/L at baseline to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservative [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only (Rx only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources [US].
