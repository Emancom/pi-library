# Editorial Product Information: sitagliptin + metformin

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **sitagliptin + metformin**
- Chemical class:
  - Sitagliptin: DPP‑4 inhibitor (mentioned in source).
  - Metformin: Not specified in available sources.
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration: Oral.

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources.
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources.
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycaemic control in adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus when treatment with both sitagliptin and metformin is appropriate.

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General dosing (adult)
- Target maximum recommended daily dose noted in the product information: **100 mg sitagliptin and 2000 mg metformin** (total daily).
- Immediate‑release combination (general guidance in source):
  - Typically administered twice daily with meals; gradually escalate metformin component to reduce gastrointestinal adverse effects.
- Modified‑release (XR) combination:
  - Typically administered once daily with a meal, preferably in the evening.
  - To preserve modified‑release properties, tablets must not be split, broken, crushed or chewed. There are reports of incompletely dissolved XR tablets in faeces; clinical assessment of glycaemic control is advised if this is repeatedly observed.
  - For the 50 mg sitagliptin / 1000 mg metformin XR tablet: two tablets taken together once daily provide sitagliptin 100 mg and metformin 2000 mg total daily.
  - For a 100 mg sitagliptin / 1000 mg metformin XR tablet: single tablet once daily provides sitagliptin 100 mg and metformin 1000 mg daily.

### Initiation and titration
- Usual recommended total daily starting dose when dual therapy is appropriate: sitagliptin 100 mg with metformin 1000 mg daily (adjusted by formulation: IR twice daily; XR once daily as above).
- Patients with inadequate glycaemic control on starting dose may be titrated up to sitagliptin 100 mg / metformin 2000 mg daily as clinically appropriate.
- When switching from sitagliptin monotherapy or from separate sitagliptin plus metformin therapy, initiate the fixed‑dose combination at the dose equivalent to the components already being taken.
- For patients switching from sitagliptin monotherapy who are already dose‑adjusted for renal impairment: do not switch to fixed‑dose sitagliptin/metformin without accounting for renal dosing requirements (see Section 10).

### Administration instructions
- Immediate‑release: give with meals to reduce GI intolerance.
- Modified‑release: give with a meal (preferably evening); do not crush, split, break or chew tablets.
- Discontinue at the time of, or prior to, intravascular iodinated contrast administration in patients with eGFR ≥ 30 to < 60 mL/min/1.73 m2, in patients with history of liver disease, alcoholism or heart failure, or in patients receiving intra‑arterial contrast; reassess renal function 48 hours post procedure before restarting.

### Missed dose
- Not specified in available sources.

### Special dosing note (internal guidance present in source)
- The product information contains both a general statement limiting metformin to a maximum of 2000 mg daily and a renal‑based dosing table that in certain eGFR ranges lists higher or lower maximum metformin doses. Clinicians should use the renal dosing recommendations below (Section 10) and local clinical guidance to determine appropriate total metformin dose for individual patients.

## 5. Contraindications
- Severe renal impairment (eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m2).
- Known hypersensitivity to sitagliptin hydrochloride monohydrate, metformin hydrochloride, or any other component of the product.
- Acute or chronic metabolic acidosis, including diabetic ketoacidosis, with or without coma.
- Sitagliptin/metformin should be temporarily discontinued for radiological studies involving intravascular administration of iodinated contrast materials in specified risk groups (see Section 6).
- Not indicated for use in children and adolescents below 18 years (per provided source).

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed or highlighted warnings
- Not specified as a boxed/labelled warning in available sources. The following clinically critical warnings are emphasised in the product information and are presented here in bold.

### General warnings and precautions
- **Lactic acidosis risk (metformin component):** Life‑threatening lactic acidosis can occur due to metformin accumulation. Risk increases with renal impairment, advanced age, and conditions associated with hypoxaemia or hypoperfusion. Use caution and monitor renal function as described below.
- **Pancreatitis (sitagliptin component):** Acute pancreatitis, including fatal and non‑fatal haemorrhagic or necrotising pancreatitis, has been reported. Counsel patients to seek prompt evaluation for persistent, severe abdominal pain. Discontinue sitagliptin/metformin if pancreatitis is suspected.
- **Hypersensitivity reactions (sitagliptin component):** Serious hypersensitivity reactions (anaphylaxis, angioedema, exfoliative skin conditions including Stevens‑Johnson syndrome) have been reported, typically within the first 3 months of therapy, including after first dose. Discontinue if suspected.
- **Hypoglycaemia risk:** When used with insulin or sulfonylureas, the risk of hypoglycaemia is increased. Consider lowering the dose of insulin or sulfonylurea when initiating combination therapy.
- **Arthralgia:** Severe joint pain reported with DPP‑4 inhibitors; consider discontinuation if severe or persistent.
- **Bullous pemphigoid:** Cases requiring hospitalisation have been reported with DPP‑4 inhibitors. Discontinue if suspected and consider dermatology referral.
- **Modified‑release tablet integrity:** For XR formulations, do not crush/split; reports of tablet remnants in faeces have been made—if seen repeatedly, reassess glycaemic control.
- **Hepatic impairment:** Metformin should generally be avoided in patients with clinical or laboratory evidence of hepatic disease because impaired hepatic function may limit lactate clearance and increase the risk of lactic acidosis.
- **Alcohol:** Excessive alcohol intake (acute or chronic) potentiates metformin effects on lactate metabolism and should be avoided.

### Monitoring requirements
- Assess eGFR before initiation and at least annually thereafter. In patients at increased risk for renal decline and in the elderly, monitor renal function more frequently (e.g. every 3–6 months).
- Reevaluate renal function 48 hours after iodinated contrast imaging if product was withheld.
- Monitor for signs and symptoms of lactic acidosis (malaise, myalgia, respiratory distress, somnolence, abdominal pain), pancreatitis (persistent severe abdominal pain), hypersensitivity, severe arthralgia, and new blistering skin disorders.
- Monitor blood glucose and signs of hypoglycaemia when used concomitantly with insulin or insulin secretagogues.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Temporarily discontinue for intravascular iodinated contrast imaging in patients with eGFR ≥ 30 to < 60 mL/min/1.73 m2, in patients with a history of liver disease, alcoholism or heart failure, or for intra‑arterial contrast; restart only after reassessment of renal function.
- Withhold metformin in circumstances associated with hypoxaemia, dehydration, sepsis, or other conditions that may increase risk of lactic acidosis; withhold prior to major surgery and in unstable or acute congestive heart failure.
- Use caution and regular renal monitoring in elderly patients.

- Serious adverse effects emphasised in the product information: lactic acidosis (fatal in ~50% of reported lactic acidosis cases), pancreatitis (including fatal/necrotising), severe hypersensitivity reactions, Stevens‑Johnson syndrome, bullous pemphigoid, severe arthralgia, and hypoglycaemia when combined with insulin/sulfonylureas.

- Additional details:
  - Lactic acidosis onset may be insidious with nonspecific symptoms (malaise, myalgia, respiratory distress, somnolence, abdominal discomfort); severe cases may include hypothermia, hypotension, bradyarrhythmias.
  - Gastrointestinal adverse effects are most common during initiation of metformin therapy; later onset GI symptoms should prompt evaluation for lactic acidosis or other pathology.

## 8. Drug Interactions

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Concomitant insulin or sulfonylurea: increased risk of hypoglycaemia; consider lowering insulin or sulfonylurea dose when initiating sitagliptin/metformin therapy.
- Alcohol: potentiates metformin effects on lactate metabolism and increases risk of lactic acidosis — avoid excessive alcohol intake.

### Pharmacokinetic/other interactions and clinical management
- Iodinated contrast media (intravascular): may cause acute alteration of renal function and increase risk of metformin accumulation and lactic acidosis. Discontinue sitagliptin/metformin at the time of or prior to contrast in patients with eGFR ≥ 30 to < 60 mL/min/1.73 m2, in those with history of hepatic disease, alcoholism or heart failure, or if intra‑arterial contrast is administered. Reassess renal function 48 hours after procedure before restarting.
- Food effect (modified‑release formulation): administration of the XR formulation with food enhances plasma concentrations of metformin; administer XR with a meal.
- Renal impairment: since both sitagliptin and metformin are substantially excreted by the kidney, reduced renal function increases exposure and risk of adverse events (see Section 10). Dose adjustment or discontinuation required based on eGFR.

## 10. Use in Special Populations

### Paediatric use
- Not to be used in children and adolescents 10 to 17 years of age because of insufficient efficacy (per source).
- Not studied in children younger than 10 years; therefore use not recommended.

### Elderly
- Use caution and monitor renal function closely as both components are renally excreted; elderly patients are at increased risk of lactic acidosis. Renal monitoring recommended more frequently in elderly patients.

### Renal impairment — dosing recommendations (eGFR‑based)
- An eGFR should be assessed before initiation and at least annually thereafter; more frequent monitoring for those at risk.
- Recommended metformin and sitagliptin maximum daily doses by eGFR stated in the source:

  - eGFR ≥ 60 to 89 mL/min/1.73 m2:
    - Metformin: maximum daily dose listed in source as 3000 mg (note: the product information also contains an overarching statement limiting metformin to 2000 mg daily; clinicians should reconcile with local guidance and specific product formulation limits).
    - Sitagliptin: maximum daily dose 100 mg.
  - eGFR 45 to 59 mL/min/1.73 m2:
    - Metformin: maximum daily dose 2000 mg; starting dose no more than half of the maximum dose.
    - Sitagliptin: maximum daily dose 100 mg.
  - eGFR 30 to 44 mL/min/1.73 m2:
    - Metformin: maximum daily dose 1000 mg; starting dose no more than half of the maximum dose.
    - Sitagliptin: maximum daily dose 50 mg.
  - eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m2:
    - Metformin: contraindicated.
    - Sitagliptin: maximum daily dose cited as 25 mg in the table (product information contraindicates use of the combination in patients with eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m2).

- Additional renal guidance:
  - The combination product is contraindicated in patients with eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m2.
  - The combination product may not be appropriate for patients whose individual component doses (particularly sitagliptin dose reductions for renal impairment) cannot be achieved with fixed‑dose combinations; patients dose‑adjusted for renal impairment on sitagliptin monotherapy should not be switched to fixed‑dose sitagliptin/metformin without careful consideration.

### Hepatic impairment
- Metformin should generally be avoided in patients with clinical or laboratory evidence of hepatic disease because impaired hepatic function may limit lactate clearance and increase the risk of lactic acidosis.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Both sitagliptin and metformin are substantially excreted by the kidney.
- Bioequivalence: sitagliptin hydrochloride monohydrate formulation used in the product is reported to be bioequivalent to sitagliptin phosphate monohydrate (clinical data in product information based on sitagliptin phosphate monohydrate).
- Food effect: administration of the modified‑release formulation with food increases plasma concentrations of metformin.
- Further pharmacokinetic parameters (absorption rates, distribution volumes, metabolism pathways, specific elimination half‑lives, etc.) are not specified in the available source.
