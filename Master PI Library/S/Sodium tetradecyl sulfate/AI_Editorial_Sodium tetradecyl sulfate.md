# Editorial Product Information: Sodium tetradecyl sulfate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes for healthcare professionals. It does not replace the official PI or mandatory labeling required for prescribing and dispensing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Sodium tetradecyl sulfate (a sclerosing agent).
- **Brand Name(s):** Fibrovein [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** C05BB04.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Solution for injection: 0.5% (10 mg/2 mL), 1% (20 mg/2 mL), and 3% (60 mg/2 mL ampoules or 150 mg/5 mL vials) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (intraluminal injection into the varicose vein).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Intermed Medical Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Sodium tetradecyl sulfate is indicated as a sclerosing agent for the treatment of uncomplicated varicose veins of the leg via compression sclerotherapy [Australian]. 

Strength selection is determined by vein size:
- **3% Strength:** Treatment of large superficial varicose veins [Australian].
- **1% Strength:** Treatment of small varicose veins and larger venules [Australian].
- **0.5% Strength:** Treatment of minor venules and spider veins (venous flares) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Elderly)
The dosage depends on the strength used and the number of injection sites. The following limits apply per treatment session:
- **3% Solution:** 0.5 to 1 mL per site at a maximum of four sites (Total maximum: 4 mL) [Australian].
- **1% Solution:** 0.25 to 1 mL per site at a maximum of ten sites (Total maximum: 10 mL) [Australian].
- **0.5% Solution:** 0.25 to 1 mL per site at a maximum of ten sites (Total maximum: 10 mL) [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Practitioner Requirements:** Must only be administered by medical practitioners experienced in injection sclerotherapy [Australian].
- **Technique:** Use strict aseptic technique. Injections should be made into an isolated, emptied segment of the vein. For spider veins, an "air block" or "shaken foam" technique may be employed [Australian].
- **Needle Recommendations:** 
    - 3%: 25 gauge.
    - 1%: 27 gauge.
    - 0.5%: 30 gauge [Australian].
- **Post-Injection Compression:** Immediate, uninterrupted compression must be applied for 6 weeks (using bandages or class 1-3 graduated compression stockings depending on the vein size) [Australian].
- **Patient Activity:** Walking should commence immediately after treatment for at least one hour, followed by daily walks of one hour [Australian]. Patients must avoid standing still for long periods [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
Treatment is contraindicated in the presence of [Australian]:
- Hypersensitivity to sodium tetradecyl sulfate or any excipients.
- Inability to walk (bedridden) or inability to walk at least 5 km daily.
- Significant obesity of the legs.
- High risk of thromboembolic events (congenital predisposition, hormonal contraception, HRT, smoking, or extended immobility).
- Recent acute superficial thrombophlebitis, deep vein thrombosis (DVT), or pulmonary embolism (PE).
- Recent surgery.
- Varicosities caused by pelvic or abdominal tumours (unless the tumour is removed).
- Uncontrolled systemic diseases (e.g., diabetes mellitus, toxic hyperthyroidism, asthma, sepsis, blood dyscrasias, or acute respiratory/skin diseases).
- Evolutive cancer.
- Significant valvular or deep vein incompetence.
- Occlusive arterial disease.
- Large superficial veins with wide communications to deep veins.
- Phlebitis migrans, acute cellulitis, or acute infections.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Anaphylaxis:** Allergic reactions, including fatal anaphylactic shock, have been reported. Emergency resuscitation equipment (adrenaline, oxygen, steroids) must be immediately available. Patients should be monitored during and after administration [Australian].
- **Intra-arterial Injection:** Strictly prohibited. Inadvertent arterial injection can lead to extensive tissue necrosis and limb loss. Duplex ultrasound guidance is recommended [Australian].
- **Extravasation:** May cause severe tissue necrosis and ulceration. Use the minimum effective volume and the lowest effective concentration [Australian].
- **Thromboembolism:** Sclerotherapy carries a risk of DVT and PE. Evaluate valvular competency and deep vein patency (e.g., via duplex ultrasound) prior to treatment [Australian].
- **Migraine:** Patients with a history of migraine are more susceptible to visual disturbances and should be treated with smaller volumes [Australian].
- **Lymphoedema:** Injections may worsen local pain and inflammation if lymphoedema is present [Australian].
- **Special Sites:** Exercise extreme caution when injecting the foot or malleolar area due to the proximity of the posterior tibial artery [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by frequency based on available clinical data [Australian].

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common** (≥1/100 to <1/10) | Local pain or burning (short-term), superficial thrombophlebitis, phlebitis. |
| **Uncommon** (≥1/1000 to <1/100) | Skin discolouration (hyperpigmentation, up to 18 months), matting (fine spider veins), local skin reactions (urticaria, dermatitis). |
| **Rare** (≥1/10,000 to <1/1000) | Local sloughing and necrosis of skin/tissues. |
| **Very Rare** (<1/10,000) | Anaphylactic shock, asthma, stroke, TIA, migraine, paraesthesia, scotoma, DVT, pulmonary embolism, circulatory collapse, vasculitis. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and pulmonary embolism are the most serious reported reactions, with fatalities documented [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Heparin:** Sodium tetradecyl sulfate is incompatible with heparin; they must not be used in the same syringe [Australian].
- **Other Interactions:** No specific data available regarding other drug-drug interactions [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B2]. Safety has not been established. Animal studies are insufficient regarding reproductive toxicity. Use in pregnancy is not recommended [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if the drug is excreted into breast milk. Use in lactation is not recommended [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Effect on fertility is unknown [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children and adolescents have not been established. Use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific dose adjustments are required; however, patients must be able to meet the post-treatment walking requirements [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Sodium tetradecyl sulfate acts as an irritant to the intima (lining) of the vein wall. When followed by compression, this irritation induces fibrosis, leading to permanent occlusion of the vein [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** No satisfactory human studies available [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** No satisfactory human pharmacokinetic studies have been conducted for the 0.5%, 1%, or 3% strengths [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No clinical trial data provided in the source documentation [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Conditions:** Do not freeze. Protect from direct sunlight. Keep the container in the outer carton to protect from light [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Once opened, use immediately and discard any unused portion. Multidose vials (5 mL) are for a single session/single patient only [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
