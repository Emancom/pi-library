# Editorial Product Information: Sertraline

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of sertraline for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local clinical guidelines. Prescribers should consult the current approved Product Information in their specific jurisdiction before initiating therapy.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Sertraline hydrochloride (INN).
- **Chemical Class:** Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitor (SSRI); naphthylamine derivative.
- **Brand Names:** APO-Sertraline [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N06AB06.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Film-coated tablets:** 50 mg and 100 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Sertraline is indicated for the treatment of the following conditions:
- **Major Depression (MDD):** Treatment and prevention of relapse in adults.
- **Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD):** Adults and paediatric patients (aged 6–18 years) [Australian].
- **Panic Disorder:** Treatment with or without agoraphobia in adults.
- **Social Phobia (Social Anxiety Disorder):** Treatment and prevention of relapse in adults.
- **Pre-Menstrual Dysphoric Disorder (PMDD):** As defined by DSM criteria in adults.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Major Depression and OCD:** Initiate at 50 mg once daily.
- **Panic Disorder and Social Phobia:** Initiate at 25 mg once daily for one week, then increase to 50 mg once daily. This gradual titration reduces the incidence of early treatment-emergent side effects.
- **Titration:** If clinical response is inadequate, the dose may be increased in 50 mg increments at intervals of no less than one week.
- **Maximum Dose:** 200 mg/day for all adult indications except PMDD.
- **Maintenance:** Use the lowest effective dose for long-term therapy.

### Pre-Menstrual Dysphoric Disorder (PMDD)
- **Continuous Dosing:** 50 mg/day daily throughout the menstrual cycle. Doses may be increased by 50 mg per cycle up to 150 mg/day.
- **Luteal Phase Dosing (Intermittent):** 50 mg/day starting 14 days prior to menstruation through the first full day of menses. If a 100 mg/day dose is required, a titration step of 50 mg/day for the first three days of each luteal phase should be used.

### Administration Instructions
- Administer once daily, either in the morning or evening.
- May be taken with or without food.

### Discontinuation
- **Tapering:** Avoid abrupt cessation. Gradually reduce the dose to minimise withdrawal symptoms (e.g., paraesthesia, dizziness, anxiety, headache).

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to sertraline or any excipients.
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Concurrent use with irreversible MAOIs, or within 14 days of discontinuing an MAOI, is contraindicated due to the risk of serotonin syndrome. Similarly, 14 days must elapse after stopping sertraline before starting an MAOI. This includes reversible MAOIs (e.g., moclobemide), linezolid, and methylene blue [Australian].
- **Pimozide:** Concomitant administration is contraindicated due to the narrow therapeutic index of pimozide and the potential for increased levels leading to QTc prolongation.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Suicidality and Clinical Worsening:** Use with caution in children, adolescents, and young adults (18–24 years), as meta-analyses show an increased risk of suicidal thinking and behaviour during early treatment. Close monitoring for agitation, irritability, or unusual changes in behaviour is mandatory.
- **Serotonin Syndrome (SS) / Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** Potentially life-threatening conditions. Risk increases with concomitant use of other serotonergic drugs (e.g., triptans, opioids, St John's Wort) or antipsychotics. Discontinue immediately if symptoms (hyperthermia, rigidity, autonomic instability, mental status changes) occur.
- **QTc Prolongation/Torsade de Pointes:** Reported post-marketing. Exercise caution in patients with pre-existing risk factors for QTc prolongation.
- **Abnormal Bleeding:** SSRIs may increase the risk of gastrointestinal bleeding, ecchymoses, and postpartum haemorrhage. Caution is advised in patients using NSAIDs, aspirin, or anticoagulants.
- **Hyponatraemia:** Particularly in elderly patients or those taking diuretics, often due to SIADH.
- **Seizures:** Avoid in patients with unstable epilepsy; monitor closely in those with controlled epilepsy. Discontinue if seizures develop.
- **Angle-Closure Glaucoma:** Mydriasis associated with SSRIs may trigger an angle-closure attack in patients with anatomically narrow angles.
- **Diabetes:** May alter glycaemic control; insulin/oral hypoglycaemic dosages may require adjustment.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally dose-related and most commonly occur during the first few weeks of therapy.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Nausea, diarrhoea, insomnia, somnolence, dizziness, headache, fatigue, tremor. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Decreased appetite, increased sweating, palpitations, hot flush, agitation, anxiety, sexual dysfunction (ejaculation disorder, decreased libido), vomiting, dry mouth, dyspepsia, rash, visual impairment. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Tachycardia, haemorrhage (skin/mucous membranes), syncope, hallucinations, aggression, pruritus, alopecia, urinary incontinence, muscle spasms. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Pancreatitis, serious liver injury, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, anaphylactoid reaction, priapism, rhabdomyolysis, bone fractures. |

- **Paediatric Considerations:** In paediatric trials, hyperkinesia, urinary incontinence, and aggression were notably reported [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **CYP2D6 Inhibition:** Sertraline is a mild-to-moderate inhibitor of CYP2D6. Concomitant use with drugs with a narrow therapeutic index (e.g., TCAs, Class 1C antiarrhythmics) may require dose reductions of the substrate.
    - **Cimetidine:** Increases sertraline exposure (AUC increased by ~50%).
    - **Protein Binding:** Sertraline is highly protein-bound; monitor for potential displacement of other drugs like warfarin or digitoxin.
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **Serotonergic Drugs:** Increased risk of Serotonin Syndrome when combined with triptans, tramadol, fentanyl, lithium, or St John's Wort.
    - **Warfarin:** May result in a small but statistically significant increase in prothrombin time.
    - **Phenytoin:** Monitor plasma levels upon initiation of sertraline.
    - **CNS Depressants/Alcohol:** While sertraline does not potentiate alcohol in healthy subjects, co-administration is not recommended in depressed patients.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category C:** SSRIs may cause harmful pharmacological effects on the foetus/neonate without necessarily causing malformations. 
    - **Risks:** Observational data indicate an increased risk of Persistent Pulmonary Hypertension of the Newborn (PPHN) and neonatal withdrawal/adaptation syndrome (e.g., respiratory distress, jitteriness, hypoglycaemia). An increased risk of postpartum haemorrhage has also been noted following exposure late in pregnancy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Sertraline is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding is generally not recommended unless the clinical benefit to the mother outweighs the potential risk to the infant.
- **Fertility:** Animal data showed a decrease in fertility in one rat study; however, the clinical significance in humans is unknown [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved **only** for OCD in patients aged 6 years and older. Not established for MDD in patients under 18 years. Monitor growth and pubertal maturation during long-term therapy.
- **Elderly Use:** No specific dose adjustment is required, but patients are at higher risk for hyponatraemia and SIADH.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Extensively metabolised by the liver. In mild cirrhosis, clearance is reduced. A lower or less frequent dose should be used in patients with hepatic disease; not studied in moderate-to-severe impairment [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required based on the degree of renal impairment, as renal excretion is a minor pathway.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Potent and selective inhibitor of neuronal serotonin (5-HT) reuptake. It has minimal effects on noradrenaline and dopamine reuptake and lacks significant affinity for adrenergic, cholinergic, GABA, dopaminergic, or histaminergic receptors.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Tmax is 4.5 to 8.4 hours. Steady state is reached after approximately one week. Food increases Cmax by ~25%.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensive first-pass metabolism via N-demethylation to N-desmethylsertraline (significantly less active).
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 26 hours. Metabolites are excreted in both urine and faeces.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **MDD:** Efficacy established in 6–8 week trials; maintenance of effect demonstrated for up to 24 weeks.
- **OCD:** Paediatric efficacy (6–17 years) demonstrated in a 12-week trial showing a 7-point reduction in CYBOCS scores vs 3 points for placebo.
- **SPRITES Study:** A 3-year observational study in children (6–16 years) found no statistically significant adverse effects on cognitive, emotional, or pubertal maturation, though a small increase in standardised weight was observed at higher doses [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
