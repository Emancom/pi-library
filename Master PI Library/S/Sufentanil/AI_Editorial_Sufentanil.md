# Editorial Product Information: Sufentanil

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: **US (FDA)**. 
- *Note: While the provided documentation specifically represents the United States (FDA) labelling for Hospira, Inc., this editorial aims to provide a harmonised overview of the clinical properties of sufentanil. Information regarding other jurisdictions such as Australian (TGA), European (EMA), or Canadian (Health Canada) was not specified in the available source document.*
- Purpose: This document serves as a harmonised editorial synthesis intended for healthcare professionals. it is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Sufentanil citrate (opioid agonist; phenylpiperidine class).
- **Brand Name(s):** Sufentanil Citrate Injection (Hospira) [US].
- **ATC Code:** N01AH03.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 50 mcg/mL (equivalent to sufentanil base).
    - Available in 1 mL single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) or Epidural.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Hospira, Inc. (a Pfizer company) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Analgesic Adjunct:** Maintenance of balanced general anesthesia in patients who are intubated and ventilated [US].
- **Primary Anesthetic Agent:** Induction and maintenance of anesthesia with 100% oxygen in patients undergoing major surgical procedures (e.g., cardiovascular surgery or neurosurgery in the sitting position) where favorable myocardial and cerebral oxygen balance is required, or where extended postoperative ventilation is anticipated [US].
- **Epidural Analgesia:** Used in combination with low-dose bupivacaine (typically 12.5 mg) for analgesia during labor and vaginal delivery [US].
- **Pediatric Use:** Indicated for intravenous use in pediatric patients as an analgesic adjunct in general anesthesia or as a primary anesthetic for cardiovascular surgery [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Instructions
- Administration must be performed only by personnel specifically trained in intravenous anesthetics and the management of respiratory effects of potent opioids.
- Resuscitative equipment, oxygen, and opioid antagonists (e.g., naloxone) must be immediately available.
- Dosage must be individualized based on age, weight, physical status, underlying pathology, concurrent drug use, and the nature of the surgical procedure.

### Intravenous Dosing (Adults) [US]
- **Adjunct to General Anesthesia (1–2 hours duration):** 1–2 mcg/kg total dose. Maintenance doses of 10–25 mcg as needed for surgical stress.
- **Adjunct to General Anesthesia (2–8 hours duration):** 2–8 mcg/kg total dose. Maintenance doses of 10–50 mcg.
- **Primary Anesthetic Agent:** ≥8 mcg/kg (range 8–30 mcg/kg) with 100% oxygen and a muscle relaxant. Maintenance doses of 0.5–10 mcg/kg. 

### Epidural Dosing (Labor and Delivery) [US]
- **Initial Dose:** 10–15 mcg sufentanil administered with 10 mL bupivacaine 0.125%.
- **Maintenance:** Doses may be repeated twice (total of three doses) at intervals of not less than one hour until delivery.

### Administration Notes
- For accurate measurement of small volumes, use of a tuberculin syringe is recommended.
- Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to sufentanil or other components of the formulation (e.g., anaphylaxis) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
- **Addiction, Abuse, and Misuse:** Sufentanil exposes users to risks of opioid addiction and misuse, which can lead to fatal overdose.
- **Life-Threatening Respiratory Depression:** Serious or fatal respiratory depression may occur; monitoring is essential, particularly during initiation.
- **Risks from Concomitant Use with Benzodiazepines or CNS Depressants:** May result in profound sedation, coma, and death.
- **CYP3A4 Interactions:** Concomitant use with CYP3A4 inhibitors may increase sufentanil plasma concentrations, potentially leading to fatal respiratory depression.

### General Precautions
- **Skeletal Muscle Rigidity:** Can cause muscle rigidity, especially involving respiratory muscles. Management may require neuromuscular blocking agents [US].
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Risk increases when used with serotonergic drugs (SSRIs, SNRIs, MAOIs). Discontinue if suspected [US].
- **Intracranial Pressure (ICP):** Use with caution in patients with head injury or brain tumors; CO2 retention can further increase ICP [US].
- **Cardiovascular Depression:** May cause severe bradycardia and hypotension; use with caution in patients with compromised blood volume or impaired circulation [US].
- **Opioid-Induced Hyperalgesia (OIH) and Allodynia:** Paradoxical increase in pain may occur; consider dose reduction or opioid rotation if suspected [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Frequencies are not specifically categorised by percentage in the source PI for all effects, but key observations include:*
- **Very Common (>10%):** 
    - Pruritus (observed in 25% of epidural labor patients) [US].
- **Common (1–10%):** 
    - Apnea, Muscle Rigidity, Bradycardia [US].
- **Other Reported Effects:** 
    - Nausea, vomiting, hypotension, hypertension, arrhythmia, tachycardia, and somnolence.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** 
    - Respiratory arrest, circulatory depression, seizures, and serotonin syndrome.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors (e.g., ritonavir, ketoconazole):** Increase sufentanil levels; require close monitoring and potential dose reduction [US].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., rifampin, carbamazepine):** May decrease efficacy or precipitate withdrawal; discontinuing an inducer may increase sufentanil levels [US].
- **CNS Depressants (Benzodiazepines, Alcohol, etc.):** Potentiate respiratory depression and sedation.
- **MAO Inhibitors:** Use of sufentanil is not recommended within 14 days of MAOI therapy [US].
- **Serotonergic Drugs:** Increased risk of serotonin syndrome.
- **Mixed Agonist/Antagonist Opioids (e.g., buprenorphine, nalbuphine):** May reduce analgesic effect and precipitate withdrawal [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified by letter, but noted that prolonged use may cause Neonatal Opioid Withdrawal Syndrome (NOWS). Use during labor may cause respiratory depression in the newborn [US]. 
    - Animal data showed embryolethality and maternal toxicity at doses near the human procedural dose [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Sufentanil is excreted in breast milk. Monitor infants for excess sedation and respiratory depression [US].
- **Fertility:** Long-term opioid use may cause reduced fertility in both males and females; reversibility is unknown [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Pediatric Use:** Safety established in infants as young as 1 day (CV surgery). Neonates have significantly lower clearance compared to older children/adults (approx. half), which is further reduced in the presence of cardiovascular disease [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased sensitivity to sufentanil. Start at the low end of the dosing range and titrate slowly due to increased risk of respiratory depression and potential renal/hepatic impairment [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Extensive hepatic metabolism necessitates cautious dosing and frequent monitoring for toxicity [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution due to renal excretion of the drug and its metabolites; monitor for signs of sedation and respiratory depression [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Potent opioid agonist interacting primarily with mu-receptors.
- **Pharmacodynamics:**
    - Potency: 5 to 10 times more potent than fentanyl intravenously [US].
    - Effects include analgesia, miosis, decreased GI motility, and respiratory depression.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** 3-compartment model. Plasma protein binding is ~93% in healthy males (binding primarily to alpha-1-acid glycoprotein) [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily in the liver and small intestine.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is 164 minutes in adults. ~80% of dose excreted within 24 hours (only 2% as unchanged drug) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Epidural Labor/Delivery:** Two double-blind studies (340 patients) evaluated 10–15 mcg sufentanil with bupivacaine. Onset occurred within 10 minutes; duration of analgesia for the first dose was approximately 95 minutes. Analgesia was rated as "profound" in 80–100% of patients [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from light; retain in carton until time of use [US].
- **Safety:** Discard unused portions as the product contains no bacteriostat [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Schedule II Controlled Substance (CII); Prescription only (Rx).
