# Editorial Product Information: Silver Sulfadiazine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview designed for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Silver sulfadiazine (1.0% w/w)
- **Chemical Class:** Sulfonamide derivative / Topical antibacterial
- **Brand Name(s):** Flamazine [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** D06BA01
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Topical cream containing 1.0% w/w silver sulfadiazine [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Smith & Nephew Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Severe Burns:** Prevention of infection in severe burn wounds [Australian].
- **Chronic Wounds:** Management and prevention of infection in pressure sores and leg ulcers [Australian].
- **Finger-Tip Injuries:** Topical treatment where haemostasis has been achieved [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Application Depth:** The cream should be applied using a sterile gloved hand or spatula to a depth of approximately 3–5 mm over the affected area [Australian].

### Dosing by Indication
- **Burns:** Re-apply at least every 24 hours. More frequent application may be required if wound exudate is high.
- **Hand Burns:** Apply cream and enclose the hand in a clear plastic bag or glove, secured at the wrist. Encourage movement of digits. Change the dressing when exudate accumulates excessively.
- **Leg Ulcers/Pressure Sores:** Fill the ulcer cavity to a depth of 3–5 mm. To prevent skin maceration, avoid spreading onto healthy (non-ulcerated) skin. Cover with an absorbent pad or gauze and apply pressure bandaging as needed. Change dressings daily or every 48 hours for less exudative wounds [Australian].
- **Finger-Tip Injuries:** Apply a 3–5 mm layer. Secure with a conventional dressing or a plastic/surgical glove finger fixed with waterproof tape. Change dressings every 2–3 days [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Wound cleansing and debridement should be performed prior to application.
- Re-apply to any areas where the cream has been removed by patient activity.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Neonates:** Contraindicated in premature infants and newborn infants during the first month of life due to the risk of sulfonamide-induced kernicterus [Australian].
- **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated at or near term pregnancy [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to silver sulfadiazine, sulfonamides, or excipients (notably cetyl alcohol and propylene glycol) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Life-threatening reactions, including Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), have been reported. Highest risk occurs within the first weeks of treatment. Discontinue permanently at the first sign of progressive rash or mucosal lesions [Australian].
- **Haematological Effects:** Transient leucopenia may occur, typically 2–3 days after starting therapy. Regular blood counts are advised for patients on long-term or extensive treatment [Australian].
- **Systemic Absorption:** In extensive burns, significant absorption of silver and sulfadiazine can occur. Monitor serum sulfonamide levels (therapeutic range 8–12 mg%), renal function, and check for sulfonamide crystalluria [Australian].
- **G6PD Deficiency:** Use with caution as haemolysis may occur [Australian].
- **Wound Management:** Application may delay the separation of the burn eschar and increase the risk of fungal invasion [Australian].
- **Hepatic and Renal Impairment:** Use with caution; monitor for systemic toxicity [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Leukopenia (3–5% of patients; usually self-limiting), application site burning, pruritus, application site rash (including eczema and contact dermatitis). |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Argyria (systemic silver accumulation causing skin discolouration), especially after prolonged use on large areas. |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Renal failure, SJS, and TEN. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Enzymatic Debriding Agents:** Silver ions may inactivate these agents; concurrent use may be inappropriate [Australian].
- **Oral Hypoglycaemics and Phenytoin:** Systemic absorption of sulfonamides may potentiate the effects of these drugs; monitor blood levels [Australian].
- **Cimetidine:** Concurrent use in large-area burns may increase the incidence of leukopenia [Australian].
- **Anticoagulants and Immunosuppressants:** Sulfonamides may alter the effects of oral anticoagulants, methotrexate, and cyclosporine [Australian].
- **Hormonal Contraceptives:** Isolated reports suggest potential interference with contraceptive effectiveness [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Sulfonamides may cause kernicterus in the first month of life by displacing bilirubin. Avoid use in the last month of pregnancy. Animal studies (subcutaneous) showed low-incidence fetal abnormalities at high doses, but the clinical significance of topical use is unknown.
- **Breastfeeding:** Use with caution. Sulfadiazine is excreted in breast milk (15–35% of serum levels) [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data on topical use; subcutaneous animal studies showed no effect on fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Contraindicated in infants <1 month old.
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical experience has not identified major differences in response compared to younger patients, though greater sensitivity in some individuals cannot be ruled out [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution due to the risk of systemic sulfonamide accumulation [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Silver sulfadiazine is bactericidal. It acts primarily on the bacterial cell membrane and cell wall. Unlike other sulfonamides, its action does not depend solely on folic acid synthesis inhibition and is not antagonised by p-aminobenzoic acid (PABA) [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Broad-spectrum antimicrobial activity against Gram-positive and Gram-negative organisms, including *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, some yeasts, and fungi [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
  - **Absorption:** Up to 10% of the sulfadiazine may be absorbed. Silver ions are minimally absorbed, though argyria can occur with extensive use.
  - **Distribution/Metabolism:** Slowly metabolised in contact with wound exudates.
  - **Elimination:** Absorbed sulfadiazine is excreted in the urine [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Protection:** Protect from light; the cream may yellow upon exposure to light.
- **Shelf Life/Handling:** 
  - Jars (250g/500g): Discard **24 hours** after opening.
  - Tubes: Discard **7 days** after opening.
  - Each container is for the treatment of a single patient [Australian].
