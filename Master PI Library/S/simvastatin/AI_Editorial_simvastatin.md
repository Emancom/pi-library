# Editorial Product Information: simvastatin

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): simvastatin
- Chemical class: HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor (statin)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral
- Physicochemical note: simvastatin is described as a crystalline powder with low aqueous solubility and greater solubility in organic solvents (details of solubility provided in source PI).

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- As an adjunct to diet for the treatment of hypercholesterolaemia.
- To reduce the risk of cardiovascular death, major cardiovascular events (including stroke), and hospitalisation for angina in patients at high risk of coronary heart disease (CHD) — defined to include patients with diabetes, prior stroke or other cerebrovascular disease, peripheral vascular disease, or existing CHD.
- As an adjunct to diet for treatment of heterozygous familial hypercholesterolaemia (HeFH) in adolescent boys and girls at least one year post-menarche, aged 10–17 years.

If an indication is approved only in particular jurisdictions, that differentiation is not specified in the available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration

General principles
- Dosing range: 10–80 mg once daily in the evening.
- Dose adjustments should be made at intervals of not less than 4 weeks.
- The 80 mg daily dose should be reserved for patients at high risk of cardiovascular complications who have not reached treatment goals on lower doses and only when expected benefits outweigh potential risks (see Warnings and Precautions: myopathy/rhabdomyolysis).

Adult dosing — routine
- Patients at high risk of CHD (including diabetes, prior stroke/cerebrovascular disease, peripheral vascular disease, existing CHD): usual starting dose 40 mg once daily in the evening. Therapy may be initiated while starting dietary/exercise measures.
- Patients with hypercholesterolaemia (not in high-risk categories): recommended starting dose 10–20 mg once daily in the evening; individualise according to response.

Dose limits and concomitant medications (dose ceiling recommendations)
- Potent CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., systemic azole antifungals, certain macrolides, HIV protease inhibitors, boceprevir, telaprevir, nefazodone, drugs containing cobicistat): concomitant use is contraindicated. If short-term potent CYP3A4 inhibitor therapy is unavoidable, simvastatin should be suspended for the duration of the inhibitor therapy.
- Gemfibrozil, ciclosporin, danazol: concomitant use is contraindicated.
- Fusidic acid (systemic): concomitant use is contraindicated; if systemic fusidic acid is essential, discontinue simvastatin for the duration of fusidic acid therapy and for 7 days after the last dose.
- Other fibrates (except fenofibrate): do not exceed 10 mg simvastatin daily.
- Fenofibrate: use with caution; no clear additive myopathy signal identified in some studies but either agent alone can cause myopathy.
- Amiodarone, verapamil, diltiazem, and products containing elbasvir or grazoprevir: do not exceed 20 mg simvastatin daily.
- Amlodipine: do not exceed 40 mg simvastatin daily.
- Niacin (nicotinic acid) ≥ 1 g/day: do not exceed 40 mg simvastatin daily; combination not recommended in Asian patients due to higher observed myopathy risk in trial data.
- Lomitapide (in HoFH): do not exceed 40 mg simvastatin daily for patients receiving lomitapide.
- Daptomycin: co-administration is not recommended; consider temporary suspension of simvastatin if daptomycin is required.
- Grapefruit juice: avoid grapefruit juice.

Renal impairment
- simvastatin is not significantly excreted unchanged in urine, but data in renal impairment are limited.
- In severe renal insufficiency (creatinine clearance <30 mL/min), dosages above 10 mg/day should be used with caution and only after careful consideration.

Paediatric (HeFH, 10–17 years)
- Recommended starting dose: 10 mg once daily in the evening.
- Recommended dosing range: 10–40 mg/day.
- Maximum recommended dose: 40 mg/day.
- Dose should be individualised according to therapeutic goal.

Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

Administration instructions
- Take once daily in the evening. Reinforce continuation of cholesterol-lowering diet during therapy.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to simvastatin or to any excipient listed in the product information.
- Active liver disease or unexplained persistent elevations of serum transaminases.
- Pregnancy and breastfeeding.
- Women of childbearing potential unless on effective contraception and highly unlikely to conceive.
- Patients with myopathy attributable to other lipid-lowering agents.
- Concomitant administration with potent CYP3A4 inhibitors (examples listed in Section 4).
- Concomitant administration with gemfibrozil, ciclosporin, or danazol.
- Concomitant administration with fusidic acid (systemic).

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

Boxed/critical warning (emphasis)
- **Myopathy and rhabdomyolysis risk:** simvastatin can cause myopathy, sometimes progressing to rhabdomyolysis with or without acute renal failure secondary to myoglobinuria; rare fatalities have occurred. The risk increases with higher doses and with concomitant medications or conditions that raise simvastatin plasma exposure. Use the lowest effective dose and observe contraindications and dose limits with interacting agents.

General warnings and precautions
- Myopathy/rhabdomyolysis:
  - Advise all patients starting therapy or whose dose is increased to promptly report any unexplained muscle pain, tenderness or weakness.
  - Discontinue simvastatin immediately if myopathy is diagnosed or suspected.
  - A CK > 10 × upper limit of normal (ULN) in the presence of muscle symptoms indicates myopathy.
  - Periodic CK measurement may be considered when initiating therapy or increasing dose; CK monitoring is recommended for patients titrating to 80 mg/day. Monitoring does not guarantee prevention of myopathy.
  - Risk factors for myopathy include higher simvastatin dose (80 mg markedly increases risk), advanced age (≥ 65 years), female sex, renal impairment, uncontrolled hypothyroidism, and interacting drugs.
  - Increased myopathy incidence observed in Chinese patients compared with non-Chinese in one large trial; exercise caution in Asian patients and use the lowest necessary dose.

- Hepatic effects:
  - Persistent transaminase elevations (>3 × ULN) have been observed; incidence varies by dose and trial. Active liver disease and persistent unexplained transaminase elevations are contraindications.
  - LFTs should be measured before initiation and thereafter when clinically indicated. Patients titrated to 80 mg should have an additional LFT at 3 months.
  - ALT elevations may originate from muscle; evaluate CK concurrently if transaminases rise.

- Myasthenia gravis/ocular myasthenia:
  - Rare reports describe new-onset or exacerbation of myasthenia gravis associated with statin therapy; discontinue simvastatin if these conditions occur.

- Perioperative and intercurrent illness:
  - Consider temporarily stopping simvastatin a few days before elective major surgery and during any major medical or surgical condition.

Monitoring requirements
- Baseline liver function tests before starting therapy.
- Repeat LFTs when clinically indicated; additional test at 3 months for patients titrating to 80 mg/day.
- Consider baseline CK measurement in patients at increased risk of myopathy and monitor if muscle symptoms occur or dose is increased; periodic CK recommended for those titrating to 80 mg/day.

Specific clinical precautions
- Evaluate and treat secondary causes of hypercholesterolaemia (e.g., uncontrolled diabetes, hypothyroidism, nephrotic syndrome, dysproteinaemias, obstructive liver disease, alcohol) before initiating therapy.
- Use caution when combining with drugs that increase simvastatin exposure or independently increase myopathy risk.

## 8. Drug Interactions

Pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic interactions and management recommendations (as specified in the available sources):

- Potent CYP3A4 inhibitors (examples: itraconazole, ketoconazole, posaconazole, voriconazole, erythromycin, clarithromycin, telithromycin, HIV protease inhibitors, boceprevir, telaprevir, nefazodone, drugs containing cobicistat):
  - Mechanism: increase simvastatin exposure by inhibiting CYP3A4-mediated metabolism → increased myopathy risk.
  - Recommendation: Concomitant use is contraindicated. If short-term potent CYP3A4 inhibitor use is unavoidable, suspend simvastatin for the duration.

- Gemfibrozil:
  - Mechanism: increases risk of myopathy when co-administered.
  - Recommendation: Concomitant use contraindicated.

- Ciclosporin:
  - Recommendation: Concomitant use contraindicated.

- Danazol:
  - Recommendation: Concomitant use contraindicated.

- Fusidic acid (systemic):
  - Recommendation: Concomitant use contraindicated; if systemic fusidic acid is essential, stop simvastatin for the duration and resume 7 days after last fusidic acid dose.

- Other fibrates (excluding fenofibrate):
  - Recommendation: Do not exceed 10 mg simvastatin daily.

- Fenofibrate:
  - Recommendation: Use caution; although some short-term studies did not show excess myopathy beyond individual risks, both agents can cause myopathy when used alone.

- Amiodarone:
  - Recommendation: Do not exceed 20 mg simvastatin daily (increased myopathy observed with higher simvastatin doses).

- Verapamil and diltiazem:
  - Recommendation: Do not exceed 20 mg simvastatin daily.

- Amlodipine:
  - Recommendation: Do not exceed 40 mg simvastatin daily.

- Lomitapide (in HoFH):
  - Recommendation: Do not exceed 40 mg simvastatin daily for patients receiving lomitapide.

- BCRP inhibitors (e.g., elbasvir and grazoprevir):
  - Mechanism: inhibition of transporters may raise simvastatin concentrations.
  - Recommendation: Do not exceed 20 mg simvastatin daily when co-administered with products containing elbasvir or grazoprevir.

- Niacin (nicotinic acid) ≥ 1 g/day:
  - Recommendation: Do not exceed 40 mg simvastatin daily. Combined use with lipid-modifying doses of niacin has been associated with increased myopathy risk; coadministration not recommended in Asian patients based on higher observed incidence.

- Daptomycin:
  - Recommendation: Co-administration is not recommended; consider temporary suspension of simvastatin if daptomycin therapy is required.

- Grapefruit juice:
  - Recommendation: Avoid grapefruit juice due to potential for increased simvastatin exposure.

- Moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors:
  - Recommendation: May increase simvastatin exposure, especially at higher simvastatin doses; dosage adjustment of simvastatin may be necessary.

Mechanistic summary
- Many clinically significant interactions increase plasma levels of simvastatin or its active acid metabolite via CYP3A4 inhibition or transporter inhibition (e.g., BCRP), thereby increasing risk of myopathy and rhabdomyolysis. Some agents independently increase the risk of myopathy (e.g., fibrates, niacin, daptomycin) and require cautious co-administration.

Jurisdiction-specific interaction differences
- Not specified in available sources beyond the recommendations and dose ceilings provided above.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- Pregnancy: simvastatin is contraindicated in pregnancy. Women of childbearing potential should use effective contraception and should only be treated if highly unlikely to conceive. (Pregnancy category systems [e.g., specific letter categories] are Not specified in available sources.)
- Breastfeeding: simvastatin is contraindicated during nursing.
- Fertility: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Use in Special Populations

Paediatric use
- Approved for use in adolescents 10–17 years with heterozygous familial hypercholesterolaemia who are at least one year post-menarche for girls.
- Recommended starting dose: 10 mg once daily in the evening.
- Dose range: 10–40 mg/day; maximum recommended dose: 40 mg/day.
- Dosing should be individualised to therapeutic goals.

Elderly
- Advanced age (≥ 65 years) is a risk factor for myopathy; exercise caution and monitor as appropriate. No routine dose reduction specified in available sources solely for age.

Renal impairment
- simvastatin is not substantially excreted unchanged by the kidney, but data in patients with impaired renal function are limited.
- In severe renal insufficiency (CrCl <30 mL/min): dosages above 10 mg/day should be used only after careful consideration and with caution.

Hepatic impairment
- Active liver disease or unexplained persistent transaminase elevations are contraindications.
- No specific dose adjustments by Child-Pugh class provided in available sources; use is contraindicated in active liver disease.

## 11. Pharmacology

Mechanism of action
- simvastatin is an inhibitor of HMG-CoA reductase, the rate-limiting enzyme in cholesterol biosynthesis, resulting in decreased cholesterol synthesis and lowered plasma LDL-cholesterol.

Pharmacokinetics
- Metabolism: simvastatin is metabolised via pathways involving CYP3A4 and is subject to effects of CYP3A4 inhibitors; uptake/efflux transporter pathways (including breast cancer resistance protein/BCRP) influence exposure.
- Plasma levels of simvastatin and its active acid metabolite determine HMG-CoA reductase inhibitory activity; increases in plasma exposure (from drug interactions or other causes) raise myopathy risk.
- Absorption, distribution and elimination numeric parameters (bioavailability, t1/2, volume of distribution, percent renal excretion) are Not specified in available sources.

Pharmacodynamics
- LDL-C lowering is dose-related; higher doses produce greater LDL-C reductions but also increase risk of adverse effects such as myopathy and transaminase elevations.

## 12. Clinical Trials

Key trials and findings (as described in available sources)

- 4S (Scandinavian Simvastatin Survival Study):
  - Design: Large randomized clinical trial including simvastatin-treated and placebo groups.
  - Follow-up/Findings relevant to safety: Median follow-up ~5.4 years. In safety analyses, single and persistent ALT elevations were reported; the number of patients with at least one ALT >3×ULN was 46 (2.2%) in the simvastatin group vs 32 (1.4%) in placebo. Myopathy events were rare in 4S (one case among 1399 on 20 mg and none among 822 on 40 mg in the dataset cited).

- SEARCH trial:
  - Design: Randomised trial comparing simvastatin 80 mg/day versus 20 mg/day in patients with prior myocardial infarction (mean follow-up 6.7 years).
  - Findings: Myopathy incidence approximately 1.0% with simvastatin 80 mg vs 0.02% with 20 mg. Rhabdomyolysis events clustered in the 80 mg group (estimated 0.1–0.2%).

- HPS (Heart Protection Study):
  - Design: Large randomized trial of simvastatin 40 mg/day versus placebo.
  - Safety data: In 20,536 randomised patients, confirmed ALT elevations >3×ULN were 0.21% for simvastatin 40 mg vs 0.09% for placebo.

- Shorter-duration controlled trials:
  - Two 6-month controlled clinical studies reported drug-related persistent hepatic transaminase elevations of 0.7% at 40 mg and 1.8% at 80 mg.

- Trials evaluating interactions/combinations:
  - Combined therapy with niacin/laropiprant plus simvastatin in a trial of patients at high cardiovascular risk (median follow-up 3.9 years) showed no incremental cardiovascular outcome benefit for addition of niacin but indicated higher myopathy incidence in Chinese patients who received extended-release niacin/laropiprant plus simvastatin versus simvastatin alone; combination therapy increased myopathy risk in Asian subjects in that trial.

- Population-specific safety observation:
  - In a trial of patients at high cardiovascular risk treated with simvastatin 40 mg/day (median follow-up 3.9 years), myopathy incidence was approximately 0.24% in Chinese patients vs 0.05% in non-Chinese patients.

Note: Trial summaries above emphasise safety findings reported in the available sources (myopathy and hepatic enzyme changes). Detailed efficacy outcomes (relative risk reductions for cardiovascular endpoints) are not comprehensively described in the provided excerpts and therefore are not detailed here.
