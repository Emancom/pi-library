# Editorial Product Information: Sucralfate

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of sucralfate product information, synthesized for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labelling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Sucralfate (an α-D-glucopyranoside, β-D-fructofuranosyl-, octakis (hydrogen sulfate), aluminum complex).
- **Chemical Class**: Gastrointestinal agent; aluminum complex of a sulfated disaccharide.
- **Brand Names**: Generic (distributed by Cardinal Health/Teva Pharmaceuticals) [US].
- **ATC Code**: A02BX02 (not specified in source; provided for clinical context).
- **Dosage Form and Strength**: 
    - Oral Tablet: 1 g [US].
- **Route of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Pliva Hrvatska (Manufacturer); Teva Pharmaceuticals/Cardinal Health (Distributor) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Active Duodenal Ulcer**: Short-term treatment (up to 8 weeks) of active duodenal ulcer. While symptomatic relief may occur within 1–2 weeks, therapy should continue for 4–8 weeks unless healing is confirmed by diagnostic imaging or endoscopy [US-approved].
- **Maintenance Therapy**: Prophylactic use at reduced dosage for duodenal ulcer patients following the healing of acute ulcers [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing
- **Active Duodenal Ulcer**: 1 g administered four times daily.
- **Maintenance Therapy**: 1 g administered twice daily.

### Administration Instructions
- **Timing**: Administer on an empty stomach (at least 1 hour before meals or 2 hours after).
- **Antacid Use**: Antacids may be used for pain relief; however, they should not be administered within 30 minutes before or after a sucralfate dose [US].
- **Drug Separation**: Due to the potential for sucralfate to alter the absorption of other medications, a 2-hour interval is recommended between sucralfate and the administration of concomitant medications where bioavailability is critical.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity**: Known hypersensitivity to sucralfate or any of the excipients (corn starch, magnesium stearate, microcrystalline cellulose) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Renal Impairment/Dialysis**: Small amounts of aluminum are absorbed following oral administration. In patients with chronic renal failure or those undergoing dialysis, the excretion of absorbed aluminum is impaired. This may lead to aluminum accumulation and toxicity (e.g., aluminum osteodystrophy, osteomalacia, encephalopathy). Concomitant use with other aluminum-containing products (e.g., antacids) increases this risk [US].
- **Aspiration Risk**: There are reports of sucralfate tablet aspiration resulting in respiratory complications. Caution is advised in patients with impaired swallowing mechanisms (e.g., history of aspiration, dysphagia, recent intubation, or conditions affecting the gag reflex) [US].
- **Bezoar Formation**: Bezoars have been reported, particularly in patients with delayed gastric emptying or those receiving concomitant enteral tube feedings [US].
- **Inadvertent Intravenous Administration**: Sucralfate is for oral use only. Intravenous injection of insoluble sucralfate has resulted in fatal complications, including pulmonary and cerebral emboli [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally minor and rarely lead to treatment discontinuation (approx. 4.7% of patients in clinical trials).

| Frequency | System Organ Class | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Gastrointestinal | Constipation (2%) |
| **Rare (<0.5%)** | Gastrointestinal | Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, gastric discomfort, indigestion, flatulence, dry mouth |
| | Dermatological | Pruritus, rash |
| | Nervous System | Dizziness, insomnia, sleepiness, vertigo |
| | Other | Back pain, headache |

### Post-marketing and Serious Effects
- **Hypersensitivity**: Anaphylactic reactions, bronchospasm, laryngeal edema, edema of the mouth/pharynx, dyspnea, and urticaria [US].
- **Mechanical**: Bezoar formation [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Absorption-Related (Pharmacokinetic)**: Sucralfate may reduce the bioavailability of several drugs when administered simultaneously. Clinically significant reductions in absorption have been noted with:
    - Cimetidine
    - Digoxin
    - Fluoroquinolone antibiotics (e.g., ciprofloxacin, norfloxacin, ofloxacin)
    - Ketoconazole
    - L-thyroxine
    - Phenytoin
    - Quinidine
    - Ranitidine
    - Tetracycline
    - Theophylline
- **Warfarin**: Reports are conflicting. Some cases suggest subtherapeutic prothrombin times when co-administered, though clinical studies have also shown no significant change in prothrombin time. Monitoring is recommended [US].
- **Aluminum-containing Antacids**: Potential for increased total body burden of aluminum, especially in renal impairment [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: **Category B** [US]. Teratogenicity studies in mice, rats, and rabbits at 50 times the human dose showed no evidence of fetal harm. Because human studies are lacking, use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: It is unknown if sucralfate is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution when administering to nursing women [US].
- **Fertility**: Rat studies at 38 times the human dose showed no impairment of fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and effectiveness in paediatric patients have not been established [US].
- **Geriatric Use**: Dose selection should be cautious (usually starting at the low end of the range) due to the higher frequency of decreased renal, hepatic, or cardiac function. Monitoring renal function may be beneficial as the drug is substantially excreted by the kidney [US].
- **Renal Impairment**: Use with caution. Patients with impaired renal function are at risk of aluminum toxicity. Monitor for signs of accumulation [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Sucralfate acts locally rather than systemically. In an acidic environment, it forms an ulcer-adherent complex with proteinaceous exudate (such as albumin and fibrinogen) at the ulcer site. This complex provides a physical barrier against hydrogen ions, pepsin, and bile salts. It also inhibits pepsin activity by approximately 32% [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Minimally absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract.
    - **Metabolism/Elimination**: The absorbed portion (sulfated disaccharide) is primarily excreted in the urine. Approximately 95% of the dose remains in the GI tract and is excreted in the feces [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics**: Provides 14–16 mEq of acid-neutralizing capacity per 1 g dose [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Acute Duodenal Ulcer**: Multicenter US trials (placebo-controlled) demonstrated significant healing at 4 weeks. One study showed 75.2% healing for sucralfate vs. 63.6% for placebo; a second study showed 92% healing for sucralfate vs. 58% for placebo [US].
- **Maintenance Therapy**: Two multicenter trials demonstrated that 1 g twice daily significantly reduced ulcer recurrence over 12 months. Recurrence rates at 12 months were 27% for sucralfate vs. 65% for placebo in one trial [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Packaging**: Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container with a child-resistant closure [US].
