# Editorial Product Information: Sotalol

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **sotalol** for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data, dosing, and safety information.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority for the most current and specific prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Sotalol hydrochloride.
- **Chemical Class:** Non-selective beta-adrenoreceptor antagonist (Class II antiarrhythmic) with Class III antiarrhythmic properties (potassium channel blockade).
- **Brand Names:** APO-SOTALOL [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** C07AA07.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - 80 mg tablets: Blue, capsule-shaped, biconvex, engraved "APO-80" and scored. [Australian]
    - 160 mg tablets: Blue, capsule-shaped, biconvex, engraved "APO-160" and scored. [Australian]
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Sponsor:** Apotex Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Supraventricular Arrhythmias:** Prevention and treatment. [Australian]
- **Ventricular Arrhythmias:** Prevention and treatment. [Australian]

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Initial Dose:** 160 mg daily, administered in two divided doses (80 mg twice daily) at approximately 12-hour intervals. [Australian]
- **Maintenance Range:** Most patients respond to 160–320 mg/day in divided doses. [Australian]
- **Maximum Dose:** For life-threatening refractory ventricular arrhythmias, doses of 480–640 mg/day may be required, provided the benefit outweighs the increased proarrhythmic risk. [Australian]

### Administration Instructions
- **Food Interaction:** **Sotalol** should be taken on an empty stomach, ideally 1–2 hours before or 2 hours after meals (bioavailability is reduced by food, especially milk). [Australian]
- **Titration:** Dose increments should occur every 2–3 days to allow for steady-state attainment and QT interval monitoring. [Australian]

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Respiratory:** Bronchospasm (e.g., bronchial asthma, COPD) and allergic disorders (e.g., allergic rhinitis) predisposing to bronchospasm. [Australian]
- **Cardiac:**
    - Sinus bradycardia (<45–50 beats/minute).
    - Second or third-degree AV block (without a functioning pacemaker).
    - Sick sinus syndrome (without a functioning pacemaker).
    - Uncontrolled congestive heart failure (CHF).
    - Right ventricular failure secondary to pulmonary hypertension.
    - Significant right ventricular hypertrophy.
    - Shock (cardiogenic and hypovolaemic).
    - Anaesthesia producing myocardial depression. [Australian]
- **Renal:** Severe renal impairment (creatinine clearance < 10 mL/min). [Australian]
- **Other:** Congenital or acquired long QT syndromes; hypersensitivity to **sotalol** or excipients. [Australian]

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Proarrhythmia
- **Sotalol** can cause serious proarrhythmias, most notably **Torsade de Pointes (TdP)**, which is dose-dependent. [Australian]
- **Risk Factors:** Female gender, history of cardiomegaly or CHF, excessive QTc prolongation (>500 msec), renal insufficiency, and electrolyte imbalances (hypokalaemia/hypomagnesaemia). [Australian]
- **Monitoring:** Therapy should be initiated in a facility capable of cardiac rhythm assessment. QTc should be monitored; consider dose reduction or discontinuation if QTc exceeds 550 msec. [Australian]

### Abrupt Withdrawal
- Avoid abrupt discontinuation in patients with coronary artery disease. Severe angina exacerbation, myocardial infarction, and ventricular arrhythmias may occur. Dosage should be tapered over 8–14 days. [Australian]

### Cardiac Failure
- Beta-blockade may further depress myocardial contractility. Use with caution in patients with left ventricular dysfunction controlled by other therapies (e.g., ACE inhibitors, diuretics). [Australian]

### Metabolic and Other Precautions
- **Diabetes:** May mask symptoms of acute hypoglycaemia (e.g., tachycardia) and impair glycaemic control. [Australian]
- **Thyroid:** May mask signs of hyperthyroidism; abrupt withdrawal may precipitate thyroid storm. [Australian]
- **Anaphylaxis:** Patients may be more sensitive to allergens and less responsive to standard doses of adrenaline. [Australian]
- **Peripheral Circulation:** May exacerbate symptoms of peripheral vascular disease. [Australian]

## 6. Adverse Effects
Approximately 18% of patients in clinical trials discontinued **sotalol** due to adverse events. [Australian]

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Proarrhythmia:** Torsade de Pointes (TdP), new or worsened ventricular tachycardia (VT), and ventricular fibrillation (VF). [Australian]
- **Cardiovascular:** Bradycardia (< 50 bpm), hypotension, syncope, and congestive heart failure. [Australian]

### Frequency of Adverse Reactions (>1%) [Australian]
| System Organ Class | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Cardiovascular** | Chest pain, palpitations, oedema, ECG abnormalities, cold extremities |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Diarrhoea, nausea/vomiting, flatulence, dyspepsia, abdominal pain |
| **Nervous System** | Dizziness (2%), headache, fatigue (4%), asthenia (2%), drowsiness, sleep disturbances, depression, paraesthesia, anxiety |
| **Respiratory** | Dyspnoea (3%) |
| **Dermatological** | Rash |
| **Urogenital** | Sexual dysfunction |

### Less Common (<1%)
- Cutaneous thickening, pruritus, unusual dreams, retroperitoneal fibrosis, facial atrophy, and thrombocytopenia (frequency unknown). [Australian]

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Antiarrhythmics:** Concomitant use with Class IA (e.g., disopyramide, quinidine), Class IB, Class IC (e.g., flecainide), or Class III (e.g., amiodarone) agents is not recommended due to additive effects on the refractory period and proarrhythmic risk. [Australian]
- **QT-Prolonging Drugs:** Avoid quinolones, tricyclic antidepressants, and certain antihistamines (terfenadine, astemizole). [Australian]
- **Calcium Channel Blockers:** Risk of additive hypotension, bradycardia, and conduction defects (especially with cardiodepressant CCBs). [Australian]
- **Digoxin:** Proarrhythmic events are more common in patients receiving digoxin, often associated with underlying CHF. [Australian]
- **Clonidine:** Beta-blockers may increase blood pressure rebound upon clonidine withdrawal; clonidine should not be stopped until several days after **sotalol** is discontinued. [Australian]
- **Catecholamine-Depleting Agents (e.g., Reserpine):** Risk of excessive reduction in sympathetic tone (hypotension/bradycardia). [Australian]
- **Alcohol:** May reduce the plasma clearance of **sotalol**. [Australian]

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. **Sotalol** crosses the placenta and may cause fetal or neonatal bradycardia. Use only if benefits outweigh risks to the fetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** **Sotalol** is actively excreted in breast milk with a milk:plasma ratio of 5.4:1. It should not be administered to nursing mothers. [Australian]
- **Fertility:** No data available. [Australian]

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children under 18 years of age have not been established. [Australian]
- **Elderly:** Volume of distribution at steady state is approximately halved in the elderly. Dose selection should be cautious, reflecting the higher frequency of renal impairment. [Australian]
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosage adjustment is mandatory as **sotalol** is primarily excreted renally.
    - Contraindicated if CrCl < 10 mL/min. [Australian]
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment necessary as **sotalol** does not undergo hepatic metabolism. [Australian]

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:**
    - **Class II:** Non-selective beta-adrenoreceptor antagonism (without ISA or membrane stabilising activity), leading to decreased heart rate and renin release.
    - **Class III:** Prolongs the action potential duration by increasing the effective refractory period in atrial, ventricular, and accessory pathways. [Australian]
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Well absorbed; peak levels reached in 1.5–5 hours. Bioavailability ~100%.
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins; low lipid solubility (minimal CNS penetration). Vd: 1.6–2.4 L/kg.
    - **Metabolism:** None (no first-pass effect).
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (75% unchanged in urine); mean half-life is 12.7 ± 1.6 hours. [Australian]

## 11. Clinical Trials
- In clinical trials of 3,257 patients with arrhythmias, **sotalol** caused new or worsened ventricular arrhythmias in 4.3% of patients, including TdP (2.4%) and sustained VT (1%). [Australian]
- In patients with sustained VT/VF, the incidence of serious proarrhythmia was dose-dependent: 0% at 80 mg/day, 1.8% at 161–320 mg/day, and 6.8% at >640 mg/day. [Australian]

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C. [Australian]
- Protect from light. [Australian]
