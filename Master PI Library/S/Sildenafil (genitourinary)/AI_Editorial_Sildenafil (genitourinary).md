# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview intended for healthcare professionals to facilitate clinical decision-making. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI or prescribing information required for clinical practice in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved labelling before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (Generic name); chemical class: Carrier molecule/amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from genetic metabolic disorders [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2–3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in patients presenting with severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Dosing Frequency:** Administer every 3 to 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** Up to 300 mg/kg has been administered in clinical practice [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Initial Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow bolus (2–3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Titration:** Dose adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of treatment if plasma levels are adequate [US].

### Administration and Monitoring
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels prior to therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma concentrations is recommended. Target plasma free carnitine levels should ideally remain between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Visual Inspection:** Inspect parenteral products for particulate matter or discoloration before administration [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** Not specified as an absolute contraindication in available sources, but caution is advised. [Note: Source states "None known" under formal contraindications but lists serious hypersensitivity in warnings].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, particularly in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and monitor the patient [US].
- **Neurologic Effects (Seizures):** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Toxic Metabolite Accumulation:** Chronic administration of high *oral* doses in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted renally [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following table summarizes adverse events reported in clinical trials of ESRD patients on hemodialysis at a frequency of ≥5% (regardless of causality) [US].

| Body System | Adverse Event (Incidence ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Pain, injection site reaction, flu syndrome, chest pain, fever, accidental injury, asthenia, headache |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, vascular disorder |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, abdominal pain, anorexia, dyspepsia |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia, hyperkalemia, weight increase/decrease, peripheral edema |
| **Nervous System** | Dizziness, paresthesia, insomnia, depression |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough, dyspnea, rhinitis |
| **Other** | Anemia, pruritus, back pain, eye disorder, taste perversion |

- **Postmarketing Experience:** Seizures and serious hypersensitivity reactions (anaphylaxis) have been identified during post-approval use [US].
- **Clinical Note:** Transient nausea, vomiting, and body odor have been observed; however, incidences are difficult to estimate due to underlying pathologies [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended to monitor INR levels upon initiation of levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned a lettered category in this source; however, reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm [US]. 
    - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** 
    - **Safety:** Not specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration [US].
    - **Recommendation:** Weigh the benefits of supplementation to the mother against potential risks to the infant. Consider discontinuing nursing or the drug based on clinical necessity [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal reproductive studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Refer to the "Dosage and Administration" section; specific metabolic dosing is based on weight (mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific data not provided in the source; standard precautions for renal function are generally applicable [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - For ESRD patients on dialysis, follow specific post-dialysis dosing protocols [US].
    - High oral doses are associated with toxic metabolite (TMA/TMAO) accumulation in renal insufficiency [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with metabolic defects or organic acidopathies, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus, ~76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Studies:** In a study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg dose), pre-dialysis (trough) levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks of therapy [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses in ESRD patients demonstrated a dose-dependent increase in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Clinical Outcomes Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the impact on specific signs, symptoms, or clinical outcomes in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C [US].
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing [US].
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial, as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature when mixed in PVC bags with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) [US].
