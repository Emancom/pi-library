# Editorial Product Information: Sunitinib

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **sunitinib** for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI used for prescribing in a particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Sunitinib (as malate), a small-molecule multi-receptor tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI).
- **Brand Name(s):** **ARX-SUNITINIB** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01EX01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Hard gelatin capsules: 12.5 mg, 25 mg, 37.5 mg, and 50 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Gastrointestinal Stromal Tumour (GIST):** Treatment of GIST following failure of imatinib mesilate due to resistance or intolerance [Australian].
- **Advanced Renal Cell Carcinoma (RCC):** Treatment of advanced/metastatic RCC [Australian].
- **Pancreatic Neuroendocrine Tumours (pNET):** Treatment of unresectable, well-differentiated pancreatic NET [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **RCC and GIST:** The recommended dose is 50 mg orally once daily for 4 consecutive weeks, followed by a 2-week rest period (Schedule 4/2), comprising a 6-week cycle [Australian].
- **Pancreatic NET:** The recommended dose is 37.5 mg orally once daily, administered continuously without a scheduled rest period [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- May be taken with or without food [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, the patient should not take an additional dose but should resume the usual prescribed dose the following day [Australian].

### Titration and Dose Adjustment
- **Dose Steps:** Modifications should occur in 12.5 mg increments based on individual safety and tolerability [Australian].
- **RCC/GIST Range:** Minimum 25 mg daily; maximum 75 mg daily [Australian].
- **pNET Range:** Maximum 50 mg daily [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Interactions:** 
    - With strong inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole): Consider reducing the **sunitinib** dose to a minimum of 37.5 mg (GIST/RCC) or 25 mg (pNET) [Australian].
    - With strong inducers (e.g., rifampicin): Consider increasing the **sunitinib** dose in 12.5 mg steps up to 87.5 mg (GIST/RCC) or 62.5 mg (pNET) [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to **sunitinib** malate or any excipients in the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Cases of hepatic failure, including fatalities (<1%), have been reported. Monitor LFTs (ALT, AST, bilirubin) at baseline, during every cycle, and as clinically indicated [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Effects:**
    - **LVEF Decrease:** Reductions in left ventricular ejection fraction have been observed. Monitor for signs of congestive heart failure (CHF); baseline and periodic LVEF evaluations are recommended [Australian].
    - **QT Prolongation:** **Sunitinib** can prolong the QT interval. Use with caution in patients with a history of QT prolongation or those taking antiarrhythmics [Australian].
- **Haemorrhagic Events:** Serious and sometimes fatal GI, respiratory, urinary tract, and brain haemorrhages have occurred. Epistaxis is the most common event [Australian].
- **Hypertension:** Very common; patients should be screened and controlled with standard anti-hypertensives. Temporary suspension is recommended for severe, uncontrolled hypertension [Australian].
- **Skin and Tissues:** Skin discolouration (yellowing) and depigmentation of hair/skin are common. Severe reactions, including Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS) and Erythema Multiforme (EM), have been reported; discontinue permanently if SJS is confirmed [Australian].
- **Thyroid Dysfunction:** Both hypothyroidism and hyperthyroidism may occur. Baseline and periodic laboratory monitoring of thyroid function is required [Australian].
- **Osteonecrosis of the Jaw (ONJ):** Primarily observed in patients receiving concomitant IV bisphosphonates. Perform dental exams prior to treatment [Australian].
- **Other Precautions:** Reversible Posterior Leukoencephalopathy Syndrome (seizures, headache, visual loss), Thrombotic Microangiopathy (TMA), Proteinuria (monitor via urinalysis), and Hypoglycaemia (monitor blood glucose in diabetics) [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Summary
- **Very Common (>10%):** Fatigue, asthenia, diarrhoea, nausea, mucositis/stomatitis, vomiting, dyspepsia, abdominal pain, constipation, hypertension, skin discolouration, hand-foot syndrome, rash, altered taste (dysgeusia), anorexia, and bleeding (all sites) [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Headache, arthralgia, back pain, dyspnoea, cough, fever, and hypothyroidism [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Pancreatitis, cardiac failure, and LVEF decrease [Australian].
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Stevens-Johnson Syndrome, Torsades de Pointes, and necrotising fasciitis [Australian].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Fatalities have been reported in relation to haemorrhage, hepatic failure, congestive heart failure, and gastrointestinal perforation [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic (CYP3A4):**
    - **Inhibitors:** Strong inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, ritonavir, clarithromycin, grapefruit juice) increase **sunitinib** plasma concentrations [Australian].
    - **Inducers:** Strong inducers (e.g., rifampicin, dexamethasone, phenytoin, carbamazepine, St. John’s Wort) decrease **sunitinib** plasma concentrations [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamic:** Caution when used with other agents known to prolong the QT interval [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. **Sunitinib** is embryotoxic and teratogenic in animal studies. It should not be used during pregnancy unless the benefit outweighs the risk. Women of childbearing potential must use adequate contraception during therapy and for at least 4 weeks post-treatment [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if **sunitinib** is excreted in human milk. Due to potential serious adverse reactions in infants, breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies indicate potential impairment of both male and female fertility (e.g., ovarian follicular development, tubular atrophy) [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No significant differences in safety or efficacy were observed between patients <65 and ≥65 years of age [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No initial dose adjustment is required for mild to severe impairment or ESRD on haemodialysis [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No initial dose adjustment is required for mild (Child-Pugh A) or moderate (Child-Pugh B) impairment. Use in severe (Child-Pugh C) impairment has not been studied [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Simultaneously inhibits multiple receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs) including VEGFR1, VEGFR2, VEGFR3, PDGFR$\alpha$, PDGFR$\beta$, KIT, FLT3, CSF-1R, and RET. This leads to inhibition of tumour growth, angiogenesis, and metastatic progression [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ 6–12 hours. Bioavailability is unaffected by food [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** High protein binding (95% for parent, 90% for metabolite); large volume of distribution ($V_d/F \approx 2230$ L) [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by CYP3A4 to a primary active metabolite, which is also further metabolised by CYP3A4 [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily via faeces (61%); renal elimination accounts for 16%. Half-life is 40–60 hours for the parent and 80–110 hours for the active metabolite [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **RCC (First-line):** A Phase 3 study (n=750) compared **sunitinib** (Schedule 4/2) to Interferon-$\alpha$. **Sunitinib** demonstrated superior Median Progression-Free Survival (PFS) (11 months vs 5 months; $p < 0.000001$) and a higher Objective Response Rate (46% vs 12%) [Australian].
- **GIST:** A Phase 3 study (n=312) in imatinib-resistant/intolerant patients showed **sunitinib** significantly prolonged Median Time to Progression (TTP) compared to placebo (27.3 weeks vs 6.4 weeks; $p < 0.0001$) [Australian].
- **pNET:** A Phase 3 study (n=171) demonstrated a median PFS of 11.4 months for **sunitinib** compared to 5.5 months for placebo ($p = 0.0001$) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C [Australian].
- Keep in original packaging to protect from moisture.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **S4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
