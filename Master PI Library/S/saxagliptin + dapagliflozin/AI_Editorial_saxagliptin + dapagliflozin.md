# Editorial Product Information: saxagliptin + dapagliflozin

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **saxagliptin + dapagliflozin**
- Chemical class:
  - Saxagliptin: dipeptidyl peptidase‑4 (DPP‑4) inhibitor
  - Dapagliflozin: sodium‑glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitor
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Indicated as an adjunct to diet and exercise, in combination with metformin, to improve glycaemic control in adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus when treatment with both saxagliptin and dapagliflozin is appropriate.
- Use in paediatric/adolescent patients (<18 years): Safety and effectiveness have not been established.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Recommended dose: one tablet containing saxagliptin 5 mg and dapagliflozin 10 mg taken once daily.
- No dose adjustment required for administration with or without food.

### Dosing by indication
- Indication-specific dosing beyond the single fixed dose above: Not specified in available sources.

### Administration instructions
- Swallow whole.
- Assess renal function prior to initiation and periodically thereafter (see Section 10 Special Populations / Renal impairment).

### Titration schedules
- Not specified in available sources.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

### Combinations not studied
- Not studied in combination with glucagon‑like peptide‑1 (GLP‑1) receptor agonists, insulin, or insulin secretagogues such as sulfonylureas. Exercise caution if co‑prescribing these classes (see Section 8 Drug Interactions and Section 6 Warnings and Precautions).

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to saxagliptin or dapagliflozin or to any excipient, including a history of serious hypersensitivity reactions (e.g., anaphylaxis or angioedema) following exposure to any DPP‑4 inhibitor or any SGLT2 inhibitor.
- Estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) persistently <45 mL/min/1.73 m2 or patients on dialysis — **do not use**.
- Other contraindications reported in source documents: None specified beyond the two items above.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed/critical warnings
- **Do not use in patients with eGFR persistently <45 mL/min/1.73 m2 or in patients on dialysis.**
- **Not for treatment of diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA); SGLT2 inhibitors have been associated with ketoacidosis, including fatal cases.**

### General warnings and precautions
- Type 1 diabetes: Use is not recommended for patients with type 1 diabetes.
- Ketoacidosis: Cases of ketoacidosis, including diabetic ketoacidosis, have been reported with dapagliflozin and other SGLT2 inhibitors. Ketoacidosis may present with only moderately elevated or near‑normal blood glucose levels (euglycaemic ketoacidosis). If suspected, suspend therapy, evaluate and treat promptly. Ketoacidosis and glucosuria may persist after discontinuation in some patients.
- Volume depletion and hypotension: Dapagliflozin causes osmotic diuresis. Correct volume depletion prior to initiation. Use caution in patients with conditions that predispose to volume depletion (e.g., elderly, patients on diuretics, patients with low systolic blood pressure, acute illness with dehydration, gastrointestinal fluid loss). Temporary interruption is recommended during acute intercurrent illness that predisposes to volume depletion.
- Renal function: Dapagliflozin can increase serum creatinine and decrease eGFR; acute kidney injury requiring hospitalisation and dialysis has been reported post‑marketing. Monitor renal function:
  - Prior to initiation and at least yearly thereafter.
  - Prior to initiation of concomitant medicines that may reduce renal function and periodically thereafter.
  - For eGFR between 45 and 60 mL/min/1.73 m2, monitor at least 2–4 times per year.
  - Discontinue if eGFR persistently falls below 45 mL/min/1.73 m2.
- Hepatic impairment: Dapagliflozin exposure is increased in severe hepatic impairment. Use is not recommended in severe hepatic impairment. May be used in mild or moderate hepatic impairment with caution.
- Acute pancreatitis: Post‑marketing reports of acute pancreatitis have occurred with saxagliptin. Suspend therapy if pancreatitis is suspected.
- Hypoglycaemia: When used in combination with insulin or insulin secretagogues, the risk of hypoglycaemia is increased. Consider reducing the dose of insulin or insulin secretagogue to minimise risk.
- Urinary tract infections (UTIs) and serious infections: SGLT2 inhibitors, including dapagliflozin, are associated with increased urinary glucose excretion and risk of UTIs; serious UTIs including pyelonephritis and urosepsis requiring hospitalisation have been reported. Evaluate and treat promptly. Consider temporary interruption when treating pyelonephritis or urosepsis; consider discontinuation for recurrent UTIs.
- Necrotising fasciitis of the perineum (Fournier’s gangrene): Post‑marketing cases reported in patients treated with SGLT2 inhibitors. If suspected, discontinue therapy and urgently treat (broad‑spectrum antibiotics and surgical debridement where required).
- Skin disorders and bullous pemphigoid: Post‑marketing reports of bullous pemphigoid requiring hospitalisation have occurred with DPP‑4 inhibitors; cases resolved with immunosuppressive therapy and discontinuation. Monitor for blistering/erosions; discontinue if suspected and refer to dermatologist.
- Arthralgia: Severe joint pain has been reported with DPP‑4 inhibitors; symptoms generally resolve after discontinuation.
- Cardiac failure: In a large cardiovascular outcomes trial, saxagliptin was associated with a small increase in the rate of hospitalisation for heart failure; a causal relationship has not been established. Use caution in patients with known risk factors for hospitalisation for heart failure or with moderate to severe renal impairment. Advise patients to report symptoms of heart failure promptly.
- Lower limb amputations: In a long‑term study of another SGLT2 inhibitor an increase in lower limb amputations (primarily of the toe) was observed; it is unknown whether this is a class effect. Regular foot examinations and counselling on foot care are recommended.
- Immunocompromised patients (e.g., post‑transplantation, HIV): Safety and efficacy not established in these populations.
- Combinations not studied: Safety and efficacy in combination with GLP‑1 receptor agonists, insulin and insulin secretagogues (e.g., sulfonylureas) not established.

### Monitoring requirements
- Renal function (eGFR/serum creatinine) prior to initiation and periodically thereafter; more frequent monitoring for eGFR 45–60 mL/min/1.73 m2.
- Monitoring for signs/symptoms of ketoacidosis (nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, malaise, dyspnoea), urinary tract infection and genital infections, volume depletion and hypotension, pancreatitis (severe persistent abdominal pain), heart failure (dyspnoea, oedema, rapid weight gain), and severe skin reactions (blistering).
- Laboratory monitoring as clinically indicated: liver enzymes, haematocrit, serum phosphorus, lipids, lymphocyte counts.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Correct hypovolaemia before initiating therapy.
- Temporarily interrupt therapy prior to major surgery or procedures associated with prolonged fasting.
- Consider postponing non‑urgent surgery if patient has not ceased dapagliflozin pre‑operatively.
- Restart when the patient is clinically stable and oral intake is normal.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Saxagliptin (general interaction profile)
- Metabolism: primarily by CYP3A4/5 to an active metabolite. CYP3A4/5 inhibitors may increase saxagliptin concentrations and decrease metabolite concentrations; inducers have the opposite effect. However, total active component exposure (parent + metabolite) is not meaningfully altered by several tested drugs.
- In vitro data: saxagliptin and its major metabolite did not inhibit CYP1A2, 2A6, 2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 2E1, or 3A4, and did not induce CYP1A2, 2B6, 2C9 or 3A4; not expected to alter clearance of drugs metabolised by these enzymes.
- Not a significant inhibitor or inducer of P‑glycoprotein (P‑gp).
- Drug interaction study data: total exposure to saxagliptin active components was not meaningfully altered by metformin, glibenclamide, pioglitazone, digoxin, simvastatin, diltiazem, ketoconazole, rifampicin, omeprazole, certain antacids, or famotidine. Saxagliptin did not meaningfully alter the pharmacokinetics of several of these agents including metformin, glibenclamide, pioglitazone, digoxin, simvastatin, diltiazem, ketoconazole or combined oral contraceptives.

### Dapagliflozin (general interaction profile)
- Metabolism: primarily by UGT1A9 glucuronidation to an inactive metabolite (dapagliflozin 3‑O‑glucuronide).
- In vitro data: dapagliflozin and its major metabolite did not inhibit or induce the main CYP enzymes studied; not expected to alter clearance of drugs metabolised by these enzymes. Dapagliflozin is a weak substrate of P‑gp.
- Concomitant medications that may reduce renal function: monitor renal function when initiating such agents and periodically thereafter because glycaemic efficacy of dapagliflozin depends on renal function.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions / Clinical management
- **Insulin and insulin secretagogues (e.g., sulfonylureas):** additive risk of hypoglycaemia. Consider reducing dose of insulin or insulin secretagogue when initiating combination therapy.
- **Diuretics / antihypertensives / agents causing volume depletion:** additive risk of volume depletion and hypotension due to dapagliflozin‑induced osmotic diuresis. Correct volume depletion before initiation and monitor blood pressure and renal function.
- **Agents that decrease renal function:** increased risk of reduced efficacy of dapagliflozin and potential renal adverse events; monitor renal function and consider risks before co‑administration.

### Jurisdiction‑specific interactions
- No jurisdiction‑specific interactions were specified in the available sources.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Safety and effectiveness in paediatric and adolescent patients (<18 years) have not been established.

### Elderly use
- No specific dose adjustment recommended based on age alone. Exercise caution due to increased risk of hypovolaemia and because elderly patients are more likely to have decreased renal function; monitor renal function and volume status.

### Renal impairment
- Assess renal function prior to initiation and periodically thereafter.
- No dose adjustment is required for patients with eGFR ≥45 mL/min/1.73 m2.
- **Do not use** in patients with eGFR persistently <45 mL/min/1.73 m2 or in patients on dialysis.
- Monitoring recommendations:
  - Prior to initiation and at least yearly thereafter.
  - Prior to initiation of concomitant medicines that may reduce renal function and periodically thereafter.
  - If eGFR is between 45 and 60 mL/min/1.73 m2, monitor renal function at least 2–4 times per year.

### Hepatic impairment
- May be used in patients with mild or moderate hepatic impairment.
- **Not recommended** in patients with severe hepatic impairment due to increased exposure to dapagliflozin.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Saxagliptin: DPP‑4 inhibitor that increases levels of incretin hormones (GLP‑1 and GIP), thereby increasing insulin release and decreasing glucagon levels in a glucose‑dependent manner.
- Dapagliflozin: SGLT2 inhibitor that reduces renal glucose reabsorption, increasing urinary glucose excretion and lowering plasma glucose independently of insulin.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Saxagliptin:
  - Metabolised primarily by CYP3A4/5 to an active metabolite.
  - Low measurable protein binding in human serum.
  - Both parent and metabolite contribute to total active pharmacologic exposure (parent + metabolite).
- Dapagliflozin:
  - Metabolised mainly via UGT1A9 glucuronidation to dapagliflozin 3‑O‑glucuronide (not an SGLT2 inhibitor).
  - Weak P‑gp substrate.
  - Dapagliflozin exposure is increased in severe hepatic impairment.
- Pharmacodynamic features:
  - Dapagliflozin produces osmotic diuresis and modest reductions in blood pressure.
  - Glycaemic efficacy of dapagliflozin is dependent on renal function.

### Notes
- Glucosuria and ketoacidosis may persist beyond expected durations based on plasma half‑lives in some patients after discontinuation.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Saxagliptin cardiovascular outcomes (SAVOR) trial:
  - Large outcomes trial in patients with type 2 diabetes.
  - Observations: a small increase in hospitalisation for heart failure with saxagliptin compared to placebo; incidence of adjudicated pancreatitis events was 0.3% in both saxagliptin‑treated and placebo groups. Decreased lymphocyte counts reported in 0.5% of saxagliptin‑treated patients versus 0.4% for placebo.
- Dapagliflozin pooled analyses (short‑term placebo‑controlled studies up to 24 weeks and longer term pools):
  - Urinary tract infections were reported more frequently with dapagliflozin 10 mg compared to control in a 24‑week pooled analysis (4.7% vs 3.5%).
  - Haematocrit increases >55% observed more frequently on dapagliflozin 10 mg versus placebo at Week 24 (1.3% vs 0.4%); similar findings at Week 102 in long‑term pooled data.
  - Hyperphosphataemia reported more frequently with dapagliflozin versus placebo in short‑term and long‑term pooled analyses (e.g., Week 24: 1.7% vs 0.9%; Week 102: 3.0% vs 1.6%).
  - ALT elevations (>3 × ULN) reported in 1.2% of dapagliflozin 10 mg patients and 1.6% of comparators in pooled analyses.
- Other clinical data:
  - Multiple clinical studies indicate no clinically meaningful pharmacokinetic interaction between saxagliptin and dapagliflozin when co‑administered; no dose adjustment required when given together.
