# Editorial Product Information: sodium oxybate

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **sodium oxybate**
- Chemical class: sodium salt of gamma‑hydroxybutyrate (GHB)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 2002 (as stated in available sources)  
- Most recent full prescribing information revision date shown in available sources: 7/2025  
- Withdrawals / suspensions / restrictions: Not specified in available sources beyond the presence of a restricted distribution program (see Warnings and Precautions).  
- Additional jurisdictional registration dates or updates: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of cataplexy in patients with narcolepsy aged 7 years and older.  
- Treatment of excessive daytime sleepiness (EDS) in patients with narcolepsy aged 7 years and older.  
- Pediatric approval: indicated for patients 7 years of age and older.  
- Any jurisdictional differences in approved indications: Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General adult dosing
- Recommended starting total nightly dose: 4.5 g per night, administered orally in two divided doses: 2.25 g at bedtime and 2.25 g taken 2.5–4 hours later.  
- Titration: increase total nightly dose by 1.5 g per night at weekly intervals (i.e., add 0.75 g at bedtime and 0.75 g at the second dose) based on efficacy and tolerability.  
- Recommended effective dosage range: 6 g to 9 g per night.  
- Doses above 9 g per night have not been studied and should not ordinarily be administered.

If total nightly dose selected:
- 4.5 g/night → 2.25 g at bedtime; 2.25 g 2.5–4 hours later  
- 6 g/night → 3.0 g at bedtime; 3.0 g 2.5–4 hours later  
- 7.5 g/night → 3.75 g at bedtime; 3.75 g 2.5–4 hours later  
- 9 g/night → 4.5 g at bedtime; 4.5 g 2.5–4 hours later

### Pediatric dosing (patients ≥7 years)
- Dosing is weight‑based; initiate, titrate, and limit total nightly dose according to weight categories:

  - <20 kg: insufficient information to provide specific dosing recommendations; consider lower starting dose, smaller weekly increases, and lower maximum total nightly dose.  
  - 20 to <30 kg: initial dose ≤1.0 g at bedtime and ≤1.0 g for the second dose; maximum weekly increase per dose 0.5 g; maximum recommended total nightly dose 3.0 g (1.5 g + 1.5 g).  
  - 30 to <45 kg: initial dose ≤1.5 g + ≤1.5 g; maximum weekly increase per dose 0.5 g; maximum recommended total nightly dose 3.75 g (1.875 g + 1.875 g).  
  - ≥45 kg: initial dose ≤2.25 g + ≤2.25 g; maximum weekly increase per dose 0.75 g; maximum recommended total nightly dose 4.5 g (2.25 g + 2.25 g).

- Note: For patients who sleep >8 hours per night, the first dose may be given at bedtime or after an initial sleep period. Some patients may respond better with unequal split doses.

### Important administration instructions (all patients)
- Prepare both nightly doses prior to bedtime. Dilute each dose with approximately 1/4 cup (≈60 mL) of water in the pharmacy‑provided container immediately before ingestion.  
- Take the first nightly dose at least 2 hours after eating.  
- Take the second nightly dose 2.5 to 4 hours after the first dose.  
- Ingest each dose while in bed and lie down immediately after dosing; remain in bed after each dose. Patients often fall asleep rapidly (often within 5–15 minutes).  
- Patients may need to set an alarm to awaken for the second dose. If the second dose is missed, skip it and resume usual dosing the next night. Never take both doses at one time.  
- Patients may fall asleep abruptly without prior drowsiness.

### Dosage modifications
- Hepatic impairment: recommended starting dosage is one‑half of the usual starting total nightly dose, divided into two doses.  
- Co‑administration with divalproex sodium: when initiating divalproex in a patient on a stable dosage of sodium oxybate, reduce the sodium oxybate dosage by at least 20% initially. When initiating sodium oxybate in a patient already taking divalproex, start at a lower sodium oxybate dose and titrate based on clinical response and tolerability.

## 5. Contraindications
- Concomitant use with alcohol (drinkable ethanol) — contraindicated.  
- Concomitant use with sedative‑hypnotics — contraindicated.  
- Succinic semialdehyde dehydrogenase deficiency — contraindicated.  
- Other contraindications listed in source documents: Not specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- **WARNING — CENTRAL NERVOUS SYSTEM DEPRESSION and ABUSE AND MISUSE** [US].  
  - Sodium oxybate is a central nervous system (CNS) depressant and can cause marked sedation and respiratory depression, including obtundation.  
  - Sodium oxybate is the sodium salt of gamma‑hydroxybutyrate (GHB). Abuse or misuse of GHB is associated with seizures, respiratory depression, decreased consciousness, coma, and death.

- CNS depression and concomitant CNS depressants:
  - Avoid concomitant use with alcohol and sedative hypnotics.  
  - Use caution when combining with other CNS depressants (e.g., opioid analgesics, benzodiazepines, sedating antidepressants or antipsychotics, sedating antiepileptic drugs, general anesthetics, muscle relaxants, illicit CNS depressants); such combinations may increase risk of respiratory depression, profound sedation, hypotension, syncope, and death. Consider dose reduction or discontinuation of one or more agents if combination is medically required. Consider interruption of sodium oxybate if short‑term opioid therapy is required (e.g., perioperative).

- Restricted distribution:
  - Sodium oxybate is available only through a restricted distribution program because of the risks of CNS depression and abuse/misuse. Program requirements include prescriber certification, dispensing by certified pharmacies, and patient enrollment with documentation of safe use.

- Respiratory depression and sleep‑disordered breathing:
  - Sodium oxybate may impair respiratory drive, particularly in patients with compromised respiratory function or sleep‑disordered breathing. Clinical studies and overdose reports have documented life‑threatening respiratory depression.  
  - Increased central apneas and clinically significant oxygen desaturation events have been observed in adults and pediatric patients during polysomnography after sodium oxybate administration. Monitor patients with preexisting sleep‑disordered breathing; consider discontinuation if clinically significant worsening occurs.

- Depression, suicidality, and other psychiatric reactions:
  - Emergence or worsening of depression and suicidal ideation/behavior have been reported; monitor patients, particularly those with a history of depression or suicidality.  
  - Other behavioral/psychiatric adverse reactions reported include confusion, anxiety, hallucinations, paranoia, psychosis, aggression, agitation, and acute psychosis (pediatric and adult). Monitor closely and evaluate emergent symptoms promptly.

- Parasomnias:
  - Sleepwalking and other parasomnias have been reported, including events with potential for injury. Evaluate and manage episodes as clinically appropriate.

- High sodium content:
  - Sodium oxybate contains substantial sodium per nightly dose; consider the sodium load in patients sensitive to high sodium intake (e.g., heart failure, hypertension, renal impairment). See sodium content table in Adverse Effects section.

- Monitoring recommendations:
  - Assess risk of abuse and misuse prior to prescribing and monitor for signs of misuse.  
  - Monitor for CNS depression‑related events after initiation and periodically during treatment.  
  - Monitor respiratory status in patients with compromised respiratory function or with evidence of sleep‑disordered breathing.  
  - Monitor mood and suicidal ideation, especially in patients with prior psychiatric history.  
  - Monitor blood pressure/volume status and fluid/sodium balance in patients sensitive to sodium.

Notes and trial‑specific adverse event data:
- Adult controlled trials pooled: 611 patients in three controlled trials (398 treated with sodium oxybate; 213 placebo). Across controlled and uncontrolled trials, 781 patients with narcolepsy treated with sodium oxybate were evaluated for adverse events.  
- Adverse reactions leading to discontinuation: 10.3% of 398 treated patients discontinued due to adverse reactions vs 2.8% of placebo in pooled controlled adult trials; nausea was the most common cause of discontinuation (2.8%). Many discontinuations occurred in the first weeks of therapy.  
- In an adult study of 50 patients with obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) receiving 9 g/night, there was no overall increase in severity of sleep‑disordered breathing for the group, but an increase in central apneas and three patients (6%) had clinically significant oxygen desaturation (≤55%) after administration. One withdrew; two continued after single brief desaturation instances.  
- Respiratory study in 21 patients with narcolepsy up to 9 g/night showed no dose‑related change in oxygen saturation in the group overall; one of four patients with moderate‑to‑severe sleep apnea had significant worsening.  
- Pediatric clinical trial (n=104) reported nausea, enuresis, vomiting, headache, weight decreased, decreased appetite, dizziness, sleepwalking (≥5%); also psychiatric adverse events including acute psychosis, confusion, anxiety, suicidal ideation (1 patient), and depression (2 patients).

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Concomitant CNS depressants:
  - Pharmacodynamic interaction: additive or synergistic CNS and respiratory depression with alcohol, sedative hypnotics, opioid analgesics, benzodiazepines, sedating antidepressants, sedating antipsychotics, sedating anti‑epileptic drugs, general anesthetics, muscle relaxants, and illicit CNS depressants. Management: avoid combination where possible; if combination is required, consider dose reduction or discontinuation of one or more agents and closely monitor for respiratory depression and excessive sedation. Consider interrupting sodium oxybate when short‑term opioid therapy is required (e.g., perioperative).

- Divalproex sodium:
  - Concomitant use may increase exposure/effects (interaction described). Management: when initiating divalproex in a patient on sodium oxybate, reduce sodium oxybate dose by at least 20% initially. When initiating sodium oxybate in a patient already taking divalproex, start sodium oxybate at a lower dose and titrate carefully.

- Alcohol:
  - Co‑ingestion with alcohol increases risk of profound CNS depression, amnesia, and other serious adverse outcomes (including coma and death). Concomitant use is contraindicated.

- Pharmacokinetic interactions (absorption, distribution, metabolism, elimination): Specific numerical PK interaction parameters and detailed mechanisms are not specified in available sources beyond the interaction with divalproex sodium.

- Other interactions and management: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
- Paediatric use:
  - Sodium oxybate is indicated for patients 7 years of age and older with narcolepsy. Pediatric dosing by weight categories is provided (see Section 4). For children weighing <20 kg, there is insufficient information to provide specific dosing; lower starting doses and lower maximum dosage should be considered. Pediatric trials (n=104) reported both efficacy and pediatric‑specific adverse reactions.

- Geriatric use:
  - Elderly patients: monitor for impaired motor and/or cognitive function when taking sodium oxybate. Specific dose adjustments for elderly patients are not specified in available sources.

- Renal impairment:
  - Specific dosage adjustments by level of renal impairment or eGFR are not specified in available sources. Consider sodium load and monitor patients with renal impairment for sodium‑sensitive adverse effects.

- Hepatic impairment:
  - Recommended starting dosage in patients with hepatic impairment: one‑half of the usual starting total nightly dose, divided into two doses. (See Dosage and Administration.)

- Other special populations:
  - Patients sensitive to high sodium intake (heart failure, hypertension, renal impairment): consider total sodium exposure from sodium oxybate dosing and monitor accordingly.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Adult trials:
  - Three placebo‑controlled clinical trials in adult patients with narcolepsy are referenced (Trials N1, N3, N4). Pooled controlled trials included 611 patients (398 receiving sodium oxybate; 213 receiving placebo). Across controlled and uncontrolled trials, 781 adult patients with narcolepsy received sodium oxybate. Detailed primary efficacy endpoints, statistical results, and numerical efficacy outcomes are not specified in the available sources provided.

  - Safety observations in trials included: two completed suicides and two suicide attempts among 781 treated adults; two subjects with profound CNS depression resolved after respiratory support; two patients discontinued for severe breathing difficulty and increased obstructive sleep apnea; parasomnias and neuropsychiatric events observed.

  - A dose‑response for confusion was observed in one controlled trial with fixed doses (3 g, 6 g, 9 g) showing 17% incidence of confusion at 9 g/night.

- Respiratory studies:
  - Adult respiratory study: 21 patients with narcolepsy assessed at doses up to 9 g/night — no dose‑related group change in oxygen saturation overall; one patient with preexisting moderate‑to‑severe sleep apnea had significant worsening.  
  - Obstructive sleep apnea study: 50 patients treated with 9 g/night — no overall increase in severity of sleep‑disordered breathing for the group, but an increase in central apneas and clinically significant oxygen desaturation in 3 patients (6%).

- Pediatric trial:
  - One pediatric clinical trial in patients with narcolepsy (n=104) is referenced. Reported pediatric adverse reactions included nausea, enuresis, vomiting, headache, decreased weight/appetite, dizziness, sleepwalking, and neuropsychiatric events (including acute psychosis); one patient experienced suicidal ideation and two reported depression. Detailed efficacy endpoints and primary outcome measures are not specified in available sources.

- Overall efficacy outcome details (magnitudes of effect, primary endpoint statistics) are not specified in available sources.
