# Editorial Product Information: serdexmethylphenidate + dexmethylphenidate

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **serdexmethylphenidate + dexmethylphenidate**
- Chemical class: central nervous system stimulant (methylphenidate class) — described as a CNS stimulant in available sources.
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 2021.
- Last revision/update of available PI text: September 2025 (document revision date reported).
- Withdrawals, suspensions, or restrictions: Not specified in available sources for any jurisdiction other than the labeling changes noted above.
- Other jurisdictions' approval dates or PI revision dates: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) in patients 6 years of age and older.
- Use in pediatric patients younger than 6 years of age is not recommended because higher plasma exposure and a higher incidence of adverse reactions (for example, weight loss) were observed in pediatric patients <6 years at the same dosages used in older children.

[No other indications were specified in the available sources.]

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General dosing (adolescents and adults)
- Recommended starting dose (adolescents 13–17 years and adults): 39.2 mg serdexmethylphenidate / 7.8 mg dexmethylphenidate orally once daily in the morning.
- After one week, increase to 52.3 mg / 10.4 mg once daily depending on response and tolerability.
- Maximum recommended dose for adolescents (13–17) and adults: 52.3 mg / 10.4 mg once daily.

### Pediatric dosing (6 to 12 years)
- Recommended starting dose: 39.2 mg / 7.8 mg orally once daily in the morning.
- After one week, dosage may be increased to 52.3 mg / 10.4 mg once daily or decreased to 26.1 mg / 5.2 mg once daily depending on response and tolerability.
- Maximum recommended dose: 52.3 mg / 10.4 mg once daily.
- Use in children <6 years: not recommended (see Section 10 and Section 5.7 warnings).

### Titration and switching
- If switching from other methylphenidate products: discontinue prior methylphenidate therapy and titrate with serdexmethylphenidate + dexmethylphenidate using the dosing schedule above. Do not substitute on a milligram-per-milligram basis because different methylphenidate products have different pharmacokinetic profiles and different methylphenidate base composition.

### Administration instructions
- Administer orally once daily in the morning, with or without food.
- Capsules may be swallowed whole or opened and contents sprinkled onto 2 tablespoons of applesauce or added to 50 mL of water. Consume the mixture immediately or within 10 minutes; do not store the prepared mixture for future use.

### Dose reduction and discontinuation
- If paradoxical aggravation of symptoms or other adverse reactions occur, reduce dosage or discontinue if necessary.
- If no improvement after appropriate dosage adjustment over a one-month period, discontinue treatment.

### Missed dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to serdexmethylphenidate, methylphenidate, or any component of the product. Reported reactions include bronchospasm, rash, pruritus; hypersensitivity reactions such as angioedema and anaphylaxis have been reported with other methylphenidate products.
- Concurrent treatment with a monoamine oxidase inhibitor (MAOI), or use of an MAOI within the preceding 14 days (risk of hypertensive crisis).

[No additional contraindications were specified in the available sources.]

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed/major warning
- **Abuse, misuse, and addiction**: The product has a high potential for abuse and misuse that can lead to substance use disorder including addiction. Misuse and abuse of CNS stimulants can result in overdose and death. Higher doses and unapproved routes of administration (e.g., insufflation, injection) increase risk. Assess patient risk for abuse/misuse before prescribing, educate patients/families on risks, safe storage, and proper disposal, and frequently reassess and monitor for signs of abuse and diversion.

### General warnings and precautions
- Risks to patients with serious cardiac disease: Avoid use in patients with known structural cardiac abnormalities, cardiomyopathy, serious cardiac arrhythmias, coronary artery disease, or other serious cardiac disease. Sudden death has been reported in patients with such conditions treated with CNS stimulants.
- Increased blood pressure and heart rate: CNS stimulants cause mean increases in blood pressure (~2–4 mmHg) and heart rate (~3–6 bpm) with the potential for larger increases in some patients. Monitor blood pressure and pulse.
- Psychiatric adverse reactions:
  - Exacerbation of pre-existing psychosis.
  - Induction of a manic or mixed mood episode in patients with bipolar disorder; screen for risk factors prior to initiation (e.g., history of depressive symptoms, family history of bipolar disorder or suicide).
  - New onset psychotic or manic symptoms (hallucinations, delusional thinking, or mania) have been reported; pooled analyses of CNS stimulants reported psychotic/manic symptoms in approximately 0.1% of treated patients.
- Priapism: Sustained or painful erections have been reported; immediate medical attention required.
- Peripheral vasculopathy, including Raynaud's phenomenon: Intermittent digital changes have been reported; sequelae have included digital ulceration and soft tissue breakdown. Observe for digital changes and consider further evaluation (e.g., rheumatology) if signs/symptoms develop.
- Long-term suppression of growth in pediatric patients: CNS stimulants have been associated with weight loss and slowing of growth rate. In a long-term pediatric safety study, mean changes in weight and height z-scores indicated a lower than expected increase in growth over 12 months. Monitor height and weight at appropriate intervals; consider interruption of treatment if growth is inadequate.
- Acute angle closure glaucoma: Cases reported with methylphenidate treatment; evaluate patients at risk (for example, significant hyperopia) by ophthalmology prior to treatment.
- Increased intraocular pressure and open-angle glaucoma: Elevation of intraocular pressure has been reported. Use only if benefit outweighs risk and monitor closely if history of increased IOP or open-angle glaucoma.
- Motor and verbal tics, and worsening of Tourette’s syndrome: Stimulants have been associated with onset or worsening of tics; assess family history and evaluate prior to initiation. Regularly monitor and discontinue if clinically appropriate.

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and periodic assessment of blood pressure and pulse.
- Baseline cardiac history and physical exam; family history of sudden death or ventricular arrhythmia.
- Screen for psychiatric history and risk factors (bipolar disorder, psychosis, history of tics/Tourette’s).
- Monitor height and weight in pediatric patients regularly.
- Observe for signs of peripheral vasculopathy, glaucoma symptoms, and priapism.
- Monitor for signs of hepatic dysfunction and hematologic abnormalities as clinically indicated.

## 8. Drug Interactions

### Major/clinically significant interactions
- Monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs): Concomitant use is contraindicated (and MAOI use within 14 days prior is contraindicated) because of the risk of hypertensive crisis.
- Antihypertensive drugs: CNS stimulants may attenuate the effect of antihypertensive agents and may increase blood pressure. Monitor blood pressure and adjust the dose of the antihypertensive medication as needed.
- Serotonergic drugs: Serotonin syndrome has been reported in combination with serotonergic agents (postmarketing). Use caution when co-administering agents that increase serotonin; monitor closely and consider the risks.
- Other methylphenidate products: Pharmacokinetic profiles differ between methylphenidate formulations. When switching, discontinue the prior product and titrate with serdexmethylphenidate + dexmethylphenidate; do not substitute on a mg-per-mg basis.

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Specific pharmacokinetic interaction data (e.g., CYP interactions, effect of renal/hepatic impairment on clearance) are not specified in available sources.

### Management notes
- Avoid co-administration with MAOIs.
- Monitor blood pressure and heart rate when combined with antihypertensive drugs and adjust therapy accordingly.
- Exercise caution and monitor for signs of serotonin syndrome when combined with serotonergic medications.
- When converting from other methylphenidate products, re-titrate rather than substituting based on milligram equivalence.

## 12. Clinical Trials

### Short-term pediatric trial (6 to 12 years)
- Design: 3-week open-label dose-optimization phase (all patients received the product; n=155), followed by a 1-week double-blind, randomized, controlled phase in which responders were randomized to continue the product (n=74) or switch to placebo (n=76).
- Findings: The study design and short randomized phase limit the utility of adverse event rates for predicting rates in general practice; specific efficacy outcomes are not described in the available excerpts.

### Long-term pediatric safety study (6 to 12 years)
- Design: Long-term, open-label safety study including patients who completed the short-term study or were de novo. After a 3-week dose optimization (if not recently treated with the product), patients entered a 12-month treatment phase.
- Safety population: 238 patients received open-label therapy and had evaluable safety data; 154 patients were treated for 12 months.
- Growth outcomes: Mean weight increase from baseline to Month 12 among completers was 3.4 kg. Mean change in weight z-score from baseline to Month 12 was -0.20 (indicating a lower-than-expected increase in weight compared to age- and sex-matched population norms). Most weight z-score decline occurred in the first 4 months. Mean height increase from baseline to Month 12 among completers was 4.9 cm. Mean change in height z-score from baseline to Month 12 was -0.21 (indicating a lower-than-expected increase in height).
- Limitations: Open-label uncontrolled design for the long-term study limits causal attribution of adverse reactions.

### Other clinical trial data
- A pooled analysis of multiple short-term, placebo-controlled studies of CNS stimulants reported new psychotic or manic symptoms in approximately 0.1% of stimulant-treated patients versus 0% in placebo-treated patients.
