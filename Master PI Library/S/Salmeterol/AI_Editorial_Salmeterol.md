# Editorial Product Information: Fluticasone Propionate / Salmeterol Xinafoate

## Editorial Note
- The information contained in this editorial was synthesized from the following jurisdictional Product Information (PI) source:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: Evocair MDI (Fluticasone propionate/salmeterol xinafoate)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory information into a single clinical reference.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Fluticasone propionate (corticosteroid) and Salmeterol xinafoate (long-acting beta-2-adrenoceptor agonist [LABA]).
- **Brand Names:** **Evocair MDI** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** R03AK06 (Salmeterol and fluticasone).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Metered Dose Inhaler (MDI):** Each actuation provides either 125 mcg or 250 mcg of fluticasone propionate and 25 mcg of salmeterol (as xinafoate) [Australian].
    - *Note:* Dosing recommendations also refer to a 50 mcg fluticasone / 25 mcg salmeterol strength for specific asthma populations [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Inhalation only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Alphapharm Pty Ltd trading as Viatris [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Asthma:** For the regular treatment of asthma where a combination product (LABA and inhaled corticosteroid [ICS]) is appropriate. This includes:
    - Patients not adequately controlled on ICS alone.
    - Patients already stabilized on maintenance doses of both a LABA and ICS.
    - Initiation of maintenance therapy in patients with moderate persistent asthma who require daily reliever medication and have moderate/severe airway limitation [Australian].
- **Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD):** Symptomatic treatment of patients with severe COPD (FEV1 < 50% predicted normal) and a history of repeated exacerbations, who remain symptomatic despite regular bronchodilator therapy. It is not indicated for the initiation of bronchodilator therapy in COPD [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Principles
- The dose should be titrated to the lowest level that maintains effective symptom control.
- **Evocair MDI** must be used regularly, even when the patient is asymptomatic.

### Asthma Dosing
- **Adults and Adolescents (12 years and over):** Two inhalations twice daily. The strength (50/25 mcg, 125/25 mcg, or 250/25 mcg) should be selected based on disease severity [Australian].
- **Initiation in Moderate Persistent Asthma:** Two inhalations of 50/25 mcg twice daily. Review after 6–12 weeks; if control is stable, consider transitioning to ICS monotherapy [Australian].
- **Children (4 years and over):** Two inhalations of 50/25 mcg twice daily [Australian].
- **Children (under 4 years):** Safety and efficacy not established; no data available [Australian].

### COPD Dosing
- **Adults:** Two inhalations of 250/25 mcg twice daily. 
- *Note:* Two inhalations of 125/25 mcg twice daily may be considered for patients at higher risk of ICS-related adverse effects [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Use of a spacer device is recommended, particularly for patients with poor inhaler technique, to improve lung delivery and reduce local side effects (e.g., oropharyngeal candidiasis) [Australian].
- Patients should rinse their mouth with water after inhalation.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to fluticasone propionate, salmeterol xinafoate, or the propellant norflurane (HFA 134a) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Acute Symptoms:** Not for the relief of acute asthma symptoms; a short-acting bronchodilator (e.g., salbutamol) must be available at all times [Australian].
- **Deterioration of Disease:** Sudden or progressive deterioration in control is potentially life-threatening. Do not initiate therapy during unstable or acutely deteriorating asthma [Australian].
- **Pneumonia in COPD:** There is an increased risk of pneumonia in patients with COPD using ICS-containing products. Physicians should remain vigilant as the symptoms of pneumonia often overlap with COPD exacerbations [Australian].
- **Systemic Corticosteroid Effects:** Potential effects include HPA axis suppression, decreased bone mineral density, cataract, glaucoma, and growth retardation in children. Risk is minimized by titrating to the lowest effective dose [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** Use with caution in patients with pre-existing cardiovascular disease (e.g., arrhythmia, hypertension) due to potential sympathomimetic effects of salmeterol [Australian].
- **Paradoxical Bronchospasm:** If an immediate increase in wheezing occurs after dosing, discontinue therapy and institute alternative treatment [Australian].
- **Other Precautions:** Use with caution in patients with pulmonary tuberculosis, thyrotoxicosis, or diabetes mellitus (due to rare reports of increased blood glucose) [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Headache.
- **Common (1–10%):** 
    - Candidiasis of the mouth and throat (thrush).
    - Pneumonia (in COPD patients).
    - Hoarseness, dysphonia, and throat irritation.
    - Muscle cramps and arthralgia.
    - Palpitations.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** 
    - Cutaneous hypersensitivity reactions.
    - Dyspnoea.
    - Tremor.
    - Tachycardia and atrial fibrillation.
    - Hyperglycaemia.
    - Anxiety and sleep disorders.
    - Cataract.
    - Contusions [Australian].
- **Rare (<0.1%):** 
    - Anaphylactic reactions, angioedema.
    - Oesophageal candidiasis.
    - Glaucoma.
    - Paradoxical bronchospasm.
    - Behavioral changes (hyperactivity/irritability, mainly in children).
    - Cardiac arrhythmias (supraventricular tachycardia and extrasystoles).
    - Cushingoid features and adrenal suppression [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Beta-blockers:** Both selective and non-selective beta-blockers should be avoided unless compelling reasons exist, as they may antagonize the effect of salmeterol [Australian].
- **Ritonavir:** This highly potent CYP3A4 inhibitor can significantly increase fluticasone plasma levels, leading to Cushing’s syndrome and adrenal suppression. Concomitant use is not recommended unless the benefit outweighs the risk [Australian].
- **Ketoconazole and other Potent CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Co-administration with ketoconazole increases salmeterol exposure, potentially prolonging the QTc interval. Caution and monitoring are advised [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B3]. Data in pregnant women are limited. Use only if the expected benefit to the mother outweighs the potential risk to the fetus. Retrospective studies have not shown an increased risk of major congenital malformations compared to other ICS [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Plasma levels of both drugs are very low after inhalation; however, there are no data on human breast milk levels. Use should only be considered if the benefit to the mother outweighs the risk to the child [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No significant effects on fertility have been observed in animal studies for either component alone [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for initiating maintenance therapy in children. In children <6 years, it should only be used after trials of ICS or leukotriene receptor antagonists. Monitor growth in children on prolonged therapy [Australian].
- **Elderly:** No dose adjustment is required [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Fluticasone propionate:** A potent glucocorticoid that reduces inflammation in the airways, improving lung function and preventing exacerbations.
    - **Salmeterol:** A selective, long-acting (12-hour) beta-2-adrenoceptor agonist that provides bronchodilation and inhibits the release of mast cell mediators [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability of fluticasone from the MDI is ~10.9%. Salmeterol acts locally; plasma levels are very low and not predictive of therapeutic effect.
    - **Distribution:** Salmeterol is 95–98% protein bound. Fluticasone has a large volume of distribution (~250 L).
    - **Metabolism:** Fluticasone undergoes extensive first-pass metabolism via CYP3A4. Salmeterol is metabolized primarily via aliphatic hydroxylation.
    - **Elimination:** Fluticasone is excreted mainly in faeces. Salmeterol elimination is slow (t½ ~67 hours) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **AUSTRI and VESTRI Trials:** Large-scale 26-week studies in adults/adolescents and children (4–11 years) respectively. Both trials demonstrated that the addition of salmeterol to fluticasone was non-inferior to fluticasone alone regarding serious asthma-related events (hospitalization, intubation, death) [Australian].
- **SMART Trial:** A US-based study of salmeterol monotherapy which showed an increase in asthma-related deaths. Subsequent meta-analyses indicate this risk is not present when salmeterol is used in a fixed-dose combination with an ICS [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 30°C.
- Protect from frost and direct sunlight.
- Pressurized container: Do not expose to temperatures above 50°C. Do not puncture or burn even when empty [Australian].
