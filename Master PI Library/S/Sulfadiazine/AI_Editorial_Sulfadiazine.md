# Editorial Product Information: Silver Sulfadiazine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of silver sulfadiazine, synthesised from official regulatory sources for use by healthcare professionals.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and clinical resource; it is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Silver sulfadiazine 1.0% w/w
- **Chemical Class:** Sulfonamide derivative
- **Brand Names:** **Flamazine** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Topical Cream: 1.0% (10 mg/g) [Australian]
- **Routes of Administration:** Topical
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Smith & Nephew Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Severe Burns:** Prevention of infection in severe burn wounds [Australian].
- **Chronic Wounds:** Management of pressure sores and leg ulcers [Australian].
- **Minor Injuries:** Treatment of finger-tip injuries (following haemostasis) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Burns:** After cleaning the wound, apply cream to all affected areas to a depth of 3–5 mm. Application should be performed using a sterile gloved hand or sterile spatula.
- **Leg Ulcers/Pressure Sores:** Fill the ulcer cavity to a depth of at least 3–5 mm. Care must be taken to avoid application to non-ulcerated skin to prevent maceration. 
- **Finger-Tip Injuries:** Apply a 3–5 mm layer over the injury after haemostasis is achieved.

### Administration Instructions
- **Re-application Frequency:**
    - **Burns:** At least every 24 hours, or more frequently if exudate volume is high [Australian].
    - **Ulcers/Sores:** Normally changed daily; for less exudative wounds, every 48 hours may be acceptable [Australian].
    - **Finger-Tip Injuries:** Dressings should be changed every 2–3 days [Australian].
- **Hand Burns:** The cream may be applied and the entire hand enclosed in a clear plastic bag or glove, closed at the wrist. Patients should be encouraged to move fingers. Dressings must be changed when exudate accumulation is excessive.

### Special Considerations
- **Cleaning:** Wounds should be cleansed and debrided prior to application.
- **Exudative Wounds:** Use is not recommended for highly exudative leg or pressure ulcers [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Neonates:** Premature infants or newborn infants during the first month of life (due to risk of kernicterus) [Australian].
- **Pregnancy:** At or near term pregnancy [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to silver sulfadiazine or excipients, specifically cetyl alcohol or propylene glycol [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Life-threatening reactions, including Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), have been reported. Highest risk occurs within the first weeks of treatment. Discontinue permanently at the first appearance of progressive skin rash, blisters, or mucosal lesions [Australian].
- **Haematological Effects:** Transient leucopenia may occur, typically 2–3 days after starting therapy. While usually self-limiting, regular blood counts are advised during long-term treatment [Australian].
- **Systemic Absorption:** Significant systemic absorption can occur when treating large body areas. Monitor serum sulfonamide concentrations (therapeutic range 8–12 mg%), renal function, and urine for sulfonamide crystals [Australian].
- **G6PD Deficiency:** Use may be hazardous as haemolysis can occur in patients with glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase deficiency [Australian].
- **Local Effects:** May delay the separation of burn eschar; fungal invasion of the wound may occur [Australian].
- **Laboratory Interference:** Absorption of the propylene glycol vehicle may affect serum osmolality, potentially interfering with laboratory test interpretations [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Leukopenia (3–5% of patients), application site burning, pruritus, application site rash (including eczema and contact dermatitis) |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Argyria (associated with systemic silver absorption in large/prolonged wounds) |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Renal failure, SJS, TEN |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Enzymatic Debriding Agents:** Silver may inactivate these agents; concomitant use may be inappropriate [Australian].
- **Systemic Drugs (Potentiation):** If systemic absorption is significant (large-area burns), the effects of oral hypoglycaemic agents and phenytoin may be increased. Monitor blood levels [Australian].
- **Cimetidine:** Co-administration in patients with large-area burns may increase the incidence of leukopenia [Australian].
- **Other Systemic Interactions:** Sulfonamides may alter the effects of oral anticoagulants, methotrexate, and cyclosporine. They may also interfere with the effectiveness of hormonal contraceptives [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category C:** Sulfonamides cross the placenta and may cause kernicterus in the neonate by displacing bilirubin from albumin. Avoid use during the last month of pregnancy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Use with caution. Sulfadiazine is excreted in breast milk at concentrations approximately 15–35% of maternal serum levels. Avoid in infants under one month of age due to kernicterus risk [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies (subcutaneous) showed no treatment-related effects on fertility in rats at doses up to 500 mg/kg/day [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Contraindicated in premature and newborn infants <1 month old [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical experience has not identified overall differences in safety or efficacy compared to younger patients, though greater sensitivity in some individuals cannot be ruled out [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution due to the risk of reduced elimination of systemically absorbed drug [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Silver sulfadiazine is a bactericidal agent that acts primarily on the microbial cell membrane and cell wall. Unlike other sulfonamides, its action does not appear to depend solely on folic acid synthesis inhibition and is not antagonised by p-aminobenzoic acid (PABA) [Australian].
- **Microbiology:** Broad-spectrum activity against Gram-positive and Gram-negative organisms, including *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, as well as some yeasts and fungi [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Metabolism:** Slowly metabolised in contact with wound exudates.
    - **Absorption:** Up to 10% of the sulfadiazine may be absorbed. Blood concentrations of 10–20 µg/mL are typical, though higher levels occur with extensive burns.
    - **Excretion:** Sulfadiazine is excreted in the urine [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Light:** Protect from light [Australian].
- **Handling:** Jars (250g/500g) must be discarded 24 hours after opening. Tubes should be discarded 7 days after opening. Each container is for the treatment of one person only [Australian].
