# Editorial Product Information: Sumatriptan

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference and is not a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics used for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Sumatriptan (as succinate), a specific vascular 5-hydroxy-tryptamine-1 (5-HT1) receptor agonist.
- **Brand Names:** APO-Sumatriptan [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Oral Tablet:** 50 mg film-coated tablets [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Migraine:** Acute relief of migraine attacks with or without aura [Australian].
- **Cluster Headache:** Acute intermittent relief of cluster headache [Australian-approved in Section 4.2; note: Section 4.1 "Therapeutic Indications" specifically names migraine relief].
- **Limitations of Use:** Not indicated for prophylactic therapy. There is no data regarding use in basilar or hemiplegic migraine [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults 18–65 years)
- **Initial Dose:** 50 mg. Some patients may require 100 mg [Australian].
- **Subsequent Doses:** If symptoms recur, further doses may be administered within 24 hours.
- **Maximum Dose:** 300 mg in any 24-hour period [Australian].
- **Non-Response:** If a patient does not respond to the initial dose, a second dose should not be taken for the same attack; however, sumatriptan may be trialled for subsequent attacks [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets must be swallowed whole with water [Australian].
- Treatment should commence at the first sign of a migraine headache or associated symptoms (nausea, vomiting, photophobia). 
- **Note:** Administration during a migraine aura prior to the onset of headache may not prevent the development of the headache [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
Sumatriptan is contraindicated in patients with:
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to sumatriptan or any excipients.
- **Cardiovascular Disease:** History of myocardial infarction (MI), ischaemic heart disease (IHD), Prinzmetal’s angina/coronary vasospasm, or peripheral vascular disease [Australian].
- **Cerebrovascular Disease:** History of cerebrovascular accident (CVA) or transient ischaemic attack (TIA) [Australian].
- **Hypertension:** Uncontrolled hypertension [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Severe hepatic impairment [Australian].
- **Migraine Subtypes:** Hemiplegic, basilar, or ophthalmoplegic migraine [Australian].
- **Concurrent Medication:** 
    - Use within 24 hours of ergotamine-containing or ergot-type medications (e.g., dihydroergotamine, methysergide) [Australian].
    - Use within 24 hours of other 5-HT1 agonists [Australian].
    - Concurrent use or use within two weeks of discontinuing Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiovascular Risk Evaluation:** It is strongly recommended that sumatriptan not be given to patients with unrecognised coronary artery disease (CAD) risk factors (e.g., hypertension, hypercholesterolaemia, smoking, obesity, diabetes, strong family history of CAD, postmenopausal females, or males >40 years) without prior cardiovascular evaluation [Australian].
- **Chest Pain/Tightness:** Sumatriptan may cause transient chest pain and tightness, which may be intense and involve the throat. If symptoms suggest IHD, further doses should be withheld pending medical investigation [Australian].
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Potentially life-threatening serotonin syndrome has been reported, particularly when triptans are combined with SSRIs or SNRIs [Australian].
- **Medication Overuse Headache (MOH):** Excessive use of acute migraine treatments can lead to exacerbation of headaches [Australian].
- **Seizure Threshold:** Use with caution in patients with a history of seizures or risk factors that lower the seizure threshold [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity to Sulphonamides:** Patients with known sulphonamide hypersensitivity may exhibit cross-sensitivity reactions ranging from cutaneous reactions to anaphylaxis [Australian].
- **Vasospastic Reactions:** Beyond coronary effects, sumatriptan may cause peripheral vascular ischaemia or colonic ischaemia [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (1–10%)
- **Neurological:** Dizziness, vertigo, malaise, fatigue, drowsiness, sedation, paraesthesia [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea and vomiting (relationship to drug is sometimes unclear) [Australian].
- **General:** Sensations of tingling, heat or cold, heaviness, pressure, or tightness (can affect any part of the body, including chest/throat) [Australian].
- **Vascular:** Flushing, transient increases in blood pressure [Australian].

### Serious or Rare Adverse Effects (<0.1%)
- **Cardiac:** Myocardial infarction, coronary artery vasospasm, transient myocardial ischaemia, cardiac arrhythmias (including ventricular tachycardia and fibrillation) [Australian].
- **Cerebrovascular:** Stroke, cerebral haemorrhage, subarachnoid haemorrhage [Australian].
- **Ophthalmic:** Flickering, diplopia, nystagmus, scotoma, and very rarely, transient loss of vision [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis (rare <1 in 10,000) [Australian].
- **Other:** Seizures, ischaemic colitis, Raynaud’s phenomenon [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Ergotamine/Ergot Derivatives:** Potential for prolonged vasospastic reactions. Avoid concurrent use; allow 24 hours after ergotamine before taking sumatriptan, and 6 hours after sumatriptan before taking ergotamine [Australian].
- **MAOIs:** Contraindicated; sumatriptan clearance is reduced by MAO-A inhibitors [Australian].
- **SSRIs/SNRIs:** Risk of serotonin syndrome. If clinically warranted, appropriate patient observation is advised [Australian].
- **Other 5-HT1 Agonists:** Co-administration within 24 hours is not recommended [Australian].
- **St John’s Wort:** May increase the incidence of adverse effects [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category B3 [Australian]:** Studies in animals have shown an increased incidence of vascular and skeletal abnormalities in foetuses. Sumatriptan should only be used if the benefit to the mother outweighs the potential risk to the foetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Sumatriptan is excreted in breast milk. The average relative infant dose is <4%. Infant exposure can be minimised by avoiding breastfeeding for 24 hours after treatment and discarding any milk expressed during this period [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No specific data available in humans; rat studies showed no evidence of impaired fertility at high doses [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use (<12 years):** Safety and effectiveness not established [Australian].
- **Adolescents (12–17 years):** Efficacy not established in clinical trials; however, the safety profile appears similar to adults. A case of MI was reported in a 14-year-old following oral use [Australian].
- **Elderly (>65 years):** Use is not recommended due to limited experience and potential for increased cardiovascular risk [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Clearance is reduced. Lower doses should be considered for patients with mild-to-moderate impairment. Contraindicated in severe impairment [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution as metabolism/excretion may be affected [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selective agonist for vascular 5-HT1 receptors (subtypes 5-HT1B/1D). It mediates vasoconstriction of cranial blood vessels in the carotid arterial circulation and inhibits trigeminal nerve activity [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; 70% of Cmax occurs by 45 minutes. Bioavailability is low (14%) due to presystemic metabolism and incomplete absorption. Absorption is not significantly affected by food [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Large volume of distribution (approx. 1204 L in adults) [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by oxidative metabolism via monoamine oxidase A (MAO-A) [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Non-renal clearance accounts for 80% of total clearance. The major metabolite (indole acetic acid analogue) is excreted in urine [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adult Efficacy:** Two placebo-controlled studies (n=332) demonstrated that oral doses of 50 mg and 100 mg provided significantly higher pain relief compared to placebo.
- **Pain Relief at 2 Hours:** 50%–54% (sumatriptan 50 mg) vs. 17%–26% (placebo) [Australian].
- **Pain Relief at 4 Hours:** 68%–72% (sumatriptan 50 mg) vs. 19%–38% (placebo) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

- **Australia:** Schedule 4 (S4) – Prescription Only Medicine [Australian].
