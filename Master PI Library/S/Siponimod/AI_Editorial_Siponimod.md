# Editorial Product Information: Siponimod

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (TGA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the official Product Information (PI) for siponimod. It is designed as a structured clinical reference for healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI. Clinicians must consult the current approved PI in their respective region before prescribing or dispensing. This document is based on a single source (Australian TGA); additional regional variations will be updated as more data becomes available.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Siponimod (as hemifumarate).
- **Chemical Class:** Sphingosine-1-phosphate (S1P) receptor modulator.
- **Brand Name:** Mayzent [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L04AE03.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 0.25 mg, 1 mg, and 2 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Novartis Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Progressive Multiple Sclerosis (SPMS):** Indicated for the treatment of adult patients with SPMS [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Patient Selection:** Before treatment initiation, the patient’s **CYP2C9 genotype** must be determined. Siponimod is contraindicated in patients with a CYP2C9*3/*3 genotype [Australian].
- **General Dosing (Adult):**
    - **Maintenance Dose:** 2 mg once daily [Australian].
    - **Maintenance Dose (CYP2C9*2/*3 or *1/*3 genotypes):** 1 mg once daily [Australian].
- **Titration Schedule:**
  Treatment must begin with a 5-day titration pack to mitigate bradyarrhythmic effects.
    - Day 1 and 2: 0.25 mg daily.
    - Day 3: 0.5 mg daily.
    - Day 4: 0.75 mg daily.
    - Day 5: 1.25 mg daily.
    - Day 6 (and thereafter): Maintenance dose (1 mg or 2 mg) [Australian].
- **Administration:** Tablets should be swallowed whole, with or without food. During titration, doses should ideally be taken in the morning [Australian].
- **Missed Doses:**
    - **During Titration (first 6 days):** If a dose is missed, treatment must be re-initiated using a new titration pack.
    - **During Maintenance:** If 4 or more consecutive daily doses are missed, treatment must be re-initiated with a new titration pack. Interruptions of up to 3 days do not require re-titration [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to siponimod or excipients (e.g., lactose).
- **CYP2C9*3/*3 genotype.**
- **Recent Cardiovascular/Cerebrovascular Events (within the last 6 months):** Myocardial infarction, unstable angina, stroke, TIA, decompensated heart failure (requiring inpatient treatment), or NYHA Class III/IV heart failure.
- **Cardiac Arrhythmias:** Second-degree Mobitz type II AV block, third-degree AV block, sino-atrial heart block, or sick-sinus syndrome, unless the patient has a functional pacemaker [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Infections:** Siponimod causes dose-dependent reduction in peripheral lymphocyte counts (20–30% of baseline). 
    - **Monitoring:** Obtain a CBC (within 6 months or after prior therapy discontinuation) before starting. 
    - **Serious Infections:** Cases of cryptococcal meningitis (CM) and progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML) have been reported. Suspend treatment if PML or CM is suspected.
    - **VZV:** Test for Varicella Zoster Virus (VZV) antibodies in patients without a confirmed history of chickenpox or vaccination. Vaccinate antibody-negative patients 1 month before starting siponimod.
- **Macular Oedema:** Incidence of 1.8% vs 0.2% for placebo. Ophthalmic evaluation of the fundus is recommended before starting and if vision changes occur. Higher risk in patients with diabetes or uveitis [Australian].
- **Bradyarrhythmia:** Transient decrease in heart rate and AV conduction delays occur upon initiation. 
    - **6-Hour Observation:** Required for patients with sinus bradycardia (HR <55 bpm), first- or second-degree [Mobitz I] AV block, or history of MI/heart failure. 
- **Liver Function:** 11.3% of patients showed increased LFTs. Obtain transaminase and bilirubin levels within 6 months of initiation. Discontinue if significant liver injury is confirmed.
- **Cutaneous Neoplasms:** Increased risk of basal cell carcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, and melanoma. Periodic skin examinations are recommended; avoid UV-B radiation or PUVA-photochemotherapy.
- **Blood Pressure:** Hypertension was reported in 12.6% of patients. Monitor and manage BP during treatment.
- **Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES):** Rare cases reported for the S1P class; monitor for unexpected neurological symptoms [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Headache (15.2%), Hypertension (12.6%), Liver function test increased (11.3%).
- **Common (1–10%):** Dizziness (6.8%), Nausea (6.7%), Diarrhoea (6.4%), Pain in extremity (6.3%), Bradycardia (6.2%), Peripheral oedema (8.1%), Herpes zoster (2.5%), Seizures (1.7%), Macular oedema (1.8%), Lymphopenia (1.3%).
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Squamous cell carcinoma (0.2%), Malignant melanoma (0.2%).
- **Frequency Not Known:** PML, Cryptococcal meningitis, Hypersensitivity reactions (rash, urticaria, angioedema) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **Anti-neoplastic/Immune-modulators:** Caution due to additive immunosuppression. When switching from alemtuzumab, the half-life must be considered.
    - **Heart Rate Lowering Drugs:** Avoid concomitant use of Class Ia or Class III anti-arrhythmics during initiation. Use with caution with beta-blockers, verapamil, diltiazem, or digoxin.
    - **Vaccines:** Avoid live attenuated vaccines during and for 4 weeks after stopping treatment.
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **CYP2C9/CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Moderate dual inhibitors (e.g., fluconazole) can significantly increase siponimod exposure (up to 2-fold).
    - **CYP2C9/CYP3A4 Inducers:** Strong inducers (e.g., rifampicin, carbamazepine) can reduce siponimod exposure by up to 65%, potentially leading to loss of efficacy [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category D [Australian].**
    - Based on animal data, siponimod may cause fetal harm (embryotoxicity, fetotoxicity, and teratogenicity). 
    - **Contraception:** Females of reproductive potential must use effective contraception during treatment and for **10 days** after the last dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Excretion in human milk is unknown; however, it is present in rat milk. Because of the potential for serious adverse reactions in infants, women should not breastfeed during treatment [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients under 18 years have not been established.
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical experience in patients >65 years is limited; however, population PK suggests no dose adjustment is generally necessary.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild to severe impairment.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment required. Caution is advised in patients with a history of significant liver disease [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selective modulator of S1P receptors 1 and 5. By binding to S1P1 on lymphocytes, it prevents their egress from lymph nodes, reducing T-cell infiltration into the CNS. It also crosses the blood-brain barrier, potentially exerting neuroprotective effects via S1P1 on astrocytes and S1P5 on oligodendrocytes.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ ~4 hours; bioavailability ~84%.
    - **Distribution:** Apparent volume of distribution 124 L; 99.9% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via **CYP2C9 (79.3%)** and CYP3A4 (18.5%).
    - **Elimination:** Apparent half-life ~30 hours; eliminated via metabolism and biliary/fecal excretion [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **EXPAND Study (A2304):** A Phase 3 trial in 1,651 SPMS patients. Siponimod 2 mg significantly reduced the risk of 3-month confirmed disability progression (CDP) by 21.2% compared to placebo (HR 0.79; p=0.0134). It also significantly reduced the annualized relapse rate (ARR) by 55% and reduced T2 lesion volume increase [Australian].
- **BOLD Study (A2201):** A Phase 2 study in RRMS patients demonstrating a dose-dependent reduction in inflammatory MRI lesions [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Prior to Dispensing:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze.
- **After Dispensing:**
    - **0.25 mg:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (refrigerated).
    - **1 mg and 2 mg:** Store below 30°C for up to 3 months.
- Keep in original package to protect from moisture [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Medicine) [Australian].
