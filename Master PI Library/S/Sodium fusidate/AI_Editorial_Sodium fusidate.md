# Editorial Product Information: Sodium fusidate

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (TGA).
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of sodium fusidate, synthesised for healthcare professionals. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Sodium fusidate.
- **Chemical Class:** Steroidal antibiotic (derived from *Fusidium coccineum*).
- **Brand Names:** Fucidin [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 250 mg [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** LEO Pharma Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Staphylococcal Infections:** Treatment of both localised and generalised infections caused by susceptible organisms [Australian].
- **Specific Clinical Scenarios:** In cases involving severe infections, deep-seated infections, methicillin-resistant *Staphylococci* (MRSA), or where prolonged therapy is anticipated, sodium fusidate must be administered in combination with other anti-staphylococcal antibiotics [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Mild to Moderate Skin/Skin-Structure Infections:** 250 mg twice daily [Australian].
- **Other Staphylococcus aureus Infections:** 500 mg (2 x 250 mg) three times daily [Australian].
- **Severe Cases:** The dosage may be doubled if clinically indicated [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing
- **Children (5 to 12 years):** 250 mg three times daily [Australian].
- **Children (>12 years):** Follow adult dosing guidelines [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Food Interaction:** Tablets should be taken without a meal. Concomitant food intake reduces both the rate and extent of absorption [Australian].
- **Duration:** Typically 5–10 days; however, more severe infections may require longer courses [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Statins:** Concomitant use with HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors (statins) is strictly contraindicated due to the risk of rhabdomyolysis [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to sodium fusidate, fusidic acid, or any excipients (including lactose, galactose, or sugars) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Rhabdomyolysis:** Must not be co-administered with statins. Fatalities have been reported. Statin therapy must be suspended during sodium fusidate treatment and should not be reintroduced until seven days after the final dose of the antibiotic [Australian].
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Metabolised by the liver and excreted in bile. Elevated liver enzymes and jaundice have occurred; these are usually reversible upon cessation. Regular liver function tests (LFTs) are required for patients on high doses, prolonged therapy, or those with pre-existing hepatic dysfunction [Australian].
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Life-threatening reactions, including Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS), Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), and Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN), have been reported. Therapy must be permanently discontinued at the first sign of a serious skin reaction [Australian].
- **Hyperbilirubinaemia:** Sodium fusidate competitively inhibits the binding of bilirubin to albumin. Caution is required in patients with impaired bilirubin transport/metabolism and in neonates due to the risk of kernicterus [Australian].
- **Resistance:** Extended or recurrent use may increase the risk of antibiotic resistance [Australian].
- **Excipients:** Tablets contain lactose; patients with rare hereditary galactose intolerance, Lapp lactase deficiency, or glucose-galactose malabsorption should avoid use [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common (1–10%)
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal pain/discomfort, dyspepsia [Australian].
- **General:** Lethargy, fatigue, asthenia [Australian].

### Uncommon (0.1–1%)
- **Hepatobiliary:** Hepatic failure, hepatitis, cholestasis, jaundice, hyperbilirubinaemia, abnormal LFTs [Australian].
- **Hematologic:** Pancytopenia, leukopenia (including neutropenia/agranulocytosis), thrombocytopenia, anaemia [Australian].
- **Musculoskeletal:** Rhabdomyolysis (symptoms include muscle weakness, swelling, dark urine, and elevated creatine kinase) [Australian].
- **Dermatologic:** Urticaria, pruritus, rash, erythema, Acute Generalised Exanthematous Pustulosis (AGEP) [Australian].
- **Neurological:** Headache, somnolence [Australian].
- **Renal:** Renal failure [Australian].
- **Immune:** Anaphylactic reaction/shock [Australian].

### Rare (<0.1%)
- **Dermatologic:** Angioedema [Australian].
- **Hepatobiliary:** Abnormal hepatic function [Australian].

### Frequency Not Known
- SJS, TEN, and DRESS syndrome [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **HMG-CoA Reductase Inhibitors (Statins):** Significantly increased risk of myopathy and rhabdomyolysis. This combination is contraindicated [Australian].
- **Oral Anticoagulants (e.g., Warfarin):** May increase plasma concentrations of coumarin derivatives, enhancing the anticoagulant effect. Requires close monitoring of INR and potential downward dose adjustment of the anticoagulant [Australian].
- **HIV Protease Inhibitors (e.g., Ritonavir, Saquinavir):** Co-administration increases plasma concentrations of both the antibiotic and the protease inhibitor, potentially leading to hepatotoxicity. Concomitant use is not recommended [Australian].
- **CYP-3A4 Substrates:** Evidence is conflicting regarding whether sodium fusidate inhibits or induces CYP-3A4. Monitor patients closely when co-administered with drugs metabolised by this pathway [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category C:** Sodium fusidate crosses the placental barrier. While animal studies do not show direct reproductive toxicity, safety in humans is not established. Use during the last month of pregnancy may cause neonatal kernicterus due to bilirubin displacement. Avoidance during pregnancy is preferred [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** The drug is excreted in human milk. Safety has not been established; caution is advised for nursing mothers [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No clinical data available. Pre-clinical studies in rats showed no effect on fertility at oral doses up to 400 mg/kg/day [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - Contraindicated in the first month of life due to the risk of kernicterus from protein-binding displacement of bilirubin [Australian].
    - Dosing established for children aged 5–12 years [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available in provided sources [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Dose reduction may be necessary as the drug is cleared via hepatic metabolism. Consider alternative antibiotics in patients with significant hepatic dysfunction [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No specific dose adjustment provided in source, though uncommon cases of renal failure have been reported as adverse effects [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits bacterial protein synthesis by preventing the translocation of elongation factor G (EF-G) on the ribosome [Australian].
- **Microbiology:** Primarily active against Gram-positive organisms, specifically *Staphylococcus aureus* (including penicillinase-producing and MRSA strains). Minimal activity against Gram-negative organisms or fungi [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Well absorbed orally. $C_{max}$ is reached in 2–4 hours. Under fasting conditions, a 500 mg dose produces a $C_{max}$ of ~38.8 µg/mL with a half-life ($T_{1/2}$) of ~8.9 hours [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** High protein binding (95%). Distributes into bone, pus, synovial fluid, and soft tissue; penetrates poorly into cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Hepatic [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Excreted primarily via bile. Only 0.15% is excreted in urine and ~2% in faeces as unchanged drug [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **European Studies (1987–1991):** Two large-scale randomised, double-blind trials (N=960 total) compared 250 mg BID, 500 mg BID, and 500 mg TID for skin and soft tissue infections. Combined results showed clinical "cure or improvement" in 91–93% of patients after 10 days across all dosing cohorts. Bacteriological eradication of *S. aureus* at day 5 ranged from 52% to 77% [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to packaging; information is maintained on the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG) [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
