# Editorial Product Information: Sebelipase alfa

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Sebelipase alfa rce
- **Chemical Class:** Recombinant human lysosomal acid lipase (rhLAL); a monomeric glycoprotein produced by recombinant DNA technology in the egg white of transgenic chickens (*Gallus*).
- **Brand Name:** Kanuma [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Concentrated solution for intravenous (IV) infusion; 20 mg/10 mL (2 mg/mL) single-use vial.
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Alexion Pharmaceuticals Australasia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Lysosomal Acid Lipase Deficiency (LAL-D):** Indicated as long-term enzyme replacement therapy (ERT) in patients of all ages [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
Treatment should be initiated as early as possible following diagnosis.

- **Infants (<6 months of age) with rapidly progressive LAL-D:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 1 mg/kg or 3 mg/kg administered once weekly.
    - Dose escalation: May be increased to 3 mg/kg or up to 5 mg/kg once weekly based on suboptimal clinical response (e.g., poor growth, worsening biochemical markers, or persistent organomegaly).
    - Doses up to 7.5 mg/kg once weekly have been studied in isolated cases, but doses exceeding this have not been evaluated [Australian].

- **Children and Adults with LAL-D:**
    - Recommended dose: 1 mg/kg administered once every two weeks.
    - Dose escalation: May be increased to 3 mg/kg once every two weeks based on suboptimal clinical response [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Preparation:** Vials should reach room temperature (15°C to 25°C) before dilution. Dilute with 0.9% sodium chloride using aseptic technique. Do not shake the vials.
- **Infusion:** Administer using a low-protein binding infusion set with an in-line, low-protein binding 0.2 μm filter.
- **Duration:** Total volume should typically be infused over approximately 2 hours. For the 1 mg/kg dose, a 1-hour infusion may be considered once patient tolerability is established.
- **Storage after Dilution:** Use as soon as practicable. If necessary, diluted solution can be held at 2-8°C for up to 24 hours or below 25°C for up to 8 hours [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known life-threatening hypersensitivity (anaphylactic reaction) to the active substance, to egg, or to any of the excipients, particularly where attempts to rechallenge have been unsuccessful [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity and Anaphylaxis:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, have been reported (approx. 4% of clinical trial patients). Symptoms include respiratory distress, chest discomfort, tachycardia, hypotension, and urticaria. Most reactions occur during or within 4 hours of infusion.
- **Management of Reactions:** Appropriate medical support must be available during administration. If a severe reaction occurs, the infusion should be stopped immediately. For milder reactions, the infusion may be temporarily interrupted or the rate slowed. Pre-treatment with antipyretics and/or antihistamines may be considered for subsequent doses.
- **Egg Allergy:** Since the enzyme is produced in transgenic egg whites, patients with a history of egg allergy were excluded from trials. Use in these patients requires a careful risk-benefit assessment.
- **Monitoring:** Periodic monitoring of clinical and biochemical parameters is required to assess treatment response. Physicians are encouraged to enrol patients in a dedicated LAL-D monitoring program [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect profiles differ significantly between the infant population and the older paediatric/adult population.

### Very Common Adverse Effects (≥10%)
| Population | Adverse Reactions |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Infants (<6 months)** | Diarrhoea (79%), Vomiting (79%), Pyrexia (79%), Hypersensitivity (68%), Cough (58%), Rhinitis (53%), Tachycardia (53%), Respiratory distress (42%), Anaemia (37%), Nasopharyngitis (47%) |
| **Children & Adults** | Nasopharyngitis (49%), Headache (44%), Hypersensitivity (43%), Pyrexia (42%), Diarrhoea (39%), Abdominal pain (32%), Oropharyngeal pain (20%), Nausea (18%), Dizziness (12%), Fatigue (11%) |

### Serious and Selected Effects
- **Anaphylaxis:** Occurred in approximately 16% of infants and 2% of children/adults in clinical studies.
- **Transient Hyperlipidaemia:** Initiation of therapy may cause asymptomatic increases in circulating cholesterol and triglycerides due to the breakdown of accumulated lysosomal lipids. These typically occur within 2–4 weeks and resolve within 8 weeks of treatment.
- **Immunogenicity:** Anti-drug antibodies (ADAs) were detected in 15% of the total trial population. A higher incidence of ADAs (53%) and neutralising antibodies (47%) was observed in infants. While generally not linked to clinical response, three patients with specific homozygous genetic deletions showed decreased clinical response associated with high inhibitory antibody activity [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Interaction Studies:** No formal drug-drug interaction studies have been performed.
- **Pharmacokinetic Profile:** As a recombinant protein, sebelipase alfa is unlikely to be affected by cytochrome P450-mediated interactions [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category B1**. Animal studies (rats/rabbits) showed no evidence of embryofoetal harm at doses significantly higher than human equivalents. There are no adequate data in pregnant women; therefore, use is generally avoided as a precautionary measure.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if the drug is excreted in human milk. A risk-benefit assessment regarding the importance of the drug to the mother versus the benefit of breastfeeding to the infant is required.
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in animal models [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Most clinical trial data (70%) involve paediatric patients. Dosing is weight-based, and infants with rapidly progressive disease may require higher/more frequent dosing.
- **Elderly Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients >65 years have not been established.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustments are recommended. Sebelipase alfa is expected to undergo peptide hydrolysis; thus, hepatic impairment is not expected to affect pharmacokinetics. Renal elimination is considered a minor pathway [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** LAL-D is an autosomal recessive disorder where the deficiency of the LAL enzyme leads to the accumulation of cholesteryl esters and triglycerides in the liver, spleen, and blood vessels. Sebelipase alfa is a recombinant form of the human enzyme. It binds to cell surface receptors, is internalised, and moves to the lysosomes where it hydrolyses the accumulated lipids.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Treatment results in reductions in liver fat content, transaminases (ALT/AST), LDL-cholesterol, and triglycerides, while increasing HDL-cholesterol.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** In a population PK analysis (n=102), the terminal half-life ($t_{1/2}$) was approximately 2.35 hours. No drug accumulation was observed with bi-weekly dosing [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Infant Survival (Study LAL-CL03):** An open-label study of 9 infants showed that 67% (6/9) survived beyond 12 months, compared to 0% in a historical cohort of untreated infants.
- **Adult/Paediatric Efficacy (Study LAL-CL02):** A double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (n=66) demonstrated significant improvements over 20 weeks. Normalisation of ALT was achieved in 31% of the treatment group vs 7% for placebo ($p=0.0271$). Significant mean reductions were also seen in LDL-cholesterol (-28%) and liver fat content (-32%) compared to the placebo group [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store in a refrigerator at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze.
- **Light:** Keep vials in the original carton to protect from light.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
