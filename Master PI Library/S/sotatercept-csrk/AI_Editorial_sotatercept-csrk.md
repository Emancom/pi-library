# Editorial Product Information: sotatercept-csrk

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): sotatercept-csrk  
- Chemical class: Activin signaling inhibitor (fusion protein biologic)  
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources  
- Route(s) of administration: Subcutaneous injection

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 2024 (stated in source)  
- Last revision/update of provided source PI: Revised 6/2026 (stated in source)  
- Withdrawals, suspensions, or restrictions: Not specified in available sources  
- Registrations/approvals in other jurisdictions and their dates: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of adults with pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH; WHO Group 1 pulmonary hypertension) to:
  - improve exercise capacity and WHO functional class; and
  - reduce the risk of clinical worsening events, including hospitalization for PAH, lung transplantation and death.
- Jurisdictional variations: Indication language as above is presented in the available source for the U.S. [US]. Approvals or indication differences in other jurisdictions: Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Recommended starting dose: 0.3 mg/kg administered subcutaneously once every 3 weeks.
- Recommended target dose: 0.7 mg/kg administered subcutaneously once every 3 weeks after verification of acceptable hemoglobin and platelet counts.

### Dose calculation and injection volume
- Reconstituted concentration: 50 mg/mL (after reconstitution as described in the Instructions for Use).
- Injection volume (example calculations):
  - Starting dose: Injection volume (mL) = (weight in kg × 0.3 mg/kg) ÷ 50 mg/mL. Round to nearest 0.1 mL. Example: 70 kg → (70 × 0.3) ÷ 50 = 0.42 mL → round to 0.4 mL.
  - Target dose: Injection volume (mL) = (weight in kg × 0.7 mg/kg) ÷ 50 mg/mL. Round to nearest 0.1 mL. Example: 70 kg → (70 × 0.7) ÷ 50 = 0.98 mL → round to 1.0 mL.

### Kit/vial selection guidance (clinical dosing relevance)
- Use appropriate vial(s) to deliver the calculated injection volume and dose. If a patient’s calculated dose requires combining contents of two vials, follow the instructions provided to combine vials to achieve the correct dose and minimize number of injections. (Specific kit/vial presentations and pack configurations are described in the source PI; clinicians should follow product-specific Instructions for Use.)

### Titration
- After confirming acceptable hemoglobin (Hgb) and platelet counts following initiation, escalate from 0.3 mg/kg to 0.7 mg/kg every 3 weeks unless dose adjustments are required due to laboratory changes or adverse events.

### Monitoring-directed dose modification
- Check hemoglobin and platelet count prior to the first dose and before each dose for the first 5 doses (or longer if values are unstable). Thereafter, monitor periodically.
- Delay treatment for at least 3 weeks if any of the following occur:
  - Hgb increases >2.0 g/dL from the previous dose and is above the upper limit of normal (ULN).
  - Hgb increases >4.0 g/dL from baseline.
  - Hgb increases >2.0 g/dL above ULN.
  - Platelet count decreases to <50,000/mm3 (<50 × 10^9/L).
- Recheck Hgb and platelet count prior to reinitiating treatment after a delay.
- For treatment interruptions lasting >9 weeks, restart at 0.3 mg/kg and re-escalate to 0.7 mg/kg only after verifying acceptable Hgb and platelet counts.

### Missed dose and scheduling
- If a scheduled dose is missed, administer as soon as possible. If not administered within 3 days of the scheduled date, adjust the schedule so that subsequent dosing maintains 3-week intervals.

### Overdose
- In case of overdose, monitor for erythrocytosis and related complications and manage supportively.

### Preparation and administration
- Reconstitution and preparation steps: allow refrigerated components to reach room temperature (~15 minutes per Instructions for Use), attach syringe to vial adapter, inject sterile diluent into vial of lyophilized product to achieve 50 mg/mL, gently swirl (do not shake), allow bubbles to dissipate; use reconstituted solution as soon as possible and within the timeframe specified in product Instructions for Use. Withdraw the calculated volume into the syringe for subcutaneous injection.
- Administration sites: abdomen (at least 2 inches from the navel), upper thigh, or upper arm. For patient/caregiver self-administration, the abdomen and upper thigh are recommended. Select a different injection site each dose; avoid scarred, tender, or bruised areas.
- Training: patients and caregivers may administer when appropriately trained and assessed for competence by the healthcare provider. Confirm proper technique at follow-up visits, especially if dose or kit presentation changes.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- **Erythrocytosis**: sotatercept-csrk may increase hemoglobin. Severe erythrocytosis can increase the risk of thromboembolism and hyperviscosity syndrome. Monitor Hgb as specified; modify or delay dosing per the dose-modification criteria in Section 4.
- **Severe thrombocytopenia**: sotatercept-csrk may reduce platelet counts; severe thrombocytopenia (<50,000/mm3) increases bleeding risk. Do not initiate treatment if platelet count is <50,000/mm3. Monitor platelets as specified and withhold dosing for low counts per criteria.
- **Serious bleeding**: serious bleeding events (including gastrointestinal and intracranial hemorrhage) have occurred; risk is greater in patients receiving concomitant prostacyclin therapy and/or antithrombotic agents or who have low platelet counts. Do not administer sotatercept-csrk to patients experiencing serious bleeding. Evaluate and manage bleeding promptly.
- **Embryo-fetal toxicity**: based on animal studies, sotatercept-csrk may cause fetal harm. Advise females of reproductive potential to use effective contraception during treatment and for at least 4 months after the final dose. Inform pregnant women of potential fetal risks.
- **Impaired fertility**: animal data indicate potential impairment of male and female fertility; counsel patients accordingly.
- **Intrapulmonary right-to-left shunting and hypoxemia**: cases have been reported in clinical studies and post-marketing, including worsening hypoxemia despite improved PAH hemodynamics. Consider evaluation for shunting if unexplained hypoxemia occurs; partial to complete improvement in oxygenation has been observed after drug discontinuation in some cases.
- **Increased blood pressure**: mean changes in systolic/diastolic blood pressure were observed in clinical studies (example: increase from baseline by approximately 2.2/4.9 mmHg at 24 weeks in one trial). Monitor blood pressure as clinically indicated.
- **Telangiectasia and skin changes**: telangiectasia has been reported, with median time to onset observed at ~36 weeks in one study. Skin hypopigmentation has been reported.
- **Pericardial effusion**: reported in post-marketing experience.
- **Injection site reactions**: reported in post-marketing experience.
- Monitoring requirements summary:
  - Hemoglobin and platelet counts prior to first dose and before each dose for first 5 doses (or longer if unstable), then periodically thereafter.
  - Clinical monitoring for signs/symptoms of bleeding, thromboembolism, hypoxemia/shunting, and injection-site reactions.
  - Pregnancy testing and counseling per clinical practice for women of reproductive potential (use of contraception as above).

- Additional safety observations:
  - Management of increased Hgb and platelet decreases in trials included dose delays and dose reductions (examples: Hgb increases managed by dose delays in 10% of patients, dose reductions in 6%, or both in 5% in STELLAR; platelet decreases managed by dose delays 2%, dose reductions 2%, or both 2%).
  - Treatment discontinuation due to adverse reactions: 4% in sotatercept-csrk group vs 7% in placebo group in STELLAR; no single adverse reaction caused discontinuation in >1% more frequently in the sotatercept-csrk arm.
  - Long‑term, uncontrolled extension data (PULSAR extension) with mean exposure ~151 weeks showed a safety profile generally consistent with STELLAR.
  - Post-marketing adverse reactions include pericardial effusion and injection site reactions.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Pharmacodynamic interactions:
  - Concomitant prostacyclin therapy and/or antithrombotic agents (e.g., anticoagulants, antiplatelet agents) were associated with higher likelihood of serious bleeding events and more frequent thrombocytopenia in clinical studies. Exercise clinical caution and monitor patients closely when co-administering these agents.
- Pharmacokinetic interactions: Not specified in available sources.
- Dosage modification or monitoring recommendations with interacting drugs:
  - Increased vigilance for bleeding and platelet monitoring is recommended when sotatercept-csrk is used with prostacyclin infusion or antithrombotic therapies. Specific dosing changes of concomitant drugs are not provided in the available sources.
- Region-specific interactions: Not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- **Risk summary:** Animal reproduction studies demonstrate that sotatercept-csrk can cause fetal harm (increased embryo-fetal mortality, growth alterations, structural variations) at exposures comparable to or above human exposures at the maximum recommended human dose. There are no adequate human data to assess risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse maternal or fetal outcomes with sotatercept-csrk.
- **Clinical recommendation:** Advise females of reproductive potential of potential fetal risk. Use effective contraception during treatment and for at least 4 months after the final dose.
- **Pregnancy category/system:** Not specified in available sources for jurisdictions other than descriptive animal-risk data. (If a jurisdiction-specific pregnancy category is required, it is not provided in the available sources.)

### Breastfeeding
- Breastfeeding: Breastfeeding is not recommended (statement in available source). Data on excretion into human milk and effects on the breastfed infant are not provided in the available sources.

### Fertility
- Animal data indicate potential impairment of male and female fertility following exposure to sotatercept-csrk. Counsel patients about potential effects on fertility.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Sotatercept-csrk is an activin signaling inhibitor (a fusion protein designed to modulate transforming growth factor‑beta superfamily signaling). The mechanism is associated with effects on hematopoiesis (e.g., increases in hemoglobin) and vascular/endothelial biology.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Detailed human pharmacokinetic parameters (absorption rate, bioavailability, distribution, metabolism, elimination, half-life) are not specified in the provided source excerpt.
- Animal exposure comparisons (AUC) were used in reproductive toxicity studies to relate doses producing adverse developmental outcomes to human exposures at the recommended dose; specific numeric PK parameters are not provided in the available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Biological effects observed include increases in hemoglobin and reductions in platelet count in some patients. Blood pressure increases and development of telangiectasia have been observed.

## 12. Clinical Trials
### STELLAR (pivotal trial summary)
- Population: Adult patients with PAH WHO Functional Class II or III.
- Design: Randomized 1:1 sotatercept-csrk vs placebo on top of background standard of care therapy.
- Dosing: starting 0.3 mg/kg subcutaneously, escalated to 0.7 mg/kg every 3 weeks; primary placebo-controlled treatment period 24 weeks with continuation into a long-term double-blind period.
- Size and exposure: 323 randomized; median duration of treatment ~313 days in sotatercept-csrk group vs 273 days in placebo group (reported for the trial populations).
- Key efficacy outcomes (as described in indication): Improvement in exercise capacity and WHO functional class and reduction in risk of clinical worsening events (including hospitalization for PAH, lung transplantation, and death). (Detailed numerical efficacy endpoints and statistical results are not specified in the available excerpt.)
- Safety highlights: Most common adverse reactions (≥10% and ≥5% more than placebo) included headache, epistaxis, rash, telangiectasia, diarrhea, dizziness, erythema, and increased hemoglobin. Serious bleeding occurred in 4% vs 1% (sotatercept-csrk vs placebo) in STELLAR.

### ZENITH
- Population: Adult patients with PAH WHO FC III or IV at high risk of mortality.
- Design: Randomized 1:1 sotatercept-csrk vs placebo on top of background standard of care therapies.
- Size and exposure: 172 randomized; median duration of exposure longer in sotatercept-csrk group (435 days) than placebo (268 days).
- Safety highlights: Overall adverse reaction incidence was higher in ZENITH than STELLAR; severe thrombocytopenia (<50,000/mm3) occurred in approximately 6% of patients receiving sotatercept-csrk. Serious bleeding occurred in 7% vs 5% (sotatercept-csrk vs placebo) in ZENITH.
- Efficacy: Patients receiving sotatercept-csrk contributed to the clinical evidence supporting the indication (specific efficacy endpoint details not provided in available excerpt).

### PULSAR (phase 2)
- Design: Randomized double-blind 24-week placebo-controlled period followed by an 18-month active-drug extension.
- Safety: Uncontrolled long-term extension period safety profile generally similar to STELLAR. Mean duration of exposure in extension population ~151 weeks (max 218 weeks) for patients treated with 0.3 or 0.7 mg/kg.

### SOTERIA (long-term open-label)
- Ongoing open-label long-term safety and efficacy study; reported cases of intrapulmonary right-to-left shunting with associated hypoxemia in a small number of participants (<0.5%) and additional post-marketing reports.

### Trial context and interpretation
- Trial adverse event rates reflect the conditions and populations studied and may not predict rates in broader clinical practice. Safety management in trials frequently included dose delays and dose reductions for hematologic abnormalities.
