# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **US (FDA)**.
- At the time of publication, PI documents for other major jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian) were not provided for comparison.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview intended for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acids.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative [(R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt].
- **Brand Names:** **Levocarnitine Injection, USP** [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. This may be administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion. 
    - *Loading Dose:* In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - *Frequency:* Dosing should be every 3 or 4 hours and never less than every 6 hours.
    - *Maximum Dose:* The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - *Timing:* Administer as a 2 to 3-minute slow bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - *Adjustment:* Dose reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels [US].

### Administration and Monitoring
- **Visual Inspection:** Parenteral products should be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use [US].
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL in PVC bags [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of injection. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with and without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Safety):** The safety of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal failure. High-dose oral administration in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].
- **Monitoring:** INR should be monitored in patients receiving concomitant warfarin therapy [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions in ESRD patients (incidence ≥5% in clinical trials) include:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (5–21%), headache (3–37%), injection site reaction (27–38%), pain (21–35%), fever (5–12%), asthenia (8–12%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%), chest pain (6–15%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%).
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hypervolemia (3–12%).
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, seizures, and laryngeal edema [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concurrently with warfarin. 
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels at treatment initiation and following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specifically assigned, but document states it should be used "only if clearly needed."
    - **Animal Data:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine following exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess intake must be weighed against maternal benefits; consider discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no evidence of impaired fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg for metabolic disorders); specific age-related safety data not further delineated [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** See Section 3 (Dosage) for ESRD-specific intravenous instructions. Note caution regarding oral formulations in this population due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is essential for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic/fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Administration to ESRD patients results in increased plasma carnitine concentrations [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, IV levocarnitine (20 mg/kg post-dialysis) increased mean pre-dialysis plasma concentrations from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. 
- **Dose-Ranging:** Trough concentrations remained stable or increased over 12–24 weeks with doses of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg. While carnitine concentrations increased, the effect on specific clinical outcomes in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial, as it contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
