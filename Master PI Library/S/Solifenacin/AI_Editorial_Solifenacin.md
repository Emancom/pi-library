# Editorial Product Information: Solifenacin

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or clinical guidelines mandated in a specific jurisdiction. Prescribers should consult the current local PI for legal and prescribing compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Solifenacin succinate (a competitive muscarinic receptor antagonist).
- **Brand Names:** AKM Solifenacin [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** G04B D08.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 5 mg (light yellow, debossed "S5") and 10 mg (light pink, debossed "S10") [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pharmacor Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Overactive Bladder:** Treatment of overactive bladder characterised by symptoms of urgency, increased urinary frequency, or urge urinary incontinence [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Elderly):** The standard starting dose is 5 mg once daily. If required and tolerated, the dose may be increased to a maximum of 10 mg once daily [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets should be swallowed whole with liquids and may be taken regardless of food intake [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Titration:** Dose escalation from 5 mg to 10 mg should be based on individual patient response and tolerability [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
Solifenacin is contraindicated in patients with:
- Urinary retention [Australian].
- Uncontrolled narrow-angle glaucoma [Australian].
- Myasthenia gravis [Australian].
- Severe gastrointestinal conditions, such as toxic megacolon or gastric retention [Australian].
- Hypersensitivity to solifenacin succinate or any tablet excipients [Australian].
- Severe hepatic impairment [Australian].
- Severe renal impairment or moderate hepatic impairment while concurrently receiving a potent CYP3A4 inhibitor (e.g., ketoconazole) [Australian].
- Requirement for haemodialysis [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Angioedema and Anaphylaxis:** Cases of angioedema with airway obstruction and anaphylactic reactions have been reported. Therapy must be discontinued immediately if these occur [Australian].
- **QT Prolongation:** Caution is required in patients with a history of QT prolongation, hypokalaemia, bradycardia, or those taking other QT-prolonging medications. Torsade de Pointes has been observed in high-risk patients [Australian].
- **Outflow Obstruction:** Use with caution in patients with clinically significant bladder outflow obstruction due to the risk of urinary retention [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal Disorders:** Caution is advised in patients with obstructive gastrointestinal disorders or decreased motility. Due to the risk of exacerbating oesophagitis, use with caution in patients with hiatus hernia or gastro-oesophageal reflux [Australian].
- **Autonomic Neuropathy:** Use with caution [Australian].
- **Excipients:** Tablets contain lactose; use with caution in patients with rare hereditary galactose intolerance, Lapp lactase deficiency, or glucose-galactose malabsorption [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following frequencies are based on pooled data from 12-week clinical trials.

| System Organ Class | Very Common (>10%) | Common (1–10%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Dry mouth (10.9% at 5mg; 27.6% at 10mg) | Constipation, nausea, dyspepsia, abdominal pain |
| **Infections** | | Urinary tract infection, influenza, nasopharyngitis |
| **Nervous System** | | Headache, dizziness |
| **Eye Disorders** | | Blurred vision |
| **General** | | Fatigue, peripheral oedema |

- **Uncommon (<1%):** Gastro-oesophageal reflux, flatulence, somnolence, dysgeusia, dry skin, and urinary retention [Australian].
- **Post-Marketing Experience:** Serious but rare/very rare events include Torsade de Pointes, atrial fibrillation, hallucinations, delirium, erythema multiforme, and exfoliative dermatitis [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Solifenacin is a substrate of CYP3A4. Concomitant use with potent inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, ritonavir, itraconazole) increases solifenacin exposure. The maximum dose must be restricted to **5 mg daily** when used with these agents [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Agents like rifampicin, phenytoin, and carbamazepine may reduce solifenacin plasma concentrations [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Concurrent use with other anticholinergic therapies may increase the frequency and severity of side effects. Solifenacin may reduce the effect of prokinetic agents (e.g., metoclopramide) [Australian].
- **Other:** No significant interactions were found with combined oral contraceptives, warfarin, or digoxin [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B3.** Animal studies show reduced foetal weight and postnatal survival at high doses. There are no adequate studies in pregnant women; use only if potential benefits outweigh risks to the foetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Solifenacin is excreted in the milk of lactating mice. Administration is not recommended during breastfeeding [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in animal models [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established; use is not recommended in children [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment is required based solely on age, though exposure (AUC and Cmax) is 20-25% higher in older patients [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - Mild to moderate: No adjustment needed.
    - Severe (CrCl <30 mL/min): Use with caution; dose must not exceed 5 mg [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - Mild: No adjustment needed.
    - Moderate (Child-Pugh B): Use with caution; dose must not exceed 5 mg.
    - Severe (Child-Pugh C): Contraindicated [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Solifenacin is a competitive muscarinic receptor antagonist with selectivity for the bladder over salivary glands in some models. It inhibits bladder contractions mediated by acetylcholine [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is ~90%; peak plasma levels (Tmax) are reached 3–8 hours post-dose. Steady state is reached after 10 days [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Apparent volume of distribution is ~600 L; 98% protein bound (primarily to $\alpha_1$-acid glycoprotein) [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised in the liver, primarily via CYP3A4. One active metabolite (4R-hydroxy solifenacin) exists but has low clinical contribution [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Elimination half-life is 45–68 hours. Approximately 70% is excreted in urine and 23% in faeces [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** Four 12-week, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials (n=3027) evaluated doses of 5 mg and 10 mg.
- **Outcomes:** Both doses showed statistically significant improvements compared to placebo in reducing micturition frequency, urgency episodes, and incontinence episodes. Improvements were typically observed within one week of starting treatment [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** 24 months [Australian].
- **Handling:** Keep in the original container (Alu-Alu blister or HDPE bottle) [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
