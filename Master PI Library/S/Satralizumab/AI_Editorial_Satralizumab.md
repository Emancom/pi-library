# Editorial Product Information: Satralizumab

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the Product Information for satralizumab. It is designed as a high-level clinical reference for healthcare professionals to aid in the understanding of the drug’s regulatory and clinical profile.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial summary and does not replace the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information (PI) required for prescribing. Clinicians must consult the current approved PI in their specific region before initiating or managing treatment.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Satralizumab (recombinant humanised IgG2 monoclonal antibody).
- **Brand Name:** Enspryng [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L04AC19.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 120 mg of satralizumab in 1 mL solution in a single-dose pre-filled syringe (PFS) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Roche Products Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Neuromyelitis Optica Spectrum Disorders (NMOSD):** Indicated as monotherapy or in combination with stable immunosuppressive therapy (IST) for the treatment of adults with NMOSD who are anti-aquaporin 4 antibody (AQP4)-IgG positive [Australian].
- **Note:** Satralizumab is not intended for the acute treatment of NMOSD relapses [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing
- **Loading Dose:** 120 mg by SC injection at Week 0, Week 2, and Week 4.
- **Maintenance Dose:** 120 mg by SC injection every 4 weeks.

### Administration Instructions
- **Method:** Subcutaneous injection only.
- **Injection Sites:** Abdomen or thigh. Sites should be rotated. Do not inject into moles, scars, or areas where the skin is tender, bruised, or hard.
- **Supervision:** The first injection must be performed under the supervision of a physician experienced in NMOSD management. Subsequent doses may be administered by the patient or caregiver if deemed appropriate by the physician [Australian].

### Missed Dose Instructions
If a dose is missed for reasons other than liver enzyme elevations, it should be administered as follows:
- **Maintenance period (missed <8 weeks):** Administer as soon as possible; reset dosing schedule to every 4 weeks from that point.
- **Maintenance period (missed 8 to <12 weeks):** Administer at "0" and 2 weeks, then every 4 weeks.
- **Maintenance period (missed ≥12 weeks):** Re-initiate loading schedule (0, 2, and 4 weeks), then every 4 weeks.
- **Loading period:** If a loading dose is missed, administer as soon as possible and adjust the subsequent loading/maintenance intervals accordingly [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to satralizumab or any excipients (including Chinese hamster ovary cell proteins) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Infections:** Treatment should be delayed in patients with active infections until the infection is controlled. If a serious infection develops, interrupt treatment until resolved. Caution is advised in patients with chronic/recurring infections or underlying conditions (e.g., diabetes) predisposing them to infection [Australian].
- **Vaccinations:** Live or live-attenuated vaccines should not be given concurrently with satralizumab. Complete all required immunisations according to local guidelines prior to initiating therapy [Australian].
- **Neutropenia:** Decreases in neutrophil counts have been observed. Monitor neutrophil counts 4 to 8 weeks after starting therapy and thereafter as clinically indicated. If the count is <1.0 x10⁹/L and confirmed, interrupt treatment until it recovers to >1.0 x10⁹/L [Australian].
- **Liver Enzyme Elevations:** ALT and AST elevations have been observed. Monitor ALT/AST every 4 weeks for the first 3 months, then every 3 months for one year, then as clinically indicated [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Systemic injection-related reactions (IRRs) may occur. Discontinue permanently if anaphylaxis or serious hypersensitivity occurs [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The safety profile is based on data from pivotal trials (monotherapy and combination with IST).

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Headache, Injection-related reactions (IRRs), Arthralgia (monotherapy), Rash (monotherapy), Decreased white blood cell count. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Migraine, Musculoskeletal stiffness, Insomnia, Peripheral oedema, Allergic rhinitis, Hypofibrinogenemia, Increased blood bilirubin, Pruritus. |

- **Injection-Related Reactions:** Most were mild to moderate, occurring within 24 hours. Systemic symptoms included headache and diarrhoea; local reactions included flushing, erythema, and pain.
- **Laboratory Abnormalities:**
    - **Neutrophils:** Decreases were generally transient and not associated with serious infections.
    - **Platelets:** Decreases occurred in 24% of patients but were not associated with bleeding.
    - **Lipids:** Elevations in total cholesterol and triglycerides were common but did not require treatment interruption.
    - **Fibrinogen:** Reductions are a known effect related to IL-6 receptor blockade; no bleeding events were reported [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP450 Substrates:** IL-6 signalling suppression may normalize the expression of CYP450 enzymes. Caution and monitoring are advised when initiating or discontinuing satralizumab in patients taking CYP450 substrates with a narrow therapeutic index (e.g., warfarin, theophylline, phenytoin), as dose adjustments may be required [Australian].
- **Concurrent IST:** Population PK analysis showed no impact of oral corticosteroids, azathioprine, or mycophenolate mofetil on the clearance of satralizumab [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category C]. There are no data on use in pregnant women. Animal studies showed no direct foetal harm but noted placental transfer and potential impairment of T-cell dependent antibody responses in infants. Use is not recommended unless potential benefits outweigh risks [Australian].
- **Contraception:** Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during and for 3 months post-treatment [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if satralizumab is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended unless the benefit to the mother outweighs the potential risk to the child [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No human data available. Animal studies showed no impact on reproductive organs or cycles [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children and adolescents <18 years have not been established [Australian]. (Note: Pivotal trials included a limited number of patients ≥12 years with consistent safety results).
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment is required in patients ≥65 years. Limited data exists for patients >74 years [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for mild impairment. Satralizumab has not been studied in moderate to severe renal impairment [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Safety and efficacy have not been studied in this population [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Satralizumab is a humanised IgG2 monoclonal antibody that binds to both soluble and membrane-bound human IL-6 receptors (IL-6R). This prevents IL-6 downstream signalling, which is implicated in NMOSD pathogenesis, including the production of AQP4 autoantibodies and Th17-cell activation [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Bioavailability:** 85.4%.
    - **Distribution:** Biphasic; central volume of distribution 3.46 L.
    - **Elimination:** Clearance is concentration-dependent. Half-life is approximately 30 days (range 22–37 days).
    - **Body Weight:** Clearance and volume increase with weight, though clinical efficacy remains comparable across weight groups; no weight-based dose adjustment is required [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Key Trials:** BN40898 (SAkuraSky) and BN40900 (SAkuraStar).
- **Design:** Randomized, multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials.
- **Outcomes:** 
    - **SAkuraSky (Combination with IST):** Showed a 62% reduction in the risk of adjudicated relapse compared to placebo plus IST.
    - **SAkuraStar (Monotherapy):** Showed a 55% reduction in the risk of adjudicated relapse compared to placebo.
    - **Subgroup Analysis:** Efficacy was significantly higher in AQP4-IgG seropositive patients, with risk reductions of 74–79% [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate). Do not freeze.
- **Protection:** Keep in the outer carton to protect from light and moisture.
- **Handling:** Do not shake. Let the PFS reach room temperature (30 minutes) before use.
- **Room Temperature Stability:** If necessary, unopened syringes may be kept at room temperature (up to 30°C) for a cumulative total of 8 days [Australian].
