# Editorial Product Information: saxagliptin + metformin

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **saxagliptin + metformin**
- Chemical class:
  - Saxagliptin: dipeptidyl peptidase‑4 (DPP‑4) inhibitor
  - Metformin: Not specified in available sources
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- As an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycaemic control in adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus when treatment with both saxagliptin and metformin is appropriate.
- Use as:
  - Initial combination therapy (recommendations described below)
  - Add‑on combination therapy to other antihyperglycaemic regimens (recommendations described below)
- Regional differences in indications: Not specified in available sources

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Maximum recommended daily dose: saxagliptin 5 mg; metformin extended‑release 2000 mg.
- Recommended usual dosing when combination tablet is used:
  - Add‑on combination therapy:
    - Saxagliptin 5 mg once daily.
    - Metformin extended‑release starting at 500 mg once daily, titrate as tolerated to a maximum of 2000 mg once daily.
    - Maximum combination tablet regimen: saxagliptin 5 mg / metformin XR 2000 mg daily (may be administered as two tablets of saxagliptin 2.5 mg / metformin XR 1000 mg once daily).
  - Initial combination therapy:
    - Start with saxagliptin 5 mg / metformin XR 500 mg once daily.
    - If glycaemic control inadequate, increase metformin component to saxagliptin 5 mg / metformin XR 1000 mg once daily, or use two tablets of saxagliptin 2.5 mg / metformin XR 1000 mg once daily as appropriate.

### Administration instructions
- Take with or after food.
- Generally administered once daily with the evening meal.
- Swallow tablets whole; do not crush, cut, or chew.
- In some patients, inactive tablet excipients may be visible in faeces as a soft hydrated mass — this can occur and does not necessarily indicate lack of absorption of active drug.

### Titration
- Gradual dose titration of the metformin component is recommended to reduce gastrointestinal adverse effects.
- When initiating in patients with moderate renal impairment or declining renal function, use lower starting doses as specified under Section 10 (Renal impairment).

### Missed dose
- Not specified in available sources

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to saxagliptin, metformin, or any excipients; or history of serious hypersensitivity reactions (including anaphylaxis and angioedema) to any DPP‑4 inhibitor.
- Metabolic acidosis, including lactic acidosis or diabetic ketoacidosis (acute or chronic), with or without coma.
- Diabetic pre‑coma.
- Severe renal impairment: estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) < 30 mL/min/1.73 m2.
- Acute conditions with potential to alter renal function, e.g. dehydration, severe infection, shock, or intravascular administration of iodinated contrast agents.
- Acute or chronic diseases that may cause tissue hypoxia, e.g. cardiac or respiratory failure, pulmonary embolism, recent myocardial infarction, shock, acute significant blood loss, sepsis, gangrene, pancreatitis.
- During or immediately following surgery where insulin is essential; elective major surgery.
- Hepatic impairment.
- Acute alcohol intoxication or alcoholism.
- Lactation (breastfeeding).

(Each item above is taken from available source material and must be observed when considering therapy.)

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed / prominent warnings
- **Life‑threatening lactic acidosis can occur due to accumulation of metformin.** The primary risk factor is renal impairment; other risk factors include advanced age associated with reduced renal function and high daily metformin doses above 2 g. If metabolic acidosis is suspected, discontinue therapy immediately and hospitalise for urgent management.

### General warnings and precautions
- Not indicated for type 1 diabetes mellitus.
- Not studied in combination with GLP‑1 receptor agonists (e.g. exenatide, liraglutide) — use with such agents: Not specified in available sources.
- Assess renal function prior to initiation and periodically thereafter; more frequent monitoring is required when eGFR approaches thresholds for dose adjustment or in elderly patients (see Monitoring below).
- Avoid initiation in patients with eGFR < 45 mL/min/1.73 m2 (see dosing adjustments). Contraindicated if eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m2.
- Avoid in patients with clinical or laboratory evidence of hepatic impairment because of association of hepatic dysfunction with metformin‑associated lactic acidosis.
- Temporarily discontinue at time of, or prior to, radiologic procedures involving intravascular iodinated contrast media and do not restart until 48 hours after the procedure and renal function confirmed stable.
- Temporarily suspend before any major surgical procedure (except minor procedures where oral intake is not restricted) and do not restart until oral intake and renal function are stable.
- Metformin may reduce vitamin B12 serum concentrations; monitor B12 when deficiency suspected (e.g. anaemia, peripheral neuropathy); periodic monitoring may be appropriate in at‑risk patients.
- Alcohol potentiates metformin’s effect on lactate metabolism — warn patients to avoid excessive alcohol.
- Caution when initiating or co‑prescribing medicinal products that may impair renal function (e.g. certain antihypertensives, diuretics, NSAIDs) — initiate with caution and monitor renal function.
- Use caution in patients with risk factors for heart failure hospitalization (see Cardiovascular safety below).
- Cases of severe and sometimes disabling arthralgia have been reported with DPP‑4 inhibitors; consider discontinuation if severe joint pain develops.
- Postmarketing reports of bullous pemphigoid have been associated with DPP‑4 inhibitors; if suspected, discontinue and refer to dermatology.
- Serious hypersensitivity reactions (including anaphylaxis and angioedema) have been reported with saxagliptin; if suspected, discontinue and evaluate.

### Monitoring requirements
- Renal function:
  - Assess before initiating therapy.
  - At least annually for most patients.
  - At least two to four times per year for patients whose eGFR is approaching 45 mL/min/1.73 m2 and for elderly patients.
  - Assess benefit/risk and consider dosage reduction or discontinuation if eGFR falls persistently below specified thresholds (see Section 10 Renal impairment dosing).
- Evaluate for signs/symptoms of lactic acidosis (e.g. unexpected vomiting, abdominal pain, hyperventilation, myalgia, malaise, somnolence, hypothermia), and for signs of pancreatitis (persistent severe abdominal pain).
- Monitor vitamin B12 levels if clinically indicated.
- Monitor for skin reactions (rash, blistering) and new or worsening heart failure symptoms (dyspnoea, oedema, rapid weight gain).

### Specific clinical precautions
- In patients with known mitochondrial disorders (e.g. MELAS, MIDD), metformin is not recommended because of risk of lactic acidosis and potential neurological deterioration; if suggestive signs occur after metformin exposure, withdraw metformin immediately and evaluate.
- Immunocompromised patients (e.g. post‑transplant, HIV) were not studied; safety and efficacy not established in these populations.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions (saxagliptin)
- Saxagliptin is primarily metabolised by CYP3A4/5 to an active metabolite.
  - CYP3A4/5 inhibitors may increase parent saxagliptin concentrations and decrease metabolite concentrations; CYP3A4/5 inducers may have opposite effects.
  - In vitro, saxagliptin and its major metabolite neither significantly inhibit nor induce major CYP enzymes assessed (CYP1A2, 2A6, 2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 2E1, 3A4) and are not significant P‑glycoprotein inhibitors or inducers.
  - Total exposure to active components (parent + metabolite) was not meaningfully altered by coadministration with metformin, glibenclamide, pioglitazone, digoxin, simvastatin, diltiazem, ketoconazole, rifampicin, omeprazole, antacids (aluminium hydroxide + magnesium hydroxide + simethicone), or famotidine in healthy volunteer studies.
  - Saxagliptin did not meaningfully alter the pharmacokinetics of metformin, glibenclamide, pioglitazone, digoxin, simvastatin, diltiazem, ketoconazole, or combined oral contraceptives in studied interactions.

### Pharmacokinetic interactions (metformin)
- Metformin is excreted unchanged by the kidney (renal tubular secretion). Cationic drugs that are eliminated by renal tubular secretion (e.g. amiloride, digoxin, morphine, procainamide, quinidine, quinine) may interact by competition for renal tubular secretion — specific clinical interaction details not provided in available sources.
- Intravascular iodinated contrast agents: can cause acute renal impairment and have been associated with lactic acidosis in patients receiving metformin; temporarily discontinue metformin at the time of the procedure and for at least 48 hours afterwards, and only resume after reassessment of renal function.
- Alcohol potentiates metformin’s effect on lactate metabolism — increased risk of lactic acidosis with excessive alcohol consumption.
- Initiate drug classes that can impair renal function (antihypertensives, diuretics, NSAIDs) with caution in patients receiving metformin.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Combination with insulin or insulin secretagogues (e.g. sulfonylureas): increased risk of hypoglycaemia — a reduction in dose of insulin or sulfonylurea may be required to reduce hypoglycaemia risk.

### Clinically significant interaction management notes
- Withhold metformin at time of procedures with iodinated contrast and reassess renal function before restarting.
- Monitor renal function when initiating or changing dose of potentially nephrotoxic drugs.
- Counsel patients to avoid excessive alcohol.
- Consider dose reduction of concomitant insulin or sulfonylurea to lower hypoglycaemia risk.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Safety and effectiveness have not been established in paediatric and adolescent patients. Use in paediatric patients: Not recommended / not established in available sources.

### Elderly use
- Saxagliptin and metformin are eliminated in part by the kidney; elderly patients are more likely to have decreased renal function. Use caution and individualise dosing based on renal function. Clinical studies did not demonstrate overall differences in safety or effectiveness by age group, but dosing must consider renal function.

### Renal impairment (dosing by eGFR)
- Assess renal function prior to initiation and periodically thereafter; do not initiate in patients with eGFR < 45 mL/min/1.73 m2 (see below).
- If eGFR falls persistently below 45 mL/min/1.73 m2 during treatment, reassess benefit‑risk and limit maximum dose to saxagliptin 2.5 mg / metformin XR 1000 mg once daily.
- Contraindicated in severe renal impairment (eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m2).

Dosage guidance by eGFR (as provided in available sources):
- eGFR 60–89 mL/min/1.73 m2:
  - Metformin XR: maximum daily dose 2000 mg; dose reduction may be considered with declining renal function.
  - Saxagliptin: maximum total daily dose 5 mg.
- eGFR 45–59 mL/min/1.73 m2:
  - Metformin XR: maximum daily dose 2000 mg; starting dose at most half of the maximum dose.
  - Saxagliptin: maximum total daily dose 5 mg.
- eGFR 30–44 mL/min/1.73 m2:
  - Metformin XR: maximum daily dose 1000 mg.
  - Saxagliptin: maximum total daily dose 2.5 mg.
- eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m2:
  - Metformin: contraindicated.
  - Saxagliptin: maximum total daily dose 2.5 mg (but combined product use contraindicated because of metformin).

### Hepatic impairment
- Avoid use in patients with clinical or laboratory evidence of hepatic impairment because of association with metformin‑related lactic acidosis.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Saxagliptin: inhibits dipeptidyl peptidase‑4 (DPP‑4), increasing incretin levels and thereby enhancing glucose‑dependent insulin secretion and suppressing glucagon release.
- Metformin: Mechanism not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Saxagliptin:
  - Metabolism primarily via CYP3A4/5 to an active metabolite.
  - In vitro data indicate low protein binding (below measurable levels) for saxagliptin and its major metabolite.
- Metformin:
  - Excreted by the kidney (renal elimination).
- Absorption, distribution, elimination parameters (e.g. Tmax, half‑life, bioavailability): Not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Not fully specified in available sources beyond DPP‑4 inhibition for saxagliptin and metformin‑associated risk of lactic acidosis.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Cardiovascular outcomes trial (SAVOR — Saxagliptin Assessment of Vascular Outcomes Recorded in Patients with Diabetes Mellitus — Thrombolysis in Myocardial Infarction):
  - Large randomized trial reporting a small increase in the rate of hospitalisation for heart failure in saxagliptin‑treated patients compared to placebo; a causal relationship has not been established.
  - Incidence of adjudicated pancreatitis events in the intent‑to‑treat population was 0.3% for both saxagliptin and placebo groups.
  - Participant demographics (as reported): of 16,492 patients randomised, 51.9% were aged ≥ 65 years and 14.1% were ≥ 75 years.
- Other clinical trial experience:
  - In six double‑blind, controlled clinical safety and efficacy studies of saxagliptin, 4,148 subjects were enrolled; subgroup analyses included elderly participants — no overall differences in safety or effectiveness observed between older and younger subjects. Specific efficacy and safety outcome measures beyond those described above are not detailed in available sources.
