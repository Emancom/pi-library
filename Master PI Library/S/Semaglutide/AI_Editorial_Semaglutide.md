# Editorial Product Information: Semaglutide

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of official Product Information (PI) for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial overview and not a substitute for the official PI or the local regulations governing prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this writing, only the Australian PI was provided for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Semaglutide (rys), a human glucagon-like-peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonist produced by recombinant DNA technology in *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*.
- **Chemical Class:** GLP-1 analogue.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Ozempic** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A10BJ06.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):**
    - Solution for injection in a pre-filled multidose disposable pen.
    - 0.25 mg or 0.5 mg/dose (1.5 mL or 3 mL pen).
    - 1 mg/dose (3 mL pen).
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) injection only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Novo Nordisk [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM):** For the treatment of adults with insufficiently controlled T2DM as an adjunct to diet and exercise:
    - As monotherapy when metformin is contraindicated or not tolerated.
    - As add-on therapy to other medicinal products for T2DM.
- **Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) and T2DM:** As an adjunct to standard of care to reduce the risk of sustained decline in kidney function and cardiovascular death in adults with both T2DM and CKD [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):**
    - **Starting Dose:** 0.25 mg once weekly for 4 weeks. (Note: This is not a maintenance dose).
    - **Escalation:** After 4 weeks, increase to 0.5 mg once weekly.
    - **Maintenance:** If further glycaemic control is required after at least 4 weeks at 0.5 mg, the dose may be increased to 1 mg once weekly.
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Administer once weekly on the same day each week, with or without meals, at any time of day.
    - Inject subcutaneously into the abdomen, thigh, or upper arm.
    - Do **not** administer intravenously or intramuscularly.
- **Titration with Combination Therapy:** 
    - When added to metformin or thiazolidinediones, existing doses can remain unchanged.
    - When added to sulfonylureas or insulin, consider a dose reduction of the secretagogue or insulin to mitigate hypoglycaemia risk.
- **Missed Dose:** 
    - If within 5 days of the missed dose, administer as soon as possible.
    - If more than 5 days have passed, skip the missed dose and resume the next dose on the regularly scheduled day.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to semaglutide or any of the excipients (dibasic sodium phosphate dihydrate, propylene glycol, phenol, hydrochloric acid, sodium hydroxide).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **General Restrictions:** Not for use in Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus or for the treatment of diabetic ketoacidosis. Not a substitute for insulin.
- **Aspiration Risk:** Cases of pulmonary aspiration have been reported in patients on GLP-1 RAs undergoing general anaesthesia or deep sedation due to delayed gastric emptying.
- **Gastrointestinal (GI) Effects:** Common GI reactions (nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea) may lead to dehydration, potentially causing acute kidney injury. Caution in patients with impaired renal function or gastroparesis.
- **Acute Pancreatitis:** Observe for characteristic symptoms (persistent severe abdominal pain). Discontinue permanently if pancreatitis is confirmed. Caution in patients with a history of pancreatitis.
- **Diabetic Retinopathy:** Rapid improvement in glucose control may be associated with temporary worsening of diabetic retinopathy. Monitor patients with a history of this condition.
- **Psychiatric Disorders:** Monitor for suicidal ideation, behaviour, or new-onset depression.
- **NAION:** Some epidemiological studies suggest an increased risk of Non-arteritic Anterior Ischaemic Optic Neuropathy (NAION) with semaglutide. Patients should be advised to seek urgent care for sudden vision loss.
- **Congestive Heart Failure:** Use is not recommended in NYHA class IV patients due to lack of therapeutic experience.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most frequent reactions are gastrointestinal.

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Nausea, Diarrhoea, Hypoglycaemia (when used with insulin/sulfonylurea) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Vomiting, Abdominal pain/distension, Constipation, Dyspepsia, Gastritis, GERD, Eructation, Flatulence, Cholelithiasis, Dizziness, Fatigue, Headache, Decreased appetite, Diabetic retinopathy complications, Increased Lipase/Amylase |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Dysgeusia, Increased heart rate, Acute pancreatitis, Delayed gastric emptying, Injection site reactions |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Anaphylactic reaction |

- **Post-Market Reports:** Acute kidney injury, urolithiasis, alopecia, angioedema, intestinal obstruction, hiccups, dysaesthesia, cholecystitis, and NAION.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Gastric Emptying Delay:** Semaglutide may influence the absorption of oral medications requiring rapid GI absorption.
- **Warfarin:** Cases of decreased INR have been reported; frequent monitoring of INR is recommended when initiating semaglutide in patients on warfarin or coumarin derivatives.
- **Clinical Trials:** No clinically relevant interactions were observed with metformin, atorvastatin, digoxin, or oral contraceptives (ethinylestradiol/levonorgestrel).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category D [Australian]:** Must not be used during pregnancy. 
    - Discontinue at least 2 months before a planned pregnancy due to the long half-life of semaglutide. 
    - Animal studies showed reproductive toxicity, including embryofetal lethality and abnormalities at subclinical or clinical exposures.
- **Breastfeeding:** Excreted in the milk of lactating rats. Use is not recommended during breastfeeding as a risk to the child cannot be excluded.
- **Fertility:** Effect on human fertility is unknown; animal studies showed no effect on male fertility but slight reductions in ovulations in female rats at doses causing maternal weight loss.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children and adolescents under 18 years have not been established.
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required based on age. Limited experience in patients ≥75 years.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild, moderate, or severe impairment. Experience in end-stage kidney disease (ESKD) is limited.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment required. Caution is advised in severe hepatic impairment due to limited data.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A GLP-1 analogue with 94% sequence homology to human GLP-1. It acts as a receptor agonist, increasing glucose-dependent insulin secretion and decreasing glucagon secretion. It also delays gastric emptying and reduces appetite/energy intake.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Reduces fasting and postprandial glucose; lowers body weight (predominantly fat mass); reduces systolic blood pressure and inflammation markers.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability 89%; $T_{max}$ 1–3 days.
    - **Distribution:** Extensively bound to plasma albumin (>99%).
    - **Metabolism:** Proteolytic cleavage of the peptide backbone and beta-oxidation of the fatty acid sidechain.
    - **Elimination:** Primary excretion via urine and faeces. Half-life is approximately 1 week.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **SUSTAIN 1-7:** Demonstrated statistically significant reductions in $HbA_{1c}$ and body weight compared to placebo, sitagliptin, exenatide ER, insulin glargine, and dulaglutide.
- **SUSTAIN 6 (CV Outcomes):** 2-year trial showing a 26% risk reduction in MACE (CV death, non-fatal MI, or non-fatal stroke) in high-risk T2DM patients.
- **FLOW (Kidney Outcomes):** Multi-centre trial in T2DM and CKD patients. Semaglutide 1 mg showed a 24% relative risk reduction in a composite kidney outcome (including persistent $\geq 50\%$ eGFR reduction, ESKD, or renal/CV death).
- **Japanese Trials:** Confirmed efficacy and safety in Japanese populations as monotherapy and add-on therapy.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Before Use:** Store in a refrigerator ($2^{\circ}C$ to $8^{\circ}C$). Keep away from cooling elements. Do not freeze.
- **After First Use (In-use):** Store for up to 42 days (6 weeks) either below $30^{\circ}C$ or in a refrigerator ($2^{\circ}C$ to $8^{\circ}C$). 
- **Handling:** Keep the pen cap on to protect from light. Remove the needle after every injection to prevent blockages and contamination.
