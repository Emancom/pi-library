# Editorial Product Information: Sulfasalazine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority:
    - **Australian** (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved PI for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Sulfasalazine (a sulfonamide derivative).
- **Chemical Name:** 2-Hydroxy-5-[2-[4-(pyridin-2-ylsulphamoyl)phenyl]diazenyl]benzoic acid.
- **Brand Names:** Pyralin EN [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A07EC01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 500 mg enteric-coated tablets [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Ulcerative Colitis:** As an adjunct in the treatment of active disease and for reducing the frequency of relapses in maintenance therapy [Australian].
- **Crohn's Disease:** Treatment of active Crohn’s disease, particularly in cases with colonic involvement [Australian].
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis:** Indicated for patients who have failed to respond adequately to non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD)
**Adults:**
- **Initial Dosage:** 1 to 2 g four times daily.
- **Maintenance Dosage:** 2 g daily administered in four divided doses.

**Paediatric (Children):**
- **Initial Dosage:** 40 to 60 mg/kg body weight daily, divided into three to six doses.
- **Maintenance Dosage:** 40 mg/kg body weight daily in four divided doses.

### Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA)
**Adults:**
- **Standard Dose:** 2 to 3 g daily, typically given as two 500 mg tablets two or three times a day.
- **Titration Schedule:** To improve tolerance, a gradual increase is recommended:
    - **Week 1:** 1 tablet (500 mg) in the evening.
    - **Week 2:** 1 tablet in the morning, 1 tablet in the evening.
    - **Week 3:** 1 tablet in the morning, 2 tablets in the evening.
    - **Week 4:** 2 tablets in the morning, 2 tablets in the evening (maintenance).

**Paediatric:**
- No dosage recommendation is currently provided for RA in children [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets should be taken after meals.
- Doses should be evenly spaced over 24 hours (overnight interval should not exceed 8 hours).
- Enteric-coated tablets must not be crushed or broken.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to **sulfasalazine**, its metabolites, sulfonamides, or salicylates.
- Haematological, renal, or hepatic dysfunction [Australian].
- History of allergic drug fever or skin eruptions due to sulfonamides, oral hypoglycaemics, or thiazides [Australian].
- Intestinal or urinary obstruction.
- Porphyria (sulfonamides may precipitate an acute attack).
- Children aged 2 years and younger.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Serious Infections and Toxicity
- **Serious Infections:** Myelosuppression-related sepsis and pneumonia have been reported. Discontinue if serious infection occurs.
- **Fatalities:** Deaths have occurred due to hypersensitivity, agranulocytosis, aplastic anaemia, renal/liver damage, and fibrosing alveolitis.
- **Severe Skin Reactions:** Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN) have been reported, typically within the first month of therapy. Discontinue at the first sign of rash or mucosal lesions.
- **DRESS:** Drug Rash with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS) may occur; early signs like fever or lymphadenopathy require immediate evaluation.

### Monitoring Requirements [Australian]
- **Blood and Liver:** Complete blood counts (CBC) and liver function tests (LFTs) should be performed:
    - Every 2 weeks for the first 3 months.
    - Monthly for the subsequent 3 months.
    - Every 3 months thereafter, or as clinically indicated.
- **Renal:** Assessment of renal function (including urinalysis) should be performed initially and monthly for the first 3 months.

### Specific Precautions
- **G-6-PD Deficiency:** Patients are at high risk for haemolytic anaemia and must be closely observed.
- **Asthma/Atopy:** Use with caution in patients with severe allergy or bronchial asthma.
- **Fluid Intake:** Ensure adequate hydration to prevent crystalluria and stone formation.
- **Laboratory Interference:** May cause false-positives for urinary normetanephrine and interfere with UV absorbance assays (e.g., ALT, AST, glucose).

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common and Dose-Related Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, anorexia, and gastric distress (more frequent with non-enteric coated forms).
- **CNS:** Headache, dizziness.
- **Reproductive:** Reversible oligospermia and male infertility (reverses 2–3 months after cessation).
- **General:** Raised temperature, erythema, pruritus.

### Serious Adverse Effects (Frequency Not Specified)
- **Haematological:** Agranulocytosis, aplastic anaemia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, megaloblastic anaemia, haemolytic anaemia.
- **Hepatobiliary:** Hepatic failure, fulminant hepatitis, jaundice.
- **Renal:** Nephrotic syndrome, interstitial nephritis, crystalluria, haematuria.
- **Respiratory:** Fibrosing alveolitis, eosinophilic infiltration.
- **Cardiovascular:** Myocarditis, pericarditis.
- **Neurological:** Peripheral neuropathy, aseptic meningitis, transverse myelitis, encephalopathy.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Methotrexate/Hypoglycaemics/Anticoagulants:** Sulfonamides may displace these from binding sites, potentiating their effects.
- **Digoxin:** Reduced absorption and non-therapeutic serum levels of digoxin.
- **Folic Acid:** Sulfasalazine inhibits folic acid absorption; deficiency may occur.
- **Thiopurines (Azathioprine/6-MP):** Inhibition of TPMT may lead to bone marrow suppression and leukopenia.
- **Antacids:** May decrease the absorption of sulfasalazine.
- **Ferrous Sulphate:** May impair sulfasalazine absorption (clinical significance is doubtful).
- **Urinary Acidifiers/Indomethacin/Salicylates:** May increase sulfonamide blood levels.
- **PABA Derivatives (Local Anaesthetics):** May antagonise sulfonamide activity.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category A [Australian]**: Used by a large number of pregnant women without proven increase in malformation frequency.
    - Folic acid absorption is inhibited; supplementation is essential.
    - Use only if clearly needed; potential for neural tube defects has been suggested but not established.
- **Breastfeeding:** Sulfasalazine concentrations in milk are negligible, but sulfapyridine levels are ~40% of maternal serum. Caution is advised, especially with premature or G-6-PD deficient infants. Reports of bloody stools or diarrhoea in infants exist.
- **Fertility:** Causes reversible male infertility (oligospermia).

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric:** 
    - Contraindicated in children $\le$ 2 years of age.
    - Not recommended in children with systemic-onset juvenile RA (risk of serum sickness-like reactions) [Australian].
- **Elderly:** No specific data available in provided sources.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in cases of dysfunction. Risk of serious toxicity in renal impairment.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Likely acts through immunosuppressive and anti-inflammatory effects. In IBD, it is cleaved by colonic bacteria into **sulfapyridine** (likely responsible for many side effects) and **5-aminosalicylic acid (5-ASA)** (the active anti-inflammatory moiety in the colon).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** ~30% absorbed from the small intestine.
    - **Metabolism:** Cleaved by azo-reductase in the colon. Sulfapyridine is absorbed and acetylated in the liver.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily via urine as metabolites; unabsorbed drug and 5-ASA metabolites are excreted in faeces.
- **Pharmacogenetics:** The rate of sulfapyridine acetylation is genetically determined. "Slow acetylators" are at higher risk of adverse reactions.
