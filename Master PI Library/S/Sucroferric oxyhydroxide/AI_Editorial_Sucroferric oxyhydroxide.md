# Editorial Product Information: Sucroferric oxyhydroxide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. At the time of writing, only the Australian source was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Sucroferric oxyhydroxide (a mixture of polynuclear iron(III)-oxyhydroxide, sucrose, and starches).
- **Chemical Class:** Phosphate binder.
- **Brand Name:** **Velphoro** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** V03AE05.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Chewable tablets containing 500 mg of iron (equivalent to 2,500 mg of sucroferric oxyhydroxide) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Seqirus Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Control of serum phosphorus levels:** Indicated for use in adult patients with chronic kidney disease (CKD) who are on dialysis [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Starting Dose:** 1,500 mg iron (3 tablets) per day, divided across the day’s meals [Australian].
- **Titration:** Monitor serum phosphorus levels and adjust the daily dose in increments or decrements of 500 mg iron (1 tablet) every 2–4 weeks until optimal levels are achieved [Australian].
- **Maintenance Dose:** Most patients achieve control at doses between 1,500 mg and 2,000 mg iron per day (3 to 4 tablets) [Australian].
- **Maximum Dose:** 3,000 mg iron (6 tablets) per day [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets **must** be taken with meals to maximise the adsorption of dietary phosphate [Australian].
- Tablets **must** be chewed or crushed; they must not be swallowed whole [Australian].
- No additional fluid intake beyond the patient’s normal requirement is necessary for administration [Australian].

### Missed Dose
- If a dose is missed, the patient should resume the normal dose with the next meal. Doubling doses is not recommended [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to sucroferric oxyhydroxide or any excipient in the formulation (including woodberry flavour, neohesperidin-dihydrochalcone, magnesium stearate, and silica) [Australian].
- **Haemochromatosis** or any other iron accumulation disorders [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Gastrointestinal Disorders:** Patients with a recent history (within 3 months) of peritonitis, major GI surgery, or significant gastric/hepatic disorders were excluded from clinical trials. Use in these populations requires careful benefit/risk assessment [Australian].
- **Stool Discolouration:** **Velphoro** frequently causes black (discoloured) stools. While this is expected for iron-based compounds, it may visually mask gastrointestinal bleeding. It does **not** interfere with guaiac-based (e.g., Hemoccult) or immunological-based faecal occult blood tests [Australian].
- **Carbohydrate Content:** Each tablet contains approximately 1.4 g of carbohydrates (sucrose and starches). Use with caution in patients with rare hereditary fructose intolerance, glucose-galactose malabsorption, or sucrase-isomaltase insufficiency [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are primarily gastrointestinal in nature and often occur early in treatment.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Diarrhoea (11.6%), Faeces discoloured (15%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Nausea, constipation, vomiting, dyspepsia, abdominal pain, flatulence, product taste abnormal, tooth discolouration |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Abdominal distension, gastritis, dysphagia, GERD, tongue discolouration, hypercalcaemia, hypocalcaemia, fatigue, pruritus, rash, headache, dyspnoea |

- **Diarrhoea:** Most episodes are mild, transient, and occur during the initiation of therapy. Discontinuation due to diarrhoea occurred in only 3.1% of trial participants [Australian].
- **Tooth/Tongue Discolouration:** These effects are generally temporary and associated with the chewable nature of the tablet [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Sucroferric oxyhydroxide** is practically insoluble; however, it may adsorb other drugs in the GI tract.
- **Separated Dosing (1 hour before or 2 hours after Velphoro):**
  - Alendronate
  - Doxycycline
  - Levothyroxine
  - Any medicinal product known to interact with iron [Australian].
- **No Clinically Significant Interaction Observed:**
  - Losartan, furosemide, digoxin, warfarin, omeprazole.
  - HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors (e.g., atorvastatin, simvastatin).
  - Oral Vitamin D analogues (though in vitro adsorption occurs, clinical iPTH lowering effects remain unchanged) [Australian].
- **Not Investigated:** Concomitant use with oral iron therapies, other phosphate binders, or antacids containing aluminium, magnesium, or calcium has not been studied [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. There are no clinical data in pregnant women. Animal studies showed reduced fetal weight and delayed ossification at 5 times the clinical dose (maternotoxic levels). Use only if clearly needed after risk/benefit assessment [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Iron absorption from this product is minimal; excretion into breast milk is unlikely. Decisions regarding breastfeeding should weigh the benefits of nursing against the maternal clinical need for the drug [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No human data. Animal studies in rats showed no adverse effects on fertility or mating performance at doses up to 20 times the maximum clinical human dose [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children under 18 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly (≥65 years):** No specific dosage adjustments are required. Clinical trials included significant numbers of elderly patients without identifying unique safety concerns [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Indicated specifically for dialysis patients. No data are available for patients in earlier stages of CKD [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Patients with severe impairment were generally excluded from trials. However, no evidence of hepatic enzyme alteration or toxicity was observed in the studied populations [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Sucroferric oxyhydroxide contains polynuclear iron(III)-oxyhydroxide. Phosphate binding occurs via ligand exchange between hydroxyl groups/water and phosphate ions within the gastrointestinal tract. This reduces dietary phosphate absorption and subsequently lowers serum phosphorus levels [Australian].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** The active pn-FeOOH moiety is insoluble and not absorbed. Mononuclear iron species can be released and absorbed; however, systemic iron uptake is minimal (0.04% in CKD patients) [Australian].
- **Metabolism:** The active moiety is not metabolised. Sucrose and starch components are digested into glucose, fructose, and maltose [Australian].
- **Elimination:** Unbound iron is recovered in the faeces. It is not excreted via the urine [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Phase 3 Study (N=1,055):** An open-label, active-controlled study compared **Velphoro** to sevelamer carbonate in CKD patients on dialysis. **Velphoro** demonstrated non-inferiority in phosphorus lowering at week 12.
- **Dose Response:** Doses of 1,000 mg to 2,500 mg iron/day were effective, while a 250 mg dose was ineffective.
- **VERIFIE Study:** A prospective, non-interventional post-authorisation safety study (N=1,365) confirmed that effectiveness in real-world settings (including concomitant use with other phosphate binders in 45% of patients) was consistent with Phase 3 results [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Moisture Protection:** Store in the original package [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** 90 days after first opening the bottle [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4** (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
- **Regulatory Status:** First approved in Australia on 27 November 2014.
