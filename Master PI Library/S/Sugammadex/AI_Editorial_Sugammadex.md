# Editorial Product Information: Sugammadex

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of sugammadex for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, dosing protocols, and safety information into a concise reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Sugammadex sodium (a modified gamma cyclodextrin).
- **Chemical Class:** Selective Relaxant Binding Agent (SRBA).
- **Brand Name(s):** BRIMADEX [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** V03AB35.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Solution for injection; 200 mg/2 mL and 500 mg/5 mL (100 mg/mL) in single-use vials [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) bolus injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pharmacor Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- Reversal of neuromuscular blockade (NMB) induced by the aminosteroid agents **rocuronium** or **vecuronium** in adults and children aged 2 years and older [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
The dose is determined by the depth of neuromuscular blockade to be reversed, monitored via neuromuscular monitoring (e.g., Train-of-Four [TOF] or Post-Tetanic Count [PTC]).

- **Routine Reversal:**
    - **4.0 mg/kg:** Recommended when recovery has reached 1–2 PTC following rocuronium or vecuronium blockade.
    - **2.0 mg/kg:** Recommended if spontaneous recovery has occurred up to the reappearance of the second twitch (T2) in a TOF stimulation.
- **Immediate Reversal (Rocuronium Only):**
    - **16.0 mg/kg:** Administered 3 minutes after a bolus dose of 1.2 mg/kg rocuronium bromide. There are no data to support immediate reversal of vecuronium-induced blockade [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Administer as a single IV bolus injection rapidly (within 10 seconds) into an existing IV line.
- **Compatibility:** Compatible with 0.9% sodium chloride, 5% glucose, Ringers lactate, and other standard infusions.
- **Incompatibility:** Physically incompatible with verapamil, ondansetron, and ranitidine.

### Re-administration of NMBA after Sugammadex
If NMB is required again after reversal:
- **Normal Renal Function:** Wait 5 minutes for 1.2 mg/kg rocuronium or 4 hours for 0.6 mg/kg rocuronium or 0.1 mg/kg vecuronium.
- **Mild/Moderate Renal Impairment:** Wait 24 hours. If required sooner, use 1.2 mg/kg rocuronium.
- **Post-Immediate Reversal (16 mg/kg):** A 24-hour waiting time is suggested.
- If NMB is needed before the waiting period ends, a non-steroidal NMBA (e.g., benzylisoquinolinium) should be used.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to the active substance (sugammadex) or any of the excipients (hydrochloric acid, sodium hydroxide, water for injections).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Monitoring Respiratory Function:** Mandatory until adequate spontaneous respiration is restored. Even after full NMB reversal, other perioperative drugs may depress respiratory function.
- **Recurrence of Blockade:** Observed in 0.2% of cases in clinical trials, often associated with inadequate dosing.
- **Haemostasis:** Transient prolongations of aPTT and PT(INR) (up to 22%) have been observed. While clinically significant bleeding is rare, coagulation parameters should be monitored in patients with known coagulopathies or those on high-dose anticoagulants receiving the 16 mg/kg dose [Australian].
- **Marked Bradycardia:** Rare cases of marked bradycardia and cardiac arrest have occurred within minutes of administration. Monitor haemodynamics closely; treat with anti-cholinergics (e.g., atropine) if required.
- **Drug Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis has been reported (frequency uncommon: 1/1000 to 1/100). Clinicians should be prepared for immediate treatment of systemic reactions.
- **Light Anaesthesia:** Signs of light anaesthesia (movement, coughing) may occur when NMB is reversed mid-procedure.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect profiles are based on pooled Phase 1-3 trials.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Dysgeusia (metal/bitter taste; mostly in healthy volunteers), Nausea. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Procedural pain, vomiting, headache, cough, airway complication of anaesthesia, anaesthetic complication (signs of light anaesthesia), procedural hypotension. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Drug hypersensitivity/Anaphylaxis. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Marked bradycardia, cardiac arrest. |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Recurrence of neuromuscular blockade, anaphylaxis, and marked bradycardia/cardiac arrest.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Toremifene:** May displace rocuronium or vecuronium from the sugammadex complex, potentially delaying recovery [Australian].
- **Fusidic Acid (IV):** Pre-operative IV use may delay recovery of the T4/T1 ratio.
- **Hormonal Contraceptives:** Sugammadex binds to progestogens. A bolus dose of sugammadex is equivalent to one missed daily dose of an oral contraceptive.
    - **Management:** Refer to the "missed dose" instructions in the contraceptive's PI. For non-oral contraceptives, an additional non-hormonal method must be used for the next 7 days [Australian].

### Laboratory Interference
- May interfere with serum progesterone assays at plasma concentrations of 100 µg/mL (typically seen after a 16 mg/kg dose).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B2** [Australian]. No clinical data available. Animal studies show no effect on foetal development at human-equivalent doses, though maternotoxic doses in rabbits showed skeletal ossification issues. Use only if benefits outweigh risks.
- **Breastfeeding:** Unknown if excreted in human milk (excreted in rat milk). Exercise caution.
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility in rat studies.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for children $\ge$ 2 years for routine reversal. Immediate reversal efficacy/safety not assessed in this population. Vials may be diluted to 10 mg/mL for dosing accuracy. Not recommended for neonates or infants < 2 years [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Recovery times may be slightly longer (median 3.6 mins vs 2.2 mins in younger adults), but the same weight-based dosing is recommended.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Mild to Moderate (CrCl 30–80 mL/min):** No dose adjustment required.
    - **Severe (including dialysis):** **Not recommended** due to lack of safety data and prolonged elimination.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment required as the drug is primarily renally excreted. Exercise caution if impairment is accompanied by coagulopathy.
- **Obese Patients:** Dosing should be based on **Actual Body Weight**.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A modified gamma cyclodextrin that acts as a "guest-host" encapsulator. It forms a high-affinity complex with rocuronium or vecuronium in the plasma, reducing the concentration of the NMBA available to bind to nicotinic receptors at the neuromuscular junction.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Steady-state volume of distribution is ~11–14 L. No binding to plasma proteins.
    - **Metabolism:** None observed.
    - **Elimination:** Excreted unchanged via the kidneys (>95%). Elimination half-life ($t_{1/2}$) is approximately 2 hours in adults with normal renal function.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** Compared to neostigmine, sugammadex provided significantly faster reversal.
    - **At 1–2 PTC:** Sugammadex (4 mg/kg) reversed rocuronium in ~2.9 mins vs ~50 mins for neostigmine.
    - **At T2 Reappearance:** Sugammadex (2 mg/kg) reversed rocuronium in ~1.5 mins vs ~18 mins for neostigmine.
- **Immediate Reversal:** Sugammadex 16 mg/kg reversed 1.2 mg/kg rocuronium (at 3 mins) with a median recovery time of 1.5 minutes, which was faster than spontaneous recovery from 1.0 mg/kg suxamethonium (7 mins) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C. 
- Do not freeze. 
- Keep vials in the outer carton to protect from light.
- **Shelf Life:** 24 months. Once diluted, the product is stable for 24 hours at 2–8°C or 6 hours at room temperature, though immediate use is recommended [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only.
