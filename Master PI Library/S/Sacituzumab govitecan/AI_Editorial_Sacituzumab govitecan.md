# Editorial Product Information: Sacituzumab govitecan

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based solely on the provided Product Information from the **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current official PI from their respective regulatory authority for the most up-to-date guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Sacituzumab govitecan, a Trop-2-directed antibody-drug conjugate (ADC).
- **Chemical Class:** Antineoplastic monoclonal antibody-drug conjugate.
- **Brand Name:** **TRODELVY** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Lyophilised powder for injection; 180 mg single-dose vial [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Gilead Sciences Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metastatic Triple-Negative Breast Cancer (mTNBC):** Treatment of adult patients with unresectable locally advanced or metastatic triple-negative breast cancer who have received at least two prior systemic therapies, including at least one for metastatic disease [Australian].
- **HR+/HER2- Metastatic Breast Cancer:** Treatment of adult patients with unresectable locally advanced or metastatic hormone receptor (HR)-positive, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative breast cancer (IHC 0, IHC 1+, or IHC 2+/ISH–) who have received endocrine-based therapy (including a CDK4/6 inhibitor) and at least two additional systemic therapies in the metastatic setting [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** 10 mg/kg administered via IV infusion on Days 1 and 8 of 21-day cycles [Australian].
- **Administration Restrictions:** Do NOT administer as an IV push or bolus. Do NOT substitute for or use with other drugs containing irinotecan or SN-38 [Australian].
- **Duration:** Continue until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.
- **Pre-medication (Required):**
    - **Infusion Reactions:** Antipyretics, H1 and H2 blockers; corticosteroids may be used for patients with prior reactions.
    - **CINV Prophylaxis:** Two or three-drug antiemetic combination (e.g., dexamethasone with a 5-HT3 antagonist or NK1 receptor antagonist) [Australian].
- **Preparation:**
    - **Reconstitution:** Reconstitute with 20 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection to a concentration of 10 mg/mL. Gently swirl for up to 15 minutes; do not shake.
    - **Dilution:** Dilute the reconstituted solution in 0.9% Sodium Chloride to a final concentration of 1.1 mg/mL to 3.4 mg/mL [Australian].
- **Infusion Rate:**
    - **First Dose:** 3-hour infusion.
    - **Subsequent Doses:** 1 to 2-hour infusion if prior doses were tolerated.
    - **Observation:** Monitor for at least 30 minutes post-infusion [Australian].
- **Dose Modifications:** Dose reductions (25% or 50%) or permanent discontinuation are required for severe neutropenia or non-haematological toxicities (Grade 3-4). Once reduced, the dose must not be re-escalated [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Severe Hypersensitivity:** History of severe hypersensitivity reactions to **TRODELVY** [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Neutropenia (Boxed Warning/Severe Warning):** Can cause severe or life-threatening neutropenia. Fatal infections have occurred. Withhold if ANC <1500/mm³ on Day 1 or <1000/mm³ on Day 8. G-CSF is recommended for primary prophylaxis in high-risk patients [Australian].
- **Diarrhoea:** Severe diarrhoea may occur. Initiate loperamide (4 mg initially, then 2 mg per episode, max 16 mg/day) at the first onset of late diarrhoea. Withhold for Grade 3-4 diarrhoea [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity and Infusion-Related Reactions:** Severe and life-threatening reactions (including anaphylaxis) have been reported. Emergency equipment must be available [Australian].
- **Nausea and Vomiting:** **TRODELVY** is emetogenic; appropriate antiemetic premedication is mandatory [Australian].
- **Reduced UGT1A1 Activity:** Patients homozygous for the UGT1A1*28 allele (e.g., Gilbert’s syndrome) are at increased risk for severe neutropenia, febrile neutropenia, and anaemia. Close monitoring is required [Australian].
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Contains a genotoxic component (SN-38). Can cause fetal harm [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following frequencies are based on pooled data (n=688) [Australian].

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Neutropenia (64–71%), Nausea (59–62%), Diarrhoea (62–65%), Fatigue (60–65%), Alopecia (47–48%), Anaemia (37–40%), Constipation (23–37%), Vomiting (24–33%), Decreased appetite (21–28%), Abdominal pain (21–27%), Cough (12–24%), Rash/Pruritus (10–12%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Febrile neutropenia (6%), Urinary tract infection, Upper respiratory tract infection, Stomatitis, Dyspepsia, Headache, Dizziness, Insomnia, Arthralgia, Back pain |
| **Serious/Fatal** | Febrile neutropenia, neutropenic colitis (1%), respiratory failure (0.4%), sepsis, and pulmonary embolism [Australian] |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No formal drug-drug interaction studies have been conducted.
- **UGT1A1 Inhibitors:** Concomitant use (e.g., propofol, ketoconazole) may increase SN-38 exposure and toxicity; avoid where possible [Australian].
- **UGT1A1 Inducers:** Concomitant use (e.g., phenytoin, carbamazepine, rifampicin) may substantially reduce SN-38 exposure; avoid where possible [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D [Australian]**. Based on mechanism of action, it can cause teratogenicity and/or embryo-fetal lethality. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment.
- **Contraception:**
    - **Females:** Effective contraception during and for 6 months after the final dose.
    - **Males:** Effective contraception during and for 3 months after the final dose [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for 1 month after the final dose due to potential serious adverse reactions in the infant [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies indicate potential impairment of female fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required for patients ≥65 years. However, in the HR+/HER2- trial, higher discontinuation rates due to adverse events were noted in patients ≥65 years (14%) compared to younger patients (3%) [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No adjustment for mild or moderate impairment. Not studied in severe renal impairment or end-stage renal disease (CrCl <15 mL/min) [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for mild impairment. Not studied/no recommendation for moderate or severe hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **TRODELVY** is an ADC. The antibody binds to Trop-2, a cell-surface antigen overexpressed in various cancers. Upon internalisation, the linker is hydrolysed, releasing SN-38 (a topoisomerase I inhibitor). SN-38 induces double-strand DNA breaks, leading to apoptosis [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Central volume of distribution is 3.58 L.
    - **Metabolism:** SN-38 is metabolised via UGT1A1.
    - **Elimination:** Median half-life is 23.4 hours for the ADC and 17.6 hours for free SN-38. Clearance is approximately 0.128 L/h [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** A positive exposure-response relationship exists between SN-38 concentration and QTc interval increase (mean change 9.7 msec) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ASCENT Study (mTNBC):** A Phase 3 trial (n=529) demonstrated superior median Progression-Free Survival (PFS) for **TRODELVY** (4.8 months) vs physician's choice chemotherapy (1.7 months; HR 0.43). Median Overall Survival (OS) was 11.8 months vs 6.9 months [Australian].
- **TROPiCS-02 Study (HR+/HER2-):** A Phase 3 trial (n=543) showed improved median PFS (5.5 months vs 4.0 months; HR 0.66) and median OS (14.4 months vs 11.2 months; HR 0.79) compared to single-agent chemotherapy [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Refrigerate at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze. Store in the original carton to protect from light [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:**
    - **Unopened:** Consult ARTG/packaging for expiry.
    - **Reconstituted/Diluted:** Use immediately. If not used, the diluted solution in the infusion bag can be refrigerated (2°C to 8°C) for up to 24 hours. After refrigeration, administration must be completed within 8 hours (including infusion time) at room temperature (up to 25°C) [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian]**.
- **Additional Monitoring:** Subject to additional monitoring (Black Triangle Scheme) in Australia to allow quick identification of new safety information [Australian].
