# Editorial Product Information: Sitagliptin and Metformin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis was developed using official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of the fixed-dose combination of sitagliptin and metformin for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Note:** This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Sitagliptin hydrochloride monohydrate (DPP-4 inhibitor) and Metformin hydrochloride (Biguanide).
- **Brand Names:** Blooms The Chemist Sitagliptin/Metformin; Blooms The Chemist Sitagliptin/Metformin XR [Australian].
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths [Australian]:**
    - **Immediate-Release (IR) Tablets:** 50 mg sitagliptin/500 mg metformin; 50 mg/850 mg; 50 mg/1000 mg.
    - **Modified-Release (XR) Tablets:** 50 mg sitagliptin/1000 mg metformin; 100 mg/1000 mg.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Sponsor/Manufacturer:** Sandoz Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus:** Indicated as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycemic control in adults when treatment with both sitagliptin and metformin is clinically appropriate [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** Dosage must be individualized based on the patient’s current regimen, effectiveness, and tolerability.
- **Maximum Daily Dose:** 100 mg sitagliptin and 2000 mg metformin [Australian].
- **Immediate-Release (IR):** Usually administered twice daily with meals. Dose escalation should be gradual to minimize gastrointestinal (GI) side effects associated with metformin.
- **Modified-Release (XR):** Administered once daily with the evening meal. 
    - **Administration Instruction:** XR tablets must be swallowed whole; they must not be split, crushed, broken, or chewed to preserve modified-release properties.
- **Initial Therapy:** For patients inadequately controlled on diet and exercise alone, the recommended starting dose is 100 mg sitagliptin and 1000 mg metformin daily.
- **Titration:** Metformin may be titrated up to 2000 mg daily if needed.
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as the patient remembers, unless it is nearly time for the next dose, in which case the missed dose should be skipped.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Renal Impairment:** Severe renal dysfunction, defined as an eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m² [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to sitagliptin, metformin, or any tablet excipients.
- **Metabolic Acidosis:** Acute or chronic metabolic acidosis, including diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA), with or without coma.
- **Radiological Studies:** Temporary discontinuation is required for patients undergoing procedures involving intravascular administration of iodinated contrast materials [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Lactic Acidosis:** A rare but potentially fatal metabolic complication due to metformin accumulation. Risk factors include renal impairment, advanced age, excessive alcohol intake, hepatic impairment, and states of hypoxemia (e.g., heart failure, sepsis).
- **Pancreatitis:** Acute pancreatitis (including fatal and non-fatal hemorrhagic or necrotizing cases) has been reported with sitagliptin. If suspected, discontinue treatment immediately.
- **Renal Function Monitoring:** eGFR should be assessed prior to initiation and at least annually thereafter. More frequent monitoring (every 3–6 months) is required in the elderly or those at risk of renal progression [Australian].
- **Bullous Pemphigoid:** Post-marketing cases requiring hospitalization have been reported with DPP-4 inhibitors. Patients should report any blisters or erosions.
- **Arthralgia:** Severe and disabling joint pain has been reported. Discontinuation may be necessary if symptoms occur.
- **Hypoglycemia:** Risk increases when used in combination with a sulfonylurea or insulin. A lower dose of the secretagogue or insulin may be required.
- **Vitamin B12 Levels:** Metformin may decrease serum B12 levels. Annual hematologic monitoring is advised.
- **Hypothyroidism:** Metformin may reduce Thyroid Stimulating Hormone (TSH) levels in patients with treated or untreated hypothyroidism.
- **Mitochondrial Disease:** Metformin is not recommended in patients with known mitochondrial diseases (e.g., MELAS syndrome) due to an increased risk of lactic acidosis exacerbation [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common (1–10%)
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, flatulence, abdominal discomfort, indigestion.
- **Infection/Respiratory:** Nasopharyngitis, upper respiratory tract infection, bronchitis.
- **Nervous System:** Headache.
### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Lactic Acidosis:** (Metformin-related; rare).
- **Acute Pancreatitis:** (Sitagliptin-related).
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Anaphylaxis, angioedema, Stevens-Johnson syndrome.
- **Renal Effects:** Worsening renal function, including acute renal failure.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Iodinated Contrast:** Must be discontinued at the time of or prior to imaging; withhold for 48 hours post-procedure until renal function is re-evaluated and confirmed stable.
- **Alcohol:** Potentiates metformin's effect on lactate metabolism; avoid excessive intake.
- **OCT2/MATE Inhibitors:** Drugs like ranolazine, vandetanib, dolutegravir, and cimetidine may increase metformin exposure and lactic acidosis risk [Australian].
- **Levothyroxine:** May reduce the hypoglycemic effect of metformin.
- **Digoxin:** Sitagliptin may cause a slight increase in plasma digoxin concentrations; clinical monitoring is generally sufficient.
- **Hyperglycemic Agents:** Thiazides, corticosteroids, and estrogens may interfere with glycemic control.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category C:** Not recommended for use during pregnancy. There are no adequate data from the use of sitagliptin/metformin in pregnant women.
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. Metformin is excreted in human milk; it is unknown if sitagliptin is excreted in human milk, though it is present in animal milk.
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in animal studies at doses significantly higher than human exposure [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended in patients aged 10 to 17 years due to insufficient efficacy. Not studied in children under 10 years [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of renal impairment; use with caution and monitor renal function closely.
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **eGFR 60 to <90:** No dose adjustment needed.
    - **eGFR 45 to <60:** No dose adjustment for sitagliptin; limit metformin to 2000 mg/day.
    - **eGFR 30 to <45:** Initiation of fixed-dose combination is not recommended. If already taking and eGFR falls to this range, limit sitagliptin to 50 mg once daily and metformin to 1000 mg/day [Australian].
    - **eGFR <30:** Contraindicated.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Generally avoid in patients with clinical or laboratory evidence of hepatic disease due to the risk of lactic acidosis.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Sitagliptin:** Inhibits the dipeptidyl peptidase 4 (DPP-4) enzyme, increasing active incretin hormones (GLP-1 and GIP), which stimulate insulin release and decrease glucagon secretion in a glucose-dependent manner.
    - **Metformin:** Reduces hepatic glucose production, decreases intestinal glucose absorption, and improves insulin sensitivity by increasing peripheral glucose uptake.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Bioavailability:** Sitagliptin ~87%; Metformin 50–60%.
    - **Metabolism:** Sitagliptin is minimally metabolized (primarily CYP3A4); Metformin is not metabolized.
    - **Elimination:** Both are primarily excreted by the kidneys. Elimination half-life of sitagliptin is ~12.4 hours; metformin is ~6.2 hours (plasma).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Initial Combination Therapy:** In a factorial study, the combination of sitagliptin and metformin provided significant improvements in HbA1c, fasting plasma glucose (FPG), and post-meal glucose compared to monotherapy.
- **TECOS Study:** A cardiovascular safety trial involving 14,671 patients showed that sitagliptin, when added to usual care, did not increase the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) or hospitalization for heart failure [Australian].
- **Add-on Therapy:** Studies confirmed efficacy when added to insulin, sulfonylureas, or SGLT2 inhibitors (e.g., ertugliflozin).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C.
- **Conditions:** Store in the original packaging to protect from moisture [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
