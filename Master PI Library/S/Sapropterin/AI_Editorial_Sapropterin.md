# Editorial Product Information: Sapropterin

## Editorial Note
- The following editorialised product information was developed using official documents from the following jurisdiction: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of clinical data for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an expert editorial overview and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Sapropterin dihydrochloride (synthetic version of the naturally occurring 6R-tetrahydrobiopterin or 6R-BH4).
- **Brand Name:** **Kuvan** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A16AX07.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - 100 mg soluble tablets (equivalent to 77 mg sapropterin) [Australian].
    - 100 mg powder for oral solution in sachets (equivalent to 77 mg sapropterin) [Australian].
    - 500 mg powder for oral solution in sachets (equivalent to 384 mg sapropterin) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** BioMarin Pharmaceutical Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Phenylketonuria (PKU):** Treatment of hyperphenylalaninemia (HPA) in sapropterin-responsive adult and paediatric patients.
- **Tetrahydrobiopterin (BH4) Deficiency:** Treatment of HPA in sapropterin-responsive adult and paediatric patients.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing Principles
- **Supervision:** Therapy must be initiated and managed by physicians experienced in treating PKU and BH4 deficiency.
- **Administration with Food:** Must be taken with a meal to enhance absorption.
- **PKU Dosing:** Administer as a single daily dose at the same time each day (preferably morning).
- **BH4 Deficiency Dosing:** The total daily dose should be divided into 2 or 3 administrations throughout the day.
- **Dietary Management:** Active management of phenylalanine (Phe) and protein intake remains mandatory to maintain nutritional balance.

### Indication-Specific Dosing
- **PKU:** Starting dose is 10 mg/kg/day. The maintenance range is typically 5 to 20 mg/kg/day, titrated to achieve target blood Phe levels.
- **BH4 Deficiency:** Starting dose is 2 to 5 mg/kg/day (total daily dose). The maintenance range is 2 to 20 mg/kg/day.

### Determination of Response
- Response is confirmed by a reduction in blood Phe levels (typically defined as ≥30% reduction or reaching individual therapeutic goals).
- **Trial Period:** If no response is seen after 1 week at the starting dose, the dose may be increased weekly to a maximum of 20 mg/kg/day. Patients failing to respond after one month at 20 mg/kg/day should be considered non-responders and treatment discontinued.

### Administration Instructions
- **Tablets/Sachets:** Dissolve in water or apple juice. Tablets may be crushed to speed dissolution. 
- **Stability:** Tablet solutions should be consumed within 15–20 minutes; sachet solutions within 30 minutes.
- **Small Children (<20 kg):** Use an oral syringe for accurate volume administration. Detailed weight-based dissolution/administration tables are provided in the source documentation.

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to sapropterin dihydrochloride or any of the excipients (including mannitol, potassium citrate, or sucralose).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None noted in the available source.
- **Gastritis and Oesophagitis:** Serious cases have been reported; monitor patients for clinical signs of upper gastrointestinal inflammation.
- **Convulsion Disorders:** Use with caution in patients predisposed to seizures. Convulsions and exacerbations of existing seizure disorders have been reported, particularly when co-administered with levodopa.
- **Low Blood Phenylalanine:** Over-treatment may lead to hypophenylalaninemia, which is associated with impaired neurodevelopmental outcomes in infants. Regular monitoring of Phe and tyrosine is essential.
- **Intercurrent Illness:** Phe levels may rise during illness; close monitoring is required.
- **Hepatic and Renal Impairment:** Safety and efficacy have not been established; exercise caution.
- **Elderly (>50 years):** Safety and efficacy have not been established.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The overall incidence of adverse events is similar to placebo.
- **Very Common (>10%):** Headache, rhinorrhoea.
- **Common (1–10%):** Pharyngolaryngeal pain, cough, nasal congestion, diarrhoea, vomiting, abdominal pain, pyrexia, rash.
- **Post-marketing/Serious:** Hypersensitivity reactions, dyspepsia, pharyngitis, gastritis, oesophagitis, and increased GGT.
- **Rebound Effect:** Cessation of treatment may result in blood Phe levels rising above pre-treatment baselines; withdrawal should be supervised by a physician.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Levodopa:** Caution is advised; co-administration may cause increased irritability/excitability and has been associated with convulsions in BH4-deficient patients.
- **Folate Antagonists (e.g., Methotrexate, Trimethoprim):** May interfere with BH4 metabolism; use with caution.
- **Nitric Oxide (NO) Donors/Vasodilators:** As BH4 is a cofactor for nitric oxide synthetase, caution is recommended with agents like glyceryl trinitrate, isosorbide dinitrate, sodium nitroprusside, PDE-5 inhibitors, and minoxidil.
- **Digoxin:** Although sapropterin may inhibit P-gp and BCRP in vitro, clinical studies showed no effect on the pharmacokinetics of a single dose of digoxin.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category B1 [Australian]:** Limited data in pregnant women. Maternal Phe levels must be strictly controlled (ideally <600 µmol/L) to prevent neurological and cardiac anomalies in the fetus. Sapropterin should only be used if dietary management is insufficient.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if sapropterin is excreted in human milk. Use is not recommended during breastfeeding.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no impact on male or female fertility at doses up to 16 times the maximum human dose.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for patients aged 1 month and older [Australian]. Dosing is weight-based and follows the same mg/kg regimen as adults.
- **Elderly Use:** Caution is advised for patients over 50 years as data are limited.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specifically studied; caution and monitoring are required.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Sapropterin is a synthetic analog of 6R-BH4. In PKU, it enhances the activity of residual phenylalanine hydroxylase (PAH), facilitating the conversion of Phe to tyrosine. In BH4 deficiency, it replaces the missing cofactor to restore PAH activity.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Blood Phe levels typically decrease within 24 hours of a single dose, though maximal effect may take up to a month.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ is 3–4 hours (fasted) or 4–5 hours (fed). A high-fat, high-calorie meal increases $C_{max}$ by 40–85%.
    - **Distribution:** Primarily to the liver and kidneys.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic; metabolised into dihydrobiopterin and dihydroxanthopterin.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 6–7 hours. Primarily excreted in faeces following oral administration.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **PKU (Study 1 & 2):** Clinical trials in patients aged 4–48 years demonstrated that approximately 20% of patients respond to sapropterin with a ≥30% reduction in blood Phe.
- **Paediatric PKU (Study 5 & 6):** Studies in children <7 years showed that sapropterin significantly increased dietary Phe tolerance while maintaining target blood levels (120–360 µmol/L). Long-term data (7 years) suggested preservation of neurocognitive function (FSIQ).
- **BH4 Deficiency:** Evidence from open-label studies and Japanese surveillance programs confirms blood Phe reduction and global clinical improvement in over 90% of patients.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Tablets:** Store below 25°C. Keep bottle tightly closed. Use within two months of opening.
- **Sachets:** Store below 25°C.
- **Safety:** Do not swallow the desiccant capsule found in tablet bottles.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
