# Editorial Product Information: Sevoflurane

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonized editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals to provide a clear, concise, and clinically accurate summary of the drug's profile.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is not a substitute for the official, full Product Information legally approved for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved PI for definitive prescribing guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Sevoflurane (100% w/v)
- **Chemical Class:** Halogenated general inhalation anesthetic
- **Brand Name(s):** Baxter Sevoflurane [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** N01AB08
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Volatile liquid for inhalation (100% w/v)
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Inhalation via a calibrated vaporizer
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- Induction and maintenance of general anesthesia in adult and pediatric patients undergoing surgery. [Australian]

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Principles
- Administration must only be performed by personnel trained in general anesthesia.
- Facilities for airway maintenance, artificial ventilation, oxygen enrichment, and circulatory resuscitation must be immediately available.

### Induction
- Dosage must be individualized and titrated to the desired effect based on age and clinical status.
- A short-acting barbiturate or other intravenous agent may be used before sevoflurane inhalation.
- **Adults:** Inspired concentrations up to 5% typically produce surgical anesthesia in <2 minutes.
- **Children:** Inspired concentrations up to 7% typically produce surgical anesthesia in <2 minutes.

### Maintenance
- Surgical anesthesia levels are usually achieved with concentrations of 0.5% to 3%, with or without nitrous oxide.

### Minimum Alveolar Concentration (MAC)
MAC values decrease with increasing age and the addition of nitrous oxide.
- **Newborns (<3 years):** 3.3–2.6% (in O₂)
- **Adult (25 years):** 2.5% (in O₂)
- **Adult (40 years):** 2.05% (in O₂)
- **Elderly (80 years):** 1.4% (in O₂)

### Administration Instructions
- Must be delivered via a vaporizer calibrated specifically for sevoflurane.
- **CO₂ Absorbents:** Do not allow CO₂ absorbents to dry out. If desiccation is suspected, the absorbent must be replaced before sevoflurane administration to prevent exothermic reactions, fire, or the formation of degradation products.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known or suspected susceptibility to **malignant hyperthermia**.
- Known sensitivity to sevoflurane or other halogenated anesthetic agents.
- History of confirmed hepatitis due to halogenated inhalational anesthetics.
- History of unexplained moderate-to-severe hepatic dysfunction (e.g., jaundice with fever/eosinophilia) following anesthesia with halogenated agents.
- Use in anesthesia circuits containing **Baralyme** (due to the risk of Compound A formation and heat). [Australian]
- General contraindications to anesthesia.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Monitoring:** Constant monitoring of ECG, blood pressure, oxygen saturation, and end-tidal CO₂ is mandatory.
- **Malignant Hyperthermia:** May trigger a skeletal muscle hypermetabolic state. Treatment includes discontinuation of sevoflurane and administration of IV dantrolene.
- **Compound A:** Sevoflurane reacts with CO₂ absorbents to produce Compound A (pentafluoroisopropenyl fluoromethyl ether). To minimize exposure, do not exceed 2 MAC-hours at fresh gas flow rates of 1 to <2 L/min. Fresh gas flow rates <2 L/min are not recommended. [Australian]
- **Perioperative Hyperkalemia:** Rare cases of increased serum potassium leading to fatal cardiac arrhythmias have occurred, particularly in patients with Duchenne muscular dystrophy.
- **QT Prolongation:** Rare reports of QT prolongation and Torsade de Pointes exist; exercise caution in susceptible patients.
- **Hepatic Injury:** Very rare cases of post-operative hepatitis and hepatic failure have been reported. Repeated exposure within a short interval may increase risk.
- **Seizures:** Rare reports in both pediatric and adult patients, even without predisposing risk factors.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Very Common (>10%)
- **Digestive:** Nausea (25%), Vomiting (18%)
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypotension (11%)
- **Respiratory:** Increased cough (11%)
- **Nervous System:** Agitation (15% in pediatric patients)

### Common (1–10%)
- **Cardiovascular:** Bradycardia (5%), Tachycardia (2-6%), Hypertension (2%)
- **Nervous System:** Somnolence (9%), Agitation (7-9%), Dizziness (4%)
- **Respiratory:** Laryngospasm (2-8%), Airway obstruction (8%), Breath holding (2-5%)
- **Other:** Shivering (6%), Increased salivation (2-4%), Fever (1%)

### Serious / Post-Marketing Reports
- **Cardiac:** Cardiac arrest, ventricular fibrillation, Torsade de Pointes.
- **Nervous System:** Seizure-like activity (convulsions), delirium, increased intracranial pressure.
- **Hepatic:** Hepatitis, hepatic failure, jaundice.
- **Renal:** Acute renal failure, tubulointerstitial nephritis.
- **Other:** Anaphylactic/anaphylactoid reactions, rhabdomyolysis, malignant hyperthermia.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Nitrous Oxide:** Reduces the MAC of sevoflurane by approximately 50% in adults and 25% in children.
- **Benzodiazepines and Opioids:** Expected to decrease the MAC of sevoflurane. Synergistic decreases in heart rate and blood pressure may occur with opioids (fentanyl, alfentanil, sufentanil).
- **Neuromuscular Blocking Agents (NMBAs):** Sevoflurane increases the intensity and duration of non-depolarizing NMBAs (e.g., vecuronium, pancuronium). Doses of NMBAs should be reduced during maintenance.
- **Epinephrine (Adrenaline):** Sevoflurane sensitizes the myocardium to catecholamines. Doses of adrenaline >5 µg/kg may produce ventricular arrhythmias.
- **Beta-Blockers:** May increase negative inotropic/chronotropic effects by blocking compensatory mechanisms.
- **CYP2E1 Inducers (e.g., Isoniazid, Alcohol):** May increase sevoflurane metabolism, leading to higher plasma fluoride concentrations.
- **St John’s Wort:** Reports of severe hypotension and delayed emergence.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category: Not specified in provided text]. No adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women; use only if clearly needed.
- **Labor and Delivery:** Safety not established; sevoflurane has a relaxant effect on the uterus, which may increase uterine hemorrhage. It has been used safely for Caesarean section.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if sevoflurane is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution when administering to nursing women.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats suggested reduced fertility after repeated anesthetic doses; human significance is unknown.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Concentration requirements are age-dependent. Higher incidence of agitation (25%) compared to other agents. Seizures have been reported, primarily in children starting from 2 months of age.
- **Elderly Use:** MAC significantly decreases with age; an 80-year-old requires approximately 50% of the concentration required by a 20-year-old.
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety not established in patients with renal insufficiency (creatinine >1.5 mg/dL). Monitor renal function post-operatively.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Can be used in mild-to-moderate impairment; not studied in severe hepatic dysfunction.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhalation anesthetic; produces loss of consciousness and analgesia.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Dose-related cardiac and respiratory depressant; reduces systemic vascular resistance.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption/Distribution:** Low blood/gas solubility (0.63–0.69) results in rapid induction and emergence.
    *   **Metabolism:** Approximately 5% is metabolized via CYP2E1 to hexafluoroisopropanol (HFIP) and inorganic fluoride.
    *   **Elimination:** Primarily via the lungs (unchanged drug); HFIP is conjugated with glucuronic acid and excreted renally.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Cardiovascular Surgery:** In a study of 273 CABG patients, sevoflurane was comparable to isoflurane regarding the incidence of myocardial infarction and ischemic events. [Australian]
- **Pediatric Anesthesia:** Clinical trials showed faster induction and emergence times for sevoflurane compared to halothane. [Australian]

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C. [Australian]
- **Handling:** Store bottle upright with the cap firmly in place. Sevoflurane is stable under normal room lighting.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Animal Remedy/Prescription Only Medicine). [Australian]
