# Editorial Product Information: Streptomycin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for educational and professional reference. It does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Streptomycin sulfate (an aminoglycoside antibiotic derived from *Streptomyces griseus*).
- **Brand Names:** Streptomycin for Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** J01GA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Lyophilized cake/powder for solution for injection; 1 gram per vial (equivalent to 1 gram streptomycin activity) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intramuscular (IM) injection only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** XGen Pharmaceuticals DJB, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
Streptomycin is indicated for the treatment of moderate to severe infections caused by susceptible strains of microorganisms in the following conditions:

- **Mycobacterium tuberculosis:** Used as part of a multi-drug regimen (typically with isoniazid, rifampin, and pyrazinamide). It is particularly indicated when there is suspected or proven drug resistance to primary agents or when primary agents are contraindicated due to toxicity [US].
- **Non-tuberculosis Infections:** Reserved for infections not amenable to less toxic agents, including:
    - **Plague** (*Yersinia pestis*) [US].
    - **Tularemia** (*Francisella tularensis*) [US].
    - **Brucella** (Brucellosis) [US].
    - **Donovanosis** (*Calymmatobacterium granulomatis*) [US].
    - **Chancroid** (*Haemophilus ducreyi*) [US].
    - **Haemophilus influenzae:** For respiratory, endocardial, and meningeal infections (concomitantly with other agents) [US].
    - **Klebsiella pneumoniae pneumonia:** (Concomitantly with other agents) [US].
    - **Urinary Tract Infections:** Caused by *E. coli, Proteus spp., Aerobacter aerogenes, K. pneumoniae,* and *Enterococcus faecalis* [US].
    - **Bacterial Endocarditis:** Caused by *Streptococcus viridans* or *Enterococcus faecalis* (concomitantly with penicillin) [US].
    - **Gram-negative bacillary bacteremia:** (Concomitantly with other agents) [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Administered via **Intramuscular Route Only**.
- Reconstitution: Dissolve the lyophilized cake in Water for Injection USP. Common concentrations include 200 mg/mL (4.2 mL solvent), 250 mg/mL (3.2 mL solvent), or 400 mg/mL (1.8 mL solvent) [US].
- Injection sites (gluteus maximus or mid-lateral thigh) should be alternated [US].

### Adult Dosing
- **Tuberculosis:** 15 mg/kg daily (max 1 g) for initial therapy; or 25–30 mg/kg (max 1.5 g) twice or thrice weekly [US].
- **Tularemia:** 1 to 2 g daily in divided doses for 7 to 14 days [US].
- **Plague:** 2 g daily in two divided doses for a minimum of 10 days [US].
- **Bacterial Endocarditis (Streptococcal):** 1 g twice daily for the first week, then 500 mg twice daily for the second week. For patients >60 years, 500 mg twice daily for the full 2 weeks [US].
- **Bacterial Endocarditis (Enterococcal):** 1 g twice daily for 2 weeks, then 500 mg twice daily for an additional 4 weeks (in combination with penicillin) [US].
- **Other Infections:** 1 to 2 g daily in divided doses every 6 to 12 hours (max 2 g/day) [US].

### Paediatric Dosing
- **Tuberculosis:** 20–40 mg/kg daily (max 1 g); or 25–30 mg/kg (max 1.5 g) twice or thrice weekly [US].
- **Other Infections:** 20 to 40 mg/kg/day in divided doses every 6 to 12 hours. Avoid excessive dosage in children [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- History of clinically significant hypersensitivity to streptomycin [US].
- Clinically significant hypersensitivity to other aminoglycosides (due to known cross-reactivity) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
- **Neurotoxicity:** Risk of severe reactions is sharply increased in patients with renal impairment or pre-renal azotemia. Includes vestibular and cochlear dysfunction, optic nerve dysfunction, peripheral neuritis, and encephalopathy.
- **Nephrotoxicity:** Renal function must be monitored; dose reductions are required for impairment.
- **Drug Interactions:** Avoid concurrent or sequential use of other neurotoxic or nephrotoxic drugs (e.g., neomycin, gentamicin, polymyxin B, cyclosporine).
- **Neuromuscular Blockade:** May result in respiratory paralysis, especially when given post-anaesthesia or with muscle relaxants.

### General Warnings
- **Ototoxicity:** Vestibular damage (headache, nausea, vomiting, equilibrium loss) usually precedes cochlear damage (high-frequency hearing loss). Risk is proportional to dose, duration, age, and renal function [US].
- **Mitochondrial DNA Variants:** Increased risk of ototoxicity in patients with specific variants in the *MT-RNR1* gene (e.g., m.1555A>G), even if serum levels are within range [US].
- **Clostridium difficile-Associated Diarrhea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all antibacterials, ranging from mild diarrhea to fatal colitis [US].

### Precautions
- **Superinfection:** May result in overgrowth of nonsusceptible organisms, including fungi [US].
- **Audiometric Monitoring:** Baseline and periodic tests are recommended for extended therapy [US].
- **Skin Sensitivity:** Care should be taken during handling to avoid contact dermatitis [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common:** Vestibular ototoxicity (nausea, vomiting, vertigo); facial paresthesia; rash; fever; urticaria; angioneurotic edema; eosinophilia [US].
- **Less Frequent:** Cochlear ototoxicity (deafness); exfoliative dermatitis; anaphylaxis; azotemia; leucopenia; thrombocytopenia; pancytopenia; hemolytic anemia; muscular weakness; amblyopia [US].
- **Serious Effects:** Irreversible vestibular damage; respiratory paralysis; fatal CDAD; severe renal failure [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Ototoxic/Nephrotoxic Agents:** Increased risk when used with neomycin, kanamycin, gentamicin, cephaloridine, paromomycin, viomycin, polymyxin B, colistin, tobramycin, and cyclosporine [US].
- **Diuretics:** Potentiation of ototoxic effects when co-administered with ethacrynic acid, furosemide, mannitol, and potentially other diuretics [US].
- **Muscle Relaxants/Anaesthetics:** Risk of neuromuscular blockade and respiratory paralysis [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category D/Fetal Harm Warning]: Streptomycin crosses the placental barrier and can cause fetal harm (specifically ototoxicity/permanent deafness) when administered to pregnant women. Patients should be apprised of potential hazards [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Small amounts are excreted in breast milk. A decision should be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the mother’s clinical need [US].
- **Fertility:** Not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use with extreme caution. Excessive dosage in infants may cause a syndrome of central nervous system depression (stupor, flaccidity, coma, respiratory depression) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Higher risk of toxicity. Reduced dosages are recommended for patients >60 years of age [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Requires strict dosage reduction and monitoring. In kidney damage, peak serum concentrations should not exceed 20 to 25 mcg/mL [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal antibiotic; interferes with normal protein synthesis by binding to the 30S ribosomal subunit [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak serum levels (25 to 50 mcg/mL) reached within 1 hour after 1 g IM dose [US].
    - **Distribution:** Found in most organ tissues except the brain. Significant concentrations in pleural fluid and tuberculous cavities. Crosses the placenta [US].
    - **Elimination:** Excreted primarily by glomerular filtration. In normal renal function, 29% to 89% of a dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
Specific pivotal trial data summaries were not provided in the source documents; however, usage is based on long-standing clinical practice and recommendations from the CDC and American Thoracic Society [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Dry Powder:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Should be protected from light. May be stored at room temperature for one week without significant loss of potency [US].
- **Visual Inspection:** Parenteral products should be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration before use [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only (Prescription Only Medicine) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not explicitly listed as a schedule number in the US source, but classified as a human prescription drug [US].
