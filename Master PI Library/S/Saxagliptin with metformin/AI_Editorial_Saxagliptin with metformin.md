## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose: 50 mg/kg.
    - Administration: Slow intravenous bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - Frequency: Often initiated with a loading dose in severe metabolic crisis, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (minimum frequency every 6 hours).
    - Maximum reported dose: 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - Administration: Slow intravenous bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - Titration: Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives.

### Monitoring
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations prior to therapy.
- Conduct weekly and monthly monitoring, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizures:** Reports exist of seizure activity occurring in patients with or without pre-existing conditions. In patients with a known seizure history, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Oral Formulation in Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of the oral form in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when co-administered with levocarnitine.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified, but text states "Category B-like" logic]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess intake must be weighed against maternal benefits [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg for metabolic disorders; 10–20 mg/kg for ESRD). See Section 3.
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. Monitor for metabolite accumulation if transitioning to oral forms [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with specific metabolic defects [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Mean distribution half-life: 0.585 hours.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (excluding endogenous carnitine). Terminal elimination half-life: 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Efficacy:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis, 20 mg/kg administered three times weekly post-dialysis resulted in trough plasma carnitine concentrations between 54 and 180 µmol/L (normal range: 40–50 µmol/L) after one week. Concentrations remained stable or increased over the study duration [US].
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg in ESRD patients demonstrated dose-proportionate increases in trough carnitine levels at 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
