# Editorial Product Information: sodium cromoglycate

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **sodium cromoglycate**
- Chemical class: disodium chromone derivative (disodium salt of a bis-chromone derivative)
- ATC code: R03BC01 (antiallergic agents, excluding corticosteroids; inhalants) — specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: inhalation

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval (as stated in available sources):
  - Australian: sodium cromoglycate inhaler (5 mg formulation) — 15 April 1999; sodium cromoglycate inhaler (1 mg formulation) — 31 March 2003.
- Date of last revision/update of the provided product information:
  - Australian product information last revised: 14 July 2020.
- Withdrawals, suspensions or restrictions: Not specified in available sources.
- Regulatory dates for other jurisdictions: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Prophylactic treatment of bronchial asthma, including prevention of exercise-induced bronchospasm. [Australian]
- Use is prophylactic; not indicated for relief of acute bronchospasm. (Consistent with available sources)

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adults)
- Sodium cromoglycate 1 mg inhaler:
  - Typical adult dose: two inhalations four times daily.
  - Suggested daily timing: two inhalations at night, two on waking, with remaining doses at 4–6 hour intervals.
  - Additional doses may be taken before exercise.
- Sodium cromoglycate 5 mg inhaler:
  - Recommended initial dose: two inhalations twice daily.
  - In patients with more severe asthma or during periods of heavy challenge, dosage may be increased up to four inhalations four times daily to achieve optimal control.
  - To prevent exercise-induced symptoms: two to four inhalations given 5–10 minutes before exercise.
- Maintenance dose: must be individually assessed; therapy is essentially prophylactic and requires regular use to maintain benefit.

### Dosing (paediatric)
- Paediatric dosing mirrors adult dosing recommendations in the available sources:
  - 1 mg inhaler: two inhalations four times daily is described.
  - 5 mg inhaler: two inhalations twice daily as the recommended initial dose; exercise prophylaxis 2–4 inhalations 5–10 minutes prior.
  - Final selection and titration should be based on individual response and clinician judgement. [Australian]

### Administration instructions
- Prime inhaler before first use:
  - 1 mg inhaler: prime with four actuations prior to first use; if not used for >48 hours, re-prime with four actuations.
  - 5 mg inhaler: prime with four actuations prior to first use; re-priming rules: not required if unused for <48 hours; two actuations after 3–7 days of non-use (>48 to ≤168 hours); four actuations after >7 days non-use.
- Regular cleaning of the mouthpiece is required to reduce the risk of blockage with certain propellant formulations. Patients should be instructed in cleaning (e.g., nightly cleaning of the mouthpiece and alternation with a spare mouthpiece where provided).
- Use of a spacer is recommended for patients with poor inhaler technique; patients should be instructed on spacer use and cleaning (wash spacer in warm water and detergent and allow to dry without rinsing or drying with a cloth; perform before initial use and at least monthly thereafter).
- Do not use sodium cromoglycate inhalers for acute symptom relief.

### Titration and withdrawal
- If sodium cromoglycate is to be withdrawn, dosage should normally be reduced gradually over about one week to avoid exacerbation of asthma; symptoms may recur if therapy is stopped abruptly.
- Where reduction of systemic corticosteroids is attempted while on sodium cromoglycate, supervise patients carefully and consider peak flow monitoring during steroid dose reduction.

### Missed dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to **sodium cromoglycate** or to any excipients of the formulation. [Australian]
- No other contraindications are specified in the available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- None specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- **Not for relief of acute bronchospasm.**
- Severe anaphylactic reactions can occur extremely rarely after administration.
- Cough and/or bronchospasm may follow administration; paradoxical bronchospasm (acute worsening of wheeze or shortness of breath immediately after inhalation) has been reported and should prompt discontinuation and alternative therapy.
- Severe bronchospasm has been reported rarely and may prevent continued use despite prior bronchodilator administration.
- Eosinophilic pneumonia has been reported; discontinue sodium cromoglycate if eosinophilic pneumonia is suspected.
- In patients in whom systemic corticosteroid dose reduction is being attempted, reduce steroids stepwise with close supervision and consider objective monitoring (e.g., peak expiratory flow).
- Therapy is prophylactic; continued regular administration is necessary to maintain benefit.
- Excessive use should be avoided because of potential hazards from propellant exposure as well as drug overdosage (preclinical propellant data indicate effects at high exposure concentrations).
- Effects on laboratory tests: Not specified in available sources.
- Use in hepatic impairment: No data available in the provided sources.
- Use in renal impairment: No data available in the provided sources.
- Use in the elderly: No data available in the provided sources.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor clinical response (symptom control, lung function) when initiating or changing therapy and when attempting steroid dose reductions.
- Monitor for paradoxical bronchospasm and hypersensitivity reactions.

### Additional adverse reactions reported (frequency not quantified in trials)
- Very rarely reported or reported but causal association not established:
  - Headache
  - Abdominal pain and diarrhoea
  - Arthralgia and myalgia
  - Urticaria and various skin rashes
  - Hypersensitivity reactions including angioedema, bronchospasm, hypotension and collapse (reported extremely rarely)
  - Very rare cases of eosinophilic pneumonia have been reported
- Paradoxical bronchospasm may occur immediately after administration; if this occurs, discontinue drug and institute alternative therapy.

### Serious adverse effects (highlighted)
- Severe bronchospasm (rare)
- Anaphylactic reactions including angioedema, hypotension and collapse (extremely rare)
- Eosinophilic pneumonia (very rare)

### Reporting

## 8. Drug Interactions
- No specific drug–drug interaction studies have been conducted with the CFC-free formulations in the available sources.
- No evidence of clinically significant interactions with other drugs was observed with the earlier CFC formulation.
- No pharmacokinetic or pharmacodynamic interactions requiring specific management are specified in the available sources.
- Clinicians should remain vigilant when co-prescribing with other respiratory medications during regimen changes; monitor clinical response when switching formulations or spacers (amount of drug delivered to the lung may vary with spacer type).

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Australian pregnancy category: B1 (sodium cromoglycate). Available sources state that sodium cromoglycate has been used in a large number of pregnant women with no observed increase in malformations or harmful effects on the fetus to date, but caution is advised, especially in the first trimester. Use in pregnancy should occur only if the expected benefit to the mother justifies the potential risk to the fetus. [Australian]

### Breastfeeding
- Sodium cromoglycate is excreted in human breast milk; safety in nursing infants has not been established. Sodium cromoglycate is not recommended for nursing mothers unless the expected benefit outweighs the potential risk to the infant. [Australian]

### Fertility
- Animal studies: no evidence of impaired fertility in rats at reported doses (subcutaneous administration; males 175 mg/kg/day, females 100 mg/kg/day). Human fertility effects: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Paediatric dosing recommendations are provided and mirror adult regimen for inhaled preparations (see Section 4). Clinical trial data in children (including a 4‑week study of 184 asthmatic children) support safety and maintenance of asthma control on switching from historical CFC formulation to CFC‑free inhaler. Specific age ranges for paediatric approval and formulation approvals by age are not detailed in the available sources.

### Elderly

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **Sodium cromoglycate** inhibits release of mediators from sensitised inflammatory (mast) cells, thereby preventing both immediate and late asthmatic responses to immunological and other stimuli in the lung. It has no intrinsic bronchodilator or antihistamine activity and does not impair normal immune defence mechanisms.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Prevents mediator release from sensitised cells in the respiratory tract resulting in attenuation of immediate and late-phase asthmatic responses; effective as prophylaxis against exercise-induced bronchospasm.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption:
  - After inhalation, up to approximately 10% of the dose is absorbed via the lungs; remainder is exhaled, deposited in the oropharynx, or swallowed.
  - Approximately 1% of the inhaled dose is absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract.
- Distribution:
  - Protein binding approximately 65% (moderate and reversible).
- Metabolism:
  - Not metabolised in humans (excreted unchanged).
- Elimination:
  - Elimination half-life approximately 1.5–2 hours.
  - Excreted unchanged in both urine and bile in roughly equal proportions.
- Pharmacokinetic data example:
  - A single dose of 2 × 5 mg in healthy subjects produced a mean peak plasma concentration (Cmax) of ~6.2 ng/mL (SD 4.1).

### Preclinical safety
- Genotoxicity: Negative in assays for gene mutations and chromosomal damage.
- Carcinogenicity: Long-term animal studies in mice, hamsters and rats showed no neoplastic effects at the doses and regimens tested.
- Propellant safety (preclinical): The propellant HFA‑227 (apaflurane) has shown narcosis and weak cardiac sensitisation to catecholamines at high exposure concentrations in animals; human data at therapeutic inhaler exposure indicate low plasma propellant concentrations, but excessive use should be avoided because of potential propellant-related hazards.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Exercise challenge study:
  - Small crossover study in 16 mild-to-moderate adult and paediatric asthmatics with exercise-induced bronchospasm demonstrated that the CFC‑free 5 mg formulation provided statistically significant protection versus placebo and was comparable to the historical CFC formulation in percent decrease in FEV1.
- Paediatric comparative safety study:
  - A 4‑week comparative study in 184 asthmatic children assessed safety and effects after changeover from the historical CFC 5 mg formulation to the CFC‑free 5 mg formulation; pulmonary function tests and parameters of asthma control improved during baseline and remained stable after switch, supporting safety and effectiveness of the CFC‑free formulation in children.
