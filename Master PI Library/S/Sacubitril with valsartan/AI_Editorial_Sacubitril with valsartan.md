# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Jurisdictions contributing to this editorial:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine Injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this publication, only the United States (FDA) source documentation was provided for analysis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Metabolic):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Initial Management:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Dosing should be every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest administered dose reported is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
- **Titration:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if concentrations are supra-therapeutic.

### Monitoring
- **Baseline:** Obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to parenteral therapy.
- **Maintenance:** Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known:** There are no listed contraindications for levocarnitine injection in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Hypersensitivity Reactions
- **Serious Reactions:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration.
- **Management:** Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs. Re-administration should only be considered after a careful risk-benefit analysis and under close monitoring [US].

### General Precautions
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation Note):** While this editorial covers the injection, it is noted that chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions in ESRD patients are often confounded by underlying pathology. The following were reported in clinical trials at a frequency ≥5% regardless of causality [US]:

| Body System | Adverse Event (Incidence ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, back pain, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain. |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, palpitations, tachycardia, cardiovascular disorder. |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, dyspepsia, anorexia. |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight changes. |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia, depression. |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis. |

**Other Adverse Effects:**
- **Very Common (>10%):** Injection site reactions (up to 38%), Pharyngitis (up to 27%), Hypertension (up to 21%).
- **Common (1–10%):** Body odor, gastritis, and various gastrointestinal upsets.
- **Serious:** Anaphylaxis, seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine therapy or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not categorized by letter, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm]. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after exogenous administration. Weigh the risks to the child against the benefits to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Recommended for the treatment of inborn errors of metabolism. Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg).
- **Geriatric Use:** Not specifically addressed in the source document.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosage for ESRD patients is specific to post-dialysis administration (10-20 mg/kg). Monitor for metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO) if oral forms are used in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Mean terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous carnitine) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients showed that 20 mg/kg post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine concentrations from 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L at baseline to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses confirmed a dose-dependent increase in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL concentration) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified for other jurisdictions in available sources.
