# Editorial Product Information: Somatropin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a clinically relevant format.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource. It is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Always consult the current local approved PI for definitive prescribing instructions.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Somatropin (recombinant protein of bacterial origin/rbe). It is a recombinant human growth hormone (rhGH) consisting of 191 amino acids.
- **Brand Names:** **GENOTROPIN**, **GENOTROPIN GoQuick**, **GENOTROPIN MiniQuick** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** H01AC01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Powder for Injection (with preservative):** 5 mg, 5.3 mg, and 12 mg two-compartment cartridges for use with specific delivery devices.
    - **Powder for Injection (preservative-free):** **MiniQuick** single-dose syringes available in strengths of 0.2 mg, 0.4 mg, 0.6 mg, 0.8 mg, 1.0 mg, 1.2 mg, 1.4 mg, 1.6 mg, 1.8 mg, and 2.0 mg.
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Somatropin** is indicated for the following populations [Australian]:

**Paediatric Patients:**
- Short stature resulting from failed or decreased secretion of endogenous pituitary growth hormone (Growth Hormone Deficiency, GHD).
- Growth disturbances associated with gonadal dysgenesis (Turner syndrome).
- Treatment of short stature and improvement of body composition in patients with Prader-Willi syndrome (PWS).
- Growth disturbance in children with chronic renal insufficiency (CRI) defined by a GFR < 50 mL/min/1.73 m² and height/growth velocity $\le$ 25th percentile.

**Adult Patients:**
- Severe growth hormone deficiency as confirmed by an insulin tolerance test (peak GH concentrations < 2.5 ng/mL).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Instructions
- Administration is via subcutaneous injection.
- Injection sites must be rotated to prevent lipoatrophy.
- The weekly dose should be divided into 6 to 7 doses per week.

### Paediatric Dosing (Weekly Dose)
- **Growth Hormone Deficiency:** 0.175 to 0.245 mg/kg.
- **Turner Syndrome:** 0.3 to 0.35 mg/kg.
- **Prader-Willi Syndrome:** 0.245 to 0.35 mg/kg. Diagnosis must be confirmed by genetic testing.
- **Chronic Renal Insufficiency:** 0.3 to 0.35 mg/kg. Growth should be monitored for one year prior to treatment to verify disturbance.

### Adult Dosing
- **Initial Dose:** 0.04 mg/kg/week, divided into 7 daily doses.
- **Titration:** Increase gradually based on clinical response, side effects, and serum insulin-like growth factor-I (IGF-I) levels.
- **Maximum Dose:** 0.08 mg/kg/week.
- **Note:** Women may require higher doses than men, particularly those on oral oestrogen replacement. Dose requirements typically decrease with age.

### Handling and Reconstitution
- Reconstitute by mixing the diluent with the powder in the two-compartment cartridge/syringe using the approved device.
- Dissolve with a slow, swirling motion; **do not shake**, as this may denature the active protein.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Active Malignancy:** Evidence of active tumours or tumour growth. Anti-tumour therapy must be completed before initiating **somatropin**.
- **Epiphyseal Closure:** Growth promotion in children with closed epiphyses.
- **Acute Critical Illness:** Complications following open heart or abdominal surgery, multiple trauma, acute respiratory failure, or extensive burns.
- **Prader-Willi Syndrome:** Contraindicated in patients with PWS who are severely obese or have severe respiratory impairment.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to metacresol (found in the diluent of multidose presentations). Preservative-free (**MiniQuick**) should be used if myositis or severe injection site pain occurs [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **PWS Fatalities:** Rare cases of sudden death have been reported in PWS patients with risk factors including severe obesity, respiratory impairment, sleep apnoea, or unidentified respiratory infection. Monitor PWS patients for signs of upper airway obstruction (e.g., snoring) and sleep apnoea.
- **Glucose Intolerance:** **Somatropin** reduces insulin sensitivity. Monitor for glucose intolerance; patients with manifest diabetes may require adjustment of anti-diabetic therapy.
- **Adrenal Function:** **Somatropin** inhibits the enzyme 11$\beta$-HSD-1, which may reduce serum cortisol. This can unmask central hypoadrenalism or require increased glucocorticoid replacement in those already being treated.
- **Thyroid Function:** Enhanced conversion of T4 to T3 may occur. Monitor thyroid function periodically, as hypothyroidism may interfere with the growth response.
- **Intracranial Hypertension:** In cases of severe headache, nausea, or visual changes, fundoscopy should be performed to rule out papilloedema. If confirmed, discontinue treatment.
- **Skeletal Disorders:** Slipped capital femoral epiphysis and progression of scoliosis may occur during rapid growth. Monitor for limping or hip/knee pain.
- **Critical Illness:** Increased mortality was observed in trials involving critically ill adults (41.9% vs 19.3% for placebo).

## 6. Adverse Effects
Approximately 10% of children and 30–40% of adults experience adverse effects, often related to fluid retention in adults.

| Frequency | Adults | Children |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** ($\ge$10%) | Peripheral oedema, Arthralgia | Not specified |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Paraesthesia, Musculoskeletal stiffness, Myalgia | Injection-site reactions |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Carpal tunnel syndrome | Paraesthesia, Musculoskeletal stiffness, Myalgia, Peripheral oedema, Rash/Urticaria |
| **Rare** (<0.1%) | Not specified | Leukaemia (incidence similar to non-GHD children), Type 2 Diabetes, Benign intracranial hypertension |
| **Not Known** | Face oedema, Rash, Pruritus | Face oedema |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Sudden death in PWS patients, slipped capital femoral epiphysis, and Legg-Calve-Perthes disease (post-marketing).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Glucocorticoids:** High-dose glucocorticoid therapy inhibits the growth-promoting effects of **somatropin**. Glucocorticoid replacement must be carefully titrated.
- **Cytochrome P450:** **Somatropin** may increase the clearance of compounds metabolised by CYP3A4 (e.g., sex steroids, corticosteroids, anticonvulsants, and ciclosporin).
- **Oral Oestrogens:** Women on oral oestrogen replacement may require higher doses of **somatropin** to achieve therapeutic IGF-I targets.
- **Anti-diabetic agents:** Requirements for insulin or oral hypoglycaemics may increase due to decreased insulin sensitivity.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B2** [Australian]. No clinical experience in pregnant women. Animal studies showed no teratogenic effects at high doses, though reproduction was inhibited in rats at $\ge$1 mg/kg/day. Not recommended for use during pregnancy.
- **Breastfeeding:** There is no information regarding the excretion of **somatropin** into breast milk. Absorption of the intact protein by the infant is considered extremely unlikely.
- **Fertility:** In animal studies, high doses (3.3 mg/kg/day) led to reduced conception rates and altered oestrus cycles in rats.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy are well-established for GHD, PWS, Turner syndrome, and CRI (see Indications). Retesting of childhood-onset GHD is required before adult-onset treatment.
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical experience in patients over 60 years of age is lacking [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** CRI dosing is established. Treatment should be discontinued at the time of renal transplantation.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Somatropin** is therapeutically equivalent to endogenous human growth hormone. It stimulates linear growth, increases body length (via effect on epiphyseal plates), and increases the number and size of muscle cells.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** 
    - **Protein Metabolism:** Increases cellular protein synthesis and nitrogen retention.
    - **Carbohydrate Metabolism:** Large doses may impair glucose tolerance; helps correct fasting hypoglycaemia in GHD children.
    - **Lipid Metabolism:** Mobilises lipids and reduces fat stores.
    - **Mineral Metabolism:** Induces retention of sodium, potassium, and phosphorus.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Approximately 80% absorbed following SC injection.
    - **Peak Serum Conc.:** Achieved approximately 5 hours post-injection.
    - **Elimination:** Half-life is approximately 4 hours. Metabolised in the liver and kidneys via protein catabolism.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Turner Syndrome:** Data from 14 studies (n$\approx$900) showed an average final height gain of 5.4 cm.
- **Prader-Willi Syndrome:** Two randomised trials showed significant increases in linear growth and lean body mass, with a decrease in fat mass over 12–24 months.
- **Adult GHD:** Largest multicentre trial (n=163) demonstrated significant reductions in fat mass and waist-to-hip ratio, alongside increases in lean body mass and total body protein after 6–12 months.
- **CRI:** Studies in 262 children showed growth velocity doubled in the first year and remained above baseline for up to 4 years.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze).
- **Light:** Protect from light.
- **In-use Stability:** 
    - **Multidose (with preservative):** Once reconstituted, cartridges may be stored in the refrigerator for up to 28 days.
    - **MiniQuick (preservative-free):** Reconstituted solution must be used immediately or stored refrigerated for up to 24 hours. Prior to reconstitution, **MiniQuick** may be stored at or below 25°C for a single period of up to 6 months [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
