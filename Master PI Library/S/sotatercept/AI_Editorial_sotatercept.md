# Editorial Product Information: sotatercept

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **sotatercept**
- Chemical class: Not specified in available sources
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Subcutaneous (SC) injection

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of adults with pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) in WHO Functional Class II, III, or IV, to be used in combination with standard background therapy.
  - Efficacy demonstrated in idiopathic and heritable PAH, PAH associated with connective tissue disease, drug- or toxin-induced PAH, and PAH associated with congenital heart disease with repaired shunts.
- If any jurisdictional differences in indications exist in source documents, they are not specified in available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General dosing (adult)
- Initiation  
  - Obtain haemoglobin (Hgb) and platelet count prior to the first dose.
  - Do not initiate treatment if platelet count is <50,000/mm3 (<50.0 x 10^9/L).
- Starting dose: 0.3 mg/kg administered subcutaneously once every 3 weeks.
- Recommended target dose: escalate to 0.7 mg/kg subcutaneously once every 3 weeks. Escalation to target dose is recommended approximately 3 weeks after the single starting dose once acceptable Hgb and platelet counts are confirmed.

### Dosing by weight
- Dosing is weight-based (mg/kg). Specific injection volumes by weight were provided in source materials; consult the product-specific administration instructions available locally for exact volume-to-weight conversions. [Note: pack/vial/container details and pack-specific volume tables are not reproduced here.]

### Dose modification and delays
- Monitor Hgb and platelet count before each dose for the first 5 doses (or longer if values are unstable), then periodically thereafter.
- Delay treatment for 3 weeks if any of the following occur:
  - Hgb increases >2.0 g/dL from the previous dose and is above the upper limit of normal (ULN).
  - Hgb increases >4.0 g/dL from baseline.
  - Hgb increases >2.0 g/dL above ULN.
  - Platelet count decreases to <50,000/mm3 (<50.0 x 10^9/L).
- Reassess Hgb and platelet count before reinitiating treatment after any delay.
- If treatment is interrupted for >9 weeks, restart at 0.3 mg/kg and re-escalate to 0.7 mg/kg after verifying acceptable Hgb and platelet counts.

### Missed dose
- If a dose is missed, administer as soon as possible.
- If the missed dose is not taken within 3 days of the scheduled date, adjust the schedule so that subsequent dosing maintains 3-week intervals.

### Overdose
- In case of overdose, monitor for erythrocytosis (clinically significant increases in haemoglobin) and manage accordingly.
- If an overdose occurred during patient/caregiver administration, consider retraining and/or switching administration to a healthcare professional.

### Administration instructions (practical)
- Intended for use under healthcare professional guidance. Patient or caregiver administration is possible after adequate training.
- Pregnancy testing is recommended for females of reproductive potential prior to initiation.
- Reconstitution and preparation: source materials contain detailed stepwise reconstitution and syringe preparation instructions (including allowance for product equilibration to room temperature, reconstitution with supplied sterile diluent, gentle swirling, visual inspection, withdrawing the appropriate volume and use within a specified time window after reconstitution). Consult the manufacturer’s Instructions for Use provided with the product for exact procedural steps.
- Injection sites: abdomen (at least 2 inches from the navel), upper thigh, or upper arm. For patient/caregiver self-administration, use only abdomen and upper thigh. Rotate injection sites; avoid scarred, tender, or bruised skin.

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to **sotatercept** or to any of the excipients.
- Other contraindications: Not specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed / highlighted warnings
- Not specified in available sources as a jurisdictional boxed/black-box warning. (If a boxed/mandatory regional warning exists, it is not specified in the available sources.)

### General warnings and precautions
- Erythrocytosis: Increases in haemoglobin have been observed. Severe erythrocytosis may increase the risk of thromboembolic events or hyperviscosity syndrome. Monitor Hgb prior to each dose for the first 5 doses (or longer if unstable) and periodically thereafter.
- Severe thrombocytopenia: Decreases in platelet count have been observed; severe thrombocytopenia (<50,000/mm3) has occurred. Thrombocytopenia was observed more frequently in patients receiving concomitant prostacyclin infusion. Do not initiate treatment if platelet count <50,000/mm3.
- Serious bleeding: Serious bleeding events (e.g., gastrointestinal, intracranial hemorrhage) have been reported. Patients experiencing serious bleeding should not receive **sotatercept**. Serious bleeding events were more likely in patients on prostacyclin background therapy and/or antithrombotic agents and in those with low platelet counts.
- Embryofetal toxicity: Animal studies indicate potential for fetal harm and increased post-implantation loss. Advise pregnant women of potential fetal risk. Pregnancy testing prior to initiation is recommended for females of reproductive potential.
- Fertility impairment: Animal data indicate potential impairment of both female and male fertility; counsel patients accordingly.

### Monitoring requirements
- Hgb and platelet count: prior to each dose for the first 5 doses (or longer if unstable), then periodically thereafter.
- Monitor for signs/symptoms of bleeding; evaluate platelet counts and haemoglobin if suspected.
- Monitor blood pressure; mean increases in systolic and diastolic blood pressure have been reported in clinical studies.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Use in renal impairment: No dose adjustment required. Data limited in severe renal impairment (eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m2).
- Use in hepatic impairment: Not studied. Sotatercept is expected to be metabolised via cellular catabolism; hepatic impairment not expected to substantially influence metabolism based on available information.
- Use in elderly: No dose adjustment required based on age. Older patients experienced bleeding events more commonly in clinical data.
- Use in paediatrics: Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients <18 years of age.
- Breastfeeding: Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for 4 months after the final dose (presence in human milk not known; adverse effects observed in animal lactation studies).

## 8. Drug Interactions
- No formal interaction studies beyond what is described in source materials were performed.
- Pharmacodynamic interactions:
  - Increased risk of bleeding observed in patients receiving prostacyclin infusion and/or antithrombotic agents; bleeding events were more frequent in such patients. Exercise caution and monitor closely if these agents are co-administered.
  - Thrombocytopenia occurred more frequently with concomitant prostacyclin infusion.
- Pharmacokinetic interactions: Not specified in available sources.
- Clinically significant interactions and management:
  - Monitor platelet count and signs of bleeding closely when co-administered with prostacyclin therapies and antithrombotic agents; consider risk–benefit before co-administration.
  - No dose-adjustment guidance with other specific drugs provided in available sources.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

### Pregnancy
- Australian pregnancy category: Category D (based on available source material).
- Risk summary: No human data available. Animal embryofetal toxicity studies showed increased post-implantation loss, reduced numbers of live fetuses and fetal body weights, delays in ossification, and other developmental effects at exposures approximating or exceeding human exposures. **sotatercept** may cause fetal harm when administered to a pregnant woman.
- Recommendation: Advise pregnant women of potential fetal risk. Pregnancy testing is recommended prior to initiation for females of reproductive potential.
- Contraception: Females of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for at least 4 months after the last dose.
- Other jurisdictions’ pregnancy categorisation or differing recommendations: Not specified in available sources.

### Breastfeeding
- There are no data on the presence of **sotatercept** in human milk or effects on the breastfed infant. Animal data demonstrate adverse effects on nursing offspring (lower pup weight, delayed sexual maturation) with maternal dosing during lactation.
- Recommendation: Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for 4 months after the final dose.

### Fertility
- Animal studies indicate potential impairment of female and male fertility (reduced pregnancy rates, increased pre- and post-implantation loss, histologic changes in male reproductive tract and decreased functional fertility endpoints at certain doses). Counsel patients on potential effects on fertility.

## 10. Use in Special Populations

### Paediatric use
- Safety and efficacy in patients <18 years: Not established.

### Elderly
- No dose adjustment required based on age.
- Clinical experience: No overall differences in efficacy between <65 and ≥65-year-old subgroups in available trials. Bleeding events occurred more commonly in older patients.

### Renal impairment
- No dose adjustment required based on renal impairment per available sources.
- Limited data in severe renal impairment (eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m2); use caution.

### Hepatic impairment
- Sotatercept metabolism is via cellular catabolism; hepatic impairment is not expected to substantially influence metabolism based on available information.
- Use in patients with hepatic impairment has not been studied; no dosing recommendations provided.

### Mechanism of action

## 12. Clinical Trials

### STELLAR (pivotal)
- Population: Adult PAH patients with WHO Functional Class II or III.
- Design: Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 1:1 **sotatercept** vs placebo on background standard of care therapies.
- Enrolment: n = 323 (randomised 1:1).
- Dosing: Starting 0.3 mg/kg SC; escalated to 0.7 mg/kg every 3 weeks for 24 weeks. After the primary 24-week period, patients could continue in a long-term double-blind treatment period.
- Median duration of treatment: **sotatercept** group 313.0 days; placebo 273.0 days.
- Safety outcomes: See Section 7 (Adverse Effects) for trial-specific frequencies (e.g., headache 20.2%, COVID-19 14.7%, diarrhoea 12.3%, epistaxis 12.3%, telangiectasia 10.4%, etc.). Moderate Hgb elevations (>2 g/dL above ULN) occurred; severe Hgb elevations (≥4 g/dL above ULN) were not observed in STELLAR.
- Efficacy primary outcomes and results: Not specified in available sources (primary endpoints and efficacy results are not detailed in the provided excerpts).

### ZENITH
- Population: Adult PAH patients WHO FC III or IV at high risk of mortality.
- Design: Randomised, 1:1 **sotatercept** vs placebo on background standard of care therapies.
- Enrolment: 172 patients.
- Median duration of exposure: **sotatercept** 434.5 days; placebo 267.5 days.
- Safety outcomes: Higher incidence of adverse reactions versus STELLAR, reflecting more advanced disease. Most commonly reported adverse reactions with higher frequency in **sotatercept** group included: epistaxis (45.3%), telangiectasia (25.6%), thrombocytopenia (15.1%), increased haemoglobin (15.1%), gingival bleeding (10.5%), erythema (7.0%), and rash (5.8%). Serious bleeding events were similar between **sotatercept** and placebo in ZENITH.
- Efficacy primary outcomes and results: Not specified in available sources.
