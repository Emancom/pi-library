# Editorial Product Information: Safinamide

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of safinamide, synthesised from official regulatory sources for the use of healthcare professionals. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Safinamide (as mesilate), an α-aminoamide derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** ARX-Safinamide [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N04BD03.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 50 mg (orange, round, biconcave, debossed "MS" and "18") [Australian].
    - Film-coated tablets: 100 mg (orange, round, biconcave, debossed "MS" and "19") [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Parkinson’s Disease:** Indicated for the treatment of adult patients with fluctuating idiopathic Parkinson’s disease (PD) as add-on therapy to a regimen containing levodopa (L-Dopa).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** 
    - Initial dose: 50 mg once daily.
    - Escalation: Based on clinical need, the dose may be increased to 100 mg once daily after two weeks of treatment.
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Tablets should be swallowed with water.
    - May be taken with or without food.
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, the patient should wait and take the next dose at the scheduled time the following day.
- **Discontinuation:**
    - 50 mg dose: May be discontinued without down-titration.
    - 100 mg dose: Should be tapered by reducing the dose to 50 mg daily for one week prior to complete cessation.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to safinamide or any excipients in the formulation.
- **Concomitant MAO Inhibitors:** Use with other monoamine oxidase (MAO) inhibitors is contraindicated due to the risk of non-selective inhibition leading to hypertensive crisis. A 7-day washout is required after stopping safinamide before starting another MAO inhibitor.
- **Pethidine:** Concomitant use with pethidine is contraindicated. A 7-day washout is required after stopping safinamide before starting pethidine.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Severe hepatic impairment [Australian].
- **Ophthalmological Conditions:** Albinism, retinal degeneration, uveitis, inherited retinopathy, or severe progressive diabetic retinopathy.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Reported in patients taking MAO inhibitors concomitantly with SSRIs, SNRIs, tricyclic/tetracyclic antidepressants, cyclobenzaprine, methylphenidate, amphetamines, and opioid drugs. Monitor for mental status changes, autonomic instability, and neuromuscular hyperactivity.
- **Retinal Pathology:** Use is not recommended in patients with a history of retinal disease or uveitis. While clinical trials did not show retinal degeneration at maximum doses, animal studies (rats) showed retinal scarring and cataracts.
- **Impulse Control Disorders (ICDs):** Though not directly associated with safinamide in trials, ICDs (e.g., pathological gambling, hypersexuality, compulsive spending) have been reported with other dopaminergic treatments and MAO inhibitors.
- **Dopaminergic Effects:** Safinamide may exacerbate pre-existing dyskinesia when used with levodopa. Reducing the levodopa dose may mitigate this effect.
- **Somnolence and Driving:** Patients may experience somnolence or dizziness. Caution is advised when operating heavy machinery or driving; patients who experience sudden sleep onset must refrain from these activities.
- **Tyramine Interaction:** Safinamide is highly selective for MAO-B; clinical studies showed no evidence of tyramine potentiation. No dietary tyramine restrictions are required at therapeutic doses.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following frequencies are based on clinical trials of safinamide 100 mg/day as add-on therapy.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (>10%) | Dyskinesia |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Insomnia, somnolence, dizziness, headache, cataract, orthostatic hypotension, nausea, fall |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Urinary tract infection, basal cell carcinoma, anaemia, leukopenia, decreased/increased appetite, hypertriglyceridaemia, hallucinations, depression, anxiety, confusional state, paraesthesia, balance disorder, blurred vision, vertigo, palpitations, tachycardia, hypertension, cough, dyspnoea, constipation, dyspepsia, vomiting, dry mouth, hyperhidrosis, back pain, arthralgia, muscle spasms, nocturia, erectile dysfunction, fatigue, peripheral oedema, weight changes, increased CPK |
| **Rare** (<0.1%) | Bronchopneumonia, furuncle, skin papilloma, eosinophilia, cachexia, hyperkalaemia, suicidal ideation, delirium, coordination abnormal, dysgeusia, amblyopia, diabetic retinopathy, myocardial infarction, hypertensive crisis, bronchospasm, peptic ulcer, hyperbilirubinaemia, alopecia, psoriasis, ankylosing spondylitis, micturition urgency |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Post-marketing reports include serious hypersensitivity reactions (swelling of the tongue/gingiva, dyspnoea, and rash).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **MAOIs and Pethidine:** Contraindicated (see Section 4).
    - **Sympathomimetics:** Use caution with decongestants containing ephedrine or pseudoephedrine.
    - **Dextromethorphan:** Use with safinamide is not recommended; use with caution if necessary.
    - **Serotonergic medications (SSRIs/SNRIs):** Use at the lowest necessary dose and monitor for serotonin syndrome. A washout period of 5 half-lives of the previous SSRI is recommended before starting safinamide.
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **BCRP Substrates:** Safinamide may inhibit BCRP. Monitor patients taking substrates such as rosuvastatin, pitavastatin, pravastatin, ciprofloxacin, methotrexate, or glyburide.
    - **OCT1 Substrates:** Caution is advised with drugs like metformin, aciclovir, or ganciclovir due to potential increased exposure.
    - **OAT3 Inhibitors:** Potential for increased exposure of the metabolite ‘safinamide acid’ (NW-1153), though likely of no clinical relevance.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category B3 [Australian]:** Safinamide should not be used during pregnancy. Women of childbearing potential should use contraception during therapy. Animal studies showed reproductive toxicity and increased embryofoetal death when used with levodopa/carbidopa.
- **Breastfeeding:** Use is not recommended. Safinamide is excreted in animal milk; a risk to the breast-fed child cannot be excluded.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed reduced implantations in females at 3x human exposure and minor sperm morphology changes in males at 1.4x human exposure.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients under 18 years have not been established.
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment required for patients over 75 years of age.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required.
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild:** No dose adjustment required.
    - **Moderate:** Maximum dose of 50 mg/day recommended.
    - **Severe:** Contraindicated. If a patient progresses from moderate to severe impairment, treatment must be discontinued.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A highly selective and reversible MAO-B inhibitor (>1000-fold selectivity over MAO-A). It increases extracellular dopamine levels in the striatum. Non-dopaminergic mechanisms include state-dependent inhibition of voltage-gated Na+ channels and modulation of Ca2+ channels, leading to the inhibition of glutamate release.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; absolute bioavailability is 95%. Tmax is reached in 1.8–2.8 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is ~165 L. Plasma protein binding is 88–90%.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily eliminated via metabolism (~5% unchanged in urine). Major metabolic pathways involve non-microsomal enzymes (cytosolic amidases and MAO-A). The main circulating metabolite is NW-1689 (N-dealkylated acid).
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is 20–30 hours. 76% of radioactivity is excreted in urine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **NW-016 & SETTLE:** Pivotal Phase III, 26-week trials evaluating safinamide as add-on to levodopa. 
- **Outcomes:** 
    - In **NW-016**, both 50 mg and 100 mg doses significantly increased daily "ON" time without troublesome dyskinesia (approx. 1.3 hours vs 0.97 hours for placebo).
    - In **SETTLE**, safinamide (50–100 mg) significantly increased "ON" time by 1.42 hours compared to 0.57 hours for placebo.
    - Improvements were also noted in "OFF" time and motor scores (UPDRS III).
- **Long-term Efficacy:** The 18-month extension study (NW-018) demonstrated that improvements in "ON" time were maintained for up to 2 years.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C. 
- Keep out of reach of children.
