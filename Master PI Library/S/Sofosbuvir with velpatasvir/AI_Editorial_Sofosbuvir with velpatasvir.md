# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine Injection for use by healthcare professionals. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the United States (FDA) source documentation was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. 
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
    - **Frequency:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often given, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours) over the next 24 hours. 
    - **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring is advised; target plasma free carnitine levels should be between 35 and 60 µmol/L.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** 
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - **Titration:** Dose adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations (normal range: 40–50 µmol/L). Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on concentrations.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate appropriate medical care [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with reports of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Levocarnitine Limitation:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) normally excreted in urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) include:

| Body System | Adverse Event | Levocarnitine (n=130) | Placebo (n=63) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain | 7–33% | 5–59% |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia | 6–20% | 5–19% |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting | 8–16% | 10–19% |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia | 5–9% | 3–17% |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, paresthesia, depression | 5–13% | 3–11% |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, rhinitis, dyspnea | 7–23% | 10–33% |

### Other Adverse Effects
- **Common:** Transient nausea and vomiting.
- **Less Frequent:** Body odor, gastritis.
- **Serious:** Anaphylaxis, seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Clinical Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specifically letter-graded in the current PI, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm.
    - **Recommendation:** Should be used during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine levels following administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against maternal benefit; consideration may be given to discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg); see Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. Safety of high-dose *oral* levocarnitine is not established in renal impairment (see Section 5) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation and energy production. In patients with metabolic defects, it promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and [³H]-γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Efficacy:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine levels from 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L at baseline to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the effect on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, malaise) in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of opened vials as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

    - **United States:** Rx-only.
