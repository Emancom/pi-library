# Editorial Product Information: Sorafenib

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information provided by the **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. 
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of sorafenib for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data, regulatory warnings, and administration guidelines.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Sorafenib (present as sorafenib tosilate), a multikinase inhibitor.
*   **Brand Name:** **Nexavar** [Australian].
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Dosage Form and Strength:** 200 mg film-coated tablets (red, round, biconvex, marked with a Bayer cross and "200") [Australian].
*   **Route of Administration:** Oral.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Bayer Australia Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC):** Treatment of patients with advanced hepatocellular carcinoma [Australian-approved].
*   **Renal Cell Carcinoma (RCC):** Treatment of patients with advanced renal cell carcinoma [Australian-approved].
*   **Differentiated Thyroid Carcinoma (DTC):** Treatment of patients with locally advanced or metastatic, progressive, differentiated thyroid carcinoma that is refractory to radioactive iodine [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
*   **Standard Dose:** 400 mg (two 200 mg tablets) taken twice daily.
*   **Administration:** Tablets should be taken either without food or with a moderate-fat meal. They should be swallowed with water.
*   **Duration:** Therapy should continue as long as clinical benefit is observed or until toxicity becomes unacceptable.

### Dose Titration and Adjustment
*   **HCC and RCC:** If dose reduction is required due to adverse reactions, the dose should be decreased to 400 mg once daily.
*   **DTC:** A stepwise reduction is recommended:
    *   Level -1: 600 mg daily (400 mg and 200 mg doses, 12 hours apart).
    *   Level -2: 400 mg daily (200 mg twice daily).
    *   Level -3: 200 mg daily (200 mg once daily).
*   **Skin Toxicity (DTC):** Specific modification protocols are based on toxicity grade (1–3) and frequency of occurrence, ranging from supportive care to permanent discontinuation.

### Missed Dose
*   Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known severe hypersensitivity to sorafenib tosilate or any of the inactive tablet components [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
*   **Squamous Cell Lung Cancer:** Increased mortality has been observed when sorafenib is added to platinum-based chemotherapies (paclitaxel/carboplatin and gemcitabine/cisplatin) in patients with squamous cell lung cancer. It is not recommended for use in this combination and population [Australian].
*   **Dermatological Toxicities:** Hand-foot skin reaction (palmar-plantar erythrodysaesthesia) and rash are very common. These typically appear within the first six weeks of treatment. Management includes topical therapy, dose interruption, or discontinuation.
*   **Hypertension:** Blood pressure elevations are usually mild to moderate and occur early in treatment. Regular monitoring is required; severe or persistent hypertension may necessitate discontinuation.
*   **Haemorrhage:** Increased risk of bleeding. If medical intervention is required, permanent discontinuation should be considered.
*   **Cardiac Ischaemia/Infarction:** Incidence is higher with sorafenib than placebo. Temporary or permanent cessation should be considered in patients developing these events.
*   **QT Interval Prolongation:** Sorafenib can prolong the QT/QTc interval, increasing the risk of ventricular arrhythmias. Caution is required in patients with congenital long QT, electrolyte disturbances, or those on anti-arrhythmic medications.
*   **Gastrointestinal Perforation:** Reported in <1% of patients; requires permanent discontinuation.
*   **Aneurysms and Artery Dissections:** VEGF pathway inhibitors may promote these events; risk should be assessed in patients with hypertension or history of aneurysm.
*   **Tumour Lysis Syndrome (TLS):** Post-marketing cases (some fatal) have occurred. Close monitoring is required for high-risk patients (high tumour burden, renal insufficiency).

### Monitoring Requirements
*   **INR Monitoring:** Required for patients taking concomitant warfarin.
*   **Electrolytes:** Periodic monitoring of magnesium, potassium, and calcium is advised, especially in patients at risk of QT prolongation.
*   **DTC-Specific Monitoring:** Close monitoring of blood calcium and TSH levels (appropriate thyroxine dose adjustment may be needed) is required for thyroid carcinoma patients [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are categorised by frequency. The most common reactions across all indications include diarrhoea, fatigue, alopecia, infection, rash, and hand-foot skin reaction.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting, constipation, fatigue, pain (mouth, abdominal, bone, headache), infection, anorexia, hypophosphataemia, haemorrhage, hypertension, rash, alopecia, hand-foot skin reaction, pruritus, dry skin, arthralgia, weight decreased, increased amylase/lipase. |
| **Common (1-10%)** | Folliculitis, leucopenia, neutropenia, anaemia, thrombocytopenia, hypothyroidism, hypocalcaemia, hypokalaemia, hyponatraemia, depression, peripheral sensory neuropathy, dysgeusia, tinnitus, CHF, myocardial ischaemia/infarction, flushing, rhinorrhoea, dysphonia, stomatitis, dyspepsia, dysphagia, GERD, jaundice, keratocanthoma/SCC of skin, exfoliative dermatitis, acne, hyperkeratosis, myalgia, muscle spasms, renal failure, proteinuria, erectile dysfunction, asthenia, fever, influenza-like illness, mucosal inflammation, transient transaminase increase. |
| **Uncommon (0.1-1%)** | Anaphylactic reaction, hypersensitivity, hyperthyroidism, dehydration, reversible posterior leukoencephalopathy, hypertensive crisis, interstitial lung disease-like events, pancreatitis, gastritis, GI perforation, cholecystitis, cholangitis, eczema, erythema multiforme, gynaecomastia, abnormal INR. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | QT prolongation, drug-induced hepatitis, nephrotic syndrome. |

*   **Post-marketing Experience:** Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, radiation recall dermatitis, leukocytoclastic vasculitis, angioedema, rhabdomyolysis, and thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Drugs like rifampicin, St. John’s wort, phenytoin, and carbamazepine may increase sorafenib metabolism and decrease plasma concentrations. Rifampicin reduced sorafenib AUC by 37% [Australian].
*   **UGT1A1 Substrates:** Sorafenib inhibits UGT1A1 and UGT1A9 pathways. Concomitant irinotecan resulted in a 67–120% increase in the AUC of its active metabolite, SN-38.
*   **Docetaxel:** Co-administration resulted in a 36–80% increase in docetaxel AUC. Caution and monitoring are advised.
*   **Neomycin:** Co-administration of this non-systemic antibiotic reduces sorafenib bioavailability by 54% due to interference with enterohepatic recycling.
*   **Warfarin:** While in vivo studies did not show CYP2C9 inhibition, infrequent bleeding or INR elevations have been reported.
*   **P-glycoprotein Substrates:** Sorafenib inhibits P-gp in vitro; it may increase concentrations of P-gp substrate drugs.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Sorafenib is embryotoxic and teratogenic in animals. It should not be used during pregnancy. Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during and for at least 2 weeks after treatment.
*   **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if sorafenib is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding must be discontinued during treatment due to potential risks to the infant.
*   **Fertility:** Animal data suggest sorafenib may impair both male and female fertility (testicular degeneration, oligospermia, and arrested follicular development observed in animals).

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established. Bone and teeth abnormalities were observed in young animals.
*   **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required based on age (>65 years).
*   **Renal Impairment:** No adjustment for mild to moderate impairment. Not studied in patients with severe impairment (CrCl <30 mL/min) or those on dialysis [Australian].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for Child-Pugh A or B. Not studied in patients with Child-Pugh C (severe) impairment; exposure may be increased as sorafenib is primarily eliminated hepatically.

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Sorafenib is a multikinase inhibitor. It targets RAF kinases (CRAF, BRAF, V600E BRAF) and receptor tyrosine kinases (VEGFR-2, VEGFR-3, PDGFR-β, KIT, FLT-3). This results in inhibition of tumour cell proliferation and angiogenesis.
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption:** Mean relative bioavailability is 38–49%. Peak plasma levels are reached in ~3 hours. High-fat meals reduce bioavailability by 29%.
    *   **Distribution:** 99.5% protein bound.
    *   **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic via CYP3A4 (oxidative) and UGT1A9 (glucuronidation).
    *   **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is 25–48 hours. Approximately 77% is excreted in faeces and 19% in urine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **HCC (Study 100554):** A Phase III trial (n=602) showed a significant survival advantage for sorafenib over placebo (Median OS 10.7 vs 7.9 months; HR 0.69) [Australian].
*   **RCC (Study 11213 - TARGET):** A Phase III trial (n=903) in patients who failed prior systemic therapy. Sorafenib doubled median PFS compared to placebo (167 vs 84 days; HR 0.44) [Australian].
*   **DTC (Study 14295):** A Phase III trial (n=417) in RAI-refractory DTC. Median PFS was 10.8 months for sorafenib vs 5.8 months for placebo (HR 0.587).
