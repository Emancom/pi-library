# Editorial Product Information: Selexipag

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory guidance into a single, structured reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Selexipag (a selective non-prostanoid prostacyclin IP receptor agonist).
- **Brand Name:** **Uptravi** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** B01AC27.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Film-coated tablets in 200, 400, 600, 800, 1000, 1200, 1400, and 1600 micrograms [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Janssen-Cilag Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Uptravi** is indicated for the treatment of pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) to delay disease progression and reduce the risk of hospitalisation [Australian]. It is approved for use in patients with WHO functional class II, III, or IV symptoms for the following aetiologies:
- Idiopathic PAH.
- Heritable PAH.
- PAH associated with connective tissue disease.
- PAH associated with congenital heart disease with repaired shunts.
- PAH associated with drugs and toxins.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Initiation:** Treatment should be started and monitored by a physician experienced in PAH management.
- **Starting Dose:** 200 micrograms taken twice daily (BID), approximately 12 hours apart.
- **Titration:** Increase in increments of 200 micrograms BID, usually at weekly intervals, until the highest tolerated dose is reached (up to a maximum of 1600 micrograms BID).
- **Maintenance:** The highest tolerated dose achieved during titration should be maintained. If tolerability decreases over time, a dose reduction to the previous level should be considered.

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets must be swallowed whole with water; they must not be split, crushed, or chewed.
- **Food Effect:** May be taken with or without food. Taking with food may improve tolerability.
- **Initial Dose Timing:** It is recommended to take the first dose of a new titration step in the evening.

### Missed Doses and Interruptions
- **Missed Dose:** Take as soon as possible. If the next dose is due within 6 hours, skip the missed dose and resume the normal schedule.
- **Treatment Interruption:** If treatment is missed for 3 days or more, therapy should be restarted at a lower dose and re-titrated.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to selexipag or any excipients.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class C).
- **Cardiac Conditions:** Severe coronary heart disease, unstable angina, myocardial infarction within the last 6 months, decompensated cardiac failure (if not under close medical supervision), or severe arrhythmias.
- **Cerebrovascular Events:** Stroke or transient ischaemic attack within the last 3 months.
- **Valvular Disease:** Congenital or acquired valvular defects with clinically relevant myocardial function disorders not related to pulmonary hypertension.
- **Drug Interactions:** Concomitant use of strong CYP2C8 inhibitors (e.g., gemfibrozil) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypotension:** Due to vasodilatory properties, selexipag may lower blood pressure. Use with caution in patients with resting hypotension, hypovolaemia, severe left ventricular outflow obstruction, or autonomic dysfunction.
- **Pulmonary Veno-Occlusive Disease (PVOD):** If signs of pulmonary oedema occur, consider PVOD. If confirmed, discontinue selexipag.
- **Hyperthyroidism:** Observed in 2% of patients. Monitor thyroid function tests as clinically indicated.
- **Increased Heart Rate:** A moderate increase in heart rate may occur after each dose.
- **Monitoring:** Blood pressure and thyroid function should be monitored based on clinical status.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The majority of adverse reactions are mild to moderate and occur more frequently during the dose titration phase.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Headache (65%), Diarrhoea (42%), Nausea (34%), Jaw pain (26%), Vomiting (18%), Pain in extremity (17%), Myalgia (16%), Nasopharyngitis (13%), Flushing (12%), Arthralgia (11%), Rash (11%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Anaemia/haemoglobin decrease (8%), Abdominal pain (8%), Decreased appetite (6%), Hypotension (5%), Asthenia (5%), Pyrexia (4%), Dyspepsia (4%), Weight decrease (3%), Nasal congestion (3%), Sinus tachycardia, Eye pain, Acute renal failure |
| **Post-marketing** | Hypersensitivity reactions, Urticaria, Angioedema |

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP2C8 Inhibitors (Strong):** Gemfibrozil increases exposure to the active metabolite 11-fold. **Contraindicated.**
- **CYP2C8 Inhibitors (Moderate):** Clopidogrel increases exposure to the active metabolite ~2.7-fold. Reduce selexipag frequency to **once daily** during co-administration.
- **CYP2C8 Inducers:** Rifampicin reduces exposure to the active metabolite by half. Dose adjustment of selexipag may be required.
- **UGT1A3/UGT2B7 Inhibitors:** Caution is recommended with inhibitors like valproic acid.
- **Transporters:** Selexipag and its metabolite are substrates of OATP1B1, OATP1B3, P-gp, and BCRP. Lopinavir/ritonavir increases selexipag exposure 2-fold.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Antihypertensives:** Potential for additive blood pressure lowering effects with diuretics or other vasodilators.
- **Anticoagulants:** No increased risk of bleeding was observed in clinical trials when co-administered with warfarin or heparin.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B1]. There is no data on the use of selexipag in human pregnancy; use should be avoided. Animal studies showed no teratogenicity.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if selexipag is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no effect on male or female fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children (<18 years) have not been established.
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required for patients $\ge$65 years. Limited experience in patients >75 years; use with caution.
- **Renal Impairment:** No adjustment for mild or moderate impairment. Exercise caution during titration in severe renal impairment (eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 $m^2$). No data for eGFR <15 mL/min/1.73 $m^2$ or dialysis.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild (Child-Pugh A):** No adjustment.
    - **Moderate (Child-Pugh B):** Reduce frequency to **once daily**.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Contraindicated.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selexipag is a selective IP prostacyclin receptor agonist. It is structurally distinct from prostacyclin analogues. Activation of the IP receptor triggers vasodilatory, anti-proliferative, and anti-fibrotic effects.
- **Metabolism:** Rapidly absorbed and hydrolysed by carboxylesterases to an active metabolite (ACT-333679), which is **37 times more potent** than the parent drug. Both undergo oxidative metabolism mainly via **CYP2C8** and to a lesser extent **CYP3A4**.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Bioavailability:** ~49%.
    - **Half-life:** Parent (~0.8–2.5 hours); Active metabolite (~6.2–13.5 hours).
    - **Elimination:** Primarily via faeces (93%) and urine (12%).
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Does not cause IP receptor desensitisation in vitro.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **GRIPHON Study:** A Phase 3, multicentre, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 1156 patients with PAH (WHO FC I-IV). 
- **Primary Endpoint:** Composite of morbidity or mortality. 
- **Outcome:** Selexipag reduced the risk of a primary endpoint event by **40%** (HR 0.60; p <0.0001) compared to placebo. The benefit was primarily driven by reductions in hospitalisation and disease progression.
- **Secondary Endpoint:** Increased 6-minute walk distance (6MWD) by a placebo-corrected median of 12 metres at Week 26.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Handling:** Protect from moisture. Keep in the original container.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
