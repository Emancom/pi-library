## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Spesolimab-sbzo
- **Chemical Class:** Humanized monoclonal immunoglobulin G1 (IgG1) antibody; Interleukin-36 receptor (IL-36R) antagonist.
- **Brand Name:** **SPEVIGO** [US]
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):**
    - **Subcutaneous Injection:** 300 mg/2 mL (150 mg/mL) solution in a single-dose prefilled syringe [US].
    - **Subcutaneous Injection:** 150 mg/mL solution in a single-dose prefilled syringe [US].
    - **Intravenous Injection:** 450 mg/7.5 mL (60 mg/mL) solution in a single-dose vial for dilution [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) or Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Boehringer Ingelheim Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Generalized Pustular Psoriasis (GPP):** Indicated for the treatment of GPP in adults and paediatric patients 12 years of age and older weighing at least 40 kg [US].
    - Includes treatment of GPP flares (IV administration) [US].
    - Includes treatment of GPP when not experiencing a flare (SC administration) [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Paediatric Patients ≥12 years and ≥40 kg)
- **Treatment of GPP Flare (Intravenous):**
    - Administer a single 900 mg dose via IV infusion over 90 minutes.
    - If symptoms persist, an additional 900 mg dose may be administered one week after the initial dose [US].
- **Treatment of GPP When Not Experiencing a Flare (Subcutaneous):**
    - **Loading Dose:** 600 mg administered as SC injections.
    - **Maintenance Dose:** 300 mg administered SC every 4 weeks, starting 4 weeks after the loading dose [US].
- **Transitioning from IV to SC:** 
    - Initiate or reinitiate SC dosing at 300 mg every 4 weeks, starting 4 weeks after the IV treatment for a flare. A loading dose is not required in this scenario [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **IV Administration:** Must be diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection to a total volume of 100 mL. Use a sterile, non-pyrogenic, low protein binding in-line filter (0.2 micron). Do not mix with other products [US].
- **SC Administration:** Administer in the upper thighs or abdomen. If multiple injections are required for a single dose, use different injection sites (at least 1 inch apart) [US].
- **Self-Administration:** Patients or caregivers may self-inject SC doses after proper training, provided a healthcare professional deems it appropriate [US].

### Missed Dose
- If a SC dose is missed, administer it as soon as possible. Resume subsequent doses at the regular scheduled time [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known severe or life-threatening hypersensitivity to spesolimab-sbzo or any of the excipients [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Infections:** Spesolimab may increase the risk of infections. Treatment is not recommended in patients with clinically important active infections. If an infection develops during treatment, withhold spesolimab until resolution [US].
- **Tuberculosis (TB):** Evaluate patients for active or latent TB prior to initiation. Do not use in patients with active TB. Consider anti-TB therapy for those with latent TB prior to starting spesolimab [US].
- **Hypersensitivity and Infusion-Related Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis and Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS), have been reported. Reactions can occur with the first or subsequent doses. Discontinue immediately for serious reactions. For mild/moderate IV reactions, the infusion may be slowed or temporarily stopped [US].
- **Vaccinations:** Avoid live vaccines during treatment and for at least 16 weeks following the final dose. Ensure all age-appropriate vaccinations are complete prior to initiating therapy [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Treatment of GPP Flare (Intravenous)
The following adverse reactions occurred in ≥1% of patients and more frequently than placebo in Study Effisayil-1 during the first week:

| Adverse Reaction | Spesolimab (N=35) | Placebo (N=18) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Asthenia and Fatigue** | 9% | 6% |
| **Headache** | 9% | 6% |
| **Nausea** | 6% | 0% |
| **Pruritus and Prurigo** | 6% | 0% |
| **Infusion site hematoma/bruising** | 6% | 0% |
| **Urinary tract infection** | 6% | 0% |

### Treatment of GPP When Not Experiencing a Flare (Subcutaneous)
Adverse reactions associated with an increased incidence (≥9 cases per 100 patient-years) compared to placebo:
- Injection site reactions (erythema, pain, swelling, induration, warmth)
- Urinary tract infection
- Arthralgia
- Pruritus

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Infections:** Serious infections (e.g., urinary tract infection, bacteremia) [US].
- **Immune System:** DRESS and anaphylaxis [US].
- **Neurological:** Guillain-Barre syndrome was reported in 3 subjects in the wider clinical development program (including unapproved indications) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No formal drug interaction studies have been conducted. Spesolimab is not expected to cause cytokine-mediated CYP450 interactions [US].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Use with live vaccines is cautioned (see Section 5) [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified under the current labeling system, but the PI notes that data in pregnant women are insufficient to inform drug-associated risks. 
    - **Clinical Considerations:** Human IgG crosses the placental barrier; spesolimab may be transmitted to the fetus. Animal studies (mice) using a surrogate antibody showed no evidence of embryo-fetal lethality or malformations [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on the presence of spesolimab in human milk or its effects on the breastfed infant. Because spesolimab is an IgG, it is expected to be present in human milk. The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the mother's clinical need and potential risks [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in mice showed no adverse effects on male or female fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness are established in patients 12 years and older weighing at least 40 kg. Use is supported by data from Effisayil-2 and PK analyses. Safety/efficacy in children <12 years or <40 kg have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies included a limited number of patients ≥65 years. Data are insufficient to determine if they respond differently than younger subjects [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No formal studies were conducted. As a monoclonal antibody, spesolimab is not expected to undergo hepatic or renal elimination [US].
- **Body Weight:** Higher body weight is associated with lower plasma concentrations; however, the clinical impact is unknown [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Spesolimab binds to the IL-36 receptor (IL-36R), preventing activation by its ligands (IL-36 $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$). This inhibits downstream pro-inflammatory and pro-fibrotic pathways [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The relationship between reduced IL-36R activity and GPP flare treatment is not fully characterized [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** SC peak plasma concentrations are reached in 5.5 to 7 days. Absolute bioavailability is approximately 58% to 72% (dose-dependent) for SC administration [US].
    - **Distribution:** Typical steady-state volume of distribution is 6.4 L [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Expected to be degraded into small peptides and amino acids via catabolic pathways [US].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 25.5 days [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Effisayil-1 (IV):** A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study in adults with GPP flares. At Week 1, 54% of the spesolimab group (n=35) achieved a GPPPGA pustulation sub-score of 0 (no visible pustules) compared to 6% in the placebo group (n=18) [US].
- **Effisayil-2 (SC):** A randomized, double-blind study evaluating SC spesolimab for GPP prevention. The 600 mg loading/300 mg maintenance (every 4 weeks) regimen significantly increased the time to first GPP flare over 48 weeks compared to placebo (Risk difference -39%) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Refrigeration:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F). Store in the original carton to protect from light. Do not freeze [US].
- **Room Temperature Stability:**
    - **300 mg/2 mL Syringes:** May be kept at room temperature (up to 25°C/77°F) for up to 14 days in the original carton. Discard if not used within this period [US].
    - **Vials:** Unopened vials may be stored at room temperature (20°C to 25°C) for up to 24 hours [US].
- **Diluted Solution (IV):** Use immediately. If necessary, refrigerate at 2°C to 8°C for no more than 4 hours. Protect from light [US].
