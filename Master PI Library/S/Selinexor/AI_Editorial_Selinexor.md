# Editorial Product Information: Selinexor

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of selinexor for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this writing, only the Australian PI was available for review; therefore, cross-jurisdictional comparisons with US, European, or other regulatory authorities are not included.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Selinexor (a reversible covalent selective inhibitor of nuclear export [SINE] compound).
- **Brand Name(s):** XPOVIO [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 20 mg film-coated tablets. The tablets are blue, round, bi-convex, and debossed with "K20" on one side [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Antengene (Aus) Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Multiple Myeloma (Combination Therapy):** In combination with bortezomib and dexamethasone for the treatment of adults who have received at least one prior therapy [Australian].
- **Relapsed or Refractory Multiple Myeloma (RRMM):** In combination with dexamethasone for the treatment of adults who have received at least three prior therapies and whose disease is refractory to at least one proteasome inhibitor (PI), at least one immunomodulatory medicinal product (IMiD), and an anti-CD38 monoclonal antibody (mAb) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Treatment should be initiated and monitored by physicians experienced in managing multiple myeloma. Prophylactic anti-nausea agents (e.g., 5-HT3 antagonists) should be provided prior to and during treatment.

### Dosing by Indication
- **Combination with Bortezomib and Dexamethasone (SVd):**
  - **Selinexor:** 100 mg orally once weekly (Days 1, 8, 15, 22, and 29 of a 35-day cycle).
  - **Bortezomib:** 1.3 mg/m² subcutaneously once weekly (Days 1, 8, 15, and 22).
  - **Dexamethasone:** 20 mg orally (Days 1, 2, 8, 9, 15, 16, 22, 23, 29, and 30).
- **Combination with Dexamethasone (Sd):**
  - **Selinexor:** 80 mg orally on Days 1 and 3 of each week (160 mg total/week).
  - **Dexamethasone:** 20 mg orally on Days 1 and 3 of each week.

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets must be swallowed whole with water. Do not crush, chew, or break to avoid skin irritation from the active substance.
- May be taken with or without food.
- Maintain adequate fluid and caloric intake; consider IV hydration for patients at risk of dehydration.

### Missed Dose Instructions
If a dose is missed, delayed, or if vomiting occurs after administration, the patient should not repeat or "double up" the dose. The next dose should be taken on the next regularly scheduled day.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to selinexor or any of the excipients (e.g., microcrystalline cellulose, croscarmellose sodium, povidone, sodium lauryl sulfate) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hematologic Toxicity:** Selinexor causes significant thrombocytopenia (can be life-threatening/fatal), neutropenia (increasing infection risk), and anemia. Monitor CBC at baseline, during treatment, and more frequently during the first two months.
- **Gastrointestinal Toxicity:** High incidence of severe nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea. Prophylactic anti-emetics are mandatory.
- **Neurological Toxicity:** Risk of life-threatening confusional states, dizziness, and fatigue. Patients must be advised against driving or operating machinery until symptoms resolve. Co-administration with other CNS depressants may increase this risk.
- **Hyponatraemia:** Severe or life-threatening cases reported. Monitor sodium at baseline and frequently during the first two months. Correct for concurrent hyperglycemia or high paraprotein levels.
- **Weight Loss and Anorexia:** Monitor nutritional status and weight. May require appetite stimulants or nutritional support.
- **Cataracts:** New onset or exacerbation can occur. Perform ophthalmic evaluations as clinically indicated; if surgery is required, hold selinexor 24 hours prior and 72 hours after.
- **Tumour Lysis Syndrome (TLS):** Reported in patients; monitor high-risk patients closely.
- **Serious Infections:** Serious and fatal infections (e.g., fungal pneumonia, herpesvirus) have occurred, often independent of Grade 3/4 neutropenia.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequencies are categorised as Very Common (>10%) and Common (1–10%).

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Pneumonia, sepsis, septic shock, acute kidney injury, and fatal hemorrhage associated with thrombocytopenia.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
  - **CYP3A4:** Selinexor is a substrate of CYP3A4. Strong CYP3A4 inducers may reduce selinexor exposure.
  - **Transporters:** In vitro, selinexor inhibits OATP1B3 but does not inhibit other major transporters (OAT1, OAT3, OCT1/2, etc.). It is not a substrate for P-gp or BCRP.
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
  - **Paracetamol:** No clinically significant effect on selinexor exposure at doses up to 1000 mg/day.
  - **CNS Depressants:** May exacerbate dizziness and neurological toxicities [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Selinexor is not recommended. Animal studies indicate embryofetal lethality and malformations (e.g., microphthalmia) at exposures below human therapeutic levels. Verify pregnancy status before initiation.
- **Contraception:** Women of childbearing potential and males of reproductive potential should use effective contraception (or abstain) during treatment and for at least 1 week after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Excretion in human milk is unknown. Because of the potential for serious adverse reactions, breastfeeding should be discontinued during treatment and for 1 week after the last dose.
- **Fertility:** Animal data suggest potential impairment of both male and female fertility (reduced sperm, decreased ovarian follicles) [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children <18 years have not been established.
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in efficacy were observed in patients >65 years. However, patients ≥75 years had higher rates of serious/fatal adverse reactions and treatment discontinuation.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for patients with mild, moderate, or severe renal impairment. Data are unavailable for patients on dialysis.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for mild to moderate hepatic impairment. Selinexor has not been studied in severe hepatic impairment.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selinexor blocks exportin 1 (XPO1), the primary nuclear export protein for tumour suppressor proteins (TSPs), growth regulators, and oncogenic mRNAs. Inhibition leads to nuclear accumulation of TSPs (e.g., p53, p21) and reduced expression of oncoproteins (e.g., c-Myc, Bcl-2), inducing cell cycle arrest and apoptosis.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ within 4 hours. High-fat meals do not significantly affect PK.
  - **Distribution:** 95% plasma protein bound; apparent Volume of Distribution ($V_d/F$) is 133 L.
  - **Metabolism:** Metabolised by CYP3A4, UGTs, and GSTs.
  - **Elimination:** Mean half-life ($t_{1/2}$) is 6 to 8 hours. Total clearance is approximately 18.6 L/h.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **BOSTON Trial (SVd):** A Phase 3 study in patients with multiple myeloma (≥1 prior therapy) comparing weekly SVd to twice-weekly bortezomib/dexamethasone (Vd). SVd demonstrated a significantly longer median Progression-Free Survival (PFS) of 13.9 months compared to 9.5 months for Vd (HR 0.70).
- **STORM Trial (Sd):** A Phase 2 study in RRMM (refractory to PI, IMiD, and anti-CD38). The Overall Response Rate (ORR) was 26%, with a median Duration of Response (DOR) of 4.4 months.
