# Editorial Product Information: succinylcholine chloride

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): succinylcholine chloride  
- Chemical class: Depolarizing neuromuscular blocking agent (depolarizing neuromuscular blocker)  
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources  
- Route(s) of administration: Intravenous (IV) and intramuscular (IM)

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 1952 [US] (as stated in source document).  
- Date of last revision/update of source PI: Revised 11/2022 [US].  
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- As an adjunct to general anesthesia to facilitate tracheal intubation.  
- To provide skeletal muscle relaxation during surgery.  
- To provide skeletal muscle relaxation during mechanical ventilation.  
- Use by IV or IM routes only.  
- Reserve use in pediatric patients for emergency tracheal intubation or instances where immediate securing of the airway is necessary (e.g., laryngospasm, difficult airway, full stomach), or for IM use when a suitable vein is inaccessible. [US]

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General principles
- Always individualize dosing after careful patient assessment.  
- Administer only by clinicians experienced in neuromuscular blockade and airway/ventilatory management. Facilities for immediate tracheal intubation, positive-pressure oxygen ventilation, and CO2 elimination must be available.  
- Do not administer succinylcholine chloride before unconsciousness has been induced to avoid patient distress.  
- Visually inspect solution for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration; do not use solutions that are not clear and colorless.  
- Single-dose vial presentations require dilution before use; multiple-dose vials do not require dilution.  
- Dilution for infusion: succinylcholine chloride may be diluted to 1 mg/mL or 2 mg/mL in compatible solutions such as 5% dextrose or 0.9% sodium chloride. Prepare diluted solution for single-patient use only; store refrigerated and use within 24 hours after preparation. [US]  
- Incompatibility: succinylcholine chloride is acidic (pH ~3.0–4.5) and may be incompatible with alkaline solutions (pH > 8.5), e.g., barbiturate solutions — do not mix with alkaline solutions.

### Adult dosing — Intravenous
- For short procedures and tracheal intubation: average IV dose 0.6 mg/kg. Usual adult IV dose range 0.3 mg/kg to 1.1 mg/kg. Onset after IV administration is about 1 minute; maximal blockade may persist ~2 minutes; typical recovery within 4–6 minutes after doses in this range. [US]  
- A 5–10 mg IV test dose may be used to determine individual sensitivity and recovery time. [US]

### Adult dosing — Continuous IV infusion (when prolonged relaxation required)
- Commonly diluted to 1–2 mg/mL for infusion. The 1 mg/mL dilution is often preferable for finer control.  
- Infusion rate range: 0.5 mg/min to 10 mg/min (i.e., 0.5 mL/min to 10 mL/min of a 1 mg/mL solution) to achieve required relaxation. Average adult infusion rate ranges between 2.5 mg/min and 4.3 mg/min.  
- Monitor neuromuscular function with a peripheral nerve stimulator when infusing to avoid overdose and detect Phase II block. [US]

### Adult dosing — Intermittent IV injections for prolonged procedures
- Initial IV injection: 0.3–1.1 mg/kg.  
- Subsequent intermittent IV maintenance doses: 0.04–0.07 mg/kg administered at appropriate intervals. [US]

### Intramuscular dosing (adults and pediatric)
- If a suitable vein is inaccessible, IM administration may be used at doses up to 3–4 mg/kg (infants, older pediatric patients, or adults). Total IM dose should not exceed 150 mg. Onset after IM is usually ~2–3 minutes. [US]

### Pediatric dosing — Intravenous
- For emergency tracheal intubation or immediate securing of airway:  
  - Infants and other small pediatric patients: IV 2 mg/kg.  
  - Older pediatric patients and adolescents: IV 1 mg/kg.  
- Note: effective pediatric doses may be higher than predicted by body-weight scaling: adult 0.6 mg/kg approximates 2–3 mg/kg in neonates and infants up to 6 months, and 1–2 mg/kg in infants up to 2 years. Reserve use in pediatric patients as described in Indications. [US]

### Preparation and handling
- Single-dose vials: dilute before use; multiple-dose vials: do not require dilution.  
- Diluted solutions (1 mg/mL) recommended for infusion preparation; prepare for single-patient use, refrigerate, and use within 24 hours. [US]

### Missed dose instructions
- Not applicable in an acute/operating-room setting; not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
List includes all contraindications present in source material:
- Known or suspected skeletal muscle myopathies (e.g., Duchenne muscular dystrophy) due to risk of acute rhabdomyolysis with hyperkalemia, ventricular dysrhythmias, cardiac arrest, and death. [US]  
- Known hypersensitivity to succinylcholine chloride. Reports of severe anaphylactic reactions have occurred. [US]  
- After the acute phase of injury following major burns, multiple trauma, extensive denervation of skeletal muscle, or upper motor neuron injury — use is contraindicated because of risk of severe hyperkalemia and cardiac arrest. [US]  
- Known or suspected genetic susceptibility to malignant hyperthermia (e.g., family history or suspected RYR1/CACNA1S variants). [US]

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warning
- **VENTRICULAR DYSRHYTHMIAS, CARDIAC ARREST, AND DEATH FROM HYPERKALEMIC RHABDOMYOLYSIS IN PEDIATRIC PATIENTS.** Acute rhabdomyolysis with hyperkalemia leading to ventricular dysrhythmias, cardiac arrest and death has occurred in apparently healthy pediatric patients given succinylcholine chloride who were later found to have undiagnosed skeletal muscle myopathy (most frequently Duchenne muscular dystrophy). When cardiac arrest occurs within minutes after administration in an apparently healthy pediatric patient, immediately institute treatment for hyperkalemia; if signs of malignant hyperthermia are present, treat concurrently. Reserve use in pediatric patients for emergency intubation or immediate airway securing situations, or IM when IV access is unavailable. [US]

### General warnings and precautions
- Anaphylaxis: Severe anaphylactic reactions to neuromuscular blocking agents, including succinylcholine chloride, have been reported and may be life-threatening or fatal. Ensure immediate availability of appropriate emergency treatment. Cross-reactivity among neuromuscular blocking agents has been reported; assess for past reactions to other agents. [US]  
- Risk of death due to medication errors: Accidental administration of a neuromuscular blocking agent to an unintended patient or without appropriate anesthesia/ventilatory support may be fatal. Store and label appropriately to avoid selection errors. [US]  
- Hyperkalemia: Succinycholine chloride may induce serious hyperkalemia resulting in arrhythmias or cardiac arrest, particularly in patients with electrolyte abnormalities, digitalis toxicity, after the acute phase of burns/trauma/denervation, or with chronic neurosurgical/neurological conditions. Consider avoiding use or verify baseline potassium prior to administration in at-risk patients. [US]  
- Malignant hyperthermia (MH): Susceptible individuals may develop MH when given succinylcholine chloride, especially when combined with volatile anesthetics. If MH is suspected, discontinue triggering agents, administer dantrolene sodium IV, and institute supportive measures (oxygenation, ventilation, temperature control, metabolic correction, hemodynamic support, maintain urine output). [US]  
- Bradycardia and asystole: IV bolus administration may cause profound bradycardia or, rarely, asystole; incidence is higher following a second dose and is more common and severe in pediatric patients. Pretreatment with anticholinergic agents (e.g., atropine) may reduce occurrence of bradyarrhythmias. [US]  
- Increase in intraocular pressure: Avoid if an increase in intraocular pressure is undesirable (e.g., penetrating eye injury, narrow-angle glaucoma) unless benefits outweigh risks. [US]  
- Prolonged neuromuscular block due to Phase II block and tachyphylaxis: Prolonged or repeated administration may shift from Phase I (depolarizing) block to Phase II (resembling non-depolarizing block) with risk of prolonged respiratory muscle paralysis. Use peripheral nerve stimulator to detect Phase II; diagnose before attempting reversal with anticholinesterase agents. If anticholinesterase reversal is used, coadminister anticholinergic to reduce cardiac disturbances and observe patient for at least 1 hour after adequate reversal. [US]  
- Reduced plasma cholinesterase activity: Patients with low or atypical plasma cholinesterase activity are at increased risk of prolonged neuromuscular block. Consider this in preoperative assessment; monitor recovery. [US]  
- Risk of additional trauma: Muscle fasciculations and spasms at onset of action may cause additional trauma in patients with fractures or compromised spine. [US]  
- Increase in intracranial pressure: Use with caution in patients where increased intracranial pressure is a concern. [US]  
- Aspiration risk: Succinycholine chloride may increase intragastric pressure; consider aspiration risk in patients with full stomach. [US]  
- Prolonged neuromuscular block in electrolyte abnormalities: Hypokalemia or hypocalcemia may increase risk of prolonged block. [US]  
- Risks due to inadequate anesthesia: Do not administer succinylcholine chloride prior to adequate anesthesia as inadequate anesthesia may lead to patient distress. [US]

### Monitoring requirements
- Continuous monitoring of ventilation, oxygenation, and hemodynamic status during and after administration.  
- Continuous ECG monitoring to detect hyperkalemia-related changes (e.g., peaked T waves) in susceptible circumstances.  
- Use peripheral nerve stimulator for neuromuscular monitoring, particularly during continuous infusion and when prolonged blockade is possible. [US]  
- Be prepared with treatments for hyperkalemia (IV calcium, bicarbonate, insulin + glucose, hyperventilation) and for malignant hyperthermia (dantrolene and supportive care). [US]

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Drugs that may enhance the neuromuscular blocking action of succinylcholine chloride (may potentiate/block clearance): promazine, oxytocin, aprotinin, certain non‑penicillin antibiotics, quinidine, β‑adrenergic blockers, procainamide, lidocaine, trimethaphan, lithium carbonate, magnesium salts, quinine, chloroquine, isoflurane, desflurane, metoclopramide, terbutaline, and drugs that reduce plasma cholinesterase activity. Use with such agents may increase intensity or duration of neuromuscular blockade. [US]  
- Volatile inhalational anesthetics (e.g., isoflurane, desflurane) in combination with succinylcholine chloride increase the risk of malignant hyperthermia. [US]  
- Drugs that reduce plasma cholinesterase activity (e.g., certain esters, anticholinesterases, atypical cholinesterase genotypes) may prolong effect and increase risk of prolonged paralysis; monitor and manage accordingly. [US]  
- Clinically significant interactions: When coadministered with agents that potentiate neuromuscular blockade or impair succinylcholine metabolism, reduce dosing and monitor neuromuscular function; be prepared for prolonged ventilation and supportive care. Specific management guidance beyond monitoring is not specified in available sources.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Indications and dosing: See Section 4 and 6 (Indications and pediatric dosing). Use in pediatric patients is associated with a risk of severe hyperkalemic rhabdomyolysis and cardiac arrest in patients with undiagnosed neuromuscular disease (e.g., Duchenne muscular dystrophy). Reserve use for emergency intubation or when immediate airway control is necessary, or for IM when IV access is not available. IV dose for emergency intubation: infants/small pediatric 2 mg/kg; older pediatric/adolescents 1 mg/kg. Effective pediatric doses may be higher than predicted by weight alone (neonates/infants up to 6 months: 2–3 mg/kg comparable to adult 0.6 mg/kg; infants up to 2 years: 1–2 mg/kg). [US]

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Succinycholine chloride is a depolarizing neuromuscular blocker that acts at the neuromuscular junction to produce skeletal muscle relaxation by sustained depolarization of motor endplates. [US]

### Pharmacodynamics
- Onset and duration (IV): Onset of neuromuscular blockade is approximately 1 minute after IV administration; maximal blockade may persist ~2 minutes; recovery generally within 4–6 minutes for usual IV doses (0.3–1.1 mg/kg). Tachyphylaxis can occur with repeated doses. Prolonged or continuous administration may lead to Phase II block with characteristics similar to non-depolarizing blockade. [US]

### Pharmacokinetics
- Specific ADME parameters (absorption metrics, plasma half-life, volume of distribution, clearance pathways) are not specified in available sources. The source material indicates clinically relevant aspects: rapid onset after IV administration, brief duration for typical single doses, and risk of prolonged effect in patients with reduced plasma cholinesterase activity. [US]

### Pharmacogenomics
- Known or suspected genetic susceptibility to malignant hyperthermia (e.g., variants in RYR1 or CACNA1S) increases risk of MH when exposed to succinylcholine chloride. Atypical plasma cholinesterase genotypes may prolong drug effect. Specific genotype–phenotype details are not specified in available sources. [US]
