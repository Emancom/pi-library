# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of the provided official Product Information (PI). It is designed to provide healthcare professionals with a coherent, clinically accurate overview of the drug's profile.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis for informational purposes and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** A naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism; specifically, a derivative of the amino acid lysine.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance haemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg, administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion. For patients in severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often administered, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours. Daily maintenance doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been used [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight), administered as a slow 2–3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Administration and Monitoring
- **Monitoring:** Plasma carnitine concentrations should be obtained prior to initiating parenteral therapy. Monitoring should continue weekly or monthly, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels (target free carnitine levels: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Titration:** In ESRD patients, dose adjustments may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, potentially down to 5 mg/kg based on trough (pre-dialysis) levels [US].
- **Visual Inspection:** Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** Not specified as a formal contraindication in available sources, though serious reactions have been reported [US].
- **General:** None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have occurred, sometimes within minutes of administration. These have been reported primarily in patients with ESRD on dialysis. Treatment should be discontinued immediately upon a severe reaction [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** While not specific to the injection, chronic high-dose *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) include:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (5–21%), headache (3–37%), infection (10–24%), injection site reaction (27–38%), fever (5–12%), and asthenia (8–12%) [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%), and chest pain (6–15%) [US].
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%) [US].
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%) [US].
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), and dyspnea (3–11%) [US].

### Other Effects
- **Common:** Transient nausea and vomiting [US].
- **Less Frequent:** Gastritis and "fishy" body odor [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended to monitor INR levels frequently after initiating levocarnitine treatment or following dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned under current labeling]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. However, no adequate, well-controlled studies exist in pregnant women. Use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate levocarnitine concentrations in milk increase following exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no evidence of impaired fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg), covering paediatric populations [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific data not provided in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency; however, the injection is specifically indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD patients on dialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, levocarnitine injection increases plasma carnitine concentrations and may alleviate metabolic abnormalities caused by toxic organic acid accumulation [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (n=12), 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma carnitine from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L. 
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing.
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL).
- **Preservative:** None; discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription only medication) [US].
- **Other Jurisdictions:** Not specified in available sources.
