# Editorial Product Information: Sodium Nitroprusside

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information from **Australia (TGA)**. No documents from the US (FDA), European Union (EMA), or other regions were provided for this specific comparison; thus, jurisdictional variations are limited to the available source.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of sodium nitroprusside for use by healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data, safety profiles, and regulatory specifics.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial is a professional synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information mandated for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Sodium nitroprusside dihydrate (Chemical name: Sodium nitrosylpentacyanoferrate (III) dihydrate).
- **Chemical Class:** Hypotensive agent; Nitric oxide donor.
- **Brand Names:** DBL Sodium Nitroprusside [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** C02DD01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Concentrated injection solution: 50 mg/2 mL vial [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypertensive Crises:** For the immediate reduction of blood pressure in hypertensive emergencies. Oral antihypertensive therapy should be initiated concurrently while the emergency is stabilised [Australian].
- **Controlled Hypotension:** To reduce surgical bleeding during anaesthesia when deemed appropriate by the surgeon and anaesthetist [Australian].
- **Acute Cardiac Failure:** Short-term therapy to improve cardiac output and decrease myocardial oxygen demand. Transition to oral therapy is recommended as soon as clinically feasible [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Administration:** **Sodium nitroprusside must be diluted prior to use and administered only as an IV infusion in 5% Glucose in Water.** Direct injection is strictly contraindicated.
- **Dilution:** Typically, 50 mg is diluted in 500–1000 mL of 5% Glucose. The solution is photosensitive and must be protected from light using opaque wrapping (e.g., aluminium foil), though the drip chamber and tubing do not require covering [Australian].
- **Adult Dosing:**
    - **Initial Rate:** 0.3 mcg/kg/min.
    - **Titration:** Upward titration every few minutes until the target blood pressure or the maximum rate is reached.
    - **Average Rate:** Approximately 3 mcg/kg/min.
    - **Maximum Rate:** 10 mcg/kg/min. Infusion at the maximum rate should not exceed 10 minutes; if blood pressure is not controlled, the infusion should be terminated [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Continuous blood pressure monitoring is mandatory (preferably via intra-arterial sensor).
- **Incompatibility:** No other medications should be co-administered in the same infusion line.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Compensatory Hypertension:** Conditions such as arteriovenous shunt or coarctation of the aorta [Australian].
- **High-Output Cardiac Failure:** Such as that associated with endotoxic sepsis [Australian].
- **Physiological Risk:** Moribund patients (ASA Class 5) or those with known inadequate cerebral circulation [Australian].
- **Existing Deficiencies/Disease:**
    - Uncorrected anaemia or hypovolaemia.
    - Severe renal disease.
    - Vitamin B12 deficiency.
    - Leber's optic atrophy or tobacco amblyopia (associated with defective rhodanase) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** Not specified in available sources, though prominent warnings regarding **excessive hypotension** and **cyanide toxicity** are emphasized [Australian].
- **Excessive Hypotension:** Can lead to irreversible ischaemic injury. If blood pressure drops too rapidly, the infusion must be slowed or stopped. Effects usually dissipate within 1–10 minutes.
- **Cyanide Toxicity:** Sodium nitroprusside metabolism releases cyanide ions. Rates >2 mcg/kg/min generate cyanide faster than the body can normally eliminate it. Monitor for metabolic acidosis, "air hunger," confusion, and venous hyperoxaemia (bright red venous blood) [Australian].
- **Thiocyanate Toxicity:** Cyanide is converted to thiocyanate, which is renally excreted. Prolonged infusions or renal impairment increase risk. Monitor levels if infusion lasts >3 days or in patients with renal failure.
- **Intracranial Pressure:** Use with extreme caution in patients with pre-existing elevated intracranial pressure, as the drug may further increase it [Australian].
- **Chemical Integrity:** Discard solutions that are blue, green, or bright red, as these indicate reaction with contaminants [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common/Expected (related to rapid BP drop):** Nausea, retching, diaphoresis, headache, restlessness, muscle twitching, palpitations, and dizziness. These typically resolve upon slowing the infusion rate.
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, bradycardia, ECG changes, and postural hypotension.
- **Dermatological:** Rash, flushing, injection site irritation, and venous streaking.
- **Neurological:** Increased intracranial pressure; miosis and hyperreflexia (associated with thiocyanate toxicity).
- **Gastrointestinal/Endocrine:** Ileus; hypothyroidism (thiocyanate inhibits iodine uptake during prolonged use).
- **Serious:**
    - **Cyanide Poisoning:** Potentially lethal.
    - **Methaemoglobinaemia:** Rare; usually only seen after doses >10 mg/kg.
    - **Thiocyanate Toxicity:** Can be life-threatening at serum levels >200 mg/L [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Antihypertensives & Anaesthetics:** Hypotensive effects are potentiated by ganglion blocking agents, other antihypertensives, volatile liquid anaesthetics (e.g., halothane, enflurane), and circulatory depressants [Australian].
- **Oral Transition:** Transitioning to oral agents may cause a sudden, severe drop in blood pressure.
- **Sodium Thiosulfate:** Co-infusion may reduce cyanide risk but may also potentiate the hypotensive effect of nitroprusside [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. It is known to cross the placenta in animals (ewes) and can cause fatal foetal cyanide levels. Should only be used if the potential benefits outweigh the risks and maternal pH/cyanide levels are monitored.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety is not established; it is not known if the drug or its metabolites are excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available on effects on human fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Considered safe if doses are calculated on a mcg/kg basis and titrated carefully [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased sensitivity to hypotensive effects. Initiate therapy at the lower end of the dosing range [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Increased risk of thiocyanate accumulation. Use with extreme caution; monitor thiocyanate levels daily during prolonged infusions [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Cyanide is metabolised to thiocyanate by the hepatic enzyme rhodanase. Patients with severe liver disease are at higher risk for cyanide toxicity [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Direct-acting vasodilator that relaxes both arterial and venous smooth muscle by releasing nitric oxide (NO). Venous dilation reduces preload; arteriolar dilation reduces afterload.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Rapid onset (within 1–2 minutes) and rapid dissipation of effect upon discontinuation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Distribution:** Approximately coextensive with extracellular space. 
    - **Metabolism:** Reacts with haemoglobin to produce cyanmethaemoglobin and four cyanide ions. 
    - **Elimination:** Cyanide is converted in the liver/kidneys to thiocyanate by rhodanase (using sulfur donors). Thiocyanate is eliminated renally with a half-life of ~3 days (extended in renal failure) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Summary:** The source document references that many trials have verified the metabolic pathways of cyanide and thiocyanate. Tachyphylaxis has been reported in young, vigorous males, though the mechanism remains unknown and is not clearly linked to cyanide levels [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store below 25°C and protect from light. Retain in the original carton until use [Australian].
- **Diluted Solution:** Should be used as soon as possible. If storage is required, hold at 2°C–8°C for no more than 24 hours, protected from light [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Not specified for other regions in provided sources.
