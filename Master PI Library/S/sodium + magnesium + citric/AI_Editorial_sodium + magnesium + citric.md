# Editorial Product Information: sodium + magnesium + citric

**sodium + magnesium + citric**

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): sodium + magnesium + citric
- Chemical class: stimulant laxative (sodium picosulfate component) combined with osmotic laxative (magnesium citrate formed in solution from light magnesium oxide and citric acid)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval: 15 May 2007 [Australian]
- Date of last revision/update of available source PI: 23 December 2024 [Australian]
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources
- Registration dates or PI revision dates for other jurisdictions: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Bowel cleansing prior to radiographic examination, endoscopy or surgery (single indication across available sources).
- Use in children aged 9 years and older is described for bowel-cleansing regimens [Australian].

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General dosing (adults and children ≥ 9 years)
- Typical active strengths per sachet (as described in available sources): sodium picosulfate 10 mg with magnesium citrate formed in solution from light magnesium oxide 3.5 g and citric acid 12 g. (Presentation details: one sachet per administration.)

### Recommended regimens for bowel preparation
- Split‑Dose Regimen (recommended if procedure is scheduled for the afternoon):
  - First sachet: taken the evening before the procedure (example window 17:00–21:00). Follow ingestion with at least five 250 mL servings of clear liquids, spread over several hours.
  - Second sachet: taken the morning of the procedure (5–9 hours before the procedure). After the second sachet, consume at least three 250 mL servings of clear liquids, spread over several hours.
  - Clear liquids may be consumed up until 2 hours before the procedure.

- Day‑Before Regimen:
  - First sachet: taken in the afternoon/early evening (example window 16:00–18:00). Follow with at least five 250 mL servings of clear liquids, spread over several hours.
  - Second sachet: taken late evening approximately 6 hours after the first (example window 22:00–00:00). After the second sachet, consume at least three 250 mL servings of clear liquids, spread over several hours.
  - Clear liquids may be consumed up until 2 hours before the procedure.

### Reconstitution and administration instructions
- Immediately before use, mix the contents of one sachet in approximately 150 mL of water. Stir for 2–3 minutes. The solution should be consumed once prepared. Do not prepare in advance. If the solution becomes warm, wait until it cools sufficiently to drink.

### Additional administration guidance
- Patients should expect frequent, loose bowel movements during the cleansing period.
- Adequate fluid intake is essential while the product is exerting its effect. In addition to the fluid volumes specified after each sachet, maintain normal thirst-driven intake of clear liquids.
- Patients should be advised to consume a balanced electrolyte-containing clear fluid rather than water alone; replacing losses with water alone can cause electrolyte disturbances (e.g., hyponatraemia, hypokalaemia) and, in severe cases, seizures or coma.
- Patients must consume only clear fluids (no solid food or milk) on the day before the procedure until 2 hours before the procedure.
- The total period of bowel cleansing should not exceed 24 hours.

### Titration, missed doses
- Titration schedules and missed dose instructions: Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
(Include all contraindications stated in available sources.)
- Congestive heart failure
- Clinically significant renal impairment
- Known or suspected gastrointestinal obstruction or perforation
- Gastric retention
- Gastro-intestinal ulceration
- Toxic colitis
- Toxic megacolon
- Ileus
- Presence of a stoma
- Nausea and vomiting
- Acute surgical abdominal conditions such as acute appendicitis
- Severe dehydration
- Rhabdomyolysis
- Hypermagnesaemia (pre-existing)
- Active inflammatory bowel disease
- Hypersensitivity to any of the ingredients
- Use in patients with undiagnosed abdominal symptoms is contraindicated
- In patients with severely reduced renal function, accumulation of magnesium in plasma may occur; product should not be used and another preparation should be used in such cases [Australian]

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed / critical warnings
- Life‑threatening dehydration and/or electrolyte disturbances may occur in ‘at risk’ groups. Monitor and use with caution in susceptible patients. **(Critical warning)**

### General warnings and precautions
- Use with caution in patients with recent gastrointestinal surgery, renal impairment, heart disease, or inflammatory bowel disease.
- Monitor renal function and electrolytes in patients with reduced renal function, kidney disease, pre-existing electrolyte disturbances, the elderly, debilitated patients, low bodyweight patients, and others at higher risk of electrolyte disturbance.
- Diabetics may need adjustment of antidiabetic therapy and close glucose monitoring during the preparation period.
- Patients using diuretics or other medications (e.g., corticosteroids, lithium) that affect water/electrolyte balance should be monitored.
- Prompt correction of fluid and electrolyte imbalance is required if signs or symptoms of hypokalaemia or hyponatraemia occur.
- Product is not intended for use as a routine laxative.
- The product contains 5 mmol (195 mg) potassium per sachet; consider this in patients on potassium‑restricted diets or with impaired renal function.
- The flavouring contains lactose (4.5 mg per sachet); patients with rare hereditary problems of galactose intolerance, Lapp lactase deficiency or glucose-galactose malabsorption should not take this medicine.
- Use in the elderly: exercise usual caution; see monitoring recommendations above.
- Use in children: not for use in children under 9 years of age.

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and follow-up assessment of hydration status, serum electrolytes (sodium, potassium, magnesium), and renal function in patients at risk of disturbances (e.g., elderly, impaired renal function, heart disease, on diuretics or other interacting medications).
- Monitor blood glucose in diabetic patients and adjust therapy as necessary.
- Observe for signs of severe diarrhoea, dehydration, or neurological symptoms (e.g., seizures, confusion) — check electrolytes and manage promptly.

### Specific clinical precautions
- The period of bowel cleansing should not exceed 24 hours.
- Avoid use in patients with severely reduced renal function due to risk of magnesium accumulation.
- Caution when co‑prescribing with drugs that may raise the risk of water retention or SIADH (see Interactions).
- The product may reduce absorption of concurrently administered oral medications due to accelerated GI transit; adjust timing as appropriate.

## 7. Adverse Effects

- The most commonly reported adverse reactions: vomiting, nausea, abdominal pain, and headache.
- Hyponatraemia is rare but is the most commonly reported serious adverse reaction; hyponatraemia may occur with or without associated convulsions.
- Seizures, including isolated reports in patients with pre‑existing epilepsy (sometimes without associated hyponatraemia), have been reported.

- Reporting of suspected adverse reactions is encouraged to permit continued safety monitoring.

## 8. Drug Interactions

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- The accelerated gastrointestinal transit caused by the purgative action may reduce absorption of concurrently administered oral medicines (e.g., antiepileptics, oral contraceptives, antidiabetics, some antibiotics); consider timing or temporary management of these therapies.
- Broad‑spectrum antibiotics may reduce the laxative effect by interfering with colonic bacterial conversion of sodium picosulfate to its active metabolite.
- Agents that can chelate magnesium (e.g., tetracyclines, fluoroquinolones, iron preparations, digoxin [note: digoxin chelation is atypical but listed in the source], chlorpromazine and penicillamine) should be administered at least 2 hours before and not less than 6 hours after this product to avoid interaction.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions / additive effects
- Concomitant use with drugs that predispose to hypokalaemia (e.g., loop or thiazide diuretics, corticosteroids) may increase the risk of clinically significant hypokalaemia; monitor electrolytes and consider management.
- Use caution with drugs where hypokalaemia increases risk (e.g., cardiac glycosides); monitor closely.
- Use caution with drugs that may induce SIADH or otherwise increase water retention or hyponatraemia risk (e.g., tricyclic antidepressants, selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors, certain antipsychotics, carbamazepine); such combinations may increase risk of water retention and electrolyte imbalance.
- NSAIDs: use with caution (may affect fluid balance and renal function).

### Management notes
- Where chelation interactions are possible, schedule interacting oral agents at least 2 hours prior to and not less than 6 hours after administration of this product.
- Monitor electrolytes and renal function when concomitant medications increase risk of dehydration, electrolyte disturbance, or renal impairment.
- Bulk‑forming laxatives reduce the efficacy of this product; avoid concurrent use immediately prior to and during preparation.

### Jurisdiction‑specific interactions
- No jurisdiction‑specific differences in interactions were specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

### Pregnancy
- Animal data: Studies with the combination showed no impairment of fertility or embryo‑fetal toxicity. Studies with sodium picosulfate alone showed embryofetal toxicity in rats and rabbits at very high doses.
- Clinical/exposure data: Clinical experience in pregnancy is limited.
- Recommendation: Use with caution during pregnancy, particularly in the first trimester. Use only if clearly indicated.

### Breastfeeding
- Sodium picosulfate and magnesium citrate have not been shown to be excreted in breast milk in the available sources.
- Clinical experience with use in nursing mothers is limited; the product should only be used if clearly indicated.

### Fertility
- Animal studies with the combination showed no impairment of fertility. Sodium picosulfate alone caused embryofetal toxicity only at very high doses in animal studies.

### Pregnancy categories / local classification
- Specific pregnancy category/classifications by jurisdiction (e.g., Australian pregnancy category) are not specified in available sources beyond the cautionary statements above.

## 10. Use in Special Populations

### Paediatric use
- Approved/Described for children aged 9 years and older with the same bowel preparation regimens as adults [Australian].
- Not to be used in children under 9 years of age.

### Elderly
- No specific dose adjustment provided; use caution due to higher risk of dehydration, electrolyte disturbances and co‑morbidities; monitor renal function and electrolytes.

### Renal impairment
- Contraindicated in clinically significant renal impairment and in patients with severely reduced renal function due to risk of magnesium accumulation. Patients with renal impairment should be monitored; consider alternative bowel preparations in severe renal impairment.

### Hepatic impairment
- Dosing adjustments or specific guidance in hepatic impairment: Not specified in available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology

### Mechanism of action
- The preparation combines:
  - Sodium picosulfate: a stimulant prodrug that is metabolised by colonic bacteria to an active metabolite (desacetylbisacodyl / bis(p‑hydroxyphenyl)pyridyl‑2‑methane) that stimulates mucosal receptors and increases intestinal motility.
  - Magnesium citrate (formed in solution from light magnesium oxide and citric acid): an osmotic laxative that increases intraluminal osmotic pressure and retains fluid within the intestinal lumen, promoting bowel evacuation.
- The combined effect results in effective mechanical evacuation of bowel contents.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption / Distribution / Metabolism / Elimination: Not specified in available sources. (Sodium picosulfate is described as pharmacologically inactive until converted by colonic bacteria; the active metabolite is insoluble in water and minimally absorbed from the GI tract.)

### Pharmacodynamics
- Not specified in quantitative detail in available sources beyond the described mechanisms of prokinetic stimulation and osmotic fluid retention.
