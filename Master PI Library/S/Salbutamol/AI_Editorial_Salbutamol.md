# Editorial Product Information: Salbutamol

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of salbutamol (as sulfate) for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource only. It is not a substitute for the official, legally binding Product Information used for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Salbutamol (as sulfate).
- **Chemical Class:** Selective beta-2 adrenoceptor agonist (sympathomimetic amine).
- **Brand Name(s):** Asmol CFC-Free Inhaler [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** R03AC02.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Pressurised metered-dose inhaler (MDI) delivering 100 micrograms of salbutamol (as sulfate) per actuation [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral inhalation.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Alphapharm Pty Ltd trading as Viatris [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Bronchospasm Relief:** Indicated for the symptomatic relief of bronchospasm in patients with asthma or chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) [Australian].
- **Acute Prophylaxis:** Prevention of exercise-induced asthma or bronchospasm triggered by other known stimuli (e.g., allergens) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Children)
- **Relief of Bronchospasm:** 1 or 2 inhalations (100–200 mcg) as required.
- **Maintenance/Frequency:** May be repeated every four hours if necessary. The bronchodilator effect typically lasts at least four hours.
- **Maximum Usage:** Frequent use (e.g., more than twice weekly, excluding prophylactic use) indicates a need for therapy reassessment [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Method:** Inhalation only, breathed through the mouth.
- **Spacers:** Recommended for most patients, particularly those with poor inhaler technique. Use of a spacer reduces oropharyngeal deposition and associated side effects (e.g., hoarseness, candidiasis) [Australian].
- **Device Maintenance:** The plastic actuator should be washed in warm water and air-dried at least once weekly to prevent blockage.
- **Dose Counter:** Inhalers equipped with a counter must be replaced when the counter reaches zero (000) [Australian].

### Specific Instructions
- If a previously effective dose fails to provide relief for at least three hours, the patient should seek immediate medical advice [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to salbutamol or any of the excipients (e.g., norflurane propellant) [Australian].
- **Tocolysis:** Non-intravenous (e.g., inhaled) formulations of salbutamol must not be used to arrest uncomplicated premature labour or threatened abortion [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Asthma Deterioration:** Increasing use of short-acting beta-2 agonists (SABA) is a marker of worsening asthma control. Sudden and progressive deterioration is life-threatening; immediate reassessment and consideration of corticosteroid therapy are required [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** Use with caution in patients with hypertension, myocardial insufficiency, or a history of cardiac arrhythmias. Potential for myocardial lesions exists with high-dose sympathomimetics based on animal studies [Australian].
- **Hypokalaemia:** Potentially serious hypokalaemia may result from beta-2 agonist therapy. This risk is potentiated by hypoxia (in acute severe asthma) and concomitant use of xanthine derivatives, steroids, or diuretics. Monitoring of serum potassium is recommended in these settings [Australian].
- **Metabolic Effects:** May induce reversible metabolic changes, including increased blood glucose levels. Use with caution in patients with diabetes mellitus [Australian].
- **Paradoxical Bronchospasm:** As with other inhalation therapies, paradoxical bronchospasm may occur immediately after dosing. Treatment should be discontinued and alternative therapy established [Australian].
- **Thyroid Disorders:** Administer cautiously to patients with thyrotoxicosis or hyperthyroidism [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally dose-related and characteristic of sympathomimetic agents.

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (>1%)** | Fine skeletal muscle tremor (notably in the hands), tension, headache, palpitations, tachycardia. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Muscle cramps, hyperactivity (reported in children), peripheral vasodilation (resulting in a small reduction in BP and compensatory heart rate increase). |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Hypersensitivity reactions (angioedema, urticaria, bronchospasm, hypotension, collapse), thrombocytopenia, atrial fibrillation, supraventricular tachycardia, extrasystoles. |
| **Other** | Mouth/throat irritation, potentially serious hypokalaemia, lactic acidosis (in the setting of high doses or overdose). |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Beta-Adrenoceptor Blocking Drugs:** Non-selective beta-blockers (e.g., propranolol) inhibit the bronchodilator action of salbutamol and may increase airway resistance. Use together is generally contraindicated [Australian].
- **Other Sympathomimetics:** Concomitant use with other sympathomimetic amines is not recommended due to the risk of excessive sympathetic stimulation [Australian].
- **Potential Interactions:** Animal studies suggest large doses of salbutamol may interact with imipramine, chlordiazepoxide, and chlorpromazine, though human clinical significance is not established [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category A:** Salbutamol has been taken by a large number of pregnant women and women of childbearing age without any proven increase in the frequency of malformations or other direct or indirect harmful effects on the fetus having been observed [Australian].
    - **Note:** Salbutamol crosses the placental barrier. Inhaled salbutamol is generally preferred over systemic forms due to lower maternal serum levels [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Salbutamol is likely secreted in breast milk. Use in nursing mothers is not recommended unless the expected benefit outweighs potential risks to the infant [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies (rats) showed no evidence of impaired fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No special precautions are generally required for children. Rare reports of hyperactivity have been noted [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Initial doses should be lower than the standard adult dose and titrated based on response [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Since approximately 90% of an inhaled dose is metabolised to an inactive form (mostly in the liver), impairment may lead to accumulation of unchanged salbutamol [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Approximately 60-70% of inhaled salbutamol is excreted unchanged in urine. Dosage reduction may be necessary to prevent exaggerated or prolonged effects [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Salbutamol is a relatively selective beta-2 adrenoceptor stimulant. It acts on the beta-2 receptors of bronchial smooth muscle to cause bronchodilation.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** While selective for beta-2 receptors, this selectivity is not absolute; high doses can stimulate beta-1 receptors in the heart. It also causes peripheral vasodilation and may induce metabolic changes (hyperinsulinaemia and hyperglycaemia) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Acts topically on bronchial smooth muscle. Systemic levels are initially undetectable; low concentrations appear in the blood 2-3 hours post-inhalation (likely from swallowed drug) [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Converted to a 4'-O-sulfate ester (inactive) in the liver.
    - **Elimination:** Excreted primarily in the urine (58-78% within 24 hours). The elimination half-life is between 2.7 and 5 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Therapeutic Equivalence:** Clinical programs involving over 1,000 patients (including smokers, elderly, and paediatric patients) established that HFA-134a (CFC-free) formulations are clinically equivalent to older CFC-containing formulations in terms of efficacy and safety for reversible obstructive airways disease [Australian].
- **Safety Profile:** Multi-week studies (up to 12 weeks) showed no detrimental effects on heart rate or safety when switching from CFC to CFC-free inhalers [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C.
- **Protection:** Protect from frost and direct sunlight.
- **Note:** The therapeutic effect may decrease if the canister is cold.
- **Warning:** Pressurised container. Do not expose to temperatures exceeding 50°C. Do not puncture or burn the canister even when empty [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 3 (Pharmacist Only Medicine) [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Available via pharmacist consultation in Australia; however, specific brand packs may be listed on the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG) as AUST R 335970 [Australian].
