# Editorial Product Information: Somatrogon

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- At the time of this writing, PI documents from other major regulatory authorities (e.g., US FDA, European EMA, Health Canada) were not provided for comparison. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved labeling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Somatrogon (a recombinant human growth hormone [hGH] analog; glycoprotein).
- **Chemical Class:** Growth hormone long-acting analog.
- **Brand Name(s):** NGENLA [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Solution for injection in a single-patient-use pre-filled pen:** 
        - 24 mg/1.2 mL (delivers doses in 0.2 mg increments) [Australian].
        - 60 mg/1.2 mL (delivers doses in 0.5 mg increments) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Paediatric Growth Hormone Deficiency:** Long-term treatment of paediatric patients (aged 3 years and older) with growth disturbance caused by insufficient secretion of growth hormone (GH) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Paediatric):** The recommended dose is **0.66 mg/kg body weight** administered **once weekly** [Australian].
- **Switching from Daily GH:** Therapy may be initiated the day following the final dose of a daily growth hormone product [Australian].
- **Dose Adjustments:**
    - Adjustments should be based on growth velocity, body weight, and serum Insulin-like Growth Factor-1 (IGF-1) concentrations.
    - **IGF-1 Monitoring:** Samples must be drawn **4 days** after the previous dose. 
    - If IGF-1 concentrations exceed the mean reference value for age/sex by >2 standard deviation scores (SDS), the dose should be reduced by 15% [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Administer via SC injection into the abdomen, thighs, buttocks, or upper arms.
    - **Rotation:** Injection sites must be rotated weekly to prevent lipoatrophy.
    - If multiple injections are required for a single dose, each must be given at a different site.
- **Missed Dose Instructions:**
    - If a dose is missed, it should be administered as soon as possible if within **3 days** of the scheduled date.
    - If >3 days have passed, skip the missed dose and resume the next dose on the regularly scheduled day.
    - At least 3 days (72 hours) must elapse between any two doses [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Active Malignancy:** Contraindicated in patients with active tumours or malignancy [Australian].
- **Acute Critical Illness:** Contraindicated in patients with complications from open-heart or abdominal surgery, multiple accidental trauma, or acute respiratory failure [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to somatrogon or any excipients (including metacresol) [Australian].
- **Closed Epiphyses:** Growth promotion must not be used in children with evidence of epiphyseal growth plate closure [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Acute Critical Illness:** Increased mortality has been reported with pharmacologic doses of daily GH in critically ill patients. Benefit-risk must be weighed if a patient becomes critically ill while on somatrogon [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions (e.g., anaphylaxis, angioedema) have occurred with GH products. Discontinue immediately if systemic reactions occur [Australian].
- **Neoplasm:** Childhood cancer survivors have an increased risk of second neoplasms (especially intracranial meningiomas following radiation). Monitor for new lesions [Australian].
- **Benign Intracranial Hypertension (IH):** Although not reported in somatrogon clinical trials, IH with papilledema and visual changes has occurred with daily GH. Perform fundoscopy if symptoms arise; discontinue treatment temporarily if IH is confirmed [Australian].
- **Glucose Metabolism:** GH may induce insulin resistance and hyperglycaemia. Monitor patients with glucose intolerance or diabetes mellitus; adjustment of antidiabetic therapy may be required [Australian].
- **Scoliosis:** Rapid growth may lead to progression of scoliosis; monitor for signs during treatment [Australian].
- **Thyroid Function:** Untreated hypothyroidism may impair the response to somatrogon. Monitor thyroid function and treat as indicated [Australian].
- **Prader-Willi Syndrome (PWS):** Not indicated for PWS unless the patient also has growth hormone deficiency. Reports of sudden death exist for PWS patients with risk factors (e.g., obesity, sleep apnoea) using daily GH [Australian].
- **Epiphyseal Disorders:** Slipped capital femoral epiphysis may occur during rapid growth or in endocrine disorders. Evaluate any child presenting with a limp or hip/knee pain [Australian].
- **Myositis:** Potentially related to the preservative metacresol. If myalgia or severe injection site pain occurs, consider myositis; switch to metacresol-free products if confirmed [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by frequency based on clinical trial data (n=265) [Australian]:

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (≥10%)** | Injection site reactions (25.1%), Headache (10.7%), Pyrexia (10.2%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Anaemia, Eosinophilia, Hypothyroidism, Allergic conjunctivitis, Arthralgia, Pain in extremity |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Adrenal insufficiency, Generalised rash |
| **Frequency Not Known** | Lipoatrophy*, Epiphysiolysis (slipped capital femoral epiphysis)* |

*\*Identified via post-marketing surveillance.*

**Injection Site Reactions (ISRs):** Include pain, erythema, pruritus, swelling, and bruising.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Cytochrome P450 (CYP3A4):** In vitro data suggest somatrogon weakly induces CYP3A4. This may decrease the plasma levels of CYP3A4 substrates (e.g., sex steroids, corticosteroids, anticonvulsants, ciclosporin). Monitor clinical response [Australian].
- **Glucocorticoids:** GH may inhibit the enzyme 11βHSD-1, reducing the conversion of cortisone to cortisol. Somatrogon may unmask secondary hypoadrenalism or require higher glucocorticoid replacement doses [Australian].
- **Insulin/Antidiabetic Agents:** Adjustments may be needed due to GH-induced insulin resistance [Australian].
- **Oral Estrogen:** May reduce the IGF-1 response to somatrogon. Females on oral estrogen may require higher somatrogon dosages [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category B1.** There are no studies in pregnant women. Animal studies (rats) showed no embryofetal development effects at 50 times the human exposure, though postnatal body weight was increased in offspring. Use only if clearly needed [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if somatrogon is excreted in human milk. Use with caution during breastfeeding only if the benefit outweighs the potential risk to the infant [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No impact on fertility indices was observed in animal studies [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:**
    - **Aged 3–11 years:** Efficacy and safety established.
    - **Aged 12–18 years:** Limited data available.
    - **Under 3 years:** Safety and efficacy not established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No data available [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not studied in patients with renal impairment [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not studied in patients with hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Somatrogon is a long-acting recombinant hGH analog. It consists of the hGH amino acid sequence fused with three copies of the C-terminal peptide (CTP) of human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG). This fusion increases the half-life by reducing renal clearance and increasing glycosylation. It binds the GH receptor to activate the STAT5b signalling pathway, increasing IGF-1 to stimulate linear growth [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak concentrations occur 6 to 18 hours post-dose.
    - **Distribution:** Apparent central volume of distribution is ~0.812 L/kg.
    - **Metabolism:** Classical protein catabolism.
    - **Elimination:** Effective half-life is approximately **28.3 hours**. It remains in circulation for about 6 days after the last dose [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Phase 3 Study:** A 12-month, multi-centre, open-label, non-inferiority study compared once-weekly somatrogon (0.66 mg/kg/week) to once-daily somatropin (Genotropin) in 224 pre-pubertal paediatric patients with GHD.
- **Primary Outcome:** Annualised Height Velocity (HV).
    - **Somatrogon:** 10.10 cm/year.
    - **Somatropin:** 9.78 cm/year.
    - **Difference:** 0.33 cm/year (95% CI: -0.24, 0.89), meeting non-inferiority criteria.
- **Long-term Data:** In open-label extensions, patients receiving somatrogon for 5 years showed a progressive gain in height SDS with no new safety signals [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Before First Use:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (refrigerate). Do not freeze. Store in original carton to protect from light [Australian].
- **After First Use:** The pen may be stored for up to **28 days** in a refrigerator (2°C to 8°C).
- **Handling:**
    - May be held at room temperature (up to 32°C) for no more than 2 hours per use.
    - Discard the pen after 28 days of use or after **5 uses**, whichever comes first.
    - Do not shake; shaking can damage the protein [Australian].
