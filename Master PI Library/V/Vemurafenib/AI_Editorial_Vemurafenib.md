# Editorial Product Information: Vemurafenib

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically focused overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved documentation.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Vemurafenib (a protein kinase inhibitor).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Zelboraf** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01XE15.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Film-coated tablets, 240 mg [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Roche Products Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metastatic Melanoma:** Treatment of unresectable stage IIIC or stage IV metastatic melanoma in patients confirmed to be positive for the BRAF V600 mutation [Australian].
- **Note:** Efficacy and safety have not been convincingly established in patients with tumours expressing BRAF V600 non-E mutations [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Standard Dose:** 960 mg (four 240 mg tablets) administered twice daily. 
- **Total Daily Dose:** 1920 mg.
- **Treatment Duration:** Continue until disease progression or development of unacceptable toxicity.

### Administration Instructions
- **Timing:** Take the first dose in the morning and the second in the evening, approximately 12 hours apart.
- **Food Interaction:** Doses must be taken either at least 1 hour before a meal or at least 2 hours after a meal [Australian].
- **Instructions:** Tablets must be swallowed whole with water; do not chew or crush.

### Missed Dose and Vomiting
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, it may be taken up to 4 hours prior to the next scheduled dose. Do not take two doses at the same time.
- **Vomiting:** If vomiting occurs after administration, do not take an additional dose; resume treatment as usual with the next scheduled dose.

### Dose Modifications
Dose reductions below 480 mg twice daily are not recommended. 
- **First dose reduction:** 720 mg twice daily.
- **Second dose reduction:** 480 mg twice daily.
- **Third appearance of intolerable Grade 2/3 or any Grade 4 toxicity:** Permanent discontinuation.
- **Cutaneous Squamous Cell Carcinoma (cuSCC):** No dose modification or interruption is required for these lesions; they are managed by simple excision.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to vemurafenib or any of the excipients in the formulation (e.g., croscarmellose sodium, iron oxide red) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Secondary Malignancies:** 
    - **Cutaneous Squamous Cell Carcinoma (cuSCC):** Occurs in approximately 20% of patients. Dermatologic evaluation is required prior to initiation and every 2 months during therapy.
    - **Non-cuSCC:** Rare cases (e.g., head and neck) reported. Monitor with head/neck exams and chest CT scans every 6 months.
    - **New Primary Melanoma:** Monitor and manage with excision.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis and DRESS, have been reported. Discontinue permanently for severe reactions.
- **Dermatologic Reactions:** Rare cases of Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS) and Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN) reported.
- **Ophthalmologic Reactions:** Uveitis, iritis, and retinal vein occlusion have occurred. Monitor for eye pain or vision changes.
- **QT Prolongation:** Exposure-dependent prolongation reported. 
    - **Monitoring:** ECG and electrolytes required at baseline, after dose changes, monthly for 3 months, then every 3 months.
    - **Threshold:** Do not initiate if QTc > 500 ms.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Liver injury and laboratory abnormalities occur. Monitor liver enzymes and bilirubin monthly.
- **Pancreatitis:** Investigate unexplained abdominal pain promptly.
- **Radiation Toxicity:** Potential for radiation recall and sensitisation. Use with caution.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Table (Summary)
| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Arthralgia (56-70%), fatigue, rash, photosensitivity, alopecia, nausea, diarrhoea, headache, pruritus, vomiting, skin papilloma, hyperkeratosis, palmar-plantar erythrodysaesthesia, GGT increased. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Basal cell carcinoma, folliculitis, vasculitis, dizziness, VIIth nerve paralysis [Australian]. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, uveitis, retinal vein occlusion, pancreatitis [Australian]. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Sarcoidosis [Australian]. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma (Grade 3), severe hypersensitivity, and QTc prolongation.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP1A2 Substrates:** Vemurafenib is a potent inhibitor. Use with caution with narrow therapeutic index drugs (e.g., theophylline, tizanidine, clozapine, warfarin).
- **CYP3A4 Substrates:** Vemurafenib is a CYP3A4 inducer; may decrease exposure of substrates (e.g., midazolam, oral contraceptives).
- **CYP2D6:** May increase exposure of substrates (e.g., dextromethorphan) via an unknown mechanism.
- **CYP2C9 Substrates:** May increase exposure (e.g., S-warfarin). Monitor INR.
- **Transporters:** 
    - Vemurafenib is an inhibitor of **P-gp** (increases digoxin exposure by ~1.8-fold) and **BCRP**.
    - Vemurafenib is a substrate of **CYP3A4** and **P-gp**.
- **Management:** Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, clarithromycin) or inducers (e.g., rifampicin, St John's Wort) may significantly alter vemurafenib concentrations and should be used with caution.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Vemurafenib may cause fetal harm. Effective contraception is required for females of childbearing potential and males during treatment and for at least 6 months after discontinuation.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if vemurafenib is excreted in human milk. A risk to newborns/infants cannot be excluded. Discontinue breastfeeding or the drug, considering the benefit to the mother.
- **Fertility:** No specific human studies; no histopathological findings in reproductive organs in animal models at subclinical levels.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients < 18 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific dose adjustment is required for patients ≥ 65 years. Elderly patients may be more prone to certain adverse events like cuSCC and decreased appetite.
- **Renal Impairment:** No starting dose adjustment for mild/moderate impairment. Insufficient data for severe impairment (CrCl < 29 mL/min).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No starting dose adjustment for mild/moderate impairment. Insufficient data for severe hepatic impairment; caution is advised.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selective inhibitor of the mutated BRAF serine-threonine kinase (activating codon 600 mutations). It blocks constitutive activation of the MAP-kinase pathway, inhibiting cellular proliferation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Median Tmax ~4 hours. Steady state reached by Day 15. Bioavailability ~58%.
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein bound (>99%). Apparent volume of distribution ~91 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily CYP3A4-mediated metabolism; however, the parent compound remains the predominant component (95%) in plasma.
    - **Elimination:** ~94% excreted in faeces; <1% in urine. Mean elimination half-life is ~57 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **BRIM3 (Phase III):** Compared vemurafenib to dacarbazine in 675 treatment-naïve patients.
    - **OS:** Significantly improved; Hazard Ratio (HR) of 0.37 at interim analysis. Median OS was 13.6 months for vemurafenib vs 9.7 months for dacarbazine.
    - **PFS:** Median 5.3 months vs 1.6 months (HR 0.26).
- **NP22657 (Phase II):** Conducted in 132 patients who failed prior therapy. Confirmed overall response rate (BORR) was 52%.
- **Brain Metastases:** Response rate of 18% in both previously treated and untreated cohorts.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30 °C.
- **Conditions:** Store in the original blister pack and outer carton to protect from moisture.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to expiry date on packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (S4)** (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
