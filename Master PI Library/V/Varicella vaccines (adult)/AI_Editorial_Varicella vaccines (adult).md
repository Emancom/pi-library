# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local clinical guidelines required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. 
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Metabolic):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It may be administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
    - **Loading Dose:** Often provided in patients with severe metabolic crisis.
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered.
    - **Frequency:** Dosing should be every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- **Compatibility:** Stable when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) and stored at 25°C for up to 24 hours in PVC bags [US].

### Monitoring
- It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to starting therapy. 
- **Target Range:** Free carnitine levels should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma concentrations is advised.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Hypersensitivity Reactions
- Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have occurred, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions manifest within minutes of intravenous administration. If a severe reaction occurs, treatment must be discontinued immediately [US].

### Seizures
- Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].

### General Precautions
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** The safety of high-dose **levocarnitine** has not been evaluated in renal impairment. Chronic high doses may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting are common. Less frequent effects include gastritis and abdominal pain [US].
- **Body Odor:** A distinct body odor has been observed, likely due to the metabolite trimethylamine [US].

### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported at a frequency ≥5% in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials (regardless of causality) include:
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%), Arrhythmia (2%).
- **Body as a Whole:** Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%), Chest pain (12%), Flu syndrome (25%), Headache (22%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%), Nausea (8%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), Cough increase (9%), Dyspnea (7%).
- **Neurological:** Dizziness (13%), Paresthesia (5%), Insomnia (4%) [US].

### Post-Marketing Experience
- Seizures (see Section 5).
- Severe hypersensitivity reactions [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concomitantly with **levocarnitine**. It is recommended to monitor INR levels frequently after initiating treatment or adjusting the dose [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Not specified in available sources (No US lettered category provided in text). Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant should be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, S. cerevisiae, S. pombe) were negative. Long-term animal studies for carcinogenic potential have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is based on mg/kg body weight, applicable to paediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the source document [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** See Warnings and Precautions regarding metabolite (TMA/TMAO) accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Levocarnitine** is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This process provides substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, **levocarnitine** clears acylCoA compounds by forming acylcarnitine, which is then excreted, helping to prevent autointoxication and acidosis [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Bioavailability:** Absolute bioavailability of oral formulations is approximately 15–16% [US].
- **ESRD Studies:** Clinical trials showed that 20 mg/kg administered three times weekly post-dialysis significantly increased plasma carnitine concentrations. Trough levels rose from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks of therapy [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only. NDC 36000-368-10 [US].
