# Editorial Product Information: Voclosporin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority:
    - **United States** (Food and Drug Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of voclosporin for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Practitioners should consult the current PI approved in their specific region before clinical use.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Voclosporin (a calcineurin-inhibitor immunosuppressant).
- **Chemical Class:** Calcineurin inhibitor; a cyclic undecapeptide. 
- **Brand Name(s):** **LUPKYNIS** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 7.9 mg soft gelatin capsules (oval, pink/orange, imprinted with "VCS").
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Manufactured for Aurinia Pharmaceuticals Inc. (Canada); Distributed by Aurinia Pharma U.S., Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Active Lupus Nephritis (LN):** Indicated for use in combination with a background immunosuppressive therapy regimen for the treatment of adult patients with active LN [US].
- **Limitations of Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in combination with cyclophosphamide; therefore, use in this combination is not recommended [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:** The recommended starting dose is 23.7 mg (three 7.9 mg capsules) taken orally twice daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Must be swallowed whole; do not open, crush, or divide.
    - Administer on an empty stomach consistently, either 1 hour before or 2 hours after a meal.
    - Maintain a 12-hour schedule whenever possible (minimum 8 hours between doses).
- **Dosing in Combination Therapy:** Use in conjunction with mycophenolate mofetil (MMF) and corticosteroids.
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as possible within 4 hours. If more than 4 hours have passed, skip the dose and resume the normal schedule. Do not double the dose.
- **Dose Modifications (Renal/eGFR):**
    - Establish baseline eGFR and blood pressure (BP) before initiation.
    - If eGFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m² and reduced from baseline by >20% to <30%, reduce the dose by 7.9 mg twice daily.
    - If eGFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m² and reduced from baseline by ≥30%, discontinue treatment. Re-initiation at a lower dose (7.9 mg twice daily) may be considered only if eGFR returns to ≥80% of baseline.
- **Discontinuation:** If therapeutic benefit is not achieved by 24 weeks, consider discontinuing the drug [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Concomitant use with drugs such as ketoconazole, itraconazole, and clarithromycin is contraindicated due to the risk of significantly increased voclosporin exposure and subsequent nephrotoxicity [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known history of serious or severe hypersensitivity to voclosporin or any of its excipients (e.g., anaphylaxis) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [US]:**
    - **Malignancies:** Increased risk of developing lymphomas and other malignancies, particularly of the skin.
    - **Serious Infections:** Increased risk of bacterial, viral, fungal, and protozoal infections (including opportunistic infections) that may lead to hospitalisation or death.
- **Nephrotoxicity:** May cause acute and/or chronic nephrotoxicity. Regular eGFR monitoring is required (every two weeks for the first month, then every four weeks for the first year, and quarterly thereafter) [US].
- **Hypertension:** Common side effect; monitor BP every two weeks for the first month and regularly thereafter. Discontinue if BP >165/105 mmHg or in hypertensive emergency.
- **Neurotoxicity:** Risk of tremors, paresthesia, headache, and severe events like Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES), seizures, or coma.
- **Hyperkalemia:** Calcineurin inhibitors may cause serious hyperkalemia; monitor serum potassium, especially when used with potassium-sparing agents.
- **QTc Prolongation:** Voclosporin may prolong the QTc interval in a dose-dependent manner. Caution is required in patients with pre-existing risk factors (e.g., hypokalemia, bradycardia).
- **Immunisations:** Avoid live attenuated vaccines during treatment. Inactivated vaccines may have reduced immunogenicity.
- **Pure Red Cell Aplasia (PRCA):** Reported with other calcineurin inhibitors; consider discontinuation if PRCA is diagnosed [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** 
    - Decreased glomerular filtration rate (26%)
    - Hypertension (19%)
    - Diarrhea (19%)
    - Headache (15%)
    - Anemia (12%)
    - Cough (11%)
- **Common (1–10%):**
    - Urinary tract infection (10%)
    - Abdominal pain/Upper abdominal pain
    - Dyspepsia
    - Alopecia
    - Renal impairment
    - Mouth ulceration
    - Fatigue
    - Tremor
    - Acute kidney injury
    - Decreased appetite
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Serious infections (pneumonia, gastroenteritis), serious renal events (acute kidney injury), and neurotoxic events including seizures and PRES [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:**
    - **Strong (e.g., ketoconazole):** Contraindicated.
    - **Moderate (e.g., verapamil, fluconazole, diltiazem):** Reduce voclosporin dose to 15.8 mg in the morning and 7.9 mg in the evening.
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Avoid concomitant use with strong or moderate inducers (e.g., rifampin, efavirenz) as they decrease voclosporin efficacy.
- **OATP1B1/B3 Substrates:** Voclosporin increases exposure to substrates like simvastatin. Limit simvastatin dose to 20 mg daily when co-administered.
- **P-gp Substrates:** Increases exposure to P-gp substrates (e.g., digoxin). Monitor for toxicity and reduce substrate dose as needed.
- **Food/Beverage:** Avoid grapefruit and grapefruit juice [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned (PLLR Risk Summary used). 
    - **Recommendation:** Use in pregnant women is recommended to be avoided due to the alcohol content (21.6 mg of ethanol per capsule) in the formulation, which may pose a risk to the fetus [US]. 
    - **Background Risks:** Lupus nephritis increases the risk of preeclampsia and preterm birth.
- **Breastfeeding:** Small amounts of voclosporin are excreted in human milk (Relative Infant Dose estimated at 1.4%). The risk to the infant should be weighed against the clinical benefit to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no effect on male or female fertility at doses up to 9–16 times the maximum recommended human dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include sufficient numbers of patients aged ≥65 years to determine if they respond differently. Dosing should be cautious, generally starting at the low end of the range [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Baseline eGFR ≤45 mL/min/1.73 m²:** Not recommended unless benefit outweighs risk.
    - **Severe Renal Impairment (at baseline):** Recommended dose is 15.8 mg twice daily [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild to Moderate (Child-Pugh A/B):** Recommended dose is 15.8 mg twice daily.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Use is not recommended [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A calcineurin inhibitor that suppresses T-cell activation by inhibiting the dephosphorylation of the transcription factor NFATc. It also stabilizes podocytes in the glomeruli.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Median Tmax is 1.5 hours on an empty stomach. Food reduces Cmax and AUC.
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (97%). Partitions into red blood cells.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized by CYP3A4.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily via feces (92.7%); minimal renal excretion (2.1%). Half-life is approximately 30 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Study (Study 1/AURORA):** A 52-week, randomised, double-blind trial (n=357). Patients received voclosporin 23.7 mg BID or placebo, both on a background of MMF (2 g/day) and low-dose oral corticosteroids following IV methylprednisolone.
- **Primary Outcome:** Complete Renal Response (CRR) at Week 52. 
    - **Results:** 40.8% of the voclosporin group achieved CRR vs. 22.5% in the placebo group (Odds Ratio 2.7, p < 0.001) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at controlled room temperature 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C.
- **Handling:** Keep capsules in the original aluminum blister packaging until use to protect from the environment.
- **Safety:** Provided in child-resistant packaging [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription drug).
