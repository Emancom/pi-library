# Editorial Product Information: Vinblastine Sulfate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory information into a clinically focused format.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and is not intended to substitute for the official PI or prescribing information in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should always refer to the current local PI when prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Vinblastine sulfate (a vinca alkaloid derived from *Vinca rosea*).
- **Brand Names:** **DBL Vinblastine Injection** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Solution for injection: 10 mg/10 mL [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** **Intravenous (IV) use only.**
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Vinblastine** is indicated for the following conditions [Australian-approved]:
- **Hodgkin’s Disease:** Stages III and IV, often in combination therapy (e.g., ABVD or modified MOPP protocols).
- **Advanced Testicular Carcinoma:** In combination with cisplatin and bleomycin.
- **Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma:** Palliative treatment of lymphocytic and histiocytic lymphoma.
- **Mycosis Fungoides:** Advanced stages.
- **Kaposi’s Sarcoma.**
- **Histiocytosis X.**
- **Choriocarcinoma:** When resistant to other chemotherapeutic agents.
- **Breast Carcinoma:** Unresponsive to endocrine surgery and hormonal therapy.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing Principles
- **Route:** Strict intravenous administration. **Fatal if given by any other route.** 
- **Frequency:** Once every 7 days.
- **Threshold for Subsequent Dosing:** A dose must not be administered until the white cell count (WBC) has returned to at least 4.0 x 10⁹/L (4000/mm³), even if 7 days have elapsed.

### Recommended Incremental Dosage Schedule
Therapy should be initiated with the first dose, and increments should only occur if the WBC remains above 3.0 x 10⁹/L. The maintenance dose is typically one increment smaller than the dose required to produce leukopenia.

| Dose Increment | Adults (mg/m² BSA) | Children (mg/m² BSA) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **First Dose** | 3.7 | 2.5 |
| **Second Dose** | 5.5 | 3.75 |
| **Third Dose** | 7.4 | 5.0 |
| **Fourth Dose** | 9.25 | 6.25 |
| **Fifth Dose** | 11.1 | 7.5 |
| **Maximum Dose** | **18.5** | **12.5** |

### Administration Instructions
- **Preparation:** Use the 'minibag' technique (dilution in small volume IV bags). **Syringes should not be used** to prevent accidental spinal (intrathecal) administration.
- **Diluents:** Compatible with 0.9% sodium chloride or 5% glucose.
- **Infusion:** Infusion should be completed within 24 hours of preparation. Prolonged infusion or large dilution volumes (100–250 mL) are not recommended due to the increased risk of irritation and extravasation.
- **Extravasation Risk:** Extremely important to ensure proper needle positioning. If extravasation occurs, stop the infusion immediately and inject the remaining dose into a different vein. Local hyaluronidase injection and heat application may be used to disperse the drug.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to vinblastine or any excipients.
- **Leukopenia:** Pre-existing significant leukopenia.
- **Infection:** Presence of bacterial infection (must be controlled before therapy initiation).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning: Fatal if Given Intrathecally
> **FOR INTRAVENOUS USE ONLY.** Fatal if given by any other route. Intrathecal administration has resulted in death. In case of accidental intrathecal administration, immediate neurosurgical intervention is required (CSF removal and irrigation with lactated Ringer’s solution) to attempt to prevent ascending paralysis.

### General Warnings
- **Expert Supervision:** Must be administered only by physicians experienced in cytotoxic chemotherapy.
- **Handling:** Pregnant staff should not handle the preparation.
- **Myelosuppression:** Dose-limiting factor (primarily leukopenia). Nadir typically occurs 5–10 days post-administration; recovery usually occurs within 7–14 days.
- **Liver Disease:** Hepatic impairment may alter biliary elimination, significantly increasing toxicity to peripheral nerves.
- **Eye Contamination:** Accidental contact may cause severe irritation or corneal ulceration.

### Monitoring Requirements
- Regular White Blood Cell (WBC) counts are mandatory to determine patient sensitivity and dose titration.
- Monitoring for infection is critical, especially if WBC drops below 2.0 x 10⁹/L.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The incidence of side effects is generally dose-related and usually does not persist beyond 24 hours, except for neurological effects.

| System Organ Class | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Blood/Lymphatic** | **Leukopenia (dose-limiting)**, anaemia, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia. |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea, vomiting, constipation, adynamic ileus, vesiculation of the mouth, anorexia, abdominal pain, rectal bleeding. |
| **Nervous System** | Numbness, paraesthesiae, peripheral neuritis, mental depression, loss of deep tendon reflexes, headache, convulsions. |
| **Cardiac/Vascular** | Hypertension, Raynaud's phenomenon; myocardial infarction/CVA (when used in combination with cisplatin/bleomycin). |
| **Skin/Subcutaneous** | **Alopecia (common)**, vesiculation of the skin, phlebitis/cellulitis at the injection site. |
| **Other** | Malaise, weakness, jaw pain, bone pain, VIIIth nerve injury (ototoxicity, vertigo). |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Phenytoin:** **Vinblastine** may reduce phenytoin blood levels, potentially increasing seizure activity.
- **Mitomycin:** Combination may cause fatal acute respiratory distress or pulmonary infiltrates. Avoid this combination.
- **Cisplatin:** May increase plasma concentrations of **Vinblastine**.
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Concurrent use with inhibitors of the CYP3A subfamily (e.g., Erythromycin) may increase toxicity and severity of side effects.
- **Live Vaccines:** May result in potentially life-threatening infections due to immunosuppression.
- **Radiation Therapy:** Delay use if the liver is included in the radiation field.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D [Australian].** Potential for genotoxicity, teratogenicity, and embryo toxicity. Should not be used unless the benefit outweighs the risk. Patients must be informed of the hazard to the fetus.
- **Contraception:** Females should use effective contraception during and for at least 7 months after the last dose. Males should use effective contraception for at least 4 months after the last dose [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety is not established. Discontinue breastfeeding during treatment and for 1 week following the last dose [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Male and female fertility may be compromised (e.g., aspermia). Fertility preservation should be discussed prior to treatment.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing established based on BSA (see Section 3).
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available, but use should be avoided in older patients suffering from cachexia or skin ulceration [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Risk-benefit assessment required; dosage modification is necessary as the drug is primarily cleared via the liver.
- **Renal Impairment:** Risk-benefit assessment required, particularly in patients with a history of urate renal stones.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A cytotoxic drug that arrests cell growth at the metaphase by interfering with microtubule assembly. It is more active against rapidly dividing cells.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Rapidly cleared from blood and distributed to tissues; crosses the blood-brain barrier poorly.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised by the liver to desacetyl-vinblastine (more active than the parent compound).
    - **Excretion:** Slow excretion in urine and faeces via the bile.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Intact Vials:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze).
- **Light Protection:** Vials should be protected from light.
