# Editorial Product Information: Vedolizumab

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: Product Information — ENTYVIO (Vedolizumab).
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Vedolizumab (a humanized IgG1 monoclonal antibody).
- **Chemical Class**: Integrin receptor antagonist (specifically targeting $\alpha_4\beta_7$ integrin).
- **Brand Name**: **ENTYVIO** [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: Not specified in available source.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**:
    - **Powder for Injection (IV)**: 300 mg lyophilised cake or powder in a single-use vial [Australian].
    - **Solution for Injection (SC)**: 108 mg in 0.68 mL in a single-dose pre-filled syringe or pre-filled pen [Australian].
- **Route of Administration**: Intravenous (IV) infusion or Subcutaneous (SC) injection.
- **Sponsor**: Takeda Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Ulcerative Colitis (UC)**: Treatment of adult patients with moderate to severe UC who have had an inadequate response with, lost response to, or are intolerant to conventional therapy or a tumour necrosis factor-alpha (TNF$\alpha$) antagonist [Australian-approved].
- **Crohn’s Disease (CD)**: Treatment of adult patients with moderate to severe CD who have had an inadequate response with, lost response to, or are intolerant to conventional therapy or a TNF$\alpha$ antagonist [Australian-approved].
- **Pouchitis**: Treatment of adult patients with moderate to severe chronic pouchitis who have undergone proctocolectomy and ileal pouch anal anastomosis (IPAA) for UC, and have had an inadequate/lost response to antibiotic therapy [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults $\ge$ 18 years)
- **Initiation**: Treatment must be initiated and supervised by specialist healthcare professionals experienced in treating UC, CD, or pouchitis.

### Ulcerative Colitis and Crohn’s Disease
- **Intravenous Regimen**: 
    - **Induction**: 300 mg by IV infusion at Weeks 0, 2, and 6.
    - **Maintenance**: 300 mg every 8 weeks thereafter.
    - **Note (CD)**: Patients not responding by Week 6 may benefit from a dose at Week 10.
    - **Loss of Response**: Some patients may benefit from increasing frequency to 300 mg every 4 weeks.
- **Subcutaneous Maintenance**:
    - **Regimen**: 108 mg SC once every 2 weeks.
    - **Transition**: The first SC dose is administered in place of the next scheduled IV dose after at least two IV infusions.
- **Discontinuation**: Stop treatment if no clinical response is observed by Week 14.

### Pouchitis
- **Intravenous Regimen**: 300 mg at Weeks 0, 2, and 6, then every 8 weeks thereafter.
- **Coadministration**: Initiate in parallel with standard of care antibiotics (e.g., ciprofloxacin for 4 weeks).
- **Discontinuation**: Consider stopping if no benefit is seen by Week 14.

### Administration Instructions
- **IV Infusion**: Reconstitute with 4.8 mL sterile water; dilute in 250 mL of 0.9% saline or Lactated Ringer’s. Infuse over 30 minutes. Do not administer as a bolus.
- **SC Injection**: Administer into the thigh, abdomen, or upper arm. Patients/caregivers may self-inject after training.

### Missed Dose
- **SC Injection**: Inject the missed dose as soon as possible and then continue every 2 weeks.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to vedolizumab or any excipients.
- Active severe infections (e.g., sepsis, tuberculosis, opportunistic infections, serious abscesses).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Infusion-Related Reactions (IRR)**: Hypersensitivity and IRRs (including anaphylaxis) have been reported. Most are mild to moderate. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and initiate emergency treatment.
- **Infections**: Vedolizumab is gut-selective, but systemic infections (including septic shock) have occurred. Avoid initiation in patients with active severe infections.
- **Tuberculosis (TB)**: Screen for TB before treatment. If latent TB is diagnosed, begin anti-TB therapy before starting vedolizumab.
- **Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy (PML)**: While no cases were identified in clinical trials, the risk cannot be ruled out. Monitor for new or worsening neurological signs. Withhold dosing at the first sign of PML and refer to a neurologist.
- **Vaccinations**: Bring all patients up to date with immunisations prior to treatment. Live and oral vaccines should only be used if benefits outweigh risks.
- **Malignancy**: Risk may be increased in UC/CD patients; immunomodulators may further increase this risk.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Data (IV Regimen)
| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Nasopharyngitis, Headache, Arthralgia |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Nausea, Pyrexia, Upper Respiratory Tract Infection, Fatigue, Cough, Bronchitis, Influenza, Back pain, Rash, Pruritus, Sinusitis, Oropharyngeal pain, Pain in extremities |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Infusion-related reactions (serious <1%) |

### Selected Serious Adverse Effects
- **Liver Injury**: Rare reports of transaminase/bilirubin elevations with hepatitis.
- **Infections**: Rate of serious infections was 0.07 per patient-year.
- **SC-Specific**: Injection site reactions occurred in 5.1% of patients (typically mild/moderate).
- **Post-Marketing**: Acute pancreatitis and tubulointerstitial nephritis have been reported.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Biological Products**: No clinical trial data exist for concomitant use with biological immunosuppressants (e.g., natalizumab, rituximab). Use is not recommended.
- **Corticosteroids/Immunomodulators**: Concomitant use (azathioprine, 6-mercaptopurine, methotrexate) did not have a clinically meaningful effect on vedolizumab pharmacokinetics.
- **Vaccines**: May reduce the seroconversion rate of oral killed cholera vaccine. Impact on other mucosal vaccines is unknown.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: **Category B1** [Australian]. Limited human data. IgG antibodies cross the placenta. Animal studies (monkeys/rabbits) at 24-fold/8-fold clinical doses showed no evidence of teratogenicity. Use only if benefits outweigh risks.
- **Breastfeeding**: Vedolizumab is detected in human milk (approx. 0.4% to 2.2% of maternal serum concentration). The effect on infants is unknown.
- **Fertility**: No human data available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric (<18 years)**: Safety and efficacy have not been established. No data available.
- **Elderly ($\ge$ 65 years)**: No dose adjustment required.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment**: Not formally studied. No dose recommendations can be made.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Binds specifically to the $\alpha_4\beta_7$ integrin, inhibiting its interaction with mucosal addressin cell adhesion molecule-1 (MAdCAM-1). This prevents the migration of memory T lymphocytes into inflamed gastrointestinal tissue.
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Distribution**: Volume of distribution is ~5 L. Does not cross the blood-brain barrier.
    - **Elimination**: Half-life is approximately 26 days. Clearance is ~0.162 L/day.
    - **Bioavailability**: SC bioavailability is ~75%.
- **Pharmacodynamics**: Gut-selective action. Does not affect leukocyte counts in peripheral blood or CSF.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **GEMINI I (UC)**: Demonstrated superior clinical response (47% vs 26%) and remission (17% vs 5%) at Week 6 compared to placebo.
- **VISIBLE 1 (UC - SC)**: Confirmed 108 mg SC every 2 weeks as effective maintenance, with 46.2% in clinical remission at Week 52.
- **GEMINI II & III (CD)**: Vedolizumab was superior to placebo for clinical remission at Week 6 and maintenance at Week 52.
- **EARNEST (Pouchitis)**: Vedolizumab was superior to placebo for clinical remission at Week 14 (31.4% vs 9.8%).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials and Pre-filled Pens/Syringes**: Store at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze. Protect from light.
- **SC Room Temperature Stability**: Can be kept at room temperature (up to 25°C) for up to 7 days; discard if not used [Australian].
- **Reconstituted IV Solution**: Stable for 8 hours at 2°C to 8°C.
- **Diluted IV Solution (Saline)**: Total combined stability (vial + bag) is 12 hours at room temperature or 24 hours at 2°C to 8°C.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia**: Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4).
