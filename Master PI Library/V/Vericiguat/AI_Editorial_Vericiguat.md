# Editorial Product Information: Vericiguat

## Editorial Note
- This editorial was developed using official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a coherent reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved PI for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Vericiguat, a soluble guanylate cyclase (sGC) stimulator.
- **Brand Name(s):** **VERQUVO** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** C01DX22.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 2.5 mg (white, round), 5 mg (brown-red, round), and 10 mg (yellow-orange, round).
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Bayer Australia Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Heart Failure:** Indicated as an adjunct to standard of care therapy for the treatment of symptomatic chronic heart failure in adults with a reduced ejection fraction (LVEF < 45%). Patients should be stabilised following a recent decompensation event that necessitated hospital admission and/or intravenous diuretic therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The recommended starting dose is 2.5 mg once daily.
- **Titration:** The dose should be doubled approximately every 2 weeks, as tolerated by the patient, to reach the target maintenance dose of 10 mg once daily.
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Tablets must be taken **with food**. 
    - For patients with swallowing difficulties, the tablet may be crushed and mixed with water immediately prior to administration.
- **Stabilisation:** Before initiation, volume status and diuretic therapy should be optimised to stabilise the patient after the decompensation event. Initiation is not recommended if systolic blood pressure (SBP) is < 100 mmHg.
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as remembered on the same day. Patients should not take two doses on the same day to make up for a missed dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to vericiguat or any of the excipients (including lactose monohydrate).
- Concomitant use of other soluble guanylate cyclase (sGC) stimulators, such as **riociguat**.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Symptomatic Hypotension:** Vericiguat may cause symptomatic hypotension. Caution is advised in patients with hypovolaemia, severe left ventricular outflow obstruction, resting hypotension, autonomic dysfunction, or those on concomitant antihypertensives/organic nitrates. If hypotension occurs, consider adjusting diuretics or treating underlying causes (e.g., dehydration).
- **Phosphodiesterase-5 (PDE-5) Inhibitors:** Concomitant use with PDE-5 inhibitors (e.g., sildenafil) is not recommended due to a potential increased risk of symptomatic hypotension.
- **Monitoring:** Clinical monitoring of blood pressure is required during initiation and titration.

## 6. Adverse Effects

| **Anemia** | Common (1–10%) | 9.6% vs. 7.4% |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **sGC Stimulators (e.g., Riociguat):** Contraindicated due to additive vasodilatory effects.
    - **PDE-5 Inhibitors:** Not recommended; potential for increased hypotension.
    - **Nitrates:** Co-administration with short-acting nitrates was well tolerated in heart failure patients. Limited experience exists with long-acting nitrates.
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **Gastric pH:** Vericiguat exposure is not affected by PPIs, H2-antagonists, or antacids when taken with food.
    - **Transporters/Enzymes:** Vericiguat is a substrate of P-gp and BCRP. It is primarily metabolised via UGT1A9 and UGT1A1. No clinically meaningful interactions were observed with ketoconazole, rifampicin, or midazolam.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category D [Australian]:** There are no data on the use of vericiguat in pregnant women. Animal studies have shown developmental toxicity (malformations and abortions at maternally toxic doses). Vericiguat should not be used during pregnancy.
    - **Contraception:** Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during treatment.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if vericiguat is excreted in human milk; however, it is present in the milk of lactating rats. A risk to the infant cannot be excluded. A decision must be made to either discontinue breastfeeding or abstain from therapy.
- **Fertility:** No human data available. Animal studies showed no effect on male or female fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients < 18 years of age. Bone toxicity (hypertrophy of growth plate) was observed in rapidly growing adolescent rats.
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment is required.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **eGFR ≥ 15 mL/min/1.73m²:** No dose adjustment required.
    - **eGFR < 15 mL/min/1.73m² or Dialysis:** Not recommended (not studied).
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild to Moderate (Child-Pugh A/B):** No dose adjustment required.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Not recommended (not studied).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Vericiguat directly stimulates soluble guanylate cyclase (sGC) independently of nitric oxide (NO) and also sensitises sGC to endogenous NO. This increases intracellular cGMP, leading to smooth muscle relaxation and vasodilation, thereby improving myocardial and vascular function.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** High absolute bioavailability (93%) when taken with food. Tmax is approximately 4 hours (fed).
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is ~98% (primarily albumin). Vd is ~44 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via glucuronidation to an inactive N-glucuronide.
    - **Elimination:** Half-life is ~30 hours in heart failure patients. Excretion is via urine (53%, mostly as metabolite) and faeces (45%, mostly as unchanged drug).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **VICTORIA Trial:** A Phase 3, randomised, double-blind study of 5,050 patients with symptomatic chronic HF (NYHA II–IV) and LVEF < 45% following a recent worsening event.
- **Outcomes:** Vericiguat (target 10 mg) was superior to placebo in reducing the risk of the primary composite endpoint of cardiovascular (CV) death or hospitalisation for heart failure (HR: 0.90; 95% CI: 0.82, 0.98; p=0.019). The annualised absolute risk reduction (ARR) was 4.2%.
