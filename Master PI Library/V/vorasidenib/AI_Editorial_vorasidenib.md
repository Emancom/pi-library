# Editorial Product Information: Vorasidenib

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) documents from the following jurisdictions:
    - **Australian** (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
    - **US** (Food and Drug Administration)
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals by integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements from the provided sources.
- This document is an editorial synthesis intended for reference and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI when prescribing or dispensing in a specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Vorasidenib (present as hemicitric acid hemihydrate)
- **Chemical Class:** Isocitrate dehydrogenase-1 (IDH1) and isocitrate dehydrogenase-2 (IDH2) inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** **VORANIGO**
- **ATC Code:** L01XM04
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 10 mg and 40 mg.
    - **10 mg tablet:** White to off-white, round, imprinted with ‘10’ in black ink.
    - **40 mg tablet:** White to off-white, oblong, imprinted with ‘40’ in black ink.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Servier Laboratories (Aust.) Pty. Ltd. [Australian]; Servier Pharmaceuticals LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Vorasidenib** is indicated for the treatment of adult and paediatric patients 12 years of age and older with Grade 2 astrocytoma or oligodendroglioma with a susceptible isocitrate dehydrogenase-1 (IDH1) or isocitrate dehydrogenase-2 (IDH2) mutation.

- **Specific Criteria:** The mutation must be detected by an approved diagnostic test.
- **Surgical Context:** Indicated following surgery, including biopsy, sub-total resection, or gross total resection [US].
- **Clinical Nuance:** Indicated for patients who are not in need of immediate chemotherapy or radiotherapy following surgical intervention [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Adolescents $\ge$ 12 years)
Dosage is determined by body weight:
- **Patients weighing $\ge$ 40 kg:** 40 mg taken orally once daily.
- **Patients weighing < 40 kg:** 20 mg taken orally once daily.

### Administration Instructions
- **Dose Timing:** Take at approximately the same time each day.
- **Food Requirements:** Tablets should be taken on an empty stomach, at least 2 hours before and 1 hour after food [Australian]. *Note: US labeling states the drug may be taken with or without food, however, clinical data shows high-fat meals increase $C_{max}$ by 3.1-fold.*
- **Method:** Swallow tablets whole with a glass of water. Do not split, crush, or chew.
- **Special Instruction:** Do not swallow the silica gel desiccant found in the bottle.

### Missed and Vomited Doses
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as possible if within 6 hours of the scheduled time. If more than 6 hours have passed, skip the dose and resume the next day at the regular time.
- **Vomited Dose:** If vomiting occurs after administration, do not take a replacement dose; resume the regular schedule the following day.

### Monitoring Requirements
- Assess complete blood counts and blood chemistries, including liver function tests (LFTs), prior to initiation.
- **Frequency:** Every 2 weeks for the first 2 months, then monthly for the first 2 years of treatment, and as clinically indicated thereafter.
- More frequent monitoring is required for patients developing transaminase elevations.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to vorasidenib or any of the excipients (e.g., lactose) [Australian].
- **US Jurisdiction:** The US PI lists no specific contraindications.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Hepatotoxicity
**Vorasidenib** can cause significant hepatic transaminase elevations, which may lead to hepatic failure, hepatic necrosis, and autoimmune hepatitis.
- **Clinical Findings:** In clinical trials, approximately 18.6% of patients experienced ALT > 3 x ULN. 
- **Management:** Dose interruption, reduction, or permanent discontinuation is required based on the severity of ALT/AST and bilirubin elevations (see Section 3 for titration). 
- **Conservative Approach:** Permanently discontinue if ALT or AST > 20 x ULN, or if transaminase elevations are accompanied by bilirubin > 2 x ULN.

### Embryo-Fetal Toxicity
Vorasidenib can cause fetal harm. 
- **Requirements:** Verify pregnancy status in females of reproductive potential prior to initiation.
- **Contraception:** Use effective non-hormonal contraception during treatment and for 3 months after the last dose. Vorasidenib may render hormonal contraceptives ineffective.

### Other Precautions
- **Lactose:** Contains lactose; not recommended for patients with rare hereditary galactose intolerance or glucose-galactose malabsorption [Australian].
- **Sodium:** Contains < 1 mmol sodium (23 mg) per tablet ("sodium-free") [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Data
Adverse effects are categorised by frequency from the INDIGO trial:

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Fatigue (37%), Headache (28%), Musculoskeletal pain (26%), Diarrhoea (25%), Nausea (22%), Seizure (16%), COVID-19 (33% [US]), Abdominal pain (13%). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Decreased appetite (9%), Vomiting (7%), Constipation (13% [US]). |

### Laboratory Abnormalities
- **Very Common (>10%):** Increased ALT (59%), Increased AST (46%), Increased GGT (38%), Increased Potassium (23%), Increased Hemoglobin (13%), Decreased Lymphocytes (11%), Decreased Neutrophils (14%).
- **Common (1–10%):** Increased Creatinine (11%), Increased Glucose (10%), Increased ALP (10%).

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Seizure:** Reported in 16% of patients (Grade 3 or 4 in 4.2%).
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Serious ALT increases occurred in 0.6% of patients.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Strong CYP1A2 Inhibitors (e.g., fluvoxamine, ciprofloxacin):** Avoid concomitant use. Ciprofloxacin increases vorasidenib AUC by 153%; fluvoxamine is predicted to increase it $\ge$ 5-fold.
- **Moderate CYP1A2 Inducers (e.g., phenytoin, rifampicin, smoking tobacco):** Avoid concomitant use as these may decrease vorasidenib plasma concentrations and reduce efficacy.
- **CYP3A Substrates:** Vorasidenib is a strong inducer of CYP3A4. Avoid use with sensitive CYP3A4 substrates where minimal concentration changes can lead to therapeutic failure (e.g., alfentanil, ciclosporin, hormonal contraceptives).
- **BCRP Substrates:** Vorasidenib is a BCRP inhibitor. Monitor for increased effects/toxicity of BCRP substrates (e.g., rosuvastatin).

### Management Note
- **Hormonal Contraceptives:** Patients using hormonal contraceptives must add or switch to a barrier or non-hormonal method during treatment and for 3 months following the final dose.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category D:** Vorasidenib is suspected to cause an increased incidence of human fetal malformations or irreversible damage. 
    - **US Perspective:** Based on animal data, vorasidenib is considered to cause embryo-fetal toxicity.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if vorasidenib is excreted in human milk. Due to potential serious adverse reactions in the breastfed child, breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for **2 months** after the final dose.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies indicate potential for impaired fertility in both males and females; effects in rats were not reversible.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy are established in patients $\ge$ 12 years old. Safety and efficacy have not been established in children < 12 years.
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment is recommended for patients $\ge$ 65 years. Limited data exists for patients $\ge$ 75 years.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - No adjustment for mild to moderate impairment ($CL_{cr} > 40$ mL/min).
    - Not studied in severe impairment ($CL_{cr} \le 40$ mL/min) or dialysis; use with caution and monitor for adverse effects [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - No adjustment for mild or moderate (Child-Pugh A or B) impairment.
    - Not studied in severe (Child-Pugh C) impairment. Use is **not recommended** in patients with pre-existing severe hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Vorasidenib is a small-molecule dual inhibitor of mutant IDH1 and IDH2 enzymes. It prevents the overproduction of the oncogenic metabolite 2-hydroxyglutarate (2-HG), thereby restoring cellular differentiation and reducing malignant cell proliferation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is 34%. Median $T_{max}$ is 2 hours.
    - **Distribution:** High protein binding (97%). Brain tumour-to-plasma ratio is 1.6.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by CYP1A2, with minor contributions from CYP2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, and 3A.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 10 days (238 hours). Excreted primarily in faeces (85%).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **INDIGO Trial (Study AG881-C-004):** A Phase 3, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of 331 patients with Grade 2 IDH-mutant glioma.
- **Primary Outcome:** Radiographic progression-free survival (PFS).
- **Results:** Vorasidenib demonstrated a significant improvement in PFS compared to placebo (HR 0.39; 95% CI: 0.27, 0.56). Median PFS was 27.7 months for vorasidenib versus 11.1 months for placebo. 
- **Secondary Outcome:** Time to next intervention (TTNI) was significantly longer in the vorasidenib group (HR 0.26).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C [Australian]. Store at 20°C to 25°C, with excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C [US].
- **Open Shelf Life:** Once the bottle is opened, the product should be used within 60 days [Australian].
- **Handling:** Keep the bottle tightly closed to protect from moisture.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Poison Schedule S4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **United States:** Rx Only.
