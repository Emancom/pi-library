# Editorial Product Information: Verapamil

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis was developed using official Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical guidance into a single, structured reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Verapamil hydrochloride
- **Chemical Class:** Calcium ion influx inhibitor (slow channel blocker or calcium ion antagonist); phenylalkylamine derivative.
- **Brand Names:** **ANPEC** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** C08DA01
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Immediate-release film-coated tablets: 40 mg and 80 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Viatris Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Verapamil is indicated for the management of the following conditions [Australian]:
- **Hypertension**
- **Angina Pectoris:** Including angina of effort, angina at rest, and vasospastic (Prinzmetal’s variant) angina.
- **Tachyarrhythmias:** Including paroxysmal supra-ventricular tachycardia (PSVT).
- **Atrial Fibrillation/Flutter:** Control of rapid ventricular response (except when associated with accessory pathways).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Initial Dose:** 80 mg administered two or three times daily [Australian].
- **Maintenance Dose:** Usually 160 mg twice daily. The dose should be individualised based on patient response and indication.
- **Maximum Dose:** Not explicitly capped in the provided source, though titration to two 80 mg tablets three times daily is common [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing
- **Dose Range:** 40 mg to 360 mg per day, administered in two or three divided doses, adjusted according to age and clinical response [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets must be swallowed whole and are not designed to be broken [Australian].

### Hepatic Impairment
- Due to extensive first-pass metabolism, verapamil should be initiated with caution. In severe hepatic dysfunction, the elimination half-life is significantly prolonged (14 to 16 hours). It is recommended to administer approximately 30% of the standard dose to these patients [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
Verapamil is contraindicated in the following scenarios [Australian]:
- **Severe Left Ventricular Dysfunction:** Ejection fraction <35% or pulmonary wedge pressure >20 mmHg.
- **Hypotension:** Systolic blood pressure <90 mmHg or cardiogenic shock.
- **Conduction Defects:** Sick sinus syndrome or second- or third-degree AV block (unless a functioning artificial pacemaker is present).
- **Accessory Bypass Tracts:** Patients with atrial fibrillation/flutter and conditions such as Wolff-Parkinson-White (WPW) or Lown-Ganong-Levine (LGL) syndromes, due to the risk of ventricular fibrillation.
- **Specific Drug Combinations:** 
    - Concomitant use with **ivabradine**.
    - Simultaneous initiation with **dabigatran etexilate**.
    - Initiation in patients following major orthopaedic surgery already treated with **dabigatran etexilate**.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to verapamil or any tablet excipients.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Heart Failure:** Verapamil has a negative inotropic effect. Avoid in severe left ventricular dysfunction. Use with extreme caution in patients receiving beta-blockers [Australian].
- **Acute Myocardial Infarction:** Use with caution if complicated by bradycardia, hypotension, or LV dysfunction.
- **Atrioventricular Block/Asystole:** Verapamil slows conduction through the AV and SA nodes. Development of high-grade block requires dose discontinuation. Risk is higher in older patients or those with sick sinus syndrome.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Elevations in transaminases (SGOT, SGPT) and bilirubin have occurred. Periodic monitoring of liver function is prudent [Australian].
- **Neuromuscular Transmission:** Use cautiously in patients with myasthenia gravis, Lambert-Eaton syndrome, or Duchenne muscular dystrophy, as verapamil may decrease transmission and prolong recovery from neuromuscular blockers like vecuronium.
- **Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (IHSS):** Serious adverse effects, including pulmonary oedema and severe hypotension, have occurred in patients with significant outflow obstruction.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Verapamil is generally well-tolerated. Adverse effects are typically associated with dose titration [Australian].

### Common Adverse Effects (1–10%)
| Adverse Effect | Immediate Release Frequency | Sustained Release Frequency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Constipation** | 7.3% | 4.1% |
| **Dizziness** | 3.3% | 2.6% |
| **Nausea** | 2.7% | 1.2% |
| **Hypotension** | 2.5% | <1.0% |
| **Headache** | 2.2% | 1.2% |
| **Oedema** | 1.9% | <1.0% |
| **Fatigue/Tiredness**| 1.7% | 1.0% |

### Serious or Notable Effects
- **Cardiovascular:** Bradycardia (HR <50 bpm), AV block, congestive heart failure, pulmonary oedema, asystole [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gingival hyperplasia (rare/long-term), ileus.
- **Skin:** Stevens-Johnson syndrome, erythema multiforme, rash.
- **Post-Marketing Reports:** Tetraparesis (associated with combined colchicine use), seizures, hyperkalaemia, and galactorrhea [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions (CYP3A4 and P-gp)
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors** (e.g., Erythromycin, Ritonavir, Claritromycin): May increase verapamil plasma levels.
- **CYP3A4 Inducers** (e.g., Rifampicin, Phenobarbital, Phenytoin, St John’s Wort): May decrease verapamil levels.
- **Verapamil as Inhibitor:** Verapamil inhibits CYP3A4 and P-glycoprotein, increasing levels of substrates such as **ciclosporin**, **sirolimus**, **everolimus**, **tacrolimus**, **buspirone**, **midazolam**, **theophylline**, and **dabigatran** [Australian].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Beta-Blockers:** Additive negative effects on heart rate, conduction, and contractility. Combined use requires close monitoring.
- **Ivabradine:** Contraindicated due to additive heart rate lowering effects.
- **Digoxin:** Verapamil increases serum digoxin levels by 50–75%. Reduce digoxin dose when initiating verapamil.
- **Antiarrhythmics:** Potential for additive depressant effects with amiodarone, flecainide, or quinidine.
- **Alcohol:** Verapamil inhibits alcohol metabolism, increasing CNS depressant effects and blood alcohol levels.
- **Grapefruit Juice:** Increases verapamil levels; avoid concomitant intake [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Verapamil crosses the placental barrier. It may produce fetal hypoxia due to maternal hypotension. Use only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Verapamil is excreted in human milk (approx. 0.01–1% of maternal dose). Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in infants, use only if essential for maternal welfare [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no impairment in female rats at 5.5x the human dose; effects on male fertility are unknown.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for use in children with a dose range of 40–360 mg/day [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Elimination half-life may be prolonged. Monitor closely for altered pharmacokinetics.
- **Renal Impairment:** Verapamil is not removed by haemodialysis. While pharmacokinetics are largely unchanged in end-stage renal failure, caution and close monitoring for PR interval prolongation are advised [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Verapamil inhibits the transmembrane influx of calcium ions into conductile and contractile myocardial cells and vascular smooth muscle cells. This results in arterial vasodilation (reducing afterload) and negative chronotropic, dromotropic, and inotropic effects.

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** >90% absorbed, but bioavailability is low (20–35%) due to extensive first-pass metabolism [Australian].
- **Distribution:** 90% protein bound. Peak plasma levels occur 1–2 hours after oral administration.
- **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised in the liver (12 metabolites). **Norverapamil** is the major active metabolite (20% of the activity of the parent drug).
- **Elimination:** 70% excreted in urine (3–4% as unchanged drug) and 16% in faeces. Half-life is 2.8–7.4 hours after a single dose, increasing to 4.5–12 hours with chronic dosing [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
Clinical trial data cited in the source primarily focused on safety and adverse effect profiles across a population of 4,954 patients for immediate-release and 4,538 patients for sustained-release formulations [Australian].
