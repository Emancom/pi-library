# Editorial Product Information: Vosevi (sofosbuvir/velpatasvir/voxilaprevir)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis incorporates Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdictions: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)** and the **United States (Food and Drug Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide healthcare professionals with a harmonised, clinically accurate, and comprehensive overview of the product by integrating data from multiple regulatory authorities.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Always consult the current local PI for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Sofosbuvir (400 mg), Velpatasvir (100 mg), and Voxilaprevir (100 mg).
- **Chemical Class:** 
    - Sofosbuvir: Nucleotide analogue NS5B polymerase inhibitor.
    - Velpatasvir: NS5A replication complex inhibitor.
    - Voxilaprevir: NS3/4A protease inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** **Vosevi**
- **ATC Code:** J05AP56 [Australian]
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Fixed-dose combination film-coated tablets. Each tablet contains 400 mg sofosbuvir, 100 mg velpatasvir, and 100 mg voxilaprevir.
- **Physical Description:** Beige, capsule-shaped, debossed with "GSI" on one side and "3" on the other.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Gilead Sciences.

## 2. Approved Indications
**Vosevi** is indicated for the treatment of chronic hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection in adults without cirrhosis or with compensated cirrhosis (Child-Pugh A) who have:
- Genotype 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, or 6 infection and have previously failed a treatment regimen containing an NS5A inhibitor.
- Genotype 1a or 3 infection and have previously failed a treatment regimen containing sofosbuvir without an NS5A inhibitor.

*Note:* Additional benefit of **Vosevi** over sofosbuvir/velpatasvir was not demonstrated in patients with genotype 1b, 2, 4, 5, or 6 previously treated with sofosbuvir without an NS5A inhibitor.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** One tablet taken orally once daily.
- **Administration Instructions:** Must be taken with food.
- **Treatment Duration:** 12 weeks for all approved populations.
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed and it is within 18 hours of the normal time, the patient should take the tablet as soon as possible and then take the next dose at the usual time. If more than 18 hours have passed, the patient should wait and take the next dose at the usual time. Patients should not take a double dose.
- **Testing Prior to Initiation:** All patients must be screened for evidence of current or prior Hepatitis B Virus (HBV) infection (HBsAg and anti-HBc) before starting therapy.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to any active ingredient or excipient.
- **Strong P-gp/CYP Inducers:** Coadministration with rifampicin (rifampin) is contraindicated due to the risk of significantly reduced plasma concentrations and loss of efficacy.
- **Statins:** Coadministration with rosuvastatin is contraindicated [Australian]. (Note: US labeling classifies this as "not recommended").

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Risk of Hepatitis B Virus (HBV) Reactivation [US]:** HBV reactivation has been reported in HCV/HBV co-infected patients undergoing or completing DAA therapy who were not receiving HBV antiviral therapy. Some cases resulted in fulminant hepatitis, hepatic failure, and death. Monitor co-infected patients for hepatitis flare or reactivation during and after treatment.
- **Serious Symptomatic Bradycardia:** Cases of symptomatic bradycardia and those requiring pacemaker intervention have occurred when amiodarone is coadministered with a sofosbuvir-containing regimen. Coadministration is not recommended. If no alternative exists, continuous in-patient cardiac monitoring is required for the first 48 hours, followed by daily heart rate monitoring for at least 2 weeks.
- **Hepatic Decompensation/Failure:** Post-marketing cases, including fatalities, have been reported, primarily in patients with advanced liver disease/cirrhosis. Monitor for signs of decompensation (jaundice, ascites, encephalopathy). Discontinue treatment if clinical signs of decompensation occur.
- **Dysglycaemia in Diabetics:** Rapid reduction in HCV viral load may improve glucose metabolism, potentially leading to symptomatic hypoglycaemia. Monitor blood glucose levels closely during the first 3 months of therapy; adjusting diabetic medications may be necessary.
- **Reduced Therapeutic Effect:** Avoid use with moderate to potent inducers of P-gp or CYP (e.g., carbamazepine, St. John’s wort).

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally mild (Grade 1 or 2).
- **Very Common (>10%):**
    - Headache (21–27%)
    - Fatigue (17–24%)
    - Diarrhoea (13–20%)
    - Nausea (10–14%)
- **Common (1–10%):**
    - Abdominal pain, asthenia, insomnia, back pain.
- **Laboratory Abnormalities:**
    - **Lipase elevations:** Isolated, asymptomatic elevations >3x ULN (approx. 2%).
    - **Creatine Kinase:** Elevations ≥10x ULN (approx. 1%).
    - **Bilirubin:** Increases up to 1.5x ULN (up to 13% in cirrhotic patients) due to voxilaprevir-mediated inhibition of OATP1B1/3; not associated with jaundice and resolves post-treatment.
- **Post-marketing Experience:** Rash, angioedema, Stevens-Johnson syndrome (rare).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Acid Reducing Agents:**
    - **Antacids:** Separate dose by 4 hours.
    - **H2-receptor antagonists:** Take simultaneously or 12 hours apart; dose should not exceed famotidine 40 mg BID equivalent.
    - **Proton pump inhibitors:** Take with food simultaneously; dose should not exceed omeprazole 20 mg equivalent.
- **Antiarrhythmics:** Amiodarone (not recommended); Digoxin (monitor levels).
- **Anticoagulants:** Dabigatran etexilate (monitor for redness/bleeding, especially in renal impairment).
- **Statins:**
    - Rosuvastatin: Contraindicated [Australian]; Not recommended [US].
    - Atorvastatin: Do not exceed 20 mg daily.
    - Pravastatin: Do not exceed 40 mg daily.
    - Others (Fluvastatin, Lovastatin, Simvastatin): Use lowest possible dose.
- **Immunosuppressants:** Cyclosporine (not recommended due to significantly increased voxilaprevir exposure).
- **Antiretrovirals:** Coadministration with atazanavir, lopinavir, or efavirenz-containing regimens is not recommended.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B1:** No evidence of increased fetal damage in animal studies. 
    - **US Perspective:** No adequate human data; use only if the potential benefit outweighs the risk. Animal studies showed no adverse developmental effects at exposures significantly higher than human therapeutic doses.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if the components are excreted in human milk. A risk to newborns cannot be excluded. The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be considered along with the mother’s clinical need for **Vosevi**.
- **Fertility:** No effects on mating or fertility were observed in animal studies (rats/mice).

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients under 18 years of age.
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required. Clinical trials included 17% of patients ≥65 years, with no significant differences in safety or efficacy compared to younger patients.
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **US Recommendation:** No dose adjustment required for any degree of renal impairment, including End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) on dialysis.
    - **Australian Recommendation:** No dose adjustment for mild/moderate impairment; safety and efficacy not established in severe impairment (eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73m²) or ESRD.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild (Child-Pugh A):** No dose adjustment required.
    - **Moderate/Severe (Child-Pugh B or C):** Not recommended due to significantly higher (up to 6-fold) voxilaprevir exposure and risk of hepatic decompensation.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Sofosbuvir:** Pantypic NS5B RNA-dependent RNA polymerase inhibitor (chain terminator).
    - **Velpatasvir:** Pantypic NS5A inhibitor.
    - **Voxilaprevir:** Pantypic non-covalent, reversible NS3/4A protease inhibitor.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Sofosbuvir Tmax 2h; Velpatasvir/Voxilaprevir Tmax 4h. Food increases absorption of all components (Voxilaprevir up to 435%).
    - **Distribution:** High protein binding (>99% for VEL/VOX; 61–65% for SOF).
    - **Metabolism:** Sofosbuvir is extensively metabolised in the liver to the active triphosphate, then to the inactive GS-331007. Velpatasvir and Voxilaprevir undergo slow CYP-mediated metabolism (CYP2B6, 2C8, 3A4).
    - **Elimination:** GS-331007 (sofosbuvir metabolite) is primarily renally excreted (80%); Velpatasvir and Voxilaprevir are primarily excreted via biliary/fecal routes as parent drugs.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **POLARIS-1:** 12 weeks of **Vosevi** in NS5A-experienced patients (GT 1-6). SVR12 was 96%.
- **POLARIS-4:** 12 weeks of **Vosevi** in DAA-experienced patients (non-NS5A, mostly SOF-experienced) (GT 1-4). SVR12 was 98% for **Vosevi** vs 90% for SOF/VEL.
- **POLARIS-2:** 8 weeks of **Vosevi** in DAA-naïve patients (GT 1-6). SVR12 was 95% (failed non-inferiority to 12 weeks of SOF/VEL).
- **POLARIS-3:** 8 weeks of **Vosevi** in DAA-naïve patients with GT 3 and compensated cirrhosis. SVR12 was 96%.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C (86°F).
- **Packaging:** Dispense only in the original container. Keep the bottle tightly closed; contains a silica gel desiccant.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4).
- **United States:** Prescription Only (Rx-only).
