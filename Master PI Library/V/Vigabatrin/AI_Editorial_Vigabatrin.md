# Editorial Product Information: Vigabatrin

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of vigabatrin for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this publication, only the Australian PI was available for synthesis; information from other regions (e.g., US, European, Canadian) is not specified in the available source.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Vigabatrin (an irreversible inhibitor of gamma-aminobutyric acid transaminase [GABA-T]).
- **Brand Name(s):** Sabril [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available source.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - 500 mg film-coated tablets (oval, biconvex, white to off-white, with break-mark and "SABRIL" or "SABRILEX" engraving) [Australian].
    - 500 mg oral powder sachets (white to off-white granular powder) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** sanofi-aventis australia pty ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Refractory Epilepsy:** Indicated for the treatment of epilepsy that is not satisfactorily controlled by other antiepileptic drugs (AEDs) [Australian].
- **Note:** Vigabatrin should be initiated as add-on therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Starting Dose:** 2 g daily, added to the current AED regimen [Australian].
- **Titration:** Increase or decrease in 0.5 g or 1.0 g increments at intervals of one week or greater, based on clinical response and tolerability [Australian].
- **Maximum Dose:** Efficacy usually does not increase beyond 4 g/day [Australian].

### Dosing by Indication
- **Epilepsy (Add-on):** See General Dosing.

### Administration Instructions
- **Tablets:** May be taken once or twice daily [Australian].
- **Oral Powder:** Contents of the sachet must be dissolved in water or a soft drink immediately prior to administration [Australian].
- **Food Effect:** Absorption is unaffected by the presence of food [Australian].

### Titration and Withdrawal
- **Withdrawal:** To avoid rebound seizures, vigabatrin must be tapered gradually over 2 to 4 weeks [Australian].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Not specified in available source.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** History of hypersensitivity to vigabatrin or any of the product excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Visual Field Defects (VFDs)
- **Prevalence:** Approximately 1/3 of patients develop VFDs [Australian].
- **Nature:** Typically presents as a permanent, irreversible, concentric constriction of the visual field in both eyes (more marked nasally). Severe cases may result in tunnel vision [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Mandatory baseline and regular (approximately every six months) perimetry (e.g., Humphrey or Octopus static perimetry) is required. Perimetry is generally only reliable in patients with a developmental age >9 years [Australian].
- **Risk Factors:** Risk increases with higher daily doses (from 1 g to >3 g) and longer duration of treatment [Australian].

### Intramyelinic Oedema (IME) and MRI Findings
- **Animal Data:** Animal studies showed IME in white matter tracts; while there is no evidence this occurs in humans, neurological function should be closely observed [Australian].
- **Infantile Spasms:** Cases of abnormal MRI findings and IME have been reported in infants treated for infantile spasms, particularly at high doses [Australian].

### Psychiatric and Neurological Effects
- **Psychiatric Events:** Agitation, depression, abnormal thinking, and paranoid reactions may occur. Use with caution in patients with a history of psychosis or depression [Australian].
- **Encephalopathy:** Rare reports of sedation, stupor, and confusion with non-specific EEG slow-wave activity, often linked to high starting doses or rapid titration [Australian].
- **Suicidal Behaviour:** Increased risk of suicidal thoughts or behaviour (common to all AEDs). Monitor for changes in mood or behaviour [Australian].

### Movement Disorders
- Dystonia, dyskinesia, and hypertonia have been reported, particularly in patients treated for infantile spasms [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are primarily CNS-related and often decrease over time [Australian].

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Somnolence, fatigue, arthralgia, visual field defects, excitation/agitation (children) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Weight increase, headache, dizziness, tremor, memory/attention impairment, speech disorder, blurred vision, diplopia, nystagmus, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, alopecia, oedema, irritability, depression, insomnia |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Ataxia, movement disorders (dystonia/dyskinesia), rash, hypomania/mania, psychosis |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Encephalopathy, retinal disorder (peripheral), angioedema, suicide attempt |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Optic neuritis, optic atrophy, hallucination |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Phenytoin:** Vigabatrin may cause a gradual reduction (approximately 20%) in phenytoin plasma concentrations [Australian].
- **Clonazepam:** Concomitant use may exacerbate sedative effects or lead to coma [Australian].
- **Retinotoxic Drugs:** Should not be used concomitantly with vigabatrin due to additive visual risks [Australian].
- **Hepatic Metabolism:** Vigabatrin is not an inducer of Cytochrome P450; interactions via this pathway are unlikely [Australian].
- **Laboratory Tests:** May lead to a 30–100% decrease in measured ALT and AST activity. May increase urinary amino acids (false positive for certain metabolic diseases) [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category D:** Known risk of human foetal malformation (cleft lip, cardiovascular, and neural tube defects). Use only if benefits clearly outweigh risks.
    - **Management:** Do not interrupt treatment suddenly; use the lowest effective dose. Folic acid (5 mg) should be started four weeks before conception [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Vigabatrin is excreted in low concentrations in breast milk. Not recommended for breastfeeding mothers unless benefits outweigh risks [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - **3–9 years:** Starting dose 1 g daily [Australian].
    - **>10 years:** Starting dose 2 g daily [Australian].
    - **Safety:** Higher risk of excitation and agitation in children [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Caution required. Start at a lower dose due to the risk of renal decline; monitor for sedation and confusion [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Vigabatrin is 70% eliminated unchanged via kidneys. Caution is advised if CrCl <60 mL/min. Dose adjustment or frequency reduction should be considered [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available source (minimal hepatic metabolism noted).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Irreversible, dose-dependent inhibition of GABA-transaminase (GABA-T), leading to increased brain concentrations of the inhibitory neurotransmitter GABA [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Efficacy is related to the rate of enzyme resynthesis rather than plasma concentrations [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Rapid; unaffected by food.
    - **Distribution:** Widely distributed; volume of distribution slightly greater than total body water.
    - **Metabolism:** Not extensively metabolised.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (70% unchanged in 24 hours); terminal half-life is 5–8 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Refractory Epilepsy:** Controlled trials demonstrate a reduction in seizure frequency when used as add-on therapy. Efficacy is particularly pronounced in complex partial seizures [Australian].
- **Neurotoxicity:** Long-term follow-up (MRI, evoked potentials) has not shown evidence of the neurotoxicity (intramyelinic oedema) observed in animal species [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].
- **Container:** PVC/Al blister packs (100 tablets) or packs of 60 sachets [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4 - S4) [Australian].
