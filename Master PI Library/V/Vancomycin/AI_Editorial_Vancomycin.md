# Editorial Product Information: Vancomycin

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
*   **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview of vancomycin product information, synthesized for use by healthcare professionals.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local approved PI for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Vancomycin (as hydrochloride).
*   **Chemical Class:** Amphoteric glycopeptide antimicrobial.
*   **Brand Name(s):** Vancomycin Alphapharm [Australian].
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    *   Powder for injection: 500 mg (500,000 IU) and 1000 mg (1,000,000 IU) vials [Australian].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion; Oral (for specific gastrointestinal indications).
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Alphapharm Pty Limited trading as Viatris [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Parenteral Administration:**
    *   Severe, potentially life-threatening staphylococcal infections (including methicillin-resistant strains) in patients who cannot receive or have failed to respond to penicillins and cephalosporins.
    *   Endocarditis: Caused by *S. viridans* or *S. bovis* (alone or with an aminoglycoside); enterococcal endocarditis (e.g., *E. faecalis*) only in combination with an aminoglycoside; diphtheroid endocarditis.
    *   Early-onset prosthetic valve endocarditis: In combination with rifampicin and/or an aminoglycoside (for *S. epidermidis* or diphtheroids).
    *   Other documented staphylococcal infections: Osteomyelitis, pneumonia, septicaemia, and skin/skin structure infections (as an adjunct to surgery where necessary).
*   **Oral Administration:**
    *   Staphylococcal enterocolitis.
    *   Antibiotic-associated pseudomembranous colitis (produced by *C. difficile*).
    *   *Note:* Parenteral administration alone is inappropriate for these indications [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults with Normal Renal Function)
*   **Intravenous:** 500 mg every 6 hours or 1000 mg every 12 hours.
*   **Infusion Rate:** Each 500 mg dose should be infused over at least 60 minutes; 1000 mg over at least 120 minutes. Rate should not exceed 10 mg/minute.
*   **Concentration:** Recommended maximum of 5 mg/mL. In fluid-restricted patients, up to 10 mg/mL may be used, though this increases the risk of infusion-related events.

### Indication-Specific Dosing
*   **Oral (C. difficile/Staphylococcal enterocolitis):** 500 mg to 2 g daily, divided into 3 or 4 doses for 7 to 10 days. Total daily dose should not exceed 2 g.

### Administration Instructions
*   **IV:** Must be administered intravenously; intramuscular (IM) injection is contraindicated due to tissue irritation and necrosis.
*   **Oral:** Reconstitute the vial, then dilute the dose in 30 mL of water for the patient to drink or administer via nasogastric tube. Flavouring syrups may be added.
*   **Reconstitution:** Reconstitute 500 mg vials with 10 mL and 1000 mg vials with 20 mL Water for Injections to reach 50 mg/mL. This must be further diluted (e.g., 500 mg in at least 100 mL; 1000 mg in at least 200 mL of 0.9% Saline or 5% Glucose).

## 4. Contraindications
*   Known hypersensitivity to vancomycin, any of the excipients, or other glycopeptides [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
*   **Infusion Reactions:** Rapid bolus administration may lead to "Red Neck Syndrome" (hypotension, flushing, erythema, urticaria, pruritus). Severe cases may involve shock or cardiac arrest. 
*   **Ototoxicity:** May be transient or permanent. Risk increases with serum levels >80 mcg/mL or concomitant use of ototoxic drugs.
*   **Nephrotoxicity:** Increased risk with high blood concentrations or prolonged treatment.
*   **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** SJS, TEN, DRESS, and AGEP have been reported. Discontinue immediately if a SCAR is suspected; do not restart.
*   **Monitoring Requirements:**
    *   Serial auditory function tests for patients with CrCl <60 mL/min or the elderly.
    *   Periodic haematological studies (leukocyte counts), urinalysis, and renal function tests for all patients.
    *   Serum concentration monitoring is mandatory in neonates and highly recommended in the elderly, renal impairment, and those with inflammatory intestinal disorders receiving oral doses.

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Frequency data not numerically quantified in source; listed by system.*

*   **Immune System:** Anaphylaxis, hypersensitivity reactions (chills, fever, rigors), Kounis syndrome.
*   **Vascular/Infusion-related:** Hypotension (rapid bolus), flushing of the upper body ("red neck"), phlebitis, vasculitis, thrombophlebitis.
*   **Ear/Labyrinth:** Sensorineural deafness (rare), tinnitus, vertigo, dizziness.
*   **Renal:** Renal failure (manifested by increased creatinine/urea), acute tubular necrosis, interstitial nephritis.
*   **Skin/Subcutaneous:** Exfoliative dermatitis, exanthema, mucosal inflammation, Linear IgA bullous dermatosis, SJS, TEN, DRESS, AGEP.
*   **Haematological:** Reversible neutropenia (usually after 1 week or >25 g total dose), thrombocytopenia, eosinophilia, pancytopenia, agranulocytosis (rare).
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, pseudomembranous enterocolitis.
*   **Serious Adverse Effects:** Cardiac arrest (associated with rapid infusion), chemical peritonitis (associated with intraperitoneal administration), Haemorrhagic Occlusive Retinal Vasculitis (associated with unapproved intracameral/intravitreal use).

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Nephrotoxic/Neurotoxic Drugs:** Concurrent or sequential use with streptomycin, neomycin, gentamicin, kanamycin, amikacin, amphotericin B, bacitracin, tobramycin, polymyxin B, colistin, cisplatin, or piperacillin/tazobactam requires careful monitoring.
*   **Diuretics:** Etacrynic acid and furosemide may aggravate ototoxicity.
*   **Anaesthetics:** Concomitant use in children is associated with erythema and histamine-like flushing. In adults, it may increase the frequency of infusion-related events.
*   **Neuromuscular Blockers:** Vancomycin may enhance the blockade produced by suxamethonium or vecuronium.
*   **Colestyramine:** Binds vancomycin in vitro; space doses several hours apart when using oral vancomycin.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B2]. Animal studies are lacking. Use only if clearly needed and monitor blood levels to minimize potential fetal toxicity. Vancomycin crosses the placenta; a risk of embryonal/neonatal ototoxicity/nephrotoxicity cannot be excluded.
*   **Breastfeeding:** Vancomycin is excreted in breast milk. Not recommended for nursing mothers unless benefits outweigh risks; consider stopping breastfeeding if treatment is necessary.
*   **Fertility:** No definitive fertility studies have been performed.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:**
    *   **Children:** 10 mg/kg every 6 hours (loading dose 15 mg/kg).
    *   **Neonates (0-1 week):** 15 mg/kg initial dose, followed by 10 mg/kg every 12 hours.
    *   **Neonates (1 week to 1 month):** 15 mg/kg initial dose, followed by 10 mg/kg every 8 hours.
    *   **Note:** Close monitoring of serum concentrations is mandatory.
*   **Elderly Use:** Dosage reduction may be necessary due to natural decrement in GFR. Serial tests of auditory function and serum levels are required [Australian].
*   **Renal Impairment:**
    *   Initial loading dose should be $\ge$ 15 mg/kg.
    *   Daily maintenance dose: Approximately 15 times the GFR (mL/min).
    *   **Anephric Patients:** Initial dose 15 mg/kg; maintenance dose 1.9 mg/kg/24h. In anuria, 1000 mg every 7 to 10 days has been used.
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources (beyond the requirement for periodic liver function tests).

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal. Inhibits bacterial cell wall mucopeptide biosynthesis at a site different from penicillins. Also alters cell membrane permeability and inhibits RNA synthesis.
*   **Microbiology:** Active against Gram-positive organisms (staphylococci, streptococci, enterococci, *C. difficile*, Listeria, Corynebacterium). Gram-negative bacteria, mycobacteria, and fungi are resistant.
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption:** Poorly absorbed orally.
    *   **Distribution:** 55% protein bound. Diffuses into pleural, pericardial, ascitic, and synovial fluids. Does NOT readily diffuse across meninges into CSF.
    *   **Metabolism:** No apparent metabolism.
    *   **Elimination:** Primarily renal (75% excreted in urine via glomerular filtration in 24 hours). Mean half-life is 4–6 hours in normal renal function; extended to 7.5 days in anephric patients.

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   Store below 25°C.
*   Keep in the original container to protect from light.
*   **Stability (Reconstituted):** Reconstituted solution (in Water for Injection, 5% Glucose, or 0.9% Saline) may be stored in a refrigerator for 24 hours.
