# Editorial Product Information: VAXNEUVANCE (Pneumococcal 15-valent Conjugate Vaccine)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on Product Information (PI) documents from the following jurisdictions: **Australian (TGA)** and **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI. Clinicians must consult the official PI approved in their specific region for prescribing and legal compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pneumococcal purified capsular polysaccharides from 15 serotypes (1, 3, 4, 5, 6A, 6B, 7F, 9V, 14, 18C, 19A, 19F, 22F, 23F, and 33F), each individually conjugated to the non-toxic diphtheria CRM197 carrier protein.
- **Chemical Class:** Multivalent conjugate vaccine.
- **Brand Name:** **VAXNEUVANCE**.
- **ATC Code:** J07AL02.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Suspension for injection. Each 0.5 mL dose contains 32 mcg of total polysaccharide (2.0 mcg of each serotype except 6B, which is 4.0 mcg) and 30 mcg of CRM197 protein, adsorbed on 125 mcg of aluminium (as aluminium phosphate adjuvant).
- **Route of Administration:** Intramuscular (IM) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Merck Sharp & Dohme (MSD).

## 2. Approved Indications
**VAXNEUVANCE** is indicated for active immunisation for the prevention of invasive disease (including sepsis, meningitis, and bacteraemic pneumonia) and pneumococcal disease caused by *Streptococcus pneumoniae* serotypes 1, 3, 4, 5, 6A, 6B, 7F, 9V, 14, 18C, 19A, 19F, 22F, 23F, and 33F.
- **Approved Age:** Individuals 6 weeks of age and older.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- **Dose:** 0.5 mL administered intramuscularly.
- **Preparation:** The vaccine is an opalescent suspension. It must be shaken vigorously horizontally immediately before use to achieve a uniform suspension. Multiple attempts at shaking may be required for complete resuspension.
- **Site:** Anterolateral aspect of the thigh (infants) or the deltoid muscle of the upper arm (children and adults). Do not inject intravascularly or into the gluteal area.

### Pediatric Schedule (Infants and Toddlers)
The standard regimen is a 4-dose series; however, regional variations exist for 3-dose options:
- **4-Dose Series (Standard):** Doses at 2, 4, 6, and 12 through 15 months of age.
- **3-Dose Series [Australian-approved]:** A primary series at 2 and 4 months, followed by a toddler dose at 11 through 15 months. 
- **Preterm Infants (<37 weeks gestation):** Should receive the 4-dose regimen, starting as early as 6 weeks of age.

### Catch-Up Vaccination (Children 7 Months to 17 Years)
For those vaccine-naïve or not fully vaccinated with lower-valency vaccines:
- **7 to 11 months:** 3 doses (first two at least 4 weeks apart; third dose after 12 months of age and at least 2 months after the second dose).
- **12 to 23 months:** 2 doses, separated by 2 months.
- **2 to 17 years:** A single dose.
- **Inter-vaccine interval:** If a previous pneumococcal conjugate vaccine was administered, wait at least 2 months before giving **VAXNEUVANCE**.

### Adult Dosage
- **18 years and older:** A single 0.5 mL dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Severe Allergic Reaction:** Contraindicated in individuals with a history of severe allergic reaction (e.g., anaphylaxis) to any component of the vaccine or to any diphtheria toxoid-containing vaccine.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Management of Allergic Reactions:** Appropriate medical treatment for anaphylaxis must be available for immediate use.
- **Altered Immunocompetence:** Individuals with impaired immune responsiveness (e.g., due to immunosuppressive therapy or genetic defect) may have a reduced immune response to the vaccine.
- **Apnoea in Preterm Infants:** The potential risk of apnoea should be considered when vaccinating infants born prematurely (<28 weeks gestation [Australian]). Vaccination should generally not be withheld or delayed, but monitoring (48-72h) should be considered.
- **Limitations of Protection:** **VAXNEUVANCE** only protects against the *S. pneumoniae* serotypes included in the vaccine and may not protect all recipients.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally mild to moderate and of short duration (≤3 days).

### Pediatric (Infants and Toddlers)
- **Very Common (>10%):** Irritability (up to 63%), somnolence, injection-site pain, decreased appetite, fever (≥38°C), injection-site induration, erythema, and swelling.
- **Common (1–10%):** Urticaria.

### Children and Adolescents (2–17 years)
- **Very Common (>10%):** Injection-site pain, myalgia, injection-site swelling, erythema, fatigue, headache.

### Adults (18 years and older)
- **Very Common (>10%):** Injection-site pain (up to 75%), fatigue, myalgia, headache, arthralgia (especially in those 18-49 years).
- **Common (1–10%):** Injection-site swelling, erythema, and fever.
- **Elderly:** Generally reported fewer solicited adverse reactions than younger adults.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Concomitant Administration (Pediatrics):** Can be administered with routine pediatric vaccines including diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis, poliomyelitis, hepatitis A and B, *Haemophilus influenzae* type b, measles, mumps, rubella, varicella, and rotavirus vaccines. 
- **Concomitant Administration (Adults):** Can be administered with inactivated quadrivalent influenza vaccine. 
- **Interference:** No clinical evidence of interference with the immune response to other vaccines was noted, though OPA GMTs were slightly lower for some pneumococcal serotypes when given with influenza vaccine.
- **Administration Note:** Always use different syringes and separate injection sites for concomitant vaccinations.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - [Australian: Category B1] — Drugs which have been taken by only a limited number of pregnant women and women of childbearing age, without an increase in the frequency of malformation or other direct or indirect harmful effects on the human fetus having been observed.
    - [US: Risk Summary] — No adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Animal studies (rats) showed no evidence of harm to the fetus or pregnancy.
- **Breastfeeding:** Not specified in available sources if excreted in human milk. A decision should be made based on the benefit of vaccination to the mother and the potential effects on the breastfed child.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no effects on female mating performance or fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric:** Safety and effectiveness not established for infants <6 weeks of age.
- **Elderly:** Safety and immunogenicity demonstrated in adults ≥65 and ≥75 years. 
- **HIV Infection:** Immunogenic and safe in children and adults living with HIV; however, the response may be lower than in immunocompetent individuals.
- **Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplant (HSCT):** Safety and immunogenicity evaluated; recipients of HSCT showed higher antibody titers after a 3-dose series of **VAXNEUVANCE**.
- **Sickle Cell Disease:** Demonstrated to be immunogenic and safe in children 5-17 years with sickle cell disease.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Induces serotype-specific antibodies (IgG) and functional antibodies (OPA) that lead to opsonization, phagocytosis, and killing of *Streptococcus pneumoniae*. The conjugation to CRM197 protein transforms the response into a T-cell dependent one, inducing immune memory and higher titers in infants.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Serotype-specific IgG concentrations ≥0.35 mcg/mL are used as the threshold value for clinical evaluation in pediatric populations.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Not applicable for vaccines.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pediatric Studies:** Pivotal trials (e.g., Protocol 025 and 029) established non-inferiority to the 13-valent conjugate vaccine (PCV13) for the 13 shared serotypes and superiority for the two unique serotypes (22F, 33F). In Protocol 029, serotype 6A narrowly missed non-inferiority criteria by GMC ratio but met it by response rate.
- **Adult Studies:** Protocol 019 (Pivotal) in adults ≥50 years showed **VAXNEUVANCE** was non-inferior to PCV13 for shared serotypes and superior for serotypes 22F, 33F, and the shared serotype 3.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C.
- **Handling:** Do not freeze. Protect from light.
- **Excursions:** Stability data indicate the vaccine is stable at temperatures up to 25°C for 48 hours [Australian].
