# Editorial Product Information: Varicella-Zoster Immune Globulin (Human)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of the clinical and regulatory profile of Varicella-Zoster Immune Globulin (Human) for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial resource and does not replace the official, legally approved PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Varicella-Zoster Immune Globulin (Human). This is a solvent/detergent-treated, purified IgG preparation containing high titers of antibodies to varicella-zoster virus (VZV).
- **Brand Name(s):** **VARIZIG** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for intramuscular (IM) injection. Available in a single-use vial containing a minimum potency of 125 international units (IU) in 1.2 mL [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intramuscular injection only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Kamada Ltd. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
**VARIZIG** is indicated for post-exposure prophylaxis of varicella in high-risk individuals [US]. High-risk groups include:
- Immunocompromised children and adults [US].
- Newborns of mothers who develop varicella shortly before or after delivery [US].
- Premature infants [US].
- Neonates and infants less than one year of age [US].
- Pregnant women [US].
- Adults without evidence of immunity [US].

The administration of this product is intended to reduce the severity of varicella infection [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
Dosing is determined by body weight. A single dose should be administered as soon as possible after exposure, ideally within 96 hours for maximum efficacy; however, treatment may be considered up to 10 days post-exposure [US].

**Table 1: Weight-Based Dosing Schedule [US]**
| Weight of Patient (kg) | Dose (International Units) | Number of Vials |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ≤ 2.0 | 62.5 | 0.5 |
| 2.1 – 10.0 | 125 | 1 |
| 10.1 – 20.0 | 250 | 2 |
| 20.1 – 30.0 | 375 | 3 |
| 30.1 – 40.0 | 500 | 4 |
| > 40.1 | 625 | 5 |

### Administration Instructions
- **Preparation:** Bring the vial to room temperature before use. Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration; the solution should not be used if cloudy [US].
- **Injection Site:** Administer via IM injection into the deltoid muscle or the anterolateral aspect of the upper thigh [US].
- **Volume Limits:** Do not exceed 3 mL per injection site. For larger doses, divide the dose and administer at multiple sites [US].
- **Precautions:** Do not use the gluteal region routinely due to the risk of sciatic nerve injury. If the gluteal region is required, use only the upper, outer quadrant [US].
- **Repeat Dosing:** A second full dose should be considered if high-risk patients are exposed again more than three weeks after the initial administration [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Severe Systemic Reactions:** History of anaphylactic or severe systemic reactions to human immune globulin preparations [US].
- **IgA Deficiency:** Patients with selective IgA deficiency who have known antibodies against IgA and a history of hypersensitivity [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Thrombotic Events:** Thrombosis may occur with immune globulin products. Risk factors include advanced age, prolonged immobilisation, history of atherosclerosis, or hyperviscosity. Baseline blood viscosity should be assessed in patients at risk for hyperviscosity (e.g., those with cryoglobulins or high triglycerides) [US].
- **Coagulation Disorders:** In patients with severe thrombocytopenia or bleeding disorders where IM injections are contraindicated, the benefit of administration must outweigh the risk of haematoma [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Severe reactions, including anaphylaxis, can occur. Administration should take place in a setting equipped for the management of shock. If a reaction occurs, discontinue use immediately [US].
- **Transmissible Infectious Agents:** As a plasma-derived product, there is a theoretical risk of transmitting infectious agents (e.g., viruses, vCJD). This risk is mitigated by donor screening and viral inactivation/removal steps during manufacturing (e.g., solvent/detergent treatment and virus filtration) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following frequencies are based on clinical trial data (n=601) [US]:

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Injection site pain (3%), headache (2%), rash/urticaria (1%), fatigue (1%), chills (1%), nausea (1%) |
| **Uncommon (<1%)** | All other reported reactions |

**Serious Adverse Effects:**
- **Serum Sickness:** Reported in one immunocompromised adolescent patient (approx. 1 in 600) [US].
- **Coagulation-related events:** While observed in an expanded access protocol (e.g., DVT, intracranial haemorrhage), these were not definitively attributed to the drug due to underlying medical conditions [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Live Attenuated Vaccines:** The passive transfer of antibodies may interfere with the immune response to live vaccines (e.g., measles, mumps, rubella, varicella). Vaccination with live virus products should be deferred for approximately three months following administration [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned/Not specified]. Animal reproduction studies have not been performed. It is unknown if the product can cause foetal harm. Use in pregnancy only if clearly required. Clinical trials in 166 pregnant women indicated the product is used for post-exposure prophylaxis in this population [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if the globulin is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution when administering to nursing mothers [US].
- **Fertility:** Data not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based. Safety and efficacy were evaluated in an expanded access protocol involving 374 paediatric patients, including preterm newborns and infants [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Clinical trials did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65 and older to determine if they respond differently. Caution is advised due to the increased risk of thrombotic events in this population [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Provides passive immunisation by delivering exogenous IgG antibodies against VZV. This reduces the severity of the infection in non-immune individuals exposed to the virus [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Intended to reduce VZV complications; there is no evidence that it reduces the incidence of infection or modifies established infections [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** In a comparative study, the mean peak concentration ($C_{max}$) of antibodies occurred within five days of IM administration. The mean half-life was approximately 26.2 ± 4.6 days, though this varies between patients [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pregnant Women:** A controlled trial in 60 non-immune pregnant women showed that the incidence of clinical varicella was approximately 33% across treatment groups. In those with more than 24 hours of exposure, the incidence was 64% [US].
- **Expanded Access Protocol (EAP):** An open-label study assessed high-risk patients. Clinical varicella was observed in 8.4% of immunocompromised patients, 6.8% of pregnant women, and 14.8% of infants. These rates were significantly lower than historical rates in untreated individuals (e.g., 70-88%) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Do not freeze. Discard any unused portion as the product contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Special Status:** Human plasma derivative [US].
