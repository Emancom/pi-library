# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: United States (FDA).
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine for use by healthcare professionals. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis intended for clinical reference and does not substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not specified in available sources, but commonly classified as such).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn metabolic errors resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **Carnitine Deficiency in End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours, and never less frequently than every 6 hours (by infusion or IV injection).
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest administered dose reported is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
- **Administration:** Administer as a 2 to 3-minute slow bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels (target range 40–60 µmol/L). Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### General Instructions
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations is recommended [US].
- **Parenteral Inspection:** Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in dialysis patients. Some reactions occurred within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks/benefits before re-administration [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine may increase seizure frequency or severity in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a prior history of epilepsy [US].
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Administration Precaution):** While the IV formulation is used in ESRD, chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse event frequencies are derived from clinical trials in hemodialysis patients [US].

**Very Common (>10%):**
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33–59%), pain (21–49%), headache (3–37%), fever (5–12%), abdominal pain (5–21%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (14–21%), hypotension (3–19%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), nausea (5–12%), vomiting (9–21%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–33%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–19%), rhinitis (6–11%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hypervolemia (3–17%).

**Common (1–10%):**
- **General:** Asthenia, back pain, chest pain, flu syndrome, infection.
- **Cardiovascular:** Arrhythmia, atrial fibrillation, palpitations, tachycardia.
- **Digestive:** Anorexia, constipation, dyspepsia, melena.
- **Neurological/Psychiatric:** Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia, depression, anxiety.
- **Other:** Body odor (transient), gastritis, anemia, peripheral edema, weight changes, pruritus, eye disorders, urinary tract infection [US].

**Serious Adverse Effects:**
- Anaphylaxis and severe respiratory distress (bronchospasm).
- Seizures.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels at the initiation of levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified, but text provided]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after exogenous administration. Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders applies to the paediatric population, though specific age-range safety studies are not detailed separately in the source [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the source document [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically in ESRD patients on dialysis (see Section 3). The safety of *oral* levocarnitine in renal failure is not established [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following a 20 mg/kg IV bolus, ~76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Total body clearance is 4.00 L/h [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, IV administration increases plasma carnitine concentrations and corrects the acylcarnitine/carnitine ratio [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis demonstrated significant increases in pre-dialysis (trough) carnitine levels from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L by week 8.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses in ESRD patients showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks.
- **Note:** While levocarnitine effectively increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the impact on specific clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, malaise) has not been definitively determined in this population [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
