# Editorial Product Information: Vandetanib

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. 
- **Note:** At the time of this editorial's composition, only the Australian PI was available for review. Consequently, jurisdictional comparisons are limited to this region.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is an editorial synthesis and must not be used as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI when prescribing or dispensing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Vandetanib (a tyrosine kinase inhibitor).
- **Brand Name:** **Caprelsa** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 100 mg film-coated tablets. The tablets are white, round, bi-convex, and intagliated with ‘Z100’ on one side [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** sanofi-aventis australia pty ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Medullary Thyroid Cancer (MTC):** Indicated for the treatment of symptomatic or progressive MTC in patients with unresectable locally advanced or metastatic disease [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Standard Adult Dose:** 300 mg taken orally once daily [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - May be taken with or without food [Australian].
    - Tablets may be dispersed in 50 mL of non-carbonated drinking water (stirred for approximately 10 minutes) and swallowed immediately. No other liquids should be used. Any residue must be rinsed with another 50 mL of water and swallowed.
    - Administration via nasogastric or gastrostomy tubes is permissible using the dispersion method.
    - **Handling Caution:** Direct contact of crushed tablets with skin or mucous membranes should be avoided. If contact occurs, wash thoroughly [Australian].
- **Dose Adjustments for Toxicity:** 
    - For CTCAE grade 3 or higher toxicity or significant QTc prolongation, interrupt treatment. 
    - Upon resolution to grade 1, resume at a reduced dose. 
    - Dose reduction steps: 300 mg $\rightarrow$ 200 mg $\rightarrow$ 100 mg [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, it should be skipped if the next dose is due within a short timeframe. The patient should take the next daily dose as prescribed; do not double the dose [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to vandetanib or any of the excipients [Australian].
- Patients with congenital long QT syndrome [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [Australian]:** Vandetanib can cause fatal or life-threatening ventricular arrhythmias (including Torsades de Pointes) and sudden death. These risks are higher in patients with significant QTc prolongation. 
    - Do not start therapy if the QTc interval is >480 ms. 
    - Maintain serum potassium $\ge$ 4 mmol/L and keep magnesium and calcium within normal ranges [Australian].
- **QTc Prolongation and Monitoring:** Vandetanib has a long half-life (~19 days), meaning QTc effects may persist for weeks after discontinuation. 
    - **Monitoring Schedule:** ECG and electrolytes (K+, Ca++, Mg++) and TSH must be obtained at baseline; at 1, 3, 6, and 12 weeks after starting; and every 3 months for at least a year thereafter [Australian].
- **Skin Reactions:** Severe reactions, including Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) and Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN), have been reported. Permanent discontinuation is recommended for severe reactions [Australian].
- **Photosensitivity:** Patients should use sunscreen and protective clothing. This precaution must continue for 4 months after stopping treatment due to the drug’s long half-life [Australian].
- **Ischaemic Cerebrovascular Events:** Observed in 1.3% of patients in clinical trials. Discontinue in patients experiencing severe events [Australian].
- **Hemorrhage:** Do not administer to patients with a recent history of significant hemoptysis ($\ge$ 1/2 teaspoon). Discontinue for severe hemorrhage [Australian].
- **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD):** Can be fatal. Interrupt therapy for new or progressive pulmonary symptoms (dyspnea, cough, fever) and discontinue if ILD is confirmed [Australian].
- **Other Precautions:** Heart failure, hypertensive crisis, reversible posterior leukoencephalopathy syndrome (RPLS), and pancreatitis have been reported [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by frequency based on clinical trial data (Study 58).

| Frequency | Adverse Events [Australian] |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Diarrhoea (56%), Rash (45%), Nausea (33%), Hypertension (32%), Headache (26%), Fatigue (24%), Decreased appetite (21%), Acne (20%), Dry skin (15%), Asthenia (15%), Abdominal pain (14%), QTc prolonged (14%), Insomnia (13%), Photosensitivity (13%), Nasopharyngitis (11%), Dyspepsia (11%), Hypocalcaemia (11%), Cough (11%), Pruritus (11%). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Weight decrease, Proteinuria, Hematuria, Epistaxis, Palmar-plantar erythrodysaesthesia, Conjunctivitis, Dysgeusia, Renal failure. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Heart failure, Torsades de Pointes, SJS, ILD/Pneumonitis, Pancreatitis, RPLS, Small intestinal perforation. |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Most common serious events ($\ge$ 1%) included pneumonia, diarrhoea, hypertensive crisis, and urinary tract infection [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Strong inducers (e.g., rifampicin, carbamazepine, St John’s Wort) can reduce vandetanib exposure by ~40%; concomitant use should be avoided [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Potent inhibitors (e.g., itraconazole) increase vandetanib levels slightly (~9%). Use caution [Australian].
- **QT-Prolonging Drugs:** Avoid concomitant use with drugs known to prolong the QT interval (e.g., ondansetron). If essential, increase ECG monitoring frequency [Australian].
- **Transporter Interactions:** 
    - **P-gp:** Vandetanib is a weak inhibitor. Monitor patients on P-gp substrates with a narrow therapeutic index (e.g., digoxin, dabigatran).
    - **OCT2:** Vandetanib inhibits OCT2. Monitor patients taking metformin; a dose reduction of metformin may be required [Australian].
- **Vitamin K Antagonists:** Increased frequency of INR monitoring is recommended [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D [Australian]**. Based on animal data, vandetanib may cause fetal harm (teratogenic and embryotoxic). Effective contraception is required for both men and women during therapy and for **4 months** after the final dose [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Breastfeeding is not recommended during therapy. Vandetanib is excreted in the milk of lactating rats; human data is unavailable [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies indicate potential impairment of both male and female fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not indicated; safety and efficacy have not been established in children [Australian].
- **Elderly (>65 years):** No starting dose adjustment required. Limited data exists for patients >75 years [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Mild:** No dose adjustment.
    - **Moderate (CrCl 30–50 mL/min):** Reduce starting dose to **200 mg**. 
    - **Severe (CrCl <30 mL/min):** Not recommended [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not recommended for use in patients with hepatic impairment as safety and efficacy have not been established [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A multi-kinase inhibitor. It targets VEGFR-2 (angiogenesis), EGFR (tumor cell proliferation/survival), and RET tyrosine kinases. It also shows activity against BRK, VEGFR-3, and is an antagonist of H1/H2 and $\alpha_{2A}$ receptors [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Slow; peak plasma concentrations achieved at median 6 hours (range 4–10). Steady state reached in ~2 months.
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (~90-94% to albumin and $\alpha$1-acid glycoprotein).
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolised via CYP3A4 to N-desmethyl-vandetanib and via FMO1/FMO3 to vandetanib-N-oxide.
    - **Elimination:** Excreted in faeces (44%) and urine (25%). Half-life is approximately **19 days** [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study 58 (ZETA Trial):** A Phase III, randomised, double-blind study of 331 patients with unresectable locally advanced or metastatic MTC. 
- **Results:** Vandetanib 300 mg showed a statistically significant improvement in Progression-Free Survival (PFS) compared to placebo (Hazard Ratio 0.46). Median PFS was predicted at 30.5 months for vandetanib vs 19.3 months for placebo. 
- **Response Rates:** Objective Response Rate (ORR) was 45% for vandetanib vs 13% for placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Handling:** Tablets should not be crushed. If dispersion is necessary, follow specific safety protocols to avoid skin contact [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian]**.
