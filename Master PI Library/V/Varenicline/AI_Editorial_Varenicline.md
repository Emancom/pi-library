# Editorial Product Information: Varenicline

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- Information regarding other major regulatory jurisdictions (e.g., US, European, Canadian, Japanese) was not provided for this synthesis.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a clinical synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Varenicline (as tartrate).
- **Chemical Class:** Partial nicotine agonist; benzazepine derivative.
- **Brand Names:** Pharmacor Varenicline [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N07BA03.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - 0.5 mg film-coated tablets (white to off-white, capsular, debossed 'VC' and '0.5') [Australian].
    - 1 mg film-coated tablets (light blue, capsular, debossed 'VC' and '1') [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pharmacor Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Smoking Cessation:** Indicated as an aid to smoking cessation in adults over the age of 18 years [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
The standard recommended dose is **1 mg twice daily** following a initial one-week titration. 

### Titration Schedule
- **Days 1–3:** 0.5 mg once daily.
- **Days 4–7:** 0.5 mg twice daily.
- **Day 8 – End of Treatment:** 1 mg twice daily.

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets should be swallowed whole with water and may be taken with or without food.
- **Quit Date:** Patients should set a date to stop smoking. Dosing should typically start 1–2 weeks before this date. 
- **Flexible Approach:** Patients may begin dosing and then quit smoking between days 8 and 35 of treatment [Australian].
- **Gradual Approach:** For patients unable or unwilling to quit abruptly, a gradual reduction (50% reduction every 4 weeks) with a goal of complete abstinence by 12 weeks may be employed [Australian].

### Duration of Treatment
- **Initial Course:** 12 weeks.
- **Maintenance:** For patients who successfully stop smoking at 12 weeks, an additional 12-week course (total 24 weeks) is recommended to increase the likelihood of long-term abstinence [Australian].

### Missed Doses
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to varenicline tartrate or any of the excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Neuropsychiatric Symptoms:** Serious events including changes in behavior, agitation, depressed mood, suicidal ideation, and suicidal behavior have been reported. Patients should stop varenicline and contact a healthcare professional immediately if these occur. Alcohol may increase these risks [Australian].
- **Seizures:** Reported in patients with or without a history of seizures. Use with caution in patients with a low seizure threshold [Australian].
- **Accidental Injury:** Somnambulism, dizziness, and loss of consciousness have been reported. Patients should avoid hazardous activities (e.g., driving, operating machinery) until they know how the drug affects them [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Events:** In patients with stable CV disease, certain events (nonfatal MI, need for revascularization) were reported more frequently in some studies. Patients should seek medical attention for new or worsening CV symptoms [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity and Skin Reactions:** Rare but serious reports of angioedema and severe cutaneous reactions (Stevens-Johnson Syndrome, Erythema Multiforme). Discontinue at the first sign of rash or mucosal swelling [Australian].
- **Smoking Cessation Effects:** Physiological changes from quitting smoking may alter the pharmacokinetics of drugs like theophylline, warfarin, and insulin (due to CYP1A2 induction by smoking) [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects typically occur in the first week of therapy and are mild to moderate in severity.

| Frequency | Adverse Event [Australian data] |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Nausea (28.2%), Headache (13.9%), Insomnia (12.0%), Nasopharyngitis (11.0%), Abnormal dreams (10.0%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Upper respiratory tract infection (6.4%), Constipation (6.3%), Irritability (5.0%), Fatigue (5.0%), Dizziness (4.8%), Vomiting (4.5%), Dyspepsia (4.3%), Weight increased (2.2%) |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Tremor, lethargy, tinnitus, palpitations, angina pectoris, tachycardia, hot flush, oropharyngeal pain, gastritis, hyperhidrosis, muscle spasms |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Platelet count decreased, polydipsia, bradyphrenia, dysarthria, visual field defect, atrial fibrillation, haematemesis, sexual dysfunction |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Suicidal ideation, seizures, angioedema, and myocardial infarction (reported in post-marketing or specific populations) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Varenicline is unlikely to alter the pharmacokinetics of drugs metabolized by cytochrome P450 enzymes (minimal inhibition/induction of CYP1A2, 3A4, etc.) [Australian].
- **Cimetidine:** Increases varenicline systemic exposure by 29% due to reduced renal clearance. **Avoid co-administration in patients with severe renal impairment** [Australian].
- **Metformin/Digoxin/Warfarin:** No clinically significant pharmacokinetic interactions noted [Australian].
- **Nicotine Replacement Therapy (NRT):** Co-administration may lead to a higher incidence of nausea, headache, vomiting, and dizziness compared to NRT alone [Australian].
- **Alcohol:** Limited data; post-marketing reports suggest increased intoxicating effects and neuropsychiatric risks [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B3:** Animal studies have shown evidence of increased occurrence of fetal damage, the significance of which is uncertain in humans. 
    - Use in pregnant women is **not recommended**. Treatment should ideally be completed before conception [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if varenicline is excreted in human milk. A decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug, weighing the importance of the drug to the mother [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility noted in animal studies at 40 times human exposure, though offspring showed decreased fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended in patients under 19 years of age [Australian]. Efficacy was not demonstrated in clinical trials involving patients aged 12 to 19 [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required. However, as elderly patients are more likely to have decreased renal function, renal status should be considered [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Mild to Moderate:** No dose adjustment required.
    - **Severe (CrCl < 30 mL/min):** Recommended dose is **1 mg once daily**. Begin with 0.5 mg once daily for 3 days, then increase to 1 mg once daily.
    - **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Not recommended due to insufficient clinical experience [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment necessary [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Varenicline is a highly selective **partial agonist** at the **$\alpha4\beta2$ neuronal nicotinic acetylcholine receptors**. It alleviates withdrawal symptoms (agonist activity) while blocking the rewarding/reinforcing effects of nicotine (antagonist activity) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Virtually complete; oral bioavailability is high and unaffected by food. Peak plasma levels (Tmax) occur within 3–4 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Low plasma protein binding (<20%).
    - **Metabolism:** Minimal (<10%).
    - **Elimination:** 92% excreted unchanged in urine via glomerular filtration and active tubular secretion (OCT2). Half-life is approximately 24 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy:** Superiority over placebo and bupropion was demonstrated in multiple 12-week trials. Continuous quit rates (weeks 9–12) were approximately 44% for varenicline vs. 17.7% for placebo [Australian].
- **Neuropsychiatric Safety (EAGLES study):** A large trial (N=8,058) in patients with and without psychiatric history showed no significant increase in the risk of serious neuropsychiatric events with varenicline compared to placebo or NRT [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Handling:** Store in the original package to protect from light [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
