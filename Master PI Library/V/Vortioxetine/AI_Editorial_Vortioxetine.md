# Editorial Product Information: Vortioxetine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)** regulatory authority. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically accurate editorial overview of **vortioxetine** for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local clinical guidelines mandated for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the Australian (TGA) source was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Vortioxetine (as hydrobromide), a multimodal serotonergic antidepressant.
- **Brand Name(s):** Brintellix [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Film-coated tablets:** 5 mg (pink), 10 mg (yellow), 15 mg (orange), and 20 mg (red). All are almond-shaped and biconvex [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Lundbeck Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Major Depressive Disorder (MDD):** Treatment of MDD in adult patients, including the prevention of relapse [Australian].
- **Note:** This medication is not indicated for paediatric use [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults <65 years):**
    - **Starting/Recommended Dose:** 10 mg once daily.
    - **Maintenance Range:** Depending on clinical response, the dose may be increased to a maximum of 20 mg daily or reduced to a minimum of 5 mg daily [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets may be taken with or without food [Australian].
- **Treatment Duration:** If symptoms resolve, treatment should continue for at least 6 months to consolidate the response [Australian].
- **Titration:** Clinical effect is typically observed starting at week 2 [Australian].
- **Treatment Discontinuation:** A gradual dose reduction may be considered to prevent discontinuation symptoms, though specific tapering schedules are not defined in the source data [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** The next dose should be taken at the scheduled time. Patients should be advised not to double the dose to compensate for a missed one [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to vortioxetine or any tablet excipients.
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** 
    - Concurrent use with non-selective, irreversible MAOIs is contraindicated due to the risk of serotonin syndrome.
    - **Washout Periods:** Vortioxetine must not be started until at least 14 days after stopping an MAOI. Conversely, vortioxetine must be stopped for at least 14 days before beginning MAOI therapy [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Suicide Risk:** Depression carries an inherent risk of suicide. Patients (particularly those under 25) should be monitored closely for clinical worsening and emergence of suicidal ideation, especially during the first few months of therapy or following dose adjustments [Australian].
- **Serotonin Syndrome/Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** Potentially life-threatening conditions may occur. Risk increases with concomitant use of other serotonergic agents (e.g., triptans, opioids, SSRIs/SNRIs) or antipsychotics. Discontinue immediately if neuromuscular excitation, autonomic dysfunction, or altered mental status occurs [Australian].
- **Activation of Mania/Hypomania:** Use with caution in patients with a history of mania. Screening for bipolar disorder is recommended prior to initiation [Australian].
- **Seizures:** Use cautiously in patients with a history of seizures or unstable epilepsy. Discontinue if new or more frequent seizures occur [Australian].
- **Haemorrhage:** Serotonergic antidepressants may increase the risk of bleeding (e.g., ecchymoses, GI bleeds, postpartum haemorrhage). Use caution with anticoagulants, NSAIDs, or aspirin [Australian].
- **Hyponatremia:** Likely due to SIADH; reported rarely. Use caution in high-risk patients (elderly, cirrhotic patients) [Australian].
- **Angle-closure Glaucoma:** Mydriasis may occur; use with caution in patients with increased intraocular pressure or those at risk of angle-closure glaucoma [Australian].
- **Sexual Dysfunction:** Prescribers should proactively inquire about sexual function, as symptoms (e.g., ejaculatory delay, decreased libido) may not be spontaneously reported [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally dose-related, transient, and mild-to-moderate.

| Frequency | Adverse Event(s) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Nausea (highest incidence: 31.2% at 15 mg) [Australian] |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Diarrhoea, dry mouth, constipation, vomiting, dizziness, pruritus (generalised), abnormal dreams [Australian] |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Flushing, night sweats, tremor, vertigo, mydriasis [Australian] |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Serotonin syndrome, anaphylactic reactions, angioedema, and severe hyponatremia have been reported [Australian].
- **Post-Market Observations:** Agitation, aggression, and hyperprolactinemia have been identified during post-approval use [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **CYP2D6 Inhibitors:** Strong inhibitors (e.g., bupropion) can more than double vortioxetine exposure. Consider a dose reduction based on response [Australian].
    - **CYP Inducers:** Broad inducers (e.g., rifampicin) may decrease vortioxetine AUC by ~72%. A dose adjustment may be necessary [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **MAOIs:** Contraindicated (see Section 4). 
    - **Selective MAO-A/B Inhibitors:** Linezolid and moclobemide are not recommended; selegiline and rasagiline require extreme caution [Australian].
    - **Serotonergic Agents:** Increased risk of serotonin syndrome with St. John’s Wort, lithium, triptans, and tramadol [Australian].
    - **Seizure Threshold:** Caution when co-administered with other drugs that lower the seizure threshold [Australian].
- **Alcohol:** No significant cognitive impairment was observed when combined with ethanol (0.6 g/kg), but avoidance is generally advised for any psychoactive drug [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B3.** 
    - Limited data available (<300 outcomes). Animal studies showed reduced foetal weight and delayed ossification at high doses. Use during pregnancy only if the potential benefit outweighs the risk to the foetus. 
    - Observation for neonatal withdrawal or serotonergic symptoms (e.g., respiratory distress, irritability) is recommended if used late in pregnancy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Vortioxetine is excreted in human milk in small amounts (Relative Infant Dose <2%). Due to limited safety data, breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No impact on fertility or sperm morphology was noted in animal models at 20 times the human exposure [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for patients under 18 years. Efficacy has not been demonstrated, and clinical trials noted a higher incidence of suicidal ideation in adolescents compared to adults [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** 
    - **Initial Dose:** 5 mg once daily in patients ≥65 years.
    - **Precaution:** Caution is advised when escalating doses beyond 10 mg daily in this population [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for any stage of renal impairment, including end-stage renal disease. However, caution is advised in severe cases [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment required for mild, moderate, or severe (Child-Pugh C) impairment, though caution should be exercised in severe cases [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Multimodal activity. Vortioxetine acts as a 5-HT3, 5-HT7, and 5-HT1D receptor antagonist; a 5-HT1B receptor partial agonist; a 5-HT1A receptor agonist; and an inhibitor of the 5-HT transporter (5-HTT) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is 75%. Peak plasma levels (Tmax) are reached within 7–8 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (98–99%). Extensive extravascular distribution (Vss 2,600 L).
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic via CYP2D6, with contributions from CYP3A4/5 and other isoforms. The major metabolite is pharmacologically inactive.
    - **Elimination:** Mean half-life is approximately 66 hours. Excretion is mainly via urine (2/3) and faeces (1/3) as metabolites [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Short-term Efficacy:** In 12-week trials for MDD, vortioxetine demonstrated significant improvement in MADRS or HAM-D24 scores across 9 of 12 studies. Efficacy was shown to increase with dose [Australian].
- **Relapse Prevention:** In a long-term study, patients in remission who continued vortioxetine (5 or 10 mg) had a significantly lower relapse rate (13%) compared to those switched to placebo (26%) over 24–64 weeks [Australian].
- **Cognition:** Some studies showed positive effects on neuropsychological tests (e.g., DSST) in adults with MDD [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
