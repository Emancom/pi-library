# Editorial Product Information: vaccinia virus (modified vaccinia Ankara

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **vaccinia virus (modified vaccinia Ankara)**
- Chemical class: Live, attenuated, non‑replicating orthopoxvirus vaccine (modified vaccinia Ankara strain)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Subcutaneous

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: One source lists a revision date of 03/2025; other source revision dates not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Prevention of smallpox disease in adults 18 years of age and older determined to be at high risk for smallpox infection.  
- Prevention of mpox (monkeypox) disease in adults 18 years of age and older determined to be at high risk for mpox infection.  
- Indication limited to adults ≥18 years in the provided sources. If a jurisdiction has different approved indications, those differences are not specified in available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Two doses of 0.5 mL each, administered subcutaneously, given 4 weeks apart.

### Dosing by indication
- Same regimen (two 0.5 mL subcutaneous doses 4 weeks apart) is used for prevention of smallpox and for prevention of mpox as described in available sources.

### Administration instructions
- Inspect the product visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration; do not administer if particulate matter or discoloration is present.
- Administer each dose subcutaneously as a 0.5 mL injection.
- Two different presentations exist in clinical program: one presentation is supplied ready-to-use, and another requires reconstitution of a lyophilized antigen with a diluent prior to administration. (Preparation/reconstitution specifics and storage after preparation are not included here beyond the requirement to reconstitute when applicable.)
- Appropriate medical treatment must be available to manage possible anaphylactic reactions following administration.

### Titration schedules
- Not applicable / Not specified in available sources.

### Missed dose instructions

## 5. Contraindications
- Specific contraindications are not listed in the available source material.  
- Note from sources: Individuals who experienced a severe allergic reaction following a previous dose of vaccinia virus (modified vaccinia Ankara) or to any component of the vaccine may be at increased risk for severe allergic reactions with subsequent doses; the risk should be weighed against the risk of disease. (This appears as a precaution in the sources; explicit formal contraindications were not provided in the available excerpts.)

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- **Severe allergic reactions**: Appropriate medical treatment and supervision should be immediately available to manage anaphylaxis or other severe allergic reactions following vaccination.
- **Syncope**: Fainting has been reported following vaccination. Procedures should be in place to avoid injury from fainting (e.g., observe patient for syncope risk after injection).
- **Altered immunocompetence**: Immunocompromised persons, including those receiving immunosuppressive therapy, may have a diminished immune response to vaccinia virus (modified vaccinia Ankara).
- **Limitations of vaccine effectiveness**: Vaccination may not protect all recipients.
- **Cardiac adverse events of special interest**: Clinical development monitored cardiac signs/symptoms, ECG changes, and troponin‑I elevations. Asymptomatic post‑vaccination elevations of troponin‑I were observed in some clinical studies; cardiac AESIs occurred at a higher rate among vaccine recipients than placebo in the pooled clinical program. Clinicians should be aware of a potential for cardiac events and evaluate patients presenting with cardiac signs or symptoms.
- **Monitoring requirements**:
  - Monitor and evaluate patients who develop symptoms suggestive of an allergic or cardiac adverse event.
  - In clinical trials, troponin measurements and cardiac assessments were performed; routine troponin monitoring recommendations for clinical use are not specified in available sources.
- **Other clinical precautions**:
  - Consider the risk–benefit for persons with prior severe allergic reactions to vaccine components.
  - Consider potential for reduced immunogenicity in immunocompromised individuals.

## 7. Adverse Effects
- The following adverse effects were reported in clinical trials and pooled safety analyses. Frequency categories used: Very common (>10%), Common (1–10%), Uncommon (0.1–1%), Rare (<0.1%), Very rare (<0.01%). When exact percentage data are available from the sources they are presented alongside the frequency category. Where multiple subgroup data are available (smallpox vaccine‑naïve, previously vaccinated, HIV‑infected, atopic dermatitis), these subgroup percentages are listed.

### Summary of key safety findings (high level)
- Pooled clinical program: 8,988 participants received at least one dose; solicited local and systemic reactions were commonly reported. Serious adverse events (SAEs) occurred in 1.5% of vaccine recipients overall; five non‑fatal SAEs could not be excluded as causally related to vaccination (Crohn’s disease, sarcoidosis, extraocular muscle paresis, throat tightness, hemolytic anemia). Cardiac AESIs were reported in 1.3% of vaccine recipients versus 0.2% of placebo recipients in the pooled data; asymptomatic troponin‑I elevations were documented in several studies.

Frequency patterns were generally similar across groups, with some subgroup variations listed above.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Clinical development program summary:
  - Across 23 clinical studies, a total of 8,988 participants aged 18–80 years received at least one dose of vaccine (8,222 smallpox vaccine‑naïve; 766 smallpox vaccine‑experienced). Of these, 6,459 received the one‑vial presentation and 2,529 received the two‑vial presentation.
- Key trials described in available sources:
  - Study 1 (randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled, US): Vaccinia‑naïve adults 18–40 years; compared two doses of vaccine (N≈3,003) vs placebo (N≈1,002) given 4 weeks apart; solicited adverse reactions recorded via diary cards for 8 days after each vaccination. This study provided primary safety frequencies (e.g., pain 84.9%, redness 60.8%, muscle pain 42.8%, headache 34.8%).
  - Study 2 (randomized, double‑blind, multicenter, US): Compared one‑vial vs two‑vial presentations in vaccinia‑naïve adults 18–55 years (one‑vial N≈327; two‑vial N≈325) with solicited adverse reactions recorded; demonstrated similar overall safety profiles with some differences in local reaction magnitudes (e.g., higher rates of large redness/swelling in two‑vial group).
  - Studies 3–5: Evaluated safety in persons previously vaccinated against smallpox (total ~409 persons) and reported solicited local and systemic adverse reaction frequencies in that subgroup.
  - Study 6 (open‑label): Evaluated safety in HIV‑infected participants (e.g., 351 HIV‑infected smallpox vaccine‑naïve participants and additional previously vaccinated HIV‑infected participants) with CD4 counts specified in study entry criteria; solicited local and systemic reactions were similar or lower than in non‑HIV participants for many reactions.
  - Study 7: Evaluated two‑vial presentation in HIV‑infected and non‑HIV participants including previously vaccinated subgroups; reported solicited reaction frequencies.
  - Study 8 (multicenter, open‑label, US/Mexico): Evaluated safety in participants with active or history of atopic dermatitis (350 with AD, 282 without AD); solicited reactions generally similar but some reactions (redness, swelling, chills, headache) reported at higher frequency in AD subgroup in one study.
  - Study 9 (open‑label, controlled, Germany): Evaluated two‑vial presentation in small cohorts with and without atopic dermatitis; some local/systemic reactions reported at higher frequency in AD subgroup.
- Outcomes reported in available sources from these trials focus primarily on safety and solicited adverse reactions; vaccine effectiveness and detailed immunogenicity endpoints are referenced in contents headings but specific efficacy outcomes and immunologic data are not included in the provided excerpts.
