# Editorial Product Information: Vecuronium Bromide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory information into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for educational and professional reference. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information approved in a specific jurisdiction, which should always be consulted for prescribing, dispensing, and administration.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Vecuronium bromide (a monoquaternary steroid derivative).
- **Brand Name(s):** **VECURE** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Powder for injection, 10 mg (lyophilised cake) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (bolus injection or continuous infusion).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Alphapharm Pty Ltd trading as Viatris [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Skeletal Muscle Relaxation:** Approved as an adjunct to general anaesthesia in adult and paediatric patients for all surgical procedures [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Initial Intubating Dose:** 0.1 mg/kg. This dose typically provides good to excellent intubating conditions within 2.5 to 3 minutes [Australian].
- **Maintenance (Incremental) Dose:** 0.02 to 0.04 mg/kg [Australian].
- **Duration of Action:** Skeletal muscle relaxation (to 25% recovery) typically lasts 20 to 40 minutes [Australian].

### Dosing with Suxamethonium
- If suxamethonium is used for intubation, the initial dose of vecuronium should be reduced to 0.04 to 0.06 mg/kg [Australian].
- Administration of vecuronium must be delayed until the patient begins recovering from suxamethonium-induced blockade [Australian].

### Continuous Infusion
- **Initiation:** Only after early evidence of spontaneous recovery from the loading dose.
- **Adult Infusion Rate:** 0.8 to 1.4 micrograms/kg/minute.
- **Titration:** Adjust to maintain twitch response at 10% of control height or 1 to 2 responses to train-of-four (TOF) stimulation [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Reconstitution:** Dissolve the 10 mg vial in 5 mL of water for injections [Australian].
- **Compatibility:** Once reconstituted, it can be mixed with sodium chloride 0.9%, glucose 5%, Ringer’s solution, or Ringer’s with glucose 2.5%. It can be injected into the line of running infusions of lactated Ringer’s (with or without glucose 5%), Haemaccel, or Dextran 40 5% in NaCl 0.9% [Australian].
- **Incompatibility:** Vecuronium is incompatible with thiopentone or solutions containing thiopentone [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to vecuronium bromide or the bromide ion [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Respiratory Paralysis:** Vecuronium causes paralysis of respiratory muscles; mandatory ventilatory support is required until spontaneous respiration is fully restored [Australian].
- **Anaphylaxis:** Severe, sometimes fatal, anaphylactic/anaphylactoid reactions have been reported. Cross-reactivity between neuromuscular blocking agents (NMBAs) is possible [Australian].
- **ICU Myopathy:** Prolonged paralysis and skeletal muscle weakness have been observed following long-term use in the Intensive Care Unit, particularly when used concomitantly with corticosteroids [Australian].
- **Residual Curarisation:** Clinicians must consider the risk of residual blockade after extubation; reversal agents (e.g., sugammadex or acetylcholinesterase inhibitors) should be considered [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Neuromuscular monitoring is strongly recommended to evaluate the degree of block and recovery [Australian].
- **Specific Clinical Conditions:** 
    - **Neuromuscular Disease:** Extreme caution is required in patients with myasthenia gravis or Eaton-Lambert syndrome. Small test doses and rigorous monitoring are advised.
    - **Burns:** Patients may develop resistance to non-depolarising NMBAs; dose titration is necessary.
    - **Hypothermia:** Neuromuscular block is increased and prolonged under hypothermic conditions [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse drug reactions (ADRs) are generally rare (<1/1,000). The most common involve changes in vital signs or prolonged blockade.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Uncommon/Rare** (<1/100 to >1/10,000) | Tachycardia, Hypotension [Australian] |
| **Very Rare** (<1/10,000) | Anaphylactic reaction, anaphylactoid reaction/shock, bronchospasm, circulatory collapse, flushing, angioneurotic oedema, urticaria, rash, flaccid paralysis, airway complications [Australian] |
| **Frequency Not Specified** | Muscular weakness or steroid myopathy (after long-term ICU use), injection site pain/reaction [Australian] |

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Increased Effect (Potentiation)
- **Anaesthetics:** Halogenated volatile anaesthetics (potentiate block and may inhibit reversal).
- **Antibiotics:** Aminoglycosides, lincosamides, polypeptide antibiotics, and acylaminopenicillins.
- **Other Medications:** Diuretics, quinidine, magnesium salts, calcium channel blockers, lithium salts, cimetidine, lidocaine, and acute administration of phenytoin or beta-blockers [Australian].

### Decreased Effect (Antagonism)
- Chronic administration of phenytoin or carbamazepine [Australian].

### Variable Effects
- Prior administration of other non-depolarising NMBAs or suxamethonium may either attenuate or potentiate the effect [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Vecuronium crosses the placenta. Animal studies show increased visceral abnormalities at maternotoxic doses. It should only be used if benefits outweigh potential risks to the fetus.
- **Caesarean Section:** Studies using doses up to 0.1 mg/kg have shown safety; it does not significantly affect Apgar scores or neonatal muscle tone [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** No human data available. Use only if benefits outweigh risks [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in rat studies [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - **Neonates/Premature Infants:** Not approved; no dosing recommendations [Australian].
    - **Infants (7 weeks to 1 year):** Moderately more sensitive than adults; onset is faster, but recovery is longer.
    - **Children (2 to 10 years):** Require higher doses (ED95) than adults, but the same intubation/maintenance doses are usually sufficient due to faster clearance/shorter duration.
- **Elderly Use:** Onset is similar to younger adults, but the duration of action is prolonged due to pharmacokinetic changes [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Clearance may be reduced, potentially prolonging the duration of action. Caution is advised [Australian].
- **Hepatic/Biliary Disease:** Prolonged duration of action has been observed, especially at higher doses (0.15–0.2 mg/kg). Vecuronium is primarily excreted in bile [Australian].
- **Obesity:** Dosing should be reduced and calculated based on **ideal body weight** [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A non-depolarising NMBA that competes with acetylcholine for nicotinic cholinergic receptors at the motor endplate.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Does not produce vagolytic or ganglion-blocking effects at clinical doses. Histamine release is not typically clinically significant.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Distributed primarily in the extracellular fluid; Vd is 0.16–0.51 L/kg in adults.
    - **Metabolism:** Partially metabolised in the liver to a 3-hydroxy derivative (approximately 50% less potent).
    - **Elimination:** Primarily via bile (40–60% within 24 hours, mostly as unchanged drug). Renal elimination accounts for 20–30%.
    - **Half-life:** Plasma elimination half-life ranges from 36 to 117 minutes [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No pivotal trial data was specified in the provided source document [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C and protect from light [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Once reconstituted, the solution should be used immediately to avoid microbial contamination; any residue must be discarded [Australian].
- **Physical Characteristics:** Reconstituted solution should be clear and free of particles [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **S4** (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
- **Excipient Warning:** Contains gluten from maize and/or wheat [Australian].
