# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction:
    - **United States**: US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) – Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation).
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is intended for clinical reference and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.
- **Note**: At the time of preparation, only the United States PI was available for review. Clinical recommendations and regulatory statuses in other regions (e.g., Australia, Europe, Canada) may vary.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Levocarnitine (INN).
- **Chemical Class**: Carrier molecule (amino acid derivative).
- **Brand Name(s)**: Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code**: Not specified in available sources (Commonly A16AA01).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s)**: 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration**: Intravenous only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism**: For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD)**: For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders**:
    - **Recommended dose**: 50 mg/kg.
    - **Administration**: Slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or infusion.
    - **Acute Crisis**: A loading dose is often given, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the next 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    - **Maintenance**: Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg may be required based on clinical response [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis**:
    - **Recommended starting dose**: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - **Administration**: Slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Dose adjustment**: Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
- **Compatibility**: Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL [US].

### Monitoring
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations.
- Conduct weekly and monthly monitoring including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions**: Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, often within minutes of administration. These are most frequently reported in patients with ESRD on dialysis. Discontinue immediately if a reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Risk**: Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Oral Formulation Caution**: The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide), which are normally excreted in urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5% in ESRD trials)
Reported in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in dialysis patients (regardless of causality):
- **Body as a Whole**: Pain (30%), injection site reaction (33%), chest pain (12%), headache (22%), infection (15%), fever (7%), flu syndrome (25%).
- **Cardiovascular**: Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%).
- **Digestive**: Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), abdominal pain (9%).
- **Nervous System**: Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), paresthesia (5%).
- **Respiratory**: Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%).

### Other Reported Effects
- **Metabolic**: Hypercalcemia (9%), hypervolemia (5%).
- **Special Senses**: Taste perversion (3%).
- **Other**: Body odor and gastritis (less frequent) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin**: Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concomitantly. **Management**: Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: [US Category: Not formally assigned letter in provided text, but consistent with Category B]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk; however, supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Consider the benefits to the mother versus the potential risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility**: No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg) as per adult metabolic disorder recommendations [US].
- **Elderly Use**: Not specifically addressed in the available source [US].
- **Renal Impairment**: Specifically indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. Note the specific dosing for this population (10-20 mg/kg post-dialysis) and the warning against chronic high-dose oral use in this group [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, facilitating oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics**: Clears accumulated toxic acyl-CoA compounds in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine, which is rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Distribution**: Not protein-bound. Mean distribution half-life: 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism**: Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide and γ-butyrobetaine [US].
    - **Elimination**: Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life: 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Dosing Studies**: Clinical studies in ESRD patients demonstrated that a dose of 20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis significantly increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations from pre-treatment levels of ~20 µmol/L (pre-dialysis) to stable trough levels between 54 and 180 µmol/L after one week of therapy [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling**: Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States**: Rx-only [US].
