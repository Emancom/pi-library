# Editorial Product Information: Von Willebrand Factor / Coagulation Factor VIII Complex (Human)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) documents from the following jurisdictions: **Australia (Therapeutic Goods Administration)** and the **United States (Food and Drug Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data from multiple regions into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI. Clinicians must consult the local PI for prescribing and legal requirements in their specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Human Von Willebrand Factor (VWF) and Human Coagulation Factor VIII (FVIII) complex.
- **Brand Names:** 
    - **Biostate** [Australian]
    - **Wilate** [US]
- **ATC Code:** B02BD06 (Von Willebrand factor and blood coagulation factor VIII in combination)
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Biostate [Australian]:** Lyophilised powder for injection (VWF:FVIII ratio 2.4:1). Available as:
        - 250 IU FVIII / 600 IU VWF (5 mL)
        - 500 IU FVIII / 1200 IU VWF (5 mL or 10 mL)
        - 1000 IU FVIII / 2400 IU VWF (10 mL)
    - **Wilate [US]:** Lyophilised powder for injection (VWF:FVIII ratio approximately 1:1). Available as:
        - 500 IU VWF:RCo / 500 IU FVIII (5 mL)
        - 1000 IU VWF:RCo / 1000 IU FVIII (10 mL)
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous injection or infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** 
    - **CSL Behring (Australia) Pty Ltd** [Australian]
    - **Octapharma USA Inc.** [US]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Von Willebrand Disease (VWD):**
    - Prophylaxis and treatment of non-surgical and surgical bleeding (perioperative management) when desmopressin (DDAVP) is ineffective or contraindicated [Australian].
    - On-demand treatment and control of bleeding episodes; perioperative management of bleeding; routine prophylaxis to reduce the frequency of bleeding episodes [US].
- **Haemophilia A:**
    - Prophylaxis and treatment of non-surgical and surgical bleeding associated with FVIII deficiency due to Haemophilia A [Australian].
    - On-demand treatment and control of bleeding episodes; routine prophylaxis to reduce the frequency of bleeding episodes in patients 12 years of age and older [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing Principles
Dosage must be individualised based on the patient's weight, the type and severity of the bleeding episode, the desired level of Factor VIII/VWF, and clinical response. 

**Note on Ratios:** Biostate [Australian] and Wilate [US] have significantly different VWF to FVIII ratios (2.4:1 vs. 1:1). Dosing calculations must be brand-specific to avoid over- or under-dosing of either component.

### Von Willebrand Disease (VWD)
- **Dosing Basis:** VWF:RCo (or VWF:Ac) and FVIII:C levels.
- **Dosing Formulas:**
    - **Wilate [US]:** Required IU = body weight (kg) x desired VWF:RCo rise (%) x 0.5.
- **Therapeutic Goals:**
    - **Minor Bleeding:** Trough levels of VWF/FVIII >30% [Australian/US].
    - **Major Bleeding:** Trough levels of VWF/FVIII >50% [Australian/US].
    - **Surgery:** Peak VWF levels should reach 50% for minor surgery and 100% for major surgery [Australian/US]. In order to decrease the risk of perioperative thrombosis, FVIII activity levels should not exceed 250% [US].

### Haemophilia A
- **Dosing Basis:** FVIII activity (FVIII:C).
- **Dosing Formulas:**
    - **Wilate [US]:** Required IU = body weight (kg) x desired Factor VIII rise (%) x 0.5.
- **General Guidelines:** 1 IU/kg of FVIII increases plasma FVIII activity by approximately 2% [Australian/US].
- **Prophylaxis:**
    - **Biostate [Australian]:** 25–40 IU/kg three times weekly.
    - **Wilate [US]:** 20–40 IU/kg every 2 to 3 days (adolescents and adults).

### Administration
- **Reconstitution:** Use the provided transfer device (Mix2Vial [Australian] or Nextaro [US]). Do not shake; swirl gently until dissolved.
- **Infusion Rate:**
    - **Biostate [Australian]:** Not to exceed 6 mL per minute.
    - **Wilate [US]:** Recommended speed of 2–4 mL per minute.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to von Willebrand factor, Coagulation Factor VIII, or any excipients (e.g., sodium, sucrose, amino acids).
- History of anaphylactic or severe systemic response to human plasma-derived products.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity:** Allergic-type reactions (hives, urticaria, chest tightness, wheezing, hypotension) may occur and can progress to anaphylaxis. If symptoms occur, discontinue use immediately.
- **Thromboembolic Risk:** Continued treatment with FVIII-containing VWF products may cause an excessive rise in FVIII:C levels, increasing the risk of thrombotic events. Monitor FVIII levels closely, especially in surgical settings.
- **Inhibitors:** 
    - **FVIII:** Neutralising antibodies (inhibitors) to FVIII are a known complication in Haemophilia A.
    - **VWF:** VWD patients (especially Type 3) may develop inhibitors to VWF, leading to inadequate clinical response and potential anaphylactic reactions.
- **Pathogen Safety:** As these products are derived from human plasma, the risk of transmitting infectious agents (viruses like HIV, HBV, HCV, HAV, and Parvovirus B19, and theoretically CJD) cannot be totally excluded.
- **Monitoring:** Periodic monitoring of plasma VWF and FVIII activity is required to confirm adequate levels and detect inhibitors.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequencies are categorised as follows:
- **Very Common (>10%):** Headache [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** 
    - Hypersensitivity reactions (tachycardia, chest pain/discomfort) [Australian/US].
    - Pyrexia (fever) [Australian/US].
    - FVIII inhibition in Haemophilia A [Australian].
    - Dizziness [US].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** 
    - Dysgeusia [Australian].
    - Thromboembolic events [Australian].
    - Abnormal liver function tests [Australian].
    - Pruritus, sleeping disorders [US].
- **Post-Marketing Experience (Frequency Not Known/Very Rare):** Hypertension, dyspnea, cough, abdominal pain, back pain, and VWF inhibition.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- No specific interaction studies have been conducted.
- Do not mix with other medicinal products or administer simultaneously in the same infusion set [Australian/US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category:** Not formally categorized, but PI states it should be used only if clearly indicated, noting the increased risk of haemorrhage during delivery.
    - **US Category:** No data in pregnant women. Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. Use only if clearly indicated.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been established. Information on the presence of the drug in human milk is unavailable. Benefit/risk assessment is required.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies have not been conducted to evaluate effects on fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - **Australian:** Approved for VWD and Haemophilia A. Prophylactic dosing for VWD in children <12 years is recommended at 40–80 IU VWF:Ac/kg 1–3 times weekly.
    - **US:** Approved for VWD (all ages) and Haemophilia A ($\ge$ 12 years). For VWD prophylaxis in children <6 years, a higher dose of 30–50 IU/kg is suggested. Safety/efficacy in Haemophilia A <12 years is not established for Wilate.
- **Elderly:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in appropriate studies; however, clinical reports suggest no significant differences compared to younger adults. Dose selection should be cautious.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **VWF:** Promotes platelet aggregation and adhesion to damaged vascular endothelium and serves as a stabilising carrier protein for Factor VIII.
    - **FVIII:** An essential cofactor for the activation of Factor X in the intrinsic pathway of blood coagulation, leading to thrombin and fibrin formation.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Replacement therapy temporarily corrects the VWF and FVIII deficiency, enabling haemostasis.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Half-life (VWF:RCo):** ~13.7 to 15.8 hours in adults.
    - **Half-life (FVIII:C):** ~13.4 to 19.6 hours in adults.
    - **Recovery:** 1 IU/kg of VWF:RCo increases activity by ~1.5–2.1% [Australian/US]. 1 IU/kg of FVIII increases activity by ~2% [Australian/US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **VWD:** Studies in adult and paediatric populations showed successful control of non-surgical and surgical bleeding in 84% to 100% of cases [Australian/US]. Routine prophylaxis significantly reduced the Annualised Bleeding Rate (ABR) in both paediatric and adult patients [US].
- **Haemophilia A:** Prospective trials demonstrated high efficacy (over 95%) in managing bleeding episodes and preventing perioperative bleeding. Prophylaxis studies showed a significant reduction in spontaneous bleeds [Australian/US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Shelf Life:** Up to 36 months [US]; refer to carton for specific expiry [Australian].
- **Temperature:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze.
- **Room Temperature Excursion:** 
    - **Biostate [Australian]:** Can be stored below 25°C for a single period of 6 months.
    - **Wilate [US]:** Can be stored at room temperature (up to 25°C) for up to 6 months.
- **Post-Reconstitution:** Reconstituted solution should be used as soon as practicable; if necessary, it may be kept for up to 4 hours [US] or 8 hours [Australian] at room temperature. Do not refrigerate after reconstitution.
- **Light:** Keep vials in the original carton to protect from light.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Unscheduled [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only Medicine (Rx) [US].
