# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions’ Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes. It is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing and dispensing in a particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); also known as L-carnitine. It is a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strengths:** 
    - **Intravenous Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Metabolic):** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Administer intravenously as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often administered in patients with severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; intervals should not be less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Dose Range:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised as clinically required [US].
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
- **Administration Timing:** Administer into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations (normal range: 40 to 50 µmol/L). Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are supratherapeutic [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None Known:** There are currently no known contraindications for levocarnitine injection [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately. Re-administration should only be considered after a thorough risk-benefit analysis [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known seizure disorder, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation Precaution):** While this document concerns the injection, clinicians should note that the safety/efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal impairment. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) normally cleared by the kidneys [US].
- **General Monitoring:** Monitoring of plasma carnitine levels is essential to guide dosing, particularly in ESRD and metabolic crisis [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are frequently confounded by the underlying pathology of the patient populations (ESRD or metabolic disorders).

### Common Adverse Effects (≥5% Frequency in ESRD Trials)
| Body System | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), flu syndrome (25%), headache (22%), chest pain (12%), infection (15%), abdominal pain (9%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%) |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%) |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%), hypervolemia (5%) |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), paresthesia (5%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%) |

### Serious and Post-Marketing Reactions
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent: body odor and gastritis [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, laryngeal edema [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
    - **Management:** Monitor INR levels closely following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. 
    - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** 
    - Supplemental levocarnitine in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. 
    - **Recommendation:** The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders are weight-based (50 mg/kg), which applies to paediatric populations [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific dose adjustments for the elderly are not detailed in the source, though general caution regarding organ function is standard clinical practice [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** In ESRD patients on dialysis, dosing is adjusted based on pre-dialysis trough levels (see Section 3). The intravenous route is preferred to avoid accumulation of metabolites associated with high-dose oral use [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production in all tissues except the brain [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with metabolic errors, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted. This reduces the risk of life-threatening acidosis [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following a 20 mg/kg IV bolus, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (n=12), 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis trough levocarnitine levels from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. While increases in carnitine levels were demonstrated, the effect on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8 mg/mL [US].
- **Handling:** Single-dose vial; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservative [US].
