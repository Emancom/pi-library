# Editorial Product Information: Velaglucerase alfa ghu

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Velaglucerase alfa ghu, a human cell line-derived glycoprotein produced by gene-activation technology.
- **Chemical Class:** Enzyme Replacement Therapy (ERT).
- **Brand Name:** **VPRIV** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A16AB10.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Lyophilised powder for solution for infusion. Supplied as 400 Units (10 mg) per single-use vial [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Takeda Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Type 1 Gaucher Disease:** Indicated for long-term enzyme replacement therapy (ERT) in both paediatric and adult patients [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Recommended Dose:** 60 U/kg administered every other week.
- **Individualisation:** Dosing may be adjusted (ranging from 15 to 60 U/kg every other week) based on the achievement and maintenance of specific therapeutic goals [Australian].
- **Switching from Other ERT:** Patients currently receiving other ERT for type 1 Gaucher disease may be transitioned to **VPRIV** at the same dose and frequency [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Preparation:** Reconstitute the 400 U vial with 4.3 mL of Sterile Water for Injections to yield a concentration of 100 U/mL (extractable volume of 4.0 mL).
- **Dilution:** The calculated volume should be further diluted in 100 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride.
- **Handling:** Mix gently; do not shake. Reconstituted solution should be clear to slightly opalescent and colourless.
- **Infusion:** Administer via a 60-minute intravenous infusion using a 0.2 µm in-line filter.
- **Home Administration:** May be considered for patients who have tolerated at least three infusions in a hospital setting. This must be performed by trained healthcare professionals [Australian].

### Storage After Preparation
- Reconstituted and diluted solutions should ideally be used immediately. If necessary, they may be stored at 2-8°C for up to 24 hours under protection from light [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Severe or life-threatening hypersensitivity to the active substance or any of its excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity and Anaphylaxis:** Hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis, have been reported. Medical support for emergency treatment must be available during administration. Caution is advised when treating patients who have previously reacted to other ERTs [Australian].
- **Infusion-Related Reactions (IRRs):** IRRs are the most common adverse effects (occurring in approximately 62% of patients). Most are mild. Management includes slowing the infusion rate or administering antihistamines, antipyretics, and/or corticosteroids. Pre-medication may be used to prevent recurrence [Australian].
- **Pulmonary Hypertension:** Patients presenting with respiratory symptoms should be evaluated for pulmonary hypertension, a known complication of Gaucher disease [Australian].
- **Immunogenicity:** Approximately 1% of patients in clinical trials developed IgG-class antibodies (some with neutralising activity). Antibody testing is recommended if there is a loss of clinical effect or severe IRR [Australian].
- **Gaucher Disease Types 2 and 3:** No clinical experience is available for the use of **VPRIV** in patients with type 2 or 3 Gaucher disease [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are classified by frequency as follows:

| Frequency | Adverse Reactions |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Infusion-related reactions, headache, dizziness, abdominal pain (including upper), bone pain, arthralgia, back pain, asthenia/fatigue, pyrexia. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Nausea, activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT) prolonged, neutralising antibody positive, chest discomfort, flushing, hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, dyspnoea, rash, urticaria, pruritus, hypersensitivity reactions. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Vomiting (noted as potentially serious in post-marketing reports). |

- **Serious Reactions:** Anaphylactoid reactions and allergic dermatitis have been reported [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Formal interaction studies have not been conducted [Australian].
- **Compatibility:** **VPRIV** should not be infused with other products in the same tubing as compatibility data are unavailable [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B2]. Animal studies do not indicate direct harm, but human data are limited. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if velaglucerase alfa is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution when prescribing to lactating women [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Data regarding the effect on human fertility are not included in the source [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Evaluated in patients aged 4 to 17 years. Safety and efficacy were similar to adults. While inclusion was allowed for children aged 2 years and older, no data are available for children under 4 years [Australian].
- **Elderly Use (≥65 years):** Limited data (n=56 in clinical studies) suggest no specific dose adjustment is required [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No clinical data are available for patients with renal or hepatic insufficiency. However, based on pharmacokinetic knowledge, no dose adjustment is recommended [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Supplements or replaces the deficient lysosomal enzyme beta-glucocerebrosidase, catalysing the hydrolysis of glucocerebroside into glucose and ceramide. This reduces the accumulation of Gaucher cells in organs.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Treatment leads to increased haemoglobin concentration, increased platelet count, and reduced volumes of the liver and spleen [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Serum concentrations rise rapidly during the first 20 minutes of infusion, reaching $C_{max}$ between 40–60 minutes. It has a high clearance (6.7 to 7.6 mL/min/kg) and a short half-life ($t_{1/2}$) of 5 to 12 minutes [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** The clinical program included five studies (025, 032, 039, 034, and 025EXT) involving 94 patients.
- **Key Outcomes:**
    - **Treatment-naïve patients:** Significant improvements in haemoglobin and platelets were seen within 3 months; liver/spleen volume reductions were seen by 6–9 months.
    - **Extension Data:** Over 7 years of treatment, patients showed sustained clinical activity and improvement in bone mineral density (BMD) Z-scores.
    - **Switching Studies:** Patients stable on imiglucerase successfully maintained therapeutic goals when transitioned to **VPRIV** at the same dose [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (refrigerate). Do not freeze.
- **Light Protection:** Keep the vial in the outer carton to protect from light [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].
