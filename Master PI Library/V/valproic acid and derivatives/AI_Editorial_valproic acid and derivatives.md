# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, legally approved Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP), a carrier molecule essential for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (quaternary ammonium compound).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection containing 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metabolic Disorders:** For the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Metabolic Disorders)
- **Standard Dose:** 50 mg/kg, administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often utilised in patients presenting with severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Typically administered every 3 or 4 hours (q3h or q4h); administration should occur no less frequently than every 6 hours (q6h) [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported daily dose is 300 mg/kg [US].

### Dosing in ESRD/Hemodialysis
- **Initial Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
- **Administration Timing:** Administer as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of treatment if levels are adequate [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].
- Discard any unused portion as the product contains no preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known:** There are currently no listed contraindications for levocarnitine in available source documents [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These typically occur in ESRD patients on dialysis, sometimes within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and monitor if re-administration is attempted [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Levocarnitine may increase the frequency or severity of seizures in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a prior history [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:**
    - **Metabolic Disorders:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Monitor weekly and monthly thereafter, including blood chemistry and vital signs. Target free carnitine levels are between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
    - **ESRD:** Monitor trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels (normal range 40 to 50 µmol/L) to guide dosing [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** 
    - **General:** Injection site reaction (27–59%), pain (21–35%), flu syndrome (15–40%), headache (3–37%), asthenia [US].
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (14–21%), hypotension (3–19%) [US].
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%), abdominal pain (5–21%) [US].
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–33%), cough (10–18%), rhinitis (6–11%), dyspnea (3–19%) [US].
- **Common (1–10%):** Fever, chest pain, back pain, tachycardia, palpitations, hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, dizziness, paresthesia, pruritus [US].
- **Other Notable Effects:** 
    - **Neurologic:** Seizures (postmarketing) [US].
    - **Digestive:** Transient nausea, vomiting, gastritis, and body odor [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalised Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended to monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Narrative summary provided]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly required, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after administration. Risks of excess intake in the infant should be weighed against the maternal benefit; discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy are established for the management of metabolic disorders resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide). The injectable form is specifically indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The distribution half-life is 0.585 hours, and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Dialysis Studies:** Clinical trials (9 weeks and dose-ranging studies) demonstrated that IV administration of 20 mg/kg post-dialysis effectively increases plasma levocarnitine concentrations from subnormal (approx. 20 µmol/L) to concentrations above the normal range (e.g., 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks). While carnitine levels increase, the specific effect on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of opened vials; the product is preservative-free [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription-only (Rx only).
