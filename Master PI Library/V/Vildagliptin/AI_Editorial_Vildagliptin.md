# Editorial Product Information: Vildagliptin / Metformin Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis incorporates information from the following jurisdiction: **Australia** (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of the fixed-dose combination of vildagliptin and metformin hydrochloride for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in specific jurisdictions. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Vildagliptin (a dipeptidyl-peptidase-4 inhibitor) and Metformin hydrochloride (a biguanide).
- **Brand Names:** ARX-VILDAGLIPTIN/METFORMIN [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A10BD08 (vildagliptin and metformin).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets in three strengths: 50 mg/500 mg, 50 mg/850 mg, and 50 mg/1,000 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM):** Indicated as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycaemic control in adults:
    - Whose diabetes is inadequately controlled on metformin hydrochloride alone.
    - Who are already receiving vildagliptin and metformin as separate tablets.
    - In triple combination with a sulfonylurea for patients inadequately controlled on metformin and a sulfonylurea.
    - As an add-on to insulin in patients where a stable dose of insulin and metformin alone do not provide adequate glycaemic control [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** Dosing must be individualised based on current therapy, effectiveness, and tolerability.
- **Maximum Dose:** The maximum daily dose of vildagliptin is 100 mg [Australian].
- **Administration:** Should be taken with meals to reduce the gastrointestinal side effects associated with metformin. 
- **Initiation:** 
    - **Inadequately controlled on metformin monotherapy:** Start with 50/500 mg, 50/850 mg, or 50/1,000 mg twice daily based on the current metformin dose.
    - **Switching from separate tablets:** Initiate based on the dose of vildagliptin or metformin already being taken.
    - **Add-on to sulfonylurea or insulin:** Dosing should provide 50 mg vildagliptin twice daily and a metformin dose similar to that already being taken.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Titration:** Should be gradual to improve gastrointestinal tolerability.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to vildagliptin, metformin hydrochloride, or any excipients.
- Severe renal impairment (GFR <30 mL/min).
- Congestive heart failure requiring pharmacological treatment.
- Acute or chronic metabolic acidosis, including lactic acidosis or diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA), with or without coma.
- Radiologic studies involving intravascular administration of iodinated contrast (temporary discontinuation required) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Lactic Acidosis:** A life-threatening metabolic complication due to metformin accumulation. Risk factors include renal impairment, advanced age (>80 years), and high doses of metformin (>2 g/day). Treatment must be stopped immediately if suspected.
- **Hepatic Monitoring:** Rare cases of hepatic dysfunction (including hepatitis) have been reported. 
    - Perform LFTs before initiation.
    - Monitor every 3 months for the first year and periodically thereafter.
    - Discontinue if AST/ALT ≥3x ULN persists or if jaundice occurs [Australian].
- **Renal Function:** GFR should be assessed prior to initiation and at least annually thereafter (more frequently in the elderly or those at risk of progression).
- **Pancreatitis:** Post-marketing reports of pancreatitis have been noted. Patients should be informed of characteristic symptoms (persistent, severe abdominal pain).
- **Bullous Pemphigoid:** Reported with DPP-4 inhibitors. Discontinue therapy if suspected and refer to a dermatologist.
- **Mitochondrial Diseases:** Not recommended in patients with MELAS syndrome or MIDD due to increased risk of lactic acidosis [Australian].
- **Hypothyroidism:** Metformin may reduce TSH levels in hypothyroid patients. Regular monitoring is recommended.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are presented by frequency based on clinical study data for the combination or individual components:

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (>10%) | Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal pain, loss of appetite (primarily during metformin initiation). |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Tremor, dizziness, headache, metallic taste (metformin), asthenia, hyperhidrosis (with sulfonylurea), decreased blood glucose. |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Constipation, peripheral oedema (vildagliptin monotherapy), flatulence. |
| **Rare** (<0.1%) | Hepatic dysfunction, hepatitis (reversible), angioedema. |
| **Very Rare** (<0.01%) | Lactic acidosis, Vitamin B12 deficiency (long-term metformin use), skin reactions (erythema, pruritus). |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Lactic acidosis, pancreatitis, and bullous pemphigoid.
- **Weight:** Vildagliptin is considered weight-neutral when added to metformin.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Vildagliptin:** Low potential for interactions. Not a CYP450 substrate, inhibitor, or inducer. No clinically relevant interactions with pioglitazone, digoxin, warfarin, or amlodipine.
- **Metformin:**
    - **Iodinated Contrast:** Must be discontinued 48 hours prior to and after the procedure to avoid contrast-induced nephropathy.
    - **OCT2/MATE Inhibitors:** Drugs like cimetidine, dolutegravir, and ranolazine may increase metformin exposure.
    - **Glycaemia-altering Drugs:** Thiazides, corticosteroids, and thyroid products may cause hyperglycaemia; ACE inhibitors and diuretics may increase lactic acidosis risk via renal impairment.
    - **Alcohol:** Potentiates metformin's effect on lactate metabolism; avoid excessive intake.
    - **Levothyroxine:** Can reduce the hypoglycaemic effect of metformin [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **[Australian]: Category C.** Not recommended for use during pregnancy unless potential benefits justify the risk to the foetus. Insulin monotherapy is generally preferred for blood glucose management during pregnancy.
- **Breastfeeding:** Metformin is excreted in human milk; vildagliptin status is unknown. The product should not be administered to breastfeeding women.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies (rats) showed no evidence of impaired fertility for either component at doses significantly exceeding human exposure [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children <18 years have not been established; use is not recommended.
- **Elderly Use:** As metformin is renally excreted, monitor renal function regularly. Treatment should not be initiated in those ≥80 years unless CrCl is normal.
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **GFR 60-89 mL/min:** No dose adjustment needed; maximum metformin 3000 mg/day.
    - **GFR 45-59 mL/min:** Max metformin 2000 mg/day; vildagliptin max 50 mg/day.
    - **GFR 30-44 mL/min:** Max metformin 1000 mg/day; vildagliptin max 50 mg/day.
    - **GFR <30 mL/min:** Contraindicated.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not recommended for use in patients with clinical or laboratory evidence of hepatic impairment (pre-treatment ALT/AST >2.5x ULN).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Vildagliptin:** Inhibits the DPP-4 enzyme, preventing the degradation of incretin hormones (GLP-1 and GIP). This increases insulin secretion and suppresses glucagon release in a glucose-dependent manner.
    - **Metformin:** Reduces hepatic glucose production, decreases intestinal absorption, and improves insulin sensitivity by increasing peripheral glucose uptake.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Vildagliptin absolute bioavailability is 85%; metformin bioavailability is 50-60%. Food delays metformin absorption but does not affect the extent of vildagliptin absorption.
    - **Metabolism:** Vildagliptin is primarily metabolised via hydrolysis (LAY151); metformin is not metabolised.
    - **Elimination:** Vildagliptin is excreted mainly in urine (85%); metformin is excreted unchanged in urine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Combination Efficacy:** Clinical studies involving over 15,000 patients showed that adding vildagliptin to metformin significantly reduced HbA1c and fasting plasma glucose. 
- **Durability:** Improvements in glucose homeostasis were maintained in studies up to 52 weeks.
- **Add-on to Insulin:** A 24-week study showed a placebo-adjusted HbA1c reduction of 0.72% when vildagliptin was added to insulin [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Safety:** A meta-analysis of 9,599 patients confirmed that vildagliptin is not associated with an increase in major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Conditions:** Store in the original package and protect from moisture.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].
