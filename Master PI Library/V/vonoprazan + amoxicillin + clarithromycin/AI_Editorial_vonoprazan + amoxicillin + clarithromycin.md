# Editorial Product Information: vonoprazan + amoxicillin + clarithromycin

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **vonoprazan + amoxicillin + clarithromycin**
- Chemical class:
  - Vonoprazan: potassium-competitive acid blocker (PCAB)
  - Amoxicillin: penicillin-class antibacterial (β‑lactam)
  - Clarithromycin: macrolide antibacterial
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral
- DO NOT include brand or trade names, dosage-form physical descriptions, or local scheduling/classification

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial approval in United States: 2022
- Last revision/update of the available product information: 11/2025 [United States]
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources
- Approvals or regulatory dates for other jurisdictions: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Indicated for the treatment of Helicobacter pylori infection in adults.
  - This indication is explicitly stated for the product as a co-packaged regimen containing the three components in adults. [United States]
- Use to reduce development of antibiotic resistance: should be used only to treat infections proven or strongly suspected to be caused by susceptible bacteria.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Standard duration for both regimens: 14 days.
- Two co-packaged regimen options described:
  - Triple regimen (vonoprazan + amoxicillin + clarithromycin)
    - Vonoprazan 20 mg twice daily (morning and evening, ~12 hours apart)
    - Amoxicillin 1,000 mg twice daily (morning and evening)
    - Clarithromycin 500 mg twice daily (morning and evening)
    - Administer with or without food.
  - Dual regimen (vonoprazan + amoxicillin) — clarithromycin omitted
    - Vonoprazan 20 mg twice daily (morning and evening)
    - Amoxicillin 1,000 mg three times daily (morning, mid-day, evening)
    - Administer with or without food.

### Dosing by special condition
- Renal impairment (adults) [United States]:
  - Estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) ≥ 30 mL/min: use standard adult dosing as above.
  - eGFR < 30 mL/min: use is not recommended.
- Hepatic impairment (adults) [United States]:
  - Child‑Pugh Class A: standard adult dosing as above.
  - Child‑Pugh Class B or C: use is not recommended.

### Administration instructions
- Take doses approximately 12 hours apart for twice-daily components.
- Amoxicillin in the dual regimen should be dosed three times daily (morning, mid-day, evening).
- Can be taken with or without food.

### Missed dose
- If a dose is missed, administer as soon as possible within 4 hours of the missed dose. If more than 4 hours have elapsed, skip the missed dose and resume the regular dosing schedule. Continue until the full course is completed.

### Titration
- Not specified in available sources

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to any component: vonoprazan, amoxicillin (or other β‑lactam antibacterials), clarithromycin (or other macrolide antibacterials), or any excipient.
- Concomitant use with rilpivirine-containing products. [United States]
- Additional contraindications applicable when clarithromycin is included (triple regimen) [United States]:
  - Pimozide
  - Lomitapide
  - Lovastatin
  - Simvastatin
  - Ergot alkaloids (ergotamine, dihydroergotamine)
  - Colchicine in patients with renal or hepatic impairment
  - History of cholestatic jaundice or hepatic dysfunction associated with prior use of clarithromycin
  - Lurasidone (coadministration may increase exposure and risk of serious adverse reactions)

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources as a distinct boxed warning. (Serious risks and contraindications are described below.)

### General warnings and precautions
- Hypersensitivity reactions: Serious and occasionally fatal hypersensitivity (including anaphylaxis, anaphylactic shock, severe cutaneous reactions) have been reported with components. Discontinue immediately if suspected.
- Acute tubulointerstitial nephritis: Reported with vonoprazan; discontinue and evaluate if suspected.
- Severe cutaneous adverse reactions (SCAR): SJS, TEN, DRESS, AGEP and other SCARs have been reported with components. Discontinue at first signs.
- Drug-induced enterocolitis syndrome (DIES): Reported with amoxicillin (more commonly in pediatric patients ≤18 years). Characterized by delayed severe vomiting 1–4 hours after ingestion; may lead to pallor, lethargy, hypotension/shock, diarrhea, leukocytosis with neutrophilia. Discontinue if suspected and treat appropriately.
- Clostridioides difficile-associated diarrhea (CDAD): Reported with acid suppression and antibacterial agents (including amoxicillin and clarithromycin). Consider CDAD in any patient with persistent or severe diarrhea after antibiotic exposure; discontinue therapy if CDAD confirmed and manage appropriately.
- Rash in infectious mononucleosis: High incidence of erythematous rash in patients with mononucleosis who receive amoxicillin; avoid in patients with mononucleosis.
- Diagnostic test interference: Gastric acid suppression increases serum chromogranin A (CgA), potentially producing false positive results in neuroendocrine tumor testing. Assess CgA at least 4 weeks after completing therapy and consider repeat testing if initial results are high.
- Development of antibacterial resistance: Avoid use when infection is not proven or strongly suspected to be bacterial.

### Additional clarithromycin-specific warnings and precautions (triple regimen)
- QT prolongation and torsades de pointes risk: Avoid triple regimen in patients with known QT prolongation, ventricular arrhythmias, congenital long QT, uncompensated electrolyte abnormalities (e.g., hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia), clinically significant bradycardia, or concomitant use of Class IA or Class III antiarrhythmics or other QT‑prolonging drugs (e.g., pimozide). Elderly patients may be at higher risk.
- Hepatotoxicity: Clarithromycin has been associated with hepatic dysfunction, hepatitis (hepatocellular and/or cholestatic), and rare cases of hepatic failure; discontinue if signs/symptoms of hepatitis occur.
- Serious adverse reactions from drug interactions: Clarithromycin can produce severe and sometimes fatal interactions with drugs metabolized by CYP3A4 (see Section 8).

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor for signs of hypersensitivity, SCAR, and DIES.
- Monitor renal function as clinically indicated; discontinue in suspected acute tubulointerstitial nephritis.
- Monitor hepatic function if clinical signs/symptoms of hepatic dysfunction occur; discontinue triple regimen if hepatitis suspected.
- Monitor ECG and electrolytes in patients at risk for QT prolongation when triple regimen is used concomitantly with other QT‑prolonging agents.
- Monitor for signs of Clostridioides difficile infection in patients with diarrhea.
- If coadministration of colchicine with clarithromycin cannot be avoided in patients with normal renal and hepatic function, reduce the colchicine dose and monitor for toxicity. Colchicine coadministration is contraindicated in patients with renal or hepatic impairment when using clarithromycin.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Overview: Components have clinically important pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic interactions, particularly clarithromycin (CYP3A4 inhibition) and vonoprazan (acid suppression affecting absorption and diagnostic assays). Careful review of concomitant medications is required.

### Key clinically significant interactions (management notes included)
- Rilpivirine-containing products: Concomitant use contraindicated. [United States]
- Clarithromycin-related contraindications/serious interactions (triple regimen) [United States]:
  - Pimozide: Contraindicated due to risk of QT prolongation and fatal arrhythmia.
  - Lomitapide: Contraindicated; concomitant use may markedly increase transaminases. If clarithromycin must be used, suspend lomitapide.
  - Lovastatin and simvastatin: Contraindicated due to increased risk of myopathy/rhabdomyolysis. Suspend statin therapy if clarithromycin must be used.
  - Ergot alkaloids (ergotamine, dihydroergotamine): Contraindicated due to risk of ergot toxicity.
  - Colchicine: Contraindicated in patients with renal or hepatic impairment; life‑threatening interactions reported. If coadministration is necessary in patients with normal renal/hepatic function, reduce colchicine dose and monitor for toxicity.
  - Lurasidone: Coadministration may increase lurasidone exposure and risk of serious adverse reactions; avoid.
- Drugs metabolized by CYP3A4: Clarithromycin is a CYP3A4 inhibitor and can increase exposure to multiple CYP3A4 substrates, potentially causing serious adverse effects (examples reported):
  - Statins (simvastatin, lovastatin, atorvastatin): increased risk of rhabdomyolysis.
  - Disopyramide: risk of hypoglycemia and cardiac arrhythmias.
  - Calcium channel blockers metabolized by CYP3A4 (verapamil, diltiazem, amlodipine, nifedipine): reports of hypotension and acute kidney injury, particularly in elderly patients.
  - Lomitapide, colchicine (see above).
- Colchicine: Life‑threatening/fatal interactions reported when combined with clarithromycin; contraindicated in renal/hepatic impairment; reduce colchicine dose and monitor in patients with normal renal/hepatic function if coadministration unavoidable.
- Statins: If clarithromycin cannot be avoided, suspend lovastatin or simvastatin.
- Calcium channel blockers: Monitor for hypotension and acute kidney injury; use with caution.
- Drugs that prolong QT interval: Concomitant use with clarithromycin increases risk of QT prolongation/torsades de pointes; avoid combinations that increase QT risk.
- Diagnostic interactions:
  - Vonoprazan (via acid suppression) increases serum chromogranin A (CgA), which may cause false positive neuroendocrine tumor tests. Measure CgA at least 4 weeks after completing therapy and consider re-testing if elevated.
- Other interactions: Not all potential interactions are listed in the available sources; consult full product literature where available.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Clarithromycin component: Based on animal studies and human observational data, embryo‑fetal toxicity concerns have been raised. The triple regimen (including clarithromycin) is not recommended for use in pregnant women except when no alternative therapy is appropriate. [United States]

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Vonoprazan: potassium-competitive acid blocker (PCAB) that reduces gastric acid secretion by inhibiting the gastric H+,K+‑ATPase via a potassium-competitive mechanism.
- Amoxicillin: bactericidal β‑lactam antibiotic inhibiting bacterial cell wall synthesis.
- Clarithromycin: macrolide antibiotic that inhibits bacterial protein synthesis via binding to the 50S ribosomal subunit.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Detailed absorption, distribution, metabolism, and elimination parameters for the combination are not specified in the available sources.
- Clinical pharmacology notes:
  - Acid suppression from vonoprazan can increase serum chromogranin A (CgA) and interfere with certain diagnostic assays. Assess CgA at least 4 weeks after therapy.
