# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: US (FDA).
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory information into a single reference.
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Class:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not explicitly stated in source, but standard for levocarnitine).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn metabolic errors resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose: 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
    - In severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 or 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours) [US].
    - Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - Administration: Slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - Dose adjustments: Based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use [US].
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** High doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD on dialysis may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Monitoring:** In metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events reported in clinical trials for ESRD patients (frequency ≥5% regardless of causality):
- **Very Common (>10%):** Headache (up to 37%), injection site reaction (up to 38%), pain (up to 35%), hypertension (up to 21%), hypotension (up to 19%), chest pain (up to 15%), nausea (up to 12%), vomiting (up to 21%), diarrhea (up to 35%), pharyngitis (up to 27%), fever (up to 12%), and asthenia (up to 12%) [US].
- **Common (1–10%):** Abdominal pain, accidental injury, flu syndrome, infection, arrhythmia, tachycardia, hypercalcemia, and dizziness [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm [US].
- **General Reactions:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent reactions include body odor and gastritis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating treatment or adjusting the dose [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not specified by letter, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm]. Should be used during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration. Risks to the child versus benefits to the mother must be weighed; consideration may be given to discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies revealed no evidence of impaired fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Refer to the "Metabolic Disorders" dosing section for weight-based dosing (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. Safety and efficacy of the *oral* formulation have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with specific metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Administration to ESRD patients on hemodialysis results in increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Trials:** In two double-blind, placebo-controlled trials (n=130 receiving levocarnitine), IV administration of 10, 20, or 40 mg/kg significantly increased trough levocarnitine concentrations. Pre-dialysis (trough) levels in treated patients rose from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ranges between 116 and 456 µmol/L over 12 to 24 weeks, depending on the dose [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].
