# Editorial Product Information: Voretigene Neparvovec

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (TGA), US (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of voretigene neparvovec product information for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory requirements from the provided sources.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Prescribing Information required for clinical practice in a specific jurisdiction. Always consult the local, current regulatory documentation before prescribing or administering this therapy.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Voretigene neparvovec (recombinant adeno-associated virus serotype 2 [AAV2] vector). In the United States, the nonproprietary name is **voretigene neparvovec-rzyl**.
- **Chemical Class:** Gene therapy (AAV2 vector carrying human RPE65 cDNA).
- **Brand Name:** **Luxturna**.
- **ATC Code:** S01XA27.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:**
    - **Concentrate:** Clear, colourless, preservative-free solution/suspension for subretinal injection.
    - **Strength:** 5 x 10¹² vector genomes (vg) per mL.
    - **Packaging:** 0.5 mL extractable volume in a 2 mL single-dose vial.
    - **Diluent:** Supplied in two 2-mL single-use vials containing 1.7 mL extractable volume.
- **Route of Administration:** Subretinal injection only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Novartis Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Limited [Australian]; Spark Therapeutics, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inherited Retinal Dystrophy:** For the treatment of patients with confirmed biallelic *RPE65* mutation-associated retinal dystrophy who have sufficient viable retinal cells as determined by the treating physician. 
- **Diagnostic Requirement:** Pathological mutations of *RPE65* must be confirmed by an accredited laboratory [Australian; US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Recommended Dose:** 1.5 x 10¹¹ vg per eye, delivered in a total volume of 0.3 mL.
- **Administration Schedule:** Each eye is treated on separate days with an interval of at least 6 days between procedures.

### Immunomodulatory Regimen
A systemic corticosteroid regimen is required to mitigate potential immune responses to the vector or transgene. 
- **Pre-operative:** Prednisone (or equivalent) 1 mg/kg/day (max 40 mg/day) starting 3 days before administration to the first eye.
- **Post-operative:**
    - Day of administration plus 3 days: 1 mg/kg/day (max 40 mg/day).
    - Following 5 days: 0.5 mg/kg/day (max 20 mg/day) [Australian].
    - Following 5 days: 0.5 mg/kg every other day (max 20 mg/day) [Australian].
    - *Note:* The US PI recommends the 1 mg/kg/day dose for a total of 7 days, followed by a 10-day tapering schedule [US].
- **Bilateral Administration:** If the second eye is treated within a close interval, the immunomodulatory regimen for the second eye supersedes the tapering schedule of the first eye.

### Administration Instructions
- **Surgeon Experience:** Treatment must be administered by a retinal surgeon experienced in macular surgery.
- **Preparation:** Must be prepared under aseptic conditions (Class II vertical laminar flow biological safety cabinet) within 4 hours of administration. A 1:10 dilution is required (0.3 mL concentrate + 2.7 mL diluent). Two syringes (primary and backup) are prepared for the surgeon.
- **Surgical Procedure:**
    - Perform a vitrectomy.
    - Under direct visualisation, inject into the subretinal space (pars plana approach).
    - Recommended site: Along the superior vascular arcade, at least 2 mm distal to the fovea center.
    - Slowly inject to create a subretinal bleb until the full 0.3 mL is delivered.
    - Follow with fluid-air exchange and immediate supine head positioning. Patients should remain supine for 24 hours post-discharge.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None:** No contraindications are listed in the US Prescribing Information [US].
- **Specific Contraindications [Australian]:**
    - Hypersensitivity to the active substance or any excipients.
    - Ocular or periocular infection.
    - Active intraocular inflammation.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Endophthalmitis:** Risk associated with any intraocular procedure. Ensure aseptic technique and monitor for infection.
- **Permanent Visual Acuity Decline:** May occur following subretinal injection; monitor for visual disturbances.
- **Retinal Abnormalities:** Risks include macular holes, foveal thinning, loss of foveal function, foveal dehiscence, retinal tears/breaks, and retinal haemorrhage. Do not inject in the immediate vicinity of the fovea.
- **Increased Intraocular Pressure (IOP):** Monitor and manage IOP elevations appropriately.
- **Expansion of Intraocular Air Bubbles:** Patients must avoid air travel, scuba diving, or travel to high elevations until the air bubble has completely dissipated (usually one week or more). Rapid altitude changes while the bubble is present can cause irreversible vision loss.
- **Cataract:** Subretinal injection and vitrectomy are associated with increased incidence and progression of cataracts.
- **Vector Shedding:** Transient, low-level shedding may occur in tears. Patients/caregivers should handle waste (dressings, nasal secretions) in sealed bags for 14 days [Australian] or 7 days [US]. Gloves should be worn by caregivers, especially those who are pregnant, breastfeeding, or immunodeficient.
- **Donation Restrictions:** Patients should not donate blood, organs, tissues, or cells for transplantation [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions may be related to the drug, the surgical procedure, corticosteroid use, or a combination.

### Ocular Adverse Reactions (Incidence ≥ 5%)
| Adverse Reaction | Frequency Category |
| :--- | :--- |
| Conjunctival hyperaemia | Very Common |
| Cataract | Very Common |
| Increased Intraocular Pressure | Very Common |
| Retinal tear | Common |
| Dellen (corneal thinning) | Common |
| Macular hole | Common |
| Subretinal deposits | Common |
| Eye inflammation/irritation/pain | Common |
| Maculopathy (wrinkling) | Common |

### Serious and Post-marketing Reactions
- **Serious:** Retinal detachment, endophthalmitis, and loss of foveal function (2% incidence). One case of optic atrophy occurred secondary to steroid treatment for endophthalmitis.
- **Post-marketing:** Chorioretinal atrophy (progressive retinal degeneration/depigmentation) occurring at and outside the bleb site.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Studies:** No formal interaction studies have been performed.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B2.** 
    - No adequate human studies exist. As a precaution, avoid use during pregnancy.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if the vector is excreted in human milk. The developmental benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need for therapy and potential risks to the infant.
- **Fertility:** No nonclinical or clinical studies have evaluated the effect on fertility. The risk of inadvertent germline transmission is considered negligible.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:**
    - Safety and efficacy established in patients aged $\ge$ 4 years [Australian; US].
    - **US Recommendation:** Use in infants < 12 months is not recommended due to potential dilution or loss of the vector during active retinal cell proliferation.
- **Elderly Use ($\ge$ 65 years):** Safety and efficacy not specifically established in this age group; however, no dose adjustment is required.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Safety and efficacy not established; no dose adjustments are required.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Voretigene neparvovec delivers a functional *RPE65* gene to retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cells. The RPE65 protein converts all-*trans*-retinol to 11-*cis*-retinol, a vital step in the visual cycle. Mutations in *RPE65* block this cycle, leading to vision loss.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Transduction of RPE cells with cDNA encoding human RPE65 protein provides the potential to restore the visual cycle.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Biodistribution:** Highest levels of vector DNA are detected in intraocular fluids. Sporadic, low levels may appear in the spleen and liver. No DNA detected in gonads.
    - **Shedding:** Vector DNA is shed transiently in tears in approximately 45% of subjects, typically resolving by Day 3, though occasionally persisting in tears until Day 14 [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Study (Study 301/Study 2):** An open-label, randomised, controlled trial of 31 subjects (age 4–44).
- **Primary Endpoint:** Multi-luminance mobility test (MLMT) score change at Year 1. 
- **Results:** The intervention group showed a statistically significant improvement in MLMT scores (median change of 2 levels) compared to the control group (median change of 0). Improvements were sustained for at least 3 years.
- **Secondary Outcomes:** Significant improvements were noted in Full-field light Sensitivity Threshold (FST) testing. Visual acuity changes were not significantly different between groups in the primary analysis, though post-hoc analysis showed significant letter improvement in the intervention group.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Frozen Storage:** Store concentrate and diluent at $\le$ -65°C.
- **Thawed State:** Once thawed, do not refreeze. Store at room temperature (< 25°C). 
- **Diluted State:** Must be used within 4 hours of preparation if stored at room temperature.
- **Handling:** Contains genetically modified organisms. Follow biohazard precautions for disposal.
