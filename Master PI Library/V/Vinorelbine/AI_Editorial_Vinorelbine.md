# Editorial Product Information: Vinorelbine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical and regulatory data into a structured format for quick reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and does not substitute for the official PI or local clinical guidelines required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Vinorelbine (as tartrate).
- **Chemical Class:** Semi-synthetic vinca alkaloid.
- **Brand Names:** Navelbine [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available source.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Concentrated injection: 10 mg/1 mL and 50 mg/5 mL single-use vials [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only. 
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pierre Fabre Australia Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Advanced Breast Cancer:** Indicated as a single agent or in combination therapy following the failure of standard treatments [Australian].
- **Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC), Advanced:** Indicated as first-line treatment, either as a single agent or in combination [Australian].
- **Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC), Adjuvant:** Indicated in combination with cisplatin for patients with completely resected NSCLC, Stage IB or higher [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Adult Dosing
- **Advanced Breast Cancer and NSCLC:**
    - **Single Agent:** 25–30 mg/m² administered weekly via the intravenous route.
    - **Combination Therapy:** The dose remains 25–30 mg/m², but frequency is typically reduced (e.g., Day 1 and 8, or Day 1 and 5, every 3 weeks).
- **Adjuvant NSCLC (Stage IB or Greater):** 
    - **Vinorelbine:** Initial dose of 25–30 mg/m² weekly for 16 weeks. 
    - **Cisplatin:** 100 mg/m² administered on Days 1, 29, 57, and 85.

### Administration Instructions
- **Route:** **Strictly Intravenous.** Intrathecal administration is fatal.
- **Preparation:** Must be diluted to a concentration of 0.5 to 2 mg/mL. **Do not use syringes** for administration; use the "minibag" technique to prevent accidental spinal administration.
- **Infusion Options:**
    - 6 to 10 minutes following dilution in 50 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride or 5% glucose.
    - 20 to 30 minutes following dilution in 125 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride or 5% glucose.
- **Post-Infusion:** Always follow with at least 250 mL of a compatible saline flush to prevent phlebitis.
- **Vesicant Nature:** Vinorelbine is a moderate vesicant. Central venous line insertion may be necessary.

### Dose Modifications (Haematological)
- **Threshold:** Neutrophil counts must be $\ge 1.5 \times 10^9$ cells/L before administration.
- **Adjustments (Advanced Cancer):**
    - ANC $\ge 1.5$: 100% dose (30 mg/m²).
    - ANC 1.0 to 1.499: 50% dose (15 mg/m²).
    - ANC < 1.0: Withhold dose. If three consecutive weekly doses are withheld, discontinue treatment.
- **Adjuvant NSCLC:** If ANC < $1.0 \times 10^9$/L or Platelets < $100 \times 10^9$/L, delay dose by 1 week. Discontinue if recovery takes more than 3 weeks.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to vinorelbine or other vinca alkaloids [Australian].
- Neutrophil count < 1500 cells/mm³ [Australian].
- Severe infection (current or within the previous 2 weeks) [Australian].
- Platelet count < 100,000 cells/mm³ [Australian].
- Severe hepatic insufficiency [Australian].
- Pregnancy and Breastfeeding [Australian].
- Concomitant use with Yellow Fever vaccine [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** Not explicitly formatted as a "Boxed Warning" in the source, but the document highlights that administration by any route other than IV is **FATAL**.
- **Myelosuppression:** Dose-limiting toxicity, primarily presenting as neutropenia. Nadir occurs 5–10 days post-dose.
- **Pulmonary Toxicity:** Risk of severe bronchospasm, interstitial pneumonitis, and Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS). Discontinue permanently for confirmed interstitial pneumonitis.
- **Extravasation:** May cause local tissue necrosis and thrombophlebitis.
- **Neuropathy:** Both peripheral (paraesthesia, loss of deep tendon reflexes) and autonomic (intestinal paresis, constipation) neuropathies are reported.
- **Cardiac Disease:** Exercise caution and monitor patients with a history of ischaemic heart disease.
- **Hepatic Irradiation:** Avoid concomitant use with radiotherapy if the treatment field includes the liver.

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Neutropenia (dose-limiting), anaemia, peripheral neuropathy (loss of reflexes, paraesthesia), stomatitis, nausea, vomiting, constipation, transient elevation of liver enzymes, alopecia, fatigue, injection site reactions (erythema, pain, phlebitis). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Thrombocytopenia, infection, diarrhoea, chest pain, asthenia, fever, jaw pain, myalgia, arthralgia. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Severe sepsis, septicaemia, SIADH, severe paresthesia, hypertension/hypotension, flushing, peripheral coldness, haemorrhagic cystitis. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Pancreatitis, paralytic ileus, interstitial pneumopathy, myocardial infarction, angina pectoris, ECG changes, collapse, local necrosis at injection site, severe hyponatraemia. |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Fatal septicaemia, cardiac failure, pulmonary oedema, tachycardia, rhythm disorders. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Neutropenic sepsis, ARDS, and intestinal paresis are high-priority clinical concerns.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, itraconazole, ritonavir):** May increase vinorelbine plasma concentrations; itraconazole specifically increases neurotoxicity.
- **CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., rifampicin, phenytoin):** May reduce vinorelbine efficacy. 
- **Phenytoin:** Risk of exacerbated convulsions due to reduced phenytoin absorption.
- **Mitomycin:** Increased risk of acute pulmonary reactions (bronchospasm, dyspnoea).
- **Lapatinib:** Suggested increase in Grade 3/4 neutropenia.
- **Cisplatin:** While PK is not affected, the incidence of granulocytopenia is higher when used in combination.
- **Live Attenuated Vaccines:** Risk of fatal generalised vaccine disease (Yellow Fever vaccine is strictly contraindicated).
- **Anticoagulants:** Increased monitoring of INR is required due to intra-individual variability and potential potentiation.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Vinorelbine is suspected to cause fetal harm and is teratogenic in animal studies.
- **Contraception:**
    - **Females:** Must use effective contraception during and for **7 months** after treatment [Australian].
    - **Males:** Must use effective contraception during and for **4 months** after treatment; advice on sperm conservation should be sought prior to therapy due to risk of irreversible infertility [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. It is unknown if vinorelbine is excreted in human milk [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No significant differences in response observed, but increased sensitivity in some older individuals cannot be ruled out [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose modification is necessary due to low renal excretion [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** For severe impairment (Bilirubin > 2xULN or Transaminases > 5xULN), reduce dose by 33%. Maximum evaluated dose in this population is 20 mg/m² [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits mitosis at metaphase by interfering with tubulin and microtubule assembly. It shows relative selectivity for mitotic microtubules over axonal microtubules.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Triphasic plasma decay. High binding to human platelets and lymphocytes. Significant penetration into pulmonary tissue.
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolised by CYP3A4. One active metabolite (17-deacetylvinorelbine) exists at low levels.
    - **Elimination:** Substantial hepatic elimination; excreted primarily in faeces. Terminal half-life ranges from 27.7 to 43.6 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adjuvant NSCLC (ANITA Trial):** A Phase III study of 840 patients showed that vinorelbine + cisplatin significantly improved median survival (5.5 years vs 3.6 years for observation) and disease-free survival (3.0 years vs 1.7 years).
- **Advanced Breast Cancer:** Phase II studies reported overall response rates ranging from 14% to 45% in mixed populations. In a Phase III trial against melphalan, vinorelbine showed superior survival (35 weeks vs 31 weeks).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze). Protect from light [Australian].
- **Diluted Solution:** Stable for up to 24 hours at 5°C to 30°C in PVC or glass containers under normal room light [Australian].
- **Handling:** Use standard cytotoxic handling procedures. If skin/mucosa contact occurs, wash immediately with soap and water.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
