# Editorial Product Information: Vismodegib

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from the official Product Information (PI) document provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is designed to facilitate clinical decision-making by consolidating regulatory data into a structured, professional format.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority for the most current legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Vismodegib (a low molecular weight, orally available Hedgehog pathway inhibitor).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Erivedge** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01XJ01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 150 mg hard capsules. The capsule features a pink opaque body (printed with "150mg") and a grey opaque cap (printed with "VISMO") [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Roche Products Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metastatic Basal Cell Carcinoma (mBCC):** Treatment of adult patients with metastatic disease [Australian].
- **Locally Advanced Basal Cell Carcinoma (laBCC):** Treatment of adult patients where surgery and/or radiation therapy are considered inappropriate or not feasible [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:** 150 mg taken once daily [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - May be taken with or without food.
    - Capsules must be swallowed whole with water; they must not be opened or crushed under any circumstances to maintain integrity and prevent accidental exposure [Australian].
- **Duration of Therapy:** Continue treatment until disease progression or the occurrence of unacceptable toxicity.
- **Monitoring:** If treatment is ceased prior to disease progression, patients should be closely monitored for recurrence or worsening of the disease [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, patients should skip the dose and resume with the next scheduled dose. They should not take a double dose to compensate for the missed one [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy:** Use is strictly contraindicated in pregnant women [Australian].
- **Women of Child-Bearing Potential:** Contraindicated unless two reliable methods of contraception are utilised during treatment and for 24 months following the final dose [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated during treatment and for 24 months after the last dose due to the potential for serious developmental defects [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Vismodegib is highly teratogenic. It can cause embryo-fetal death or severe, irreversible birth defects (e.g., midline defects, missing digits).
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Post-marketing reports include Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS), and Acute Generalised Exanthematous Pustulosis (AGEP). If SCARs occur, treatment must be permanently discontinued and never restarted [Australian].
- **Blood Donation:** Patients must not donate blood or blood products while on therapy and for 24 months after the last dose [Australian].
- **Semen Donation and Transmission:** Vismodegib is present in semen. Male patients must use condoms (with spermicide if available) during intercourse with female partners, even after vasectomy, during treatment and for 2 months after the final dose. Males must not donate semen during this period [Australian].
- **Paediatric Safety:** Premature fusion of the epiphyses (EPF) and precocious puberty have been reported. In some cases, EPF continued even after drug discontinuation [Australian].

### Other Notable Reactions
- **Common (1–10%):** Abdominal pain, asthenia, dehydration, musculoskeletal pain (back, chest, flank), myalgia, and hypogeusia.
- **Laboratory Abnormalities:** Grade 3 decreases in sodium (n=7) and potassium (n=2), and elevated BUN (n=3) were observed [Australian].
- **Post-Marketing (Frequency Unknown):** Drug-induced liver injury, SJS/TEN, DRESS, AGEP, and epiphyseal premature fusion.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP450 Interactions:** Clinically significant interactions with CYP450 substrates, inhibitors, or inducers (e.g., rosiglitazone, fluconazole, itraconazole, carbamazepine) are not expected [Australian].
- **Transporter-Mediated Interactions:**
    - **OATP1B1:** Vismodegib inhibits OATP1B1 in vitro. Caution is advised when co-administering with OATP1B1 substrates such as statins, bosentan, ezetimibe, and valsartan, as exposure to these drugs may increase [Australian].
    - **OATP1B3:** Vismodegib is a weak inhibitor; interactions cannot be entirely ruled out [Australian].
    - **BCRP:** While an in vitro inhibitor, clinically significant interactions are not expected at therapeutic concentrations.
- **Gastric pH Agents:** Co-administration with proton pump inhibitors (e.g., rabeprazole) does not result in clinically significant PK changes [Australian].
- **Oral Contraceptives:** No significant PK interaction observed with ethinyloestradiol and norethisterone.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category X** [Australian]. Highly teratogenic. A pregnancy test must be performed within 7 days prior to starting treatment and monthly thereafter. Immediate notification to a physician is required if pregnancy occurs.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety is not established. Nursing is contraindicated during treatment and for 24 months after the final dose due to potential developmental toxicity [Australian].
- **Fertility:** May impair fertility. Amenorrhoea is common (30%). Discussion of fertility preservation is recommended for women of child-bearing potential before starting therapy [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children <18 years have not been established. Use is associated with a risk of irreversible epiphyseal closure [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required for patients >65 years. However, data are limited for patients in this category regarding comparative efficacy/safety [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required. Population PK analysis showed no impact on exposure. Limited data exist for severe impairment; monitor these patients closely for adverse reactions [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild, moderate, or severe hepatic impairment based on dedicated clinical studies [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Vismodegib binds to and inhibits the Smoothened (SMO) transmembrane protein. This prevents the activation and nuclear translocation of GLI transcription factors, thereby inhibiting the Hedgehog signalling pathway, which is often aberrantly activated in BCC [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Does not have a relevant effect on QTc intervals or other ECG parameters (heart rate, PR interval, QRS duration) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is 31.8%. Absorption is saturable. Steady state is typically reached within 7 days of daily dosing.
    - **Distribution:** Low volume of distribution (16.4 to 26.6 L). Extremely high protein binding (>99% ex vivo), primarily to human serum albumin and alpha-1-acid glycoprotein (AAG).
    - **Metabolism:** Predominantly excreted as unchanged drug. Minor metabolism via CYP2C9 and CYP3A4/5.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily hepatic. Terminal half-life is ~12 days after a single dose, but decreases to ~4 days at steady state due to non-linear kinetics [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ERIVANCE BCC (Pivotal Study):** A single-arm, open-label trial (n=104).
    - **mBCC Cohort:** Objective Response Rate (ORR) was 30.3% (Primary Analysis) and 33.3% (12-month update). Median Duration of Response (DoR) was 7.6 months.
    - **laBCC Cohort:** ORR was 42.9% (Primary Analysis) and 47.6% (12-month update). Median DoR was 9.5 months. 54% of patients had a histopathologic response at 24 weeks [Australian].
- **STEVIE Study:** A large post-approval study (n=1215 evaluable). ORR was 68.5% for laBCC and 36.9% for mBCC. Median DoR was 23.0 months for laBCC and 13.9 months for mBCC [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Packaging:** Keep the bottle tightly closed to protect the capsules from moisture [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging.
