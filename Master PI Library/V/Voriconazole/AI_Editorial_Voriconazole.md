# Editorial Product Information: Voriconazole

## Editorial Note
- This editorial was developed using the following jurisdiction’s Product Information: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the pharmaceutical and clinical profile of voriconazole. It is intended for use by healthcare professionals as a concise clinical reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial summary and does not replace the official Product Information mandated for prescribing in specific jurisdictions.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Voriconazole (Triazole antifungal)
- **Brand Names:** **VFEND** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** J02AC03
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Film-coated Tablets:** 50 mg and 200 mg [Australian].
    - **Powder for Injection:** 200 mg lyophilised powder (contains 160 mg sulfobutyl betadex sodium [SBECD] per mL after reconstitution) [Australian].
    - **Powder for Oral Suspension:** 40 mg/mL after reconstitution (contains sucrose and sodium benzoate) [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion; Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Voriconazole is indicated for the treatment of:
- **Invasive Aspergillosis** [Australian].
- **Serious Candida Infections:** Including *C. krusei*, oesophageal and systemic infections (candidaemia, disseminated candidiasis, and hepatosplenic candidiasis) [Australian].
- **Serious Scedosporium spp. and Fusarium spp. infections** [Australian].
- **Refractory/Intolerant Fungal Infections:** Other serious fungal infections in patients failing or intolerant of other therapies [Australian].
- **Prophylaxis:** Prevention of invasive fungal infections in high-risk patients (e.g., haematopoietic stem cell transplant [HSCT] recipients) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
Therapy **must** be initiated with a loading dose regimen (IV or oral) on Day 1 to achieve steady-state concentrations rapidly.

| Indication | Loading Dose (First 24h) | Maintenance Dose (After 24h) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Invasive Aspergillosis / Scedosporium / Fusarium** | IV: 6 mg/kg q12h; Oral (≥40kg): 400 mg q12h | IV: 4 mg/kg q12h; Oral (≥40kg): 200 mg q12h |
| **Serious Candida Infections** | IV: 6 mg/kg q12h; Oral (≥40kg): 400 mg q12h | IV: 3 mg/kg q12h; Oral (≥40kg): 200 mg q12h |
| **Oesophageal Candidiasis** | IV: Not recommended; Oral (≥40kg): 400 mg q12h | Oral (≥40kg): 200 mg q12h |
| **Prophylaxis** | IV: 6 mg/kg q12h; Oral (≥40kg): N/A | IV: 4 mg/kg q12h; Oral (≥40kg): 200 mg q12h |

*Note: For patients <40 kg, oral doses are typically halved (200 mg loading, 100 mg maintenance) [Australian].*

### Administration Instructions
- **Oral:** Take at least one hour before or one hour after a meal [Australian].
- **Intravenous:** Do **not** give as a bolus. Infuse at a maximum rate of 3 mg/kg/hour over 1 to 2 hours. Reconstitute to 10 mg/mL and dilute to 0.5–5 mg/mL before use [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specifically detailed; follow institutional protocols for antifungals.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to voriconazole or excipients (e.g., lactose, SBECD) [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Substrates (Risk of QT/Torsades):** Coadministration with Terfenadine, Pimozide, Quinidine, Ivabradine, Lurasidone, or Cisapride [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Substrates (Other):** Sirolimus, Voclosporin, Naloxegol, Tolvaptan, Finerenone, Eplerenone, and Ergot alkaloids [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers (Significant Loss of Efficacy):** Rifampicin, Carbamazepine, long-acting Barbiturates (e.g., Phenobarbital), and St John’s Wort [Australian].
- **Specific Drug Contraindications:** 
    - **Ritonavir:** High doses (400 mg q12h or higher) [Australian].
    - **Efavirenz:** Doses of 400 mg/day or higher [Australian].
    - **Venetoclax:** During the initiation and titration phase [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatic Toxicity:** Serious reactions including liver failure and fatalities have occurred. Monitor Liver Function Tests (LFTs) at initiation and at least **weekly** for the first month [Australian].
- **Visual Events:** Approximately 30% of patients experience visual changes (blurred vision, altered colour perception, photophobia). Advise patients not to drive at night [Australian].
- **Dermatological Reactions:** 
    - **SCARs:** Discontinue if Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS) or Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN) is suspected.
    - **Photosensitivity/SCC:** Long-term use is associated with Squamous Cell Carcinoma (SCC) of the skin and melanoma. Avoid direct sunlight and use SPF [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular:** Risk of QT interval prolongation and Torsades de Pointes. Correct electrolyte disturbances (K+, Mg++, Ca++) prior to initiation [Australian].
- **Adrenal Events:** Reversible adrenal insufficiency or Cushing’s syndrome (the latter when used with corticosteroids) may occur [Australian].
- **Skeletal:** Periostitis has been reported with long-term therapy [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Visual impairment, pyrexia, rash, vomiting, nausea, diarrhoea, headache, peripheral oedema, abdominal pain, and LFT abnormalities [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Sinusitis, anaemia, thrombocytopenia, hypokalaemia, depression, hallucinations, anxiety, dizziness, hypotension, and acute respiratory distress syndrome [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Pseudomembranous colitis, hypersensitivity, adrenal insufficiency, pancreatitis, and Stevens-Johnson Syndrome [Australian].
- **Serious Effects:** Hepatic failure, QT prolongation, Torsades de Pointes, SCARs, and SCC (with chronic use) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
Voriconazole is a substrate and inhibitor of **CYP2C19, CYP2C9, and CYP3A4**.
- **Significant Inducers:** Rifabutin, Letermovir, and Flucloxacillin (may require voriconazole dose increase) [Australian].
- **Specific Adjustments Required:**
    - **Ciclosporin/Tacrolimus:** Halve the dose of Ciclosporin and reduce Tacrolimus to one-third when starting voriconazole [Australian].
    - **Phenytoin:** Increase voriconazole maintenance dose (e.g., to 5 mg/kg IV q12h or 400 mg oral q12h) [Australian].
    - **Omeprazole:** Halve the omeprazole dose if it is ≥40 mg daily [Australian].
    - **Warfarin:** Monitor Prothrombin Time (PT) closely [Australian].
    - **Statins/Benzodiazepines/Vinca Alkaloids:** Consider dose reduction due to increased plasma levels [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. Not recommended unless the benefit to the mother clearly outweighs the risk to the fetus. Evidence of embryotoxicity and teratogenicity (e.g., cleft palate) in animal studies [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Discontinue breastfeeding upon initiation of voriconazole [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No impairment observed in rat studies at exposures 4–6 times the human maintenance dose [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for children **<2 years** [Australian].
    - **Ages 2 to <12 years:** IV dosing (7 mg/kg q12h) is recommended to achieve adult-equivalent exposure. Oral dosing in this age group is not yet established [Australian].
    - **Adolescents (12–16 years):** Dose as adults [Australian].
- **Elderly:** No dose adjustment required [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Oral:** No adjustment needed.
    - **IV:** If CrCl <50 mL/min, the vehicle SBECD accumulates. Switch to oral voriconazole unless the benefit/risk justifies IV use [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use standard loading doses. For mild-to-moderate cirrhosis (Child-Pugh A and B), **halve** the maintenance dose. Not studied in severe cirrhosis (Child-Pugh C) [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits fungal cytochrome P450-mediated 14α-sterol demethylation, leading to the depletion of ergosterol and accumulation of methylated sterols in the fungal cell membrane [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** 96% bioavailability. Peak plasma levels (Cmax) reached in 1–2 hours [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Vd 4.6 L/kg; 58% protein bound. Penetrates the cerebrospinal fluid [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP2C19 (highly polymorphic). 
    - **Elimination:** Non-linear (saturated) kinetics. Terminal half-life is approximately 6 hours at standard doses [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Invasive Aspergillosis:** A pivotal study (Study 307/602) showed voriconazole was superior to amphotericin B, with a 53% satisfactory global response compared to 31% for the comparator at Week 12. Survival was 71% vs 58% [Australian].
- **Candidaemia:** In non-neutropenic patients, voriconazole showed comparable success rates to the regimen of amphotericin B followed by fluconazole [Australian].
- **Prophylaxis:** Voriconazole (48.7% success) was superior to itraconazole (33.2% success) in preventing breakthrough IFI in HSCT recipients [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Tablets/Powder for Injection:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Powder for Oral Suspension:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (refrigerate) before reconstitution. After reconstitution, store the suspension below 30°C and discard after 14 days [Australian].
- **Reconstituted IV Concentrate:** May be stored at 2°C to 8°C for up to 24 hours [Australian].
