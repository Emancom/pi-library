# Editorial Product Information: Venlafaxine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)** regulatory authority. 
- **Note:** At the time of this publication, only the Australian source document was available for review. Information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., US, European, Canadian, Japanese) is noted as "Not specified in available sources" where applicable.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. It is a clinical synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Venlafaxine (as hydrochloride).
- **Chemical Class:** Structurally novel antidepressant; Serotonin and Noradrenaline Reuptake Inhibitor (SNRI).
- **Brand Name(s):** APO-Venlafaxine XR [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
  - Modified Release Capsules: 75 mg and 150 mg [Australian].
  - *Note: 37.5 mg strength is required for certain titration/tapering schedules but is not available in the APO-Venlafaxine XR brand [Australian].*
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Major Depressive Disorder (MDD):** Treatment of major depression, including the prevention of relapse and recurrence where appropriate [Australian].
- **Generalised Anxiety Disorder (GAD):** [Australian-approved].
- **Social Anxiety Disorder (SAD):** [Australian-approved].
- **Panic Disorder:** Treatment and prevention of relapse [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **MDD, GAD, and SAD:** Initial dose is 75 mg once daily. If clinical improvement is not achieved after two weeks, the dose may be increased to 150 mg once daily, and further to a maximum of 225 mg once daily [Australian].
- **Panic Disorder:** Initial dose is 37.5 mg/day for 4 to 7 days, then increased to 75 mg once daily. Non-responders may increase to a maximum of 225 mg/day [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Administer once daily at approximately the same time each day.
- Must be taken with food.
- Capsules must be swallowed whole with fluid; do not divide, crush, chew, or dissolve [Australian].

### Titration and Tapering
- **Increments:** Dosage increases should occur at intervals of at least four days, though approximately two weeks is generally recommended [Australian].
- **Discontinuation:** Dose must be tapered gradually when treatment exceeds one week to minimise withdrawal symptoms. In clinical trials, tapering involved reducing the daily dose by 75 mg at one-week intervals. Some patients may require very gradual tapering over months [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to venlafaxine or any excipients in the formulation [Australian].
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** 
  - Concomitant use with MAOIs (including reversible MAOIs like moclobemide, linezolid, and methylene blue) is contraindicated.
  - A washout period of at least 14 days is required after stopping an MAOI before starting venlafaxine.
  - A washout period of at least 7 days is required after stopping venlafaxine before starting an MAOI [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Suicide Risk:** Patients of all ages, particularly those under 25, should be monitored for clinical worsening and emergence of suicidality, especially at treatment initiation or during dose changes [Australian].
- **Serotonin Syndrome/NMS:** Potentially life-threatening conditions may occur, particularly with concomitant serotonergic or antidopaminergic agents. Discontinue immediately if symptoms (agitation, tremor, hyperthermia, autonomic instability) appear [Australian].
- **Sustained Hypertension:** Dose-related increases in blood pressure have been observed. Regular blood pressure monitoring is required. Pre-existing hypertension must be controlled before starting therapy [Australian].
- **Cardiac Precautions:** Use with caution in patients with recent myocardial infarction or unstable heart disease. Venlafaxine has been associated with QTc prolongation and Torsade de Pointes, typically in overdose [Australian].
- **Ocular Effects:** Mydriasis may occur; monitor patients with raised intraocular pressure or risk of angle-closure glaucoma [Australian].
- **Sexual Dysfunction:** SNRIs may cause persistent sexual dysfunction [Australian].
- **Bone Fractures:** Increased risk observed in patients taking SRIs/SNRIs [Australian].
- **Metabolic Effects:** May alter glycaemic control in diabetic patients; serum cholesterol increases may occur during long-term treatment [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often dose-dependent and most common during the first few weeks of therapy.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects [Australian] |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Nausea, dry mouth, headache, insomnia, dizziness, sedation, hyperhidrosis, constipation. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Anorexia, weight loss/gain, abnormal dreams, nervousness, libido decreased, tremor, paraesthesia, dysgeusia, palpitations, tachycardia, hypertension, flushing, yawning, vomiting, rash, erectile dysfunction, ejaculation disorder. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Hallucination, mania, hypomania, apathy, syncope, myoclonus, hypotension, gastrointestinal haemorrhage, liver function test abnormalities, alopecia, menorrhagia, urinary retention, bone fracture. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Anaphylaxis, SIADH, hyponatraemia, thrombocytopenia, pancreatitis, hepatitis, rhabdomyolysis, convulsion, QTc prolongation, Torsade de Pointes. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
  - **Serotonergic agents:** (SSRIs, SNRIs, triptans, lithium, tramadol, St John's Wort) Increase the risk of serotonin syndrome.
  - **CNS Depressants/Ethanol:** Potential for additive effects; patients should avoid alcohol [Australian].
  - **Anticoagulants/Antiplatelets:** Increased risk of bleeding due to interference with platelet aggregation [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
  - **CYP2D6 Inhibitors:** May increase venlafaxine plasma levels.
  - **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** (e.g., ketoconazole) May increase levels of both venlafaxine and its active metabolite, ODV.
  - **Haloperidol/Risperidone:** Venlafaxine may increase the AUC of these agents [Australian].
  - **Metoprolol:** Venlafaxine may increase metoprolol concentrations and reduce its blood pressure-lowering effect [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy Category:** **Category B2** [Australian]. Safety in human pregnancy is not established. Use only if benefits outweigh risks. Exposure in the third trimester may lead to neonatal complications (respiratory distress, feeding difficulties, or withdrawal symptoms).
- **Postpartum Haemorrhage:** Increased risk noted if used within one month of delivery [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Venlafaxine and ODV are excreted in human milk. Total infant ingestion can reach 9.2% of the maternal dose. Use is not recommended [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not indicated for patients under 18 years. Increased risk of suicidality, hostility, and self-harm observed in clinical trials [Australian].
- **Elderly:** No specific dose adjustment required based on age alone, but use caution when increasing doses due to potential renal decline [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Reduce total daily dose by 25% to 50% for GFR 10–70 mL/min. Reduce dose by 50% for patients on haemodialysis [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Reduce dose by 50% in mild-to-moderate impairment. Greater reductions may be necessary for severe cases [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Potentiates neurotransmitter activity in the CNS. Venlafaxine and its major metabolite, O-desmethylvenlafaxine (ODV), are potent inhibitors of serotonin and noradrenaline reuptake and weak inhibitors of dopamine reuptake [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Well absorbed (at least 92%); absolute bioavailability is ~42% due to first-pass metabolism. Food does not affect absorption.
  - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised in the liver via CYP2D6 to ODV and CYP3A4 to N-desmethylvenlafaxine.
  - **Elimination:** Primary renal excretion (87% in urine within 48 hours). Half-life: Venlafaxine ~5 hours; ODV ~11 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **MDD:** Three 12-week studies showed modified-release venlafaxine to be superior to placebo and paroxetine.
- **GAD:** Five placebo-controlled trials (up to 6 months) demonstrated superiority over placebo across multiple anxiety scales (HAM-A, CGI).
- **SAD:** Four 12-week and one 6-month study showed significant improvement on the Liebowitz Social Anxiety Scale (LSAS).
- **Panic Disorder:** Studies confirmed a significantly higher percentage of patients free of full-symptom panic attacks compared to placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
