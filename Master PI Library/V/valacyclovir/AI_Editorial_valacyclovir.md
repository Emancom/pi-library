# Editorial Product Information: Valacyclovir

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Valacyclovir hydrochloride (a deoxynucleoside analogue DNA polymerase inhibitor).
- **Brand Names:** Not explicitly specified by brand name in the source, but provided as Valacyclovir Tablets, USP (Northwind Health Company, LLC).
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available source.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Tablets (Oral):** 500 mg (unscored) and 1 gram (partial break line) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Northwind Health Company, LLC (Distributor: Mylan Pharmaceuticals Inc.) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Adult Indications:**
    - **Cold Sores (Herpes Labialis):** Treatment of labial herpes.
    - **Genital Herpes:** 
        - Treatment of initial episodes in immunocompetent patients.
        - Treatment of recurrent episodes in immunocompetent patients.
        - Chronic suppressive therapy of recurrent episodes in immunocompetent and HIV-1–infected patients.
        - Reduction of transmission in immunocompetent patients (in conjunction with safer sex practices).
    - **Herpes Zoster (Shingles):** Treatment of localized zoster in immunocompetent patients.
- **Pediatric Indications:**
    - **Cold Sores (Herpes Labialis):** For patients aged $\ge$ 12 years.
    - **Chickenpox:** For immunocompetent patients aged 2 to < 18 years.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing
- **Cold Sores:** 2 grams every 12 hours for 1 day (total 2 doses). Start at earliest symptoms (e.g., tingling).
- **Genital Herpes (Initial):** 1 gram twice daily for 10 days. Most effective when started within 48 hours of onset.
- **Genital Herpes (Recurrent):** 500 mg twice daily for 3 days.
- **Genital Herpes (Suppressive):** 
    - Immunocompetent: 1 gram once daily. 
    - Alternative for $\le$ 9 recurrences/year: 500 mg once daily.
    - HIV-1–infected (CD4 $\ge$ 100 cells/mm³): 500 mg twice daily.
- **Reduction of Transmission:** 500 mg once daily for the source partner.
- **Herpes Zoster:** 1 gram three times daily for 7 days. Start within 72 hours of rash onset.

### Pediatric Dosing
- **Cold Sores ($\ge$ 12 years):** 2 grams every 12 hours for 1 day.
- **Chickenpox (2 to < 18 years):** 20 mg/kg three times daily for 5 days. Do not exceed 1 gram three times daily.

### Administration Instructions
- May be taken without regard to meals.
- **Oral Suspension:** A 25 mg/mL or 50 mg/mL suspension can be prepared extemporaneously using 500 mg tablets for patients unable to swallow solids.
- **Missed Dose:** Take as soon as remembered. Do not double the next dose [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Demonstrated clinically significant hypersensitivity (e.g., anaphylaxis) to valacyclovir, acyclovir, or any component of the formulation [US].
- Patients who have developed Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs) to valacyclovir or acyclovir [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Thrombotic Thrombocytopenic Purpura/Hemolytic Uremic Syndrome (TTP/HUS):** Reported in patients with advanced HIV or transplant recipients receiving 8 grams/day. Discontinue if clinical signs/laboratory findings occur.
- **Acute Renal Failure:** Risk is increased in elderly patients, those with underlying renal disease, those taking nephrotoxic drugs, or patients with inadequate hydration. Ensure adequate fluid intake.
- **Central Nervous System (CNS) Effects:** Agitation, hallucinations, confusion, and encephalopathy may occur. Risk is higher in the elderly and those with renal impairment. Discontinue if CNS symptoms develop.
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Including SJS, TEN, AGEP, and DRESS. Discontinue immediately if a painful rash with mucosal involvement or progressive severe rash develops [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Adult Frequencies (General)
- **Very Common (>10%):** Headache, nausea, abdominal pain.
- **Common (1–10%):** Dizziness, ALT elevation (>2x ULN), asthenia (fatigue), diarrhea, dyspepsia.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, rash.

### Pediatric Frequencies
- **Very Common (>10%):** Headache (>10% in patients aged < 18 years).
- **Common (1–10%):** Diarrhea, pyrexia, dehydration, rhinorrhea.

### Postmarketing Experience (Serious/Rare)
- **CNS:** Aggressive behavior, seizures, coma, psychosis.
- **Renal:** Renal failure, renal pain (associated with renal failure).
- **Allergic:** Anaphylaxis, angioedema.
- **Hepatobiliary:** Hepatitis, liver enzyme abnormalities.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No clinically significant drug-drug or drug-food interactions are known.
- **Specific Management:** 
    - **Probenecid and Cimetidine:** Increase the AUC of acyclovir by reducing renal clearance; however, no dose adjustment is required in patients with normal renal function [US].
    - **Nephrotoxic Drugs:** Exercise caution as they may increase the risk of renal failure.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified by letter category, but clinical data over decades have not identified a drug-associated risk of major birth defects or miscarriage [US].
    - Animal studies (rats/rabbits) showed no adverse developmental outcomes at exposures 4–7 times the human dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Acyclovir (the active metabolite) is present in human milk. Following a 500 mg maternal dose BID, the child would receive approximately 0.6 mg/kg/day of acyclovir. Use should consider the clinical need of the mother and potential infant effects.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established for:
    - Cold sores < 12 years.
    - Genital herpes or herpes zoster < 18 years.
    - Chickenpox < 2 years.
- **Elderly Use:** More likely to have reduced renal function and CNS adverse events. Dose reduction is recommended based on renal status.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dose adjustments required for adults based on Creatinine Clearance (CrCl):
    - **CrCl 30–49 mL/min:** No reduction for most indications; Zoster reduced to 1g q12h.
    - **CrCl 10–29 mL/min:** Reductions for all indications (e.g., Zoster 1g q24h; Suppressive 500mg q24h).
    - **CrCl < 10 mL/min:** Further reductions (e.g., Zoster 500mg q24h; Cold sores 500mg single dose).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Dosage modification not required for patients with cirrhosis.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Valacyclovir is a prodrug rapidly converted to acyclovir. Acyclovir is phosphorylated by viral thymidine kinase into acyclovir triphosphate, which inhibits viral DNA replication by competing with viral DNA polymerase and terminating the growing DNA chain.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; absolute bioavailability of acyclovir is ~54.5%. Not affected by food.
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is low (13.5% to 17.9%).
    - **Metabolism:** Converted to acyclovir and L-valine via first-pass intestinal/hepatic metabolism.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily excreted in urine (89% as acyclovir). Half-life is ~2.5 to 3.3 hours in normal renal function.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Cold Sores:** Two trials in 1,856 subjects showed the 1-day treatment (2g BID) reduced episode duration by approximately 1 day compared to placebo [US].
- **Genital Herpes:** Trials showed 500 mg once daily for 8 months reduced the risk of transmitting HSV-2 to susceptible partners by 75% for symptomatic acquisition [US].
- **Herpes Zoster:** Trials in patients > 50 years showed valacyclovir was comparable or superior to acyclovir in reducing the duration of zoster-associated pain.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Tablets:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Prepared Suspension:** Store between 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) in a refrigerator. Discard after 28 days.
- **Packaging:** Keep in a tightly closed, light-resistant container with a child-resistant closure.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription-only (Rx Only). Classified as a deoxynucleoside analogue DNA polymerase inhibitor [US].
