# Editorial Product Information: Valaciclovir

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)** regulatory authority. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of valaciclovir for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data, safety warnings, and dosage requirements into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official PI when prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians must consult local regulations and current official labeling for final prescribing decisions.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Valaciclovir (as hydrochloride); a L-valine ester prodrug of the purine nucleoside analogue aciclovir.
- **Chemical Class:** Antiviral (Nucleoside analogue).
- **ATC Code:** J05AB11.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Tablets:** 500 mg (unscored) and 1000 mg (partially scored) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Herpes Zoster (Shingles):** Treatment of shingles and ophthalmic zoster in adults. Therapy should ideally commence within 72 hours of rash onset [Australian].
- **Herpes Labialis (Cold Sores):** Treatment of recurrent episodes in adults and adolescents [Australian].
- **Genital Herpes:** 
    - Treatment of initial and recurrent clinical episodes.
    - Chronic suppressive therapy (prevention) of recurrent outbreaks.
    - Reduction of transmission risk in immunocompetent heterosexual adults with a history of <10 recurrences per year, used in conjunction with safer sex practices [Australian].
- **Cytomegalovirus (CMV):** Prophylaxis of CMV infection and disease following solid organ transplantation in patients at risk [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing
- **Herpes Zoster:** 1000 mg three times daily for 7 days.
- **Herpes Labialis:** 2000 mg twice daily for one day (doses taken approximately 12 hours apart; no sooner than 6 hours). Initiate at the earliest prodromal symptom.
- **Genital Herpes (Treatment):**
    - **Initial episode:** 500 mg twice daily for 5 to 10 days.
    - **Recurrent episodes:** 500 mg twice daily for 5 days.
- **Genital Herpes (Prevention/Suppression):**
    - **Immunocompetent (<10 recurrences/year):** 500 mg once daily.
    - **Immunocompetent (≥10 recurrences/year):** 1000 mg once daily.
    - **Immunocompromised:** 500 mg twice daily.
- **Reduction of Transmission:** 500 mg once daily (infected partner).
- **CMV Prophylaxis:** 2 g four times daily for 90 days, initiated as soon as possible post-transplant.

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets may be taken without regard to meals. 
- **Hydration:** Adequate fluid intake is essential for all patients, particularly the elderly and those at risk of dehydration, to prevent aciclovir precipitation in renal tubules.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to valaciclovir, aciclovir, or any excipient in the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Thrombotic Microangiopathy (TMA):** Cases of Thrombotic Thrombocytopenic Purpura (TTP) and Haemolytic Uraemic Syndrome (HUS), sometimes fatal, have occurred in patients with advanced HIV or transplant recipients receiving high doses (8 g/day) for prolonged periods [Australian].
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Life-threatening reactions, including DRESS, SJS, TEN, and AGEP, have been reported. Discontinue immediately and permanently if signs of SCARs appear.
- **Central Nervous System (CNS) Effects:** Reversible neurological reactions (confusion, hallucinations, agitation, encephalopathy) may occur. Risk is elevated in patients with renal impairment, the elderly, and those on high-dose regimens (e.g., CMV prophylaxis).
- **Renal Impairment:** Dose reduction is mandatory (see Section 9). Acute renal failure may occur, often linked to inadequate hydration or concomitant nephrotoxic agents.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Caution is advised with high doses (≥4 g/day) in patients with liver disease or liver transplant, as data are limited.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Valaciclovir is generally well-tolerated. The most frequent side effects across trials are headache and nausea.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Headache, Nausea |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Diarrhoea, Vomiting, Dizziness, Abdominal pain, Asthenia, Constipation, Rash, ALT increase |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Leukopenia, Thrombocytopenia, Confusion, Hallucinations |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Anaphylaxis, Ataxia, Dysarthria, Convulsions, Encephalopathy, Coma, Acute renal failure |

**Serious Post-Marketing Events:** TTP/HUS, SCARs (DRESS, SJS, TEN), hepatitis, and photosensitivity reactions [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Nephrotoxic Agents:** Concurrent use with aminoglycosides, organoplatinum compounds, foscarnet, ciclosporin, or tacrolimus requires regular renal monitoring.
- **Renal Secretion Inhibitors:** Cimetidine and probenecid increase the AUC of aciclovir by ~65% via inhibition of active renal secretion. While generally not requiring adjustment at 1 g doses, caution is needed at high-dose CMV regimens.
- **Mycophenolate Mofetil:** Co-administration increases the AUC of both aciclovir and the inactive metabolite of mycophenolate.
- **Tenofovir:** May compete for active tubular secretion, potentially increasing concentrations of either agent [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category B3.** Data from pregnancy registries show no increase in birth defects compared to the general population. Valaciclovir should only be used if potential benefits outweigh risks.
- **Breastfeeding:** Aciclovir is excreted in breast milk. Following a 500 mg dose, breast milk concentrations ranged from 0.5 to 2.3 times maternal serum levels. Use with caution.
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in rat studies at therapeutic doses. No changes in sperm count or motility were seen in humans following 6 months of aciclovir treatment [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children have not been established [Australian]. Dosage for CMV prophylaxis is indicated for adolescents from 12 years of age.
- **Elderly Use:** Higher risk of renal impairment and CNS toxicity. Maintain adequate hydration and adjust dosage based on creatinine clearance (CrCl).
- **Renal Impairment:** Mandatory dose adjustments are required:
    - **Zoster:** CrCl 15–30 mL/min: 1000 mg bid; <15 mL/min: 1000 mg daily.
    - **Genital Herpes (Treatment):** <15 mL/min: 500 mg daily.
    - **Cold Sores:** CrCl 31–49 mL/min: 1000 mg bid; 15–30 mL/min: 500 mg bid; <15 mL/min: 500 mg once.
    - **CMV Prophylaxis:** Scaled reductions from 1.5 g qid to 1.5 g daily depending on CrCl severity.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose modification is required for mild-to-moderate cirrhosis. Data for advanced cirrhosis are limited.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Valaciclovir is rapidly converted to aciclovir. Aciclovir is phosphorylated by viral thymidine kinase to its active triphosphate form, which competitively inhibits viral DNA polymerase and terminates the growing viral DNA chain.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; 54% bioavailability (unaffected by food).
    - **Distribution:** Low protein binding (9–33%); CSF penetration is ~25% of plasma levels.
    - **Metabolism:** Converted to aciclovir and L-valine by first-pass intestinal/hepatic metabolism (valaciclovir hydrolase).
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal excretion (>80% as aciclovir). Elimination half-life is ~3 hours (prolonged to 14 hours in end-stage renal disease).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Herpes Zoster:** A trial of 1141 patients (≥50 years) demonstrated that valaciclovir 1 g tid significantly reduced the duration of zoster-associated pain and post-herpetic neuralgia (PHN) compared to aciclovir [Australian].
- **Cold Sores:** Two trials in 1856 patients showed a 1-day treatment (2 g bid) reduced episode duration by approximately one day compared to placebo.
- **CMV Prophylaxis:** In renal transplant recipients, high-dose valaciclovir (2 g qid) reduced CMV disease by 78–82% compared to placebo over a 6-month period [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the original packaging.
