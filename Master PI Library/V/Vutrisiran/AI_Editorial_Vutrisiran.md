# Editorial Product Information: Vutrisiran

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) from Australia (Therapeutic Goods Administration). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of vutrisiran for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource only. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Vutrisiran (as vutrisiran sodium).
- **Chemical Class:** Chemically stabilised double-stranded small interfering ribonucleic acid (siRNA) conjugated to a triantennary N-acetylgalactosamine (GalNAc) ligand.
- **Brand Name:** **Amvuttra** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N07XX18.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Solution for injection in a single-use pre-filled syringe. Each 0.5 mL contains 25 mg of vutrisiran.
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Medison Pharma Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hereditary transthyretin-mediated (hATTR) amyloidosis:** Treatment of polyneuropathy (stage 1 or stage 2) in adult patients [Australian].
- **Transthyretin-mediated (ATTR) amyloidosis (wild-type or hereditary):** Treatment of cardiomyopathy in adult patients [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 25 mg administered via subcutaneous injection once every three months.
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Therapy must be initiated under the supervision of a physician experienced in managing amyloidosis. 
    - Injection sites include the abdomen, thighs, or upper arms.
    - If injecting into the upper arm, administration must be performed by a healthcare professional or caregiver.
    - Solution should be inspected for particles and discolouration; if stored cold, allow the syringe to reach room temperature (approx. 30 minutes) before use.
- **Vitamin A Supplementation:** Patients must take oral Vitamin A supplementation at the recommended daily allowance (RDA) to mitigate the risk of ocular symptoms due to treatment-induced Vitamin A reduction.
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, it should be administered as soon as possible. Subsequent doses should then be resumed every three months from the date of the most recent administration.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** History of severe hypersensitivity (e.g., anaphylaxis) to vutrisiran or any of the excipients (sodium phosphate, sodium chloride, sodium hydroxide/phosphoric acid).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Vitamin A Deficiency:** Vutrisiran reduces serum levels of transthyretin (TTR), the carrier protein for retinol-binding protein. This results in reduced serum Vitamin A levels.
    - Pre-existing Vitamin A deficiency should be corrected before starting therapy.
    - Ocular symptoms (e.g., night blindness, dry eyes, corneal inflammation) should prompt an ophthalmological assessment.
- **Laboratory Test Interference:** Serum Vitamin A levels do not accurately reflect total body stores during vutrisiran treatment and must not be used to guide supplementation.
- **Hepatic and Renal Impairment:** Data are limited for patients with severe hepatic impairment or end-stage renal disease (ESRD). Use in these populations should be based on a benefit-risk assessment.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The safety profile is based on data from the HELIOS-A and HELIOS-B clinical studies.

| System Organ Class | Adverse Reaction | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| General Disorders | Injection site reaction (bruising, erythema, pain) | Common (≥1/100 to <1/10) |
| Investigations | Alanine transaminase (ALT) increased | Common (≥1/100 to <1/10) |
| Investigations | Blood alkaline phosphatase (ALP) increased | Common (≥1/100 to <1/10) |

- **Immunogenicity:** Low-titre, transient anti-drug antibodies (ADA) were observed in a small percentage of patients (approx. 0.3% to 3.3%) without discernible impact on efficacy or safety.
- **Liver Function:** Mild ALT elevations (≤3x ULN) were observed but were typically asymptomatic and resolved with continued dosing.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No clinical interaction studies have been performed. Vutrisiran is not expected to interact with or be affected by cytochrome P450 enzymes or transporters.
- **General Management:** As an siRNA metabolized by nucleases, clinically significant drug-drug interactions are considered unlikely.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category D [Australian]:** Due to the reduction in serum Vitamin A levels, which are essential for normal foetal development, vutrisiran is not recommended during pregnancy.
    - **Precautions:** Pregnancy must be excluded before treatment initiation. Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception.
    - **Management:** If pregnancy is planned, vutrisiran and Vitamin A supplementation should be discontinued, and serum Vitamin A levels should return to normal before conception.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if vutrisiran is excreted in human milk. A decision must be made to either discontinue breastfeeding or the drug, weighing the benefit of therapy for the mother against the benefit of breastfeeding for the infant.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats showed no adverse effects on male or female fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children and adolescents <18 years have not been established.
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment is required for patients ≥65 years of age.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for mild or moderate renal impairment (eGFR ≥30 to <90 mL/min/1.73 m²). Vutrisiran has not been studied in severe renal impairment.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for mild or moderate hepatic impairment. It has not been studied in severe hepatic impairment.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Vutrisiran utilizes RNA interference (RNAi) to target and degrade both mutant and wild-type TTR messenger RNA (mRNA) in the liver. This leads to a sustained reduction in serum TTR protein levels and reduced amyloid deposition in tissues.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Rapid TTR reduction is observed by Day 22. Steady-state mean TTR reduction is approximately 83–88% with quarterly dosing.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed after subcutaneous injection (Tmax approx. 3 hours).
    - **Distribution:** Primarily distributes to the liver; protein binding is >80%.
    - **Metabolism:** Degraded by endo- and exo-nucleases into short nucleotide fragments.
    - **Excretion:** Median apparent plasma clearance is 21.4 L/h; 15–25% of the dose is eliminated unchanged in urine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **HELIOS-A (hATTR-PN):** A phase 3 study where vutrisiran demonstrated significant improvement in the modified Neuropathy Impairment Score +7 (mNIS+7) and Norfolk Quality of Life-Diabetic Neuropathy (QoL-DN) score compared to an external placebo group at 18 months.
- **HELIOS-B (ATTR-CM):** A phase 3 study in patients with ATTR-cardiomyopathy. Vutrisiran significantly reduced the risk of all-cause mortality and recurrent cardiovascular events compared to placebo over a 36-month period, including patients on background tafamidis therapy.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C. Do not freeze.
- **Handling:** Keep the pre-filled syringe in the outer carton to protect it from light.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Subject to additional monitoring in certain jurisdictions to allow rapid identification of new safety information [Australian].
