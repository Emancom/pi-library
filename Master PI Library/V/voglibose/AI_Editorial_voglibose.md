# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA). 
- **Note:** Only the United States (FDA) Product Information was provided for this synthesis. Information for other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian, Japanese) is "Not specified in available sources" unless otherwise stated.
- Purpose: This document is a harmonised editorial overview intended for healthcare professionals. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** A carrier molecule; (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources (Note: Commonly A16AA01).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Intravenous injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose: 50 mg/kg.
    - Administration: Slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - Acute Management: A loading dose is often given in severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the following 24 hours. Doses should be administered every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    - Subsequent daily doses should remain in the 50 mg/kg range or as clinically required. The maximum recorded dose is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - Administration: Slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - Titration: Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels <40–50 µmol/L. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration before use.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate the risks/benefits before any attempt to re-administer [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring:** 
    - **ESRD:** Monitor trough (pre-dialysis) plasma carnitine levels weekly or monthly. Levels should ideally be between 35 and 60 µmol/L.
    - **Metabolic Disorders:** Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events reported in ESRD clinical trials at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) include:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (9%), headache (22%), infection (15%), injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), fever (7%), flu syndrome (25%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), chest pain (12%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), nausea (8%), vomiting (15%).
- **Metabolic/Nutritional:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hypervolemia (5%).
- **Nervous:** Dizziness (13%), paresthesia (5%), depression (5%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%).

**Serious/Postmarketing Adverse Effects:** 
- Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm [US].
- Seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. Monitoring of INR is recommended after initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine in patients on anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - [US]: Category not explicitly lettered in provided text, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** 
    - [US]: Not specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine in milk after administration. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother. Consider discontinuing nursing or the drug.
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impairment in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing guidelines for metabolic disorders apply to paediatric patients; see Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources; however, dose selection should be cautious, generally starting at the low end of the range [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - Oral levocarnitine safety/efficacy has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. 
    - Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD may lead to accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Levocarnitine clears acylCoA compounds by forming acylcarnitine, which is rapidly excreted [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Study:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly showed that trough levocarnitine levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. While the drug effectively increases carnitine concentrations, the impact on specific clinical outcomes in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) when mixed in PVC bags with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL [US].
