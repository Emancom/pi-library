# Editorial Product Information: Vancomycin Hydrochloride (Oral)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of oral vancomycin hydrochloride for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI. Clinicians should consult the local approved labeling for prescribing requirements in their specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Vancomycin hydrochloride (a tricyclic glycopeptide antibacterial).
- **Brand Names:** Vancomycin hydrochloride (Wilshire Pharmaceuticals) [US]. 
- **ATC Code:** A07AA09.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Powder for Oral Solution:** Kits containing 3.75 g, 7.5 g, or 15.0 g of vancomycin (as hydrochloride), supplied with a grape-flavored diluent [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Wilshire Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- ***Clostridioides difficile*-associated diarrhea (CDAD):** Treatment of diarrhea caused by *C. difficile* in adult and pediatric patients (under 18 years of age) [US].
- **Staphylococcal Enterocolitis:** Treatment of enterocolitis caused by *Staphylococcus aureus*, including methicillin-resistant strains, in adult and pediatric patients (under 18 years of age) [US].

**Important Limitations of Use:**
- **Oral vancomycin** is not effective for systemic infections. It must be administered orally for the treatment of CDAD and staphylococcal enterocolitis.
- To prevent the development of drug-resistant bacteria, use should be limited to infections proven or strongly suspected to be caused by susceptible bacteria.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Patients (18 years and older)
- ***C. difficile*-associated diarrhea:** 125 mg administered orally 4 times daily for 10 days [US].
- **Staphylococcal enterocolitis:** 500 mg to 2 g administered orally in 3 or 4 divided doses for 7 to 10 days [US].

### Pediatric Patients (less than 18 years of age)
- **Both indications:** 40 mg/kg in 3 or 4 divided doses for 7 to 10 days. The total daily dosage must not exceed 2 g [US].

### Administration and Preparation
- **Reconstitution:** The powder must be reconstituted by a healthcare provider (e.g., a pharmacist) using the provided diluent.
- **Instructions:** Patients should be instructed to shake the solution well before use and use an appropriate oral dosing device.
- **Storage after Reconstitution:** Store the solution in a refrigerator (2°C to 8°C). Discard any remaining solution after 14 days [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **vancomycin** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Oral Use Only:** Parenteral administration of vancomycin is ineffective for the treatment of CDAD or staphylococcal enterocolitis. Conversely, oral vancomycin is not effective for systemic infections [US].
- **Potential for Systemic Absorption:** While poorly absorbed, significant systemic levels can occur in patients with renal insufficiency or inflammatory mucosal disorders (e.g., colitis). Serum concentration monitoring may be appropriate in these cases [US].
- **Nephrotoxicity:** Renal impairment, including failure, has occurred during or after therapy. Risk is significantly higher in geriatric patients (>65 years). Monitor renal function during treatment [US].
- **Ototoxicity:** Hearing loss (transient or permanent) has been reported, primarily in patients with underlying hearing loss, renal impairment, or concomitant use of ototoxic agents (e.g., aminoglycosides) [US].
- **Severe Dermatologic Reactions:** Serious reactions including Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), and DRESS have been reported. Discontinue **vancomycin** immediately at the first sign of rash or mucosal lesions [US].
- **Hemorrhagic Occlusive Retinal Vasculitis (HORV):** Reported following off-label intracameral or intravitreal administration. Vancomycin is not approved for ophthalmic use [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reaction data derived from clinical trials for CDAD (N=260 adults) [US]:

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Nausea (17%), Abdominal pain (15%), Hypokalemia (13%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Vomiting (9%), Diarrhea (9%), Pyrexia (9%), Flatulence (8%), UTI (8%), Headache (7%), Peripheral edema (6%), Back pain (6%), Fatigue (5%) |

**Other Noted Effects:**
- **Nephrotoxicity:** Occurred in 5% of all subjects, increasing to 6% in those over 65 years [US].
- **Postmarketing Experience:** Anaphylaxis, drug fever, neutropenia (usually reversible), and thrombocytopenia. 
- **Infusion-like Reaction:** Symptoms similar to IV-induced "red man syndrome" (hypotension, flushing, pruritus, wheezing) have been reported with oral use, typically resolving within 20 minutes to several hours [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No formal drug interaction studies have been conducted with oral **vancomycin** [US].
- **Clinically Significant Interactions:** Concomitant use with other nephrotoxic or ototoxic drugs (e.g., aminoglycosides) may increase the risk of systemic toxicity if absorption occurs [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned a letter, but animal data (IV) at human equivalent doses showed no fetal malformations. Human data on 2nd/3rd-trimester exposure showed no association with birth defects or sensorineural hearing loss in neonates [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Systemic absorption following oral administration is minimal. The developmental benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need and potential infant exposure [US].
- **Fertility:** No definitive fertility studies have been conducted [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for patients <18 years. Dosing is weight-based (40 mg/kg/day) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Patients >65 years are at increased risk of nephrotoxicity and may respond more slowly to therapy. Renal function monitoring is essential [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Patients with renal impairment are at higher risk of systemic accumulation and toxicity (nephrotoxicity/ototoxicity) if absorption occurs. Monitoring of serum levels may be warranted [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No specific dose adjustments provided in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal. Inhibits bacterial cell-wall biosynthesis by binding to the D-alanyl-D-alanine portion of cell wall precursors. It also alters cell membrane permeability and RNA synthesis [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Active against *C. difficile* and *S. aureus* (including MRSA) [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
  - **Absorption:** Poorly absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract. In healthy subjects, serum levels are typically undetectable.
  - **Excretion:** Primarily excreted in the feces. After 2 g daily, fecal concentrations can exceed 3100 mcg/g [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **CDAD Efficacy:** Two Phase 3 trials in 266 adults compared **vancomycin** 125 mg 4 times daily. Clinical success (resolution of diarrhea) was achieved in 81.3% and 80.8% of subjects across the two trials. Median time to diarrhea resolution was 4 to 5 days [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Kit Storage:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) [US].
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Must be refrigerated (2°C to 8°C). Stable for 14 days. Protect from light and do not freeze [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources [Australian].
