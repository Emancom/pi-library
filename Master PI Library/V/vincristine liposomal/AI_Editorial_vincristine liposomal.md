# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data into a concise, structured format.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing in any particular region. At the time of writing, only the United States PI was available for review; therefore, regional variations for other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European) are "Not specified in available sources."

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Metabolic):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients presenting with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It may be administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion. 
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - Subsequent daily maintenance doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** 
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
    - **Adjustment:** Dose adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration before use.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

### Monitoring
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
- **ESRD:** Monitor trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels (normal range 40–50 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occurred within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate the risks versus benefits before re-administration [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine (both oral and IV) has been associated with reports of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with a history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** In patients with ESRD, monitor for accumulation of metabolites (TMA and TMAO) if high doses of oral levocarnitine are used; however, this is primarily a concern for oral administration in renal impairment, as these metabolites are normally excreted in urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic errors.

### Very Common (>10%)
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33–59%), Pain (30–49%), Headache (22%), Fever (12% in 40 mg/kg dose), Asthenia (12% in 40 mg/kg dose) [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), Hypotension (14–19%) [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (16–35%), Vomiting (15–21%), Nausea (8–12%) [US].
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23–33%), Cough increase (10–18%), Dyspnea (11–19%), Rhinitis (11%) [US].

### Common (1–10%)
- **General:** Abdominal pain, chest pain, flu syndrome, accidental injury [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Arrhythmia, atrial fibrillation, tachycardia, palpitations [US].
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight changes [US].
- **Neurological:** Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia, anxiety, depression [US].
- **Special Senses:** Amblyopia, eye disorder, taste perversion [US].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Anaphylaxis/Hypersensitivity** [US].
- **Seizures** [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels frequently in patients on warfarin therapy following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustment [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not assigned/Not specified]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been specifically studied. In dairy cows, levocarnitine concentrations in milk increase following exogenous administration. Weigh the benefits of maternal supplementation against the potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is calculated by weight (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65+ to determine if they respond differently than younger subjects. Dose selection should be cautious, starting at the low end of the range [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **IV Use:** Specifically indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis [US].
    - **Oral Use (Precautions):** Safety and efficacy of high-dose oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD on dialysis have not been evaluated and may lead to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This process facilitates beta-oxidation and subsequent energy production in tissues, particularly skeletal and cardiac muscle [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) and γ-butyrobetaine [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following a single IV dose, approximately 76% is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Study:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly, mean pre-dialysis (trough) carnitine levels rose from ~20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While carnitine levels increased, the impact on clinical outcomes in this specific population remains to be fully determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours when mixed in PVC bags with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL at room temperature [US].
