# Editorial Product Information: viloxazine hydrochloride

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **viloxazine hydrochloride**
- Chemical class: Selective norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor (noradrenergic agent)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 2021 [US]
- Date of last revision/update of the available PI: Revised 1/2025 [US]
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources
- Registration/approval dates for jurisdictions other than the United States: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) in adults and pediatric patients 6 years and older. [US]
- Indication status in jurisdictions other than the United States: Not specified in available sources

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Recommended starting dose: 200 mg orally once daily.
- Titration: May increase in weekly increments of 200 mg once daily to a maximum recommended dose of 600 mg once daily, depending on response and tolerability. Periodically reassess need for continued therapy.

### Dosing by age group (pediatric)
- Pediatric patients 6 to 11 years: recommended starting dose 100 mg orally once daily. May titrate in 100 mg increments at weekly intervals to a maximum recommended dose of 400 mg once daily, based on response and tolerability.
- Pediatric patients 12 to 17 years: recommended starting dose 200 mg orally once daily. After 1 week, dosage may be titrated by an increment of 200 mg to a maximum recommended dose of 400 mg once daily, based on response and tolerability.

### Renal impairment
- Severe renal impairment (eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m2): recommended starting dose 100 mg once daily. Titrate in weekly increments of 50 to 100 mg once daily to a maximum recommended dose of 200 mg once daily.
- Mild to moderate renal impairment (eGFR 30–89 mL/min/1.73 m2): no dosage adjustment recommended.

### Administration instructions
- Administer orally with or without food.
- Do not cut, crush, or chew the extended‑release capsule. Swallow whole, or open the capsule and sprinkle the entire contents over a spoonful of pudding or applesauce. Consume the mixture in its entirety without chewing: within 15 minutes if mixed with pudding, or within 2 hours if mixed with applesauce. Do not store mixed product for future use.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources

## 5. Contraindications
- Concomitant treatment with a monoamine oxidase inhibitor (MAOI), or use within 14 days after discontinuing an MAOI, because of risk of hypertensive crisis.
- Concomitant administration of sensitive CYP1A2 substrates or CYP1A2 substrates with a narrow therapeutic range (viloxazine is a strong CYP1A2 inhibitor; coadministration is contraindicated).

(These contraindications are stated for the available jurisdictional PI information.) [US]

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warning
- **Suicidal thoughts and behaviors:** Higher rates of suicidal thoughts and behaviors were reported in clinical trials of patients with ADHD treated with **viloxazine hydrochloride** compared with placebo. Closely monitor patients for clinical worsening and for emergence of suicidal ideation or behavior, particularly during the initial months of therapy and at times of dose changes. Advise caregivers to monitor and report emergence of suicidal ideation, worsening depression, or behavioral change promptly. [US]

### General warnings and precautions
- Blood pressure and heart rate increases: assess heart rate and blood pressure prior to initiating therapy, after dose increases, and periodically during treatment. Documented increases in heart rate and diastolic blood pressure occurred in pediatric and adult clinical trials.
- Activation of mania or hypomania: noradrenergic agents may precipitate a manic or mixed episode in patients with bipolar disorder. Screen patients for bipolar disorder risk (personal or family history) prior to starting therapy.
- Somnolence and fatigue: **viloxazine hydrochloride** can cause somnolence (including sedation/lethargy) and fatigue. Advise patients to use caution when performing activities requiring mental alertness (e.g., driving, operating machinery) until they know how they are affected.
- Precursor symptoms: insomnia, irritability, depressed mood, anxiety, agitation, akathisia, mania/hypomania, panic attacks, impulsive behavior, or aggression may presage emergent suicidal ideation or behavior; observe patients for these symptoms and consider changing therapy if they emerge and are severe or abrupt in onset.
- Pregnancy: animal reproductive studies indicate potential for fetal toxicities and maternal toxicities at exposures approximating the maximum recommended human dose; discontinue when pregnancy is recognized unless benefits justify continued use. Pregnancy exposure registry available for women exposed during pregnancy. [US]

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and periodic assessment of heart rate and blood pressure.
- Psychiatric monitoring for emergence or worsening of suicidal ideation, depression, mania/hypomania, or other behavioral changes.
- Monitor for somnolence/fatigue and counsel regarding activities requiring alertness.
- Monitor for adverse effects of coadministered drugs affected by CYP1A2, CYP2D6, or CYP3A4 inhibition (see Drug Interactions).

## 7. Adverse Effects
The following adverse reactions were observed in clinical trials. Frequencies are derived from placebo‑controlled studies and long‑term exposure data reported in the available PI. Frequency categories used: very common (>10%), common (1–10%), uncommon (0.1–1%), rare (<0.1%), very rare (<0.01%).

- Serious adverse events highlighted in trials: suicidal ideation and behavior, increases in blood pressure and heart rate, activation of mania or hypomania, somnolence and fatigue.

- Discontinuations due to adverse reactions:
  - Pediatric: approximately 3% discontinued due to adverse reactions (most common causes included somnolence, nausea, headache, irritability, tachycardia, fatigue, decreased appetite).
  - Adult: approximately 9% discontinued due to adverse reactions (most common causes included fatigue, insomnia, constipation, headache).

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs): Concomitant use with an MAOI or within 14 days after discontinuing an MAOI is contraindicated due to risk of potentially life‑threatening hypertensive crisis. [US]
- CYP1A2 substrates:
  - **viloxazine hydrochloride** is a strong inhibitor of CYP1A2.
  - Concomitant use with sensitive CYP1A2 substrates or CYP1A2 substrates with a narrow therapeutic range is contraindicated (coadministration may substantially increase total exposure of these substrates and risk of adverse effects). [US]
  - For moderate sensitive CYP1A2 substrates, coadministration is not recommended; if coadministration is necessary, dose reduction of the CYP1A2 substrate may be warranted.
  - The interaction increases total exposure (AUC) but not necessarily peak exposure (Cmax) of CYP1A2 substrates.
- CYP2D6 substrates:
  - **viloxazine hydrochloride** is a weak inhibitor of CYP2D6 and can increase exposure of CYP2D6 substrates.
  - Monitor patients for adverse reactions of CYP2D6 substrates and adjust doses as clinically indicated.
- CYP3A4 substrates:
  - **viloxazine hydrochloride** is a weak inhibitor of CYP3A4 and can increase exposure of CYP3A4 substrates.
  - Monitor patients for adverse reactions of CYP3A4 substrates and adjust doses as clinically indicated.
- Pharmacodynamic interactions:
  - Combining with other agents that increase heart rate or blood pressure, or that can precipitate agitation, insomnia, or suicidal ideation, warrants caution and monitoring.
- Region‑specific interaction management: The contraindications and interaction precautions described above reflect the available U.S. product information. Interaction recommendations for other jurisdictions are not specified in the available sources.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Pregnancy exposure registry: A pregnancy exposure registry that monitors outcomes in women exposed to viloxazine during pregnancy is available; healthcare providers are encouraged to register patients. [US]
- Risk summary: Based on animal reproduction studies, viloxazine may cause fetal toxicities and maternal toxicities at exposures approximating the maximum recommended human dose. Discontinue viloxazine when pregnancy is recognized unless the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus and mother. Data from human pregnancies are insufficient to establish drug‑associated risk for major birth defects or miscarriage. [US]

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **viloxazine hydrochloride** is a selective norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor (noradrenergic agent). Exact link between mechanism and clinical efficacy in ADHD is not fully described in the available sources.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Metabolic enzyme interactions:
  - Strong inhibitor of CYP1A2 (increases total exposure of CYP1A2 substrates).
  - Weak inhibitor of CYP2D6 and CYP3A4 (can increase exposure of substrates for these enzymes).
- Other ADME parameters (absorption rate, bioavailability, distribution volumes, half‑life, routes of elimination, specific metabolites, effects of food on pharmacokinetics) are not specified in the available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Clinical pharmacodynamic effects include increases in heart rate and diastolic blood pressure observed in trials; somnolence and fatigue were also observed as pharmacodynamic adverse effects.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Pediatric trials:
  - Short‑term randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trials enrolled pediatric patients aged 6–17 years with doses 100–400 mg; pooled randomized dataset included 826 pediatric patients; total pediatric exposure reported across trials was 1,118 patients.
  - Longer‑term exposure: 682 pediatric patients treated ≥6 months; 347 patients treated ≥12 months in extension studies.
  - Suicidality: Among 1,019 pediatric patients exposed to 100–400 mg, nine patients (0.9%) reported suicidal ideation or behavior (six ideation, one behavior, two both) versus 2/463 (0.4%) on placebo reporting ideation; no completed suicides.
  - Heart rate/BP findings and adverse event profiles summarized in Sections 6 and 11.
- Adult trials:
  - Short‑term randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial enrolled adults 18–60 years with flexible dosing 200–600 mg; 189 adults received viloxazine in primary trial; total adult exposure reported was 277 patients.
  - Longer‑term exposure: 84 adults treated ≥6 months; 22 adults treated ≥12 months.
  - Suicidality: 3/189 adults (1.6%) treated with viloxazine reported suicidal ideation versus 0/183 on placebo; no attempts or completed suicides reported in the trial.
- Efficacy endpoints, primary outcome measures, and numerical efficacy results are not specified in the available sources.
