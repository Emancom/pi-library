# Editorial Product Information: Sodium Valproate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of sodium valproate for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved labelling for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Sodium valproate (sodium 2-propylpentanoate).
- **Chemical Class:** Fatty acid derivative; branched-chain carboxylic acid.
- **Brand Names:** APO-Sodium Valproate [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N03AG01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
  - Enteric-coated tablets: 200 mg and 500 mg [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Epilepsy:** 
  - Primary generalised epilepsy, including petit mal absences, myoclonic epilepsy, and tonic-clonic (grand mal) seizures. 
  - Partial (focal) epilepsy, utilised as either monotherapy or adjunctive therapy.
- **Mania:** For the treatment of manic episodes associated with bipolar disorder in instances where alternative therapies are inadequate or inappropriate [Australian-approved; efficacy not established in patients <18 years].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Initial Dose:** 600 mg daily.
- **Titration:** Increase by 200 mg/day at three-day intervals until clinical control is achieved.
- **Maintenance Range:** Typically 1,000 mg to 2,000 mg daily (approx. 20–30 mg/kg/day).
- **Maximum Dose:** 2,500 mg/day.

### Indication-Specific Dosing
- **Mania:** Dosing follows the same titration schedule as epilepsy, starting at 600 mg daily with a typical maintenance range of 1,000–2,000 mg daily. Optimal efficacy in mania has been associated with serum levels above 45 µg/mL; adverse events increase significantly above 125 µg/mL [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing (Epilepsy)
- **Children >20 kg:** Start at 400 mg/day with spaced increases; maintenance is usually 20 to 30 mg/kg/day.
- **Children <20 kg:** 20 mg/kg/day. In severe cases, this may be increased, provided plasma valproic acid levels and haematological/clinical chemistry parameters are monitored (especially above 40 mg/kg/day).

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets should be taken with or after food to minimise gastric irritation.
- Enteric-coated tablets must be swallowed whole; they should not be crushed or chewed.
- The 500 mg strength is recommended for patients requiring high daily dosages [Australian].
- Initial clinical effects may take several days; maximum effect may require two to six weeks of therapy.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - Contraindicated for the treatment of **mania** during pregnancy [Australian].
  - Contraindicated for the treatment of **epilepsy** during pregnancy unless no suitable alternative exists [Australian].
- **Women of Childbearing Potential:** Contraindicated unless the specific conditions of a pregnancy prevention program are met.
- **Hepatobiliary:** Pre-existing acute or chronic hepatic dysfunction; personal or family history of severe hepatitis (especially drug-related).
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
  - Known Urea Cycle Disorders (UCD).
  - Known systemic primary carnitine deficiency with uncorrected hypocarnitinemia.
  - Hepatic porphyria.
- **Mitochondrial Disorders:** Patients with known or suspected mutations in the POLG gene (e.g., Alpers-Huttenlocher Syndrome), particularly in children under two years of age.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to sodium valproate or any excipients.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Severe, potentially fatal liver damage has occurred, typically within the first six months (highest risk 2–12 weeks). Risk is greatest in children <3 years, those on polytherapy, and those with metabolic or mitochondrial disorders.
- **Pancreatitis:** Life-threatening haemorrhagic pancreatitis has been reported in children and adults. Onset can be shortly after initiation or after several years of use. Immediate withdrawal is required if diagnosed.
- **Teratogenicity:** High risk of major congenital malformations (approx. 11%) and neurodevelopmental disorders (approx. 30–40%) following *in utero* exposure.
- **Suicidality:** Antiepileptic drugs increase the risk of suicidal ideation and behaviour. Patients should be monitored for changes in mood or behaviour.
- **Male Reproductive Risk:** Retrospective data suggest an increased risk of neurodevelopmental disorders in children born to fathers treated with valproate in the three months prior to conception. Males should not donate sperm during treatment or for three months after cessation [Australian].
- **Aggravated Convulsions:** Some patients may experience a reversible increase in seizure frequency or severity.
- **Blood Disorders:** Thrombocytopenia and impaired platelet aggregation may occur; monitor full blood count and coagulation before surgery.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequency is categorised as Very Common (>10%), Common (1–10%), Uncommon (0.1–1%), and Rare (<0.1%).

| Organ System | Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Very Common | Nausea (20%), vomiting, upper abdominal pain, diarrhoea. |
| **Nervous System** | Very Common | Tremor (16%), somnolence, headache, dizziness, stupor. |
| **Skin** | Very Common | Transient/dose-related alopecia (hair may regrow curly). |
| **Metabolic** | Common | Weight gain (risk factor for polycystic ovary syndrome), increased appetite. |
| **Blood/Lymphatic** | Common | Anaemia, thrombocytopenia. |
| **Psychiatric** | Common | Confusional state, aggression, agitation, learning disorders. |
| **Hepatobiliary** | Common | Liver injury, transiently raised liver enzymes. |
| **Metabolic** | Rare | Hyperammonaemia (may occur with normal LFTs), SIADH, hypothyroidism. |
| **Dermatological** | Rare | Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN), DRESS. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic (Valproate as Inhibitor):** Valproate inhibits CYP450, glucuronyl transferase, and epoxide hydrolase. It increases levels of:
  - **Lamotrigine:** Significant risk of serious skin rash; dose reduction required.
  - **Phenobarbital:** Increases levels leading to severe sedation, especially in children.
  - **Phenytoin:** Displaces from protein binding and inhibits metabolism.
  - **Zidovudine, Tricyclic Antidepressants, and Benzodiazepines (Diazepam, Lorazepam).**
- **Pharmacokinetic (Effects on Valproate):**
  - **Enzyme Inducers (Carbamazepine, Phenytoin, Rifampicin):** Decrease valproate levels.
  - **Carbapenem Antibiotics:** Rapid, significant decrease in valproate levels; co-administration is not recommended.
  - **Aspirin:** Displaces valproate from protein binding and inhibits its metabolism, increasing free levels.
- **Pharmacodynamic:**
  - **Alcohol:** Potentiates CNS depression; intake is not recommended.
  - **Cannabidiol:** Increases risk of transaminase elevations.
  - **Topiramate/Acetazolamide:** Increased risk of encephalopathy and hyperammonaemia.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy Category:** **Category D** [Australian].
- **Teratogenicity:** Exposure *in utero* is associated with neural tube defects (1–2%), craniofacial defects, and cardiovascular malformations. Significant risk of reduced IQ and autism spectrum disorders in offspring.
- **Clinical Management:** Pregnancy must be excluded before initiation. Women of childbearing potential must use at least one highly effective (user-independent) or two complementary forms of contraception. Folate 5 mg daily is recommended for those planning pregnancy, though it may not prevent valproate-induced defects.
- **Breastfeeding:** Valproate is excreted in breast milk (1–10% of serum levels). As a general rule, breastfeeding should not be undertaken during treatment [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use with extreme caution in children <3 years due to hepatotoxicity risk. Monotherapy is preferred in this age group. Efficacy in mania is not established for those <18 years.
- **Elderly Use:** Increased volume of distribution and decreased albumin binding lead to a higher free drug fraction. Dosage should be determined by seizure control rather than total plasma levels.
- **Renal Impairment:** Lower doses may be required due to increased free drug levels (lowered albumin) and poor urinary excretion of metabolites.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in acute or chronic hepatic dysfunction.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Not fully established. Attributed to the blockade of voltage-dependent sodium channels and increased brain levels of gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) via inhibition of degradative enzymes (GABA transaminase).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Enteric coating delays peak levels to 1–5 hours. Food further delays absorption but does not affect extent.
  - **Distribution:** 90% protein bound (only 60% to albumin). Binding is saturable at levels >120 µg/mL.
  - **Metabolism:** Complex; primarily via glucuronidation (40–60%) and beta-oxidation (30–40%).
  - **Excretion:** Primarily renal. Mean half-life is approx. 15 hours (range 10–23 hours).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Mania:** The Bowden et al. study (1994) demonstrated that valproate (as divalproex) was superior to placebo and comparable to lithium in treating acute mania. 48% of valproate-treated patients showed >50% improvement on the Mania Rating Scale compared to 25% for placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C.
- Protect from moisture (tablets are hygroscopic).
- Keep in protective foil/blister until immediately before administration [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only.
