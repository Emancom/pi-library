# Editorial Product Information: ACAM2000 (Smallpox and Mpox [Vaccinia] Vaccine, Live)

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of ACAM2000 for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data provided in official product information.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the local approved PI before administration.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Vaccinia virus (live), derived from plaque purification cloning of the New York City Board of Health (NYCBH) strain.
- **Brand Name:** **ACAM2000** [United States].
- **ATC Code:** J07AN01 (Vaccinia vaccine).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 
  - Lyophilized Antigen Component (Live): Provided in multiple-dose vials containing purified live vaccinia virus.
  - After reconstitution with 0.3 mL of the provided Diluent Component, the concentration is 1.0 - 5.0 x 10⁸ plaque-forming units (PFU) per mL. 
  - A single dose is approximately 0.0025 mL (2.5 - 12.5 x 10⁵ PFU).
- **Route of Administration:** Percutaneous use via scarification only. Do not administer by injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Emergent Product Development Gaithersburg Inc. [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Active Immunization:** Indicated for the prevention of smallpox and mpox disease in individuals determined to be at high risk for infection [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** A single dose (approximately 0.0025 mL) of the reconstituted vaccine is administered percutaneously.
- **Administration Technique (Scarification):**
  - Use only a sterile bifurcated needle.
  - Dip the needle into the vaccine vial so that the fork of the bifurcation picks up a droplet.
  - Administer 15 rapid, vigorous jabs perpendicular to the skin through the droplet on the upper arm (over the deltoid). 
  - The jabs must be firm enough to cause a trace of blood to appear at the site within a 5 mm diameter.
- **Interpretation of Response ("Take"):**
  - **Primary Vaccinees:** A successful "take" is confirmed by the development of a major cutaneous reaction (pustule) by Day 6–11. The lesion evolves from a papule (Day 2–5) to a vesicle, then a pustule, followed by a scab which separates in 3–6 weeks.
  - **Revaccinated Individuals:** Success is confirmed by a major cutaneous reaction observed 6–8 days post-vaccination. Note that pre-existing immunity may reduce the intensity of the reaction.
- **Training:** Personnel must be specifically trained in the safe and effective administration of the vaccine and the interpretation of the "take."

## 4. Contraindications
- **Severe Immunodeficiency:** Individuals with severe immunodeficiency, including those undergoing bone marrow transplantation or those with primary or acquired immunodeficiency states requiring isolation [United States].
- **Emergency Use Exception:** In a high-risk outbreak/emergency, there are no absolute contraindications; the risk of fatal smallpox or mpox must be weighed against vaccine risks.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning (United States): Serious Complications**
  - **Myocarditis and Pericarditis:** Observed at a rate of 5.7 per 1000 primary vaccinees. 
  - **Neurological/Systemic Events:** Encephalitis, encephalomyelitis, encephalopathy, progressive vaccinia (vaccinia necrosum), and generalized vaccinia have occurred.
  - **Dermatological:** Severe skin infections, erythema multiforme major (including Stevens-Johnson Syndrome), and eczema vaccinatum.
  - **Ocular:** Accidental eye infection (ocular vaccinia) may lead to blindness.
  - **Transmission:** Live virus is shed from the site and can be transmitted to close contacts.
- **Cardiac Disease:** Increased risk of serious adverse events in patients with known cardiac conditions (previous MI, angina, CHF, stroke, etc.) or those with ≥3 risk factors for ischemic coronary disease (e.g., hypertension, diabetes, smoking).
- **Eczema/Skin Conditions:** History of eczema (even if inactive) or other exfoliative skin disorders increases the risk of eczema vaccinatum.
- **Secondary Transmission:** Vaccinees must keep the site covered and wash hands thoroughly to prevent spreading the virus to household or sexual contacts until the scab falls off.
- **Blood/Organ Donation:** Avoid donation for 6 weeks following vaccination.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** 
  - Injection site reactions: Pruritus (92%), erythema (74%), pain (67%), swelling (48%).
  - Systemic: Lymph node pain (57%), headache (50%), myalgia (46%), fatigue (48%), malaise (37%), feeling hot (32%), rigors (21%), nausea (19%), diarrhea (16%).
- **Common (1–10%):** Constipation, vomiting, dyspnea, rash, exercise tolerance decreased.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:**
  - Myocarditis/pericarditis.
  - Post-vaccinial encephalitis/myelitis.
  - Eczema vaccinatum and progressive vaccinia.
  - Ocular vaccinia.
  - Fetal death (in pregnant vaccinees).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Laboratory Test Interference:** 
  - **Syphilis:** May induce false-positive RPR results for up to 6 weeks. Confirm with a more specific test (e.g., FTA-ABS).
  - **Tuberculosis:** May induce temporary false-negative tuberculin skin tests (PPD) or blood tests. Delay TB testing for 6 weeks post-vaccination if possible.
- **Immunosuppressants:** Corticosteroids or other immunomodulatory drugs may increase the risk of severe localized or systemic vaccinia infection.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **Category:** Not explicitly categorized by letter, but the document notes ACAM2000 has not been studied in pregnant women [United States].
  - **Risk:** Historical data indicates live vaccinia virus can cause fetal vaccinia, stillbirth, or neonatal death.
  - **Clinical Note:** Not recommended in non-emergency situations. Vaccinees should avoid pregnancy for 6 weeks post-vaccination.
- **Breastfeeding:** ACAM2000 has not been studied in lactating women. Live vaccinia virus may be transmitted from mother to infant via breastfeeding or close physical contact, posing a risk of serious complications for the infant.
- **Fertility:** No animal data available for male or female fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Increased risk of serious complications in children, particularly those <12 months of age. Effectiveness in pediatric age groups is based on extrapolation from adult data.
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include sufficient numbers of subjects ≥65 years to determine if they respond differently.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Induces a localized infection of the epidermis. This triggers the production of neutralizing antibodies and memory B and T cells that cross-protect against other orthopoxviruses (variola and mpox).
- **Pharmacodynamics:** 
  - Successful "take" (cutaneous response) indicates virus replication. 
  - Neutralizing antibodies develop in >95% of primary vaccinees by Day 15–20.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Not applicable for this vaccine type (local replication). Virus is shed from the site until re-epithelialization is complete (3–6 weeks).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Study 1 (Vaccinia-naïve):** ACAM2000 was non-inferior to the previous standard (Dryvax) regarding successful vaccination rates (96% vs 99%).
- **Pivotal Study 2 (Vaccinia-experienced):** ACAM2000 did not meet non-inferiority for cutaneous response (84% vs 98%) but was non-inferior regarding neutralizing antibody strength. 
- **Nonclinical Data:** 100% of vaccinated Cynomolgus macaques survived a lethal mpox virus challenge, compared to 0% in the placebo group.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Frozen Storage:** Store at -25°C to -15°C (-13°F to 5°F).
- **Refrigerated Storage (Unopened):** May be stored at 2°C to 8°C for up to 18 months or until the 18-month expiry date, whichever is first.
- **Reconstituted Stability:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) for up to 30 days. May be kept at 20°C to 25°C for only up to 8 hours.
- **Diluent Storage:** Store at 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F).

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only. 
- **Handling:** Reconstituted vaccine and contaminated materials (needles, gauze) must be handled and disposed of as biohazardous material.
