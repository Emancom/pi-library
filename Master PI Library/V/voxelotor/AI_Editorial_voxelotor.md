# Editorial Product Information: Voxelotor

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **United States (Food and Drug Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory information into a coherent clinical reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Voxelotor, a hemoglobin S (HbS) polymerization inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** **Oxbryta** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Film-coated tablets:** 300 mg and 500 mg [US].
    - **Tablets for oral suspension:** 300 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Global Blood Therapeutics, Inc. (a subsidiary of Pfizer Inc.) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Sickle Cell Disease (SCD):** Indicated for the treatment of SCD in adults and pediatric patients aged 4 years and older [US].
- **Regulatory Status:** This indication was granted under accelerated approval based on increases in hemoglobin (Hb) levels. Continued approval may be contingent upon verification of clinical benefit in confirmatory trials [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Adults and Pediatric Patients (12 years and older):** 1,500 mg orally once daily [US].
- **Pediatric Patients (4 to <12 years):** Dosing is weight-based [US]:
    - $\ge$ 40 kg: 1,500 mg once daily.
    - 20 kg to < 40 kg: 900 mg once daily.
    - 10 kg to < 20 kg: 600 mg once daily.

### Administration Instructions
- **General:** May be taken with or without food [US].
- **Film-coated tablets:** Must be swallowed whole. Do not cut, crush, or chew [US].
- **Tablets for oral suspension:**
    - Must be dispersed in a room-temperature clear liquid (e.g., water, clear soda, apple juice, clear sports drinks) immediately before administration. 
    - The minimum volume of liquid is 5 mL for 300 mg, up to 40 mL for 2,400 mg.
    - The mixture will not fully dissolve; swirl the cup to ensure clumps are suspended.
    - Do not swallow whole, cut, or chew these tablets [US].
- **Substitution:** Tablets for oral suspension may be substituted for film-coated tablets in patients $\ge$ 12 years who have difficulty swallowing [US].

### Missed Dose
- If a dose is missed or not entirely consumed, resume dosing the following day; do not double the dose [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** History of serious drug hypersensitivity to voxelotor or any excipients [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including generalized rash, urticaria, facial swelling, and eosinophilia, have occurred in <1% of patients. **Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS)** has been reported in postmarketing experience. If suspected, discontinue voxelotor immediately and initiate appropriate therapy. Do not reinitiate the drug [US].
- **Laboratory Test Interference:** Voxelotor interferes with the measurement of hemoglobin subtypes (HbA, HbS, and HbF) using High-Performance Liquid Chromatography (HPLC). If precise quantitation is required, chromatography should be performed at least 10 days after the last dose [US].
- **Monitoring:** Observe for signs of severe skin reactions or systemic organ involvement (hepatic, renal, pulmonary) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency in Patients 12 Years and Older
| Adverse Reaction | Voxelotor 1,500 mg (N=88) | Placebo (N=91) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Headache** | 32% | 25% |
| **Diarrhea** | 23% | 11% |
| **Abdominal Pain** | 23% | 16% |
| **Nausea** | 19% | 10% |
| **Rash** | 15% | 11% |
| **Pyrexia** | 15% | 8% |

### Frequency in Pediatric Patients (4 to <12 Years)
- Common adverse reactions (>10%) include pyrexia (36%), vomiting (33%), rash (20%), abdominal pain (18%), diarrhea (18%), and headache (18%) [US].

### Serious and Postmarketing Adverse Effects
- **Serious:** Pulmonary embolism, drug hypersensitivity, and headache (approx. 3%) [US].
- **Postmarketing:** Pruritus, angioedema (eyelid, face, lip, or periorbital swelling), and DRESS [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Strong or Moderate CYP3A4 Inducers:** Coadministration decreases voxelotor concentrations, potentially reducing efficacy. Avoid concomitant use (e.g., rifampin, St. John’s wort). If unavoidable, increase voxelotor dose [US]:
    - Adults/Pediatrics $\ge$ 12 years: 2,500 mg (strong inducer) or 2,000 mg (moderate inducer) QD.
- **Sensitive CYP3A4 Substrates:** Voxelotor increases the systemic exposure of CYP3A4 substrates. Avoid use of sensitive substrates with a narrow therapeutic index (e.g., midazolam). If unavoidable, consider substrate dose reduction [US].
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Concomitant use with itraconazole increased voxelotor AUC by 11% (not clinically significant) [US].
- **Acid-reducing agents:** Omeprazole does not alter voxelotor exposure [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not assigned under current labeling rules]. No available human data on birth defect or miscarriage risk. Animal studies (rats/rabbits) showed no developmental toxicity at 2.8-times and 0.3-times human exposure, respectively. Use only if benefits outweigh risks [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on the presence of voxelotor in human milk. Voxelotor was detected in the milk of lactating rats. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in the breastfed child, breastfeeding is **not recommended** during treatment and for at least 2 weeks after the final dose [US].
- **Fertility:** In animal studies, no effect on fertility was noted, though decreased sperm motility and morphology changes occurred at 5-times the human recommended dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established in patients $\ge$ 4 years. Not established in children < 4 years [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects $\ge$ 65 years to determine if they respond differently [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No clinically significant differences in PK observed in mild to severe renal impairment (eGFR 15–89 mL/min). Not evaluated in end-stage renal disease requiring dialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild to Moderate (Child-Pugh A and B):** No dose adjustment required.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Reduce dose to 1,000 mg QD (Adults/$\ge$ 12 yrs) or weight-based reduction (4 to < 12 yrs) [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Voxelotor is an HbS polymerization inhibitor that binds with 1:1 stoichiometry to the alpha chain of hemoglobin. It increases hemoglobin's affinity for oxygen, which maintains HbS in an oxygenated state, thereby inhibiting polymerization and subsequent RBC sickling [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Median $T_{max}$ of 2 hours.
    - **Distribution:** High partition into RBCs (blood-to-plasma ratio 17:1); 99.8% plasma protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolized via Phase I (oxidation/reduction) and Phase II (glucuronidation). Metabolism is mediated by CYP3A4, CYP3A5, CYP2B6, CYP2C19, CYP2C9, UGT1A1, and UGT1A9.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 38.7 hours. Excretion is 62.6% in feces and 35.5% in urine [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **HOPE Trial (Adults and Pediatrics $\ge$ 12 years):** A Phase 3, randomized, placebo-controlled trial. The voxelotor 1,500 mg group achieved an Hb response rate (>1 g/dL increase) of 51.1% vs. 6.5% for placebo (p < 0.001) at Week 24. Significant reductions in hemolysis markers (indirect bilirubin and reticulocyte count) were also observed [US].
- **Phase 2 Open-label Study (Pediatrics 4 to < 12 years):** Supported efficacy with an Hb response rate of 36% at Week 24 [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store at 20°C to 30°C (68°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Bottles contain a desiccant canister and polyester coil; do not eat these. Dispense tablets for oral suspension only in the original bottle [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Schedule:** Not a scheduled controlled substance [US].
