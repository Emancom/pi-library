# Editorial Product Information: Vorapaxar

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of vorapaxar Product Information (PI) for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial reference and does not replace the official, legally approved Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Vorapaxar (as sulfate)
- **Chemical Class:** Tricyclic himbacine-derived selective inhibitor of platelet aggregation; protease-activated receptor-1 (PAR-1) antagonist.
- **Brand Name(s):** ZONTIVITY [US]
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Tablet:** 2.08 mg vorapaxar (equivalent to 2.5 mg vorapaxar sulfate). [US]
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** WraSer Pharmaceuticals, LLC [US]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Reduction of Thrombotic Cardiovascular Events:** Indicated for patients with a history of myocardial infarction (MI) or with peripheral arterial disease (PAD). It has been shown to reduce the rate of a combined endpoint including cardiovascular death, MI, stroke, and urgent coronary revascularization (UCR). [US]

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** 2.08 mg (one tablet) taken orally once daily. [US]
- **Administration:** May be taken with or without food. [US]
- **Coadministration:** Intended for use with aspirin and/or clopidogrel according to their respective indications or standard of care. There is no clinical experience using vorapaxar as a monotherapy. [US]
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Titration:** No titration schedule is required. [US]

## 4. Contraindications
- **History of Cerebrovascular Events:** History of stroke, transient ischemic attack (TIA), or intracranial hemorrhage (ICH). Use in these patients increases the risk of ICH. [US]
- **Active Bleeding:** Pathological bleeding, such as ICH or active peptic ulcer. [US]

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning - Bleeding Risk:** [US] Vorapaxar, like other antiplatelet agents, increases the risk of bleeding, including life-threatening and fatal events. It must not be used in patients with a history of stroke, TIA, or ICH.
- **General Bleeding Risk:** Risk increases in proportion to the patient's underlying risk factors, such as older age, low body weight, reduced renal/hepatic function, or history of bleeding disorders. [US]
- **Concomitant Medications:** Avoid use with anticoagulants (e.g., warfarin) or fibrinolytic therapy due to increased bleeding risk. Concomitant use of NSAIDs, SSRIs, or SNRIs also increases risk. [US]
- **Irreversibility:** Vorapaxar has a long half-life (approximately 8 days), making its antiplatelet effect effectively irreversible. There is no known reversal agent. Withholding the drug for a short period is not useful for managing acute bleeding. [US]
- **CYP3A modulators:** Avoid concomitant use with strong CYP3A inhibitors or inducers. [US]

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** 
    - **Bleeding:** [US] Any GUSTO bleeding (25.0% vs 17.6% for placebo).
    - **Clinically Significant Bleeding:** [US] (13.4% vs 9.5% for placebo).
- **Common (1–10%):**
    - **Anemia:** 5.0% [US]
    - **Gastrointestinal bleeding:** 4.0% [US]
    - **Depression:** 2.4% [US]
    - **Rashes, eruptions, and exanthemas:** 2.2% [US]
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):**
    - **Intracranial Hemorrhage:** 0.4% [US]
    - **Fatal bleeding:** 0.2% [US]
    - **Diplopia/oculomotor disturbances:** 0.2% [US]
- **Other identified effects:** Iron deficiency, retinopathy or retinal disorder. [US]

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** 
    - **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, clarithromycin, ritonavir):** Increase vorapaxar exposure; avoid concomitant use. [US]
    - **Strong CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., rifampin, carbamazepine, phenytoin, St. John's Wort):** Decrease vorapaxar exposure; avoid concomitant use. [US]
    - **P-glycoprotein (P-gp):** Vorapaxar is a weak inhibitor of intestinal P-gp; however, no dosage adjustment for P-gp substrates like digoxin is required. [US]
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **Anticoagulants/Antiplatelets:** Increased risk of bleeding. Avoid concomitant use with warfarin. [US]

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: No lettered category provided]. Discontinue vorapaxar when pregnancy is detected. Animal data showed no embryo-fetal toxicities at 56 times human exposure, but an increase in malformations was seen in rabbits at 89 times human exposure. Potential for serious maternal bleeding/hemorrhage exists. [US]
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. While human data are absent, the drug was secreted in rat milk. Due to the risk of serious adverse reactions (bleeding) in the infant, breastfeeding should be avoided. [US]
- **Fertility:** No effect on male or female fertility was observed in animal studies at exposures up to 67 times the recommended human dose. [US]

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients <18 years of age have not been established. [US]
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed between elderly (≥65 years) and younger patients, but older patients generally have a higher inherent bleeding risk. [US]
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for patients with renal impairment. [US]
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild/Moderate:** No dose adjustment required. [US]
    - **Severe:** Not recommended due to increased inherent risk of bleeding. [US]

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Reversible antagonist of the PAR-1 receptor on platelets. PAR-1 is the primary receptor for thrombin. By inhibiting this pathway, vorapaxar prevents thrombin-induced platelet aggregation. [US]
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Achieves ≥80% inhibition of TRAP-induced platelet aggregation within one week. Inhibition of ~50% remains 4 weeks after discontinuation. Does not affect ADP-induced aggregation or coagulation parameters (PT/aPTT). [US]
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is ~100%. Peak plasma concentrations occur at ~1 hour. [US]
    - **Distribution:** Vd is ~424 L; >99% protein bound (primarily to albumin). [US]
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised via CYP3A4 and CYP2J2. The major active metabolite is M20. [US]
    - **Elimination:** Primarily via feces (58%) and urine (25%) as metabolites. Effective half-life is 3–4 days; terminal elimination half-life is ~8 days. [US]

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **TRA 2°P TIMI 50:** A multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (n=26,449). In patients with a history of MI or PAD (without stroke/TIA), vorapaxar significantly reduced the primary composite endpoint of CV death, MI, stroke, and UCR compared to placebo (10.1% vs 11.8%; HR 0.83; p<0.001). [US]

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C. [US]
- **Handling:** Store in the original package. For bottles, keep the bottle tightly closed and do not discard the desiccant (protects from moisture). [US]
