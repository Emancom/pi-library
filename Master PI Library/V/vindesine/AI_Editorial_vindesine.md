# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine product information, synthesised for use by healthcare professionals. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local regulatory approved PI for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (generic).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - [US]: Sterile injection solution, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials.
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn metabolic defects [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
Levocarnitine must be administered intravenously.

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often utilised in patients presenting with severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Administered every 3 to 4 hours; intervals should not be less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg/day or as clinically required. Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if concentrations are supra-normal [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- No known contraindications are specified in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Serious Hypersensitivity Reactions
- **Warning:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Symptoms may occur within minutes of administration [US].
- **Management:** Discontinue immediately and initiate appropriate medical treatment. Re-administration should only occur after a careful risk-benefit analysis and under close monitoring [US].

### Seizure Risk
- Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].

### General Precautions
- **Oral Formulation in Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been established in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted renally [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- **ESRD:** Monitor trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels (normal range 40–50 µmol/L) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trials Experience (ESRD Patients)
In placebo-controlled trials, the following adverse events occurred at a frequency of ≥5% regardless of causality:

| Body System | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain. |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, arrhythmia, palpitations. |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, anorexia, constipation, dyspepsia. |
| **Endocrine** | Parathyroid disorder. |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight changes. |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, depression, insomnia, paresthesia. |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis. |

### Other Adverse Effects
- **Common:** Transient nausea and vomiting.
- **Less Frequent:** Body odor, gastritis.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Anaphylaxis and severe respiratory distress (laryngeal edema/bronchospasm) [US].
- Seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Warfarin
- **Interaction:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin [US].
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not explicitly lettered, but text-based]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders are applicable to paediatric patients. Specific age-related secondary deficiency in newborns is defined by plasma concentrations below age-related normals [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosing adjustments are required for ESRD patients post-dialysis (see Section 3). High-dose oral therapy is cautioned against due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Clearance:** Mean total body clearance is 4.00 L/h [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg post-dialysis showed that trough concentrations reached 54–180 µmol/L (normal: 40–50 µmol/L) within one week. While levocarnitine effectively increases carnitine concentrations in this population, effects on clinical outcomes have not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C.
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8 mg/mL [US].
