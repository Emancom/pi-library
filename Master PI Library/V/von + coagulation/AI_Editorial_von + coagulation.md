## 4. Dosage and Administration

General principles
- Dose should be individualized based on the patient’s weight, clinical situation, severity of disease, desired hemostatic target (VWF:RCo and/or FVIII levels), and, where available, pharmacokinetic parameters (e.g., in vivo recovery [IVR], half‑life).
- Product activity is expressed as VWF:RCo (ristocetin cofactor activity) and FVIII activity in International Units (IU). The activity ratio in the product is approximately 1:1 (VWF:RCo : FVIII).
- Empirical recovery:
  - VWF:RCo: 1 IU VWF:RCo per kg body weight raises plasma VWF activity by approximately 2 IU/dL (2% of normal).
  - FVIII: 1 IU FVIII per kg body weight raises plasma FVIII activity by approximately 2 IU/dL when measured by one‑stage clotting assay.
- A general dosing formula (where empirical recovery ~2 IU/dL per IU/kg) may be used:
  - Required IU = body weight (kg) × desired rise (IU/dL) × 0.5 (IU/kg per IU/dL)
- Recommended intravenous infusion (injection) rate: 2–4 mL/minute (monitor pulse during infusion).

Dosage strengths available per single‑dose vial (for calculation purposes)
- 500 IU VWF:RCo and 500 IU FVIII activities in 5 mL
- 1000 IU VWF:RCo and 1000 IU FVIII activities in 10 mL

Dosing in von Willebrand disease (VWD)

On‑demand treatment and control of bleeding episodes (general)
- Use recommended loading and maintenance doses and adjust based on severity, location of bleeding, clinical response, and laboratory monitoring.
- Monitor VWF:RCo and FVIII activities; adjust dose/frequency to maintain target troughs.

Dosing guidance (by severity and age)
- Patients ≥ 6 years
  - Minor bleeding: Loading 20–40 IU VWF:RCo/kg; Maintenance 20–30 IU/kg every 12–24 hours; target troughs > 30% (VWF:RCo and FVIII)
  - Major bleeding: Loading 40–60 IU VWF:RCo/kg; Maintenance 20–40 IU/kg every 12–24 hours; target troughs > 50%
- Patients < 6 years
  - Minor bleeding: Loading 30–50 IU/kg; Maintenance 30–40 IU/kg every 12–24 hours; target troughs > 30%
  - Major bleeding: Loading 50–80 IU/kg; Maintenance 30–50 IU/kg every 12–24 hours; target troughs > 50%
- Duration: Varies by bleed — minor up to ~3 days; major up to ~5–7 days or longer as clinically indicated. Repeat doses guided by clinical and laboratory monitoring. Patients with VWD type 3 or GI bleeding may require higher doses.

Perioperative management (VWD)
- Minor surgery (including tooth extraction):
  - Loading: 30–60 IU VWF:RCo/kg within 3 hours pre‑op; aim for VWF:RCo peak ~50%
  - Maintenance: 15–30 IU/kg (or half the loading dose) every 12–24 hours to maintain troughs > 30%; continue until wound healing (up to ~3 days)
- Major surgery:
  - Loading: 40–60 IU VWF:RCo/kg within 3 hours pre‑op; aim for VWF:RCo peak ~100%
  - Maintenance: 20–40 IU/kg (or half the loading dose) every 12–24 hours to maintain troughs > 50%; at least two doses in the first 24 hours; continue until wound healing (up to ~6 days or more)
- To reduce perioperative thrombosis risk, avoid sustained FVIII activity > 250%.

Example individualized dosing using IVR (perioperative)
- Calculate incremental in vivo recovery (IVR) measured as IU/dL rise per IU/kg:
  - Measure baseline plasma VWF:RCo
  - Infuse 60 IU VWF:RCo/kg IV
  - Measure plasma VWF:RCo 30 minutes after infusion
  - IVR = (post‑infusion VWF:RCo − baseline VWF:RCo) / 60 IU/kg
- Use IVR to calculate the loading dose: Dose (IU) = (target peak − baseline) / IVR × body weight. If measured IVR > 2.5, use 2.5 for calculations to avoid underdosing. If IVR not available, assume IVR = 2.0 IU/dL per IU/kg.

Routine prophylaxis (VWD)
- Patients ≥ 6 years: 20–40 IU/kg two or three times weekly
- Patients < 6 years: 30–50 IU/kg two or three times weekly
- Individualize dose and frequency according to bleeding phenotype and pharmacokinetics.

Dosing in Hemophilia A (patients ≥ 12 years)
- On‑demand treatment of bleeding episodes (general guidance)
  - Minor bleeds: 30–40 IU/kg every 12–24 hours for at least 1 day or until bleeding resolves
  - Moderate bleeds: 30–40 IU/kg every 12–24 hours for 3–4 days or more
  - Major bleeds: 35–50 IU/kg every 12–24 hours for 3–4 days or more
  - Life‑threatening bleeds: 35–50 IU/kg every 8–24 hours until threat resolved
- Routine prophylaxis (adolescents and adults): 20–40 IU/kg every 2–3 days; individualize by response and monitoring.
- Monitor FVIII activity by validated assay (e.g., one‑stage clotting assay) and adjust dosing to achieve and maintain therapeutic FVIII levels.

Preparation and administration (reconstitution and IV administration)
- Reconstitute the lyophilized powder immediately prior to injection using the provided transfer device and diluent (product contains no preservative); use solution within 4 hours after reconstitution.
- Warm vials to room temperature before reconstitution; if using a water bath, avoid direct contact with stoppers and do not exceed 37°C.
- Attach transfer device to diluent vial first, then to powder vial to allow vacuum transfer; swirl to dissolve — do not shake (to minimize foaming). The final solution should be clear or slightly opalescent and colorless to slightly yellow. Do not use if particulate matter or aggregates are present.
- Withdraw reconstituted product into syringe and administer by slow IV injection or infusion at recommended rate (2–4 mL/min). Monitor pulse before and during injection; reduce or interrupt administration if marked tachycardia occurs.
- Do not mix with other medicinal products in the same infusion set.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity and anaphylaxis:** Severe allergic reactions, including anaphylaxis, angioedema and other systemic hypersensitivity symptoms, have been reported. Monitor patients during infusion; if an anaphylactic reaction occurs, discontinue administration and initiate appropriate emergency measures. Evaluate patients who experience anaphylaxis for possible development of inhibitors.
- **Thromboembolic events:** Excessive or sustained elevations of FVIII activity may increase risk of thromboembolism. Monitor plasma VWF:RCo and FVIII activities and avoid maintaining excessively high FVIII levels; specifically, avoid sustained FVIII activity > 250% in the perioperative setting.
- **Neutralizing antibodies (inhibitors):**
  - VWD: Antibodies to VWF and FVIII have been reported. If expected increases in VWF:RCo or FVIII are not achieved or bleeding is uncontrolled after dosing, evaluate for inhibitors using appropriate laboratory tests. In patients with anti‑VWF antibodies, VWF replacement may be ineffective and may be associated with severe adverse reactions; consider alternative therapies.
  - Hemophilia A: Monitor for development of FVIII inhibitors; perform a Bethesda assay when expected FVIII levels are not achieved or bleeding is unresponsive.
- **Transmissible infectious agents:** Product is derived from human plasma and carries a risk of transmitting infectious agents (e.g., viruses; theoretical risk of variant Creutzfeldt‑Jakob disease). Risk reduction steps (donor screening, testing, viral inactivation/removal during manufacture) are employed, but residual risk may remain. Record batch number at each administration and consider vaccination against hepatitis A and B in patients receiving repeated treatment.
- **Monitoring and laboratory tests:** Routinely monitor plasma VWF:RCo and FVIII activities to guide dosing and to detect excessive levels or inadequate response. Monitor for development of inhibitors when clinical or laboratory response is suboptimal.
- **Other specific clinical precautions:** Patients who develop anaphylactic or severe hypersensitivity reactions should be evaluated for inhibitors. Use caution in patients with risk factors for thrombosis; individualize dosing and monitoring.

## 7. Adverse Effects

- The following adverse reactions were reported in clinical trials and postmarketing experience with this product. Where trial data provided a frequency statement, that frequency category is used; where no frequency is provided in available sources, frequency is listed as "Not specified in available sources."

- Most common adverse reactions reported in VWD clinical trials (≥ 1%): hypersensitivity reactions, urticaria, chest discomfort, dizziness.

## 11. Pharmacology

Mechanism of action
- **von + coagulation** supplies exogenous human von Willebrand factor and coagulation factor VIII. VWF mediates platelet adhesion/aggregation at sites of vascular injury and stabilizes circulating FVIII; replacement increases VWF:RCo and FVIII activities to restore hemostasis.

Pharmacodynamics
- Replacement increases plasma VWF:RCo and FVIII activity. Empirical in vivo recovery:
  - VWF:RCo: ~2 IU/dL rise per IU/kg infused.
  - FVIII: ~2 IU/dL rise per IU/kg infused (one‑stage clotting assay).
- Ratio of VWF:RCo to FVIII in product ≈ 1:1.

Pharmacokinetics
- Specific numeric pharmacokinetic parameters (e.g., half‑life, clearance, volume of distribution) are not specified in available sources. The product label references potential inter‑patient variability in in vivo recovery and half‑life; individual PK assessments (e.g., IVR) are recommended for perioperative dosing and when tailoring prophylaxis.
