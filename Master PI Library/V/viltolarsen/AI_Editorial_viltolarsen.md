# Editorial Product Information: Viltolarsen

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of viltolarsen, synthesised for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this publication, only the United States Product Information was available for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Viltolarsen (antisense oligonucleotide of the phosphorodiamidate morpholino oligomer subclass).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Viltepso** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Injection: 250 mg/5 mL (50 mg/mL) in a single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** NS Pharma, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy (DMD):** Indicated for the treatment of DMD in patients who have a confirmed mutation of the *DMD* gene that is amenable to exon 53 skipping [US].
- **Regulatory Note:** In the United States, this indication was granted under accelerated approval based on an increase in dystrophin production in skeletal muscle. Continued approval may be contingent upon verification of clinical benefit in confirmatory trials [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** 80 mg/kg of body weight administered once weekly [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Administer via intravenous infusion over 60 minutes [US].
    - Use a peripheral or central venous catheter. Flush the line with 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection after the infusion [US].
    - Do not mix or infuse with other medications concomitantly [US].
- **Preparation and Dilution:**
    - Calculate the dose based on weight.
    - If the required volume of **Viltepso** is <100 mL, it must be diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride to a total volume of 100 mL [US].
    - If the required volume is ≥100 mL, no dilution is required; transfer the volume directly to an empty infusion bag [US].
    - Vials should be inverted 2–3 times to mix; do not shake [US].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, it should be administered as soon as possible after the scheduled time [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None specified in available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Kidney Toxicity:** Although not observed in clinical trials for viltolarsen, kidney toxicity (including potentially fatal glomerulonephritis) has been associated with other antisense oligonucleotides. Animal studies of viltolarsen showed renal tubular degeneration [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:**
    - **Baseline:** Measure serum cystatin C, urine dipstick, and urine protein-to-creatinine ratio prior to treatment initiation. Consider measuring GFR using an exogenous marker [US].
    - **Ongoing:** Monitor urine dipstick monthly; monitor serum cystatin C and urine protein-to-creatinine ratio every three months [US].
    - **Creatinine Reliability:** Note that serum creatinine is not a reliable marker of renal function in patients with DMD due to reduced skeletal muscle mass [US].
- **Urine Sampling:** Collect urine samples prior to infusion or at least 48 hours after the most recent infusion to avoid interference/false positives [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):**
    - Upper respiratory tract infection (63%)
    - Injection site reactions (e.g., bruising, erythema, swelling) (25%)
    - Cough (19%)
    - Pyrexia (19%)
    - Contusion (13%)
    - Arthralgia (13%)
    - Diarrhoea (13%)
    - Vomiting (13%)
    - Abdominal pain (13%)
    - Ejection fraction decreased (13%)
    - Urticaria (13%)
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** While not frequent in the trials, potential for kidney toxicity and immunogenicity is noted as a clinical precaution [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Based on *in vitro* data, viltolarsen has a low potential for drug-drug interactions.
- **CYP Enzymes:** It does not inhibit or induce major CYP450 enzymes (1A2, 2A6, 2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 2E1, 3A4/5) [US].
- **Transporters:** Viltolarsen is not a substrate or inhibitor of major drug transporters (including OAT1, OAT3, OCT1, OCT2, BCRP, MDR1) [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** There are no human or animal data available to assess the use of viltolarsen during pregnancy [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on the presence of viltolarsen in human milk or its effects on the breastfed infant. The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the mother’s clinical need for the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** In animal studies (male mice), no adverse effects on fertility were observed at doses up to 18 times the human equivalent dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Indicated for DMD in paediatric patients (efficacy established in patients 4 to <10 years old; safety data included patients up to 18 years old) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No experience in patients aged 65 years or older; DMD is primarily a disease of children and young adults [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not studied in patients with renal impairment. As viltolarsen is primarily excreted renally, increased exposure is expected in these patients. Close monitoring is recommended for patients with known renal impairment [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not studied; however, viltolarsen is metabolically stable and hepatic metabolism is not a major elimination pathway [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Viltolarsen binds to exon 53 of the dystrophin pre-mRNA. This induces "exon skipping" during mRNA processing, allowing for the production of an internally truncated, but functional, dystrophin protein in patients with specific genetic mutations [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Clinical data showed a mean increase in dystrophin levels to 5.9% of normal (from a baseline of 0.6%) after 20–24 weeks of treatment at the 80 mg/kg dose [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is 300 mL/kg; plasma protein binding is approximately 39%–40% [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolically stable; no metabolites detected in plasma or urine [US].
    - **Elimination:** Excreted primarily unchanged in the urine. The elimination half-life is approximately 2.5 hours, with a plasma clearance of 217 mL/hr/kg [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study 1 (US/Canada):** A multicenter, dose-finding study in 16 ambulatory males (4 to <10 years). Patients received 40 mg/kg or 80 mg/kg. The 80 mg/kg group showed a statistically significant increase in dystrophin protein expression (mean change of 5.3% of normal by Western blot; p=0.01) [US].
- **Study 2 (Japan):** A multicenter, open-label study in 16 males (ambulatory and non-ambulatory, 5 to <18 years) receiving 40 mg/kg or 80 mg/kg for 24 weeks. Results were supportive of the safety and PK profile [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Do not freeze. Store in original carton to protect from light [US].
- **Shelf Life After Preparation:** 
    - Infusion should begin as soon as possible after dilution.
    - If stored at 20°C to 26°C, complete within 6 hours.
    - If refrigerated (2°C to 8°C), the solution is stable for up to 24 hours [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only.
