# Editorial Product Information: Vibegron

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States** (Food and Drug Administration).
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data into a single, coherent reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and must not be used as a substitute for the official, current Product Information approved in the specific jurisdiction of practice. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Vibegron (a selective beta-3 adrenergic agonist).
- **Brand Names:** **GEMTESA** [US].
- **ATC Code:** G04BD13.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Film-coated tablets, 75 mg. The tablets are oval, light green, and debossed with "V75" on one side [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sumitomo Pharma America, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
Vibegron is indicated for the treatment of:
- **Overactive Bladder (OAB):** Treatment of symptoms including urge urinary incontinence, urgency, and urinary frequency in adults [US].
- **OAB in Males with Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (BPH):** Treatment of urge urinary incontinence, urgency, and urinary frequency in adult males who are also receiving pharmacological therapy for BPH [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** The recommended dose is 75 mg taken once daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Tablets may be taken with or without food [US].
    - Tablets should be swallowed whole with a glass of water [US].
    - **Crushing Option:** For patients with swallowing difficulties, the 75 mg tablet may be crushed, mixed with approximately one tablespoon (15 mL) of applesauce, and consumed immediately with a glass of water [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specifically detailed in the source beyond once-daily instruction.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Vibegron is contraindicated in patients with a known history of hypersensitivity to the active ingredient or any excipients. Reported reactions include angioedema [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Urinary Retention:** Postmarketing cases have been reported. The risk is elevated in patients with bladder outlet obstruction and those concurrently taking muscarinic antagonist medications for OAB. Clinicians should monitor for signs of retention and discontinue vibegron if it develops [US].
- **Angioedema:** Allergic reactions involving the face, lips, tongue, and larynx have occurred, sometimes hours after the first or subsequent doses. Angioedema involving the upper airway can be life-threatening. If such symptoms occur, vibegron must be discontinued immediately and a patent airway secured [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are categorised by frequency based on clinical trial data (12-week OAB study and 24-week OAB/BPH study).

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Headache (4.0%), Urinary tract infection (2.5%–6.6%), Nasopharyngitis (2.8%), Diarrhea (2.2%), Nausea (2.2%), Upper respiratory tract infection (2.0%), Hypertension (9.0% in BPH population), Bronchitis (2.9% in long-term use) |
| **Uncommon (<2%)** | Dry mouth, constipation, hot flush, increased residual urine volume |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Angioedema and urinary retention have been identified as clinically significant risks [US].
- **Postmarketing Experience:** Additional reported reactions include pruritus, rash, drug eruption, eczema, and urticaria [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Digoxin:** Vibegron increases the maximal concentration ($C_{max}$) and systemic exposure (AUC) of digoxin. Serum digoxin levels should be measured prior to starting vibegron and monitored during treatment for dose titration. Monitoring should continue upon discontinuation of vibegron [US].
- **Other Interactions:** No clinically significant pharmacokinetic interactions were observed with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (ketoconazole), moderate inhibitors (diltiazem), strong inducers (rifampin), or with tolterodine, metoprolol, oral contraceptives, or warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not Categorised]. There are no available human data to evaluate for drug-associated risks of major birth defects or miscarriage. Animal studies showed no embryo-fetal developmental toxicity at exposures ~275 to 285 times the human dose. Toxicity (delayed skeletal ossification, reduced fetal weight) was only seen at maternally toxic doses (approx. 898-fold clinical exposure) [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There is no data on the presence of vibegron in human milk or its effects on the infant. In animal studies, vibegron was present in the milk of lactating rats. The developmental benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the mother's clinical need and any potential adverse effects on the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** In rat studies, decreased fecundity and fertility were only observed at doses associated with 1867-fold clinical exposure [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy have been observed between patients $\ge$ 65 years and younger adults [US].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Mild, Moderate, or Severe (eGFR 15 to <90 mL/min/1.73 $m^2$):** No dosage adjustment required [US].
    - **End-Stage Renal Disease (eGFR <15 mL/min/1.73 $m^2$):** Not recommended, as this population has not been studied [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild to Moderate (Child-Pugh A and B):** No dosage adjustment required [US].
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Not recommended, as this population has not been studied [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Vibegron is a selective human beta-3 adrenergic receptor agonist. It relaxes the detrusor smooth muscle during the bladder filling phase, thereby increasing bladder capacity [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** At doses 5.3 times the recommended dose, vibegron does not prolong the QT interval to a clinically relevant extent [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Median $T_{max}$ is 1–3 hours. Steady state is reached within 7 days [US].
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is 6304 L; plasma protein binding is approximately 50% [US].
    - **Metabolism:** CYP3A4 is the primary enzyme involved in its limited metabolism [US].
    - **Elimination:** Effective half-life is 30.8 hours. Excretion is primarily via feces (59%, 54% as unchanged) and urine (20%, 19% as unchanged) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study 3003 (OAB Adults):** A 12-week, randomised, double-blind trial ($n=1,515$). Vibegron 75 mg demonstrated statistically significant reductions from baseline in average daily micturitions (-1.8 vs -1.3 for placebo) and urge urinary incontinence (UUI) episodes (-2.0 vs -1.4 for placebo) [US].
- **Study 3005 (OAB in Males with BPH):** A 24-week trial ($n=1,105$) in men on BPH therapy. Vibegron significantly reduced daily micturitions (-2.04 vs -1.30 for placebo) and urgency episodes (-2.88 vs -1.93 for placebo) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Packaging:** Marketed in HDPE bottles of 30 or 90 tablets [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription-only (Rx Only) [US].
