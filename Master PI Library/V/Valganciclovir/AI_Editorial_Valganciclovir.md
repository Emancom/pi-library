# Editorial Product Information: Valganciclovir

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians must consult local regulatory guidelines before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Valganciclovir hydrochloride (an L-valyl ester prodrug of ganciclovir).
- **Chemical Class:** Synthetic nucleoside analogue of 2’-deoxyguanosine.
- **Brand Names:** **Valganciclovir Sandoz** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J05A B14.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Film-coated tablets:** 450 mg (as 496 mg valganciclovir hydrochloride) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sandoz Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **CMV Retinitis:** Treatment of cytomegalovirus (CMV) retinitis in adult patients with acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS) [Australian].
- **CMV Prophylaxis:** Prevention of CMV disease in adult solid organ transplantation (SOT) patients who are at risk [Australian].
- **Note on Paediatrics:** While dosing algorithms exist for paediatric SOT patients, certain brands (e.g., Sandoz) are not officially indicated for paediatric use in Australia; however, clinical data for this population is provided for prescriber information [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Administration:** Valganciclovir must be taken **with food** to maximise bioavailability. Tablets should not be broken or crushed due to potential teratogenic and carcinogenic risk.
- **Adult CMV Retinitis:**
    - **Induction:** 900 mg (two 450 mg tablets) twice daily for 21 days.
    - **Maintenance:** 900 mg once daily.
- **Adult CMV Prophylaxis (SOT):**
    - **Kidney Transplant:** 900 mg once daily, starting within 10 days of transplant, until 200 days post-transplantation.
    - **Other SOT (Non-Kidney):** 900 mg once daily, starting within 10 days of transplant, until 100 days post-transplantation.
- **Paediatric Dosing (SOT):** Recommended once-daily dosing is based on Body Surface Area (BSA) and Creatinine Clearance (CrCl) using the Schwartz formula:
    - `Dose (mg) = 7 x BSA x CrCl` (Max CrCl capped at 150 mL/min/1.73m²; max dose 900 mg).
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to **valganciclovir**, ganciclovir, or any excipients.
- **Cross-Hypersensitivity:** Potential for cross-reaction with aciclovir, valaciclovir, penciclovir, or famciclovir due to structural similarities [Australian].
- **Haematological Thresholds:** Therapy must **not** be initiated if:
    - Absolute Neutrophil Count (ANC) < 500 cells/μL.
    - Platelet count < 25,000/μL.
    - Haemoglobin < 8 g/dL.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Myelosuppression:** Severe leukopenia, neutropenia, anaemia, thrombocytopenia, and bone marrow failure have occurred. Frequent monitoring of CBC and platelets is mandatory, especially in renal impairment and neonates.
- **Teratogenicity and Carcinogenicity:** Based on animal data, ganciclovir (and thus valganciclovir) is considered a potential human teratogen and carcinogen. It may cause birth defects and cancers.
- **Infertility:** Valganciclovir is likely to cause temporary or permanent inhibition of spermatogenesis in males.
- **Renal Impairment:** Requires strict dosage adjustment based on creatinine clearance. Increased serum creatinine has been observed during treatment.
- **Acute Toxicity:** Rapid conversion to ganciclovir results in 10-fold higher bioavailability compared to oral ganciclovir; strict adherence to dosing is essential to avoid overdose.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally consistent with those observed with ganciclovir.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Diarrhoea (30–38%), Neutropenia (8–24%), Pyrexia (13–26%), Anaemia (12–22%), Oral candidiasis, Headache, Fatigue, Insomnia [Australian]. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Nausea, Vomiting, Abdominal pain, Tremors, Dizziness, Dermatitis, Dyspnoea, Increased blood creatinine, Rejection of graft [Australian]. |
| **Serious** | Pancytopenia, Aplastic anaemia, Febrile neutropenia, Convulsions, Psychotic disorders, Hallucinations, Sepsis, and Life-threatening bleeding associated with thrombocytopenia. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Imipenem-cilastatin:** Reports of convulsions; co-administration should be avoided unless benefits outweigh risks.
- **Myelosuppressive Drugs:** Potential for additive toxicity with zidovudine, didanosine, stavudine, mycophenolate mofetil, and various antineoplastic or anti-infective agents (e.g., flucytosine, amphotericin B).
- **Didanosine:** Valganciclovir significantly increases didanosine plasma concentrations (AUC increases of 38–124%); patients must be monitored for didanosine toxicity (e.g., pancreatitis).
- **Probenecid:** Reduces renal clearance of ganciclovir, increasing systemic exposure (AUC increase ~40%).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. The drug is potentially teratogenic. Use only if benefits to the mother outweigh risks to the foetus. 
- **Contraception:**
    - **Females:** Use effective contraception during and for at least 30 days post-treatment.
    - **Males:** Use condoms during and for at least 90 days post-treatment.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety not established; however, ganciclovir excretion in breast milk is likely. Breastfeeding is not recommended.
- **Fertility:** Clinical data confirms inhibition of spermatogenesis in humans; potential for permanent male infertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Higher risk of cytopenias in neonates. Extreme caution required due to carcinogenic potential. Dosing must follow the specific BSA/CrCl formula provided in Section 3.
- **Elderly Use:** Pharmacokinetics not specifically established for patients > 65 years. Dose selection should be cautious, reflecting likely reductions in renal function.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dose adjustments are mandatory [Australian]:

| CrCl (mL/min) | Induction Dose | Maintenance/Prophylaxis Dose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **≥ 60** | 900 mg twice daily | 900 mg once daily |
| **40 – 59** | 450 mg twice daily | 450 mg once daily |
| **25 – 39** | 450 mg once daily | 450 mg every 2 days |
| **10 – 24** | 450 mg every 2 days | 450 mg twice weekly |
| **< 10** | Not recommended | Not recommended |

- **Hepatic Impairment:** Safety and efficacy have not been established.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Valganciclovir is rapidly converted to ganciclovir by intestinal and hepatic esterases. Ganciclovir is phosphorylated to ganciclovir monophosphate by the viral kinase UL97, then to ganciclovir triphosphate by cellular kinases. Triphosphate inhibits viral DNA synthesis by competing with deoxyguanosine triphosphate for incorporation into viral DNA, causing chain termination.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is ~60% (with food).
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is low (1–2%). Penetrates cerebrospinal fluid.
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly hydrolysed to ganciclovir; no other significant metabolites.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal via glomerular filtration and active tubular secretion. Terminal half-life is ~4.2 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study WV15376 (CMV Retinitis):** A randomised study of 160 AIDS patients showed valganciclovir 900 mg BID was as effective as IV ganciclovir 5 mg/kg BID for induction therapy, with similar progression rates at 4 weeks.
- **PV16000 (SOT Prophylaxis):** A double-blind study of 372 heart, liver, and kidney transplant recipients demonstrated that valganciclovir 900 mg daily was non-inferior to oral ganciclovir 1000 mg TID.
- **IMPACT Study (Kidney Prophylaxis):** Extension of prophylaxis from 100 to 200 days in high-risk kidney transplant patients reduced the incidence of CMV disease from 36.8% to 16.1% over 12 months [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Tablets:** Store below 25°C.
- **Protection:** Protect from light [Australian].
- **Handling:** Tablets should not be broken or crushed. If contact with broken tablets occurs, wash skin/mucosa thoroughly with soap and water.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
