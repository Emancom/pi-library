# Editorial Product Information: Vonoprazan, Amoxicillin, and Clarithromycin (VOQUEZNA Triple Pak / VOQUEZNA Dual Pak)

## Editorial Note
This editorial product information document has been synthesised using official Product Information (PI) documents from the **US (FDA)**. Its purpose is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. This document is an editorial synthesis and should not be considered a substitute for the official PI for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
-   **Active Ingredients (INN/generic name) and Chemical Class:**
    -   **Vonoprazan fumarate**: A potassium-competitive acid blocker (PCAB).
    -   **Amoxicillin**: A penicillin-class antibacterial.
    -   **Clarithromycin**: A macrolide antimicrobial.
-   **Brand Name(s) by Jurisdiction:**
    -   **VOQUEZNA Triple Pak** [US]
    -   **VOQUEZNA Dual Pak** [US]
-   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.

        -   **Clarithromycin tablets**: 500 mg
    -   **VOQUEZNA Dual Pak** [US]:
        -   **Vonoprazan tablets**: 20 mg

## 2. Approved Indications
Both **VOQUEZNA Triple Pak** and **VOQUEZNA Dual Pak** are indicated for:
-   Treatment of *Helicobacter pylori* (*H. pylori*) infection in adults [US].
To reduce the development of drug-resistant bacteria and maintain effectiveness, these products should only be used to treat or prevent infections proven or strongly suspected to be caused by susceptible bacteria [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
-   **General Dosing (Adult):**
    -   **VOQUEZNA Triple Pak:**
        -   **Vonoprazan** 20 mg, **amoxicillin** 1,000 mg, and **clarithromycin** 500 mg, each taken twice daily (morning and evening, 12 hours apart) with or without food, for 14 days [US].
    -   **VOQUEZNA Dual Pak:**
        -   **Vonoprazan** 20 mg twice daily (morning and evening), and **amoxicillin** 1,000 mg three times a day (morning, mid-day, and evening) with or without food, for 14 days [US].
-   **Administration Instructions:** Administer with or without food [US].
-   **Missed Dose Instructions:**
    -   If a dose is missed, administer as soon as possible within 4 hours. If more than 4 hours have passed, skip the missed dose and resume the normal dosing schedule. Patients should complete the entire course of therapy [US].

### Dosing in Special Populations
-   **Renal Impairment:**
    -   Estimated GFR ≥ 30 mL/minute:
        -   **VOQUEZNA Triple Pak**: **Vonoprazan** 20 mg twice daily, **amoxicillin** 1,000 mg twice daily, **clarithromycin** 500 mg twice daily [US].
        -   **VOQUEZNA Dual Pak**: **Vonoprazan** 20 mg twice daily, **amoxicillin** 1,000 mg three times daily [US].
    -   Estimated GFR < 30 mL/minute: Use is not recommended for either product [US].
-   **Hepatic Impairment:**
    -   Child-Pugh Class A:
        -   **VOQUEZNA Triple Pak**: **Vonoprazan** 20 mg twice daily, **amoxicillin** 1,000 mg twice daily, **clarithromycin** 500 mg twice daily [US].
        -   **VOQUEZNA Dual Pak**: **Vonoprazan** 20 mg twice daily, **amoxicillin** 1,000 mg three times daily [US].
    -   Child-Pugh Class B or C: Use is not recommended for either product [US].

## 4. Contraindications
-   Known hypersensitivity to **vonoprazan**, **amoxicillin** (or other beta-lactam antibacterials), **clarithromycin** (or other macrolide antimicrobials), or any component of **VOQUEZNA Triple Pak** or **VOQUEZNA Dual Pak** [US].
-   Concomitant use with rilpivirine-containing products [US].
-   **VOQUEZNA Triple Pak** is additionally contraindicated due to its **clarithromycin** component with concomitant use of:
    -   **Pimozide** [US].
    -   **Lomitapide**, **lovastatin**, and **simvastatin** [US].
    -   **Ergot alkaloids** (e.g., ergotamine or dihydroergotamine) [US].
    -   **Colchicine** in patients with renal or hepatic impairment [US].
    -   History of cholestatic jaundice/hepatic dysfunction with prior clarithromycin use [US].
    -   **Lurasidone** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
-   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious and occasionally fatal reactions (e.g., anaphylaxis, anaphylactic shock, rash, erythema multiforme, Henoch-Schonlein purpura) have been reported. Discontinue immediately and institute appropriate treatment if hypersensitivity reactions occur. Prior inquiry about hypersensitivity to penicillins, cephalosporins, macrolides, or other allergens is recommended [US].
-   **Acute Tubulointerstitial Nephritis:** Has been reported with **vonoprazan**. Discontinue and evaluate if suspected [US].
-   **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCAR):** Including Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), drug reaction with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms (DRESS), and acute generalized exanthematous pustulosis (AGEP) have been reported with components. Discontinue at first signs or symptoms of SCAR or other hypersensitivity [US].
-   **Drug-Induced Enterocolitis Syndrome (DIES):** Reported with **amoxicillin**, particularly in pediatric patients. Characterized by protracted vomiting 1 to 4 hours post-ingestion, without skin or respiratory symptoms. May be associated with pallor, lethargy, hypotension, shock, and diarrhea. Discontinue and institute appropriate therapy if DIES occurs [US].
-   **Clostridioides difficile-Associated Diarrhea (CDAD):** Reported with acid-suppressing therapies and nearly all antibacterial agents, including **amoxicillin** and **clarithromycin**. Evaluate if diarrhea occurs; discontinue if confirmed and institute appropriate management [US].
-   **Rash in Patients with Mononucleosis:** Avoid use in patients with mononucleosis due to a high incidence of erythematous skin rash with **amoxicillin** [US].
-   **Interactions with Diagnostic Investigations for Neuroendocrine Tumors:** **Vonoprazan** reduces gastric acidity, which can increase serum chromogranin A (CgA) levels, leading to false-positive results. Assess CgA levels at least 4 weeks after treatment discontinuation and repeat if initial levels are high [US].
-   **Development of Drug-Resistant Bacteria:** Prescribing these products in the absence of a proven or strongly suspected bacterial infection increases the risk of drug-resistant bacteria [US].

### Warnings and Precautions Due to Clarithromycin Component (**VOQUEZNA Triple Pak** only)
-   **QT Prolongation:** **Clarithromycin** is associated with QT interval prolongation and infrequent arrhythmias, including *torsades de pointes*. Avoid in patients with known QT prolongation, those receiving QT-prolonging drugs (e.g., **pimozide**), or with ongoing proarrhythmic conditions (uncorrected hypokalemia/hypomagnesemia, significant bradycardia, Class IA/III antiarrhythmics). Elderly patients may be more susceptible [US].
-   **Hepatotoxicity:** Hepatic dysfunction (e.g., increased liver enzymes, hepatocellular and/or cholestatic hepatitis) has been reported with **clarithromycin**, which can be severe and is usually reversible, though fatal outcomes have occurred. Discontinue immediately if signs and symptoms of hepatitis occur [US].
-   **Serious Adverse Reactions Due to Concomitant Use with Other Drugs:** Serious adverse reactions (e.g., colchicine toxicity, rhabdomyolysis with statins, hypoglycemia/cardiac arrhythmias with disopyramide, hypotension/acute kidney injury with calcium channel blockers) can occur due to **clarithromycin** interactions with CYP3A4 substrates [US].
-   **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Based on animal and human observational studies with **clarithromycin**, **VOQUEZNA Triple Pak** is not recommended in pregnant women unless no alternative therapy is appropriate. Advise pregnant women of potential fetal risk [US].
-   **Exacerbation of Myasthenia Gravis:** Exacerbation or new onset of symptoms of myasthenic syndrome has been reported with **clarithromycin** therapy. Monitor patients for symptoms [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
Interactions apply to the individual components within **VOQUEZNA Triple Pak** and **VOQUEZNA Dual Pak**. Drug interaction studies with the co-packaged products have not been conducted [US].

### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
-   **Strong or Moderate CYP3A Inducers** (e.g., rifampicin, efavirenz):
    -   **Clinical Effect**: May decrease exposure of **vonoprazan** and **clarithromycin**, potentially reducing effectiveness [US].
    -   **Management**: Avoid concomitant use [US].
-   **Probenecid**:
    -   **Clinical Effect**: May increase **amoxicillin** exposure by blocking its renal tubular secretion, increasing risk of adverse reactions [US].
    -   **Management**: Closely monitor for signs or symptoms of increased or prolonged adverse reactions associated with **amoxicillin** [US].
-   **Allopurinol**:
    -   **Clinical Effect**: Increased incidence of rash reported with concomitant **allopurinol** and **amoxicillin** [US].
    -   **Management**: Discontinue **allopurinol** at the first appearance of skin rash [US].
-   **Omeprazole** (with **VOQUEZNA Triple Pak**):
    -   **Clinical Effect**: Increased **clarithromycin** concentrations in gastric tissue and mucus [US].
    -   **Management**: Avoid concomitant use with **omeprazole** [US].
-   **Itraconazole** (with **VOQUEZNA Triple Pak**):
    -   **Clinical Effect**: Both **clarithromycin** and **itraconazole** are CYP3A substrates and inhibitors. Concomitant use may increase **clarithromycin** exposure, increasing risk of adverse reactions related to **itraconazole** and **clarithromycin** [US].
    -   **Management**: Monitor closely for signs or symptoms of increased or prolonged adverse reactions [US].
-   **Antivirals** (with **VOQUEZNA Triple Pak**):
    -   **Clinical Effect**: **Clarithromycin** is a CYP3A substrate and inhibitor. Bi-directional interactions can alter exposure of **clarithromycin** and/or CYP3A substrates, affecting safety or effectiveness [US].
        -   **Rilpivirine-containing products**: Contraindicated [US].
        -   **Atazanavir, Nelfinavir**: Avoid concomitant use [US].
        -   **Saquinavir (with or without ritonavir)**: Use with caution; consult **saquinavir** PI [US].
        -   **Ritonavir (if decreased renal function)**: Not recommended [US].
        -   **Etravirine**: Avoid concomitant use [US].
        -   **Zidovudine**: Administer **VOQUEZNA Triple Pak** and **zidovudine** at least two hours apart [US].
-   **Drugs Dependent on Gastric pH for Absorption** (e.g., certain antiretrovirals, iron salts, erlotinib, dasatinib, nilotinib, mycophenolate mofetil, ketoconazole/itraconazole):
    -   **Clinical Effect**: **Vonoprazan** reduces intragastric acidity, which may alter absorption of these drugs, reducing their effectiveness or increasing their toxicity [US].
    -   **Management**: Consult prescribing information for specific drugs. Contraindicated with rilpivirine-containing products [US]. Avoid atazanavir and nelfinavir with **VOQUEZNA Triple Pak** [US].
-   **Certain CYP3A Substrates where minimal concentration changes may lead to serious toxicities** (with **VOQUEZNA Triple Pak**):
    -   **Immunosuppressants** (e.g., **tacrolimus, cyclosporine**):
        -   **Clinical Effect**: **Clarithromycin** and **vonoprazan** (weak CYP3A inhibitor) may increase exposure of CYP3A4 substrates, increasing risk of adverse reactions [US].
        -   **Management**: Frequent monitoring of concentrations and/or adverse reactions. Dosage reduction may be needed [US].
    -   **Antiarrhythmics** (e.g., **disopyramide, amiodarone, dofetilide, procainamide, sotalol, quinidine**):
        -   **Clinical Effect**: **Clarithromycin** may increase exposure of CYP3A-substrate antiarrhythmics, increasing risk of cardiac arrhythmias (e.g., QT prolongation, *torsades de pointes*) [US].
        -   **Management**: Avoid concomitant use. If unavoidable, monitor for QTc prolongation and changes in blood glucose levels (disopyramide) [US].
    -   **Colchicine**:
        -   **Clinical Effect**: **Clarithromycin** increases **colchicine** exposure (CYP3A and P-gp substrate), increasing risk of toxicity [US].
        -   **Management**: Contraindicated in patients with renal or hepatic impairment. In patients with normal renal and hepatic function, reduce **colchicine** dose and monitor for toxicity [US].
    -   **Antipsychotics** (e.g., **pimozide, lurasidone, quetiapine**):
        -   **Clinical Effect**: **Clarithromycin** may increase exposure of CYP3A-substrate antipsychotics, increasing risk of somnolence, orthostatic hypotension, altered consciousness, neuroleptic malignant syndrome, and cardiac arrhythmias (QT prolongation, *torsades de pointes*) [US].
        -   **Management**: Concomitant use with **pimozide** or **lurasidone** is contraindicated. For **quetiapine**, refer to its prescribing information for dosage reduction [US].
    -   **Tolterodine** (in CYP2D6 deficient patients):
        -   **Clinical Effect**: **Clarithromycin** may increase **tolterodine** exposure (metabolized via CYP3A in CYP2D6 poor metabolizers) [US].
        -   **Management**: **Tolterodine** 1 mg twice daily is recommended [US].
    -   **Lipid-lowering Agents** (statins):
        -   **Clinical Effect**: **Clarithromycin** may increase statin plasma concentrations, increasing risk of myopathy, including rhabdomyolysis [US].
        -   **Management**: **Lomitapide**, **lovastatin**, and **simvastatin** are contraindicated. Use with caution for **atorvastatin** or **pravastatin**; limit **atorvastatin** to 20 mg/day and **pravastatin** to 40 mg/day if unavoidable. Consider statins not dependent on CYP3A (e.g., **fluvastatin**) [US].
    -   **Phosphodiesterase Inhibitors** (e.g., **sildenafil, tadalafil, vardenafil**):
        -   **Clinical Effect**: **Clarithromycin** may increase exposure of CYP3A-substrate PDE5 inhibitors, increasing risk of adverse reactions [US].
        -   **Management**: Avoid concomitant use. If unavoidable, consult individual PDE5 inhibitor PI for dose recommendations [US].
-   **P-glycoprotein (P-gp) Substrates** (e.g., **digoxin**):
    -   **Clinical Effect**: **Clarithromycin** is a P-gp inhibitor and may increase exposure of P-gp substrates, increasing risk of adverse reactions, including potentially fatal arrhythmias with **digoxin** [US].
    -   **Management**: Carefully monitor serum concentrations and adjust dose of **digoxin** [US].
-   **Drugs Metabolized by CYP450 Isoforms Other than CYP3A** (e.g., **hexobarbital, phenytoin, valproate, theophylline**):
    -   **Clinical Effect**: **Clarithromycin** may increase exposure of these drugs by inhibiting their metabolism [US].
    -   **Management**: Use with caution. Closely monitor **theophylline** concentrations [US].
-   **CYP2C19 Substrates** (e.g., **clopidogrel, citalopram, cilostazol**):
    -   **Clinical Effect**: **Vonoprazan** is a CYP2C19 inhibitor. It may reduce plasma concentrations of **clopidogrel**'s active metabolite, leading to reduced platelet inhibition. It may also increase exposure of **citalopram** and **cilostazol** [US].
    -   **Management**: For **clopidogrel**, carefully monitor efficacy and consider alternative anti-platelet therapy. For **citalopram** and **cilostazol**, monitor for adverse reactions and consult their PI for dosage adjustments [US].
-   **Oral Anticoagulants** (e.g., **warfarin**):
    -   **Clinical Effect**: Abnormal prolongation of prothrombin time (increased INR) has been reported with concomitant **amoxicillin** and oral anticoagulants [US].
    -   **Management**: Monitor INR and prothrombin times frequently; adjust anticoagulant dose to maintain desired level of anticoagulation [US].

### Diagnostic Test Interactions
-   **Chromogranin A (CgA) Test for Neuroendocrine Tumors:**
    -   **Clinical Effect**: **Vonoprazan** reduces intragastric acidity, increasing CgA levels and potentially causing false-positive results [US].
    -   **Management**: Assess CgA levels at least 4 weeks after treatment. If initial levels are high, repeat test using the same commercial laboratory [US].
-   **Secretin Stimulation Test:**
    -   **Clinical Effect**: May cause a hyper-response in gastrin secretion, falsely suggesting gastrinoma [US].
    -   **Management**: Perform test at least 4 weeks after stopping treatment to allow gastrin levels to normalize [US].
-   **Glucose Tests:**
    -   **Clinical Effect**: High urine concentrations of **amoxicillin** can cause false-positive results with Benedict's copper reduction reaction tests for glucose [US].
    -   **Management**: Use enzymatic glucose oxidase reactions when testing for urine glucose [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

### Pregnancy
-   **Pregnancy Exposure Registry:** A registry monitors pregnancy outcomes in women exposed to **VOQUEZNA Triple Pak** or **VOQUEZNA Dual Pak**. Healthcare providers are encouraged to register patients [US].
-   **Risk Summary for VOQUEZNA Triple Pak:** Not recommended in pregnant women due to the **clarithromycin** component, unless no alternative therapy is appropriate. Based on animal and human observational studies, **clarithromycin** has shown adverse effects on pregnancy outcomes, including an increased risk of miscarriage and in some studies, an increased incidence of fetal malformations. If used during pregnancy, advise of potential fetal risk [US].
-   **Risk Summary for VOQUEZNA Dual Pak:** No adequate and well-controlled studies to evaluate drug-associated risks of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse maternal or fetal outcomes [US].
-   **Individual Components:**
    -   **Vonoprazan:** Insufficient human data. Animal studies in rats showed ventricular septal defects, tail abnormalities, and small anal openings at high doses. In rabbits, no embryo-fetal mortality or toxicity observed. Liver discoloration in offspring occurred from exposure during lactation in rats [US].
    -   **Amoxicillin:** Published epidemiologic and pharmacovigilance data have not established drug-associated risks of major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse maternal or fetal outcomes. Animal studies showed no harm to the fetus [US].
    -   **Clarithromycin:** Observational studies in pregnant women demonstrate an increased risk of miscarriage; some studies report increased incidence of fetal malformations (atrioventricular septal defects, genital malformations, orofacial clefts), while others found no difference. Animal reproduction studies with oral **clarithromycin** in mice and rats, and intravenous in rabbits, showed malformations (e.g., cardiovascular anomalies, cleft palate) at clinically relevant doses. Maternal toxicity observed at higher doses [US].

### Breastfeeding
-   **Risk Summary:** Consider the developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding, the mother's clinical need for therapy, and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child or from the underlying maternal condition [US].
-   **Individual Components:**
    -   **Vonoprazan:** Present in human breast milk in amounts less than 0.03% of the administered maternal dose. Estimated mean daily infant dose is very low (0.0015 mg/kg/day for 20 mg BID maternal dose, 0.0004 mg/kg/day for 20 mg once daily maternal dose), representing <0.3% of maternal weight-adjusted dose. No data on effects on breastfed child or milk production. Liver injury observed in offspring from lactating rats [US].
    -   **Clarithromycin:** Present in human milk at less than 2% of the maternal weight-adjusted dose. Adverse effects on breastfed children (rash, diarrhea, loss of appetite, somnolence) have been reported, comparable to **amoxicillin**. No data on effects on milk production [US].
    -   **Amoxicillin:** Present in human milk. No data on effects on milk production [US].
-   **Clinical Considerations:** Monitor infants exposed to **clarithromycin** or **amoxicillin** through breast milk for diarrhea, vomiting, or rash [US].

### Fertility
-   **Males:** **Clarithromycin** may impair fertility in males of reproductive potential, based on animal study findings. Testicular atrophy occurred in rats, dogs, and monkeys at high doses [US].
-   **Vonoprazan:** No effect on fertility or reproductive performance in rats at doses up to 133 times the MRHD. Elongation of the estrous cycle was observed [US].
-   **Amoxicillin:** No impairment of fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
-   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established in pediatric patients. Drug-induced enterocolitis syndrome (DIES) has been reported with **amoxicillin** in pediatric patients ≤18 years of age [US].
-   **Elderly Use:**
    -   **Vonoprazan:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed between elderly patients (≥65 years) and younger adults in clinical studies [US].
    -   **Amoxicillin and Clarithromycin:** These drugs are substantially excreted by the kidney, and the risk of toxic reactions may be greater in patients with impaired renal function. Monitoring renal function may be useful. Elderly patients may be more susceptible to *torsades de pointes* arrhythmias with **clarithromycin** and increased risk of acute kidney injury with concomitant CYP3A4-metabolized calcium channel blockers. Reports of **colchicine** toxicity with **clarithromycin** have occurred in elderly patients, some with renal insufficiency [US].
-   **Renal Impairment:** Refer to Section 3. Dosage and Administration for specific dosing recommendations. **Vonoprazan** systemic exposure is increased in patients with mild (1.7-fold), moderate (1.3-fold), or severe (2.4-fold) renal impairment compared to normal renal function. It is not recommended for eGFR < 30 mL/min [US].
-   **Hepatic Impairment:** Refer to Section 3. Dosage and Administration for specific dosing recommendations. **Vonoprazan** systemic exposure is increased in patients with mild (1.2-fold), moderate (2.4-fold), or severe (2.6-fold) hepatic impairment compared to normal hepatic function. It is not recommended for Child-Pugh Class B or C [US].

## 10. Pharmacology

### Mechanism of Action
-   **Vonoprazan:** A potassium-competitive acid blocker (PCAB). It suppresses basal and stimulated gastric acid secretion by inhibiting the H+, K+-ATPase enzyme system (proton pump) in gastric parietal cells in a potassium-competitive, noncovalent, and reversible manner. It does not require acid activation and may selectively concentrate in parietal cells in both resting and stimulated states [US].
-   **Amoxicillin:** A penicillin-class antibacterial that exerts bactericidal action by inhibiting bacterial cell wall biosynthesis during active multiplication [US].
-   **Clarithromycin:** A macrolide antimicrobial that exerts its antibacterial activity by binding to the 50S ribosomal subunit of susceptible bacteria, leading to inhibition of protein synthesis [US].
-   **Combination:** Acid suppression enhances the replication of *H. pylori* and the stability and effectiveness of the antimicrobials [US].

### Pharmacodynamics
-   **Vonoprazan Antisecretory Activity:** A single 20 mg dose elevates intragastric pH within 2-3 hours, maintaining it for over 24 hours. The inhibitory effect increases with repeated daily dosing, reaching steady state by Day 4 (mean 24-hour intragastric pH of 6.0 with 20 mg once daily). The antisecretory effect decreases within 24-48 hours after discontinuation [US].
-   **Cardiac Electrophysiology:** At 6 times the maximum recommended dose, **vonoprazan** does not prolong the QT interval clinically [US].
-   **Serum Gastrin Effects:** In clinical trials, **vonoprazan** increased baseline serum gastrin concentrations, which remained elevated with recommended daily dosing. Increased gastrin can cause enterochromaffin-like cell hyperplasia and increase serum CgA levels, potentially leading to false-positive results in diagnostic investigations for neuroendocrine tumors [US].
-   **Antimicrobial Activity:** **Clarithromycin** and **amoxicillin** are active *in vitro* against most *H. pylori* isolates. Resistance to **amoxicillin** is primarily mediated by beta-lactamases. **Clarithromycin** resistance involves modification of 23S rRNA or drug efflux pumps. If *H. pylori* is not eradicated, resistance to **clarithromycin** may develop [US].
-   **Effects on Gastrointestinal Microbial Ecology:** Decreased gastric acidity may increase gastric counts of bacteria normally present in the GI tract, potentially increasing risk of infections (e.g., *Salmonella*, *Campylobacter*, and *C. difficile*) [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
-   **Vonoprazan:**
    -   **Absorption:** Exhibits time-independent pharmacokinetics, reaching steady state by Day 3-4. A high-fat meal results in a 5% increase in Cmax, 15% increase in AUC, and a 2-hour delay in median Tmax, which are not considered clinically significant [US].
    -   **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding ranges from 85-88% [US].
    -   **Metabolism:** Metabolized to inactive metabolites by multiple pathways, primarily by CYP3A4/5, CYP2C19, CYP2D6, and CYP2B6, along with sulfo- and glucuronosyl-transferases. No clinically meaningful differences based on CYP2C19 or CYP2D6 metabolizer status. *In vitro*, **vonoprazan** directly and time-dependently inhibits CYP2B6, CYP2C19, and CYP3A4/5. It inhibits MATE1 and OCT1 at concentrations higher than clinically relevant [US].
    -   **Elimination:** Approximately 67% of a radiolabeled dose is recovered in urine (8% as unchanged **vonoprazan**) and 31% in feces (1.4% as unchanged **vonoprazan**) [US].
    -   **PK Parameters (20 mg dose):** Tmax (median): 2.5-3.0 h (single to steady state). Cmax: 25.2-37.8 ng/mL. AUC0-12h: 154.8-272.5 ng\*hr/mL. t1/2: 6.8-7.1 h [US].
-   **Amoxicillin:**
    -   **Absorption:** Stable in gastric acid and rapidly absorbed orally. Peak blood levels occur 1-2 hours after a 500 mg dose [US].
    -   **Distribution:** Diffuses readily into most body tissues and fluids (except cerebrospinal fluid, unless meninges are inflamed). Approximately 20% protein-bound [US].
    -   **Metabolism and Excretion:** Half-life is 61.3 minutes. Approximately 60% of an orally administered dose is excreted unchanged in urine within 6-8 hours. Its excretion can be delayed by concomitant **probenecid** [US].
-   **Clarithromycin:**
    -   **Absorption:** Food slightly delays onset of absorption (peak time 2-2.5 hours) and increases peak plasma concentration by about 24%, but does not affect the extent of bioavailability. It can be given with or without food [US].
    -   **Distribution:** Distributes readily into body tissues and fluids, with tissue concentrations generally higher than serum concentrations. No data on CSF penetration [US].
    -   **Metabolism and Elimination:** Steady-state peak plasma concentrations are attained within 3 days. Elimination half-life is 5-7 hours with a 500 mg dose every 8-12 hours. Urinary excretion is approximately 30%. The major metabolite is 14-OH **clarithromycin** [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
The effectiveness and safety of **VOQUEZNA Triple Pak** and **VOQUEZNA Dual Pak** were evaluated in a randomized, controlled, double-blind triple therapy/open-label dual therapy study conducted in the United States and Europe in treatment-naïve *H. pylori*-positive adult patients. Patients were randomized to receive either **VOQUEZNA Triple Pak**, **VOQUEZNA Dual Pak**, or a lansoprazole-based triple therapy (LAC) for 14 days [US].

-   **Primary Outcomes:** *H. pylori* eradication was confirmed by a negative 13C urea breath test at ≥27 days post-therapy [US].
-   **Key Findings:**
    -   Both **VOQUEZNA Triple Pak** and **VOQUEZNA Dual Pak** were noninferior to LAC in patients without **clarithromycin** or **amoxicillin** resistant *H. pylori* at baseline [US].
    -   Both products were superior to LAC in patients with **clarithromycin** resistant *H. pylori* at baseline and in the overall population [US].
    -   **Eradication Rates (mITT population) [US]:**
        -   Patients with *H. pylori* infection not resistant to **clarithromycin** or **amoxicillin**:
            -   **VOQUEZNA Triple Pak**: 84.7% (n=222)
            -   **VOQUEZNA Dual Pak**: 78.5% (n=208)
            -   LAC: 78.8% (n=201)
        -   All randomized patients with *H. pylori* infection:
            -   **VOQUEZNA Triple Pak**: 80.8% (n=273)
            -   **VOQUEZNA Dual Pak**: 77.2% (n=250)
            -   LAC: 68.5% (n=226)
        -   Patients with **clarithromycin** resistant *H. pylori* at baseline:
            -   **VOQUEZNA Triple Pak**: 65.8% (n=48)
            -   **VOQUEZNA Dual Pak**: 69.6% (n=39)
            -   LAC: 31.9% (n=23)
