# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of levocarnitine clinical information synthesized from the provided regulatory source. It is intended for use by healthcare professionals as a clinical reference.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP), a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport.
*   **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative.
*   **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
*   **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Metabolic Disorders:** For the acute and chronic management of patients diagnosed with an inborn error of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are receiving maintenance hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
*   **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg [US].
*   **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
*   **Loading Dose:** In cases of severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
*   **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours, and never less frequently than every 6 hours.
*   **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
*   **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
*   **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
*   **Titration:** Adjustments may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine levels (target range 40 µmol/L to 50 µmol/L). Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be required [US].

### General Instructions
*   Parenteral products should be visually inspected for discoloration or particulate matter before use.
*   **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These can occur within minutes of administration, particularly in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. Treatment should be discontinued immediately upon a severe reaction [US].
*   **Neurologic Effects (Seizures):** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
*   **Monitoring:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (recommended range 35–60 µmol/L) is advised [US].
*   **Oral Administration Cautions:** High doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD)
Adverse events reported in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) include:
*   **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), chest pain (12%), tachycardia (6%), palpitations (5%).
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), abdominal pain (9%), nausea (8%).
*   **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%).
*   **Neurological:** Headache (22%), dizziness (13%), paresthesia (5%), insomnia (4%).
*   **Body as a Whole:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), flu syndrome (25%), infection (15%), asthenia (9%), fever (7%).
*   **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%), hypervolemia (5%).

### Post-Marketing Experience
*   Transient nausea and vomiting.
*   Body odor.
*   Gastritis.
*   Seizures.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
*   **Management:** It is recommended to monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [US: Not categorized by letter in source]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits using 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate, well-controlled studies exist in pregnant women. Use only if clearly needed [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against maternal benefits. Discontinuation of nursing or treatment may be considered [US].
*   **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg), appearing consistent with adult recommendations [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Renal Impairment:** In ESRD patients on hemodialysis, IV administration is indicated. However, chronic high-dose *oral* therapy is cautioned in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO) [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    *   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    *   **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; the mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ESRD Pharmacokinetic Study:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis showed mean pre-dialysis trough levels increased from 21.3 µmol/L at baseline to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
*   **Dose-Ranging Study:** In a study (N≈30 per group), doses of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg resulted in mean trough concentrations of 148, 240, and 456 µmol/L respectively after 24 weeks [US].
*   **Note:** While IV levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the impact on specific clinical outcomes in this population has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
*   **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
*   **Stability:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription Only Medicine).
