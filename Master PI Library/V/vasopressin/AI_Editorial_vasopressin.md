# Editorial Product Information: Vasopressin

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information provided by the United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a clinical reference format.
- **Note:** This synthesis is an editorial resource and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Vasopressin (synthetic 8-L-arginine vasopressin), a polypeptide hormone.
- **Brand Names:** Vasopressin Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available source.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 
    - Sterile aqueous solution for injection. 
    - Available as 20 units/mL in 1 mL single-dose vials and 200 units/10 mL (20 units/mL) in multiple-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous infusion (after dilution).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** American Regent, Inc. (distributed by HF Acquisition Co LLC) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Vasodilatory Shock:** Indicated to increase blood pressure in adults with vasodilatory shock who remain hypotensive despite adequate fluid resuscitation and the administration of catecholamines [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Septic Shock:** Initial dose of 0.01 units/minute [US].
- **Post-cardiotomy Shock:** Initial dose of 0.03 units/minute [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Dilution:** Must be diluted with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or 5% Dextrose (D5W) prior to administration [US].
    - **Standard concentration:** 0.1 units/mL (e.g., 50 units in 500 mL).
    - **Fluid-restricted concentration:** 1 unit/mL (e.g., 100 units in 100 mL).
- **Stability:** Once diluted, discard the solution after 18 hours at room temperature or 24 hours if refrigerated [US].
- **Inspection:** Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration before use.

### Titration and Tapering
- **Titration:** Increase the dose by 0.005 units/minute every 10 to 15 minutes until target blood pressure is achieved. 
- **Dose Limits:** Data is limited for doses exceeding 0.1 units/minute in post-cardiotomy shock or 0.07 units/minute in septic shock [US].
- **Tapering:** Once target blood pressure is maintained for 8 hours without other catecholamines, taper by 0.005 units/minute every hour as tolerated [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity or allergy to 8-L-arginine vasopressin or to chlorobutanol (a preservative in the multiple-dose vial) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiac Function:** Vasopressin may decrease cardiac index and worsen cardiac function [US].
- **Reversible Diabetes Insipidus:** Following cessation of treatment, patients may experience polyuria, dilute urine, and hypernatremia. This may require the reintroduction of vasopressin or administration of desmopressin to correct electrolyte shifts [US].
- **Monitoring:** Close monitoring of blood pressure, cardiac index, fluid status, and serum electrolytes is required during and after treatment.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Frequencies are not specifically quantified in the source document; however, the following reactions have been identified:
- **Common/Reported Reactions:** Decreased cardiac output, bradycardia, tachyarrhythmias, hyponatremia.
- **Ischemic Events:** Coronary ischemia, mesenteric ischemia, digital ischemia, and skin ischemia.
- **Post-marketing/Literature Reports:** 
    - *Cardiovascular:* Right heart failure, atrial fibrillation, myocardial ischemia.
    - *Gastrointestinal/Hepatobiliary:* Mesenteric ischemia, increased bilirubin levels.
    - *Renal:* Acute renal insufficiency.
    - *Hematologic:* Decreased platelets, hemorrhagic shock, intractable bleeding.
    - *Integumentary:* Ischemic lesions.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Catecholamines:** Expect additive effects on mean arterial blood pressure. Dose adjustments of vasopressin may be necessary [US].
- **Indomethacin:** May prolong the effect of vasopressin on cardiac index and systemic vascular resistance [US].
- **Ganglionic Blocking Agents:** May increase the pressor response of vasopressin [US].
- **SIADH-inducing Drugs:** (e.g., SSRIs, tricyclic antidepressants, haloperidol, enalapril) May increase both the pressor and antidiuretic effects [US].
- **Diabetes Insipidus-inducing Drugs:** (e.g., lithium, demeclocycline, clozapine) May decrease the pressor and antidiuretic effects [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** No adequate data on risk of major birth defects or miscarriage. Vasopressin may induce tonic uterine contractions [US].
- **Pregnancy Clearance:** Clearance of vasopressin increases significantly during the second and third trimesters (up to 4- to 5-fold at term) due to placental vasopressinase. Doses may need upward titration [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** No data available regarding presence in human milk or effects on the infant or milk production [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in children with vasodilatory shock have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65+ to determine if they respond differently. Dosing should be cautious, typically starting at the lower end of the range [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Specific dose adjustment protocols are not provided, but caution is advised in the elderly due to the higher frequency of decreased organ function [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **V1 Receptors:** Binds to V1 receptors on vascular smooth muscle to cause vasoconstriction via the Gq/11-phospholipase C pathway.
    - **V2 Receptors:** Stimulates antidiuresis via V2 receptors in the kidneys coupled to adenyl cyclase [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Increases systemic vascular resistance and mean arterial blood pressure. The pressor effect is proportional to the infusion rate and reaches a peak within 15 minutes [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is 140 mL/kg; does not appear to bind to plasma proteins [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Cleaved by serine protease, carboxipeptidase, and disulfide oxido-reductase. Metabolites are pharmacologically inactive [US].
    - **Elimination:** Half-life is ≤10 minutes at therapeutic infusion rates. Approximately 6% is excreted unchanged in the urine [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Evidence for the use of vasopressin is based on 7 studies in septic shock and 8 studies in post-cardiotomy vasodilatory shock, which demonstrated increases in systolic and mean blood pressure [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Refrigerated:** Store between 2°C and 8°C (36°F and 46°F). Do not freeze [US].
- **Room Temperature (1 mL Vials):** Unopened 1 mL vials may be stored at controlled room temperature (20°C to 25°C) for up to 12 months. Once removed from refrigeration, the vial must be marked with a revised expiration date and cannot be returned to the refrigerator [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
