# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the following jurisdiction's Product Information: **United States (FDA)**. 
- **Note:** Information from other major regulatory jurisdictions (e.g., Australian TGA, European EMA, Health Canada, Japanese PMDA) was not specified in the provided source material and is therefore noted as "Not specified in available sources" where applicable.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a clinical synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
    - In severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - Frequency: Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours [US].
    - Daily maintenance doses are typically in the range of 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
    - Administration: Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - Dose Adjustments: Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:**
    - **Metabolic Disorders:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
    - **ESRD:** Initiation of therapy may be prompted by trough concentrations below 40–50 µmol/L [US].
- **Renal Impairment/Oral Therapy:** High-dose oral levocarnitine is not evaluated in renal insufficiency; chronic oral use in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Serious Effects:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, laryngeal edema, and seizures [US].
- **Common Effects ($\ge$5% in ESRD clinical trials):**
    - **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (21%), headache (37%), injection site reaction (38%), pain (35%), fever (12%), flu syndrome (29%) [US].
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (21%), hypotension (19%), chest pain (15%), tachycardia (9%) [US].
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhea (35%), vomiting (21%), nausea (12%) [US].
    - **Metabolic/Nutritional:** Hypercalcemia (15%), hypervolemia (12%) [US].
    - **Nervous System:** Dizziness (18%), paresthesia (12%) [US].
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (27%), cough increase (18%), dyspnea (11%) [US].
- **Other Reported Effects:** Transient nausea/vomiting, gastritis, and body odor [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. **Management:** Monitor INR levels closely following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned a letter category in this PI; however, reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal constituent of human milk. Supplementation has not been specifically studied in nursing mothers. Benefits to the mother should be weighed against potential risks to the infant from excess carnitine intake [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies revealed no evidence of impaired fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy are established for metabolic disorders and ESRD; dosing is weight-based (see Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** In ESRD patients on dialysis, intravenous administration is the preferred route to avoid metabolite accumulation associated with high-dose oral therapy [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic/fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg post-dialysis), trough levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. While increased carnitine concentrations were demonstrated, the specific impact on clinical outcomes in this population was not determined in these trials [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Special Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) at room temperature [US].
