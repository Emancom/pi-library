# Editorial Product Information: Verteporfin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal and prescribing compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Verteporfin (INN).
- **Chemical Class:** Benzoporphyrin derivative; photoenhancer.
- **Brand Names:** **Visudyne** [US].
- **ATC Code:** S01LA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Lyophilised powder for injection; 15 mg per single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous infusion (followed by activation via nonthermal laser light).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Bausch Health US LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Age-Related Macular Degeneration (AMD):** Treatment of patients with predominantly classic subfoveal choroidal neovascularisation (CNV) [US].
- **Pathologic Myopia:** Treatment of subfoveal CNV [US].
- **Presumed Ocular Histoplasmosis:** Treatment of subfoveal CNV [US].
- *Note:* There is insufficient evidence for the treatment of predominantly occult subfoveal CNV [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Standard Dose:** 6 mg/m² of body surface area [US].

### Administration Instructions
Verteporfin therapy is a two-step process:
1. **Drug Infusion:** The calculated dose is administered intravenously over 10 minutes at a rate of 3 mL/minute [US].
2. **Light Activation:** Activation by a nonthermal diode laser (689 ± 3 nm) must begin 15 minutes after the start of the 10-minute infusion [US].

### Reconstitution and Dilution
- **Reconstitution:** Each 15 mg vial is reconstituted with 7 mL of Sterile Water for Injection to yield 7.5 mL of a 2 mg/mL solution.
- **Dilution:** The required volume of the 2 mg/mL solution is further diluted with **5% Dextrose for Injection (D5W)** to a total volume of 30 mL. 
- **Incompatibility:** Do **not** use normal saline or other parenteral solutions, as the drug may precipitate [US].

### Light Dosimetry
- **Recommended Light Dose:** 50 J/cm² of the neovascular lesion.
- **Intensity:** 600 mW/cm².
- **Duration:** 83 seconds.
- **Spot Size:** Should be 1,000 microns larger than the greatest linear dimension (GLD) of the lesion [US].

### Repeat Treatment
- Patients should be re-evaluated every 3 months. If leakage is detected on fluorescein angiography, therapy may be repeated [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Patients with porphyria [US].
- Known hypersensitivity to **verteporfin** or any component of the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Photosensitivity:** Patients become temporarily photosensitive for 5 days following infusion. Avoid exposure of skin and eyes to direct sunlight or bright indoor light (e.g., tanning salons, halogen lighting, high-power surgical/dental lights) [US].
- **Extravasation:** Severe pain, inflammation, and local necrosis can occur, especially if the site is exposed to light. Ensure a free-flowing IV line and use the largest arm vein possible (avoiding the back of the hand). If extravasation occurs, stop the infusion immediately and protect the area from light until swelling fades [US].
- **Vision Decrease:** A severe decrease in vision (≥4 lines) within 7 days of treatment has been reported. Retreatment should be withheld until vision recovers to baseline [US].
- **Anaphylaxis:** Rare but serious allergic reactions, including syncope and dyspnea, have occurred during or after infusion [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Very Common (>10%)
- **Injection Site Reactions:** Pain, edema, inflammation, and discoloration.
- **Visual Disturbances:** Blurred vision, flashes of light, decreased acuity, and visual field defects (e.g., scotoma) [US].

### Common (1–10%)
- **Systemic:** Back pain (primarily occurring during infusion), asthenia, fever, and flu-like syndrome.
- **Ocular:** Blepharitis, cataracts, conjunctivitis, dry eyes, and subretinal/vitreous hemorrhage.
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension, atrial fibrillation, and peripheral vascular disorders.
- **Dermatologic:** Photosensitivity reactions (sunburn-like) and eczema [US].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Retinal detachment (nonrhegmatogenous).
- Retinal pigment epithelial tear.
- Severe vision loss (1–5% of patients) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Photosensitising Agents:** Concomitant use of drugs like tetracyclines, sulfonamides, phenothiazines, or thiazide diuretics may increase the risk of skin photosensitivity [US].
- **Antioxidants/Radical Scavengers:** Compounds that quench active oxygen (e.g., dimethyl sulfoxide, β-carotene, ethanol, mannitol) may decrease **verteporfin** efficacy [US].
- **Vascular Effectors:** Calcium channel blockers, polymyxin B, or radiation therapy may enhance drug uptake by the endothelium. Conversely, drugs affecting clotting or vasoconstriction (e.g., thromboxane A2 inhibitors) may decrease efficacy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned]. There are no adequate data in pregnant women. Animal studies in rats showed increased incidences of anophthalmia and wavy ribs at 40-fold human exposure. Use only if the potential benefit justifies the risk [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** **Verteporfin** and its diacid metabolite are present in human milk. Breast milk levels can reach 66% of plasma levels. Because of the potential for serious reactions in infants, a decision should be made to either discontinue nursing or postpone treatment for at least 48 hours [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Approximately 90% of clinical trial participants were >65 years. A reduced treatment effect was observed with increasing age [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** In patients with mild hepatic insufficiency, the elimination half-life was increased by approximately 20%. No data are available for severe impairment [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A two-stage process. **Verteporfin** is transported by lipoproteins to the neovasculature. Upon activation by red light in the presence of oxygen, it generates singlet oxygen and free radicals, causing local vascular endothelial damage, platelet aggregation, and vessel occlusion [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Metabolism:** Limited metabolism by liver and plasma esterases to a diacid metabolite. Cytochrome P450 does not play a role.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is 5–6 hours. Elimination is primarily fecal (>99%); less than 0.01% is recovered in urine [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **TAP Study (AMD):** Two randomized trials (n=609) showed that **verteporfin**-treated patients with predominantly classic CNV were more likely to lose <3 lines of vision compared to placebo (67% vs 40% at 12 months) [US].
- **VIP-PM Study (Pathologic Myopia):** One study (n=120) showed statistical benefit at 1 year (86% vs 67% losing <3 lines) but the effect was no longer significant at 2 years [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store between 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Must be protected from light and used within 4 hours [US].
- **Handling:** Spills should be wiped with a damp cloth. Use of rubber gloves and eye protection is recommended during preparation [US].
