# Editorial Product Information: Vorinostat

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of vorinostat for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Clinicians should consult the local PI for definitive regulatory guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Vorinostat (a histone deacetylase (HDAC) inhibitor).
- **Brand Name:** Zolinza [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Hard gelatin capsules, 100 mg. The capsules are white, opaque, and printed with "568" over "100 mg" in black ink [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Merck Sharp & Dohme (Australia) Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Cutaneous T-cell Lymphoma (CTCL):** Indicated for the treatment of cutaneous manifestations in patients with CTCL who exhibit progressive, persistent, or recurrent disease following prior systemic therapies [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The standard recommended dose is 400 mg taken orally once daily [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** Doses must be taken with food [Australian]. Patients should be instructed to maintain a high fluid intake (at least 2 L/day) to prevent dehydration [Australian].
- **Dose Titration/Modification for Toxicity:** 
    - If a patient is intolerant to the initial dose, the dosage may be reduced to 300 mg once daily with food.
    - If further reduction is required, the dose may be adjusted to 300 mg once daily with food for five consecutive days per week [Australian].
- **Treatment Duration:** Continue treatment as long as there is no evidence of progressive disease or unacceptable toxicity [Australian].
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to vorinostat or any excipients in the formulation [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Severe hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Thromboembolism:** Reports of pulmonary embolism (PE) and deep vein thrombosis (DVT) have occurred. Clinicians should monitor for signs/symptoms, particularly in patients with a history of thromboembolic events [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal Disturbances:** Nausea, vomiting, and diarrhoea are common and may require antiemetics or antidiarrhoeals. Pre-existing symptoms should be controlled before starting therapy [Australian].
- **Hydration:** To mitigate the risk of dehydration associated with GI side effects, patients must drink at least 2 L of fluid daily [Australian].
- **Haematologic Effects:** Dose-related thrombocytopenia and anaemia occur. Severe reductions in counts require dose modification or discontinuation [Australian].
- **Hyperglycaemia:** Observed in patients receiving vorinostat. Serum glucose monitoring is required, especially in patients with diabetes or those at risk [Australian].
- **Monitoring Requirements:**
    - Perform blood cell counts and chemistry tests (including electrolytes, glucose, and serum creatinine) every 2 weeks during the first 2 months of therapy.
    - Perform these tests monthly thereafter [Australian].
- **Bowel Surgery:** Caution is advised in the perioperative period for patients undergoing bowel surgery, as some reports indicate potential interference with anastomotic healing [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):**
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhoea (45.3%), Nausea (37.2%), Anorexia (23.3%), Weight decreased (19.8%), Dry mouth (16.3%), Vomiting (11.6%), Constipation (10.5%).
    - **Constitutional:** Fatigue (45.3%), Chills (10.5%).
    - **Haematologic/Laboratory:** Thrombocytopenia (25.6%), Anaemia (12.8%), Blood creatinine increased (11.6%), Hyperglycaemia (detected in laboratory tests in 69% of patients; clinically reported in 4.7%).
    - **Other:** Dysgeusia (23.3%), Alopecia (16.3%), Muscle spasms (15.1%).
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Pulmonary embolism (4.7%), dehydration (3.7%), deep vein thrombosis, ischaemic stroke, and gastrointestinal haemorrhage [Australian].
- **Note on Dose-Dependency:** Frequencies of severe fatigue, anaemia, and thrombocytopenia increase at doses higher than 400 mg daily [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Coumarin-Derivative Anticoagulants:** Concomitant use may prolong Prothrombin Time (PT) and International Normalized Ratio (INR). Frequent monitoring of PT/INR is required [Australian].
- **Other HDAC Inhibitors (e.g., Valproic Acid):** Should not be used concomitantly due to the risk of additive adverse reactions, specifically severe (Grade 4) thrombocytopenia with associated GI bleeding and anaemia [Australian].
- **CYP Pathways:** Vorinostat is not eliminated via CYP pathways and only inhibits CYP2C9 and CYP3A4 at concentrations much higher than clinical levels. Drug-drug interactions via CYP are unlikely [Australian].
- **P-glycoprotein (P-gp):** Vorinostat is not a P-gp substrate and does not inhibit P-gp-mediated transport at clinical concentrations [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category D [Australian]:** There is potential for foetal harm. Animal studies showed decreased foetal weight, incomplete ossification, and gallbladder malformations.
    - **Contraception:** Females of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during and for at least 6 months after the last dose. Males with female partners of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during and for at least 3 months after the last dose [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if vorinostat is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding is advised against due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal data shows dose-dependent increases in corpora lutea and resorptions in females. Male fertility in rats was unaffected, though testicular degeneration was noted in dogs [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients ≥65 years are comparable to younger patients; no dose adjustment is required [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specifically evaluated in patients with renal impairment. However, renal excretion is a minor pathway for elimination [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild to Moderate:** Use with caution. Recommended dose is 300 mg daily for mild impairment and 200 mg daily for moderate impairment [Australian].
    - **Severe:** Contraindicated [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits HDAC Class I (HDAC1, 2, 3) and Class II (HDAC6). This leads to the accumulation of acetylated histones and proteins, inducing cell cycle arrest, differentiation, or apoptosis of transformed cells [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Oral bioavailability is approximately 43% (fasted). Administration with a high-fat meal increases the extent of absorption (AUC) by 33% [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Approximately 71% protein bound [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via glucuronidation and hydrolysis followed by beta-oxidation. Two major inactive metabolites: O-glucuronide and 4-anilino-4-oxobutanoic acid [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Predominantly metabolic; <1% of the dose is recovered unchanged in urine. Terminal half-life is ~2 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Study (Phase IIb):** An open-label, single-arm study of 74 patients with advanced CTCL (Stage IIB and higher) refractory to at least two systemic therapies.
- **Outcomes:** The overall objective response rate was 29.7%. Median time to response was 55 days. The median duration of response was not reached but estimated to exceed 6 months [Australian].
- **Clinical Benefit:** Beyond objective response, 81.1% of patients achieved some reduction in skin disease as measured by the Severity Weighted Assessment Tool (SWAT) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 30°C (86°F) [Australian].
- **Handling:** Capsules should not be opened or crushed. Direct contact with the powder should be avoided; if contact occurs, wash the area thoroughly [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
