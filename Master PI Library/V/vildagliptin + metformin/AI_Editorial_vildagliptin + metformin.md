# Editorial Product Information: vildagliptin + metformin

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **vildagliptin + metformin**
- Chemical class: Not specified in available sources (note: vildagliptin is referenced as belonging to the DPP‑4 inhibitor class in adverse event discussions)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History

- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- As an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycaemic control in adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) who:
  - are inadequately controlled on metformin monotherapy, or
  - are already treated with vildagliptin plus metformin given as separate tablets (fixed‑dose combination not to be used to initiate therapy).
- As triple therapy: in combination with a sulfonylurea for patients inadequately controlled on metformin plus a sulfonylurea.
- As add‑on to insulin (in patients on a stable insulin dose with metformin when insulin plus metformin alone do not provide adequate glycaemic control).

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Fixed strengths available in source: vildagliptin 50 mg combined with metformin hydrochloride 500 mg, 850 mg, or 1,000 mg.
- Maximum recommended total daily dose of vildagliptin: 100 mg (given as 50 mg twice daily).
- Metformin dosing should follow the patient’s established regimen; when higher metformin doses than available in the fixed combinations are required, consider using individual components.

### Starting dose guidance
- Patients inadequately controlled on metformin monotherapy: initiate the fixed combination based on the patient’s current metformin dose — the 50/500 mg, 50/850 mg or 50/1000 mg strengths may be used, typically administered twice daily as appropriate for metformin dose equivalence.
- Patients switching from separate vildagliptin + metformin tablets: choose the fixed strength corresponding to the patient’s current doses of vildagliptin and/or metformin.
- When used in combination with a sulfonylurea or with insulin: provide vildagliptin as 50 mg twice daily and a metformin dose similar to the patient’s pre‑existing metformin dose.

### Administration instructions
- Give with meals to reduce gastrointestinal adverse effects associated with metformin.
- To minimise risk of metformin accumulation and lactic acidosis, prescribe and use only one strength of the fixed combination at a time and advise patients to discard their previous metformin product when switching to the fixed combination.

### Dose adjustments and special populations
- Renal impairment (see Section 10 for details and table):
  - GFR ≥ 60 mL/min: metformin maximal daily dose up to 3,000 mg*; vildagliptin maximal daily dose 100 mg.
  - GFR 45–59 mL/min: start metformin ≤ 1,000 mg/day; maximum metformin 2,000 mg/day; vildagliptin maximal daily dose 50 mg.
  - GFR 30–44 mL/min: start metformin ≤ 500 mg/day; maximum metformin 1,000 mg/day; (vildagliptin dosing adjustments as above).
  - GFR <30 mL/min: fixed combination is contraindicated; use individual components if required and appropriate.
  - *If metformin doses higher than those achievable with fixed combinations are needed, use individual components.
- If no appropriate fixed strength is available for a specific renal dose or other requirement, use individual components.

### Titration schedules
- Not specified in available sources beyond renal dosing guidance and the need to individualise therapy.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to vildagliptin or metformin hydrochloride or to any excipient.
- Severe renal impairment (GFR < 30 mL/min): **contraindicated** because of the metformin component.
- Congestive heart failure requiring pharmacologic treatment.
- Acute or chronic metabolic acidosis, including lactic acidosis or diabetic ketoacidosis (with or without coma).
- Use of the fixed combination is contraindicated when any other source‑specified contraindication to metformin or vildagliptin applies.
- Radiologic studies with intravascular iodinated contrast: metformin-containing therapy should be temporarily discontinued around the time of such procedures (see Section 6); this is an instruction for temporary discontinuation rather than an absolute contraindication.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed/Highlighted warnings
- No region‑specific boxed warnings are specified in available sources. If a boxed warning exists in any jurisdiction, it is not stated in the provided documents.

### General warnings and precautions
- Not a substitute for insulin in type 1 diabetes or diabetic ketoacidosis.
- Fixed combination should not be used to initiate therapy; start based on existing vildagliptin and/or metformin therapy.
- Only one fixed‑dose strength should be prescribed and used at any time; patients should dispose of prior metformin products after switching.

### Renal function and lactic acidosis
- Assess GFR before initiation and at least annually thereafter; monitor more frequently (e.g., every 3–6 months) in the elderly and in patients at increased risk of renal decline.
- Metformin is substantially renally excreted; accumulation increases the risk of lactic acidosis, a very rare but potentially life‑threatening complication.
- Patients with conditions that may acutely alter renal function (e.g., dehydration, severe diarrhoea/vomiting, shock), patients undergoing major surgery or intravascular iodinated contrast procedures, or patients receiving medications that impair renal function should temporarily discontinue metformin‑containing therapy.
- Intravascular iodinated contrast: discontinue metformin prior to or at the time of the procedure and do not restart until at least 48 hours after, only after renal function has been reassessed and found to be stable.
- In case of signs/symptoms suggestive of lactic acidosis (acidotic dyspnoea, abdominal pain, muscle cramps, asthenia, hypothermia, progressing to coma), discontinue metformin and hospitalise for immediate evaluation (labs: blood pH, plasma lactate, anion gap, lactate/pyruvate ratio).

### Hepatic function
- Not recommended in patients with clinical or laboratory evidence of hepatic impairment.
- Pre‑treatment ALT or AST > 2.5 × ULN: fixed combination is not recommended.
- Rare cases of hepatic dysfunction (including hepatitis) have been reported with vildagliptin; liver enzymes should be measured prior to initiation and monitored at three‑month intervals during the first year and periodically thereafter.
- If persisting elevation of AST/ALT ≥ 3 × ULN occurs, discontinue therapy; if jaundice or signs of liver dysfunction appear, discontinue and evaluate. Do not reinitiate after confirmed hepatic injury and recovery.

### Cardiac disease
- Contraindicated in patients with congestive heart failure requiring pharmacologic treatment.
- Limited clinical experience in patients with advanced heart failure (NYHA class III–IV); not recommended in NYHA class IV.

### Endocrine / thyroid
- Metformin can reduce serum TSH in patients with treated or untreated hypothyroidism; monitor TSH in hypothyroid patients and monitor blood glucose closely when levothyroxine therapy is initiated, changed or stopped (levothyroxine can reduce hypoglycaemic effect of metformin).

### Mitochondrial disease
- Not recommended in patients with known mitochondrial disorders such as MELAS or maternally inherited diabetes and deafness (MIDD) because of risk of lactic acidosis exacerbation and neurologic complications.

### Alcohol
- Advise patients to avoid excessive alcohol intake, which can potentiate the effect of metformin on lactate metabolism and increase the risk of lactic acidosis.

### Surgery and acute illness
- Discontinue metformin‑containing therapy at the time of surgery under general, spinal or epidural anaesthesia (except for minor procedures with no restriction of food/fluids); may be restarted no earlier than 48 hours after surgery and only after renal function is re‑evaluated and stable.
- In settings of acute illness, hypoxia, or conditions associated with reduced oral intake, consider temporary discontinuation.

### Vitamin B12 monitoring
- Metformin is associated with decreased serum vitamin B12 concentrations in approximately 7% of patients; measure haematological parameters at least annually and investigate abnormalities. In predisposed patients, consider serum vitamin B12 measurement every 2–3 years.

### Hypoglycaemia
- Fixed combination alone usually does not cause hypoglycaemia, but risk increases with deficient caloric intake, excessive exercise without compensation, ethanol intake, concomitant insulin or insulin secretagogues, or in elderly/debilitated patients. Monitor and advise accordingly.

### Skin and immune-mediated reactions
- Post‑marketing reports: severe joint pain (arthralgia) and bullous pemphigoid have been associated with the class of DPP‑4 inhibitors (vildagliptin is a member); consider discontinuation and dermatology referral if bullous pemphigoid suspected.

### Concomitant medications affecting renal function or metformin disposition
- Use caution with drugs that impair renal function, cause major haemodynamic changes, or inhibit renal tubular transporters (e.g., OCT2/MATE), as these may increase systemic exposure to metformin and risk of lactic acidosis.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Summary
- Vildagliptin: low potential for drug interactions; not a cytochrome P450 substrate and does not inhibit or induce CYP450 enzymes tested; no clinically relevant interactions seen with commonly co‑prescribed agents in available interaction studies.
- Metformin: primarily renally excreted; interactions are commonly related to changes in renal function, urinary excretion, or effects on absorption.

### Clinically significant interactions and management
- Levothyroxine: may reduce the hypoglycaemic effect of metformin — monitor blood glucose when thyroid hormone therapy is initiated, changed, or stopped; adjust diabetes therapy as needed.
- Furosemide: furosemide increased metformin Cmax and AUC (no change in metformin renal clearance); metformin decreased furosemide Cmax/AUC (no change in furosemide renal clearance). Monitor clinical response and consider dosage adjustments if indicated.
- Nifedipine: increased absorption, Cmax and AUC of metformin and increased urinary excretion of metformin. Monitor for metformin adverse effects; adjust therapy as clinically indicated.
- Glyburide (glibenclamide): no consistent changes in metformin PK/PD; decreases in glyburide exposure were variable and of unclear clinical significance. When combining with sulfonylureas, monitor for hypoglycaemia.
- Iodinated contrast agents: discontinue metformin at or prior to procedure; do not resume until at least 48 hours after if renal function is stable.
- Drugs affecting renal tubular transporters (e.g., OCT2/MATE): concomitant use may increase systemic exposure to metformin — use caution and monitor renal function and for signs of metformin accumulation/lactic acidosis.
- Drugs that impair renal function or cause haemodynamic changes (e.g., certain antihypertensives, diuretics, NSAIDs): initiate with caution and monitor renal function.

### Additional notes
- No clinically relevant interactions were observed when vildagliptin 100 mg once daily was co‑administered with metformin 1,000 mg once daily in available studies.
- Extensive interaction studies with vildagliptin did not show clinically relevant PK interactions with several commonly co‑prescribed medicines (including other oral antidiabetics, certain cardiovascular agents, statins and warfarin) according to the available information.

(Region‑specific interaction differences: Not specified in available sources.)

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Safety and effectiveness in paediatric patients have not been established. The fixed combination is not recommended for use in children below 18 years of age.

### Elderly
- Because metformin is renally excreted and renal function tends to decline with age, monitor renal function regularly in elderly patients. Use only in elderly patients with normal renal function; individualise dosing and use the lowest effective dose.

### Renal impairment (dosing guidance)
- Assess GFR prior to initiation and periodically thereafter. Recommended adjustments:

| GFR (mL/min) | Metformin guidance | Vildagliptin guidance |
|---:|---|---:|
| ≥ 60 | Maximum daily metformin dose up to 3,000 mg* | Maximal daily vildagliptin 100 mg |
| 45–59 | Start metformin ≤ 1,000 mg/day; maximum metformin 2,000 mg/day | Maximal daily vildagliptin 50 mg |
| 30–44 | Start metformin ≤ 500 mg/day; maximum metformin 1,000 mg/day | Use with caution; see above |
| < 30 | Fixed combination contraindicated | Fixed combination contraindicated |

- *If metformin doses greater than those available in fixed combination are required, use separate components.

### Hepatic impairment
- Not recommended in patients with clinical or laboratory evidence of hepatic impairment. Pre‑treatment ALT/AST > 2.5 × ULN: do not use. Monitor liver enzymes during therapy (every 3 months in the first year and periodically thereafter).

## 12. Clinical Trials
- A clinical study in patients with congestive heart failure (NYHA functional classes I–III) showed that vildagliptin treatment was not associated with change in left ventricular function or worsening of pre‑existing congestive heart failure versus placebo; clinical experience in patients with NYHA class III was limited and inconclusive. No experience was reported in NYHA class IV; use is not recommended in these patients.
- No other pivotal trial summaries, trial designs, or primary outcome data are specified in the provided excerpts.
- Interaction studies: no clinically relevant pharmacokinetic interactions were observed when vildagliptin (100 mg once daily) was co‑administered with metformin (1,000 mg once daily) in available studies.
