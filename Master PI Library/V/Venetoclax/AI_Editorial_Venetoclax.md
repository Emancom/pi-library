# Editorial Product Information: Venetoclax

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically focused overview of venetoclax for use by healthcare professionals.
- This document is an editorial synthesis intended for reference and is not a substitute for the official, legally approved Product Information in any specific jurisdiction. Prescribers should always consult the local PI for the specific country in which they are practicing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Venetoclax; an orally bioavailable, small-molecule inhibitor of B-cell lymphoma (BCL)-2.
- **Brand Name:** **Venclexta** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** L01XX52
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):**
    - Film-coated tablets: 10 mg (round, pale yellow), 50 mg (oblong, beige), and 100 mg (oblong, pale yellow). [Australian]
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AbbVie Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Venetoclax is indicated for the following haematological malignancies [Australian-approved]:

**Chronic Lymphocytic Leukaemia (CLL) / Small Lymphocytic Lymphoma (SLL):**
- In combination with obinutuzumab for the treatment of adult patients.
- In combination with ibrutinib for the treatment of previously untreated adult patients.
- In combination with rituximab for adult patients who have received at least one prior therapy.
- As monotherapy for relapsed or refractory disease in patients with 17p deletion, or for those with no other suitable treatment options.

**Acute Myeloid Leukaemia (AML):**
- In combination with azacitidine or low-dose cytarabine for the treatment of newly diagnosed adult patients who are ineligible for intensive chemotherapy.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Tablets must be taken with a meal and water at approximately the same time each day.
- Tablets must be swallowed whole; do not chew, crush, or break.

### Chronic Lymphocytic Leukaemia (CLL/SLL)
Venetoclax requires a 5-week ramp-up schedule to mitigate the risk of Tumour Lysis Syndrome (TLS).
- **Week 1:** 20 mg daily
- **Week 2:** 50 mg daily
- **Week 3:** 100 mg daily
- **Week 4:** 200 mg daily
- **Week 5 and beyond:** 400 mg daily

**Combination Therapy Durations:**
- **With Obinutuzumab:** 12 cycles total (6 cycles with obinutuzumab, followed by 6 cycles of venetoclax monotherapy).
- **With Ibrutinib:** 15 cycles total (3 cycles of ibrutinib lead-in, followed by 12 cycles of combination).
- **With Rituximab:** Venetoclax 400 mg daily for 24 months from Cycle 1 Day 1 of rituximab.

### Acute Myeloid Leukaemia (AML)
- **Ramp-up (Days 1–3 or 4):**
    - Day 1: 100 mg
    - Day 2: 200 mg
    - Day 3: 400 mg
    - Day 4+: 400 mg (with azacitidine) or 600 mg (with low-dose cytarabine).
- Continue treatment until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.

### Missed Dose
- If within 8 hours of the usual time: Take as soon as possible.
- If >8 hours: Skip the dose and resume the normal schedule the next day.
- If vomiting occurs: Do not take an additional dose; take the next dose as scheduled.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to venetoclax or any excipients.
- **Concomitant use with strong CYP3A inhibitors** during the initiation and 5-week ramp-up phase in patients with CLL/SLL [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Tumour Lysis Syndrome (TLS):** Significant risk of rapid tumour reduction leading to life-threatening TLS. Risk is highest during the ramp-up phase. Prophylaxis with hydration and anti-hyperuricaemics (e.g., allopurinol) is mandatory. Monitor blood chemistry (potassium, uric acid, phosphorus, calcium, and creatinine) pre-dose and at 6–8 and 24 hours post-dose during ramp-up.
- **Neutropenia:** Grade 3 or 4 neutropenia is very common. Monitor complete blood counts (CBC) throughout treatment. Consider growth factors (G-CSF) and antimicrobials for signs of infection.
- **Serious Infection:** Sepsis and pneumonia (including fatal cases) have occurred. Dosing should be interrupted for serious infections.
- **Immunisation:** Safety of live attenuated vaccines is not established; they should not be administered during or immediately following treatment until B-cell recovery occurs.
- **Multiple Myeloma (Non-approved use):** Increased mortality observed when venetoclax was added to bortezomib and dexamethasone in clinical trials for multiple myeloma. This combination is not recommended outside controlled trials.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect profiles vary by indication and combination partner.

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction (CLL Monotherapy) | Adverse Reaction (AML + Azacitidine) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Neutropenia, Anaemia, Diarrhoea, Vomiting, Nausea, Fatigue, URTI | Thrombocytopenia, Neutropenia, Febrile neutropenia, Nausea, Diarrhoea, Vomiting, Fatigue, Sepsis, Decreased appetite |
| **Common (1-10%)** | Febrile neutropenia, Sepsis, Urinary tract infection, TLS | Stomatitis, Dizziness, Hypotension, Cholecystitis |

**Serious Adverse Effects:**
- **TLS, Febrile neutropenia, and Pneumonia** are the most frequently reported serious reactions across all indications.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP3A Inhibitors:** Venetoclax exposure significantly increases (e.g., ketoconazole increased AUC by 540%). Avoid strong/moderate inhibitors during ramp-up. If use is essential during steady-state, reduce venetoclax dose by at least 50% (moderate) or to 100 mg or less (strong).
- **CYP3A Inducers:** Strong inducers (e.g., rifampicin, St. John's Wort) decrease venetoclax exposure. Avoid concomitant use.
- **P-gp Substrates:** Venetoclax may increase concentrations of P-gp substrates (e.g., digoxin). Narrow therapeutic index P-gp substrates should be taken 6 hours before venetoclax.
- **Warfarin:** Monitor INR closely due to potential increases in warfarin levels.

### Dietary Interactions
- Avoid **grapefruit products, Seville oranges, and starfruit** as they inhibit CYP3A.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category C. Based on animal data, venetoclax may cause fetal harm. Use is not recommended during pregnancy. Women of childbearing potential must have a negative pregnancy test before starting and use highly effective contraception during and for 30 days after the last dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Discontinue breastfeeding during treatment. It is unknown if venetoclax is excreted in human milk.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies suggest a risk of compromised male fertility (testicular toxicity). Reversibility has not been demonstrated.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children <18 years have not been established.
- **Elderly Use:** No specific dose adjustment is required. Efficacy and safety profiles were generally similar between older (≥65 years) and younger patients.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild, moderate, or severe impairment (CrCl ≥15 mL/min). Patients with CrCl <80 mL/min require more intensive TLS prophylaxis. No data for CrCl <15 mL/min or patients on dialysis.
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - Mild/Moderate: No dose adjustment.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Reduce dose by 50% and monitor closely for toxicity.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Venetoclax selectively binds to the BCL-2 protein, an anti-apoptotic protein overexpressed in many haematological malignancies. By blocking BCL-2, venetoclax restores the apoptotic process, leading to programmed cell death of malignant cells.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak plasma concentration reached 5–8 hours post-dose. High-fat meals increase exposure by ~5-fold compared to fasting.
    - **Metabolism:** Predominantly metabolised by CYP3A4.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily faecal elimination (>99%). Terminal half-life is approximately 26 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **CLL14:** Demonstrated superior Progression-Free Survival (PFS) for venetoclax + obinutuzumab compared to obinutuzumab + chlorambucil in previously untreated CLL.
- **MURANO:** Showed significantly higher PFS for venetoclax + rituximab versus bendamustine + rituximab in relapsed/refractory CLL.
- **VIALE-A:** In newly diagnosed AML, venetoclax + azacitidine demonstrated superior overall survival (14.7 months vs 9.6 months) and higher remission rates compared to placebo + azacitidine.
