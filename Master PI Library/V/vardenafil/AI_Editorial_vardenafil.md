# Editorial Product Information: Vardenafil Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of vardenafil hydrochloride for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesizes clinical data, regulatory requirements, and prescribing safety information.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Prescribing Information required for clinical practice in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Vardenafil hydrochloride (a selective inhibitor of cyclic guanosine monophosphate (cGMP)-specific phosphodiesterase type 5 (PDE5)).
- **Chemical Class:** Piperazine derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Not specified in available source (Generic provided by AvKARE).
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available source.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Oral Tablets [US]:** 2.5 mg, 5 mg, 10 mg, and 20 mg. 
    - **Physical description [US]:** Orange, round, film-coated tablets embossed with the strength (e.g., "10").
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AvKARE [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Erectile Dysfunction (ED):** Indicated for the treatment of erectile dysfunction in men [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 General Dosing (Adult)
- **Standard Dose:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg, taken orally approximately 60 minutes before sexual activity.
- **Titration:** Based on efficacy and tolerability, the dose may be increased to 20 mg or decreased to 5 mg.
- **Frequency:** The maximum recommended dosing frequency is once per day.
- **Requirement for Efficacy:** Sexual stimulation is required for a treatment response.

### 3.2 Administration Details
- **Food:** May be taken with or without food. Notably, a high-fat meal can reduce the maximum plasma concentration (Cmax) by 18%–50% [US].

### 3.3 Concomitant Medication Adjustments [US]
- **Ritonavir:** Maximum dose of 2.5 mg in a 72-hour period.
- **Indinavir, Saquinavir, Atazanavir, Ketoconazole (400 mg daily), Itraconazole (400 mg daily), Clarithromycin:** Maximum dose of 2.5 mg in a 24-hour period.
- **Ketoconazole (200 mg daily), Itraconazole (200 mg daily), Erythromycin:** Maximum dose of 5 mg in a 24-hour period.
- **Alpha-blockers:** Patients should be stable on alpha-blocker therapy before initiating vardenafil at a starting dose of 5 mg (or 2.5 mg if used with certain CYP3A4 inhibitors).

## 4. Contraindications
- **Nitrates:** Concomitant use with nitrates or nitric oxide donors in any form (regular or intermittent) is strictly contraindicated due to the risk of severe hypotension [US].
- **Guanylate Cyclase (GC) Stimulators:** Co-administration with GC stimulators, such as riociguat, is contraindicated [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiovascular Status:** Physicians should evaluate the cardiac risk of sexual activity. Use is not recommended in patients for whom sex is inadvisable or those with unstable angina, severe heart failure, or recent (within 6 months) stroke/myocardial infarction [US].
- **Priapism:** Patients should seek immediate medical help for erections lasting >4 hours. Untreated priapism can result in permanent penile tissue damage.
- **Ocular Effects (NAION):** Sudden loss of vision may be a sign of non-arteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy (NAION). Use with caution in patients with a history of NAION or "crowded" optic disc [US].
- **Sudden Hearing Loss:** Discontinue use if sudden decrease or loss of hearing occurs.
- **Alpha-blocker Co-administration:** Risk of symptomatic hypotension/syncope if used in hemodynamically unstable patients. 
- **QT Prolongation:** Avoid in patients with congenital long QT syndrome or those taking Class IA (e.g., quinidine) or Class III (e.g., amiodarone) antiarrhythmics [US].
- **Phenylketonuria (PKU):** Tablets contain aspartame. Phenylalanine content per tablet [US]:
    - 2.5 mg tablet: 0.7 mg
    - 5 mg tablet: 1.4 mg
    - 10 mg tablet: 2.8 mg
    - 20 mg tablet: 5.6 mg

### 6.2 Serious Adverse Effects
- **Cardiovascular:** Angina pectoris, myocardial infarction, ventricular tachyarrhythmias, and hypotension.
- **Neurologic:** Seizures and transient global amnesia [US-Postmarketing].
- **Urogenital:** Priapism.
- **Special Senses:** NAION, visual color distortions, and sudden hearing loss.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### 7.1 Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Potent inhibitors (e.g., ritonavir, ketoconazole) significantly increase vardenafil exposure (AUC). Dose reduction is mandatory (see Section 3.3).
- **Cimetidine/Antacids:** No significant effect on vardenafil bioavailability.

### 7.2 Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Nitrates:** Potentiation of hypotensive effects; combined use is contraindicated.
- **Alpha-blockers:** Potential for additive blood-pressure-lowering effects leading to symptomatic hypotension.
- **Antihypertensives:** Potential for small additive reductions in blood pressure.
- **Alcohol:** Single doses of 20 mg did not potentiate the hypotensive effects of alcohol (0.5 g/kg).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category B [US]:** Not indicated for use in women. Animal studies (rats/rabbits) showed no evidence of teratogenicity at doses significantly higher than the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD).
- **Breastfeeding:** Not indicated for use in women. It is unknown if vardenafil is excreted in human milk; rat studies confirmed excretion into milk.
- **Fertility:** No effect on sperm motility or morphology was observed in healthy volunteers after a 20 mg dose.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not indicated for use in pediatric patients; safety and efficacy have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use (≥65 years):** Plasma concentrations are significantly higher than in younger males (AUC increased by 52%). A starting dose of 5 mg should be considered [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - Mild to moderate: No dose adjustment required.
    - **End-stage renal disease/Dialysis:** Not studied; use is not recommended [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild (Child-Pugh A):** No dose adjustment.
    - **Moderate (Child-Pugh B):** Recommended starting dose is 5 mg; maximum dose should not exceed 10 mg [US].
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Not studied; use is not recommended [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Vardenafil inhibits PDE5, the enzyme responsible for degrading cGMP in the corpus cavernosum. Increased cGMP levels lead to smooth muscle relaxation and increased blood flow to the penis.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Bioavailability:** Approximately 15% absolute bioavailability.
    - **Absorption:** Median Tmax is 60 minutes.
    - **Distribution:** 95% protein bound; Vss is 208 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Predominantly hepatic via CYP3A4 (primary) and CYP3A5/2C isoforms. The major metabolite M1 has 28% of the parent drug’s potency.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is 4–5 hours. Excretion is primarily fecal (91%–95%) and minimally renal (2%–6%).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** Evaluated in four major double-blind, placebo-controlled trials (N=2431).
- **Primary Outcomes:** Statistically significant improvements were observed in the Erectile Function (EF) Domain of the IIEF, SEP2 (penetration), and SEP3 (maintenance of erection).
- **Efficacy in Subpopulations:** Clinical benefit was maintained in patients with diabetes mellitus and those who had undergone radical prostatectomy.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F).
- **Excursions:** Permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F–86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Keep out of reach of children.
