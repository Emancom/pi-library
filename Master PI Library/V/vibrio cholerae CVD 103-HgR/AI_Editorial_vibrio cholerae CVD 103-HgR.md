# Editorial Product Information: vibrio cholerae CVD 103-HgR

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **vibrio cholerae CVD 103-HgR**
- Chemical class: live, attenuated bacterial vaccine (Vibrio cholerae O1, genetically attenuated)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 2016 (stated as "Initial U.S. Approval: 2016")
- Date of last revision/update in source PI: revised 2/2025
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Active immunization against disease caused by Vibrio cholerae serogroup O1 in persons 2 through 64 years of age traveling to cholera-affected areas.
- Limitations of use:
  - Effectiveness has not been established in persons living in cholera-affected areas.
  - Effectiveness has not been established in persons with pre-existing immunity from prior exposure to V. cholerae or from prior cholera vaccination.
  - Not shown to protect against disease caused by V. cholerae serogroup O139 or other non-O1 serogroups.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing
- Single oral dose administered a minimum of 10 days before potential exposure to cholera.
- Safety and effectiveness of revaccination: Not established in available sources.

### Dose volumes by age
- Persons 6 through 64 years of age: single dose volume after reconstitution = 100 mL.
- Children 2 through 5 years of age: single dose volume after reconstitution = 50 mL (prepared by discarding half of the reconstituted buffer solution prior to adding the active component).

### Administration instructions (preparation and ingestion)
- Prepare and administer in a healthcare setting equipped for medical waste disposal.
- Use only bottled water (purified, spring, or sparkling/carbonated) — do not use tap/faucet water or other beverages/fluids — to reconstitute buffer packet. Pour 100 mL of bottled water into a clean disposable cup.
- Reconstitution sequence is mandatory: add the buffer component to the water first (effervescence will occur), stir until dissolved; for children 2–5 years discard half the buffer solution after mixing; then add the active (lyophilized) component and stir for at least 30 seconds until dispersed to a slightly cloudy suspension that may contain white particulates. If packets are reconstituted in the wrong order, the vaccine must be discarded.
- Optional sweetening: up to 4 g (1 teaspoon) sucrose or up to 1 g (1/4 teaspoon) non‑flavored stevia may be added and stirred into the suspension. Do not add any other sweeteners or medicated/flavored agents (these may reduce effectiveness).
  - If sucrose or non‑flavored stevia is added, the suspension should be consumed within 30 minutes of preparation.
  - If no sucrose/non‑flavored stevia is added, the suspension should be consumed within 4 hours of reconstitution (at room temperature around 21 °C).
- The recipient should drink the entire contents of the cup; any residue should be discarded with the cup.
- Observe the recipient for appropriate ingestion; dispose of cup, packets and stirrer as medical waste. Inactivate spills and clean non‑disposable equipment with 70% isopropyl alcohol or 10% bleach solution.

### Restrictions on eating and drinking
- Instruct recipients to avoid eating or drinking for 60 minutes before and after oral ingestion.

### Missed dose/titration
- Not applicable (single-dose regimen). Missed-dose/revaccination guidance: Not specified in available sources beyond "safety and effectiveness of revaccination have not been established."

## 5. Contraindications
- History of severe allergic reaction (e.g., anaphylaxis) to any ingredient of the vaccine.
- History of severe allergic reaction to a previous dose of any cholera vaccine.
- No other specific contraindications are stated in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- **Altered immunocompetence**: Safety and effectiveness have not been established in immunocompromised persons. Immunocompromised individuals may have a diminished immunologic response.
- **Shedding and transmission**: Vaccine strain may be shed in stool for at least 7 days following vaccination. There is potential for transmission of the vaccine strain to non‑vaccinated close contacts (for example, household contacts). Use caution when considering vaccination of individuals who have immunocompromised close contacts.
- Prepare and administer in healthcare settings capable of appropriate disposal of medical waste.
- Avoid concomitant administration with systemic antibiotics (see Drug Interactions); do not administer to patients who have received oral or parenteral antibiotics within 14 days prior to vaccination.
- Co-administration with chloroquine may diminish immune responses; administer vaccine at least 10 days before starting chloroquine for antimalarial prophylaxis.
- Immunosuppressive therapies (including irradiation, antimetabolites, alkylating agents, cytotoxic drugs, and corticosteroids at greater than physiologic doses) may reduce immune response to the vaccine.
- Pregnancy considerations:
  - The vaccine is not systemically absorbed following oral administration and maternal systemic exposure is not expected; however, maternal cholera disease itself is associated with adverse pregnancy outcomes (including fetal death).
  - The vaccine strain may be shed in stool of vaccinated mothers for at least 7 days, with potential for transmission to the infant during vaginal delivery.
- Breastfeeding: vaccine is not systemically absorbed by the mother and breastfeeding is not expected to expose the infant to vaccine (based on absence of systemic absorption).

### Monitoring requirements / specific clinical precautions
- Consider risk of transmission to immunocompromised household contacts prior to vaccination.
- No routine laboratory monitoring is specified in available sources.
- Observe for adverse reactions in the week following vaccination (solicited adverse reactions recorded in clinical trials for 7 days).

- Serious adverse events (SAEs):
  - Pooled clinical studies: 0.6% (20/3,235) of vaccine recipients reported an SAE within 6 months post-vaccination; 0.5% (3/562) of placebo recipients reported SAEs. None of these were considered related to vaccination.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Antibiotics:
  - Avoid concomitant administration of systemic antibiotics that may be active against the vaccine strain, as they may prevent sufficient replication and impair immune response.
  - Do not administer to patients who have received oral or parenteral antibiotics within 14 days prior to vaccination.
- Antimalarial (chloroquine):
  - Immune responses may be diminished if vaccine is administered concomitantly with chloroquine. Administer vaccine at least 10 days before beginning chloroquine prophylaxis.
- Vaccines:
  - No data available on concomitant administration with other vaccines.
- Immunosuppressive treatments:
  - Immunosuppressive therapies (irradiation, antimetabolites, alkylating agents, cytotoxic drugs, and corticosteroids at greater than physiologic doses) may reduce immune response to the vaccine.
- Pharmacokinetic interactions: Not specified in available sources.
- Any other clinically significant interactions or management recommendations not explicitly described: Not specified in available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Live, attenuated V. cholerae strain CVD 103-HgR replicates in the gastrointestinal tract. The strain was constructed by deleting the catalytic domain sequence of both copies of the ctxA gene to prevent synthesis of active cholera toxin; it retains the ctxB gene encoding the immunogenic non‑toxic B subunit of cholera toxin. A genetic marker was inserted into the hemolysin gene locus (hlyA) to enable differentiation from wild-type V. cholerae O1.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Systemic absorption: vaccine is not absorbed systemically following oral administration (stated as not systemically absorbed).
- Shedding: vaccine strain may be shed in stool for at least 7 days post-vaccination; potential for transmission to close contacts.
- Quantitative data on dose CFU per reconstituted dose: Not specified in available sources (exacted CFU values in source text are not fully specified in available excerpts).

### Pharmacodynamics
- Immune correlate: increases in serum vibriocidal antibody measured 10 days after vaccination were associated with protection in a human challenge study (immunogenicity linked to protective effect in challenge study).

## 12. Clinical Trials
### Adult efficacy and safety studies
- Four randomized, placebo-controlled, multicenter clinical trials in adults:
  - Total adult exposure: 3,235 adults aged 18–64 received vaccine; 562 received placebo (physiologic saline or lactose).
  - Demographics (pooled adults): mean age 32.5 years; 53.8% female; race/ethnicity distribution provided in source PI.
- Key safety trial (Study 1):
  - Design: multicenter, double-blind, randomized (8:1 vaccine:placebo), placebo-controlled in the United States and Australia.
  - Safety analysis set: 2,789 vaccine recipients (adults 18–45).
  - Solicited adverse reactions recorded daily for 7 days post‑vaccination; rates reported in Section 7 above.

### Pediatric trial
- Study 5:
  - Design: multicenter, double-blind, randomized (6:1 vaccine:placebo), placebo-controlled trial in the United States.
  - Pediatric cohorts: 12–<18 yrs (Cohort 1), 6–<12 yrs (Cohort 2), 2–<6 yrs (Cohort 3).
  - Enrollment and exposure: 468 children 2–17 years received vaccine; 75 received placebo.
  - Solicited adverse reactions recorded daily for 7 days post‑vaccination; rates by cohort provided in Section 7 above.
  - Unsolicited adverse events considered related to treatment occurred in 13.2% of vaccine recipients vs 9.3% placebo; loose stool was the most frequent unsolicited event in pediatric cohorts.

### Challenge/immunogenicity data
- Human challenge study (Study 2) and immunogenicity studies:
  - Rises in serum vibriocidal antibody at 10 days post-vaccination were associated with protection in a challenge model.
  - Details of challenge efficacy endpoints and numerical efficacy outcomes: Not specified in available sources beyond the association with vibriocidal antibody rise.

### Serious adverse events
- Pooled adult SAEs within 6 months: 0.6% vaccine vs 0.5% placebo; none considered related to vaccination.
- Pediatric SAEs within 6 months: 0.2% vaccine vs 1.3% placebo; none considered related to vaccination.
