# Editorial Product Information: Vaccinia Immune Globulin Intravenous (Human)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (Food and Drug Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Vaccinia Immune Globulin Intravenous (Human) (VIGIV) for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for legal and regulatory compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Vaccinia Immune Globulin Intravenous (Human). VIGIV is a purified IgG fraction derived from human plasma of donors boosted with the vaccinia vaccine.
- **Brand Name(s):** CNJ-016 [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 5% (50 mg/mL) sterile liquid for injection. Available in a 20 mL single-use vial containing ≥50,000 Units [US]. 
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Emergent BioSolutions Canada Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
VIGIV is indicated for the treatment and/or modification of severe complications resulting from vaccinia vaccination [US]:
- Eczema vaccinatum.
- Progressive vaccinia.
- Severe generalized vaccinia.
- Vaccinia infections in individuals with pre-existing skin conditions (e.g., burns, impetigo, varicella-zoster, or eczematous lesions).
- Aberrant infections induced by vaccinia virus (except in cases of isolated keratitis), including accidental implantation in the eyes, mouth, or other hazardous areas.

**Limitations of Use:**
- VIGIV is not considered effective for, and is not indicated for, the treatment of postvaccinial encephalitis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Standard Dose:** 6000 Units/kg administered intravenously as soon as symptoms of severe vaccinia-related complications appear [US].
- **Repeat/Escalation Dosing:** If the patient does not respond to the initial 6000 Units/kg dose, clinicians may consider repeat dosing or higher doses (e.g., 9000 Units/kg or 24,000 Units/kg). While doses up to 24,000 Units/kg were well tolerated in healthy volunteers, clinical data on repeat dosing in symptomatic patients are limited [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Preparation:** Vials should be brought to room temperature before use. If frozen, thaw in a refrigerator (2°C to 8°C) for approximately 14 hours, or rapidly at room temperature for one hour followed by a 37°C water bath. **Do not shake** the vial to avoid foaming. Do not microwave or refreeze [US].
- **Dilution:** VIGIV may be administered undiluted or diluted with 0.9% Sodium Chloride USP (maximum dilution 1:2 v/v) [US].
- **Infusion Rate:**
    - Administer via a dedicated line.
    - **Initial rate:** Do not exceed 2 mL/min.
    - **Patients <50 kg:** Do not exceed 0.04 mL/kg/minute (133.3 Units/kg/minute).
    - Rate should be reduced for patients experiencing minor adverse reactions (e.g., flushing) or those at risk for thrombosis or renal insufficiency [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Isolated vaccinia keratitis:** VIGIV is contraindicated as it may increase corneal scarring [US].
- **Anaphylaxis:** History of prior severe systemic or anaphylactic reactions to human immune globulin preparations [US].
- **IgA Deficiency:** Patients with selective IgA deficiency who have antibodies against IgA and a history of hypersensitivity (VIGIV contains trace IgA ≤40 mcg/mL) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning: Interactions with Glucose Monitoring Systems [US]
**VIGIV contains maltose, which can interfere with certain blood glucose monitoring systems (e.g., those using GDH-PQQ or glucose-dye-oxidoreductase methods). This interference may result in falsely elevated glucose readings, leading to inappropriate insulin administration and life-threatening hypoglycemia. True hypoglycemia may also go untreated. Clinicians must ensure that only glucose-specific testing methods are used for patients receiving VIGIV.**

### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity:** Severe allergic reactions, including anaphylaxis, may occur. Adrenaline and resuscitative equipment must be available during administration [US].
- **Acute Renal Dysfunction/Failure:** Use with caution in patients at risk (e.g., pre-existing renal insufficiency, diabetes, age >65, volume depletion, or nephrotoxic drug use). VIGIV is sucrose-free, reducing but not eliminating the risk of osmotic nephropathy. Monitor BUN and serum creatinine [US].
- **Thrombosis:** Thrombotic events have occurred with IGIV products. Risks include advanced age, cardiovascular history, and hyperviscosity. For at-risk patients, the **maximum daily dose should not exceed 12,000 Units/kg** [US].
- **Hemolysis:** VIGIV may contain blood group antibodies (hemolysins). Monitor for clinical signs of hemolysis and perform confirmatory laboratory testing if suspected [US].
- **Aseptic Meningitis Syndrome (AMS):** May occur within hours to two days of treatment. Usually resolves without sequelae following discontinuation [US].
- **TRALI:** Noncardiogenic pulmonary edema (Transfusion-Related Acute Lung Injury) may occur. Monitor for pulmonary adverse reactions [US].
- **Infectious Agents:** As a product derived from human plasma, there is a theoretical risk of transmitting infectious agents, including viruses and the Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease (CJD) agent [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most common adverse drug reactions (frequency >10%) observed in clinical trials were:
- **Headache**
- **Nausea**
- **Rigors**
- **Dizziness**

Other reactions (frequency ≥5%) include:
- **General:** Feeling cold, pain, pyrexia, fatigue, asthenia.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Vomiting.
- **Nervous System:** Paraesthesia, tremor.
- **Musculoskeletal:** Muscle spasm, back pain.
- **Integumentary:** Increased sweating.

**Serious Adverse Effects:**
- Post-marketing reports for IGIV products include cardiac arrest, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), and coma [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Live Attenuated Vaccines:** VIGIV may impair the efficacy of vaccines such as measles, mumps, rubella, and varicella. Vaccination with live viruses should be deferred for approximately three months after VIGIV administration [US].
- **Laboratory Interferences:**
    - Falsely elevated blood glucose readings with non-specific monitoring systems [US].
    - Passively transferred antibodies may result in positive serological tests (e.g., Coombs’ test) [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** There are no data on VIGIV use in pregnant women. Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. It is unknown if VIGIV can cause fetal harm or affect reproduction capacity [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data regarding the presence of VIGIV in human milk or its effects on the breastfed infant or milk production [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 16 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients over 65 years of age have not been established. Use with caution due to increased risks of renal and cardiac dysfunction [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Requires careful monitoring and administration at the minimum rate of infusion practicable [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** VIGIV provides passive immunity by supplying neutralizing antibodies against the vaccinia virus [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Clinical studies show VIGIV reduces the local and immunologic response to vaccinia vaccination when administered prior to or concurrently with the vaccine [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Mean Peak Plasma Concentration (Cmax):** 161 Units/mL (at 6000 Units/kg dose).
    - **Tmax:** Approximately 1.8 to 2.6 hours.
    - **Half-life:** Approximately 30 days (range 13–67 days).
    - **Volume of Distribution:** 6630 mL [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
The safety and PK/PD profiles were evaluated in three clinical trials involving healthy, vaccinia-naïve volunteers (N=142). Studies demonstrated that VIGIV (9000 Units/kg and 24,000 Units/kg) reduced the "take" or pox reaction and erythema area following vaccinia vaccination compared to placebo [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Frozen Storage:** Store at or below -15°C (≤5°F).
- **Thawed Storage:** Once thawed, store at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) and use within 60 days.
- **In-use Stability:** Infusion should begin within 4 hours after entering the vial. 
- **Handling:** Discard any partially used vials as the product contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription Only) [US].
