# Editorial Product Information: Vestronidase alfa

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: US (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory information into a single clinical reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official PI for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the local approved PI for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Vestronidase alfa-vjbk; a recombinant human lysosomal beta-glucuronidase (GUS) produced by recombinant DNA technology in Chinese Hamster Ovary (CHO) cells.
- **Brand Name:** **MEPSEVII** [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AB18 (Vestronidase alfa).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injection: 10 mg/5 mL (2 mg/mL) as a sterile, preservative-free, colorless to slightly yellow liquid in a single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Ultragenyx Pharmaceutical Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Mucopolysaccharidosis VII (MPS VII, Sly syndrome):** Indicated for pediatric and adult patients for the treatment of MPS VII.
- **Limitations of Use:** The impact of vestronidase alfa on the central nervous system manifestations of MPS VII has not been determined [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric):** The recommended dosage is 4 mg/kg administered every two weeks via intravenous infusion [US].
- **Premedication:** Administration of a non-sedating antihistamine, with or without an antipyretic, is recommended 30 to 60 minutes prior to the start of the infusion to improve patient comfort [US].
- **Preparation Instructions:**
    - Use aseptic technique.
    - Calculate the number of vials based on patient weight (4 mg/kg dose).
    - Allow vials to reach room temperature; do not heat or microwave. Do not shake.
    - Dilute 1:1 with 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection. A higher dilution may be used if required by the patient’s cardiac or fluid status.
    - Inspect for particulate matter; the solution should be colorless to slightly yellow. Flocculation (thin translucent fibers) may occur but is normal for this protein solution.
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Infuse over approximately 4 hours.
    - **Initial rate:** Infuse 2.5% of the total volume over the first hour.
    - **Subsequent rate:** Increase the rate to infuse the remaining 97.5% over the following 3 hours as tolerated.
    - Use an infusion set with an in-line, low-protein binding 0.2-micron filter.
    - Do not flush the line during the infusion to avoid bolus dosing.
    - Do not infuse with other products.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- None listed in available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Anaphylaxis [US]:**
    - Anaphylaxis has occurred as early as the first dose.
    - Appropriate medical support must be readily available during administration.
    - Observe patients closely during the infusion and for at least 60 minutes post-infusion.
    - If anaphylaxis occurs, immediately discontinue the infusion and initiate appropriate medical treatment.
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Close clinical observation for signs of hypersensitivity reactions is required during and after the infusion.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** 
    - Infusion site extravasation (33%)
    - Diarrhea (25%)
    - Rash (25%)
    - Anaphylaxis (10%)
- **Common (1–10%):**
    - Infusion site swelling
    - Peripheral swelling
    - Pruritus
- **Serious Adverse Effects:**
    - **Anaphylaxis:** Reported in 2 out of 20 patients in the clinical program. Symptoms included respiratory distress, cyanosis, decreased oxygen saturation, and hypotension.
    - **Febrile Convulsion:** One instance was reported in a patient receiving 4 mg/kg; the reaction resolved with medical management, and the patient was successfully rechallenged.
- **Immunogenicity:** 78% of patients developed anti-drug antibodies (ADA). 55.6% of ADA-positive patients tested positive for neutralizing antibodies (NAb). ADA status did not appears to affect the reduction in urinary glycosaminoglycans (uGAGs) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** No formal drug interaction studies have been conducted. As a recombinant human protein, vestronidase alfa is unlikely to be affected by cytochrome P450 inhibitors or inducers.
- **Clinically Significant Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** No lettered category provided (current labeling). 
    - **Clinical Data:** No available data in pregnant women. 
    - **Animal Data:** In rats, an increased number of stillbirths was observed at exposures less than the recommended human dose. Embryofetal studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 10 times the human exposure showed no adverse developmental outcomes.
- **Breastfeeding:** No data available on the presence of the drug in human or animal milk, or its effects on the breastfed infant. The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the mother's clinical need and potential risks.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats showed no adverse effects on male or female fertility or reproductive performance [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have been established in patients from 5 months of age to adulthood [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include patients aged 65 and over. It is unknown if they respond differently than younger patients [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosing adjustments not specified; vestronidase alfa is not expected to be eliminated via renal excretion.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Dosing adjustments not specified; vestronidase alfa is expected to undergo proteolytic degradation.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** MPS VII is characterized by a deficiency of the GUS enzyme, leading to GAG accumulation in tissues. Vestronidase alfa provides exogenous GUS enzyme. It is taken up into cellular lysosomes via mannose-6-phosphate (M6P) receptors on the cell surface, where it catabolizes accumulated GAGs.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Treatment results in a sustained reduction of urinary excretion of GAGs.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Cmax:** 17.3 ± 9.6 mcg/mL (at 4 mg/kg dose).
    - **AUC:** 50.9 ± 32.2 mcg*hr/mL.
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution (Vss) is 251 ± 140 mL/kg.
    - **Metabolism:** Proteolytic degradation into small peptides and amino acids.
    - **Elimination:** Mean half-life (t1/2) is approximately 2.33 hours. Clearance is 83.6 ± 43.2 mL/hr/kg. Not excreted in urine or feces [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study 301 (Pivotal Trial):** A randomized start trial involving 12 patients (ages 8 to 25 years). Patients were randomized to four placebo durations before crossing over to vestronidase alfa 4 mg/kg every two weeks. 
    - **Outcome:** The mean difference in 6-minute walk test (6MWT) distance increased with treatment duration (18-meter improvement at 24 weeks compared to placebo).
- **Study 203:** An open-label, uncontrolled study in 8 patients under 5 years of age. Long-term treatment showed sustained decreases in urinary GAG levels. Growth remained near the 5th percentile relative to age-matched norms.
- **Expanded Access:** A pediatric patient requiring continuous ventilatory support was able to tolerate 9 hours daily off the ventilator after 164 weeks of treatment.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store under refrigeration at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F). Do not freeze or shake. Protect from light [US].
- **Diluted Solution:** If not used immediately, store under refrigeration (2°C to 8°C) for up to 36 hours, followed by up to 6 hours at room temperature (up to 25°C). Total time from dilution to completion of infusion must not exceed 42 hours [US].
