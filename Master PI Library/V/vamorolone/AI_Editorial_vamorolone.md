# Editorial Product Information: Vamorolone

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
*   **Purpose:** This document is a harmonized editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesizes official product information to provide a concise clinical reference.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labeling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Vamorolone (a corticosteroid)
*   **Brand Name(s):** AGAMREE [US]
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Oral suspension, 40 mg/mL (orange-flavored, white to off-white) [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Oral.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Catalyst Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy (DMD):** Indicated for the treatment of DMD in patients aged 2 years and older [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult/Pediatric)
*   **Standard Dosage:** 6 mg/kg taken orally once daily, preferably with a meal [US].
*   **Maximum Dosage:** 300 mg daily for patients weighing more than 50 kg [US].
*   **Titration:** Depending on individual tolerability, the dose may be titrated down to 2 mg/kg/day [US].

### Administration Instructions
*   **Preparation:** Shake the suspension for approximately 30 seconds before use [US].
*   **Device:** Use only the provided oral syringe for administration. Discard the suspension 3 months after the initial bottle opening [US].

### Switching from Other Corticosteroids
*   Patients may transition from oral corticosteroids (e.g., prednisone, deflazacort) to vamorolone without a washout period or dose reduction to prevent adrenal insufficiency. The starting dose should be 6 mg/kg/day [US].

### Discontinuation
*   If treatment has exceeded one week, the dosage must be tapered gradually to avoid adrenal crisis [US].

### Missed Dose Instructions
*   Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to vamorolone or any excipients in the formulation (e.g., sodium benzoate) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Endocrine Function:** Chronic use may cause hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis suppression, Cushingoid features, and hyperglycemia. Monitoring for these conditions is required. Avoid abrupt withdrawal after prolonged use [US].
*   **Immunosuppression and Infection:** Increased susceptibility to new infections and risk of exacerbating or reactivating latent infections (e.g., Tuberculosis, Hepatitis B, Amebiasis). Signs of infection may be masked. Avoid exposure to varicella (chickenpox) and measles in non-immune patients [US].
*   **Cardiovascular and Renal Effects:** Potential for elevated blood pressure, sodium and water retention, and potassium loss. Monitor blood pressure and serum potassium regularly [US].
*   **Gastrointestinal Perforation:** Increased risk in patients with peptic ulcers, diverticulitis, or ulcerative colitis. Symptoms may be masked by corticosteroid therapy [US].
*   **Behavioral and Mood Disturbances:** Potential for euphoria, insomnia, irritability, mood swings, personality changes, severe depression, or psychosis. Symptoms usually emerge within days or weeks of treatment initiation [US].
*   **Bone Health:** Long-term use decreases bone mineral density and may cause avascular necrosis. Monitor bone mineral density in chronic users [US].
*   **Ophthalmic Effects:** Risk of cataracts, glaucoma (with optic nerve damage), and secondary ocular infections. Monitor intraocular pressure if treated for more than 6 weeks [US].
*   **Vaccinations:** Do not administer live or live-attenuated vaccines to patients on immunosuppressive doses. Complete the vaccination schedule at least 4 to 6 weeks before starting vamorolone [US].
*   **Thromboembolic Events:** Use with caution in patients predisposed to thromboembolic disorders [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors (e.g., Itraconazole):** Significantly increase vamorolone exposure (Cmax by 8%, AUC by 44%). **Management:** Reduce vamorolone dose to 4 mg/kg/day (max 200 mg) when used with strong inhibitors [US].
*   **Moderate/Weak CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** No dosage adjustment required [US].
*   **CYP3A4 Substrates:** Vamorolone induces CYP3A4 in vitro; concomitant use may decrease the concentration of other CYP3A4 substrates. No clinical studies have confirmed the magnitude of this effect [US].
*   **Neuromuscular Blocking Agents:** Increased risk of acute myopathy when used concomitantly [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [US: No assigned letter category]. There are no data on vamorolone use in pregnant women. Corticosteroids are generally associated with increased risks of orofacial clefts and intrauterine growth restriction. Infants exposed to high doses in utero should be monitored for hypoadrenalism [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** No data on the presence of vamorolone in human milk. Systemic corticosteroids can appear in milk and may suppress growth or interfere with endogenous corticosteroid production in the infant [US].
*   **Fertility:** Animal studies in dogs showed testicular toxicity (spermatid degeneration) at high doses. Clinical significance in humans is unknown [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established for DMD in patients aged 2 years and older. Use in patients under 2 years is not established [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** No clinical experience; DMD is primarily a disease of children and young adults [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:**
    *   **Mild to Moderate (Child-Pugh A & B):** Exposure increases significantly. **Dosage Adjustment:** Reduce to 2 mg/kg/day (maximum 100 mg/day for patients >50 kg) [US].
    *   **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Not recommended due to lack of clinical data [US].
*   **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution; specific dosing adjustments are not provided in available sources [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** A corticosteroid that binds to glucocorticoid receptors to exert anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive effects. The exact mechanism in DMD is not fully elucidated [US].
*   **Pharmacodynamics:** Produces dose-dependent suppression of morning cortisol and increases in leukocyte counts [US].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption:** Median Tmax is 2 hours. Food (high-fat) slightly increases AUC (13%) and delays Tmax by 1 hour [US].
    *   **Distribution:** 88.1% protein bound. Volume of distribution is ~162 L in a 20 kg patient [US].
    *   **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolized via Phase I (CYP3A4/5, CYP2C8) and Phase II (UGT1A3, UGT2B7, UGT2B17) pathways [US].
    *   **Elimination:** Half-life is approximately 2 hours. Excretion is primarily renal (48% as metabolites) and fecal (30%) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Study 1 (NCT03439670):** A 24-week, randomized, double-blind study of 121 motor-symptomatic males (aged 4 to <7 years) with DMD.
*   **Primary Endpoint:** Change from baseline to Week 24 in Time to Stand Test (TTSTAND) velocity.
*   **Outcomes:** AGAMREE 6 mg/kg/day showed a statistically significant improvement in TTSTAND velocity compared to placebo (p=0.002). Secondary endpoints, including the 6-Minute Walk Test (6MWT) and Time to Run/Walk 10 meters (TTRW), also showed significant improvement at the 6 mg/kg dose [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Unopened:** Store upright at room temperature, 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C [US].
*   **After Opening:** Store upright and refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F). Do not freeze [US].
*   **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion 3 months after opening the bottle [US].
