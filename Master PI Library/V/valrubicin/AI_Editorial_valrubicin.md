# Editorial Product Information: Valrubicin

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA). 
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of valrubicin for healthcare professionals, synthesised from official regulatory sources.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory approved PI for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Valrubicin (N-trifluoroacetyladriamycin-14-valerate)
- **Chemical Class:** Anthracycline topoisomerase inhibitor (semisynthetic analog of doxorubicin)
- **Brand Names:** **VALSTAR** [US]
- **ATC Code:** L01DB09 
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile, nonaqueous solution concentrate for intravesical instillation; 200 mg/5 mL (40 mg/mL) in single-use vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravesical use only. Do NOT administer via intravenous or intramuscular routes.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Endo USA, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **BCG-Refractory Carcinoma in Situ (CIS):** Indicated for the intravesical treatment of BCG-refractory carcinoma in situ of the urinary bladder in patients for whom immediate cystectomy would result in unacceptable morbidity or mortality [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Recommended Dose:** 800 mg administered intravesically once per week for six weeks [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Timing:** Delay administration for at least two weeks following transurethral resection (TURB) or fulguration [US].
- **Preparation:**
    - Use caution during handling; follow cytotoxic drug disposal protocols. Goggles, gloves, and protective gowns are recommended [US].
    - Valrubicin contains polyoxyl castor oil, which leaches DEHP from PVC. Prepare and store only in glass, polypropylene, or polyolefin containers. Use non-DEHP (e.g., polyethylene-lined) administration sets [US].
    - Vials should be slowly warmed to room temperature before use. If waxy precipitates are present (common below 4°C), warm in the hand until clear. Do not heat [US].
- **Dilution:** Withdraw 20 mL (four 200 mg/5 mL vials) and dilute with 55 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection, USP, to reach a total volume of 75 mL [US].
- **Instillation:** Insert a urethral catheter under aseptic conditions and drain the bladder. Instill the 75 mL solution slowly via gravity flow.
- **Retention:** The patient should retain the drug in the bladder for two hours before voiding. Maintain adequate hydration following treatment [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Perforated bladder or compromised bladder mucosa [US].
- Known hypersensitivity to anthracyclines or polyoxyl castor oil [US].
- Concurrent urinary tract infection (UTI) [US].
- Small bladder capacity (patients unable to tolerate 75 mL instillation) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning - Risk of Metastatic Bladder Cancer:** Delaying cystectomy can lead to lethal metastatic bladder cancer. Valrubicin induces a complete response in only approximately 1 in 5 patients. If a complete response is not achieved after 3 months, or if CIS recurs, cystectomy must be reconsidered [US].
- **Bladder Integrity:** Do not administer to patients with a perforated bladder. Evaluate bladder status before instillation. Perforation allows systemic absorption, which may lead to severe myelosuppression (leukopenia and neutropenia) [US].
- **Transurethral Resection (TURB):** To avoid systemic exposure, wait at least two weeks after TURB/fulguration before initiating therapy [US].
- **Irritable Bladder Symptoms:** Use with caution in patients with severe irritable bladder. Spontaneous discharge of the instillate may occur; clamping the catheter is not advised [US].
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Valrubicin can cause fetal harm. Advise patients of reproductive potential to use effective contraception (see Section 8) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Approximately 84% of patients experience local adverse reactions, typically resolving within 1 to 7 days after voiding the instillate [US].

| **Urinary Frequency** | 61% |
| **Urinary Urgency** | 57% |
| **Dysuria** | 56% |

### Systemic and Other Effects
- **Common (1–10%):** Urinary tract infection (15%), abdominal pain (5%), nausea (5%), headache (4%), asthenia (4%), malaise (4%), dizziness (3%), rash (3%), fever (2%), anemia (2%), vomiting (2%) [US].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Hyperglycemia, peripheral edema, pneumonia, myalgia, flatulence [US].
- **Other:** Red-tinged urine is expected for the first 24 hours following administration [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Formal drug interaction studies have not been conducted [US].
- **Management:** Due to lack of compatibility data, valrubicin should not be mixed with other drugs [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not assigned, but documentation states "can cause fetal harm"]. Animal studies show embryo-fetal toxicity and malformations at 0.2 times the human intravesical dose.
- **Contraception:**
    - **Females:** Use effective contraception during treatment and for 6 months after the final dose [US].
    - **Males:** Use effective contraception during treatment and for 3 months after the final dose [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on presence in human milk. Due to potential serious adverse reactions in infants, breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for 2 weeks after the last dose [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies suggest valrubicin may impair male fertility (testicular degeneration and atrophy of the prostate observed in dogs) [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in paediatric patients have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific precautions are required for geriatric patients in good health. The mean age in clinical trials was 69.5 years [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Valrubicin inhibits the incorporation of nucleosides into nucleic acids, causes chromosomal damage, and arrests the cell cycle in G2. While it does not bind strongly to DNA, its metabolites interfere with the DNA breaking-resealing action of topoisomerase II [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Penetrates the bladder wall. Systemic absorption is negligible in patients with intact bladder mucosa; only nanogram quantities reach the plasma.
    - **Distribution:** Under conditions of bladder perforation, systemic exposure is significantly increased (AUC can increase up to 200-fold) [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Negligible metabolism in bladder tissue during the retention period.
    - **Elimination:** Almost completely excreted (approx. 99%) by voiding the bladder instillate [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Data:** In a study of 90 patients with BCG-refractory CIS, 18% (n=16) achieved a complete response (CR) at 6 months, documented by biopsy and cytology. The median duration of response ranged from 13.5 to 21 months depending on the analysis method [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store under refrigeration at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F).
- **Shelf Life:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Special Handling:** Do not freeze. Diluted solution is stable for 12 hours at temperatures up to 25°C (77°F) [US].
