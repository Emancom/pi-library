# Editorial Product Information: Vaxelis

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) documents of the following jurisdictions: **Australian (TGA)** and **US (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically focused overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Always consult the current local approved PI for definitive legal and clinical guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Diphtheria toxoid, tetanus toxoid, acellular pertussis (DTPa) antigens [pertussis toxoid (PT), filamentous haemagglutinin (FHA), pertactin (PRN), and fimbriae types 2 and 3 (FIM)], hepatitis B (HepB) surface antigen (HBsAg), inactivated poliovirus (IPV) types 1, 2, and 3, and *Haemophilus influenzae* type b (Hib) capsular polysaccharide conjugated to meningococcal protein.
- **Chemical Class:** Combined bacterial and viral vaccine (hexavalent).
- **Brand Name:** **Vaxelis**.
- **ATC Code:** J07CA09.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Suspension for injection. Each 0.5 mL dose contains:
    - Diphtheria Toxoid: $\geq$ 20 IU [Australian]; 15 Lf [US]
    - Tetanus Toxoid: $\geq$ 40 IU [Australian]; 5 Lf [US]
    - Pertussis Toxoid (PT): 20 mcg
    - Filamentous Haemagglutinin (FHA): 20 mcg
    - Pertactin (PRN): 3 mcg
    - Fimbriae Types 2 and 3 (FIM): 5 mcg
    - Hepatitis B surface antigen: 10 mcg
    - Poliovirus (Inactivated): Type 1 (29 D-antigen units), Type 2 (7 D-antigen units), Type 3 (26 D-antigen units)
    - Hib Polysaccharide: 3 mcg (conjugated to 50 mcg meningococcal protein)
- **Route of Administration:** Intramuscular (IM) injection only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** 
    - [Australian]: Sanofi-aventis australia pty ltd; Maxx Pharma Pty Ltd.
    - [US]: MSP Vaccine Company (partnership between Merck & Co. and Sanofi).

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Vaxelis** is indicated for active immunisation against diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis, hepatitis B, poliomyelitis, and invasive disease caused by *Haemophilus influenzae* type b.
- **Jurisdictional Age Limits:**
    - [Australian]: Indicated for primary and booster vaccination in infants and toddlers from the age of 6 weeks.
    - [US]: Approved for use as a 3-dose series in children from 6 weeks through 4 years of age (prior to the 5th birthday).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- A single 0.5 mL dose administered intramuscularly.

### Vaccination Schedule
- **Primary Series:** 
    - [US]: Administered as a 3-dose series at 2, 4, and 6 months of age.
    - [Australian]: Consists of 2 or 3 doses with an interval of at least 1 month between doses, starting from 6 weeks of age, in accordance with official recommendations.
- **Booster Dose:**
    - [Australian]: A booster dose may be given at least 6 months after the last priming dose. Vaxelis may be used as a booster if the child received a different hexavalent vaccine for the primary series.
    - [US]: Vaxelis does NOT constitute a primary series against pertussis; an additional dose of a pertussis-containing vaccine (e.g., DTaP) is required to complete the series [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Shake the pre-filled syringe gently to obtain a homogeneous, whitish, cloudy suspension. 
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration. 
- **Site:** Anterolateral thigh (preferred for infants <1 year) or deltoid muscle of the upper arm. 
- **Contraindicated Routes:** Do not administer intravascularly, intradermally, or subcutaneously.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** History of anaphylactic reaction after a previous dose of **Vaxelis** or any vaccine containing the same components/residues (including neomycin, streptomycin, or polymyxin B).
- **Neurological Disorders:** 
    - Encephalopathy of unknown aetiology occurring within 7 days of a previous pertussis-containing vaccine.
    - [US]: Progressive neurologic disorder (including infantile spasms, uncontrolled epilepsy, or progressive encephalopathy) until the condition is stabilised.
    - [Australian]: Uncontrolled neurologic disorder/epilepsy (pertussis vaccination should be deferred until stabilised).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Acute Disease:** Postpone administration in children suffering from moderate to severe acute febrile illness. Minor illness/low-grade fever is not a contraindication.
- **Bleeding Disorders:** Administer with caution to individuals with thrombocytopenia or bleeding disorders due to the risk of bleeding following IM injection.
- **Guillain-Barré Syndrome (GBS):** If GBS occurred within 6 weeks of a previous tetanus-toxoid vaccine, the decision to give **Vaxelis** should be based on a careful benefit-risk assessment.
- **Pertussis History:** If any of the following occurred after a pertussis vaccine, subsequent doses should be carefully considered:
    - Temperature $\geq$ 40°C ($\geq$ 40.5°C [US]) within 48 hours not due to another cause.
    - Collapse or shock-like state (HHE) within 48 hours.
    - Persistent, inconsolable crying $\geq$ 3 hours within 48 hours.
    - Convulsions (with or without fever) within 3 days.
- **Premature Infants:** Consider the potential risk of apnoea; respiratory monitoring for 48–72 hours is recommended for very premature infants (born $\leq$ 28 weeks gestation), especially those with respiratory immaturity.
- **Protection:** Vaccination may not protect 100% of individuals. It will not prevent diseases caused by other types of *H. influenzae* or other microorganisms (e.g., *N. meningitidis*).

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common ($\geq$ 10%):** Irritability, crying, somnolence, injection site reactions (pain, erythema, swelling), pyrexia ($\geq$ 38.0°C), decreased appetite, and vomiting.
- **Common (1–10%):** Diarrhoea, injection site bruising, induration, or nodules.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Rhinitis, lymphadenopathy, sleep disorders (insomnia, restlessness), hypotonia, pallor, cough, abdominal pain, rash, hyperhidrosis, injection site rash/warmth, and fatigue.
- **Post-Marketing Reports (Frequency Not Known):**
    - **Immune System:** Anaphylactic reaction, urticaria, angioedema.
    - **Nervous System:** Convulsions (febrile and non-febrile), Hypotonic-hyporesponsive episodes (HHE).
    - **General:** Extensive swelling of the vaccinated limb (sometimes involving adjacent joints).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Concomitant Vaccines:** **Vaxelis** may be administered simultaneously with pneumococcal conjugate vaccines (PCV13), rotavirus vaccines, MMR, varicella, and meningococcal B/C vaccines. Use separate syringes and injection sites.
- **Fever Risk:** Rates of fever are reported to be higher when **Vaxelis** is co-administered with PCV13 [Australian].
- **Immunosuppressants:** Immunosuppressive therapy may reduce the immune response to the vaccine.
- **Laboratory Interference:** Hib capsular polysaccharide antigen is excreted in the urine. Sensitive urine tests may yield a false-positive result for *H. influenzae* type b for at least 30 days following vaccination.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - [Australian]: Category B2. Not intended for use in women of child-bearing age.
    - [US]: Not approved for individuals $\geq$ 5 years; no human or animal data available.
- **Breastfeeding:** Not intended for use in women of child-bearing age. No data available on the effect on milk production or the breastfed infant.
- **Fertility:** **Vaxelis** has not been evaluated for impairment of fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - Safety and efficacy in infants <6 weeks of age have not been established.
    - [US]: Safety and efficacy in children $\geq$ 5 years not established.
- **Elderly Use:** Not applicable; the vaccine is not indicated for adult populations.
- **Immunocompromised:** Immune response may be diminished. Vaccination is recommended in children with chronic immunodeficiencies (e.g., HIV), though clinical protection may be limited.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Induces active immunity by stimulating the production of specific antibodies against the antigens included in the vaccine. 
- **Pharmacokinetics:** As with all vaccines, standard pharmacokinetic studies (absorption, distribution, metabolism, elimination) are not performed.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** 
    - Seroprotection thresholds: Anti-diphtheria ($\geq$ 0.01 IU/mL), Anti-tetanus ($\geq$ 0.01 IU/mL), Anti-HBs ($\geq$ 10 mIU/mL), Anti-Polio (titre $\geq$ 1:8), Anti-PRP ($\geq$ 0.15 mcg/mL).
    - No established correlate of protection exists for pertussis, but efficacy is inferred from the response to PT, FHA, PRN, and FIM.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Immunogenicity was evaluated in multiple pivotal Phase 3 studies (e.g., Study 005, 006, 007, 008). 
- **Results:** Following a 3-dose primary series, seroprotection/vaccine response rates were generally $> 90\%$ for most components. 
- **US Study 005:** **Vaxelis** was found to be non-inferior to the concomitant administration of Pentacel and RECOMBIVAX HB regarding antibody responses for all antigens.
- **Long-term Persistence:** [Australian] reports that 8 years after primary vaccination, 40.9% to 49.1% of children maintained anti-HBs levels $\geq$ 10 mIU/mL, with 100% demonstrating an anamnestic response after a challenge dose.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store in a refrigerator at 2°C to 8°C. 
- **Freezing:** Do not freeze. Discard if the vaccine has been frozen.
- **Light:** Keep the syringe in the outer carton to protect from light.
- **Stability Excursion:** Stability data indicate the vaccine is stable at temperatures up to 25°C for 150 hours [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** 48 months [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction

    - [Australian]: Schedule 4 (Prescription Medicine).
    - [US]: Rx-only.
