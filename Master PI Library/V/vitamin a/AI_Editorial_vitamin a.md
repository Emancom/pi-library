# Editorial Product Information: Vitamin A (Retinol)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis incorporates Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdictions: 
    - **Australian (TGA)**: Phebra Vitamin A Capsules
    - **US (FDA)**: Apotex Multiple Vitamins (Pediatric) Pharmacy Bulk Package
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating data for both oral high-dose monotherapy and parenteral multivitamin components.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulations and official documents for specific prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Retinol (Vitamin A). Biologically active form is all-(E)-retinol. It is commonly supplied as the ester Vitamin A palmitate.
- **Brand Names:** 
    - **Phebra Vitamin A Capsules** [Australian]
    - **Multiple Vitamins (Pediatric)** [US]
- **ATC Code:** A11CA01
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Capsule (Oral):** 15,000 microgram retinol equivalents (RE), equal to 50,000 international units (IU) [Australian].
    - **Injection (Intravenous):** Supplied as a pharmacy bulk package (two-vial system). After mixing, Vitamin A concentration varies by weight-based aliquot: 0.2 mg (690 IU), 0.5 mg (1,495 IU), or 0.7 mg (2,300 IU) per daily dose volume [US].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral; Intravenous infusion (after dilution).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Phebra Pty Ltd [Australian]; Apotex Corp [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Deficiency Treatment (Oral):** Treatment of vitamin A deficiency manifesting as chronic infections, dermatitis, acne, skin ulcers, and eye lesions [Australian].
- **Supplemental/Prophylactic Use (Oral):** Premenstrual tension and prophylaxis against respiratory infections [Australian].
- **Parenteral Nutrition (Intravenous):** Prevention of vitamin deficiency in pediatric patients up to 11 years of age receiving parenteral nutrition [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Oral Administration [Australian]
- **Adults and Children over 8 years:** 
    - **Severe Deficiency:** Two capsules (30,000 mcg RE/100,000 IU) daily for three days, followed by one capsule (15,000 mcg RE/50,000 IU) daily for two weeks.
- **General Guidance:** Poor dietary habits should be corrected, and a balanced diet should be directed alongside supplementation.

### Intravenous Administration (Pediatric ≤ 11 years) [US]
- **Recommended Daily Dosage (based on actual weight):**
    - **< 1 kg:** 1.5 mL of combined mixture (0.2 mg Vitamin A).
    - **1 kg to < 3 kg:** 3.25 mL of combined mixture (0.5 mg Vitamin A).
    - **≥ 3 kg:** 5 mL of combined mixture (0.7 mg Vitamin A).
- **Preparation:** Transfer the contents of Vial 2 to Vial 1. The resulting solution must be diluted in at least 100 mL of intravenous dextrose or saline. 
- **Administration:** Do not give as a direct, undiluted bolus as it may cause tissue irritation and dizziness.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypervitaminosis A:** Pre-existing vitamin A toxicity.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known sensitivity to retinol or any inactive ingredients, including:
    - Soya oil, methyl hydroxybenzoate, and propyl hydroxybenzoate [Australian].
    - Polysorbate 80 (found in parenteral formulations) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypervitaminosis A Risk:** Risk of toxicity is increased in patients with renal failure or liver disease [US]. Blood levels of Vitamin A should be monitored periodically [US].
- **Light Sensitivity:** Vitamin A is highly sensitive to light; both capsules and parenteral solutions must be protected from light exposure.
- **Aluminum Toxicity:** Parenteral products may contain aluminum. Prototypical risk exists for premature neonates and patients with renal impairment receiving prolonged therapy [US].
- **Parenteral Handling:** Vitamin A can adhere to plastic (PVC) in IV administration sets, which may lead to lower delivered concentrations. This should be considered, particularly in low-birth-weight infants [US].
- **Monitoring:** Monitor blood vitamin concentrations to ensure adequate levels and prevent deficiency or excess, especially during long-term parenteral nutrition [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are primarily associated with acute or chronic toxicity (hypervitaminosis A).

| Toxicity Type | Symptoms |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Acute** | Drowsiness, irresistible desire to sleep, dizziness, severe headache (due to increased intracranial pressure), nausea, vomiting, papilloedema, erythema, pruritus, and skin peeling. In infants: Bulging fontanelles [Australian]. |
| **Chronic** | Dry/itchy skin, dermatitis, hair growth disturbance, cracked lips, bone pain, hyperostosis, loss of appetite, weight loss, fatigue, irritability, and hepatosplenomegaly [Australian]. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Pathological liver changes (fibrosis, cirrhosis leading to portal hypertension), hypercalcemia, and premature closure of epiphyses in children [Australian]. Anaphylaxis and dermatologic reactions (rash, erythema) have been reported post-approval [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Vitamin K (often co-administered in parenteral multivitamins) antagonizes the anticoagulant effect of warfarin. Monitor INR closely [US].
- **Antibiotics:** Certain vitamins (C, B-complex) in multivitamin mixtures may decrease the activity of erythromycin, kanamycin, streptomycin, and tetracyclines [US].
- **Bisulfites:** Vitamin A may react with bisulfite solutions (e.g., sodium bisulfite) in parenteral mixtures; avoid such combinations [US].
- **Anti-epileptics:** Folic acid (in multivitamin products) may decrease phenytoin serum levels [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:**
    - **Australian Category D:** Drugs which have caused or are suspected to cause an increased incidence of human foetal malformations or irreversible damage. 
    - **Teratogenic Risk:** Daily doses exceeding 1,500 mcg RE should be avoided due to the risk of birth defects. The recommended adult daily requirement is 750 mcg RE [Australian].
    - **US Guidance:** Follow US Recommended Daily Allowances (RDA). Requirements may exceed those of non-pregnant patients.
- **Breastfeeding:** Administration of RDA-compliant doses is not expected to cause harm to breastfed infants [US].
- **Fertility:** Carcinogenicity and fertility studies have not been performed for these products [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric:** Oral capsules are indicated for children over 8 years [Australian]. Parenteral multivitamin use is approved for children up to 11 years [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Higher risk of hypervitaminosis A; monitor renal function and vitamin A blood levels closely [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Absorption of oral Vitamin A may be reduced [Australian]. Monitor blood levels closely as liver disease increases toxicity risk [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Essential for the growth and differentiation of epithelial cells, bone growth, and reproduction. In the retina, retinol is converted to 11-cis-retinol and incorporated into rhodopsin, which is vital for vision in dim light.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Readily absorbed from the GI tract (as esters). Reduced in hepatic or pancreatic dysfunction [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Stored in the liver until saturation. Released into the blood 95% bound to retinol-binding protein (RBP) [Australian]. 
    - **Metabolism/Excretion:** Metabolised into glucuronide conjugates and oxidized to retinoic acid. Excreted in both urine and faeces [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Oral Capsules:** Store below 25°C. Protect from light [Australian].
- **Parenteral (Mixed Solution):** Store under refrigeration (2°C to 8°C). Use within 24 hours of dilution. Protect from light [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australian (TGA):** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **US (FDA):** Rx-only.
