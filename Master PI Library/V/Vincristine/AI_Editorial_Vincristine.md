# Editorial Product Information: Vincristine Sulfate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial harmoniously synthesizes product information from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the local approved PI for full prescribing details and legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Vincristine sulfate (a salt of an alkaloid obtained from the periwinkle plant, *Cantharanthus roseus*).
- **Chemical Class:** Antineoplastic vinca alkaloid; microtubule inhibitor.
- **Brand Names:** DBL Vincristine Sulfate Injection [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01CA02.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Clear, colourless solution for injection; 1 mg/1 mL and 2 mg/2 mL vials [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** **Intravenous (IV) use only.**
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Vincristine sulfate is primarily indicated for the treatment of acute leukaemia [Australian]. 

It is frequently utilised as a component of polychemotherapy regimens for:
- Hodgkin’s disease and non-Hodgkin’s malignant lymphomas (including lymphocytic, mixed-cell, histiocytic, undifferentiated, nodular, and diffuse types) [Australian].
- Rhabdomyosarcoma, neuroblastoma, Wilm’s tumour, Ewing’s sarcoma, and osteogenic sarcoma [Australian].
- Mycosis fungoides [Australian].
- Carcinoma of the uterine cervix and breast cancer [Australian].
- Malignant melanoma [Australian].
- Oat-cell carcinoma of the lung [Australian].
- Gynaecological tumours of childhood [Australian].
- Idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura (true ITP) refractory to splenectomy and short-term adrenocortical steroids (not recommended as primary treatment) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
Vincristine is typically administered at **weekly intervals**. Extreme caution is required in dose calculation, as overdosage may be fatal [Australian].

- **Adults:** The usual dose is 0.4 to 1.4 mg/m² body surface area (BSA) [Australian].
- **Paediatric Patients:** The usual dose is 1.5 to 2.0 mg/m² BSA. For children weighing 10 kg or less, or having a BSA less than 1 m², the starting dose should be 0.05 mg/kg once weekly [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Route:** Strictly intravenous. **Intrathecal administration is usually fatal.** 
- **Preparation:** To prevent fatal administration errors, the "minibag" technique is recommended. Vincristine should be diluted in a small-volume flexible plastic container (e.g., 50 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride or 5% glucose) and prominently labelled: **"WARNING – FOR INTRAVENOUS USE ONLY - FATAL IF GIVEN BY OTHER ROUTES"** [Australian].
- **Syringes:** Syringes should not be used for administration to protect against accidental spinal route delivery [Australian].
- **Infusion:** Administer over 5 to 10 minutes. The vein must be flushed thoroughly following administration [Australian].

### Clinical Management
If a patient fails to respond after 3 to 6 doses, it is unlikely that additional doses will be beneficial [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Intrathecal administration:** Contraindicated and usually results in death [Australian].
- **Neurological Disorders:** Patients with the demyelinating form of Charcot-Marie-Tooth Syndrome [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to vincristine, other vinca alkaloids, or any excipients [Australian].
- **Radiation Therapy:** Must not be administered while patients are receiving radiation therapy through ports that include the liver [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated during treatment [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- **FATAL IF GIVEN BY OTHER ROUTES:** Vincristine is for **intravenous use only**. Intrathecal administration causes ascending paralysis and is usually fatal [Australian].

### General Warnings
- **Neurotoxicity:** This is the most common dose-limiting side effect. It is generally sequential: sensory impairment/paraesthesia, followed by neuritic pain and motor difficulties. Discontinuation may be necessary if symptoms persist [Australian].
- **Vesicant Nature:** Vincristine is highly irritating. Extravasation can cause severe local ulceration and cellulitis. If leakage occurs, stop the infusion immediately and apply local heat and hyaluronidase to disperse the drug [Australian].
- **Pulmonary Toxicity:** Acute shortness of breath and severe bronchospasm have occurred, particularly when used with mitomycin. Onset can occur minutes to weeks after administration [Australian].

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Haematological:** A complete blood count (CBC) must be performed before each dose. While leucopenia is less common than with other vincas, both physician and patient must remain alert for signs of infection [Australian].
- **Hepatic:** Liver function tests (LFTs) are recommended at initiation and periodically due to hepatic metabolism [Australian].
- **Serum Uric Acid:** Frequent monitoring is required during the first 3 to 4 weeks of induction in acute leukaemia to prevent urate nephropathy [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally reversible and dose-related.

| Frequency | System Organ Class | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Skin | Alopecia (20% to 70% of patients) |
| | Metabolism | Decreased appetite |
| **Common (1-10%)** | Nervous System | Sensory loss, paraesthesia, difficulty walking, loss of deep-tendon reflexes, muscle wasting |
| | Gastrointestinal | Constipation, abdominal cramps |
| **Uncommon (0.1-1%)** | Haematological | Leucopenia, anaemia, thrombocytopenia |
| | Gastrointestinal | Paralytic ileus (especially in children/elderly) |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Immune System | Anaphylaxis, angioedema |
| | Endocrine | SIADH (hyponatraemia with high urinary sodium) |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Sensory | Vestibular/auditory damage (8th cranial nerve), deafness |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Intestinal necrosis/perforation, venoocclusive liver disease (especially in children), convulsions (often with hypertension), and myocardial infarction (in patients with prior mediastinal radiation) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors/Inducers:** Itraconazole and fluconazole may inhibit vincristine metabolism, leading to earlier or more severe neurotoxicity. St. John’s wort (inducer) should be used cautiously [Australian].
- **Neurotoxic Agents:** Additive neurotoxicity with L-asparaginase, isoniazid, and other drugs acting on the peripheral nervous system [Australian].
- **L-asparaginase:** Vincristine should be administered 12 to 24 hours **before** L-asparaginase to minimise toxicity; giving L-asparaginase first may reduce vincristine clearance [Australian].
- **Phenytoin:** Vincristine may reduce phenytoin blood levels and increase seizure activity [Australian].
- **Digoxin:** Absorption of digoxin tablets may be decreased during chemotherapy [Australian].
- **G-CSFs:** (Filgrastim/Pegfilgrastim) should be administered at least 24 hours after vincristine to avoid increased risk of myelosuppression [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category D.** Vincristine can cause fetal harm and is teratogenic in animal studies. Highly effective contraception is required during treatment and for at least **7 months** (females) or **4 months** (males) following the last dose [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. If treatment is necessary, breastfeeding should be discontinued or the mother should wait 1 month after the last dose before resuming [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Azoospermia and amenorrhoea may occur in post-pubertal patients; recovery is variable. Pre-pubertal patients are less likely to experience permanent infertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use in various childhood malignancies is established. See Section 3 for weight-based dosing in infants [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** More susceptible to neurotoxic effects and urinary retention (especially males with obstructive uropathy). Paralytic ileus is more common in this group [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No specific data available; however, only 10-20% of the dose is excreted renally [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** A **50% dose reduction** is recommended for patients with a serum bilirubin > 3 mg/100 mL [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Vincristine binds to microtubular proteins (tubulin) of the mitotic spindle, preventing polymerisation and causing metaphase arrest in dividing cells [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Rapid and extensive tissue binding; 75% protein bound. Does not penetrate the blood-brain barrier effectively [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic via CYP3A4 [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Triphasic serum decay (half-lives: 5 min, 2.3 hours, and a terminal half-life ranging from 19 to 155 hours). Excretion is primarily biliary (80% in faeces) [Australian].
