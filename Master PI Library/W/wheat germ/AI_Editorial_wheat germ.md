# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the **United States (FDA)** for Levocarnitine Injection.
- **Purpose:** This document is intended as a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial interpretation and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Clinical decisions should be based on the official PI approved in the practitioner's specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic management of patients with genetic metabolic defects resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose: 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
    - In severe metabolic crises: A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - Dosing frequency: Every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
    - Maximum recorded dose: 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
    - Administration: Slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - Dose adjustments: May be reduced to 5 mg/kg as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma carnitine levels [US].

### Administration and Monitoring
- **Administration:** Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use. Discard any unused portion as the product contains no preservatives [US].
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels before starting parenteral therapy. Monitor weekly and monthly, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** No specific contraindications are listed in the provided source, though hypersensitivity to the active ingredient is a clinical deterrent [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and monitor if re-administration is attempted [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with the occurrence of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known seizure disorder, an increase in frequency or severity of seizures has been reported [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulations):** While the injection is indicated for ESRD, high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severe renal impairment may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events reported in clinical trials for ESRD patients at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) include:

| Body System | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain (9%), Asthenia (9%), Chest pain (12%), Fever (7%), Headache (22%), Infection (15%), Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%) |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea (16%), Nausea (8%), Vomiting (15%) |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia (9%), Hyperkalemia (6%), Hypervolemia (5%) |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness (13%), Paresthesia (5%), Depression (5%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (23%), Cough increase (9%), Rhinitis (9%) |

- **Serious/Post-Marketing Effects:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm, and seizures [US].
- **Other Notable Effects:** Transient nausea/vomiting, gastritis, and body odor [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended to monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category C]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of fetal harm or impaired fertility. Use during pregnancy only if clearly required [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after exogenous administration. Weigh the benefits to the mother against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg), which applies to paediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the source document.
- **Renal Impairment:** Indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis via the intravenous route. Caution is advised regarding the accumulation of metabolites (TMA/TMAO) if oral formulations are used in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation and energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn metabolic errors, it promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly cleared [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and [3H]-γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis showed that trough levocarnitine levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses over 12 and 24 weeks demonstrated dose-proportionate increases in trough carnitine concentrations. Note: While carnitine levels increase, the effect on specific clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and avoid excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Once mixed in Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL), the solution is stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
