# Editorial Product Information: WEZLANA (ustekinumab)

## Editorial Note
This editorial Product Information (PI) document has been synthesised from the official regulatory documents of the following jurisdictions:
*   **Australian (TGA)**: Australian Product Information – WEZLANA (ustekinumab)
*   **US (FDA)**: WEZLANA (ustekinumab-auub) Prescribing Information

The purpose of this document is to provide healthcare professionals with a harmonised, clinical overview of **WEZLANA**. This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient**: Ustekinumab (human IgG1κ monoclonal antibody). [US: Referred to as ustekinumab-auub].
*   **Brand Name**: **WEZLANA**
*   **Chemical Class**: Interleukin-12 and -23 antagonist.
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths**:
    *   **Subcutaneous Injection**: 45 mg/0.5 mL (Single-dose vial and pre-filled syringe); 90 mg/1 mL (Single-dose pre-filled syringe).
    *   **Intravenous Infusion**: 130 mg/26 mL (5 mg/mL) (Single-dose vial).
*   **Routes of Administration**: Subcutaneous (SC) or Intravenous (IV) infusion.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Amgen Australia Pty Ltd [Australian]; Amgen Inc. (for Nuvaila) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Plaque Psoriasis (PsO)**: Treatment of moderate to severe plaque psoriasis in adults and paediatric patients 6 years of age and older who are candidates for phototherapy or systemic therapy.
*   **Psoriatic Arthritis (PsA)**: Treatment of active psoriatic arthritis in adults [Both] and paediatric patients 6 years of age and older [US-approved; not Australian-approved for paediatrics]. May be used alone or in combination with methotrexate.
*   **Crohn’s Disease (CD)**: Treatment of adult patients with moderately to severely active Crohn’s disease. [Australian PI specifies use in patients with inadequate response, loss of response, or intolerance to conventional therapy or TNFα antagonists].
*   **Ulcerative Colitis (UC)**: Treatment of adult patients with moderately to severely active ulcerative colitis.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Plaque Psoriasis and Psoriatic Arthritis (Adults)
*   **Weight $\le$ 100 kg**: 45 mg SC at Week 0 and Week 4, then every 12 weeks thereafter.
*   **Weight > 100 kg**: 90 mg SC at Week 0 and Week 4, then every 12 weeks thereafter. [US notes 45 mg was also efficacious in this group, but 90 mg resulted in greater efficacy].
*   **Dose Adjustment**: If a patient inadequately responds to 12-weekly dosing, consideration may be given to dosing every 8 weeks [Australian].
*   **Discontinuation**: Treatment should be discontinued if no response is observed after 28 weeks [Australian].

### Paediatric Plaque Psoriasis (Ages 6-17 years)
Dosing is weight-based, administered SC at Week 0, Week 4, and every 12 weeks thereafter:
*   **Weight < 60 kg**: 0.75 mg/kg.
*   **Weight 60 kg to 100 kg**: 45 mg.
*   **Weight > 100 kg**: 90 mg.

### Paediatric Psoriatic Arthritis (Ages 6-17 years)
*   **[US-approved only]**: Dosing follows the same weight-based regimen as paediatric plaque psoriasis.

### Crohn’s Disease and Ulcerative Colitis (Adults)
*   **Initial Induction (IV)**: A single tiered weight-based IV infusion:
    *   $\le$ 55 kg: 260 mg.
    *   > 55 kg to $\le$ 85 kg: 390 mg.
    *   > 85 kg: 520 mg.
*   **Maintenance (SC)**: 90 mg SC starting 8 weeks after the initial IV dose, then every 8 weeks thereafter. 
*   **Alternative Maintenance**: Some patients may be transitioned to 90 mg SC every 12 weeks according to clinical judgment; however, if response is lost, dosing should return to every 8 weeks [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
*   **Subcutaneous**: For use under the guidance of a healthcare professional. Patients may self-inject after proper training. Rotate injection sites (upper arms, abdomen, buttocks, or thighs). Avoid psoriatic plaques.
*   **Intravenous**: Must be diluted in 0.9% or 0.45% [US only] sodium chloride and administered over at least one hour using an in-line, sterile, non-pyrogenic, low protein-binding filter (0.2 micrometer).

## 4. Contraindications
*   Severe hypersensitivity to ustekinumab or any excipient.
*   **Clinically important active infection**: [Australian-specific contraindication].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Additional Monitoring**: **WEZLANA** is subject to additional monitoring in Australia [Australian].
*   **Serious Infections**: May increase risk of bacterial, fungal, and viral infections and reactivation of latent infections (e.g., TB, shingles). Do not initiate during a clinically important active infection. 
*   **Tuberculosis (TB)**: Evaluate patients for TB prior to initiation. Treat latent TB before starting **WEZLANA**.
*   **Malignancies**: As an immunosuppressant, **WEZLANA** may increase the risk of malignancy. Non-melanoma skin cancers (NMSC) have been reported; monitoring is required, especially in patients over 60 or those with prior PUVA/prolonged immunosuppression.
*   **Hypersensitivity**: Serious reactions including anaphylaxis and angioedema have occurred. Discontinue immediately if a reaction occurs.
*   **Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES)**: A rare neurological condition. Symptoms include headache, seizures, confusion, and visual disturbances. Discontinue if suspected.
*   **Non-infectious Pneumonia**: Cases of interstitial pneumonia, eosinophilic pneumonia, and cryptogenic organising pneumonia reported post-marketing. 
*   **Immunisations**: Avoid live vaccines (e.g., BCG) during treatment. BCG should be avoided for 1 year before, during, and 1 year after treatment [US; Australian recommends 15 weeks wait after last dose before live vaccines]. 

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Very Common (>10%)
*   Nasopharyngitis, Headache [Both].
*   Upper respiratory tract infection [Both].
*   COVID-19 [Australian].

### Common (1–10%)
*   Dizziness, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea.
*   Fatigue, pruritus, back pain, myalgia, arthralgia.
*   Injection site erythema and pain.
*   Sinusitis, oropharyngeal pain.

### Uncommon (0.1–1%)
*   Depression.
*   Herpes zoster, cellulitis, dental infections.
*   Injection site reactions (haemorrhage, haematoma, induration, swelling).

### Serious Adverse Effects
*   Serious infections (pneumonia, sepsis, abscesses).
*   Anaphylaxis.
*   Malignancies (prostate, colorectal, breast, NMSC).
*   PRES.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **CYP450 Substrates**: Formation of CYP450 enzymes can be altered by increased cytokines during chronic inflammation. **WEZLANA** may normalise these levels. Monitor drugs with narrow therapeutic indices (e.g., warfarin, cyclosporine) upon initiation or discontinuation.
*   **Live Vaccines**: Should not be given concurrently.
*   **Allergen Immunotherapy**: **WEZLANA** has not been evaluated in patients undergoing allergy shots; it may decrease tolerance and increase the risk of anaphylaxis.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy**:
    *   **[Australian: Category B1]**: No evidence of harm in animal studies; human data is limited.
    *   **[US]**: Notes IgG is actively transported across the placenta, particularly in the third trimester.
    *   **Contraception**: Women of childbearing potential should use effective contraception during and for at least 15 weeks after treatment.
*   **Breastfeeding**: Ustekinumab is excreted in very small amounts in human milk. It is a large protein likely degraded in the infant's GI tract. A risk-benefit decision should be made.
*   **Infant Exposure in Utero**: Administration of live vaccines to infants exposed in utero should be withheld for 6 months after birth [Both].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use**: Safety and efficacy established for PsO in patients $\ge$ 6 years. [US] also includes PsA $\ge$ 6 years. No data for CD/UC or children < 6 years.
*   **Elderly Use**: No major age-related differences in PK or safety observed, but the number of subjects $\ge$ 65 years is insufficient to determine if they respond differently.
*   **Renal/Hepatic Impairment**: Not studied; no dose recommendations can be made.

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action**: Binds to the shared p40 protein subunit of IL-12 and IL-23 cytokines. This prevents them from binding to the IL-12Rβ1 receptor on T-cells and natural killer cells, interrupting Th1 and Th17 cytokine pathways.
*   **Pharmacokinetics**:
    *   **Absorption**: Median $T_{max}$ is 7 to 13.5 days (SC). Bioavailability is ~57%.
    *   **Distribution**: Steady-state volume of distribution is 4.4 to 4.6 L in IBD populations.
    *   **Elimination**: Mean half-life is approximately 3 weeks (range 15–45 days).
    *   **Metabolism**: Expected to be degraded into small peptides and amino acids via catabolic pathways.

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Plaque Psoriasis**: Efficacy demonstrated in PHOENIX 1 and 2 trials. PASI 75 at Week 12 was achieved by 66–76% of patients on **WEZLANA** vs 3–4% on placebo.
*   **Psoriatic Arthritis**: Efficacy demonstrated in PSUMMIT I and II trials. ACR 20 at Week 24 was significantly higher in **WEZLANA** groups (~42–50%) vs placebo (~20–23%).
*   **Crohn’s Disease**: UNITI-1 (TNF-antagonist failures) and UNITI-2 (conventional therapy failures) showed significantly higher clinical remission rates at Week 8 with IV induction.
*   **Ulcerative Colitis**: UNIFI trial showed clinical remission at Week 8 (19% vs 7% placebo) and Week 44 (44% vs 24% placebo).
*   **Biosimilarity**: Study 20190230 (PK similarity) and Study 20190232 (clinical similarity in PsO) demonstrated that **WEZLANA** is highly similar to the reference product (Stelara).

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   Store at 2°C to 8°C. Refrigerate. Do not freeze.
*   Keep in the original carton to protect from light.
*   **Room Temperature Excursion**: If necessary, single-use vials or PFS may be stored at room temperature (up to 30°C) for a single period of up to 30 days. Once removed from the refrigerator, do not return it. Discard if not used within the 30-day period.
