# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- At the time of writing, official documents from other regional authorities (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian, Japanese) were not provided for comparison.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a clinical synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, current PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. 
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) sterile solution in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic management of patients with metabolic defects resulting in secondary deficiency [US].
- **Carnitine Deficiency in End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients undergoing chronic hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2-to-3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours (q3h or q4h) and must not be less frequent than every 6 hours (q6h) [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically remain at 50 mg/kg or as clinically required. Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Maintenance of plasma free carnitine concentrations between 35 and 60 µmol/L is suggested [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2-to-3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine concentrations (target range 40 to 50 µmol/L). Downward titrations (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None Known:** There are no listed contraindications in the provided source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued immediately and appropriate medical management initiated [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Levocarnitine has been associated with reports of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with known epilepsy, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Accumulation of Metabolites (Oral Form Caution):** While not specific to the injection, it is noted that chronic high-dose oral levocarnitine in patients with severely impaired renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Monitoring:** Periodic monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations is recommended [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions in the ESRD population are often confounded by the underlying pathology of renal failure.

| Frequency | Adverse Event [US] |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site reaction, pain (unspecified), headache, hypertension, hypotension, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, pharyngitis, dizziness, fever, cough increase. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, flu syndrome, infection, arrhythmia, cardiac disorder, tachycardia, dyspepsia, anorexia, anemia, hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight changes, leg cramps, depression, insomnia, paresthesia, rhinitis, pruritus, rash, eye disorder, urinary tract infection, kidney failure. |

- **Postmarketing Experience:** In addition to the above, serious hypersensitivity (anaphylaxis) and neurologic reactions (seizures) have been confirmed in the postmarketing setting [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and laryngeal edema are the most critical serious adverse effects identified [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate that levocarnitine may increase the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended to monitor INR levels more frequently after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not specified; formally "Category B" logic applied]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. In dairy cows, carnitine concentrations in milk increase following administration. The benefits of maternal therapy must be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no impairment of fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders apply to paediatric patients. Specific age-range safety profiles are not further detailed beyond general metabolic crisis management [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis (see Section 3). The safety of the **oral** form has not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrates for oxidation and subsequent ATP production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Levocarnitine can promote the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with specific metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted in the urine [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following intravenous administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; the mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Pharmacokinetic Study:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg after dialysis showed that trough levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** A study (N≈30 per group) comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses over 12 and 24 weeks demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations [US].
- **Note:** While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations is proven, the direct effect on specific clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
